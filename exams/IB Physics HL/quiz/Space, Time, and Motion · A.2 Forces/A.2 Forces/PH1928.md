# PH1928

An object floats at rest in two different liquids, $X$ and $Y$, as shown. The object is less submerged in liquid $X$ than in liquid $Y$.

![Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 9.45.36 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 9.45.36 AM.png>)

The density of liquid $X$ is $\rho$, and the buoyant force acting on the object in liquid $X$ is $F_b$.

Which of the following correctly compares the density of liquid $Y$ and the buoyant force acting on the object in liquid $Y$ with those in liquid $X$?

| | Density of $Y$ | Buoyant Force |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Equal to $\rho$ | Equal to $F_b$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Equal to $\rho$ | Different from $F_b$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Different from $\rho$ $\hspace{1.5em}$| Equal to $F_b$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Different from $\rho$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$ Different from $F_b$ $\hspace{1.5em}$|

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Since the object is floating at rest in both liquids, the buoyant force equals the weight of the object in each case. Therefore, the buoyant force is the same in liquid $X$ and liquid $Y$.

The object is less submerged in liquid $X$ than in liquid $Y$. This means that a smaller volume of liquid is displaced in liquid $X$. Using Archimedes' principle:

- $F_b=\rho Vg$

For the same buoyant force, a smaller displaced volume implies a larger liquid density. Hence, liquid $X$ is more dense than liquid $Y$, so the density of liquid $Y$ is different from $\rho$.
