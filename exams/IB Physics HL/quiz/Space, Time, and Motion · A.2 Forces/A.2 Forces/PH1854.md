# PH1854

A sphere of volume $V$ and density $\rho$ is immersed in a liquid of density $\rho_l$.

The sphere remains stationary at a certain depth held by a string which exerts a tension $T$.

![ph1854q](images/ph1854q.png)

The string suddenly breaks which allows the sphere to accelerate towards the surface of the liquid.

Which of the following correctly describes the magnitude of the tension $T$ before the string breaks and the acceleration $a$ of the sphere immediately after the string breaks?

$\\$

| | $\bf T$ | $\bf a$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\rho_l Vg$ | $\hspace{1.5em}\dfrac{\rho_l-\rho}{\rho_l}g\hspace{1.5em}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $(\rho_l-\rho)Vg$ | $\dfrac{\rho_l-\rho}{\rho_l}g$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\rho_l Vg$ | $\dfrac{\rho_l-\rho}{\rho}g$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $(\rho_l-\rho)Vg$ | $\dfrac{\rho_l-\rho}{\rho}g$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Before the string breaks, the sphere is held stationary and is acted upon by three forces. Two forces act downwards: weight and tension, and one force acts upwards: buoyant force.

Given that the forces are balanced it can be stated that:

- weight + tension = buoyant force

- $mg+T=\rho_l Vg$

The mass $m$ of the sphere can be expressed as $\rho V$, leading to:

- $\rho Vg+T=\rho_l Vg$

Which can be solved for $T$, giving:

- $T=(\rho_l-\rho)Vg$

To find the acceleration of the sphere, right after the string breaks ($T$ becomes zero) we must use Newton's 2$^{\textrm{nd}}$ law:

- buoyant force - weight $=ma$

Substituting in for these values and replacing $m$ with $\rho V$ gives

- $\rho_l Vg-\rho Vg=\rho Va$

Dividing this equation by $\rho V$ leads to:

- $\dfrac{\rho_l-\rho}{\rho}g=a$
