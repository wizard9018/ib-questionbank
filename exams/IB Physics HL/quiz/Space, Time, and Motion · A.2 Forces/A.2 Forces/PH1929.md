# PH1929

Two objects of masses $M$ and $m$ are connected by a light, inextensible string that passes over a smooth, frictionless pulley, as shown.

![Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 9.52.27 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 9.52.27 AM.png>)

The object of mass
$m$ moves vertically downward with a constant speed $v$ at the instant shown. The object of mass $M$ moves along a rough horizontal surface. The string makes an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal at the point where it is attached to $M$.

What is the coefficient of dynamic (kinetic) friction between the object of mass $M$ and the surface?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{m}{M}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{m}{M\cos\theta}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{m\cos\theta}{M-m\sin\theta}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{m\cos\theta}{M}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The vertical forces on mass
$m$ are its weight $W$ acting downward and the tension $T$ in the string acting upward. Because the mass moves at constant speed, the net force is zero, which means the upward and downward forces are equal.

- $T=mg$

The vertical forces on $M$ are the weight downward, and the normal force $F_N$ and vertical component of the tension upward. Since the mass $M$ is not accelerating, the resultant force on it must be zero.

- $F_N+T\sin\theta=Mg$

- $F_N+mg\sin\theta=Mg$

- $F_N=Mg-mg\sin\theta$

The horizontal forces on $M$ are the horizontal component of the tension to the right and friction $F_f$ to the left. These forces are equal and opposite.

- $F_f=T\cos\theta$

- $\mu_dF_N=mg\cos\theta$

- $\mu_d=\dfrac{mg\cos\theta}{F_N}=\dfrac{mg\cos\theta}{Mg-mg\sin\theta}=\dfrac{m\cos\theta}{M-m\sin\theta}$
