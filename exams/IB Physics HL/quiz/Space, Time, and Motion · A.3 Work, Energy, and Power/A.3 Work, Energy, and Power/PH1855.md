# PH1855

A ball with a mass of 200 g falls vertically downwards and bounces off of the ground. Just before hitting the ground, the ball is moving at a speed of 6.0 m s$^{-1}$. The graph below shows the net force exerted on the ball during the bounce.

![Screenshot 2025-09-23 at 4.14.40 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-09-23 at 4.14.40 PM.png>)

Which of the following best describes the energy lost while bouncing on the floor?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 1.6 J

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 2.0 J

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 3.6 J

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 5.2 J

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The area under the graph represents the magnitude of the change in momentum of the ball.

- Area under graph $=\dfrac{(40\times10^{-3}\textrm{ s})(100\textrm{ N})}{2}=2$ N s

We can now use the magnitude of the change in momentum to find the velocity of the ball after bouncing. It is important to consider that the change in momentum is upwards, while the initial velocity of the ball is downwards. Using upwards as positive we get.

- $\begin{aligned}
\ \Delta p&=mv_f-mv_i\\
\\\
\ 2\textrm{ N s}&=(0.200\textrm{ kg})v-(0.200\textrm{ kg})(-6.00\textrm{ m s}^{-1})\\
\end{aligned}$

This can be solved to find $v=4.00$ m s $^{-1}$.

The speed of the ball after bouncing is then $4.00$ m s$^{-1}$.

The initial and final kinetic energies are then given by:

- $K_i=\dfrac{1}{2}mv_i^2=\dfrac{1}{2}(0.200\textrm{ kg})(6.00\textrm{ m s}^{-1})^2=3.6$ J

- $K_f=\dfrac{1}{2}mv_f^2=\dfrac{1}{2}(0.200\textrm{ kg})(4.00\textrm{ m s}^{-1})^2=1.6$ J

This means that the energy lost during the collision is $\Delta E_k=2.0$ J.
