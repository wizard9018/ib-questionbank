# PH1932

A uniform disk is free to rotate about a fixed horizontal axis through point $O$. A light, inextensible thread is wrapped around the disk and attached to a box of mass $M$. The box is released from rest and begins to fall, causing the disk to rotate.

![Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 10.13.36 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 10.13.36 AM.png>)

Consider the following statements about the system:

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The box $M$ has no angular momentum about point $O$. |
  | II. | The total angular momentum of the disk-box system about point $O$ is conserved. |
  | III. | The moment of inertia of the disk about point $O$ remains constant. |

Which of the statements are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The angular momentum of the box about point $O$ could be found using the following equation:

- $L=mvr$

Where $v$ is the velocity of the box, and $r$ is the perpendicular distance between the line of action of velocity and the pivot. Since none of them is zero, the angular momentum of the box about point $O$ is non-zero.

As the speed of the box increases, the angular speed of the disc also increases. Hence, the magnitude of the angular momentum increases and is not conserved. This conclusion is consistent with the presence of an external torque acting on the system, due to the weight of the box.

The moment of inertia of the disc depends only on its mass distribution and the axis of rotation. Since neither changes, the moment of inertia of the disc about point $O$ remains constant.
