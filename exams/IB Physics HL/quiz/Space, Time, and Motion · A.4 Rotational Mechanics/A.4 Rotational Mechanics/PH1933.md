# PH1933

A disk rotates about a fixed vertical axis with an initial angular speed $\omega$. It experiences a constant angular deceleration and comes to rest after completing 4 full revolutions.

![Screenshot 2026-02-26 at 3.08.48 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-26 at 3.08.48 PM.png>)

What is the magnitude of the angular deceleration of the disk?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\omega^2}{16\pi}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{16\pi}{\omega^2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\omega^2}{8\pi}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{8\pi}{\omega^2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The disk undergoes uniformly decelerated rotational motion, so the kinematic equations for angular motion apply. The disk starts with an initial angular speed $\omega$ and comes to rest, so the final angular speed is zero. During this time, it completes 4 full revolutions, which corresponds to an angular displacement $\theta$:

- $\theta=(4)(2\pi)=8\pi$ rad

Using the rotational kinematic equation:

- $(\omega_f)^2=(\omega_i)^2+2\alpha\theta$

- $0=\omega^2+2\alpha(8\pi)$

- $\alpha=\dfrac{-\omega^2}{16\pi}$
