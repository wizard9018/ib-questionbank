## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Superposition/interference of light from each slit $\checkmark$

        Path difference is any odd multiple of $\pi$ / odd multiple of $180\degree$ / odd multiple of half wavelength. $\checkmark$

        Leading to destructive interference. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        At certain specific points on the screen the path difference for light arriving from each slit will have a phase difference of an odd multiple of half the wavelength of light (phase difference of an odd multiple of $\pi$).
This would lead to (partially) destructive interference which will be observed as darker regions on the screen.

    2. At point P a single slit diffraction minimum occurs. $\checkmark$

        Double slit interference pattern is modulated by single slit diffraction. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The double-slit interference pattern is formed by a series of alternating maxima and minima of intensity.

        At point $P$ a bright fringe from the double slit pattern ($n=8$) would be seen. However, the double slit interference pattern is modulated by the single slit diffraction pattern.

        A diffraction minimum is formed at $P$ which causes the missing maximum.

    3. $s=\dfrac{16.0\times 10^{-3}\, \ce{m}}{8}=2\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m}=0.002$ m  $\checkmark$

        $\ll s=\dfrac{\lambda D}{d}\gg$

        $d=\ll \dfrac{\lambda D}{s}=\dfrac{(436 \times 10^{-9}\, \ce{m})(2.00\, \ce{m})}{0.002\, \ce{m}}\gg=$ :answer[$4.36\times 10^{-4}\, \ce{m}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The distance from the central maximum to point $P$ (on the screen) is $16.0$ mm which corresponds to the eighth-order maximum ($n=8$) of the double slit interference pattern.

        The separation between fringes is then:

        - $s=\dfrac{16.0\, \ce{mm}}{8}=2.00$ mm

        This value can also be expressed in terms of the wavelength $\lambda$, the distance to the screen $D$ and the distance between the slits $d$ according to this equation from the formula booklet:

        - $s=\dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$

        Solving this equation for $d$ leads to

        - $d=\dfrac{\lambda D}{s}=\dfrac{(436 \times 10^{-9}\, \ce{m})(2.00\, \ce{m})}{0.002\, \ce{m}}$

        - $d=4.36\times 10^{-4}$ m

    4. $\theta = \dfrac{8.00\times 10^{-3}\, \ce{m}}{2.00\,\ce{m}}=0.004\ll \text{rad}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{b}$

        $b=\dfrac{\lambda}{\theta}=\dfrac{436\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{m}}{0.004}=$ :answer[$1.09\times 10^{-4}$ m] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Given that the distance from the slits to the screen ($D=2.00$ m) is much higher than the distance from the central maximum to $P$ ($16.0\, \ce{mm}$), the angle of the first order diffraction minimum can be approximated according to:

        - $\theta = \sin{\theta}=\tan{\theta}=\dfrac{s}{D}$

        On the screen, the distance from the central maximum to the first minimum of the single-slit pattern is $\dfrac{16.0\, \ce{mm}}{2}=8.00$ mm

        Therefore:

        - $\theta=\dfrac{8.00\times 10^{-3}\,\ce{m}}{2.00\,\ce{m}}=0.004$

        This angle corresponds to the first order diffraction minimum which can also be expressed in terms of the wavelength $\lambda$ and the width of the slit $b$ with this equation from the formula booklet:

        - $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{b}$

        Solving this equation for $b$ leads to

        - $b=\dfrac{\lambda}{\theta}=\dfrac{436\times 10^{-9}\, \ce{m}}{0.004}=1.09\times 10^{-4}$ m

        This value represents the width of each of the two slits.

2. 1. de Broglie's hypothesis states that matter can behave both as a wave and as a particle. $\checkmark$

        Electron diffraction shows that electrons (classically assumed to be particles), can also behave as waves. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Electricity and magnetism experimental evidence suggested that electrons were negatively charged particles with a given mass.

        De Broglie hypothesis suggested matter could behave both as a particle and as a wave (just like light)

        Electron diffraction provided experimental evidence to support de Broglie's hypothesis because the results could only be explained by using a wave model to describe the electrons.

    2. Photoelectric effect / Compton scattering $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The photo-electric effect described in Einstein's work involves the interaction between particles of light (photons) and electrons which are emitted from a surface.
This experiment could not be explained by a wave-model for light.

        Compton scattering involves a decrease in frequency (increase in wavelength) of photons which are incident on electrons. Due to this collision, there is an energy transfer from the photons to the electrons causing a decrease in frequency on the photons. This change in frequency could not be explained by a wave-model for light.
