## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{(1.2)(0.15)^2}{0.02}}$ $\checkmark$\
        $\ll v=1.16\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}\gg$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Since the rails are smooth, the elastic energy of the spring equals the kinetic energy of the rod

        - $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

        - $(1.2)(0.15)^2=(0.02)v^2$

        - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{(1.2)(0.15)^2}{0.02}}=1.16\,\ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

    2. $\varepsilon\ll=(13)(1.16)(0.04)\gg=0.60\,\ll \ce{V}\gg$ $\checkmark$\
        $I\ll=\dfrac{0.60}{2}\gg=0.30\,\ll \ce{A}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The rod is moving perpendicular to the magnetic field. This induces emf in the circuit

        - $\varepsilon=BvL$

        - $\varepsilon=(13)(1.16)(0.04)=0.60\, \ce{V}$

        Using the relationship between voltage and resistance,

        - $I=\dfrac{V}{R}=\dfrac{0.60}{2}=0.30$ A

2. 1. Horizontal force to the left $\checkmark$

        Current is upwards. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Current is induced in the rod due to its motion in the magnetic field. With the motion of the rod, the enclosed area and therefore the flux into the page decreases. Current will be induced to oppose this change, so the induced current will create a magnetic field into the page. This will be a clockwise current, therefore the direction of this current is upwards.\
        A magnetic force will act on the wire carrying current to oppose the change in flux. The direction of this force is to the left.

        ![PH1156bi2](images/PH1156bi2.png)

    2. The rod slows down/decelerates $\checkmark$\
        Induced current decreases $\checkmark$\
        Acceleration decreasing $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The rod is slowing down due to the magnetic force acting opposite to the direction of its motion.

        As the rod slows down, the induced emf decreases

        - $\varepsilon=BvL$

        Then the current decreases.\
        Since the force is magnetic, then it decreases

        - $F=BIL$

        Using Newton's second law

        - $F=ma$

        The acceleration of the rod decreases as it moves through the magnetic field.

    3. Horizontal line before $t_1$ $\checkmark$\
        Curve from $t_1$ to $t_2$ with decreasing gradient $\checkmark$\
        Same curve continues after $t_2$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The rod moves with constant speed after being released from the spring till it enters the first magnetic field.

        The magnetic force in the first magnetic field opposes its motion and its speed is decreasing with a decreasing deceleration.

        When the rod enters the second magnetic field, the current will be in the opposite direction. The magnetic force will also be to the left opposing the direction of motion. This leads to the same decrease in deceleration.

        ![PH1156biii3](images/PH1156biii3.png)

3. 1. Lightly damped oscillation $\checkmark$\
        Because of the magnetic force $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        As the spring is released it will oscillate.\
        Current will be induced in the rod and a magnetic force will act on the rod.

        As the rod moves to the right, current is induced in the rod and magnetic force will be to the left. Both current and magnetic force are decreasing.

        As the rod moves to the left, current will be in opposite direction and magnetic force is to the right. Both current and magnetic force are decreasing.

        The oscillation is lightly damped oscillation due to the presence of the magnetic force.

        The acceleration of the spring depends on the sum of the restoring force of the spring and the magnetic force.

        The amplitude of the oscillation decreases slowly with time until the rod-spring system stops

    2. Wider and lower amplitude $\checkmark$\
        Frequency of maximum amplitude is less $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In the absence of the magnetic field, there’s no damping. The maximum amplitude is at the resonance frequency.

        When there's damping due to the magnetic field, the maximum amplitude is lower and at a lower frequency.

        ![PH1156cii2](images/PH1156cii2.png)

4. 1. Centripetal force is the magnetic force / $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}=qvB$ $\checkmark$\
        $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The electron moves in a circular path inside the magnetic field.\
        The centripetal force is the magnetic force.

        - $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}=qvB$
        - $mv=qBr=eBr$

        Since

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}=\dfrac{h}{mv}$

        Then

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{eBr}$

    2. Same initial wavelength. $\checkmark$\
        Electron in magnetic field its wavelength doesn't change. $\checkmark$\
        Electron in electric field its wavelength decreases. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Since both electrons have the same initial velocity, this means they have the same initial wavelength as

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}=\dfrac{h}{mv}$

        For the electron entering the magnetic field, it takes a circular path with a constant speed, then its wavelength doesn’t change.

        For the second electron entering the electric field, it accelerates since it is moving opposite to the electric field.

        As the speed increases, its wavelength decreases.
