# PH0735

A mass of 700 g is placed onto a flat piece of card that is attached to a vertical spring with a spring constant ($k$) of $140\ \ce{N}\ \ce{m}^{-1}$ and the lower end of the spring is fastened to the floor. The mass of the spring and the card is considered to be negligible. When pressed lightly and released the mass will move with simple harmonic motion.

What is the minimum amplitude of the motion that will cause the mass to lose contact with the card? [Take $g = 10\ \ce{N kg}^{-2}$]

![PH0735](images/PH0735.jpg)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.025 m

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.05 m
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.075 m
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.10 m

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

As the block oscillates, the net force on it varies. If we draw a free-body diagram for the mass, we see that it experiences a normal force upwards from the card and the downward force of gravity. Note that there is no force from the spring that acts directly on the block.

![Screenshot 2024-10-30 at 3.08.42 PM](<images/Screenshot 2024-10-30 at 3.08.42 PM.JPEG>)

The normal force will vary, as will the net force. At the point where the block is about to lose contact with the card, the normal force will approach zero, and therefore the net force is $F_g$.

- $F_{net} = F_g$
- $ma = mg$
- $a = g$

Therefore the block will lose contact with the card when the maximum acceleration required is equal to the acceleration due to gravity.

We also know that for the system,

- $F = -kx$

and

- $ma = -kx$

So

- $a = -\dfrac{k}{m}x$

We can solve for the amplitude $x_o$ and substitute $10\,\ce{m s}^{-2}$ for the acceleration to get

$x_o = -(10)\dfrac{m}{k}=-(10)\dfrac{0.7}{140}= 0.05\,\ce{m}$
