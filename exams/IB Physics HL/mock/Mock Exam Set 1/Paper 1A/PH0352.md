# PH0352

A longitudinal wave is travelling in a medium from left to right. The graph shows the variation of the displacement of the particles of this medium with distance along the medium $x$.

![PH0352aaa](images/PH0352aaa.png)

$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

Which point on the graph is at the center of a rarefaction? Displacements to the right of equilibrium positions are positive.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

The particles of the medium in which a longitudinal wave is travelling undergo compressions and rarefactions:

![PH0352-1](images/PH0352-1.png)

 

At rarefactions particles are moving away from each other. At compressions, particles are moving towards each other.

At the center of both compressions and rarefactions the displacement of a particle is zero.

Displacements to the right of equilibrium positions are positive, and displacements to the left of equilibrium positions are negative.

 

![PH0352-2](images/PH0352-2.png)

$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

Particle A has therefore been displaced to the left of its equilibrium position, and particle C has been displaced to the right of its equilibrium position. 

Therefore B is at the center of a rarefaction, as particles immediately to its right are displaced farther to the right, and particles immediately to B’s left are displaced farther to the left.

If we consider point D, we know that point C has been displaced in the positive direction which is to the right. Therefore C is displaced closer to D. This supports the fact that point D is in the middle of a compression.
