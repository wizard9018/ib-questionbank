# AA1358

**Maximum mark: 9**

The diagram shows a parallelogram $OABC$ with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$.

The angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ is $\theta$ where $0 < \theta < \pi$.
Point $P$ lies on $[AB]$ such that $\overrightarrow{AP} = k\,\overrightarrow{AB}$ with $0 < k < 1$.

![AA1358-q](images/AA1358-q.JPEG)

1.    Write down $\overrightarrow{OP}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ in terms of $\bm{a}$, $\bm{c}$ and $k$. [2 marks]

It is known that $|\mathbf{a}| = 2$, $|\mathbf{c}| = 3$, $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} = 3$ and that $\overrightarrow{OP}$ is perpendicular to $\overrightarrow{AC}$.

2.    1.    Show that $\displaystyle k = \frac{|\mathbf{a}|^{2} - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}}{|\mathbf{c}|^{2} - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}}$.

    2.    Hence, write down the value of $k$. [3 marks]

2.    Show that
    1.    $\overrightarrow{OP} \times \overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{c}$;

    2.    $\cos \theta =\dfrac{1}{2}$. [2 marks]

3.    Hence, find the area of triangle $OPC$. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    We are given $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$.
Since $OABC$ is a parallelogram, $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$.

    Then, using $\overrightarrow{AP} = k\,\overrightarrow{AB}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \overrightarrow{OP} &= \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AP}\\[6pt]
        &= \mathbf{a}+ k\mathbf{c} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    For $\overrightarrow{AC}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \overrightarrow{AC} &= \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{OC}\\[6pt]
        &= -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}\\[6pt]
        &= \mathbf{c}- \mathbf{a} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.    1.    Since $\overrightarrow{OP} \perp \overrightarrow{AC}$, their scalar product is zero. Hence we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                \overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC} &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
            \end{aligned}
        $$
        Substituting the expression from part a
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                (\mathbf{a} + k\mathbf{c}) \cdot (\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}) &= 0\\[6pt]
                \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{a} + k\mathbf{c} \cdot \mathbf{c} - k\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} &= 0\\[6pt]
            \end{aligned}
        $$
        Recall that $\bm{a}\cdot\bm{a}=|\bm{a}|^2$, hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} - |\mathbf{a}|^2 + k|\mathbf{c}|^2 - k(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
            \end{aligned}
        $$
        Rearranging to solve for $k$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                k|\mathbf{c}|^2 - k(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) &= |\mathbf{a}|^2 - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}\\[6pt]
                k(|\mathbf{c}|^2 - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) &= |\mathbf{a}|^2 - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}\\[6pt]
                k&= \dfrac{|\mathbf{a}|^2 - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}}{|\mathbf{c}|^2 - \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Substituting the given values $|\mathbf{a}| = 2$, $|\mathbf{c}| = 3$, and $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} = 3$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            k &= \frac{4 - 3}{9 - 3}\\[6pt]
            &= \dfrac{1}{6} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.    1.    Expanding the vector cross product, using its distributive property, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                \overrightarrow{OP} \times \overrightarrow{OC} &= (\mathbf{a} + k\mathbf{c}) \times \mathbf{c} \\[6pt]
                &= \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{c} + k(\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{c})\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
                &= \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{c} + \mathbf{0}\\[6pt]
                &= \mathbf{a}\times \mathbf{c}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
            \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.    Using the __scalar product formula (3.13)__, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} &= |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{c}|\cos\theta\\[6pt]
                3 &= 2 \times 3 \times \cos\theta \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
                \cos\theta &= \frac{3}{6}\\[6pt]
                \cos\theta  &=\dfrac{1}{2}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
            \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  The area of triangle $OPC$ is half of the area of the parallelogram with adjacent sides $OP$ and $OC$.

    Using this and the formula for the __area of a parallelogram (3.16)__ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \text{Area} &= \frac{1}{2}|\overrightarrow{OP} \times \overrightarrow{OC}|\\[12pt]
        &= \frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{c}|\\[12pt]
        &= \frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{c}|\sin\theta
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since we know $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ from part c(ii), we have $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

    Therefore $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

    Substituting in these values, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &= \frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 3 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
        \text{Area} &= \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
