# AA1361

**Maximum mark: 19**

Consider the family of functions $f_n(x)=\sin^n x$, where $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$.

1.    1.    By writing $\sin^n x=\sin^{\,n-1}x\,\sin x$,

        show that $\displaystyle \int \sin^n x\,\mathrm{d}x = -\,\sin^{\,n-1}x\cos x + (n-1)\int \sin^{\,n-2}x\,\cos^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x$.

    2.    Hence deduce that, for $n>1$,

        $\displaystyle \int f_n(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = -f_{n-1}(x)\cos x + (n-1)\int f_{n-2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-(n-1)\int f_n(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$. [4 marks]

2.    Hence, show that for $n>1$,

    $\displaystyle \int f_n(x)\,\mathrm{d}x= -\frac{1}{n}\,f_{n-1}(x)\cos x + \frac{n-1}{n}\int f_{n-2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$. [2 marks]

3.    Hence, show that $\displaystyle \int \sin^4 x\,\mathrm{d}x=-\dfrac{1}{4}\sin^3 x\cos x - \dfrac{3}{8}\sin x\cos x + \dfrac{3}{8}x + C$.

    Where $C$ is the constant of integration. [3 marks]

The region $R$ is enclosed by the graph of $y=\arcsin{\sqrt{x}} $, the $y$-axis and the line $y=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

$R$ is rotated by $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis to form a solid of revolution with a volume of $a\,\text{units}^3$.

4.    Find the value of $a$. [5 marks]

Consider $g$, which is function of $t$ such that $g(t)=\displaystyle \int_0^t \sin^4{x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{1}{4}\sin^3{t}\cos{t}-\dfrac{3}{4}t$.

5.    Find $\displaystyle \lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\dfrac{g(t)}{\tan{t}+2t}\right)$. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  Let's begin by integrating $f$ using $\sin^n x = \sin^{n-1} x \sin x$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \int f_n(x)\,\mathrm{d}x&=\int \sin^n x \,\mathrm{d}x\\[12pt]
        &= \int \sin^{n-1} x \sin x \, \mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We now have a product of functions for which we can use integration by parts.

        If we let $u = \sin^{n-1} x$ and $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} = \sin x$,
        Then $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} = (n-1)\sin^{n-2} x \cos x$ and $v = -\cos x$
        $\\[-2.5em]$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Using the __integration by parts formula (5.16)__
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \displaystyle \int u \, \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\,\mathrm{d}x = uv - \int v \, \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}\,\mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        and substituting in the parts above, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        & \sin^{n-1} x \cdot  (-\cos x)- \int (-\cos x) \cdot (n-1)\sin^{n-2} x \cos x \, \mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &= -\sin^{n-1}x \cos x + (n-1) \int \sin^{n-2} x \cos^2 x \, \mathrm{d}x\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using $f_n(x)=\sin^n x$ we can write
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \sin^{n-1} x&=f_{n-1}(x)\\[6pt]
        \sin^{n-2} x&=f_{n-2}(x)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, we can rewrite the result in a(ii) as
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \int f_n(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
        = -\,f_{n-1}(x)\cos x \;+\; (n-1)\!\int f_{n-2}(x)\,\cos^2 x\,\mathrm{d}x \hspace{2em}[1]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        However, this isn't quite in the desired form as we have a $\cos^2{x}$ term in the second term. We need to rewrite the second term of the above.

        To do that let's use the identity $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$, which gives

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \int f_{n-2}(x)\cos^2 x \, \mathrm{d}x\tag*{\textbf{M1}}
        &= \int f_{n-2}(x)(1 - \sin^2 x)\, \mathrm{d}x \\[15pt]
        &= \int \left[ f_{n-2}(x) - f_{n-2}(x)\sin^2 x \right] \mathrm{d}x \\[15pt]
        &= \int \left[ f_{n-2}(x) - \sin^{n-2} x \sin^2 x \right] \mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[15pt]
        &= \int \left[ f_{n-2}(x) - \sin^{n} x \right] \mathrm{d}x \\[15pt]
        &= \int f_{n-2}(x)\, \mathrm{d}x - \int f_n(x)\, \mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Substituting this result into [1] we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &\int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x =\\[12pt]
        &\displaystyle -f_{n-1}(x) \cos x + (n-1) \int f_{n-2}(x) \, \mathrm{d}x - (n-1) \int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x\\[12pt]
        &\hspace{5em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  From part (a)(ii), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x &= -f_{n-1}(x) \cos x + (n-1) \int f_{n-2}(x) \, \mathrm{d}x - (n-1) \int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Adding $(n-1) \displaystyle \int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x$ to both sides and simplifying, we have
    $\\[-2.5em]$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x + (n-1) \int f_n(x) \, \mathrm{d}x &= -f_{n-1}(x) \cos x + (n-1) \int f_{n-2}(x) \, \mathrm{d}x\\[15pt]

    Then, dividing both sides by $n$, we have
    $$

    &= \displaystyle-\frac{1}{n} f_{n-1}(x) \cos x + \frac{n-1}{n} \int f_{n-2}(x) \, \mathrm{d}x \hspace{1em}\text{... as required}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}

3.  Using the formula from part (b) with $n = 4$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \sin^4 x \, \mathrm{d}x &= -\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 x \cos x + \frac{3}{4} \int \sin^2 x \, \mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We could attempt to integrate $\sin^2{x}$ using a trig identify or we could use the formula again with $n = 2$, which gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \sin^2 x \, \mathrm{d}x &= -\frac{1}{2} \sin x \cos x + \frac{1}{2} \int \sin^0 x \, \mathrm{d}x\\[15pt]
    &= -\frac{1}{2} \sin x \cos x + \frac{1}{2} \int 1 \, \mathrm{d}x\\[15pt]
    &= -\frac{1}{2} \sin x \cos x + \frac{1}{2} x + C_1 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Combining those results we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \sin^4 x \, \mathrm{d}x &= -\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 x \cos x + \frac{3}{4} \left(-\frac{1}{2} \sin x \cos x + \frac{1}{2} x + C_1\right)\\[15pt]
    &= -\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 x \cos x - \frac{3}{8} \sin x \cos x + \frac{3}{8} x + \frac{3}{4}C_1 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[15pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{4} \sin^3 x \cos x - \dfrac{3}{8} \sin x \cos x + \dfrac{3}{8} x + C\hspace{1em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    where $C = \dfrac{3}{4}C_1$ is the constant of integration.

4.  We have a formula for __volume of revolution around the $y$-axis (5.17)__.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \pi \int_a^{b} x^2 \, \mathrm{d}y
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    To use the formula, first we need to find $x$ in terms of $y$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y&=\arcsin {\sqrt x}\\[6pt]
    \sin y&=\sqrt x\\[6pt]
    x&=(\sin y)^2\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting this and the boundaries $a=0$ and $b=\frac{ \pi}{2}$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \pi \int_0^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{2}} (\sin^2 y)^2 \, \mathrm{d}y\\[15pt]
    &= \pi \int_0^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^4 y \, \mathrm{d}y \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the result from part (c), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \pi \left[-\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 y \cos y - \frac{3}{8} \sin y \cos y + \frac{3}{8} y\right]_0^{ \textstyle \frac{\pi}{2}} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[15pt]
    &= \pi \left[\left(-\frac{1}{4}(1)^3(0) - \frac{3}{8}(1)(0) + \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \left(-\frac{1}{4}(0)^3(1) - \frac{3}{8}(0)(1) + \frac{3}{8}(0)\right)\right]\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[15pt]
    &= \pi \left[\frac{3\pi}{16} - 0\right]\\[15pt]
    V&= \frac{3\pi^2}{16}\hspace{1em}\text{units}^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $a=\dfrac{3\pi^2}{16}$

5. Let's evaluate the first term of $g(t)$ using the result from part (c).
    Hence, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_0^t \sin^4 x \, \mathrm{d}x &= \left[-\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 x \cos x - \frac{3}{8} \sin x \cos x + \frac{3}{8} x\right]_0^t\\[15pt]
    &= -\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 t \cos t - \frac{3}{8} \sin t \cos t + \frac{3}{8} t - 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g(t) &= -\frac{1}{4} \sin^3 t \cos t - \frac{3}{8} \sin t \cos t + \frac{3}{8} t + \frac{1}{4} \sin^3 t \cos t - \frac{3}{4} t\\[15pt]
    &= -\frac{3}{8} \sin t \cos t + \frac{3}{8} t - \frac{3}{4} t\\[15pt]
    g(t)&= -\frac{3}{8} \sin t \cos t - \frac{3}{8} t \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we want to find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{t \to 0} \left(\frac{-\frac{3}{8}\sin t\cos t-\frac{3}{8}t}{\tan t + 2t}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As $t \to 0$, both the numerator and denominator tend to $0$, giving the indeterminate form $\frac{0}{0}$.

    This means we can apply L'Hôpital's rule.

    Finding $g'(t)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g'(t) &= -\frac{3}{8}(\cos t \cos t + \sin t(-\sin t)) - \frac{3}{8}\\[15pt]
    &= -\frac{3}{8}(\cos^2 t - \sin^2 t) - \frac{3}{8}\\[15pt]
    g'(t)&= -\frac{3}{8} \cos 2t - \frac{3}{8} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Finding the derivative of the denominator, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (\tan t+2t)'&=\sec^2t+2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{t \to 0} \left(\frac{g(t)}{\tan t + 2t}\right) &= \lim_{t \to 0} \left(\frac{-\frac{3}{8}\cos 2t-\frac{3}{8}}{\sec^2 t + 2}\right)\\[15pt]
    &= \frac{-\frac{3}{8}(1) - \frac{3}{8}}{1 + 2}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[15pt]
    &= \frac{-\frac{3}{4}}{3}\\[15pt]
    \lim_{t \to 0} \left(\frac{g(t)}{\tan t + 2t}\right)&= -\dfrac{1}{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
