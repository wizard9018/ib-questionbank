# AA1350

**Maximum mark: 5**

Find the range of possible values of $k$ such that $\dfrac{5}{\ln{x}}-2=\dfrac{3e^k}{(\ln{x})^2}$ has two distinct solutions.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let's start by multiplying the entire equation by $(\ln x)^2$, to remove the fractions, and then simplifying.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{2.5em}\left[\dfrac{5}{\ln{x}}-2=\dfrac{3e^k}{(\ln{x})^2}\right]\times(\ln x)^2\\[12pt]
    &5\ln x - 2(\ln x)^2=3e^k\\[6pt]
    &2(\ln x)^2-5\ln x + 3e^k=0
\end{aligned}
$$
Notice we have $(\ln x)^2$, $\ln x$ and a constant. This indicates that we have a disguised quadratic.

Let's use $u = \ln{x}$, where $u \neq 0$, to see the quadratic more clearly.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    2u^2-5u+3e^k &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
We need to find two distinct solutions so we can use the formula for the __discriminant (2.7)__ to investigate the nature of the roots of this quadratic.

Recall that for $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ if we have
- $\Delta <0$ then the equation has no real roots.
- $\Delta = 0$ then the equation has one real root.
- $\Delta>0$  then the equation has two real, distinct roots.

Hence we are looking for $\Delta>0$.
$\\[-2.5em]$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting $a=2$, $b=-5$ and $c=3e^k$ into the formula we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    (-5)^2-4(2)(3e^k)&>0\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
    25 - 24e^k &> 0 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[6pt]
    e^k &< \frac{25}{24}\\[6pt]
    k &< \ln\left(\frac{25}{24}\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore $k < \ln\left(\dfrac{25}{24}\right)$.
