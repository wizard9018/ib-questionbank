# AA1349

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider the two positive integers $n$ and $n + 2$.

Show that the mean of their squares is one greater than the square of their mean.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let's write an expression for what we want to show.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{mean of squares}&=\text{square of the mean}+1
\end{aligned}
$$
Now let's work out the L.H.S.
The squares of the given integers are $n^2$ and $(n+2)^2$, so their mean is
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \text{Mean of squares} &= \frac{n^2 + (n+2)^2}{2} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Let's expand $(n+2)^2$ and simplify
$$
\begin{aligned}
    &=\frac{n^2 + (n+2)^2}{2}\\[6pt]
    &= \frac{n^2 + n^2 + 4n + 4}{2} \\[6pt]
    &= \frac{2n^2 + 4n + 4}{2}\\[6pt]
   \text{Mean of squares} &= n^2 + 2n + 2 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Next let's work out the R.H.S.
The mean of $n$ and $n+2$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \text{Mean} &= \frac{n + (n+2)}{2}\\[6pt]
    &= \frac{2n+2}{2} \\[6pt]
    \text{Mean} &= n+1 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore, the square of their mean is
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \text{Square of mean} &= (n+1)^2\\[6pt]
    &= n^2 + 2n + 1 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting the two results into our original expression, we see that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{mean of squares}&=\text{square of the mean}+1\\[6pt]
n^2 + 2n + 2 &=( n^2 + 2n + 1)+1\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
 n^2 + 2n + 2&= n^2 + 2n + 2\hspace{2em}\checkmark
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}\dfrac{n^2 + (n+2)^2}{2}=\left(\dfrac{n + (n+2)}{2}\right)^2+1\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}
\end{aligned}
$$
(The mean of their squares is one greater than the square of their means.)
