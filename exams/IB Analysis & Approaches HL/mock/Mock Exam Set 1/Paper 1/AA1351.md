# AA1351

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the functions $f(x) = \sin(x)$ and $g(x) = \cos 2x$, where $0\leq x \leq \pi$.

The graphs of $f$ and $g$ are shown below.

![AA1351-q](images/AA1351-q.JPEG)

Find the area enclosed by the two functions.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

To find the area enclosed by $f(x) = \sin(x)$ and $g(x) = \cos(2x)$, shown in red,

![AA1351-a1](images/AA1351-a1.JPEG)

we need to find their intersection points.
For this we set $f(x)=g(x)$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \sin x&=\cos 2x
\end{aligned}
$$
We can rewrite the R.H.S. using the __cosine double angle identities (3.6)__
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \sin x&=1-2\sin^2 x\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Notice we have $\sin^2{x}$, $\sin{x}$ and a constant. This indicates that we have a disguised quadratic.

Let's use $a = \sin{x}$ to see the quadratic more clearly.
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sin^2x + \sin x - 1 &=0\\[6pt]
2a^2+a-1&=0\\[6pt]
(2a - 1)(a + 1)&=0
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the null factor theorem we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    2a-1&=0&\hspace{3em}&a + 1=0\\[6pt]
    a &=\frac{1}{2}&&a = -1\\[6pt]
     \sin x &=\frac{1}{2}&&\sin x = -1
\end{aligned}

$$
Then considering the graph of $y=\sin x$ we can determine the values of $x$ which satisfy these equations.

![AA1351bi](images/AA1351bi.JPEG)

As the given domain is from $0\leq x \leq \pi$ we have $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
$\\[-20pt]$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
To find the area between curves we must find the integral of the upper curve minus the lower curve from intersections point $a$ to $b$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=\int_{a}^{b} (\text{upper curve }-\text{ lower curve})\,dx
\end{aligned}
$$
$f(x)=\sin x$ is the upper curve, therefore we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&= \int_{\tfrac{\pi}{6}}^{\tfrac{5\pi}{6}} \left[\sin(x) - \cos(2x)\right] dx\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[15pt]
    &= \left[-\cos(x) - \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)\right]_{\tfrac{\pi}{6}}^{\tfrac{5\pi}{6}}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Evaluating the upper and lower boundaries
$$
\begin{aligned}
&= \left[-\cos\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{3}\right)\right] - \left[-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\right]\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
We know that $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\cos\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$,

![AA1351bii](images/AA1351bii.JPEG)

and $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sin\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

![AA1351biii](images/AA1351biii.JPEG)

Hence we can substitute in these values and simplify.
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\left[-\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) -\frac{1}{2}\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\right] - \left[-\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) -\frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\right]\\[15pt]
&=\left[\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right] - \left[-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right]\\[15pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\\[9pt]
&= \sqrt{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[6pt]
A&= \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
