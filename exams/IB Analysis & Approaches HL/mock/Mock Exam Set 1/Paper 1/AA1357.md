# AA1357

**Maximum mark: 8**

The continuous random variable $X$ has probability density function
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{8}{\pi \sqrt{9 - 16x^2}}, & 0 \leq x \leq k, \\[1em]
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $k \in \mathbb{Q}$.

1.    Find the value of $k$. [4 marks]

It is known that $\mathrm{E}(X)=\dfrac{a}{b \pi }$ where $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}.$

2.    Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    As $f$ is a probability density function, the total area under the graph must be $1$.
    Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using this we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \int_{0}^{k} \frac{8}{\pi \sqrt{9 - 16x^2}}\,\mathrm{d}x &= 1 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we take out the factor of $\dfrac{8}{\pi}$ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \frac{8}{\pi}\int_{0}^{k} \frac{1}{\sqrt{9 - 16x^2}}\,\mathrm{d}x &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let's compare the integral only to the __standard integral for arcsine__ $(\bm{5.15})$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{3^2 - (4x)^2}}\,\mathrm{d}x & &&&\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}}\,\mathrm{d}x &= \arcsin{\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)}+C
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We are almost there. The difference is, on the left we have the term $4x$ and for the standard integral we have the term $x$.

    To help us, let's use a substitution of $u=4x$. This means $\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=4$ therefore $\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}u$.

    Ignoring the limits (for now) and using the above we can write an integral in terms of $u$ to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \frac{8}{\pi}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{3^2 - u^2}}\times\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}u &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Simplifying the L.H.S. and applying the standard integral we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \frac{2}{\pi}\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{3^2 - u^2}}\,\mathrm{d}u &= 1 \\[15pt]
            \frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin{\left(\frac{u}{3}\right)}&=1\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the substitution $u=4x$ and reintroducing the limits we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{2}{\pi}\left[\arcsin{\left(\frac{4x}{3}\right)}\right]_{0}^{k}&=1\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving for $k$ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \left[\arcsin{\left(\frac{4x}{3}\right)}\right]_{0}^{k}&=    \frac{\pi}{2}\\[15pt]
        \arcsin{\left(\frac{4k}{3}\right)}-\arcsin{0}&=\frac{\pi}{2}\\[15pt]
        \arcsin{\left(\frac{4k}{3}\right)}&=\frac{\pi}{2}\\[15pt]
        \frac{4k}{3}&=\sin{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}\\[15pt]
        \frac{4k}{3}&=1\\[15pt]
        k&=\dfrac{3}{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.    We use the formula for the __expected value of a continuous random variable__ $\bm{(4.14)}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(X) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and replace $f$ with our function and the value of $k$ from part (a), getting
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &= \int_{0}^{\textstyle \frac{3}{4}}  \frac{8x}{\pi \sqrt{9 - 16x^2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The form of this integral has changed, we now need a different technique.

    Notice that the numerator is multiple of the derivative of the inner function of the denominator. I.e. $8x$ is a multiple of $(9-16x^2)'$.

    This means we can use the substitution $u=9-16x^2$.

    Let's first find $\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=-32x$. Therefore $\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{1}{32x}\mathrm{d}u$.

    Ignoring the limits (for now) and rewriting our integral in terms of $u$ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &= \int  \frac{8x}{\pi \sqrt{u}}\times-\frac{1}{32x}\mathrm{d}u
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Simplifying this gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &= -\frac{1}{4\pi}\int  \frac{1}{ \sqrt{u}}\,\mathrm{d}u\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now use the __integral of $\bm{x^n}$ (5.5)__ formula.

    But first, we should write the integral in index form.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &= -\frac{1}{4\pi}\int  u^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}u\\[15pt]
        &=-\frac{1}{4\pi} \times (2u^{\frac{1}{2}})
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now we can rewrite the integral in terms of $x$ and reintroduce the limits to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=-\frac{1}{4\pi} \left[2(9-16x^2)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]_{0}^{\textstyle \frac{3}{4}}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating the definite integral we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(X)&=-\frac{1}{4\pi} \left[2\left(9-16(\tfrac{3}{4})^2\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-2\left(9-16(0)^2\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\\[12pt]
        &=-\frac{1}{4\pi} \left[\left(2\times 0\right)-\left(2\times 3\right)\right]\\[12pt]
        &=\frac{6}{4\pi}\\[12pt]
        &=\frac{3}{2\pi}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $a=3$ and $b=2$.
    $\\[-2.5em]$

    \end{aligned}
