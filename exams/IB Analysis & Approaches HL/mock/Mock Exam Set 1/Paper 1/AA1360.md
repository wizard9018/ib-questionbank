# AA1360

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the expression $\sqrt{1-4x}-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(1+px)^3}}, \text{where } p \in \mathbb{Z}, \; p \neq 0.$

The binomial expansion of this expression, in ascending powers of $x$, as far as the term in $x^2$ is $\dfrac{q}{2}x + 2qx^2, \text{where } q \in \mathbb{Z}.$

Find the value of $p$ and the value of $q$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

To expand each term we can use the __extension of the binomial theorem (1.10)__.

$$

\begin{aligned}

(a+b)^n=a^n\left(1 + n\left(\frac{b}{a}\right) + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^2 + \cdots \right)

\end{aligned}

$$

Rewriting the first term of the expression given in the question,

$$

\begin{aligned}

\sqrt{1-4x}=(1-4x)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\end{aligned}

$$

and comparing it to the L.H.S. of the formula above we have $a=1$, $b=-4x$ and $n=\frac{1}{2}$.

Substituting these values into the R.H.S. of the formula we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

(1-4x)^{\frac{1}{2}}&= 1^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{-4x}{1}\right) + \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-1\right)}{2!}\left(\dfrac{-4x}{1}\right)^2 + \ldots\right) \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[16pt]

&= 1 - 2x + \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2}(16x^2) + \ldots\\[9pt]

&= 1 - 2x - 2x^2 + \ldots \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

As mentioned in the question, the expansion ends at the $x^2$ term.

Using a similar approach for the second term,

$$

\begin{aligned}

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(1+px)^3}} = (1+px)^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\end{aligned}

$$

we have $a=1$, $b=px$ and $n=-\frac{3}{2}$. 

Hence

$$

\begin{aligned}

(1+px)^{-\frac{3}{2}} &= 1^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1 + \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{px}{1}\right) + \frac{\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{3}{2}-1\right)}{2!}\left(\dfrac{px}{1}\right)^2 + \ldots\right)\\[16pt]

&= 1 - \frac{3}{2}px + \frac{\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)}{2}p^2x^2 + \ldots\\[12pt]

&= 1 - \frac{3}{2}px + \frac{15}{8}p^2x^2 + \ldots \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Combining both expansions, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

\sqrt{1-4x} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+px)^3}}

&= \left(1 - 2x - 2x^2\right) - \left(1 - \frac{3}{2}px + \frac{15}{8}p^2x^2\right) + \ldots\\[16pt]

&= -2x + \frac{3}{2}px - 2x^2 - \frac{15}{8}p^2x^2 + \ldots\\[12pt]

&= \left(-2 + \frac{3p}{2}\right)x + \left(-2 - \frac{15p^2}{8}\right)x^2 + \ldots

\end{aligned}

$$

Comparing our result with $\dfrac{q}{2}x + 2qx^2$, we can write two equations in $p$ and $q$.

$\\[-2.3em]$

$$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(M1)}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

For the coefficient of $x$, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

-2 + \frac{3p}{2} &= \frac{q}{2} \\[12pt]

q&=-4+3p\hspace{3em}[1]

\end{aligned}

$$

For the coefficient of $x^2$, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

-2 - \frac{15p^2}{8} &= 2q \hspace{4em}[2]

\end{aligned}

$$

Then, substituting [1] into [2], we can solve for $p$.

$$

\begin{aligned}

-2 - \frac{15p^2}{8} &= 2(-4 + 3p)\\[9pt]

-2 - \frac{15p^2}{8} &= -8 + 6p\\[9pt]

-16-15p^2 &= -64+48p\\[9pt]

15p^2 + 48p - 48 &= 0\\[6pt]

5p^2 + 16p - 16 &= 0\\[6pt]

(5p - 4)(p + 4) &= 0

\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore $p = \dfrac{4}{5}$ or $p = -4$.

We were given that $p \in \mathbb{Z}$, hence we conclude that 

$p = \boxed{-4}$.

$\\[-2.3em]$

$$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Substituting $p$ back into [1] we can find $q$.

$$

\begin{aligned}

q &= -4 + 3(-4)\\[6pt]

q&= -16\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$
