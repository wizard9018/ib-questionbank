# AA1356

**Maximum mark: 16**

A geometric sequence has a common ratio $r = \frac{3}{4}$ and the sum of the first two terms is $175$.

1.    Find the first term of the geometric sequence. [2 marks]

$S_n$ is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the geometric sequence.

$S_{\infty}$ is the value of $S_n$ as $n$ approaches infinity.

2.    Find $S_{\infty}$. [2 marks]

The first three terms of an arithmetic sequence are $3^x$, $3^{x+1}$, and $5 \times 3^{x}$, where $x\in\mathbb{Z}$.

3.    Show that the common difference is $2\times3^x$. [2 marks]

$A_n$ is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the arithmetic sequence.

4.    Show that $A_n = n^2\times 3^x$. [2 marks]

5.    Find the value of $x$ for which $A_5$ is equal to $S_{\infty}$. Give your answer in the form $\log_p{q}$ where $p,\,q\,\in\mathbb{Z^+}$. [4 marks]

6.    Using your $x$ value from part (e), find the smallest integer $k$ such that the $k$-th term of the arithmetic sequence exceeds $S_{\infty}$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let $u_1$ and $u_2$ be the first and second term respectively of the geometric sequence. 

    As the sequence is geometric we can say that $u_2=u_1r$.

    Hence, using $S_2=175$ and $r=\frac{3}{4}$, we can write

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    S_2&=u_1+u_1r\\[6pt]

    175&=u_1+\frac{3}{4}u_1\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Solving this to find $u_1$ we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    700&=4u_1+3u_1\\[6pt]

    700&=7u_1\\[6pt]

    u_1&=100\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The question asks us to find the sum to infinity, therefore we can assume one exists!

    Note that as $r=\frac{3}{4}$, which is between $-1$ and $1$, we can confirm that a sum to infinity does indeed exist.

    We can use the __sum of an infinite geometric sequence (1.8)__ formula with $u_1 = 100$ and $r = \frac{3}{4}$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    S_{\infty} &= \frac{u_1}{1-r}\\[12pt]

    &= \frac{100}{1 - \frac{3}{4}}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[12pt]

    &= \frac{100}{\frac{1}{4}}\\[12pt]

    & = 100 \times \frac{4}{1}\\[6pt]

    S_{\infty}&= 400\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. The common difference $d$ of an arithmetic sequence is given by $d = u_2 - u_1$. 

    Hence we can write an expression for $d$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d = 3^{x+1} - 3^x\tag*{\textbf{M1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can rewrite the first exponent using the exponent law $x^{a+b}=x^a\times x^b$, which gives

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d &= 3 \times 3^x - 3^x \\[6pt]

    &=3^x(3-1)\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    &=2\times 3^x\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. We can use the formula for the __sum of $\bm{n}$ terms of an arithmetic sequence (1.2)__ with  $u_1=3^x$ and $d=2\times 3^x$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A_n &= \frac{n}{2} [2u_1 + (n-1)d]\\[16pt]

    &= \frac{n}{2} \left[ 2(3^x) + (n-1)(2\times 3^x) \right]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can factorise $2\times3^x$ from the square brackets and simplify to get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A_n &= \frac{n}{\cancel{2}} \times \cancel{2} \times 3^x\left[ \cancel{1} + (n\cancel{-1}) \right]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[16pt]

    &=n\times3^x\times n\\[6pt]

    &=n^2 \times 3^x\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. We are looking for the value of $x$ such that $S_{\infty}= A_5$.

    From part (b) we found that $S_{\infty} = 400$.

    From part (d), we know that $A_n = n^2\cdot3^x$ so let's use that to find $A_5$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A_5 &= 5^2(3^x)\\[6pt]

    &=25\times3^x \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using $S_{\infty} = 400$ we can form an equation and then simplify to get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    400 &= 25\cdot3^{x}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    16&=3^x\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using __exponents and logarithms (1.5)__ which states if $a^x=b$ then $x=\log_ab$ we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x &= \log_3{16}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. In an earlier part we found that $S_{\infty}=400$ and we are asked to find the smallest value of $k$ when $u_k>S_{\infty}$. Hence we need to solve the inequality $u_k>400$. 

    For this we can use the formula for __the $\bm{n}$th term of an arithmetic sequence (1.2)__.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_n&=u_1+(n-1)d

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using $x=\log_3{16}$ with $u_1=3^x$ and $d=2\times 3^x$ we can write simplified expressions for $u_1$ and $d$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_1&=3^{\log_3{16}}\hspace{2em}d=2(3^{\log_3{16}})\\[6pt]

    &=16\hspace{4em}=2(16)\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{6.3em}=32

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Recall, $a^{\log_a{b}}=b$.

    Substituting these into the general formula for the $k$-th term we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_k&=16+(k-1)(32)\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &=32k-16\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Next we replace $u_k$ from the earlier inequality with this result to get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_k&>S_{\infty}\\[6pt]

    32k-16&>400\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Solving this we find

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    32k&>416\\[6pt]

    8k&>104\\[6pt]

    k&>13

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Choosing the first integer greater than $13$ we have $k=14$.

    $\\[-2.3em]$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
