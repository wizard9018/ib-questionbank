# AA1362

**Maximum mark: 19**

The graph of a polynomial function $f$ of degree $4$ is shown below. It crosses the $x$-axis at $(-2, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$, and the $y$-axis at $(0, -20)$.

![AA1362q](images/AA1362q.JPEG)

1.    Given that the curve passes through the point $(2, -24)$, find $f(x)$ in the form $f(x)=(x-a)(x-b)(x^2+cx+d)$, where $a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{Z}$. [5 marks]

2.    Find the two complex roots of $f$ in Cartesian form. [2 marks]

3.    Sketch the four roots on an Argand diagram. [2 marks]

Let $w_1$ be the complex root of $f$ with a positive imaginary part.

4.    1.    Show that $w_1=\sqrt{2}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{i}}$ 

    2.    Hence write down the remaining roots in the form $r e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}$ where $r>0$ and $-\pi < \theta \leq \pi$. [5 marks]

$w_1$ is rotated __clockwise__ through an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}\,$radians about the origin to form a new complex number $w_1'$.

5.    Find $w_1'$ in the form $r e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}$ where $r>0$ and $-\pi < \theta \leq \pi$. [2 marks]

Consider the equation

$$(w_1')^n = p,$$

where $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ and $p \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$.

6.    Find the smallest value of $n$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Since the polynomial only crosses the $x$-axis at $(-2, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$, then we know it has only 2 real roots. Therefore we know that $(x+2)$ and $(x-5)$ are factors.

    We are given that $f$ is a degree $4$ polynomial, so we can write

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(x) &= (x+2)(x-5)(x^2+cx+d) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    where $c, d \in \mathbb{Z}$.

    Next, we can substitute the $y$-intercept $(0, -20)$ into this equation.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(0) &= (0+2)(0-5)(0^2+c(0)+d) = -20  \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When we simplify, we obtain a value of $d$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2 \times (-5) \times d &= -20\\

    -10d &= -20\\

    d &= 2\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can use the point $(2, -24)$ to solve for $c$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(x) &= (x+2)(x-5)(x^2+cx+d)\\[6pt]

    -24 &= (2+2)(2-5)(2^2+2c+2)  \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Simplifying

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    -24&=4 \times (-3) \times (6+2c)\\[6pt]

    -24&=-12(6+2c)\\[6pt]

    2&=6+2c\\[6pt]

    -4 &= 2c\\[6pt]

    c &= -2\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore $$\boxed{f(x) = (x+2)(x-5)(x^2-2x+2)}$$.

2. To find the complex roots, we need to solve $x^2-2x+2 = 0$. 

    Using the $\textbf{quadratic formula (2.7)}$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x &= \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{(-2)^2 - 4(1)(2)}}{2(1)}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    &= \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4-8}}{2}\\[6pt]

    &= \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{-4}}{2}\\[6pt]

    &= \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4}\times \sqrt{\mathrm{-1}}}{2}\\[6pt]

    x&= \frac{2 \pm 2\mathrm{i}}{2}\\[6pt]x = 1 + \mathrm{i}\hspace{2em}&\text{ and }\hspace{2em}x = 1 - \mathrm{i}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Note, as this is a real polynomial (the coefficients are real) then the complex roots come in conjugate pairs.

4.  1. The complex number with a positive imaginary part is $1+\mathrm{i}$.

        Hence this is $w_1=1+\mathrm{i}$.

        To show how to write this in Euler's form, we need to find the modulus and argument.

        $$

        \begin{aligned} r &= \sqrt{1^2+1^2} \\[6pt]

        &= \sqrt{2}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}} \\[6pt]

        \theta &= \arctan\left(\frac{1}{1}\right) \\[6pt]

        &= \frac{\pi}{4}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        1+\mathrm{i} = \sqrt{2}e^{\tfrac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{i}}\\[6pt]w_1 = \sqrt{2}e^{\tfrac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{i}}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. Let's call the remaining roots $w_2, w_3$ and $w_4$.

        For $w_2 = -2$,

        $$

        \begin{aligned} r &= |-2| \\[6pt]

        &= 2\\[6pt]

        \theta &= \pi\\[6pt]

        &\hspace{-1.5em}w_2 = 2e^{\pi\mathrm{i}}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}

        $$

        For $w_3 = 1-\mathrm{i}$,

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        r &= \sqrt{1^2+(-1)^2} \\[6pt]

        &= \sqrt{2}\\

        \theta &= \arctan\left(\frac{-1}{1}\right)\\[6pt]

        &= -\frac{\pi}{4}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence

        $$

        \begin{aligned}w_3 = \sqrt{2}e^{-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{i}}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        For $w_4 = 5$,

        $$

        \begin{aligned} r &= |5| \\[6pt]

        \theta &= 0\\[6pt]

        &\hspace{-1.5em}w_4 = 5e^{(0)\mathrm{i}}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

5. Rotating $w_1$ __clockwise__ through an angle of $\frac{\pi}{3}$ means we subtract $\frac{\pi}{3}$ from the argument.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    w_1' &= \sqrt{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4} - \tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    &= \sqrt{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{12} - \tfrac{4\pi}{12}\right)}\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-1.5em}w_1' = \sqrt{2}e^{-\tfrac{\pi}{12}\mathrm{i}}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. We are asked to consider the equation $(w_1')^n = p$ where $w_1' = \sqrt{2}e^{-\tfrac{\pi}{12}\mathrm{i}}$ and $p \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$. 

    Substituting $w_1'$ in the L.H.S. and using the laws of exponents we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    (\sqrt{2}e^{-\tfrac{\pi}{12}\mathrm{i}})^n &= p\\[6pt]

    (\sqrt{2})^n e^{-\tfrac{n\pi}{12}\mathrm{i}} &= p\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Since $p \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$, we need the L.H.S. to be a positive real number.

    This means the number must lie on the right side of the horizontal axis of an Argand diagram.

    Hence, the argument must be $0$ or a multiple of $2\pi$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    -\frac{n\pi}{12} &= 2k\pi \hspace{2em} \text{where } k \in \mathbb{Z}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    -n &= 24k\\[6pt]

    n &= -24k

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Since $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ (positive), we need $k < 0$.

    Through inspection we can see the smallest positive value of $n$ occurs when $k = -1$.

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    n &= -24(-1)\\[6pt]

    &n = 24\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
