# AA1371

**Maximum mark: 14**

A data-collecting buoy is anchored in the ocean. The height, $H(t)$, of the buoy's sensor above the seabed, measured in metres, can be modelled by the function

$$

\begin{aligned}

    H(t)=a\cos(bt)+d

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $t$ is measured in hours and $0\leq t <24$. 

The  minimum height of the sensor from the seabed is $8\,$m and maximum height is $14\,$m. The length of time between the minimum and the maximum is 6 hours.

At time $t=0$, the sensor is at its minimum height above the seabed.

1.    Determine the values of $a$, $b$, and $d$ for $H(t)$. [4 marks]

2.    Calculate the value of $H$ when $t = 5$ hours. [2 marks]

3.    Find the first two times when the height of the sensor above the seabed is $10$ metres. Give your answers correct to two decimal places. [2 marks]

The buoy functions correctly only when its sensor is at a height of $10$ metres or greater above the seabed.

4.    If the buoy is checked at a random time in the interval $0\leq t < 24$, determine the probability that the buoy is functioning correctly. [3 marks]

The buoy was checked some time between $t=1$ and $t=3$ hours.

5.    Calculate the probability that it was functioning correctly. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The value of $|a|$ (known as the amplitude) is the distance from the centre line of the curve to either the maximum or the minimum.

    To determine $|a|$ we can use the following formula.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    |a|&=\frac{\text{Maximum}-\text{Minimum}}{2} \\[6pt]

    &= \frac{14-8}{2}\\[6pt]

    |a|&= 3

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Next we decide if $a$ is positive or negative.

    To answer this, consider a basic cosine curve

    ![AA1371a1](images/AA1371a1.JPEG)

    As the sensor is at its minimum height when $t=0$ and there is no horizontal translation, we must use the negative cosine function shown below.

    ![AA1371aii](images/AA1371aii.JPEG)

    Hence $a=-3$.

    $\\[-2.3em]$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    To determine $d$, which is the vertical translation, we can use the following formula.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d&=\frac{\text{Maximum}+\text{Minimum}}{2} \\[6pt]

    &= \frac{14+8}{2} \\[6pt]

    d&= 11\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    To determine $b$, the horizontal stretch, we can use the following formula.

    The period is the time taken for one complete wavelength, which is the time to go from the minimum, to the maximum then back to the minimum.

    From the information given we can see this as $6 \times 2 = 12$ hours.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{Period}&=\frac{2\pi}{b}\\[6pt]

    12& = \frac{2\pi}{b}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    b&=\dfrac{\pi}{6}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore, the function is $H(t) =-3 \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6} t\right) + 11$.

2. Using the function from part (a) we can substitute $t=5$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    H(5)&=-3 \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\times 5\right) + 11\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[9pt]

    &=13.59...\\[6pt]

    &=13.6\text{ metres }\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Let the first two times that $H(t)=10$ be $t_1$ and $t_2$.

    Using a G.D.C. we can find the first two solutions to $H(t)=10$.

    To do this we sketch the two functions $y=H(t)$ and $y=10$ and find the coordinates of their points of intersection.

    ![AA1371c](images/AA1371c.JPEG)

    The first two intersection points give us $t_1$ and $t_2$.

    Hence $t_1=2.35\text{ hours}$ and  $t_2=9.65\text{ hours}$.

    $\\[-2.3em]$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{{A1A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. The $\textcolor{#8c6188}{\text{purple}}$ part, shown below, is when $H(t)>10$, this is when the buoy is functioning correctly.

    

    ![AA1371d](images/AA1371d.JPEG)

    We found the times where $H(t)=10$ in part (c).

    By subtracting these, we can find the amount of time that $H(t)>10$ for the first half of the interval.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{Time for first part} &= 9.649 \ldots - 2.351\ldots\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &=7.298...\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Due to the symmetry of the function the total time in the interval that $H(t)>10$ is $7.298... \times 2 = 14.59...$.

    Recall, the buoy can be checked at any time in the interval therefore we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(\text{functioning})&=\frac{\text{Time $H$ is above 10}}{\text{Total interval time}}\\[16pt]

    &=\frac{14.59...}{24}\\[12pt]

    &=0.6081...\\[6pt]

    \mathrm{P}(\text{functioning})&=0.608\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. We need to find the amount of time between $t=1$ and $t=3$ that the buoy is above $10$ metres. This is shown in $\textcolor{#8c6188}{\text{purple}}$ below.

    ![AA1371e](images/AA1371e.JPEG)

    We know that the height is equal to $10$ metres when $t_1=2.350...$ from part (c).

    Hence, we can subtract this value from $3$ to find the length of time the buoy is above $10$ metres between $t=1$ and $3$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{Time above 10\,m} &= 3 - t_1\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= 3 - 2.350... \ldots\\[6pt]

    &=0.6490...\text{hours}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can use the same formula as the previous part.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(\text{functioning})&=\frac{\text{Time $H$ is above 10 in the new interval}}{\text{New interval total time}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the new time interval of $3-1=2$ and the answer we just found, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(\text{functioning})&=\frac{0.6490...}{2}\\[6pt]

    &=0.3245...\\[6pt]

    \mathrm{P}(\text{functioning})&=0.325\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
