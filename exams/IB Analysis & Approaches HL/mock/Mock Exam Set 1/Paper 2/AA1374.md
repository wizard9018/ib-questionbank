# AA1374

**Maximum mark: 6**

A laboratory is investigating how the brightness $B$ (in lumens) recorded by a photographic sensor changes over time while it is illuminated by a controlled light source.

The sensor is exposed to the light continuously starting from $t = 0$ minutes.

The energy, measured in $Q$ joules, is absorbed by the sensor at a constant rate such that $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}t} = 4 \text{ joules min}^{-1}$.

The relationship between $Q$ and $B$ is given by $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}B} = 8(\ln(B -4))^{2}.$

1.    Find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t}$. [2 marks]

The value of $B$ is controlled to be between $4$ and $5$ lumens.

However, it is discovered that the sensor malfunctions when $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}B}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}} > 3$ which occurs if $B>k $ lumens.

2.    Hence, find the value of $k$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. In this question we have $3$ variables. $Q, B$ and $t$.

    We have also been given two rates of change.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 4 \\[9pt]

    \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}B} &= 8(\ln(B -4))^{2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    To relate the $3$ variables we can use the __chain rule (5.6)__

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}u} \times \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    and replace $y$ with $B$, $x$ with $t$ and $u$ with $Q$ to get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}Q} \times \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}t} \\[12pt]

    &= \frac{1}{\frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}B}} \times \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}t} \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting in the known expressions we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{1}{8(\ln(B -4))^{2}} \times 4 \\[12pt]

    &= \dfrac{1}{2(\ln(B -4))^{2}} \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. To find $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}B}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}$ we must differentiate the result from part (a) with respect to $t$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{1}{2(\ln(B -4))^{2}}\\[12pt]

    &=\frac{1}{2}(\ln(B -4))^{-2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    However, the function we have in part (a) is a function of $B$ so we must use implicit differentiation.

    Therefore we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}^2B}{\mathrm{d}t^2}&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}B}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\ln(B -4))^{-2}\right)\times \frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can use the answer from part (a) to rewrite $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t}$ to get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}B}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\ln(B -4))^{-2}\right)\times \frac{1}{2}(\ln(B -4))^{-2}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now use a G.D.C. to sketch $\displaystyle y=\frac{\mathrm{d}^2B}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$ using the derivative function of the G.D.C.

    $\\[-2.5em]$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
