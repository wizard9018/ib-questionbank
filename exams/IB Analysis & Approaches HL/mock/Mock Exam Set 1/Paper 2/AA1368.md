# AA1368

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the expansion $(1+ax)\left(bx-1\right)^6=1-7x+\dfrac{63}{4}x^2+...$, where $a,b\in\mathbb{Q}$.

Given that the expansion has no $x^3$ term, find the values of $a$ and $b$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let's start by expanding the binomial $(bx-1)^6$ using the __binomial theorem formula (1.9)__.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    (a+b)^n&=a^n + \binom{n}{1} a^{\,n-1} b + \cdots + \binom{n}{r} a^{\,n-r} b^{\,r} + \cdots + b^n

\end{aligned}

$$

Comparing the L.H.S. of above to the binomial $(-1+bx)^6$ we can see that $n=6$, $a$ corresponds to $-1$ and $b$ corresponds to $bx$.

Substituting those into $\bm{1.9}$ we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

(bx-1)^6 &=(-1)^6+\left(\begin{matrix}6\\1\end{matrix}\right)(-1)^5(bx)^1+\left(\begin{matrix}6\\2\end{matrix}\right)(-1)^4(bx)^2+\left(\begin{matrix}6\\3\end{matrix}\right)(-1)^3(bx)^3+...\\[6pt]

&= 1 - 6bx + 15b^2x^2 - 20b^3x^3 + ...\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Next, we multiply the above by $(1+ax)$. 

$$

\begin{aligned}

(1+ax)(bx-1)^6 &= (\textcolor{#b52209}{1}+\textcolor{#039855}{ax})(\textcolor{#039855}{1} \textcolor{#b52209}{\,-\, 6bx} + 15b^2x^2 - 20b^3x^3 + ...)

\end{aligned}

$$

We know that the final expansion has an $x$ term of $-7x$. 

From observation, we can see there are two combinations of terms that will sum to give the $x$ term.

The sum of the product of the $\textcolor{#b52209}{\text{red}}$ and $\textcolor{#039855}{\text{green}}$ terms.

Hence we have the equation 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textcolor{#039855}{ax \times 1}-  \textcolor{#b52209}{1 \times 6bx}&=-7x\\[6pt]

    ax-6bx&=-7x\\[6pt]

    x(a-6b)&=-7x

\end{aligned}

$$

Equating coefficients we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

    a-6b&=-7\hspace{3em}[1]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

In a similar manner we can determine which combinations of products will result in an $x^2$ term.

$$

\begin{aligned}

(1+ax)(bx-1)^6 &= (\textcolor{#b52209}{1}+\textcolor{#039855}{ax})(1 \textcolor{#039855}{\,-\, 6bx} +\textcolor{#b52209}{ 15b^2x^2} - 20b^3x^3 + ...)

\end{aligned}

$$

Picking out the required terms we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textcolor{#039855}{ax \times (-6bx)}+\textcolor{#b52209}{1 \times 15b^2x^2}&=-6abx^2+15b^2x^2\\[6pt]

    &=(-6ab+15b^2)x^2

\end{aligned}

$$

Comparing this to the coefficient to $x^2$ in the given expansion we can form a second equation in $a$ and $b$.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    -6ab+15b^2&=\frac{63}{4}\hspace{3em}[2]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Solving [1] and [2] simultaneously on the G.D.C. we obtain 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    a&= -4,\,\,b=0.5\\[6pt]

    a&=2,\,\,b=1.5 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

We have two possible answers. Recall, in the question we are told that the $x^3$ is zero.

To decide which answer pair is correct we must consider the $x^3$ term.

Again, in a similar manner we can determine which combinations of products will result in the $x^3$ term.

$$

\begin{aligned}

(1+ax)(bx-1)^6 &= (\textcolor{#b52209}{1}+\textcolor{#039855}{ax})(1 - 6bx +\textcolor{#039855}{ 15b^2x^2} \textcolor{#b52209}{\,-\, 20b^3x^3} + ...)

\end{aligned}

$$

We have 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    -20b^3x^3+15ab^2x^3&=0x^3\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    15ab^2-20b^3&=0

\end{aligned}

$$

The most straightforward approach now (as we only have 2 answer pairs) is test which answer pair gives a value of zero.

First we test $a=-4$ and $b=0.5$ to get

$$

\begin{aligned}

    -20(0.5)^3+15(-4)(0.5)^2&=-17.5

\end{aligned}

$$

Next, we test $a=2$ and $b=1.5$, which gives

$$

\begin{aligned}

    -20(1.5)^3+15(2)(1.5)^2&=0\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\hspace{1em}\checkmark

\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, $a= 2$ and $b= 1.5$

$\\[-2.5em]$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

\end{aligned}
