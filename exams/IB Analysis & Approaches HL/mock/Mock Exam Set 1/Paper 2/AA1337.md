# AA1337

**Maximum mark: 7**

A bird at point B, flying $20$ metres above the ground, is searching for a tree to nest in.
It spots a vertical tree CD, which is $8$ metres tall.

The bird’s horizontal distance from the tree is $x$ metres.

Point A lies directly above the tree, so $\text{B}\hat{\text{A}}\text{C}$ is a right-angle.

The bird's viewing angle of the tree is $\theta$ degrees.

This is shown in the following diagram.

![AA1337-q](images/AA1337-q.JPEG)

1.    Write down
    1.    $\tan \text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{C}$;

    2.    $\tan \text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{D}$. [2 marks]

2.    Hence, show that  $\tan\theta = \dfrac{8x}{x^2 + 240}$. [3 marks]

3.    Hence or otherwise, find the value of $x$ that maximises the bird’s viewing angle. [2 marks]

1.  1. Consider triangle ABC

        to help us write down
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tan \text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{C}&=\dfrac{12}{x}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2. Likewise, consider triangle ABD

        ![AA1337-a2](images/AA1337-a2.JPEG)

        to help us write down
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tan \text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{D}&=\dfrac{20}{x}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2. Let's begin by writing $\theta$ in terms of the angles we just found
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \theta &= \text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{D}-\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{C}\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next, take the tangent of both sides
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan{\theta} &= \tan{(\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{D}}-\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{C})
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the tangent compound angle identity we can rewrite the R.H.S. to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan{\theta} &=\frac{\tan{\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{D}}-\tan{\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{C}}}{1+\tan{\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{D}}\tan{\text{A}\hat{\text{B}}\text{C}}}\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the answers from part (a) and simplifying we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan{\theta} &=\frac{\frac{20}{x}-\frac{12}{x}}{1+\frac{20}{x}\times \frac{12}{x}}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{\frac{8}{x}}{1+\frac{240}{x^2}}\\[12pt]
    &=\dfrac{8x}{x^2+240}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. As we want to maximise $\theta$ we can sketch $\theta=\arctan{\left(\dfrac{8x}{x^2+240}\right)}$ for $\theta >0$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![AA1337-a3](images/AA1337-a3.JPEG)

    The maximum point is $(15.49...,14.47...)$.

    This tells us that when $x=15.5$ , the value of $\theta$ is maximised.
