# AA1372

**Maximum mark: 7**

A school music club consists of 3 singers, 4 guitarists and 2 drummers.

The members are to be placed in a line to have their photograph taken.

1.    In how many ways can the members be arranged if

    1.    the first and last positions in the line must be occupied by drummers; 

    2.    the singers must stand together, the guitarists must stand together, and the drummers must stand together. [4 marks]

Five members are chosen to perform in a talent show. The selection must include exactly 2 singers, at least 2 guitarists, and at most 1 drummer.

2.    Find the number of possible selections for the talent show. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1. There are $2$ drummers, so there are $2!$ ways to arrange them in the first and last positions.

        The remaining $7$ members (singers and guitarists) can be arranged in the remaining spaces in $7!$ ways.

        

        Therefore, the total number of arrangements is

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        2! \times 7!&=2 \times 5\,040 \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

        &= 10\,080\text{ ways} \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. We treat the singers, guitarists, and drummers as three distinct blocks.

        There are $3!$ ways to arrange these three blocks.

        Inside the blocks, the members can be arranged in $3!$ (singers), $4!$ (guitarists), and $2!$ (drummers) ways respectively.

        Therefore, the number of arrangements is

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        3! \times (3! \times 4! \times 2!)&=6 \times (6 \times 24 \times 2) \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

        &= 1\,728\text{ ways}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. We require a selection of 5 members with exactly 2 singers, at least 2 guitarists, and at most 1 drummer.

    There are two possible cases that satisfy these conditions:

    

    Case 1: 2 Singers, 3 Guitarists, 0 Drummers.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \binom{3}{2} \times \binom{4}{3} \times \binom{2}{0} &= 3 \times 4 \times 1 \\[6pt]

    &= 12\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Case 2: 2 Singers, 2 Guitarists, 1 Drummer.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \binom{3}{2} \times \binom{4}{2} \times \binom{2}{1} &= 3 \times 6 \times 2 \\[6pt]

    &= 36\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Summing the cases gives the total number of selections

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    12 + 36  &= 48\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
