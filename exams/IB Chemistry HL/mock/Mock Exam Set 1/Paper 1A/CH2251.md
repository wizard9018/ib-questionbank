# CH2251

A student burns $0.460 \textrm{\ g}$ of ethanol in a simple calorimeter containing $250 \textrm{\ g}$ of water. The temperature$-$time graph below was obtained.

![CH2251](images/CH2251.png)

What is the enthalpy of combustion of ethanol, in $\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}?$

($M_r$ of ethanol = 46.0 and $c = 4.0 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}$ $^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1})$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}-1050 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}-1200 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}-1360 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}-3050 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To determine the enthalpy of combustion, we must first extract the correct temperature change from the graph. The water remains at $20.0^\circ\textrm{C}$ until heating begins at 60 seconds, after which it rises sharply before gradually cooling due to heat loss to the surroundings. The key insight is that we need to correct for this heat loss by extrapolating the linear cooling portion of the curve backward to the moment heating began. This extrapolation intersects at $32.0^\circ\textrm{C},$ representing the temperature the water would have reached if no heat had been lost during the experiment.

The corrected temperature change is

$\Delta T = 32.0^\circ\textrm{C} - 20.0^\circ\textrm{C} = 12.0^\circ\textrm{C}$

Using this temperature change, the energy absorbed by the water can be calculated using $q = mc\Delta T.$ Since the density of water is $1.00 \textrm{\ g cm}^{-3},$ the mass of $250 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of water is $250 \textrm{\ g},$ which gives

$q_\ce{H2O} = 250 \textrm{\ g} \times 4.0 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\textrm{\,}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}\times 12.0 ^\circ\textrm{C} = 12000 \textrm{\ J} = 12.0 \textrm{\ kJ}$

Since energy is conserved, the energy released by the combustion of ethanol equals the energy absorbed by the water.

$q_{comb} = -12.0 \textrm{\ kJ}$

The number of moles of ethanol burned is

$n = \dfrac{m}{M} = \dfrac{0.460 \textrm{\ g}}{46.0 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.0100 \textrm{\ mol}$

Therefore, the enthalpy of combustion per mole is

$\Delta H_{comb} = \dfrac{q_{comb}}{n} = \dfrac{-12.0 \textrm{\ kJ}}{0.0100 \textrm{\ mol}} = -1200 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

where the negative sign indicates that combustion is an exothermic process.
