Which orbital box diagrams are drawn correctly?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Orbital box diagrams must follow three fundamental principles: the Aufbau principle (electrons fill lowest energy orbitals first), Hund's rule (electrons singly occupy orbitals of equal energy before pairing), and the Pauli exclusion principle (maximum two electrons per orbital with opposite spins).

Chromium has the ground$-$state electron configuration $\ce{[Ar]}4s^13d^5.$ This is an exception to the Aufbau principle where one electron from the $4s$ orbital moves to the $3d$ sublevel to achieve a half$-$filled $d$ sublevel, providing extra stability. The correct configuration would have all five $d$ electrons unpaired (one in each orbital) and one unpaired electron in the $4s$ orbital.

![CH@@43d](images/CH@@43d.png)

Diagram I is incorrect.

Phosphorus has the configuration $\ce{[Ne]}3s^2 3p^3.$ According to Hund's rule, the three $3p$ electrons should occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before any pairing occurs. The diagram correctly shows this arrangement, making diagram II correct.

The oxide ion has gained two electrons, giving it the configuration $\ce{[He]}2s^2 2p^6,$ which is isoelectronic with neon. All electrons are properly paired in the $2s$ and $2p$ orbitals, making diagram III correct.

This question assesses understanding of electron configuration exceptions, Hund's rule, and the relationship between atomic structure and ionic charge.
