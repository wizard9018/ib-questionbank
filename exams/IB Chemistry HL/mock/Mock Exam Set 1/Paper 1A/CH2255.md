# CH2255

Consider the dimerization of nitrogen(IV) oxide:

$2\ce{\,NO2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{N2O4}\textrm{(g)}$

Predict the signs of $\Delta H^{\minuso}$ and $\Delta S^{\minuso},$ then determine when this reaction is thermodynamically favorable:

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta H^{\minuso} < 0,$ $\Delta S^{\minuso} < 0;$ spontaneous only at high temperatures

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta H^{\minuso} < 0,$ $\Delta S^{\minuso} < 0;$ spontaneous only at low temperatures

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta H^{\minuso} < 0,$ $\Delta S^{\minuso} > 0;$ spontaneous at at all temperatures

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta H^{\minuso} > 0,$ $\Delta S^{\minuso} < 0;$ spontaneous at all temperatures

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To predict the signs of $\Delta H^{\minuso}$ and $\Delta S^{\minuso},$ consider what happens during dimerization. Bond formation between two $\ce{NO2}$ molecules releases energy, making the reaction exothermic with $\Delta H^{\minuso} < 0.$ When two gas molecules combine to form one gas molecule, the system becomes more ordered with fewer particles, so $\Delta S^{\minuso} < 0.$

Next, apply the Gibbs equation: $\Delta G^{\minuso} = \Delta H^{\minuso} - T\Delta S^{\minuso}.$ Since both  $\Delta H^{\minuso}$ and  $\Delta S^{\minuso}$ are negative, this becomes

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = -\left |  \Delta H^{\minuso} \right | + T \left |  \Delta S^{\minuso} \right| $

where the first term is negative and the second term is positive.  A reaction is spontaneous when $\Delta G^{\minuso} < 0.$

At low temperatures, the $T\Delta S^{\minuso}$  term is small, so the negative enthalpy term dominates and $\Delta G^{\minuso}$ remains negative, making the reaction spontaneous.

At high temperatures, the positive $T\Delta S^{\minuso}$ term becomes large and eventually exceeds the magnitude of the negative enthalpy term, making $\Delta G^{\minuso}$ positive and the reaction nonspontaneous.

The reaction is thermodynamically favorable only at low temperatures.
