# CH2235

In the free radical chlorination of methane, the following steps occur:

$\mbox{}\\[-40pts]$

|$\ $|$\ $
--:|:--
Step 1:| $\ce{Cl_2} \longrightarrow 2 \ce{\,Cl}$•
Step 2:| $\ce{Cl}$• $+ \ce{\ CH4} \longrightarrow $ •$\ce{CH_3} + \ce{HCl}$
Step 3: |•$\ce{CH3} + \ce{Cl2} \longrightarrow \ce{CH3Cl} + \ce{Cl}$•
Step 4:|•$\ce{CH3} +  \ce{\,Cl}$• $\longrightarrow \ce{CH3Cl}$

Which statement about these steps is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Steps 2 and 3 are both propagation steps.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Step 2 involves heterolytic bond breaking.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Step 4 must occur after every propagation cycle.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Step 1 is highly exothermic.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
In a free radical substitution mechanism, steps are categorized as initiation, propagation, or termination based on how radicals are produced or consumed.

Step 1 is the initiation step, involving homolytic cleavage of $\ce{Cl2}$ to form two chlorine radicals. This step requires an external energy source (typically UV light or heat) because breaking the $\ce{Cl-Cl}$ bond is endothermic.

Steps 2 and 3 are both propagation steps that work together as a repeating cycle. In Step 2, a chlorine radical reacts with methane via homolytic bond breaking (not heterolytic which forms ions) to form HCl and a methyl radical. In Step 3, the methyl radical reacts with $\ce{Cl2}$ to form chloromethane and regenerate a chlorine radical. This cyclical nature where each step consumes one radical and produces another defines a chain reaction. Steps 2 and 3 may repeat hundreds or thousands of times for each initiation event.

Step 4 is a termination step where two radicals combine to form a stable product. Other termination steps which could occur are:

$2 \ce{\,Cl}$• $\ \longrightarrow \ce{Cl2}$\
$2 \ $•$\ce{CH3} \longrightarrow \ce{C2H6}$

Termination steps end the chain reaction and do not occur after each propagation step.
