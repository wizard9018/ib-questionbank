# CH2244

A fixed amount of gas at $27\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$ occupies a volume of $300 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ at a pressure of $150\textrm{\ kPa}.$ Half of the gas is then removed, and the remaining gas expands to a volume of $450 \textrm{\ cm}^3.$ If the pressure remains constant throughout, what is the final temperature of the gas?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}81\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}354\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}627\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}900\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
This problem involves applying the ideal gas law when pressure remains constant throughout the process. Starting with $PV = nRT$ and rearranging for constant pressure, we get $P =nRT/V,$ which means that $nT/V$ must remain constant when pressure is fixed.

The initial conditions are $T_1 = 27\ ^\circ\textrm{C} = 300 \textrm{\ K},$ $V_1 = 300 \textrm{\ cm}^3,$ and initial amount $n_1.$ Gas laws require temperature to be in absolute units (Kelvin). After the changes, we have $n_2 = \frac{1}{2}n_1$ because half of the gas is removed, $V_2 = 450 \textrm{\ cm}^3,$ and an unknown final temperature $T_2.$

Since pressure is constant,

$\dfrac{n_1T_1}{V_1} = \dfrac{n_2T_2}{V_2}$

Substituting the given values:

$\dfrac{n_1 \times 300 \textrm{\ K}}{300 \textrm{\ cm}^3} = \dfrac{\frac{1}{2}n₁ × T₂}{450 \textrm{\ cm}^3}$

Rearranging and simplifying:

$T_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \times 300 \textrm{\ K} \times 450 \textrm{\ cm}^3}{\frac{1}{2}n_1 \times 300 \textrm{\ cm}^3} = 900 \textrm{\ K}$

Converting from Kelvin back to Celsius gives

$T_2 = 900 - 273 = 627\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$

The temperature must increase substantially to offset both the reduction in gas particles and the increase in volume, each of which would lower pressure unless compensated by a dramatic rise in thermal energy. To maintain the original pressure of 150 kPa despite these two pressure$-$reducing effects working together, heating to $627\ ^\circ\textrm{C}$ is required.
