# CH2261

A complex ion $\ce{[ML6]^{2+}},$ where M is a transition metal and L is a ligand, appears blue in solution. The same metal forms a complex $\ce{[MQ6]^{2+}},$ in which ligand Q produces a weaker crystal field than ligand L.

What colour is $\ce{[MQ6]^{2+}},$ most likely to appear?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Red

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Green

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Yellow

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Violet

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
This question assesses the student's conceptual understanding of crystal field theory, specifically the connection between ligand strength, $d-$orbital splitting energy, and observed color.

If $\ce{[ML6]^{2+}}$ appears blue, it absorbs light in the orange region of the visible spectrum (complementary to blue, around 600 nm). When a weaker$-$field ligand such as Q replaces L, the crystal field splitting becomes smaller. This reduces the energy required for $d\text{--}d$ transitions, meaning that $\ce{[MQ6]^{2+}}$ will absorb light of longer wavelength, shifting absorption from orange toward red.

If the complex absorbs red light, it will appear green, the complementary color to red. Therefore, the expected color of $\ce{[MQ6]^{2+}}$ is green.
