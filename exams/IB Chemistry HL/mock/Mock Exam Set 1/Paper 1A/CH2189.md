# CH2189

Sulfur trioxide is produced in the Contact process according to the following equilibrium:

$\ce{2 SO2(g) + O2(g) <=>[V2O5] 2 SO3(g)} \qquad \Delta H = -197\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Which of the following changes would increase the yield of sulfur trioxide at equilibrium?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | Adding $\ce{V2O5}$ |
    | II. | Increasing the temperature |
    | III. | Increasing the pressure |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Le Chatelier's principle predicts that a system at equilibrium will respond to external changes by shifting in a direction that minimizes the effect of the change.

Although vanadium(V) oxide is an essential catalyst in the Contact process, it increases the rate at which equilibrium is reached but has no effect on the position of equilibrium itself.  Catalysts provide an alternate reaction pathway with a lower activation energy speeding up the forward and reverse reactions equally but having no effect on the yield or the equilibrium constant.

Increasing the temperature favors the reverse reaction because the forward reaction is exothermic. Higher temperature decreases the equilibrium yield of $\ce{SO3}$ and decreases the equilibrium constant.

The forward reaction results in a decrease in the total number of gas molecules (from three moles to two). Increasing the pressure compresses the system.  The equilibrium shifts to the right to relieve that pressure increase, thereby increasing the yield of sulfur trioxide.  There is no effect on the equilibrium constant.
