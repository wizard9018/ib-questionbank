# CH2234

Consider the following standard electrode potentials for iron species:

$\ce{Fe(C_{12}H_8N_2)_3^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e⁻} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Fe(C_{12}H_8N_2)_3^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$| $E^{\minuso} = +1.06 \textrm{\ V}$
:-:|:-:
$\ce{Fe^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Fe}^{2+}\textrm{(aq)}$ | $E^{\minuso} = +0.77 \textrm{\ V}$

where $\ce{C_{12}H_8N_2}$ is the neutral bidentate ligand $1,10-$phenanthroline.  What does this data suggest about the effect of the phenanthroline ligand on the iron center?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$It makes $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ easier to reduce.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$It makes $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$⁺ easier to oxidize.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$It stabilizes the lower oxidation state.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$It stabilizes the higher oxidation state.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The increase in standard electrode potential from $+0.77 \textrm{\ V}$ to $+1.06 \textrm{\ V}$ indicates that $\ce{Fe(C_{12}H_8N_2)_3^{3+}}$ is more easily reduced than $\ce{Fe^{3+}}.$ This occurs because phenanthroline preferentially stabilizes $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ (the lower oxidation state) relative to $\ce{Fe^{3+}}.$

As shown in the energy diagram, both oxidation states are stabilized by complexation, but $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$
experiences much greater stabilization. This differential stabilization increases the energy gap between the oxidized and reduced forms.

![CH2234](images/CH2234.png)

The relationship between free energy and electrode potential is given by $\Delta G^{\minuso}=-nFE^{\minuso}.$ Since both reduction potentials are positive, both reductions are thermodynamically favorable. However, the larger electrode potential $(+1.06 \textrm{\ V} \textit{\ vs.} +0.77 \textrm{\ V})$ for the phenanthroline complex indicates a more negative  $\Delta G^{\minuso},$ making this reduction even more favorable.

When the reduced form is preferentially stabilized, the reduction becomes more thermodynamically favorable, resulting in a higher (more positive) $E^{\minuso}$ value. If the higher oxidation state were more stabilized instead, the electrode potential would decrease rather than increase.
