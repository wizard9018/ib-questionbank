# CH2248

Trichloromethane is a volatile liquid, while polychloroethene is a rigid solid at room temperature.

![CH2248a](images/CH2248a.png)|![CH2248b](images/CH2248b.png)
:-:|:-:|
trichloromethane | polychloroethene

What best explains the difference in their physical states?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Polychloroethene has stronger covalent bonds than $\ce{CHCl3}.$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CHCl3}$ molecules are held together by weaker intermolecular forces than polychloroethene chains.
-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Polychloroethene has ionic character while $\ce{CHCl3}$ is purely covalent.
-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CHCl3}$ has hydrogen bonding while polychloroethene does not.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
This question assesses the distinction between intramolecular bonding and intermolecular forces in determining the physical state of substances.

While both trichloromethane $(\ce{CHCl3})$ and polychloroethene contain polar $\ce{C-Cl}$ bonds, they differ dramatically in their melting point due to the difference in the  strength of their intermolecular interactions.

Trichloromethane exists as small, discrete molecules. These molecules interact through dipole$-$dipole attractions due to the polar $\ce{C-Cl}$ bonds and the asymmetric molecular geometry. They also have weaker London dispersion forces between the molecules. However, because the molecules are small, the total intermolecular attraction between molecules is moderate, allowing them to move freely past each other at room temperature, resulting in a liquid.

Polychloroethene is composed of long polymer chains with thousands of repeat units. Although the individual bonds in each repeat unit are similar to those in $\ce{CHCl3},$ the extended chain structure increases the surface area for interaction. The cumulative dipole$-$dipole forces between polar $\ce{C-Cl}$ bonds and the much larger London dispersion forces between chains result in strong intermolecular attractions. These prevent the chains from sliding past one another, giving polychloroethene its rigid, solid character.
