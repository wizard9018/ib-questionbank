# CH2250

Which of the following statements explain why magnesium has a stronger metallic bond than sodium?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}$Magnesium ions have a greater charge than sodium ions.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Magnesium ions have a smaller radius than sodium ions.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Each magnesium atom contributes more electrons to the delocalized electron sea than each sodium atom.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The strength of a metallic bond is determined by the electrostatic attraction between the lattice of metal cations and the delocalized electrons that surround them. This attraction depends on several key factors:

-   * ***Charge on the metal cations:*** Higher charged cations create stronger electrostatic attraction with the delocalized electrons. Magnesium forms $\ce{Mg^{2+}}$ ions, whereas sodium forms $\ce{Na+}$ ions, so magnesium has a higher charge on its cations. Statement I is correct.
-   * ***Size of the metal ions:*** Smaller cations mean the positive charge is concentrated in a smaller volume, creating higher charge density. This brings the positive nuclei closer to the delocalized electrons, strengthening the electrostatic attraction. Magnesium ions have a smaller ionic radius than sodium ions (72 pm *vs.* 102 pm), meaning the positive nuclei are closer to the sea of delocalized electrons. Statement II is correct.
-   * ***Number of delocalized electrons per atom:*** More electrons in the electron sea means greater electron density available for bonding. Metals that contribute more valence electrons to the delocalized system will have stronger bonding. Magnesium has two valence electrons to contribute to the delocalized electron sea, while sodium only has one. Statement III is correct.

These three factors work together to make metallic bonding in magnesium stronger than in sodium.
