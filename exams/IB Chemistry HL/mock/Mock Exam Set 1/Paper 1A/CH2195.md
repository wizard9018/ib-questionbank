# CH2195

The structure of curcumin is shown below.

![CH2195](images/CH2195.png)

How many $\textbf{additional}$ stereoisomers of curcumin exist?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}0$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}1$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}2$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}3$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textbf{\underline{Explanation:}}$

To determine the number of unique stereoisomers of curcumin, we need to analyze the stereogenic elements in the molecule. Curcumin has no chiral centers but does contain two double bonds capable of *cis*/*trans* isomerism.

Since there are two independent $\ce{C=C}$ bonds, each of which can be *cis* or *trans*, the total number of stereoisomers is:

$2^2 = 4$

| ![CH2195transtrans](images/CH2195transtrans.png) $\\ trans,trans$ | ![CH2195ciscis](images/CH2195ciscis.png) $\\ cis,cis$ |
| :-: | :-: |
| ![CH2195cistrans](images/CH2195cistrans.png) $\\ cis,trans$ | ![CH2195transcis](images/CH2195transcis.png) $\\ trans,cis$ |

However, curcumin is symmetric, meaning that some isomers identified above may be the same molecule and must be subtracted from the total. Since the molecule is symmetric, the (*cis*,*trans*) and (*trans*,*cis*) forms are identical (imagine rotating the molecule 180 degrees around the carbonyl bond at its center). There are 3 unique stereoisomers of curcumin, so there are :answer[**2 more**].
