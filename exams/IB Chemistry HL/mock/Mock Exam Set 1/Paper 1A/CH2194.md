# CH2194

Three elements have increasing atomic number and outer electron configurations of:

X: $ns^2\ np^2$\
Y: $ns^2\ np^3$\
Z: $ns^2\ np^5$

Which correctly ranks these elements from smallest to largest ***magnitude*** for each property?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Atomic Radius | Ionization Energy | Electron Affinity |
    | :- | :-: | :-:| :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{Z < Y < X}$ | $\ce{X < Y < Z}$ | $\ce{Y < X < Z}$ |
    | B. | $\ce{Z < Y < X}$ | $\ce{Z < Y < X}$ | $\ce{Z < Y < X}$ |
    | C. | $\ce{Z < X < Y}$ | $\ce{Z < X < Y}$ | $\ce{Y < Z < X}$ |
    | D. | $\ce{Z < Y < X}$ | $\ce{X < Y < Z}$ | $\ce{X < Y < Z}$ |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

This question assesses understanding of periodic trends.

Atomic radius decreases across a period because nuclear charge increases while electrons are added to the same principal energy level. Shielding remains nearly constant, so the stronger nuclear attraction pulls the outer electrons closer to the nucleus. This results in the trend:

Atomic Radius: $\ce{Z < Y < X}$

First ionization energy generally increases across a period as effective nuclear charge increases. Electrons are held more tightly and require more energy to remove. Exceptions occur at Group 13 $(ns^2\ np^1)$ and Group 16 $(ns^2\ np^4),$ where ionization energies are slightly lower than expected. In Group 13, the $p$ electron is shielded by a full $s$ subshell and more weakly held; in Group 16, electron$\text{--}$electron repulsion within the doubly occupied $p$ orbital makes removal easier. Since none of the configurations listed fall into these categories, the general trend holds:

Ionization Energy: $\ce{X < Y < Z}$

Electron affinity becomes more negative across a period as effective nuclear charge increases, strengthening the electrostatic attraction between the nucleus and an added electron. However, important exceptions occur at Group 2 $(ns^2),$ Group 15 $(ns^2\ np^3),$ and Group 18 $(ns^2\ np^6).$ In Group 2, the added electron must enter a new $p$ orbital, which is more shielded and less tightly held. In Group 15, the added electron pairs in an already half$\text{--}$filled $p$ orbital, increasing electron$\text{--}$electron repulsion and reducing the energy released. In Group 18, the added electron enters a new principal shell where it is fully shielded from the nucleus. In this question, element Y has a half$\text{--}$filled $p$ subshell $(ns^2\ np^3),$ which reduces its electron affinity. This explains the trend in magnitude (or absolute value):

Electron Affinity: $\ce{Y < X < Z}$
