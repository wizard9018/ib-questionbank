# CH2267

For the reaction $2 \ce{\,A}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{B}\textrm{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,C}\textrm{(g)},$ $K = 100$ at $500 \textrm{\ K}.$

At a particular moment, the concentrations are $\ce{[A]} = 0.2 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3},$ $\ce{[B]} = 0.1 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3},$ and $\ce{[C]} = 1.0 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$ What will happen to reach equilibrium?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$The reaction will proceed to the right because $Q < K$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The reaction will proceed to the left because $Q > K.$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The reaction will proceed to the right because $Q > K.$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The reaction will proceed to the left because $Q < K.$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\
To determine the direction of reaction, calculate the reaction quotient $Q$ and compare it to the equilibrium constant $K.$

The reaction quotient uses the same expression as the equilibrium constant but with current concentrations rather than equilibrium concentrations. For the reaction $2 \ce{\,A}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{B}\textrm{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,C}\textrm{(g)},$ the expression is

$Q = \dfrac{\ce{[C]}^2}{\ce{[A]}^2\ce{[B]}}$

Substituting the given concentrations:

$Q = \dfrac{1.0^2}{0.2^2 \times 0.1} = \dfrac{1.0}{0.004} = 250 $

Since $Q = 250$ and $K = 100,$ we have $Q > K.$ When the reaction quotient exceeds the equilibrium constant, the system has too much product relative to reactants compared to the equilibrium position. Therefore, the reaction must proceed in the reverse direction (left) to consume some product C and form more reactants A and B until $Q$ decreases to equal $K.$

This fundamental principle allows prediction of reaction direction: when $Q < K,$ the reaction proceeds forward; when $Q > K,$ the reaction proceeds backward; and when $Q = K,$ the system is at equilibrium with no net change.
