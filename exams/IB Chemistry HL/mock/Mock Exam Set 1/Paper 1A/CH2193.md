# CH2193

Propanone has a low boiling point $(56^\circ\text{C})$ but is very soluble in water.  What is the best explanation?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Propanone has a low molar mass, so it evaporates easily and dissolves well.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Propanone is polar and forms hydrogen bonds with itself and with water.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Propanone cannot hydrogen bond with itself, but can accept hydrogen bonds from water.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Propanone contains a carbonyl group, which makes it nonpolar and volatile.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Propanone is a small, weakly polar aprotic molecule containing a carbonyl group $(\ce{C=O}),$ which has a highly electronegative oxygen atom capable of accepting hydrogen bonds. However, it does not have an $\ce{-OH}$ or $\ce{-NH}$ group, so it cannot donate hydrogen bonds and therefore cannot form hydrogen bonds with itself. This limits the strength of its intermolecular forces contributing to its relatively low boiling point of $56^\circ\text{C}.$

![CH2193b](images/CH2193b.png)

Despite this, propanone is very soluble in water because it can act as a hydrogen bond acceptor. Water molecules, which have hydrogen atoms bonded to oxygen, can act as hydrogen bond donors to the lone pairs on the carbonyl oxygen in propanone. This favorable interaction allows propanone to mix readily with water, even though it doesn't hydrogen bond with itself.
