# CH2266

Using Section 13 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, what is the enthalpy change, in $\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1},$ for the combustion of 2 moles of methanol to produce carbon dioxide and water?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$2\ce{\,CH3OH}\textrm{(l)} + 3\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}(2\times-393 + 4\times-286) - (2\times-239)$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}2\times(-393 - 239 + 2\times-286)$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}(2\times-239) - (2\times-393 + 4\times-286)$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}(2\times-393 + 4\times-286) + (2\times-239)$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To calculate the enthalpy change of a reaction using enthalpies of formation, apply Hess's Law:

$\Delta H^{\minuso}_{rxn}=\sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_f\textrm{(products)}-\sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_f\textrm{(reactants)}$

Oxygen is an element and has $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f = 0.$ Each of the standard enthalpies of combusiont must be multiplied by the coefficient in the balanced chemical reaction. So,

$\Delta H^{\minuso} = \left[2(-393)+4(-286)]-[2(-239)\right]$
