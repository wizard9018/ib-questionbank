# CH2190

A student wants to determine the rate law for the reaction

$\ce{H2O2 + 3 I- + 2 H+ -> I3- + 2 H2O}$

using the ascorbic acid (vitamin C) clock reaction. Ascorbic acid reacts very quickly with the triiodide ion

$\ce{I3- + C6H8O6 ->[fast] 2 H+ + 3 I- + C6H6O6}$

When the ascorbic acid is consumed, the triiodide ion will persist and react with starch in the reaction mixture to make a blue$\text{--}$black complex.

$\ce{I3- + starch ->[fast]  \text{starch---}I3-}$

The student conducted this experiment using a range of concentrations and tabulated the time taken for a color change:

| Experiment | [H$_\mathbf{2}$O$\mathbf{_2}$] $\\$ (mol dm$^\mathbf{-3}$) | [I$^\mathbf{-}$] $\\$ (mol dm$^\mathbf{-3}$) | Time $\\$ (s) |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | 0.88 | 0.148 | 35 |
| 2 | 0.44 | 0.148 | 70 |
| 3 | 0.22 | 0.072 | 279 |

What is the rate law?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\text{rate} = k[\ce{H2O2}]^{-1}[\ce{I-}]^{-1}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\text{rate} = k[\ce{H2O2}]^{1}[\ce{I-}]^{1}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\text{rate} = k[\ce{H2O2}]^{1}[\ce{I-}]^{0}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\text{rate} = k[\ce{H2O2}]^{-1}[\ce{I-}]^{1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

This question requires recognizing that the time to color change in a clock reaction is inversely related to the rate of formation of $\ce{I3-};$ in other words, a shorter time corresponds to a faster rate.

To determine the order with respect to $\ce{H2O2},$ compare Experiments 1 and 2, where the concentration of $\ce{I-}$ remains constant. In this comparison, the concentration of $\ce{H2O2}$ is halved from 0.88 to $0.44\text{ mol dm}^{-3},$ and the time for the appearance of the color doubles from 35 seconds to 70 seconds. This inverse relationship indicates that the rate is directly proportional to $[\ce{H2O2}],$ and thus the reaction is first order with respect to hydrogen peroxide.

To determine the order with respect to $\ce{I-},$ we examine Experiments 2 and 3, where both $[\ce{H2O2}]$ and $[\ce{I-}]$ are halved. In this case, the time increases from 70 seconds to 279 seconds, approximately a fourfold increase. Since we have already established that halving $[\ce{H2O2}]$ on its own causes the rate to halve, the remaining factor of two in the rate change must be due to halving $[\ce{I-}],$ which also suggests a first$\text{--}$order dependence on $[\ce{I-}].$

Therefore, based on the experimental data, the overall rate law is: :answer[**rate = k[H$_\mathbf{2}$O$_\mathbf{2}$][I$^\mathbf{-}$]**]
