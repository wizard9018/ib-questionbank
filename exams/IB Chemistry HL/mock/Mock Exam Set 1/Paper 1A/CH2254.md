# CH2254

Which of the following statements are true when comparing biofuels with fossil fuels?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}$Biofuels are considered carbon neutral.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Biofuels can be obtained through fermentation of sugar or esterification of fatty acids present in plant sources.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$The specific energy of biofuels is greater than that of fossil fuels.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only
-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$II and III only
-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Biofuels are considered carbon neutral because the carbon dioxide emitted during their combustion is approximately balanced by the $\ce{CO2}$ absorbed by plants during photosynthesis. However, a more nuanced analysis reveals that biofuels are not perfectly carbon neutral when considering the energy required for cultivation, harvesting, processing, and transportation adds to their carbon footprint. Despite this, biofuels still represent a key sustainability advantage over fossil fuels, which release carbon that has been stored underground for millions of years, adding new $\ce{CO2}$ to the atmosphere. Statement I is true.

Biofuels can be produced through fermentation of carbohydrates (*e.g.* producing ethanol from glucose) and esterification of plant$-$derived fatty acids and alcohols (*e.g.* producing biodiesel). Statement II is true.

Fossil fuels generally have a higher specific energy (energy per unit mass) than biofuels. This represents one of the key technical limitations of biofuels, particularly for applications requiring high specific energy such as aviation and long-distance transportation. The lower energy content of biofuels results from the presence of oxygen atoms in their molecular structure, which means the carbon atoms are already partially oxidized. This means less energy is available when they undergo complete combustion compared to the hydrocarbons found in fossil fuels. Statement III is false.
