# CH2232

Two acids, X and Y, each with a concentration of $0.100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3},$ were titrated separately with $0.100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ sodium hydroxide.  The titration curves are shown below.

Acid X | Acid Y
:-:|:-:
![CH2232a](images/CH2232a.png)|![CH2232b2](images/CH2232b2.png)

Which indicator would be suitable for detecting the equivalence point in the titration of Acid X, but not of Acid Y?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$methyl orange

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$phenolphthalein

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$universal indicator

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$phenol red

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To select a suitable indicator, its pH transition range must overlap with the steep vertical region of the titration curve—this is where the pH changes most rapidly, indicating the equivalence point.

For Acid X, the titration curve begins at pH 1, shows a sharp rise around pH 3 to 10, and levels off near pH 13. The equivalence point lies at $\textrm{pH} = 7,$ consistent with a strong acid$-$strong base titration. Because the pH change is steep and spans a wide range, nearly all indicators will change color sharply within this region, including those that transition in either acidic or basic ranges.

For Acid Y, the titration curve starts near pH 3, rises more gradually, and has its equivalence point near pH 9, characteristic of a weak acid$-$strong base titration.  In this case, indicators that change color in the alkaline region are required to detect the endpoint accurately.

Compare the indicator ranges (Section 18 of the IB Chemistry data booklet) to equivalence point of each titration.

|Indicator|    pH Transition Range
:-:|:-:
methyl orange    | 3.1$-$4.4
phenol red |    6.4$-$8.0
phenolphthalein    | 8.0$-$10.0
universal indicator    | inappropriate for titration

**Universal indicator:**
This mixture is not meant to be used in titrations.  Many indicators are combined together to provide gradual color changes across a wide pH range to enable qualitative pH determination.  This is not an analytical tool.
