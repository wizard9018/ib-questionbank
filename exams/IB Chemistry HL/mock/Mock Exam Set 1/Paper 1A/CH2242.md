# CH2242

The diagram shows the energy levels of hydrogen. Which electronic transition results in the emission of a photon with the longest wavelength?

![CH2242](images/CH2242.png)

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Emission occurs when an electron moves from a higher energy level to a lower energy level, releasing energy as a photon. Of the given transitions, the transition $n = 1 \longrightarrow n = 5$ is an absorption since the electron moves to a higher energy level.  The other transitions are emissions.

The wavelength of an emitted photon is inversely related to the energy gap between the initial and final energy levels through the relationship $E = hc/\lambda,$ where $h$ is Planck's constant, $c$ is the speed of light, and $\lambda$ is wavelength. A larger energy gap results in a higher energy photon with shorter wavelength, while a smaller energy gap produces a lower energy photon with longer wavelength.

Among the emission transitions, the transition from $n = 5 \longrightarrow n = 1$ has the largest energy change and will emit the shortest wavelength (UV) light. The transition from $n = 5 \longrightarrow n = 2$ has the smallest energy change and will emit the longest wavelength (visible) light.

This question assesses understanding of atomic energy levels, the relationship between energy and wavelength, and the distinction between emission and absorption processes.
