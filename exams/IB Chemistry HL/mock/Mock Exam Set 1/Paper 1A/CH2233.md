# CH2233

A hydrogen$-$oxygen fuel cell using a polymer electrolyte membrane is shown.

![CH2233](images/CH2233.png)

Which of the following correctly describes the direction of charge carriers during operation?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Electrons flow from cathode to anode; $\ce{H+}$ ions migrate toward the cathode

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Electrons flow from anode to cathode; $\ce{H+}$ ions migrate toward the cathode

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Electrons flow from anode to cathode; $\ce{OH-}$ ions migrate toward the cathode

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Electrons flow from cathode to anode; $\ce{OH-}$ ions migrate toward the cathode

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Fuel cells convert chemical energy into electrical energy using redox reactions at separated electrodes. Depending on the electrolyte, the identity and direction of charge carriers differ, as shown below:

|Feature|Acidic Conditions|Alkaline Conditions
:-:|:-:|:-:
||![CH2233ms1](images/CH2233ms1.png)|![CH2233ms2](images/CH2233ms2.png)
Electrolyte type|    Proton$-$conducting polymer membrane |    Hydroxide$-$conducting membrane
Anode reaction    | $2 \ce{\,H2} \longrightarrow 4\ce{\,H+} + 4\ce{\,e-}$ |    $\ce{2\,H2} + \ce{4\,OH-} \longrightarrow \ce{4\,H2O} + \ce{4\,e-}$
Cathode reaction    | $\ce{O2} + \ce{4\,H+} + \ce{4\,e-} \longrightarrow \ce{2\,H2O}$|    $\ce{O2} + \ce{2\,H2O} + \ce{4\,e-} \longrightarrow \ce{4\,OH-}$
Electron flow $\newline$ (external)  |    anode $\longrightarrow$ cathode    | anode $\longrightarrow$ cathode
Ion flow  $\newline$ (electrolyte) |     $\ce{H+}$ ions migrate toward cathode | $\ce{OH-}$  ions migrate toward anode

In both cells, electrons flow from anode to cathode through the external circuit. What distinguishes them is the direction and identity of ion flow through the electrolyte. In acidic fuel cells, hydrogen is oxidized at the anode, producing $\ce{H+}$ ions, which migrate through the membrane to the cathode.  In alkaline fuel cells, oxygen is reduced at the cathode, generating $\ce{OH-}$ ions that migrate to the anode.
