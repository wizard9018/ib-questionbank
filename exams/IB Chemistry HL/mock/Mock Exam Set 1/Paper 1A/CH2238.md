# CH2238

Which reagents are required to produce chlorobenzene from benzene?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Cl2}$ and UV light

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Cl2}$ and $\ce{FeCl3}$
catalyst

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{HCl}$ and heat

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Cl2}$ and high pressure

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Benzene undergoes chlorination via electrophilic aromatic substitution, a reaction that preserves the resonance stability of its delocalized $\pi$ electrons. Unlike alkenes, benzene does not readily undergo addition reactions or free$-$radical processes because its aromatic ring is unusually stable and resists disruption.

Chlorine gas is not sufficiently electrophilic to react with benzene. A Lewis acid catalyst such as $\ce{FeCl3}$ is required to polarize the $\ce{Cl-Cl}$ bond and generate a $\ce{Cl+}$ electrophile:

$\ce{FeCl3} + \ce{Cl2} \longrightarrow \ce{[FeCl4]^-} +\ce{Cl+}$

The $\ce{Cl+}$ then reacts with benzene, forming a non$-$aromatic carbocation intermediate. Aromaticity is restored when the intermediate loses a proton, yielding chlorobenzene.

![CH2238v2](images/CH2238v2.png)

The $\ce{FeCl3}$ catalyst is regenerated during the reaction.
 
***

*Cl$_\mathit{2}$ and UV light:*  Promotes radical chemistry, not electrophilic aromatic substitution. With benzene, photochemical/radical conditions lead to addition products like $\ce{C6H6Cl6}.$

*HCl and heat:*  No chlorinating agent/electrophile is generated; $\ce{HCl} cannot deliver $\ce{Cl+ }$ to an aromatic ring. No reaction.

*Cl$_\mathit{2}$ and high pressure:*
Pressure does not solve the key issue (insufficient electrophilicity). In harsh non$-$catalytic conditions benzene favors addition (loss of aromaticity), not the selective substitution required for chlorobenzene.
