# CH2200

Which of the following molecules violate the octet rule?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{ClF3}$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}\ce{NO2}$
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}\ce{O3}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$II and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To determine whether a molecule violates the octet rule, carefully draw its Lewis structure and account for the number of valence electrons surrounding each atom in a completed form.

$\ce{ClF3}:$ Chlorine contributes 7 valence electrons, and each fluorine contributes 7: $7+(3\times7)=28$ valence electrons. Start by placing chlorine in the center with three single bonds to fluorine atoms (6 electrons). Complete the octets on each fluorine with 6 more electrons each (18 electrons total). This accounts for 24 electrons. The 4 remaining electrons are placed as two lone pairs on chlorine. Chlorine has 3 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs, 10 total electrons, in its valence. This exceeds the octet. Chlorine is in Period 3, meaning it has access to vacant $3d$ orbitals, which allow it to expand its valence shell beyond eight electrons.  $\ce{ClF3}$ violates the octet rule by expanding the octet of the central Cl atom.

NO₂ :
Nitrogen contributes 5 valence electrons, and each oxygen contributes 6: $5+(2\times6)=17$ valence electrons. Attach both oxygens to the central nitrogen atom with single bonds (4 electrons), and place three lone pairs on each oxygen to complete their octets (12 more electrons). Sixteen electrons have been used. The 17th electron is unpaired and placed on the central nitrogen atom, creating a radical.  One oxygen will promote a lone pair of electrons to form a double bond so that nitrogen has 7 electrons in its valence shell. This molecule has two resonance structures. $\ce{NO2}$ violates the octet rule by being an odd$-$electron species.

$\ce{O3}:$  Each oxygen contributes 6 valence electrons: $3\times6=18$ valence electrons. Attach the oxygens in a line. Connect them with single bonds (4 electrons). Give each outer oxygen three lone pairs (6 electrons each). 16 electrons have been used so far. The remaining 2 electrons go on the central oxygen as a lone pair. The central oxygen has only 6 electrons. To satisfy the octet, one lone pair from an outer oxygen is promoted to form a double bond. There are two resonance structures where each atom achieves an octet. $\ce{O3}$ obeys the octet rule.
