## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

A polyamide is formed through the condensation of an amine and either a carboxylic acid $(\ce{-COOH})$ or an acyl chloride $(\ce{-COCl}).$ Each monomer must have two functional groups for polymerization to occur. In this case, two different bifunctional monomers are required: one containing two amine groups and the other containing two acyl chloride groups. This arrangement ensures that each new amide bond forms between an $\ce{-NH2}$ group on one monomer and a $\ce{-COCl}$ group on a different monomer, resulting in a polymer where the amide linkages alternate in direction along the chain.

![CH2192ans](images/CH2192ans.png)

This alternating orientation, where the carbonyl group of one amide points in the opposite direction from the next, is only possible when two distinct monomers are used, each contributing one side of the amide linkage. In contrast, a single monomer containing both an amine and an acyl chloride group would form amide bonds in the same direction, as is seen in protein primary structures.
