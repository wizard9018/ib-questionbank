# EC0460

In 2023, food and beverage sectors grappled with a $\mathrm{CO_2}$* shortage. Soda manufacturers faced production hurdles due to this scarcity, originating from a basic supply-demand imbalance. $\mathrm{CO_2}$ primarily comes from industries like ethanol, ammonia, and oil refineries, alongside geological sources.

*$\mathrm{CO_2}$ is crucial for beverage carbonation

[Bell, J., 2023. Jan. 17,“What's Causing the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ Shortage and How to Remedy it.” Food Logistics. Retrieved 1 May 2024, from https://www.foodlogistics.com/transportation/cold-chain/article/22618121/university-of-tennessee-knoxvilles-haslam-college-of-business-whats-causing-the-co2-shortage-and-how-to-remedy-it. Source adapted.]

1.  Using an appropriate demand and supply diagram, sketch a likely outcome in the soda market due to the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ shortage. [3 marks]

2.  Using an appropriate demand and supply diagram, sketch the impact of a shortage of $\mathrm{CO_2}$  on a substitute good for carbonated beverages. [2 marks]

Economists state the price elasticity of demand for carbonated beverages in a market is greater than 1.

    

3.    Explain why the PED for carbonated beverages is greater than 1. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

    ![EC0460 fig1](<images/EC0460 fig1.png>)

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$: \

    A decrease of the availability of an input leads to a decrease in the supply of the product, causing an increase in price at every given quantity. The shortage of safe carbon dioxide will cause a rise in the price of soda for consumers. This is because the shortage of $\mathrm{CO_2}$  will cause the suppliers to have to charge more for every given quantity of soda they are able to supply. Ultimately, this will lead to market inefficiency, causing people who want to drink soda to be unable to afford as much of it, thus decreasing the consumer surplus that would be enjoyed under normal market conditions. 

    For showing: 

    -   a correctly labelled supply and demand diagram ($\checkmark$)

    -   a shift in supply to the left ($\checkmark$)

    -   an increase in equilibrium price and a decrease in quantity demanded ($\checkmark$)

2.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

    ![EC0460 fig2](<images/EC0460 fig2.png>)

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$: \

    As the availability of $\mathrm{CO_2}$  becomes limited, the price for carbonated beverages will rise, causing the quantity demanded to decrease.  However, consumers may want to purchase other beverages, causing the demand for substitute goods (such as non-carbonated drinks) to increase.

    For including:

    -   Rightward shift due to the increase of price of a substitute good ($\checkmark$)

    -   an increase in equilibrium price and quantity ($\checkmark$)

3.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

    Soda is not necessary for survival and has a high elasticity of demand, meaning that a  change in price will lead to a larger change in quantity demanded. The rising cost of carbon dioxide is expected to lead to a significant decrease in demand for soda and other carbonated beverages. As a result, consumers are likely to seek substitute products that are not affected by the supply disruption caused by the reduced availability of $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$: \

    -   Stating that when the price of carbonated beverages rises, the quantity demanded falls by a greater percentage. ($\checkmark$)

    -   Stating that the PED for carbonated beverages tends to be high ($\checkmark$)
