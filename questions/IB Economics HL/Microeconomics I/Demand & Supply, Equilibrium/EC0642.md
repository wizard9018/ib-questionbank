# EC0642

Mobility is essential to economic and social development. Well-targeted infrastructure investment and network design are crucial for a transport system that provides accessibility to people and businesses. For the European Union, rail transport plays a key role in such a transport system and is an important priority.

Passengers switching from air travel to rail travel on routes where rail is faster would reduce the total amount of $\ce{CO2}$ emissions from air trips by 17$\%$ and passenger travel time on these routes by 4.2$\%$.

**Figure 1: Overall travel time for rail and air trips on two routes in 2019**

![EC0642](images/EC0642.jpg)

Brons M., Dijkstra, L., & Poelman H. (2023). *How fast are rail trips between EU cities and is rail faster than air?* Publications Office of the European Union. Retrieved 19 August 2024, from https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/sources/work/2023-rail-vs-air_en.pdf. Source adapted. CC BY 4.0.

1. Explain why rail travel can be considered a private good. [3 marks]

2. Using information from **Figure 1**, explain why air travel and rail travel can be considered close substitutes in the market for travel between Madrid and Granada. [2 marks]

3. Explain why passengers switching from air travel to rail travel on certain routes can reduce negative externalities. [2 marks]

4. Using the information in **Figure 1**, explain how the price elasticity of demand for air travel on the two routes would differ. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer:}}$\

    Rail travel is both rival and excludable, making it a private good. It is rival as, for example, the same seat cannot be shared by two or more people. It is excludable as travellers can be stopped from using trains if they do not pay for tickets.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For stating that a private good must be both rival and excludable ($\checkmark$).

    For explaining the meaning of rival and excludable ($\checkmark$).

    

     - A good is rival if only one person can use it at a time.

     - A good is excludable if a firm can force a person to pay to access it.

    

    For applying the concepts of rival and excludable to rail travel ($\checkmark$).

     - Rail travel is rival because train capacity is limited.

     - Rail travel is excludable because access to trains is easily controlled.

2.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer:}}$\

    As the total travel times between Madrid and Granada by flight and train are approximately the same, consumers may consider the two means of transport as very similar alternatives. Therefore, they can be viewed as close substitutes. 

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For explaining that the duration of the trip between Madrid are Granada is approximately the same for either air or rail travel ($\checkmark$). For this reason, consumers will see the two means of transport as very similar, meaning they can be considered close substitutes ($\checkmark$).

3.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer:}}$\

    By using rail instead of air travel, passengers can reduce their total $\ce{CO2}$ emissions by 17$\%$, lowering the negative consequences for society of global warming and climate change. 

    

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For explaining that using rail instead of air travel can lead to a reduction in $\ce{CO2}$ emissions ($\checkmark$) which can reduce negative externalities such as global warming, climate change or extreme weather events ($\checkmark$).

4.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer:}}$\

    As the train and flight travel times for the Madrid-Granada route are approximately the same, the two means of transport can be considered close substitutes. On the contrary, the significant difference in train and flight travel times for the Rotterdam-Strasbourg route makes the two services weaker substitutes. Because the number and closeness of substitutes affect PED, air travel between Granada and Madrid will be relatively more elastic than air travel between Rotterdam and Strasbourg.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For stating that the PED would likely be relatively more elastic for air travel on the Madrid-Granada route ($\checkmark$).

    - N.b. this could be explained by suggesting a PED value for the Madrid-Granada route that is greater than a suggested PED value for the Rotteredam-Strasbourg route

    For explaining the similar train and flight travel times between Madrid and Granada result in the two services being close substitutes ($\checkmark$).

    For explaining the differences in travel time between Rotterdam and Strasburg makes them weak substitutes ($\checkmark$). For explaining that the presence of more, or more suitable, substitutes makes the demand for a good relatively more elastic ($\checkmark$)
