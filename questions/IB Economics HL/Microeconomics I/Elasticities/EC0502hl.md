# EC0502hl

The price of a bushel of wheat in 2023 was \$6.08, and in that year 1.8 billion bushels were produced in the USA. 

A bushel is a unit of measurement equal to 35.2 litres.

[Economic Research Service, United States Department of Agriculture. 2024, March 27. Wheat Sector at a Glance. Retrieved May 20, 2024, from https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/crops/wheat/wheat-sector-at-a-glance/.]

1.  Calculate the number of bushels of wheat that would be produced if the price elasticity of supply of wheat is 0.2, and the price of wheat increases by 25\% to \$7.60 per bushel. [3 marks]

2.  Explain **two** reasons why primary commodities tend to have a lower PES than manufactured (secondary) goods. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

    $\dfrac{\%\Delta Qs}{\%\Delta P} = PES$

    $\dfrac{x}{25\%} = 0.2$

    $x = 0.2 \times 25\%$

    $x = 5\%$

    $1.8\ billion \times 1.05 = 1.89\ billion\ bushels$

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$: 

  

    $\dfrac{\%\Delta Qs}{\%\Delta P} = PES$

    $\%\Delta P \times PES = \%\Delta Qs$

    

    $\ new \ wheat \ production = 1.8\ billion \times \dfrac{(100 + \%\Delta Qs)}{100}$

    Showing correct calculations for:

    -   percentage change in quantity supplied ($\checkmark$)

    -   new quantity supplied of wheat ($\checkmark$)

    For the correct answer of :answer[**1.89 billion**] ($\checkmark$)

2.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

    The PES of primary products tends to be lower than that of secondary goods as it takes longer to increase output in the agricultural and extractive industries than it does in the manufacturing sector. 

    This can be because growing crops like wheat and rice takes a long time due to the seasonal nature of agriculture, whereas the production of goods like home appliances and electronics does not face that kind of constraint, so the quantity supplied can be more responsive. 

    Capital goods in manufacturing also tend to be more substitutable than in primary production. Machinery used to produce men's shirts could also be used to produce women's dresses at short notice, while equipment used in agriculture, like combine harvesters, or in mining, like large drills are more specialised, and cannot be used to produce something else, even if demand for that other good were to increase rapidly. 

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$:\

    For an explanation including **two** of the reasons below (a-e):

    a. It takes longer to produce ($\checkmark$) primary goods than secondary goods ($\checkmark$).\

    **or**\

    b. Agricultural output tends to be perishable ($\checkmark$), while manufactured goods are not, so the PES for manufactured goods tends to be higher as they can be stored when demand is low, and released to the market when demand is high ($\checkmark$).\

    **or**\

    c. Marginal costs for manufactured goods tend to be lower than for primary commodities($\checkmark$), which makes the supply of primary goods less responsive as it is more costly to increase production($\checkmark$).\

    **or**\

    d. Capital equipment in manufacturing is substitutable ($\checkmark$), while equipment in primary production is more specialised ($\checkmark$). \

    **or**\

    e. Manufacturing is more capital-intensive ($\checkmark$), while primary products are land-intensive. Land is fixed, while more capital goods can be acquired if needed ($\checkmark$).
