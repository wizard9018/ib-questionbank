# BM0897

MegaMart is a US-based retail MNC that has recently opened five large stores in Malaysia. MegaMart offers a vast range of products at prices up to 30% lower than local retailers, made possible by its global economies of scale and established international supply chains spanning 40 countries. MegaMart has also launched a MegaMart Rewards loyalty app and invested heavily in social media advertising targeted at Malaysian consumers aged 18–35. Local retailers in the areas surrounding MegaMart's new stores have reported a 25% drop in footfall since its opening. Several independent grocery and household goods shops have already closed.

**Explain** two ways MegaMart's entry into Malaysia could threaten local businesses. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**Sample response:**

One way MegaMart's entry into Malaysia could threaten local businesses is through lower prices. MegaMart can offer products at prices up to 30% lower than local retailers because it benefits from global economies of scale and international supply chains. As a result, customers may switch to MegaMart, reducing sales and revenue for local businesses, making it difficult for them to compete.

A second way MegaMart could threaten local businesses is through more effective marketing, attracting customers away from local retailers through its MegaMart Rewards loyalty app and targeted social media advertising. By encouraging repeat purchases and appealing to consumers aged 18–35, MegaMart may increase customer loyalty and market share. As a result, local businesses could experience reduced footfall, as shown by the reported 25% drop, leading to lower profits and potentially forcing some stores to close.

**Mark allocation:**

**Mark as 2 + 2**

Award **[1]** for identifying the threat and **[1]** for application to the case study.

Accept other valid threats e.g. attracting skilled local workers away from smaller businesses with higher wages, brand recognition driving consumer preference.
