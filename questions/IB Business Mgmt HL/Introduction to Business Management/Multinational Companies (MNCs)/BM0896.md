# BM0896

StreamMax is a US-based MNC offering online video streaming services. It operates in over 80 countries, generating billions in subscription revenue globally. In most markets, StreamMax pays minimal local tax by routing profits through a subsidiary in Luxembourg. It spends heavily on original content produced in the US, meaning little of its revenue is reinvested in the local creative industries of host countries. Local broadcasters and independent film producers in host nations argue that StreamMax has an unfair competitive advantage, as it benefits from local audiences and infrastructure without contributing proportionally to local economies.

**Analyse** two challenges StreamMax presents to its host countries. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**Sample response:**

MNCs can exploit differences in international tax laws to shift profits to low-tax jurisdictions, depriving host governments of significant corporation tax revenues [1]. StreamMax routes profits through Luxembourg, meaning that despite generating billions in subscription revenue from host country audiences, governments in those countries collect minimal tax from its operations. This reduces funding available for public services and creates an uneven playing field - local broadcasters pay full domestic tax rates while competing against a company that does not [1].

MNCs with vast financial resources can outspend and outcompete local businesses, making it difficult for domestic industries to survive [1]. StreamMax's heavy investment in US-produced original content, combined with its global marketing power and low subscription prices made possible by scale, makes it extremely difficult for local broadcasters and independent film producers in host countries to compete for audience attention or investment. Over time, this could lead to the decline of local creative industries and a reduction in locally produced cultural content [1].
