# BM0866

NovaStar PLC is a publicly traded technology company. Its shares are traded on the London Stock Exchange, with institutional investors holding 60% of shares. The board of directors recently approved a costly five-year expansion into Southeast Asia. Many shareholders are unhappy, preferring the company to focus on short-term dividend payments rather than long-term growth.

**Analyse** two implications for NovaStar PLC of its ownership structure. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**Sample response:**

One implication is the conflict between shareholders and management over business strategy. In publicly held companies, separation of ownership and control creates conflict, with shareholders seeking short-term returns and managers focusing on long-term growth. “The fact that ‘many shareholders are deeply unhappy' with the ‘costly five-year expansion' and instead prefer ‘short-term dividend payments' demonstrates conflicting objectives between NovaStar's investors and the board.”

Another implication is increased pressure from shareholders that may limit or influence strategic decision-making. Publicly held companies are accountable to a large number of public shareholders, who can exert significant influence by threatening to sell shares, affecting share price and stability. For NovaStar PLC, “institutional investors holding 60% of shares” have “threatened to sell their stakes,” which could further reduce the share price (already down “12%”) and pressure management to reconsider the expansion.
