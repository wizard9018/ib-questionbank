# HI0511

What does Source B suggest about the role of women in Hutu extremist ideology?
[2 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  An excerpt from the “Hutu Ten Commandments,” published in 1990 in the Rwandan extremist magazine *Kangura*. |

[Source: Wikipedia (n.d). *Hutu Ten Commandments.* Retrieved April 30, 2025 from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu_Ten_Commandments. CC BY-SA 4.0.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Women's value was defined primarily by their domestic roles and loyalty, as the source highlights that they were seen as better wives, mothers, and secretaries, and that their value was defined by their domestic roles and loyalty, not by holding public or political power
- Women were expected to act as ideological enforcers within the private sphere, as the source urges Hutu women to be “vigilant” and to “bring your husbands, brothers and sons back to reason.”
- Hutu women were portrayed as morally and ideologically superior to Tutsi women, as seen in the claim that Hutu women were “more suitable and conscientious,” reinforcing ethnic and gender hierarchies.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source B suggests that Hutu extremist ideology saw women's primary value in their domestic roles, as the source describes them as better wives, mothers, and secretaries than Tutsi women ($\checkmark$).  The source also describes Hutu women as “more suitable and conscientious,” portraying them as better wives, mothers, and secretaries than Tutsi women, reinforcing both gender and ethnic hierarchies ($\checkmark$).
