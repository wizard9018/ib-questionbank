# HI0508

Explain **two** ways the Hutu Ten Commandments sought to marginalise the Tutsi population.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting details:
- Social and political exclusion
    - The Hutu Ten Commandments explicitly called for Hutu to dominate Rwanda’s public institutions, banning Tutsi from positions of power or influence.
    - For example, Tutsis were systematically removed from government jobs and leadership roles, reinforcing Hutu political control.
- Economic discrimination
    - The Ten Commandments reinforced the idea that Tutsi were "dishonest" in business and should not be trusted in professional relationships, ensuring economic exclusion and systemic marginalisation.
    - Hutu employers were encouraged to hire only Hutus, leading to systemic economic exploitation of Tutsis.
- Promotion of anti-Tutsi hate and violence
    - Propaganda encouraged hostility toward Tutsis, fuelling public support for discriminatory policies and, later, acts of violence.
    - The commandments declared that any Hutu who married or befriended a Tutsi would be labeled a "traitor" to their own ethnic group, cutting social ties and deepening the division between the Hutus and Tutsis.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The commandments declared that Tutsi were not to be trusted in public or business life, urging Hutu to dominate leadership positions and avoid working with Tutsi ($\checkmark$). This policy created economic and institutional barriers for the Tutsi, pushing them to the fringes of society ($\checkmark$). They also forbade any personal relationships between Hutu and Tutsi, such as marriages or friendships, with violators being branded as traitors ($\checkmark$). This measure deepened the social isolation of the Tutsi and increased hostility between the two groups ($\checkmark$).
