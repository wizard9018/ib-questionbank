# HI0993

What does Source B suggest about the United States' position on the UNAMIR mission in Rwanda?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |A confidential telegram sent on 15 April 1994 by the US Department of State to its mission at the United Nations in New York. The telegram provided official guidance on the immediate withdrawal of UNAMIR personnel from Rwanda during the early days of the genocide. |

[Source: U.S. Department of State. (1994, April 15). *Talking Points for UNAMIR Withdrawal.* National Security Archive. Retrieved July 24, 2025, from https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu//NSAEBB/NSAEBB53/index.html. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The United States firmly opposed maintaining a UN peacekeeping force in Rwanda because it believed that UNAMIR could not operate effectively or safely under the circumstances.
- The source suggests the US prioritised the safety of peacekeepers over continued humanitarian intervention.
- The US felt the situation in Rwanda did not justify a military presence and that negotiations should be handled politically, not by peacekeeping forces.
- The US commended the efforts of those who had participated in UNAMIR but endorsed their withdrawal.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source suggests that the United States firmly opposed the continuation of the UNAMIR mission in Rwanda, arguing that there was no viable role for peacekeepers in the ongoing conflict ($\checkmark$). It also implies that the US prioritised the safety of peacekeeping personnel, as it commended troop-contributing nations for their efforts and supported their decision to withdraw ($\checkmark$).  Furthermore, the source indicates that the US believed any future international involvement should focus on political negotiations led by a representative of the Secretary-General, not through continued peacekeeping operations ($\checkmark$).
