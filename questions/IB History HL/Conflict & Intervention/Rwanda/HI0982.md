# HI0982

Explain **two** ways foreign powers were involved in the Rwandan Civil War between 1990 and 1994.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- France sent troops to Rwanda in 1990 under Opération Noroît, officially to protect French nationals, but in practice to support the Habyarimana regime against the RPF. France also provided weapons and training to the Rwandan Armed Forces throughout the conflict.
- Uganda served as the base from which the RPF launched its initial invasion in October 1990. Although Uganda officially denied involvement, the RPF operated with the logistical advantages of Ugandan territory.
- Belgium, as part of the UN peacekeeping mission (UNAMIR), contributed troops tasked with monitoring the ceasefire and implementation of the Arusha Accords. However, they withdrew their forces after 10 Belgian soldiers were killed in April 1994.
- The United Nations, through UNAMIR, was involved in overseeing the peace process after the Arusha Accords in 1993, but was underfunded, limited in mandate, and unable to prevent escalating violence.
- Zaire provided support to the Habyarimana government by offering military assistance, though on a smaller scale than France.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One way foreign powers were involved was through French military support ($\checkmark$). In 1990, French troops entered Rwanda supposedly to protect French citizens, but they also provided backing to the Rwandan government by helping train its army and supplying weapons ($\checkmark$).

Another example is Uganda's indirect role in supporting the RPF ($\checkmark$). The rebel group launched its invasion from Ugandan territory, and while the Ugandan government denied official involvement, it allowed the RPF to organise and operate across the border ($\checkmark$).
