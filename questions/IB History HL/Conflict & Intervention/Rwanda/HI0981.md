# HI0981

Explain **two** ways the Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF) challenged the authority of the Habyarimana regime between 1990 and 1994.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- The RPF launched a military invasion from Uganda in October 1990, initiating a civil war that exposed the weakness of the Rwandan Armed Forces and forced the regime to rely on foreign military support.
- The RPF maintained a guerrilla war in northern Rwanda, destabilising key regions and creating a sense of national insecurity that undermined public confidence in Habyarimana's leadership.
- The RPF's presence and influence pressured the government into negotiations, leading to the signing of the Arusha Accords in 1993, which proposed power-sharing and military integration—both seen as threats by Habyarimana's hardline supporters.
- The RPF was supported by Tutsi exiles, whose return challenged the government's ethnic nationalism and revived demands for refugee repatriation and political reform.
- The RPF's military professionalism and discipline, especially under Paul Kagame after 1990, made it a credible alternative to the government's disorganised and politicised armed forces.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The RPF challenged the regime by launching an armed invasion from Uganda in 1990 ($\checkmark$). This triggered a civil war that forced the Rwandan government to divert resources to defence and exposed their reliance on foreign allies like France and Zaire for military support ($\checkmark$).

The RPF also challenged the regime through their involvement in political negotiations, particularly the Arusha Peace Agreement in 1993 ($\checkmark$). The RPF's demands for power-sharing and inclusion in the national army threatened Habyarimana's control and provoked opposition from Hutu extremists within his own government ($\checkmark$).
