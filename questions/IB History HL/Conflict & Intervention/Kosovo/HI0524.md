# HI0524

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source A for a historian studying foreign intervention in Kosovo.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpts from United Nations Security Council Resolution 1244, adopted 10 June 1999. |

[Source: *Resolution 1244*. (1999). UN Security Council. Retrieved March 18, 2025 from https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/274488?ln=en&v=pdf. Public Domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:
- The source is an official United Nations Security Council Resolution (1244), adopted by the most powerful international decision-making body. It represents the consensus (or at least the formal agreement) of major global powers at the time, including NATO members and Russia.
- The purpose was to authorize foreign intervention, establish peacekeeping and administrative roles, and lay out a framework for restoring peace and autonomy in Kosovo. As a legal document, it clearly outlines what the international community intended to achieve, making it valuable for understanding the goals and legal justification for intervention.
- The content provides specific details on the mandates for both military (KFOR) and civilian (UNMIK) intervention—demands for Yugoslav withdrawal, the demilitarization of the KLA, and the establishment of an interim administration. It also reflects the legal basis for intervention, representing the scope and nature of foreign intervention.

For limitations, any combination of the following:
- It reflects the official stance of the international community, not necessarily the reality on the ground or the perspectives of local actors such as Kosovar Albanians, Serbs, or other stakeholders.
- As a document produced at the start of the intervention, it does not provide information about what actually happened afterward, including the long-term outcomes, challenges, or unintended consequences of foreign intervention.
- The purpose was to justify intervention, so its language may be idealistic and focused on what should happen, rather than acknowledging the practical difficulties, opposition, criticisms, or potential consequences of intervention.
- The content provides broad goals and mandates, but lacks details on implementation, operational challenges, or local reactions to the intervention.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is a United Nations Security Council resolution adopted in June 1999, at the time when foreign intervention was being formalized. This makes it valuable because it reflects the consensus of major powers, including NATO members and Russia, providing insight into the international community’s agreed approach to ending the conflict and legitimizing intervention ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The purpose of the resolution was to authorize international security and civil presences, specifically KFOR and UNMIK, and to establish a legal framework for foreign intervention, providing insight into the goals of the intervention, including the aim to protect civilians, restore order, and create conditions for self-governance in Kosovo ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The content outlines specific mandates, such as the withdrawal of Yugoslav forces, the demilitarization of the KLA, and the creation of a secure environment for refugees and international agencies, which is useful for understanding the planned scope and nature of foreign intervention ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

However, because it is a formal UN resolution, it reflects only the official stance of the UN and major powers, excluding the perspectives of local actors or critics of intervention ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). The purpose of legitimizing intervention means the document focuses on presenting the intervention in a positive light, and does not acknowledge potential problems, such as challenges in enforcing peace or post-conflict tensions ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).
