# HI0516

Explain the significance of **two** ways in which the international community responded to the Kosovo crisis.   [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Award **one** mark for each way and **one** mark for explaining the significance.

Any of the following:

- NATO bombing campaigns against Serbia

    - NATO launched an aerial bombing campaign in 1999 to pressure Serbia into withdrawing from Kosovo. The campaign forced Serbia to end its campaign against Kosovar Albanians and demonstrated the willingness of Western powers to intervene militarily in ethnic conflicts

- The United Nations deployed the Kosovo Force (KFOR) and established UNMIK

    - KFOR's peacekeeping mission helped prevent further violence and maintained stability in the region following the conflict. UNMIK's administration of Kosovo laid the institutional and political groundwork for Kosovo's transition to self-governance and its eventual unilateral declaration of independence in 2008, though this status remains contested.

- The international community attempted to negotiate a settlement through the Rambouillet talks.

    - The failure of these negotiations exposed the limits of diplomacy in resolving deep-rooted ethnic conflicts. The breakdown of talks justified, in the eyes of NATO and its allies, the necessity of military action and accelerated the decision to launch airstrikes against Serbia.

- The ICTY indicted Slobodan Milošević and other Serbian leaders for war crimes.

    - The indictments sent a clear message that leaders could be held personally accountable for crimes against humanity and war crimes. This reinforced the role of international law in addressing serious human rights violations and helped establish legal precedents for prosecuting sitting or former heads of state.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One way the international community responded to the Kosovo crisis was through NATO's bombing campaign against Serbia in 1999 ($\checkmark$). This intervention forced Serbian forces to pull out of Kosovo, bringing an end to the violence against Kosovar Albanians. It also marked a new phase in international relations, as NATO acted without direct authorization from the United Nations, raising important questions about the use of military force for humanitarian purposes ($\checkmark$).

Another example was the deployment of the United Nations Kosovo Force (KFOR), along with the establishment of the UN administration known as UNMIK ($\checkmark$). These measures helped restore order and prevent further clashes between ethnic groups in the aftermath of the war. They also played a key role in managing Kosovo's transition toward self-government ($\checkmark$).
