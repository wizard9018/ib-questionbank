# HI1480

Evaluate the impact of McCarthyism on the domestic and foreign policies of the United States.
[15 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

**General Guidance:**

The command term evaluate requires candidates to assess the extent to which McCarthyism influenced domestic and foreign policy in the United States. Responses should move beyond description and reach a judgment about McCarthyism's significance relative to other forces shaping early Cold War policy. Candidates should distinguish the domestic dimension by analyzing how McCarthyism affected political institutions, civil liberties, and public debate. This includes assessing the influence of investigations, loyalty programs, surveillance, and blacklisting, and determining how far these mechanisms reshaped governance and limited dissent. Candidates should assess the extent to which McCarthyism shaped Cold War the political context within which Cold War decisions were made. This involves examining how domestic accusations of weakness influenced containment, diplomacy and military commitments, and weighing these influences against broader drivers such as geopolitical developments, presidential leadership and long-term strategic priorities. Strong responses will reach a balanced judgment on the overall impact of McCarthyism. They should consider variation across institutions and contexts, recognize where its influence was decisive and where it was limited, and assess its significance relative to the structural Cold War dynamics that also shaped United States policy.

**Possible suitable examples include:**

**Domestic Examples**
- **Hollywood and HUAC**
    - Congressional investigations into alleged communist influence in Hollywood revealed how McCarthyism operated through public hearings, accusations, and political pressure.
    - The Hollywood Ten case demonstrated the limits of civil liberties when refusal to testify led to imprisonment and professional punishment.
    - Blacklisting reshaped media production, narrowing ideological diversity and discouraging criticism of government policy.
    - Studios and writers adapted content to avoid suspicion, revealing how fear reshaped cultural expression and public debate.
    - These mechanisms revealed how domestic institutions struggled to balance national security concerns with constitutional protections.
    - $\underline{\textrm{Counterargument:}}$ Some historians argue that Hollywood’s response reflected preexisting industry conservatism, meaning that McCarthyism intensified, but did not create, ideological control.

- **Loyalty Review Program**
    - The federal Loyalty Review Program required millions of government employees to undergo loyalty screenings.
    - Investigations relied on vague criteria, anonymous accusations, and broad definitions of subversive conduct.
    - The program expanded surveillance, encouraged self-censorship, and contributed to dismissals that eroded civil liberties.
    - It embedded suspicion in bureaucratic procedures, narrowing acceptable political participation.
    - It demonstrated how McCarthyism reshaped governance by redefining the boundaries of legitimate dissent.
    - $\underline{\textrm{Counterargument:}}$ Some scholars contend that the program reflected broader Cold War anxieties rather than McCarthyism alone.

- **Army–McCarthy Hearings**
    - The hearings revealed that McCarthyism destabilized federal institutions through nationally televised confrontations.
    - Public exposure of McCarthy’s tactics weakened his credibility and revealed the limits of fear-based politics.
    - They illustrated how institutional resistance could curb McCarthyist pressure.
    - They highlighted tensions between national security concerns and political opportunism.
    - $\underline{\textrm{Counterargument:}}$ Some historians argue that the hearings reflected personal rivalry rather than structural resistance.

**Foreign Policy Examples**

- **State Department Purges**
    - Accusations led to dismissals and resignations, reducing diplomatic expertise during key Cold War crises.
    - The purges narrowed policy debate and discouraged nuanced analysis of Asia and Eastern Europe.
    - They reinforced rigid containment thinking and eroded institutional capacity.
    - They showed how domestic political pressure could distort the formulation of foreign policy.
    - $\underline{\textrm{Counterargument:}}$ Some scholars argue that broader Cold War priorities, not McCarthyism alone, drove personnel changes.

- **“Loss of China” and escalation in Asia**
    - The controversy over China's loss showed how domestic accusations of weakness shaped foreign policy decisions.
    - Policymakers feared being blamed for another strategic failure, making anti-communist credibility paramount.
    - This pressure shaped decisions during the Korean War, reinforcing military containment and limiting diplomatic flexibility.
    - Domestic vulnerability pushed officials toward more rigid interpretations of global communist expansion.
    - Congressional criticism and media rhetoric amplified the belief that assertive action abroad was necessary to prevent political damage.
    - $\underline{\textrm{Counterargument:}}$ Some scholars argue that escalation reflected broader containment priorities rather than McCarthyite pressure.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

The extent to which McCarthyism influenced United States domestic and foreign policy has been widely debated by historians. This essay examines two key examples, Hollywood and the “loss of China,” to assess how far McCarthyite pressures shaped political institutions, civil liberties, public debate, and Cold War decision-making. ($\checkmark$ focus)While both examples reveal significant effects, they also show that McCarthyism interacted with broader structural forces, including containment strategy, geopolitical developments, and presidential leadership. A balanced assessment requires analyzing the specific mechanisms through which McCarthyism operated and weighing their significance against other drivers of early Cold War policy. ($\checkmark$ organization)
McCarthyism profoundly reshaped domestic political culture, and Hollywood became one of its most visible arenas. Congressional investigations into alleged communist influence in the film industry demonstrated how McCarthyism operated through public hearings, accusations, and political pressure. ($\checkmark$ examples) The House Un-American Activities Committee (HUAC) used highly public congressional hearings to expose supposed subversive networks, creating a climate in which refusing to cooperate was equated with disloyalty. The Hollywood Ten case revealed the limits of civil liberties when individuals who declined to testify were imprisoned for contempt of Congress and subsequently blacklisted. This episode illustrated how McCarthyism redefined acceptable dissent by linking silence or resistance to accusations of communist sympathy. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

Hollywood offers clear examples of how McCarthyism reshaped cultural production. Studios and writers adapted content to avoid suspicion, narrowing ideological diversity and discouraging criticism of government policies. ($\checkmark$ examples) Blacklisting kept hundreds of actors, screenwriters and directors from working, demonstrating how political fear could restrict professional opportunities and reshape an entire industry. The pressure extended beyond Hollywood into universities, unions and federal agencies, revealing how domestic institutions struggled to balance national security concerns with constitutional protections. These mechanisms show that McCarthyism did not merely punish alleged communists but also transformed the boundaries of public debate by embedding suspicion in everyday professional life. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

Historians disagree about how fully Hollywood’s experience reflects McCarthyism’s broader domestic impact. Some, such as Ellen Schrecker, argue that the film industry became a symbolic battleground where McCarthyism demonstrated its capacity to distort democratic norms and suppress dissent. Others contend that Hollywood’s response reflected preexisting conservatism within the industry, suggesting that McCarthyism intensified but did not create ideological control. ($\checkmark$ perspectives) This debate highlights the need to evaluate McCarthyism’s influence relative to structural and cultural tendencies that also shaped political expression in the early Cold War.

McCarthyism also influenced foreign policy, particularly through the political controversy surrounding the “loss of China.” After the Chinese Communist Party triumphed in 1949, accusations of domestic weakness became central to United States political discourse. Policymakers feared being blamed for another strategic failure, making anti-communist credibility a crucial factor in Cold War strategy. ($\checkmark$ knowledge) The belief that officials had been insufficiently vigilant in Asia created intense pressure on the Truman administration, reinforcing the idea that assertive action abroad was necessary to avoid political damage at home. This dynamic linked foreign policy decisions directly to domestic vulnerability, demonstrating how McCarthyism shaped perceptions of global communist expansion. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

The “loss of China” controversy had significant consequences for United States policy in Asia. Congressional criticism and media rhetoric amplified the belief that diplomatic flexibility risked further strategic setbacks. ($\checkmark$ examples) During the Korean War, policymakers adopted more rigid interpretations of containment, supporting military intervention to demonstrate resolve and avoid accusations of appeasement. Domestic political fears limited the space for negotiation and encouraged escalation, revealing how McCarthyism could distort foreign policy formulation by prioritizing political credibility over strategic nuance. These pressures contributed to a broader pattern in which Cold War decisions were shaped not only by geopolitical conditions but also by internal accusations of disloyalty. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

Historians offer contrasting interpretations of the significance of the “loss of China” in shaping United States foreign policy. Some, such as John Lewis Gaddis, argue that domestic political pressures played a decisive role in pushing policymakers toward more aggressive containment strategies. Others contend that escalation in Asia reflected broader structural priorities of the Cold War, suggesting that McCarthyite rhetoric intensified but did not fundamentally redirect strategy. ($\checkmark$ perspectives) This debate underscores the importance of evaluating McCarthyism’s influence relative to long-term strategic concerns that also shaped United States policy.

Comparing Hollywood and the “loss of China” demonstrates that McCarthyism exercised fundamentally different forms of influence over domestic and foreign policy. In Hollywood, McCarthyism directly reshaped institutions, civil liberties and cultural production by embedding suspicion in professional practices and narrowing acceptable dissent. These effects were immediate, visible and deeply personal, affecting careers, artistic expression and public debate. In contrast, the “loss of China” controversy influenced foreign policy more indirectly, shaping perceptions of credibility and vulnerability rather than producing specific institutional mechanisms. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

The comparison also highlights variation in the significance of McCarthyism relative to other factors. Hollywood shows that McCarthyism could be a primary driver of institutional change, while foreign policy decisions in Asia reveal that it often interacted with broader geopolitical dynamics. Domestic accusations of weakness intensified containment but did not replace structural strategic priorities. This difference is essential for evaluating the extent to which McCarthyism shaped United States policy, showing that its influence was uneven across contexts. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

Overall, McCarthyism had a substantial impact on both domestic and foreign policy, though its significance varied. In Hollywood, it was a major force that redefined dissent, restricted civil liberties, and reshaped cultural production. In foreign policy, it influenced decision-making by amplifying fears of political vulnerability, but its effects were mediated by broader Cold War dynamics. ($\checkmark$ conclusion) Therefore, McCarthyism was highly influential in shaping domestic institutions and public debate, while its impact on foreign policy, though significant, was less decisive and operated alongside structural geopolitical factors.
