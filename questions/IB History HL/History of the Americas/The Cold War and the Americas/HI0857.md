# HI0857

According to Source A, describe three methods used by the CIA to undermine the Arbenz government in Guatemala.
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  A Memorandum created by the CIA in 1975 about the actions taken by the Agency in Guatemala in the 1950s. |

:box[In August 1953, the Operations Coordinating Board directed CIA to assume responsibility for operations against the Arbenz regime. Appropriate authorization was issued to permit close and prompt cooperation with the Departments of Defense, State and other Government agencies in order to support the Agency in this task. The plan of operations called for cutting off military aid to Guatemala, increasing aid to its neighbors, exerting diplomatic and economic pressure against Arbenz and attempts to subvert and or defect Army and political leaders, broad scale psychological warfare and paramilitary actions. During the period August through December 1953 a CIA staff was assembled and operational plans were prepared.\
\
Following are the specific operational mechanisms utilized by the Agency in the overall missions against the Arbenz government:\
\
$\hspace{2em}$a. Paramilitary Operations. Approximately 85 members of the Castillo Armas group received training in Nicaragua. Thirty were trained in sabotage, six as shock troop leaders and 20 others as support-type personnel. Eighty-nine tons of equipment were prepared. The support of this operation was staged inside the borders of Honduras and Nicaragua. [1-1/2 lines of source text not declassified] There were an estimated 250 men in Honduras and El Salvador for use as shock troops and specialists, outside of the training personnel that had been sent to Nicaragua.\
$\hspace{2em}$b. Air Operations. The planning for providing air operational support was broken down into three phases; i.e. the initial stockpiling of equipment; the delivering of equipment to advance bases by black flight; and the aerial resupply of troops in the field. Thirty days prior to D-day, a fourth phase, fighter support, was initiated. There were approximately 80 missions flown during the 14–29 June 1954 period, by various type aircraft such as C-47's, F-47's and Cessnas which were used to discharge cargo, distribute propaganda and for strafing and bombing missions.\
$\hspace{2em}$c. Clandestine Communications. A clandestine radio broadcasting station was established in Nicaragua. The purpose of these broadcasts was to intimidate members of the Communist Party and public officials [Page 449]who were sympathetic to the Communist cause. The radio station, prior to D-day, broadcasted programs on why they were on the air; dramatized examples of Communist tyranny; the ideologies and aims of the Liberation Movement and what effect was intended vis-à-vis each individual who was listening; an aggressive program outlining the activities which would ultimately bring down the Communist threat, etc.\
$\hspace{2em}$d. Q Program. The objective was spreading responsibility for the operation throughout as many Latin American countries as possible in order to lessen the impact of United States participation.\
$\hspace{2em}$e. Indigenous agent radio operator training program. This included 13 radio operators, including seven residents and six tactical, and one cryptographer who were trained in Nicaragua from 6 March-9 June 1954.]

[Source: Central Intelligence Agency (May 12, 1975) *Memorandum Prepared in the Central Intelligence Agency.* Office of the US Historian. Retrieved June 30, 2025 from https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54Guat/d287. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Paramilitary operations - The CIA trained approximately 85 members of the Castillo Armas group in Nicaragua in sabotage, shock leadership, and support roles. An additional 250 men were staged in Honduras and El Salvador to be used as shock troops and specialists.
- Air operations - The CIA flew about 80 missions between June 14–29, 1954, using aircraft like C-47s and F-47s. These were used for supply drops, propaganda distribution, and for conducting bombing and strafing missions.
- Psychological warfare (Clandestine communications) - A clandestine radio station was established in Nicaragua that broadcast propaganda to intimidate Guatemalan Communist Party members and sympathizers. Programming dramatized communist oppression and promoted the goals of the “Liberation Movement.”
- Q Program - This was intended to involve multiple Latin American countries in the operation to reduce the appearance of direct U.S. involvement.
- Indigenous agent training - Thirteen radio operators and one cryptographer were trained in Nicaragua between March and June 1954 to support CIA communications.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The CIA attempted to destabilize the Arbenz government by building up an armed opposition force. According to the source, dozens of Castillo Armas's supporters were trained in Nicaragua in specialized roles, including sabotage and leadership, while hundreds more were positioned in neighboring Honduras and El Salvador to serve as combat troops ($\checkmark$).

They also relied on air support to aid the mission. Aircraft were used over a two-week period in June 1954 to deliver supplies, spread propaganda leaflets, and carry out attacks such as bombings and strafing, as part of a coordinated military effort to pressure the Guatemalan regime ($\checkmark$).

In addition to physical operations, the CIA conducted psychological warfare through a secret radio station based in Nicaragua. This station broadcast anti-communist propaganda aimed at intimidating both officials and citizens loyal to Arbenz, portraying the Liberation Movement as a powerful force and communism as a direct threat to Guatemalan society ($\checkmark$).
