# HI0094

What does Source B suggest about the signing of the nuclear test treaty?
[2 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  A cartoon by Ed Valtman, a well-known editorial cartoonist from the mid-20th century, which depicts the tense negotiations between the USA and the USSR around nuclear arms control and the Nuclear Test Ban Treaty. |

![HI0094](images/HI0094.png)

[Source: Valtman, E. (1962, April 21). *He's driving me nuts - I'm on the verge of blowing my top*. Retrieved 1 October, 2024 from https://www.loc.gov/item/2015645656/. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- The cartoon suggests that the USSR is to blame for the delays in the signing of the nuclear test ban treaty by labelling the Soviet figure "Soviet Intransigence."
- The Soviet figure is depicted as sitting calmly with a smug expression, suggesting an unwillingness to compromise, while the U.S. figure, labelled "U.S. A-Tests," appears frustrated and visibly agitated with the Soviet figure and close to losing control.
- The clock on the table adds a sense of urgency, implying that the delays are critical, with the Soviet Union's stubborn stance being blamed for stalling the treaty negotiations.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
In Source B, the cartoonist suggests that the Soviet Union is responsible for the delays in signing the nuclear test ban treaty by depicting the Soviet figure, labelled "Soviet Intransigence," sitting calmly with a smug expression   indicating a refusal to compromise ($\checkmark$). On the other hand,  the American figure, labelled "U.S. A-Tests," is shown as frustrated and agitated and on the verge of losing control, suggesting that he blames the USSR for the lack of progress on the signing of the nuclear test treaty ($\checkmark$).
