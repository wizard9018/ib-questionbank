# HI0354

Identify and explain **two** ways atomic diplomacy contributed to the origins of the Cold War and the breakdown of the Grand Alliance by 1946.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation/evidence:

 - Created a shift in power dynamics

     - The use of the atomic bomb highlighted the U.S.'s military and technological superiority, shifting global power dynamics in its favor by 1945. 

 - Excluded of the Soviet Union from atomic knowledge

     - The U.S. intentionally excluded the Soviet Union from the Manhattan Project, even though they were allies during the war. This decision reflected deep-seated mistrust of Stalin's intentions and a desire to maintain a strategic advantage in the post-war period. 

 - Demonstrated US military superiority

     - By demonstrating the bomb's immense destructive power in Japan, the U.S. aimed to strengthen its position in future negotiations and deter Soviet aggression in Europe and Asia. 

 - Eroded Allied trust

     - The U.S. decision to reveal its nuclear capability at the Potsdam Conference heightened mistrust within the Grand Alliance. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Atomic diplomacy caused the breakdown of the Grand Alliance because it represented immense US military strength globally as well as relative to the USSR ($\checkmark$). The use of two nuclear weapons in Hiroshima and Nagasaki publicly demonstrated to the world both the destructive power the US wielded and its willingness to use it ($\checkmark$).

The US's decision to exclude the Soviet Union from atomic knowledge demonstrated its distrust despite the alliance between the two ($\checkmark$). The U.S. kept the Manhattan Project secret from Stalin, making him suspicious of American intentions. In response, Stalin tightened Soviet control in Eastern Europe, breaking agreements made at Yalta and worsening tensions ($\checkmark$).
