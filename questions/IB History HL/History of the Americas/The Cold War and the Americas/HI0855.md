# HI0855

Explain **two** consequences of Vice President Richard Nixon's 1958 visit to Latin America.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Nixon's hostile reception, especially the violent protests in Caracas, exposed widespread public anger at U.S. policies. These reactions stemmed from long-standing resentment over U.S. support for authoritarian regimes and economic control in the region.
- The attack on Nixon's motorcade in Venezuela became a global spectacle. It suggested the U.S. had lost influence and respect, damaging the image of American leadership during the Cold War.
- The violence forced U.S. officials to reassess their approach to the region. This led to increased attention on public diplomacy and economic development, paving the way for later initiatives like the Alliance for Progress.
- The U.S. mobilized military forces (Operation Poor Richard) in case of further threats. While the forces weren't deployed, it showed how Cold War-era Washington saw Latin unrest as a national security issue.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One consequence of Nixon's 1958 trip to Latin America was that it triggered a serious reevaluation of U.S. policy toward the region ($\checkmark$). After facing unexpected and intense hostility, American officials began to realize that military alliances and anti-communism alone were not enough—there was a growing recognition that economic inequality and public resentment needed to be addressed through more comprehensive engagement ($\checkmark$).

Another consequence was that the U.S. government prepared for a possible military intervention in Venezuela following the attack on Nixon's convoy ($\checkmark$). Although the troops were never deployed, the swift mobilization revealed how quickly Cold War tensions could escalate and how the U.S. viewed unrest in Latin America as a potential threat to hemispheric stability ($\checkmark$).
