# HI0352

Explain how the friction between Truman and Stalin represented the larger breakdown of the Grand Alliance by 1946.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting evidence/examples: 

 - Ideological differences: 

    - The U.S., under Truman, promoted democratic governance and capitalist economies, whereas Stalin sought to expand communism and establish a buffer zone of Soviet-controlled states in Eastern Europe. 

 - Leadership styles: 

    - Truman's direct and assertive approach clashed with Stalin's calculated and authoritarian methods. 

 - Disagreements over postwar arrangements: 

    - Disagreements over Germany and Eastern Europe widened the divide.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The breakdown of the Grand Alliance was largely due to ideological differences between Truman and Stalin. Truman believed in promoting democracy and free markets, while Stalin focused on spreading communism and securing a buffer zone of Soviet-dominated states ($\checkmark$). For example, in Poland, Stalin disregarded the Yalta agreement by installing a pro-Soviet communist government ($\checkmark$). Their leadership styles also contributed to rising tensions. Truman’s assertive approach, such as pushing for free elections in Eastern Europe, contrasted sharply with Stalin's authoritarian tactics intended to maintain the Soviet sphere of influence ($\checkmark$). An example of this occurred at the Potsdam Conference in 1945 where Truman revealed the U.S. had successfully tested the atomic bomb to show American strength.  However, this increased Stalin's distrust leading him to keep the Red Army in Eastern Europe to strengthen Soviet control, breaking promises of free elections and further damaging Allied trust ($\checkmark$).
