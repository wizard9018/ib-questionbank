# HI0995

Describe the emerging sentiments in the United States that gave rise to the Red Scare.

[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - After World War II, American society was worried and anxious about the post-war period.

 - Americans feared radical ideologies, such as communism.

 - Labor strikes, bombings, and the Russian Revolution generated distrust towards immigrants and those who professed other ideologies.

 - Fear of subversive activities created a paranoia in American society and government that gave rise to the Red Scare.

 - Anti-immigrant sentiments led to a growing fear and suspicion toward all foreigners.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One of the main aspects was the fear of radical ideologies such as communism, as most Americans associated them with strikes and social demonstrations, and they provoked fears of a possible revolution in society. ($\checkmark$) The Russian Revolution generated greater concern that these movements could also occur in the United States, while labor strikes and bombings further increased public anxiety. ($\checkmark$)

Widespread anti-immigrant sentiment was another key aspect, as immigrants were blamed for promoting dangerous ideas that could lead to social instability. Immigrants became prime suspects during the Red Scare, leading to calls for government anti-immigration measures and increased surveillance of foreign-born residents. ($\checkmark$)
