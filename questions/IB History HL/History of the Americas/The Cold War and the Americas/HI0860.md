# HI0860

What are **two** messages conveyed by Source C about Marshall Aid?
[2 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  A 1949 cartoon about Marshall Aid by Clive Uptton. |

![HI0860](images/HI0860.JPEG)
[Source: Uptton, C. (1949). *Marshall Aid.* The National Archives INF 3/1295. Retrieved on July 19, 2025 from https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/cold-war-on-file/marshall-aid-cartoon/. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Marshall Aid was intended to be temporary - The aid is described as something to “keep you going till 1952,” implying short-term support until Europe can recover independently.
- The aid is productive and supportive, not controlling - The man receiving the aid (likely representing a European country) is grateful and confident he can become self-sufficient, suggesting Marshall Aid was enabling rather than dominating.
- Marshall Aid provided essential materials to rebuild Europe - The labeled items ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$Food and Raw Material”) and the farming metaphor suggest the U.S. supplied basic inputs to help Europe $\text{\textquotedblleft}$grow" its economy again.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One message of the cartoon is that Marshall Aid was meant to support European recovery only for a limited time, not permanently. This is shown by the man saying it will $\text{\textquotedblleft}$keep you going till 1952" ($\checkmark$).

Another message is that the aid was empowering rather than controlling—the farmer says he'll be able to provide for himself soon, suggesting that the U.S. help was designed to make Europe self-sufficient again ($\checkmark$).
