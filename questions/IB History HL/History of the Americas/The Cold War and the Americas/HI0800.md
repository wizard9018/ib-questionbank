## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- This excerpt is from a public address at the new Washington Headquarters of the American Legion Public Papers, 1951 and shows an official governmental perspective on the response to the fear of increased communist infiltration in society
- The speech was given at a time when the fear of communism was growing in the US, providing insight into the context of Truman's response.
- The source shows Truman's perspective that the fear of Communism was undermining American values.
- The address was given to show that the main focus of Truman's government is to defend people's constitutional rights, providing some justification for Truman's response.

For limitations, any of the following:
- The source is a public speech at the Headquarters of the American Legion, so the content is focused on the President saying what this organization would expect to hear, rather than an objective message.
- The language used is very emotional. Truman talks about Americanism being $\lq$under attack', which is aimed at getting support for his campaigns.
- The response does not acknowledge real concerns about the fear of communist infiltration that existed in society, so it provides a one-sided view of the threat.
- Truman's response is also to protect his political position as he suggests that $\lq$These people claim to be against communism. But they are chipping away at our basic freedoms just as insidiously and far more effectively than the Communists have ever been able to do.'
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The origin of the source is valuable as it is a public address given by President Truman himself, thus providing direct insight into his perspective about the threat of Communism at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). Because the content of the speech is based on the freedoms described in the US Bill of Rights, it helps provide the logic and justification for Truman's response ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). However, the language used is very emotional, with Truman saying Americanism is $\lq$under attack' and that $\lq$slander mongers' are trying to instill in people a $\lq$fear of being called a communist' ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$). Truman is being pressured both by anti-communist critics and civil liberties advocates, so the source seeks to defend his political position more than address his objective views about the threat of Communism ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).
