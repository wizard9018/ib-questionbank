# HI0220

With reference to origin, purpose, and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying the perception of the Soviet threat during the Cuban Missile Crisis.
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

Values:
- A speech by Kennedy reflects the official U.S. political stance and its intent to shape public opinion.
- Aims to justify the U.S. response to the crisis, helping to understand the rationale behind their actions.
- Explains the narrative of the Soviet threat, focusing on hemispheric security and the balance of power.

Limitations
- Only presents the U.S. perspective, excluding the Soviet viewpoint and providing a one-sided view of the crisis.
- Designed to mobilize internal and external support, it does not provide an objective evaluation of the facts.
- Its rhetorical language can make it difficult to identify precise factual information.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The origin of the source, being a speech by Kennedy, reflects the official U.S. political stance and provides insight into how the U.S. government publicly articulated the Soviet threat during the crisis ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The purpose of the speech is to justify the U.S. response, offering valuable context about the rationale behind actions taken, such as the naval blockade ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The content of the source emphasizes the narrative of the Soviet threat, focusing on hemispheric security and the balance of power, shedding light on how the U.S. framed the crisis to its domestic and international audiences ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

The purpose of the speech is to mobilize internal and external support for U.S. policies, which limits its objectivity and excludes a balanced perspective of the Soviet viewpoint ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). The content employs rhetorical language, potentially exaggerating the threat and making it difficult to extract precise factual information ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$). As an official speech, it lacks the depth of classified or internal communications, focusing only on the public narrative and possibly omitting strategic or controversial details ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).
