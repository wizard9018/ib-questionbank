# HI0870

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War.
[4 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from President Johnson's Message to Congress August 5, 1964 following the Gulf of Tonkin incident. |

[Source: Johnson, L. B. (1964, August 5). *The Tonkin Gulf Incident,* 1964. Department of State Bulletin, August 24, 1964. Retrieved on June 29, 2025 from  https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/tonkin-g.asp. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- The origin of the source is valuable as it comes from an address by the President of the United States during the time that the United States became officially involved in the Vietnam War.
- The purpose of the source is valuable as it is meant to convince the representatives of the U.S. government that their joining the war effort was the necessary and legal thing to do.
- The content of the source is valuable as it explains President Nixon's justification for becoming more involved in the Vietnam War.

For limitations, any of the following
- The purpose of the source is limited as it is a public speech being made in order to convince the U.S. government and people that they are justified in becoming more involved in war without considering alternate reasons for the attack on the U.S. ships.
- The content of the source is limited as it is solely based on why the United States should become more involved in the war and doesn't give any context on their history in the region up to this point.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The origin of the source is valuable as it is a speech to Congress by the President of the United States, who clearly gives his reasons for becoming more involved in the Vietnam War ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). However, the purpose may be considered limited as President Johnson is clearly trying to sway members of Congress to his way of thinking and may therefore be leaving out other bits of information ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). Still, the content of the source is valuable as it explains President Johnson's justification for further military action with great detail ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). The content is also limited, however, as it only focuses on why the US should become involved and gives no detail or context about prior involvement ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
