# HI0867

What does Source A suggest about the nature of the war in Vietnam?
[2 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | “Stick ‘em up!” is a cartoon by Edmund Valtman published in June 1964. In it, a caricature of President Lyndon B. Johnson is portrayed as a cowboy with a caricature of an unnamed Vietnamese soldier. |

![HI0867](images/HI0867.jpg)
[Source: Valtman, E. (1964). *Stick $\lq$Em Up!* Library of Congress. Retrieved on June 29, 2025 from www.loc.gov/exhibits/pointing-their-pens-editorial-cartoons/vietnam-war.html#obj018. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- President Johnson is portrayed as a cowboy and larger than the Vietnamese soldier pictured, indicating his power.
- President Johnson is claiming to be very powerful and clinging to hydrogen bombs, indicating that the United States has the power to win the war.
- The concerned look on Johnson's face indicates that even though they have nuclear weapons, they are not winning the war.
- The laughing Vietnamese soldier is labeled as “guerrilla warfare” and pointing a conventional weapon at the surprised Johnson, indicating that the United States was not prepared to fight the Vietnamese in this way.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source A shows a Vietnamese soldier holding President Johnson at gunpoint using conventional weapons and guerrilla warfare. The happy look on his face indicates that the Vietnamese have taken President Johnson by surprise with their fighting methods ($\checkmark$). It also indicates that though the United States has many nuclear weapons and could be considered the more powerful military, they are holding them rather than using them, and that is not helping them fight the Vietnamese ($\checkmark$).
