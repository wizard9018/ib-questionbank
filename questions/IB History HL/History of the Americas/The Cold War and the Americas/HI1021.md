# HI1021

To what extent did the US achieve its aims in the Korean War?
[15 marks]

**Three** aims can be analyzed to answer this question.
- Containment: to what extent did the Korean War stop the spread of Communism?
- Domestic Political Aims: To what extent did the Korean War improve Truman's domestic approval?
    - By 1950 his administration was accused of having “lost China” to the Chinese Communist Party and Senator McCarthy had accused the State Department of being infiltrated by Communists. Truman needed to clear his reputation in light of the upcoming elections.
- Help the United Nations in its peacekeeping role: To what extent did the US help or hurt the UN's mission in Korea?
    - The United Nations was created in 1945, and the Truman administration wanted to avoid the fate of its predecessor, the League of Nations. To do that, it had to prove that the principle of collective security would work this time.

Considerations:
- A “to what extent” question requires an analysis of the degree of, in this case, achievement.
- A clear answer must first identify the aims. The categories provided are general and can be categorized into more specific aims. A good answer does not need to include all the aims.

**Indicative content (not prescriptive):**
Containment
- Success:
    - South Korea was “saved” from Communism
    - Canadian Historian David Bercuson explains that Korea was the first effort by a communist state to take over a non-communist neighbor. The fact that this was prevented makes it the first victory of the West in the Cold War.
    - Japan remained safe and a staunch US ally in the region.
    - U.S. intervention encouraged the creation of new alliances such as the Southeast Asian Treaty Organization (1954) to contain communism in Asia.
- Failure:
    - A peace treaty was never signed. Therefore, the US had to protect the fledgling Republic of South Korea by stationing thousands of troops along the 38th parallel.
    - After the success at Inchon in 1950, the aim was no longer to defend South Korea but to unite the peninsula; this was not achieved.
    - US historian John Gaddis sees the war as a precedent for the Vietnam War: the superpowers would get involved in bloody proxy wars.
    - The war strained relations with the USSR and with China even further

Domestic Political Aims
- Success:
    - Early polls showed ¾ of Americans supported assistance to South Korea
    - Truman was able to show a tough stance on Communism after being accused of having “lost China” (McCarthyism was in full swing)
- Failure:
    - In the congressional elections of 1950, the Democrats, Truman`s party, lost their majority in Congress.
    - Since Truman had not obtained a declaration of war from Congress, he was the only one to blame for the war
    - The war cost the US $67 billion, which led to inflation. In January 1951, the government introduced a price freeze to combat it.
    - The war's unpopularity made Truman realize he could not be a candidate in the 1952 election, which a Republican won for the first time after 20 years.

Help the United Nations in its peacekeeping role
- Success:
    - The UN asked Truman to appoint a commander for the United Nations Command. The US contributed 9/10 of the US troops to the fight.
    - It was the first time one country invaded another, and the UN was able to avoid repeating the mistake of appeasement and implement collective security. 15 nations committed troops to the struggle.
- Failure:
    - General MacArthur was named commander of the United Nations Command. Truman insisted he communicate only with Washington and not with the UN authorities, which undermined the UN's authority.
    - Many UN members felt that crossing the 38th parallel after Inchon was an act of aggression against North Korea and a violation of the UN's peacekeeping mission.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Korean War (1950-1953) was the first proxy war of the Cold War. Initially, it was a purely Korean confrontation between the North and the South, with both seeking to reunify the peninsula under their respective governments. North Korean aggression in 1950 motivated the participation of the UN to defend the Republic of Korea. Even though the war was officially between the two Koreas and the United Nations, with the addition of China at the end of 1950, the United States played a significant role ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). President Truman decided to assist the UN in taking a stand in favor of collective security and to present a strong position against the spread of communism ($\checkmark\, \text{organisation}$). The US was also bound to the words of the 1947 Truman Doctrine, in which it pledged to "support free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation by armed minorities or by outside pressures" ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). The situation in Korea was a clear test of the US's containment pòlicy as established in this Doctrine ($\checkmark\, \text{organisation}$). This essay will analyze the extent to which these two aims were achieved ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$).

US participation in the Korean War strengthened the fledgling United Nations' ability to react while at the same time undermining it ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). Created after the failure of the League of Nations in part due to its inaction in the face of aggression, the invasion of South Korea was the first test of the UN's peacekeeping capabilities. Truman was determined to uphold the structure of collective security, and for that he knew the US had to participate and not repeat the policies of isolationism and appeasement that had partly led to the Second World War ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). Thus, when the UN asked Truman to appoint a commander for the United Nations Command, General MacArthur was named to lead the UN forces. The US also contributed 9 out of every 10 troops to the UN forces and considerable resources. These contributions made the war both possible and partly successful. However, the preponderance of US participation and the limited role of the other 15 nations that contributed troops to the UN forces also undermined the authority of the United Nations. Furthermore, Truman ordered MacArthur to communicate only with Washington and not with the UN authorities, further undermining the concept of a joint effort ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This situation led to the UN effort being perceived, instead, as a US effort, with the US determining the goals of the struggle. While to most UN members it was clearly necessary to defend the invaded South Korea, the US incursion into North Korea and the advance to the Chinese border were explicit acts of aggression that were contrary to the spirit of the United Nations. Therefore, even though US resources and men allowed the success of the UN mission, their domination ended up sidelining the United Nations, thus eroding its authority ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

On the other hand, the Truman administration achieved its objective of containing communism by participating in the Korean War ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). First of all, it prevented the spread of communism to yet another country in Asia by pushing back the North Korean invasion and securing the Republic of Korea as an American ally to this day. This can also be said for Japan, which would have had its development seriously thwarted if both the whole Korean peninsula and China were communist ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). As Canadian historian Bercuson states, “saving” South Korea from communism was the first true Western victory in the Cold War, since the expansion of a communist state over a non-communist neighbor had been successfully blocked. This showed communist leaders in the USSR and China that the US would not allow communist aggression to go unchecked. However, in this assessment, Bercuson ignores the importance of the Berlin Airlift of 1948-1949. In this instance, the Western allies also successfully resisted what can be interpreted as a Soviet incursion into a capitalist country as the USSR was forced to end its blockade of West Berlin ($\checkmark\, \text{perspectives}$). Moreover, the success was limited. A formal peace treaty was never signed, forcing the US to maintain tens of thousands of troops along the 38th parallel since South Korea was and is still technically at war with the North ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). Furthermore, after the successful Inchon landing in 1950, the US aims expanded from simply defending South Korea to reunifying the peninsula under non-Communist rule, which would have meant a successful offensive move against the spread of communism ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$).  This goal had to be abandoned after China entered the conflict. Moreover, John Gaddis observes that the war set a dangerous precedent for future conflicts ($\checkmark\, \text{perspectives}$). After Korea, the US would become entangled in bloody, inconclusive proxy wars instead of thinking of diplomatic solutions ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Consequently, the US did achieve its immediate containment objectives in the Korean War, but at the same time, it came to see the use of force as a valid way to stop communism.

In conclusion, the United States mostly achieved the aims it set for itself in the Korean War ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$).  Containment was upheld in South Korea, and the UN demonstrated its ability to respond to aggression. However, these achievements were limited by the failure to reunify Korea, the burden of an indefinite US military commitment in the region, and the dangerous precedent of proxy wars ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Ultimately, the Korean War would determine the aims the US set for itself in future conflicts ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
