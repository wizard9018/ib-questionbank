# HI0856

Explain **three** reasons why some countries in the Americas chose not to participate in the Korean War.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with supporting explanation:
- Desire to maintain neutrality: Mexico opted to avoid Cold War entanglements, maintaining a policy of non-intervention and neutrality in global conflicts.
- Domestic political instability or transition: Argentina, under Juan Perón, focused on internal political consolidation and economic nationalism rather than foreign military commitments.
- Limited military or financial capacity: Paraguay and Honduras lacked the logistical means and resources to contribute troops to a distant conflict.
- Skepticism of U.S. foreign policy: Uruguay and others were cautious about appearing to serve U.S. interests without clear national benefit, especially in a distant war.
- Public or political opposition: In Chile, there was no broad political consensus or public will to justify military intervention in East Asia.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Several countries in the Americas chose not to take part in the Korean War due to concerns about sovereignty and avoiding alignment in global power struggles ($\checkmark$). For instance, Mexico avoided involvement because of its traditional foreign policy principle of non-intervention, which aimed to keep the country out of external military conflicts after a long history of foreign interference ($\checkmark$).

In Argentina, President Juan Perón prioritized national development and a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$third position" foreign policy that rejected both U.S. and Soviet influence ($\checkmark$). As such, military participation in a distant Asian war would have contradicted his nationalist agenda and risked internal political backlash ($\checkmark$).

Meanwhile, many smaller countries in Central America, such as El Salvador and Nicaragua, simply lacked the military infrastructure and financial resources to deploy troops abroad ($\checkmark$). For them, the war in Korea had little strategic value and was seen as a distant conflict better left to the major powers ($\checkmark$).
