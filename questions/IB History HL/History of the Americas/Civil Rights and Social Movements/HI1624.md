# HI1624

Compare and contrast the roles and significance of Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X in the U.S. Civil Rights Movement.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence**.

For **comparison**, any **two or more** of the following **similarities**:
- **Political ideas:**
    - Both wanted to improve the lives and rights of Black Americans. Both had large numbers of followers and were influential leaders.
- **Influence:**
    - Both were powerful and inspiring speakers. Both believed in the importance of organisations, preaching, and publications to spread their ideas.
- **Later Developments and Legacy:**
    - Both were involved in influential civil rights organisations. Their views changed over time, making them more similar in their later years. Both were controversial figures and were assassinated.

For **contrast**, any **two or more** of the following **differences**:
- **Main Political Goal:**
    - King sought integration of Black and White Americans, while Malcolm X initially supported separatism and Black self-determination.
- **Political Focus:**
    - King focused mainly on ending segregation in the South, while Malcolm X focused on problems in urban ghettos in the North.
- **Political Views:**
    - King increasingly supported economic equality and social justice in his later years, while Malcolm X emphasised Black nationalism and later moved toward a broader human rights approach.
- **Methodology and Approach to Violence:**
    - King promoted non-violent protest, while Malcolm X accepted self-defence and justified violence when necessary for protection.
- **Attitude Towards White Americans:**
    - King worked towards cooperation and integration with White Americans, while Malcolm X was initially more critical and distrustful of White Americans, though he became less antagonistic later in life.
- **Achievements and Legacy:**
    - King is generally seen as achieving more concrete civil rights successes, including helping secure the Voting Rights Act of 1965, while Malcolm X had a major impact on Black pride and Black consciousness but achieved fewer legislative changes.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X had several similarities. Both aimed to improve the lives and rights of Black Americans and became influential leaders with large followings ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Both were powerful speakers who used important civil rights organisations to reach out to people to spread their ideas. Martin Luther King Jr. utilised and collaborated with the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC), which he helped found, and the National Association for the Advancement of Coloured People (NAACP). Malcolm X was associated with the Nation of Islam (NOI) and the Organization of Afro-American Unity (OAAU) ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Their views evolved over time, making them more similar in their later years ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Both men were controversial figures and were eventually assassinated ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).

However, there were also significant differences between them. King believed in integration and sought equality between Black and White Americans, whereas Malcolm X initially supported separatism and Black self-determination ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). King focused mainly on ending segregation in the South, while Malcolm X concentrated on the problems faced by Black people in northern ghettos ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). King promoted non-violent protest, but Malcolm X accepted self-defence and the use of violence when necessary ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Furthermore, King encouraged cooperation with White Americans, whereas Malcolm X was initially more critical and distrustful of them ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Finally, King achieved major legislative successes, including helping secure the Voting Rights Act of 1965, while Malcolm X's greatest impact was promoting Black pride and Black consciousness ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
