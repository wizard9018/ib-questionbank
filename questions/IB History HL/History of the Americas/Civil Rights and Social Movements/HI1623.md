# HI1623

Define the term Black Power and provide **two** examples.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **one** of the following to define “Black Power” with **two** supporting examples:
- **Black supremacy**
    - Some supporters believed Black Power meant reversing historical racial domination, with Black people ruling over White people.
    - This was a minority and controversial interpretation.
- **Black working-class revolution**
    - Black workers in Detroit's automobile industry saw Black Power as a movement for economic justice and workers' rights.
    - The League of Revolutionary Black Workers organised Black workers and pushed for greater representation and better work conditions.
- **Black pride and dignity**
    - Although initially critical of the slogan, Martin Luther King Jr. later argued that Black Power could represent racial pride, self-respect, and empowerment.
    - He emphasised the need for Black Americans to gain confidence, dignity, and a sense of belonging.
- **Economic empowerment**
    - Nathan Wright and later Richard Nixon interpreted Black Power as increasing Black ownership of businesses and economic resources.
    - They believed economic strength would lead to greater opportunities and influence.
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Black Power was a movement that encouraged African Americans to gain political, economic, and social power, while promoting racial pride, self-respect, and independence ($\checkmark$). Rather than having a single meaning, it was understood in different ways by different groups. One example is that Black workers in Detroit's automobile industry viewed Black Power as a struggle for economic justice and workers' rights, leading them to form the League of Revolutionary Black Workers to fight workplace discrimination and improve conditions ($\checkmark$). Martin Luther King Jr. later interpreted Black Power as a way of developing Black pride, dignity, and a sense of belonging among African Americans. Thus, Black Power was both a call for racial empowerment and a demand for greater equality and opportunity ($\checkmark$).
