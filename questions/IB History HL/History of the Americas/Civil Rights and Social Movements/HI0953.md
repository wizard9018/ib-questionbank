# HI0953

Define redlining and explain **three** ways it was used to legitimise racial discrimination between 1954 and 1965.
[1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
For define:
- Redlining was a form of racial discrimination in which banks and other financial institutions refused to offer services such as home loans, mortgages, or insurance to people living in certain areas, primarily because those neighbourhoods were inhabited by racial or ethnic minorities.
\
[1 marks]

For explanation, any **three** of the following:
- Institutionalised housing segregation: Redlining provided a bureaucratic and financial justification for keeping African Americans confined to segregated, under-resourced neighbourhoods. Banks and federal programs refused to offer loans to Black families in redlined zones, while making loans easily available to white families in “green” or “desirable” areas.
- Blocked access to suburbanisation: As white Americans moved to newly developed, federally subsidised suburbs, African Americans were excluded due to redlining and racially restrictive covenants. This deepened economic and social divides by denying Black families access to the same housing opportunities, wealth accumulation, and education systems.
- Reinforced cycles of poverty: By cutting off investment in Black neighbourhoods, redlining led to urban decay, lower property values, and a lack of access to public services like quality schools, healthcare, and infrastructure. African Americans were effectively trapped in impoverished communities, unable to accumulate home equity or escape economic hardship.
- Racial discrimination under the guise of “risk management”: Redlining framed racism as objective economic policy, legitimising racial segregation as a matter of sound business rather than overt prejudice. This provided a veneer of legality and professionalism to racial discrimination, making it harder to challenge.
- Undermined desegregation efforts: Even after Brown v. Board of Education (1954) began to challenge school segregation, redlining reinforced residential segregation, which in turn perpetuated de facto school segregation. This limited the impact of legal victories by keeping communities and their schools racially divided.
- Contributed to long-term wealth disparities: Homeownership is a major avenue for building wealth in the U.S.; by denying loans, redlining systematically excluded African Americans from wealth-building. These disparities have had long-lasting effects that persisted well beyond the official end of Jim Crow laws.
\
[3 marks]
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Redlining was a discriminatory practice in which banks and federal housing agencies marked certain neighbourhoods, usually those with large African American populations, as “high-risk” areas on residential maps. While it appeared to be an economic assessment, redlining was in fact based on racial composition rather than financial risk, and it served to reinforce segregation and inequality ($\checkmark$).

Redlining legitimised racial discrimination by institutionalising residential segregation, preventing African American families from moving into white neighbourhoods or accessing loans to buy homes outside of redlined zones ($\checkmark$). Second, it restricted economic mobility by cutting off opportunities for Black Americans to build wealth through homeownership, an option that remained available to most white families during the postwar suburban expansion ($\checkmark$). Third, redlining helped justify unequal access to public services, including education, by ensuring that Black communities remained underfunded and isolated. These policies framed racial exclusion as a matter of financial prudence, masking systemic racism behind the language of economic risk ($\checkmark$).
