# HI0392

Evaluate the impact of the NAACP on the civil rights movement of the 1950s

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Since the command term is “evaluate”, the response must contain both positive and negative results to reach more than four marks. To reach full marks, there must be a conclusion. 

Positive results: 

 - It was responsible for taking legal steps to overturn laws that promoted racial segregation. For example, Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka (1954) seemed to remove all constitutional sanctions for racial segregation by overturning Plessy v. Ferguson.

 - It helped other groups even if it did not agree with their methods. For example, it posted bail for hundreds of Freedom Riders and gave legal and financial aid to students arrested in sit-ins. 

 - It was the initial driving force behind the Montgomery bus boycott and Little Rock Nine in 1957.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP) had an important role in the civil rights movement during the 1950s. Its main tactic was to fight against segregation in the courts, and this proved successful in landmark cases like Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka (1954). With its ruling, the Supreme Court decision overturned Plessy v. Ferguson (1896), thus removing constitutional support for segregation in education. This legal triumph was a turning point in the fight for civil rights, setting a precedent that segregation was inherently unequal ($\checkmark$). Moreover, although it was not its main focus, the NAACP participated in other types of actions during the 1950s that contributed to the civil rights movement. For example, the NAACP initiated events like the Montgomery Bus Boycott of 1956 and the integration of Little Rock Central High School in 1957 ($\checkmark$).

However, the impact of the NAACP on the civil rights movement during the 1950s was also limited. The slow pace of change through the court system frustrated many, leading to disagreements over tactics. Leaders like Martin Luther King Jr. called for direct action and mass mobilization, resulting in the formation of organizations such as the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC) ($\checkmark$). Additionally, a legal victory did not always mean things improved for people in real life. For example, while the Brown decision called for the desegregation of schools, its implementation varied widely. In some urban areas, schools complied relatively quickly, while some districts in the Deep South remained segregated for many more years ($\checkmark$). Moreover, local authorities often restricted the NAACP's ability to act. For example, after its involvement in organizing the Montgomery Bus Boycott, Alabama prohibited the organization from operating within the state ($\checkmark$). 

The NAACP's contributions to the civil rights movement in the 1950s were significant, as its litigation tactic successfully attacked institutional racism and inspired broader activism. However, its achievements were limited by the slow pace of change, internal divisions within the movement, and resistance in regions like the Deep South ($\checkmark$conclusion ).
