# HI0958

Analyse **two** ways the Freedom Rides (1961) were significant in challenging segregation in the American South.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** with analysis.
- Tested federal enforcement of desegregation rulings: Exposed the federal government's failure to uphold the law in Southern states and pressured the Kennedy administration to intervene and uphold civil rights rulings.
- Exposed violent resistance in the South: Media coverage of attacks (e.g., firebombing in Anniston, beatings in Birmingham) shocked national and international audiences.
- Increased federal involvement: Resulted in the Interstate Commerce Commission (ICC) issuing regulations (Nov 1961) banning segregation in interstate transport facilities.
- Galvanised the Civil Rights Movement: Inspired greater activism and cooperation among groups (CORE, SNCC, SCLC) and proved young activists could take the lead in high-risk direct action.
- Highlighted the courage of civil rights activists: Demonstrated the power of nonviolent protest even in the face of extreme danger, and raised the profile of student activism and nonviolent resistance.
- Deepened North-South divide on civil rights: Caused tension between Southern local authorities and the federal government and forced national attention on the persistence of de facto segregation.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Freedom Rides were significant because they directly challenged the federal government to enforce its desegregation rulings. Although the Supreme Court had ruled that segregation in interstate travel was unconstitutional, Southern states ignored these decisions ($\checkmark$). When Freedom Riders were violently attacked in places like Anniston and Birmingham, it exposed the federal government's reluctance to act. This public pressure eventually led the Interstate Commerce Commission to issue regulations banning segregation in interstate bus terminals, making the Freedom Rides a turning point in federal enforcement of civil rights law ($\checkmark$).

The Freedom Rides were also significant for the way they galvanised the Civil Rights Movement ($\checkmark$). The bravery of the young Black and white activists, many of whom were students, inspired a new wave of grassroots protest. Groups like the SNCC gained momentum, and the rides demonstrated that strategic, nonviolent action, even in the face of violent opposition, could bring national attention to the issue of injustice. This helped expand the movement beyond courtrooms and into direct action campaigns across the South ($\checkmark$).
