# HI0804Q6

Evaluate the impact of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Lima Declaration” reached at the 8th Inter-American Conference (Lima, 1938) on the United States' policies in the Americas. 

[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Since the command term is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate”, the response must contain both positive and negative results to reach more than **four** marks. To reach full marks, there must be a conclusion. To respond to an $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate” command term, it is valid to say that the whole goal was not reached as something negative. 

$\\$

Positive results: The U.S. obtained an agreement for cooperation in case the troubles in Europe spread to the Americas. 

 - The nations reiterated their compromise with continental solidarity, promising to help each other in case of need 

 - In case the peace of the continent was endangered, they agreed to coordinate a response

 - The declaration was signed by all Latin American countries

$\\$

Negative results: The U.S. did not obtain the unity of action it desired. 

 - The different republics would act independently as sovereign states

 - The U.S. had to give up the idea of creating an American League of Nations.  

 - To get this Declaration approved by all 21 nations of the Pan American Union, the U.S. had to agree to another declaration condemning intervention in the domestic or foreign affairs of other states. 

 \

[4 marks]

Concluding remark:

 - Responses are likely to conclude that the impact of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Lima Declaration” was positive for the U.S..  

 - The strongest responses will mention the overall positive results while acknowledging that it did not have the full desired results.

[1 marks]

[5 marks]

$\\$

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The impact of the “Lima Declaration” on the United States' continental policy was positive in that it obtained an agreement from all Latin American countries to cooperate if conflicts in Europe threatened the security of the continent ($\checkmark$). To achieve cooperation, the nations agreed to have a coordinated response ($\checkmark$). All 21 nations of the Pan American Union signed this declaration, showing the unanimous support for continental solidarity that the Good Neighbor Policy aimed for ($\checkmark$). 

However, the U.S. did not achieve all of its objectives. First of all, it failed to secure a commitment to unified action. The declaration stated that each republic retained its right to act independently as a sovereign state  ($\checkmark$). Along with this, the U.S. had to abandon its proposal for a permanent inter-American body, or an American League of Nations ($\checkmark$). To get all the nations to sign this declaration, the U.S. also had to accept a separate declaration that condemned intervention in the domestic or foreign affairs of other states, limiting its ability to influence regional affairs  ($\checkmark$). 

In conclusion, while the Lima Declaration strengthened the principle of hemispheric cooperation and was a diplomatic win for the U.S., it fell short of achieving the full level of unity that U.S. policymakers had initially hoped for ($\checkmark\text{ conclusion}$).
