# HI0804Q8

Explain how the U.S. responded to the challenges faced by the Good Neighbor Policy up to 1945.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any combination of at least **two** of the below with supporting details, examples and/or explanation:

 - Expropriation of U.S. oil companies in Bolivia in 1937 and Mexico in 1938: To put pressure on Bolivia, it was denied loans from the Export-Import Bank, and negotiations lasted over three years. In 1940, with Europe and Asia at war, the U.S.A. was much more willing to concede, and an agreement was reached in which Standard Oil received $\text\textdollar$1.5 million for the sale of the company to Bolivia.

 - There was a lack of cultural understanding between the U.S. and its Southern neighbors: Roosevelt created the Cultural Relations Division of the State Department, with Latin America as a special focus.. It encouraged educational and technical exchanges. Moreover,  passenger lines to South America were developed that sailed from New York to Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay to establish cultural understanding. The U.S. media was encouraged to showcase Latin American culture.

 - Resistance to join the U.S. war effort after 1941. The U.S. pressured the governments of Argentina and Bolivia into compliance. While it did not militarily intervene in these countries' domestic affairs, it applied considerable diplomatic and economic pressure and supported factions rivaling the established governments of both countries. 

\

[4 marks]

$\\$

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Between 1933, when it was first proclaimed, and 1945, the U.S. faced many challenges in achieving the aims of the Good Neighbor Policy. 

One key problem was the lack of trust and cultural understanding between the U.S. and Latin American nations ($\checkmark$). To address this, in 1938, President Roosevelt created the Cultural Relations Division of the State Department, with a special focus on Latin America. This division encouraged educational and technical exchanges and promoted Latin American culture in U.S. media. In addition, new passenger lines were developed between New York and South American countries like Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay ($\checkmark$). The aim was to increase the connections between the U.S. and South America to improve relations. 

Another issue was presented by the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, which forced the U.S. to participate in WWII actively. For this, it aimed at hemispheric solidarity and unity of action, but some nations did not want to follow U.S. policy ($\checkmark$). In the case of Argentina, although the U.S. did not resort to military intervention, it responded by applying significant diplomatic and economic pressure, like freezing Argentinian funds and refusing to sell any strategic resource to the South American nation ($\checkmark$).
