# HI0589

Compare and contrast the reasons for Brazil and Mexico's participation in WWII. 

[6 marks]

The following material is an indication of what candidates may elect to write about in their responses. It is neither prescriptive nor exhaustive and no set answer is required. The answer need not be balanced but it requires at least **four** arguments with their respective evidence. 

For comparison, any **one to three** of the following similarities:

 - Initial neutrality

     - Both countries were initially neutral. In the case of Brazil, because it thought its commerce would benefit from a neutral position and, in the case of Mexico, because the government's policy was to remain neutral unless Mexico was attacked directly. 

 - German sinking of their ships

     - The catalyst for declaring war on the Axis was their ships being sunk by German submarines. Almost 40 Brazilian merchant ships were sunk between 1941-2, resulting in about 1700 dead. In May 1942, the Mexican oil tanker Potrero del Llano was sunk by a German submarine. After the Mexican government demanded an official apology, the German government's response was to torpedo another Mexican ship.  

 - Desire to obtain US assistance 

     - The USA gave two-thirds of its overall assistance via the Lend-Lease Act to Brazil. In 1942, Varga's government signed an agreement whereby the USA would help to equip the Brazilian army, and Brazil would provide key materials for the war effort, such as iron ore. A reciprocal defense agreement was signed between the US and Mexico in 1941, which allowed the US to use Mexican military bases and, in return, it helped train Mexican air pilots. That same year, Mexico agreed to sell all its strategic minerals to the US and the US agreed to give the Mexican government a loan of $\text{\textdollar}$40 million. 

For contrast, any **one to three** of the following differences:

- Mexico feared a Japanese attack, while Brazil initially feared one from Argentina. 

     - Due to its proximity to Japan, Mexico feared a Japanese attack after Pearl Harbour. On the other hand, Brazil initially did not want to join the war since it did not want to focus on an external threat when its rival, Argentina, could take advantage of the situation. 

 - Mexico had ideological differences with the Axis, while Brazil's government had many similarities. 

     - President Avila Camacho used Nazi ideology to unify the population against the Axis. He explained that Hitler's racial policies would certainly oppress the indigenous and Mestizo populations. On the other hand, Vargas was a dictator who had come to power through a coup in 1930 and was creating a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$New State” in Brazil with many of the same ideas as Fascist Italy. 

 - The Mexican public was reluctant to get involved in an armed conflict, while a large part of the Brazilian population demanded war 

     - After years of civil war, the Mexican population was tired of fighting. On the other hand, Vargas' nationalistic rhetoric and the many ships sunk by German submarines led to popular protests in all major cities demanding a declaration of war against Germany. 

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

Both Mexico and Brazil initially remained neutral during World War II, though for different reasons ($\checkmark$ similarity). For Brazil, the decision had to do with the idea that being able to trade with both sides in conflict would benefit its economy. On the other hand, in Mexico, the government had a strict policy of neutrality unless directly attacked. However, both nations entered the war on the side of the Allies in 1942 partly in response to the sinking of their merchant ships by German submarines ($\checkmark$ similarity). Between 1941 and 1942, nearly 40 Brazilian merchant ships were sunk, while in May 1942, the German navy torpedoed the Mexican oil tanker Potrero del Llano. When the Mexican government demanded an apology, Germany responded by sinking yet another Mexican vessel ($\checkmark$ evidence). The idea that an entry into WWII would encourage US military and economic assistance also played a part in the declaration of war for both countries ($\checkmark$ similarity). This idea proved true as Brazil received two-thirds of all Lend-Lease aid allocated to Latin America. At the same time, Mexico signed a reciprocal defense agreement with the U.S. in 1941, allowing American forces access to Mexican military bases in exchange for pilot training and a $\text{\textdollar}$40 million loan ($\checkmark$).

Besides these common grounds, each nation had its own reasons for participating in WWII. In the case of Mexico, there was an ideological component as President Avila Camacho resented the Nazis' racist ideology. On the other hand, Brazil's leader, Getúlio Vargas, had established a dictatorship since 1930 and shared many ideological similarities to Fascist Italy ($\checkmark$ contrast and evidence). Support for participation in war was also different. Mexicans were tired of war after 25 years of civil strife and preferred to stay out of the conflict, while in Brazil, the many ships sank by the Germans caused public outrage, and nationalist protests erupted in all major cities, which demanded war against the Axis ($\checkmark$ contrast and evidence).
