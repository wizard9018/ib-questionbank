# HI0594

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following: 

 - The source contains insight as the picture was taken in 1943, a year after the internment of people of Japanese origins started.  (origin).

 - The photograph was made to show the reality of what Japanese Americans were living. 

 - From the source we can learn that even small children were confined and that you could get out of the camps to serve in the army. We also learn that despite the injustice suffered, Japanese Americans stood out for their bravery in combat defending the US (content).

For limitations, any combination of the following: 

 - The source lacks hindsight. The photographer could not know how the lives of Japanese Americans would change in the upcoming years (origin).

 - The source seeks to criticize the internment camps as it highlights the injustice of a man who has risked his life for his country while his country was depriving his family of their freedom (purpose).

 - The source is only one small window into the conditions in which those in the internment camps lived. It portrays only one moment in time for two people. Because it's a photograph, it cannot show the reasons for the establishment of internment camps (content).

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source A has a valuable origin since it has insight as the photograph was taken in 1943, a year after the internment of Japanese Americans began ($\checkmark$ VO). Its purpose is also valuable since it seeks to show the reality of the internment camps, showing that even young children were confined ($\checkmark$ VP). The source shows that some internees were allowed to leave the camps to serve in the army. From the purple heart earnt by Army Sergeant Kazuo Komoto we can also learn that, despite the injustice they endured, Japanese Americans demonstrated remarkable bravery in combat, defending the United States ($\checkmark$ VC).

However, the source also has limitations for a historian studying the internment of Japanese Americans during WWII. Its origin is limited since it lacks hindsight, as the photographer could not predict how the lives of Japanese Americans would change in the following years ($\checkmark$ LO). The purpose is limited as it seeks to criticize the internment camps by emphasizing the injustice of a man risking his life for a country that was simultaneously depriving his family of freedom ($\checkmark$ LP). Moreover, as it is the image of only 2 individuals in one moment in time, the content we learn is limited. For example, it does not provide context regarding the reasons behind the establishment of internment camps ($\checkmark$ LC).
