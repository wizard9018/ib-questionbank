# HI0472

Explain the motivations behind the policy of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$cash and carry.”

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with its corresponding evidence:

 - Many in the US were isolationists and FDR had promised to keep the US out of the war. Congress approved of this policy since it maintained a semblance of neutrality for the United States while helping the Allied Powers. 

 - It removed the danger of US ships being targets for the Axis powers since the buyers were responsible for transporting (carrying) the purchased materials. This was important since it prevented a Lusitania-like situation from repeating itself and pushing the US into war and the British had naval dominance. 

 - By the Neutrality Acts of 1935, 1936, 1937 and 1939, the US could not help either side in the war. The “cash and carry” policy respected the law established by those acts while allowing the US to supply the United Kingdom and France with war materials. The policy allowed the US to supply any belligerent country and not take sides as long as it paid in cash for the weapons purchased. However, since Germany was cash-poor, only the French and the British could take significant advantage of US production. 

 - The US was still experiencing the effects of the Great Depression (17$\%$ unemployment by 1939) and this policy allowed US production into European markets. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The $\text{\textquotedblleft}$cash and carry” policy helped remove the danger of US ships being targeted by the Axis powers by placing the responsibility for transporting purchased materials on the buyers ($\checkmark$). This was crucial in preventing a repeat of incidents like the sinking of the Lusitania, which was one of the reasons the US entered WWI ($\checkmark$). This aspect of the policy was also aimed at helping the Allies while remaining neutral. Because the United Kingdom was still in control of the seas, it was the only one that could take advantage of US production ($\checkmark$). This is also true of the second element of the policy.  The policy permitted the US to supply any belligerent nation as long as it paid in cash for the war materials purchased. However, since Germany was cash-poor, it was effectively only the French and the British who could benefit from the US's production ($\checkmark$).
