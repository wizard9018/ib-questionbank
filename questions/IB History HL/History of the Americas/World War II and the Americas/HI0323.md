# HI0323

$\text{\textquotedblleft}$Argentina's policy of neutrality during most of Second World War had the nation's best interests in mind.” Provide **three** arguments that support this perspective.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following with its supporting evidence.  

Possible arguments: 

 - Argentina had a large population of Italians, plus their descendants. War against the Axis would mean they would have to choose what nation to be loyal to. 

     - Given the large population of ethnic Italians, neutrality helped avoid domestic conflict.   

 - There was division in the Armed Forces about what position to take. To avoid internal struggles, neutrality was the best option. 

     - The Navy wanted to join the Allies while the Army was divided. 10$\%$ of the Army was pro-Nazi, and 20$\%$ was pro-Allies. 

 - Argentina had a long tradition of neutrality in armed conflicts. 

     - It had been neutral in First World War and this position had benefited the country's economy by allowing them to maintain economic relations with countries on both sides of the conflict.

 - It was easier to assist Argentina`s main trading partner, the UK by being neutral. 

     - In 1939, Argentina decided to sell its goods to the United Kingdom on credit at 0% interest for as long as the conflict lasted. Neutrality meant Argentina`s merchant ships would not be military targets. Argentina supplied around 40$\%$ of the UK's mean consumption during the war.

 - Argentina did not want to depend on the United States because the two economies were competitive. The government feared the necessities of war would push Argentina to become dependent on the US. 

     - Since 1927, the US had prohibited meat imports from Argentina, one of the country's main export commodities. 

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

For most of World War II, Argentina made the controversial decision to remain neutral. For many historians and contemporaries, this was a terrible decision, but for others, it was what suited the nation's best interests. The government wanted to prevent internal divisions and, with that in mind, it had to consider the makeup of the population with mostly immigrant origins and the characteristics of the Armed Forces ($\checkmark$). With a significant portion of its population—now estimated between 60$\%$ and 70$\%$—having Italian ancestry, declaring war against the Axis would have forced many citizens to choose a side in the war ($\checkmark$ evidence). Argentina accepted dual citizenship, so many in its population were both Italian and Argentinian citizens. Furthermore, divisions within the Armed Forces made neutrality sound policy. While the Navy favored joining the Allies, the Army was split, with 10% supporting the Nazis and 20$\%$ backing the Allies ($\checkmark$ evidence).

Economically, neutrality was also perceived as something positive. In a way, Argentina was a provider of the Allies since the war began. In 1939, the Argentine government decided to sell goods on credit at 0$\%$ interest to the United Kingdom in light of the difficult situation it was going through. This benefit was only allowed to the UK because it was Argentina's leading trading partner. Neutrality allowed merchant ships with the Argentinian flag to sail the seas without being legal targets of German submarines ($\checkmark$). Thanks to this, Argentina provided around 40$\%$ of the UK's meat consumption during the war  ($\checkmark$ evidence).
