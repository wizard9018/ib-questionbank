# HI0349

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for a historian studying the reasons for the United States’ choice to use atomic weapons on Japan.

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Letter from President Harry S. Truman to Samuel Cavert (General Secretary of the Federal Council of the Churches of Christ in America) in response to his call for an end to the use of atomic weapons, August 11, 1945.

[Source: Truman, H. S. (1945, August 11). *Correspondence Between Samuel Cavert and President Harry S. Truman*. National Archives Catalog. Retrieved February 8, 2025 from https://catalog.archives.gov/id/296762538. Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following: 

 - The source contains insight as it was made on August 11, 1945, only a few days after the bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Moreover, it was made by the president of the United States, the man who made the final decision to use the bombs (origin).

 - The source mentions some of the reasons why the bombs were dropped, reminding Mr. Cavert of Japan's previous actions to explain that the Japanese only understand violence (content).

For limitations, any combination of the following: 

 - The source lacks hindsight. It was made before Japan`s actual surrender, so the president could not know if the bombs would have their intended purpose. The source contains only Truman's point of view (origin).

 - The source seeks to justify the decision to use the atomic bombs. It is made in response to a letter criticizing his actions (purpose).

 - The source leaves out many other reasons for the use of atomic weapons. It also exaggerates, speaking of the Japanese as beasts and saying, “The only language they seem to understand is the one we have been using to bombard them.” The source also contradicts what Truman himself said in source B (content).

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source C`s origin is valuable since it provides insight as it was created on August 11, 1945, just days after the atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Therefore, Truman has very fresh in his mind the reasons why he says he had to use atomic weapons. Truman was also the president of the United States at the time and thus the person with the final authority over the use of the bombs ($\checkmark$ VO). The content of the source is valuable since he mentions some of the reasons behind the bombings, specifically justifying them by referring to Japan's previous actions and suggesting that the Japanese only understand violence ($\checkmark$ VC). 

However, the source also has limitations for a historian studying the reasons for US use of atomic weapons on Japan. First of all, due to its origin, it lacks hindsight as it was produced before Japan's official surrender, meaning the president could not yet know whether the bombings would in fact be $\text{\textquotedblleft}$understood” by the Japanese ($\checkmark$ LO). Also, it only presents Truman's point of view as part of a reply to a previous letter by Ms. Cavert, so the information it gives is limited to that conversation. That is why it leaves out many other reasons for the use of atomic weapons, like the fact that he wanted to save American lives. This is a limitation of the content ($\checkmark$ LC).  The purpose is also limited since he writes in response to a letter criticizing his decision. Therefore, his purpose is to justify his actions, not to explain all his real motivations ($\checkmark$ LP).
