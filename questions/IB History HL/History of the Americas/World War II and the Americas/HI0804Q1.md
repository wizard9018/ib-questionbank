# HI0804Q1

What does Source A suggest about the relationship between Argentina and the U.S. in 1942?

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Cartoon by Clifford Berryman, January 24, 1942. It refers to US attempts to convince Argentina of breaking relations with the Axis Power. The cartoon portrays Secretary of State Cordell Hull, Assistant Secretary of State Sumner Welles and a female character labeled as Argentina.

![HI0804Q1](images/HI0804Q1.JPEG)

[Source: Berryman, C. K. (1942, January 24). *Untitled*. National Archives NextGen Catalog. Retrieved June 25, 2025 from  from https://catalog.archives.gov/id/6012250. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

- Argentina has not accepted Cordell Hull`s previous U.S. proposals

- The U.S. is interested in getting Argentina on its side

- Cordell Hull thinks Sumner Welles' proposals have a good chance of being accepted

- The relationship is peaceful as U.S. diplomats are trying to convince Argentina to join them. 

\

[2 marks]

$\\$

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source A suggests that Argentina had not agreed to follow U.S. policy by 1942. We can see this in that Cordell Hull, as Secretary of State, is not directly negotiating with Argentina and says $\text{\textquotedblleft}$they can`t resist my boy Sumner”, implying that there was previous resistance ($\checkmark$). The Source also suggests that the U.S. is not giving up and is still actively trying to win Argentina over and, to do so, it changes the negotiator ($\checkmark$).
