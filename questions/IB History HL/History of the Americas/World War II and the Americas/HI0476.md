$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources B and C recognize that there was a debate in the US regarding its participation in WWII ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). In Source B, Roosevelt says, “We have just now engaged in a great debate. It was not limited to the halls of Congress. It was argued in every newspaper, on every wave length-over every cracker barrel in the land” and Source C mentions there was “a major foreign policy debate” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Both sources also mention that, once the debate was resolved, the US was committed to supplying weapons to those fighting for democracy ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source B states that “The great task of this day, the deep duty which rests upon us, is to move products from the assembly lines of our factories to the battle lines of democracy-Now!”. At the same time, Source C mentions that  FDR  “believed the United States should serve as a great arsenal of democracy” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). In both sources, the needs of Great Britain are highlighted as well as the fact that the US will come to its aid ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).  Furthermore, both sources say that the US supplying the democracies of the world is essential for its own security ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). In Source B Roosevelt recognizes that this effort is “the only way to guarantee ultimate safety" while in Source C the Secretary of War says “We are buying our own security while we prepare” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, the sources also have key contrasts. One important difference concerns whether American soldiers will fight in the war ($\checkmark\, \text{contrast}$). Roosevelt says in Source B that “we started the training of millions of men.” while in Source C he says “your boys are not going to be sent into any foreign wars” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). If they are not going to be sent to war, why would they be trained? Another key difference has to do with the time the US has started preparing for war ($\checkmark\, \text{contrast}$). Speaking in 1941, Roosevelt said  “Beginning a year ago, we started the erection of hundreds of plants and we started the training of millions of men.” In contrast, Source C says it started in 1941 with the passing of the Lend-Lease Act: “Congress passed the Lend-Lease Act, meeting Great Britain's deep need for supplies and allowing the United States to prepare for war while remaining officially neutral” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
