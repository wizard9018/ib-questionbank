# HI1000

Compare and contrast the involvement of Canada and the USA in the Second World War.

[15 marks]

**Three** aspects can be analyzed to answer this question. 

 - Diplomatic policies

 - Economic contribution

 - Military contribution 

Considerations:

 - In the exam, it will be up to the candidate to choose which countries to use to answer the question.

 - Students should attempt to analyze at least two similarities and two differences between the countries chosen. 

 - It is important that the information regarding both countries mentioned in the question is balanced and that a running comparison structure is attempted, rather than an isolated discussion of each. 

Indicative content (not prescriptive):

 - Diplomatic policies: 

     - US Neutrality Acts of 1935, 1936, 1937, and 1939. Did not enter the war until the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbour in 1941

     - Canada was the only country in the region to declare war on the Axis Powers in 1939, one week after the British did. 

     - Canada declared war on Germany, while the US declared war on Japan, and as a result, Germany declared war on the US.

     - While the US tried to organize a hemispheric united response to the conflict, Canada followed its own policy and ignored the rest of the Americas. 

     - Both countries considered the United Kingdom a vital partner and assisted it since 1939. The US and Canada both provided supplies, with Canada also contributing militarily.

     - Both countries played an important role in the diplomatic conferences organized to plan the war. Canada's diplomatic status increased when it hosted the Quebec Conferences, although Prime Minister Mackenzie King was always aware that Roosevelt and Churchill were the leading players, and he remained in the background. The US was the main participant in all the wartime conferences, not only in Quebec. 

 - Economic contribution 

     - The US assisted in the war effort even before it entered the war through its Cash and Carry scheme, the Destroyers for Bases plan, and the Lend-Lease Act. Canada implemented the Mutual Aid programme to assist the Allies with food, raw materials, and munitions from May 1943 until the end of the Second World War.

     - Canada assisted the US in its implementation of Lend-Lease

 - Military contribution

     - Canada supported the US and British plans for the development of the war, while the US determined the course of action. 

     - Canada's navy was fundamental during the Battle of the Atlantic, and it was the seat of the British Commonwealth Air Training Force, which was instrumental in the bombing campaigns in Europe and in defending Britain from the Luftwaffe.  

     - US navy and Air Force determined the result of the war in the Pacific

     - The Canadian army's first major engagement in Europe, the Battle of Dieppe in 1942, was a failure. 

     - Both countries had a meaningful participation in D-Day, 6 June 1944.

     - In the Pacific theater, Canada played an important role only in the 1941 battle for the defense of Hong Kong, while the US was almost completely responsible for the defeat of Japan. 

     - Because of the war, the US suffered over 1 million casualties. One million Canadians served in the military during the conflict.

[15 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Second World War was a conflict that, in one way or another, involved most of the countries of the world. Two countries that had an impact on the outcome were Canada and the United States. The following essay will compare and contrast the involvement of these nations in the conflict ($\checkmark\, \text{ focus}$). To do so, it will refer to why these countries got involved in the war as well as their economic and military contributions to the victory of the Allies ($\checkmark\, \text{ organization}$). 

One of the ways the US and Canada differed was in the reasons for becoming involved in the war ($\checkmark\, \text{ focus}$) ($\checkmark\, \text{ knowledge}$). Canada was the first to declare war on the Axis Powers out of a loyalty to the British Commonwealth of which it was part. Canada was an independent country, however, its official head of state was the British monarch. When the UK declared war on Germany in 1939, there was a two-day debate in the Canadian parliament that determined Canada's entrance into the war. On the other hand the US's military participation came as a response to the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbour in 1941. In this case, the US only declared war on Japan, and the Germans declared war on the US due to their alliance with Japan through the Tripartite Pact.  

However, participation in WWII was not limited to the military aspect. The US, like Canada, got involved in the war in 1939 as a way to help out the British. The US's initial participation concerned its economic contribution to the British war effort ($\checkmark\, \text{ knowledge}$). Through the Cash and Carry scheme of 1939, belligerents could purchase goods from the United States as long as the recipients paid immediately in cash and assumed all risk in transportation using their own ships ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). Since the British were the only ones with readily available cash and ships to transport, this was a way to assist them while remaining nominally neutral in the conflict ($\checkmark\, \text{ analysis}$).  

Both Canada and the US contributed to the war effort by supplying the Allies with the necessary materials for success ($\checkmark\, \text{ focus}$). When Prime Minister Churchill informed Roosevelt they were out of cash to keep the Cash and Carry scheme going, it was replaced with the Lend-Lease Act. Like Canada's Mutual Aid programme of 1943, the objective was to assist the Allies with food, raw materials, and munitions ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). Due to the US's production capabilities, its economic impact on the war's outcome was much bigger ($\checkmark\, \text{ analysis}$). In fact, Roosevelt called his country “the arsenal of democracy” since without its supplies, the UK and the USSR would have found it impossible to continue the war. Roosevelt also recognized Canada's contributions by calling it the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$aerodrome of democracy" due to its production of more than 16,000 aircraft ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). Although Roosevelt's views are tainted with bias, it is undeniable that US production was able to keep the Allies supplied during the conflict ($\checkmark\, \text{ perspectives}$). Canada's contributions, though smaller in scale, were vital in supplementing the US, as noted by Roosevelt. 

When speaking about the military involvement of both nations, it can also be said that the US's role was critical, but that Canada's support was also crucial for Allied victory ($\checkmark\, \text{ focus}$). The Royal Canadian Navy was indispensable in the Battle of the Atlantic, ensuring the safe passage of supplies across the ocean ($\checkmark\, \text{ knowledge}$). By the war's end in Europe, Canadians escorted 25,343 merchant ships to help the UK continue its war effort and keep morale high during the Blitz ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). On the battlefield, Canada's first major participation was in the battle for the French port of Dieppe in 1942. While it failed in capturing the town, the information gathered was crucial for the proper planning of what would become the D-Day invasion of 1944 ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). On the other hand, the US military role was vital in the success of all fronts during the war, except for the Eastern Front in Europe ($\checkmark\, \text{ knowledge}$).  This was especially so in the Pacific, where the US determined the war strategy and could be recognized as the leading actor in the defeat of Japan. From the Battle of Midway of 1942, which ended Japanese dominance of the Pacific, to the dropping of the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945,  it can be said that the US played a decisive role in the defeat of Japan ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). Canada's only notable participation in the Pacific Theater was in the failed defense of Hong Kong in 1941 ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). The different impact of the involvement of both nations had a lot to do with the amount of soldiers each was able to provide ($\checkmark\, \text{ analysis}$). While one million Canadian citizens served in the military during the war, the US suffered over one million casualties, with far more total participants. The overall population of each country and the magnitude of their armed forces clearly show that their military involvement would be different ($\checkmark\, \text{ examples}$). 

In conclusion, the involvement of Canada and the USA in the Second World War had many similarities and differences ($\checkmark\, \text{ focus}$). While they declared war at different moments and for different reasons, both got involved initially to help Britain resist the Germans. Due to the size of their populations, it can be said that even though the impact of their involvement was different, their intentions were the same, and they complemented each other in the search for victory ($\checkmark\, \text{ analysis}$) ($\checkmark\, \text{ conclusion}$).
