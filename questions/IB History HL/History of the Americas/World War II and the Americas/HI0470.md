# HI0470

Evaluate the economic effects of the Good Neighbor Policy for the United States.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Since the command term is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate”, the response must contain both positive and negative results to reach more than four marks. To reach full marks, there must be a conclusion. To respond to an $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate” command term it is valid to say that the whole goal was not reached as something negative. 

Positive results:

 - The Export-Import Bank was created to lend money to foreign companies that wanted to import goods from the USA. This favored US producers who were hard hit by the Great Depression. Before WWII, the efforts of this bank concentrated on Latin America. 

 - The US government passed the Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act by which it could negotiate bilateral trade agreements with Latin American countries. This allowed the US to become the main trading partner of almost all Latin American countries.  

Negative results:

 - One the goals of the Good Neighbor Policy was to dominate trade in the Americas. This goal was not reached. Argentina did not sign a bilateral trade agreement with the US, choosing instead to sign one with the United Kingdom. 

 - Many in the US business community felt their interests threatened, as this implied the US government would no longer intervene should the country's domestic policies become less friendly to US businesses. By 1933, the USA held $\text{\textdollar}$5 billion in investments in Latin America ($1.5 billion in portfolio, $3.5 billion in direct investment).

\

[4 marks]

Concluding remark:

 - Responses are likely to conclude that the overall economic effects of the Good Neighbor Policy  were positive for the US.  

 - The strongest responses will mention the overall positive results while acknowledging it did not have the desired results with all the countries of the region..

\

[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Good Neighbor Policy had many positive economic effects for the US. It led to the creation of the Export-Import Bank, which provided loans to Latin American companies looking to import goods from the United States. This helped U.S. producers that were suffering the effects of the Great Depression by opening up new markets for their goods ($\checkmark$). By 1940 Congress allowed it to lend $\text{\textdollar}$700 million to support exports to Latin America. Additionally, the U.S. government passed the Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act, allowing it to negotiate bilateral trade agreements. For example, in 1935, a reciprocal trade agreement placed 90$\%$ of Brazilian exports on the duty-free list, allowing it to become the fifth largest supplier to the USA. As a result, the United States became the primary trading partner for almost all nations in the region ($\checkmark$).

However, not all aspects of the Good Neighbor Policy were successful. Argentina, for instance, chose not to sign a bilateral trade agreement with the United States and instead aligned itself economically with the United Kingdom. This showed the limitations of the Good Neighbor Policy since not all countries in the region were willing to conform to U.S. trade interests ($\checkmark$). Moreover, many in the US business community were afraid of losing their investments in Latin America which by 1933 had reached $\text{\textdollar}$5 billion ($\checkmark$). Up to then, they had enjoyed the support of their government in case of conflict with local authorities.

In conclusion, the economic effects of the Good Neighbor Policy were mostly positive for the United States, as it facilitated trade and strengthened economic relations with most Latin American countries at a time when the nation needed new markets to incentivize a production hard hit by the Great Depression. However, its impact was not uniform across the region, as seen in Argentina's decision to maintain economic ties with Britain. Moreover, the Latin American market was not enough to take the US out of the Depression ($\checkmark$ conclusion).
