# HI0804Q5

Compare and contrast what Sources A and F reveal about U.S. policies regarding Argentina in 1942. 

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |

| :-- | :-- |

|Includes clear points of comparison (similarity)| ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) |

|Includes clear points of contrast (difference)| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |

|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

For comparison, any of the following similarities:

 - Both sources agree that Argentina was not yet convinced to follow U.S. policy. 

     - In Source A, the character labeled Argentina is on a balcony, away from the U.S.

     - Source F says the purpose of the measure of freezing funds is to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$to coerce Argentina into greater collaboration with the war effort of the United Nations.”

 - Both sources agree that the U.S. should persuade Argentina to follow its policy. 

     - In Source A the character labeled as Sumner Welles is trying to lure Argentina by singing a serenade and bring it down from the balcony

     - In Source F, Cordell Hull explains the measure put in place saying, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$in order to persuade Argentina of where its vital interests lie”

 - In both sources, Cordell Hull oversees U.S. policy toward Argentina.

For contrast, any of the following differences:

 - While in Source A, the issue concerns only Argentina, in F, Hull recognizes the impact the relationship could have on other countries in the region, such as Chile and the other nations in general. 

     - In Source A, only Argentine and U.S. officials are present in the cartoon

     - Source F says $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the other American republics would begin to wonder whether we had repudiated our present policy and returned to the days of the Big Stick.”

 - While in Source A the issue is being handled by members of the State Department, in Source F, the proposal to freeze funds is from the Board of Economic Warfare 

 - While in Source A the way to convince Argentina is by giving her a nice song, the measures mentioned in Source F are the opposite of giving. Source F says $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the United States, since Rio has withheld delivery of military and naval supplies to Argentina (...) and is furnishing scarce nonmilitary goods(...).”

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Sources A and F have some similarities regarding U.S. policies towards Argentina in 1942. In both, we can see that Argentina was not convinced to follow U.S. policy ($\checkmark\text{ similarity}$).  In Source A, Argentina is shown as a lady who is physically distant from the United States, who is trying to lure her. Similarly, Source F states that the purpose of freezing funds was $\text{\textquotedblleft}$to coerce Argentina into greater collaboration with the war effort of the United Nations.”  ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$). Another similarity is that the U.S. is trying to convince Argentina to align with its policies despite the country`s hesitation ($\checkmark\text{ similarity}$). In Source A, the character labeled Sumner Welles tries to lure Argentina by singing a serenade. At the same time, in Source F, Cordell Hull explains that the measure was intended $\text{\textquotedblleft}$to persuade Argentina of where its vital interests lie.”  ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$). Furthermore, in both sources, Cordell Hull is shown as the key figure responsible for handling U.S. policy toward Argentina.

However, the sources also contain differences regarding the same topic. Source A focuses only on the relationship between Argentina and U.S. officials, while Source F acknowledges the wider regional impact this relationship could have  ($\checkmark\text{ difference}$). Hull notes that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the other American republics would begin to wonder whether we had repudiated our present policy and returned to the days of the Big Stick,” highlighting concerns about U.S. relations with other Latin American nations if Argentina was treated too harshly ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$). Another difference has to do with the way to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$convince” Argentina to align with the U.S.  ($\checkmark\text{ difference}$). While in Source A, Welles sings a serenade to Argentina, the measures mentioned in Source F are more forceful saying, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the United States, since Rio has withheld delivery of military and naval supplies to Argentina (...) and is furnishing scarce nonmilitary goods(...).” ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$).
