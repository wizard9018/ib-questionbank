# HI0804Q9

Evaluate the success of the Rio Conference of 1942 from the perspective of the U.S.

[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Since the command term is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate”, the response must contain both positive and negative results to reach more than four marks. To reach full marks, there must be a conclusion. To respond to an $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate” command term it is valid to say that the whole goal was not reached as something negative. 

$\\$

Positive results:

 - The Conference recommended the breaking of relations with the Axis Powers after the attack on Pearl Harbor. Most of the countries in the region did so.  

 - Assistant Secretary of State Sumner Welles made promises of economic assistance in exchange for continental solidarity in the face of attacks. This was to be done by including Latin American countries in the Lend-Lease policy. 

 - The hemispheric nations established the Inter-American Defense Board to coordinate the region's defense.  

$\\$

Negative results:

 - Chile did not break off relations with the Axis Powers until 1943, when concern for Japanese naval strength disappeared. Argentina waited until March 1944 to break off relations with the Axis Powers.  

 - The rift between Assistant Secretary of State Sumner Welles` diplomatic approach and Secretary of State Cordell Hull`s desire to pressure the countries in the region to commit to war was made evident. Welles would resign under pressure in 1943.  According to Gellman, the Good Neighbor policy effectively ended with the departure of Sumner Welles.

 \

 [4 marks]

Concluding remark:

 - Responses are likely to conclude that the overall results of the Conference were positive for the U.S.

 - The strongest responses will mention the overall positive results while acknowledging it did not have the desired results with all the region's countries.

 \

$\\$

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Rio Conference of 1942 was mostly successful from the point of view of the U.S. in its aim of achieving continental solidarity in its fight against the Axis Powers. 

Firstly, the conference recommended breaking diplomatic relations with the Axis Powers and, except for Argentina and Chile, the region's countries did so  ($\checkmark$). To strengthen ties and achieve the United States' wartime interests, the Inter-American Defense Board was established to coordinate the region's defense. Up to this moment, cooperation on defensive matters was limited to periodic meetings and the individual decisions of the states. With the  Inter-American Defense Board, there was finally a regional organization ($\checkmark$). 

Moreover, Assistant Secretary of State Sumner Welles` promise of economic assistance in exchange for continental solidarity tied the Latin American economies closer to the U.S. economy  ($\checkmark$). This was done through the inclusion of Latin American countries in the Lend-Lease policy ($\checkmark$).

However, the U.S. was not able to accomplish all its goals. Chile delayed breaking relations with the Axis until 1943, and Argentina only did so in March 1944. These delays undermined the goal of unified action against the Axis Powers  ($\checkmark$). The conference also laid bare internal divisions within the U.S. administration on how to achieve its objectives. Assistant Secretary of State Sumner Welles favored a diplomatic approach based on incentives, while Secretary of State Cordell Hull preferred applying pressure  ($\checkmark$). This conflict led to Welles's resignation in 1943. 

In conclusion, while the Rio Conference did not achieve total regional alignment and led to important disagreements among U.S. policymakers, it was overall a success from the U.S. perspective. Most countries broke ties with the Axis, regional defense was coordinated, and the U.S. secured broader cooperation in the war effort ($\checkmark\text{ conclusion}$).
