# HI0475

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying US participation in WWII by 1941.
[4 marks]

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:
- The source contains insight as it was made in 1940 by the president of the United States, a key decision-maker in his country's policies (origin).
- The source wants to explain how the US is contributing to the fight to save democracy (purpose).
- The source mentions the initial debate on whether the US should get involved. It also describes the situation in March 1941 when the “Aid-for-Democracies Bill” (aka the Lease-and-Lend Bill) was passed and what the US was doing to contribute as much as possible to the fight (content).

For limitations, any combination of the following:
- The source lacks hindsight as it was made in March 1941 and does not know that the US entered the war as a belligerent by the end of the year (origin).
- The source seeks to persuade the correspondents that the entire US population, 130 million Americans, will give their best to help democracy and that it is the right thing to do. Since this is a speech to journalists, convincing them it is right for the US to become an arsenal of democracy will help him get the message across to the general public (purpose).
- The source does not provide the perspective of those who disagreed with the president. It also contains some exaggerations, such as implying that all 130 million Americans will do their part. The source also lacks specific information, such as “we started the erection of hundreds of plants and we started the training of millions of men” (content).
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source B's origin is valuable to a historian studying US participation in WWII by 1941 since it was made in that same year and thus provides contemporary insight. It is also made by the president of the US, a key decision-maker in shaping his country`s foreign policy ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The purpose is also valuable as Roosevlet wants to explain how the U.S. is contributing to the fight to save democracy ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). With this in mind, the content provides information on the initial debate over U.S. involvement, the situation in March 1941 when the aid-to-democracies bill was passed, and the country's efforts to contribute as much as possible to winning the war ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

However, the source also has limitations. Because it was made in March of 1941 the origin makes it lack hindsight as Roosevelt could not know that the US would enter the war as a belligerent that same year ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). The purpose is also limited since, with this speech, Roosevelt wants to persuade the correspondents that the 130 million Americans are going to give their best to help democracy and that it is the right thing to do. Since this is a speech to journalists, convincing them the US should become an arsenal of democracy will help him get the message across to the general public ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). This makes the content limited as it leaves out any mention of the many who still opposed US involvement in the war in any way and it makes Roosevelt exaggerate with the idea that all 130 million Americans would give their total effort to win the war ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
