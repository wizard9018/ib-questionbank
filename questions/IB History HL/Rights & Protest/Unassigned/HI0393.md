# HI0393

Provide **two** arguments to justify the following statement: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Malcolm X was a controversial figure of the civil rights movement.”

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Arguments need evidence to back them up. **Two** of the following arguments with supporting evidence: 

 - He was accused of incentivizing the use of violence. In the televised documentary $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Hate that Hate Produced” (1959), Malcolm X said blacks should defend themselves $\text{\textquotedblleft}$by any means necessary."

 - His relationship with the Nation of Islam (NOI) ended badly. Even though it was a small religious sect until Malcolm X became its spokesman, he was suspended in 1963 and left the organization in 1964. In February 1965, Malcolm was assassinated by NOI gunmen. 

 - He made controversial remarks after Kennedy's assassination. When asked by reporters to comment on the assassination of President Kennedy, Malcolm X said that it was a case of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$chickens coming home to roost" and added that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$chickens coming home to roost never did make me sad; they've always made me glad." For his comments, he was suspended from preaching by the NOI.

 - It was said by many that his influence actually set back the civil rights movement. Time Magazine wrote an article after his death describing him as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$a disaster to the civil rights movement,” and Martin Luther King said of him, "urging Negroes to arm themselves and to prepare to engage in violence, as he has done, can reap nothing but grief."

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Today, Martin Luther King and Malcolm X are remembered as some of the most influential figures of the civil rights movement.  However, at the time, Malcolm X was considered a very controversial figure. As part of the Black Power movement, he rejected the peaceful protests of other organizations ($\checkmark$) and called national attention to himself by stating in the televised documentary $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Hate that Hate Produced” (1959) that African Americans should defend themselves “by any means necessary” ($\checkmark$). Moreover, the Nation of Islam, of whom he was the main spokesman, ended up suspending his preaching ($\checkmark$). Malcolm X disagreed with some of the behaviour and beliefs of the leader of the NOI, Elijah Muhannad, and he left the organization in 1964. In February 1965, Malcolm was assassinated by NOI gunmen ($\checkmark$).
