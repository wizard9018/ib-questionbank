# HI0398

What, according to the source, were the reasons for the passage of the Voting Rights Act of 1965?
[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Though the 1964 Civil Rights Act called for an end to state discrimination in voter registration, the Justice Department did not have the means to enforce it, so more measures were necessary.
- In March, Martin Luther King organized a march on Selma, Alabama, to dramatize the need for a voting rights bill. The violence used by Alabama officials on the peaceful protesters generated a national crisis with tens of thousands of volunteers heading south to join King.
- Johnson delivered a powerful speech to Congress that called for a respect of American democracy, urging it to pass the bill.
- Both the public revulsion over southern white violence and Johnson's political skills led to the passing of the Voting Rights Act
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
According to the source, the passing of the Voting Rights Act of 1965 was because the provisions of the previous Civil Rights Act of 1964 did not give enough power to the federal government to make sure ending discrimination in voter registration was achieved ($\checkmark$). Due to this failure, Martin Luther King Jr. organized a march on Selma, Alabama to show the need for a voting rights bill. The violent response to the march by white racists led to a national crisis and showed the need for further federal government action ($\checkmark$). Johnson was able to convince Congress to pass the Voting Rights Act thanks to a speech he gave appealing to the need to respect American democracy ($\checkmark$).
