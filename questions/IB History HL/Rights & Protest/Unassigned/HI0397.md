# HI0397

Explain the significance of the Montgomery bus boycott for the civil rights movement. 

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following with its corresponding evidence:

 - It demonstrated the power of a community using direct but non-violent action. 

     - Most of the 50.000 members of the African American community of Montgomery participated in the boycott for more than a year. 

 - It demonstrated the importance of the Churches in the fight for equality.

     - Martin Luther King Jr.  was the minister of the Dexter Avenue Baptist Church which served as the backbone of the organization of the boycott. 

 - This form of protest served as a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$model" for other grass-roots demonstrations. 

     - There were similar successful bus boycotts in 20 Southern cities. For example, the Tallahassee Bus Boycott of 1956. 

 - It led to more Northern white support and more co-operation between Northern and Southern blacks.

 - It showed the economic importance of African Americans.

     - White business men began to work against segregation because the boycott cost businesses around \$1 million in sales since shoppers could not get downtown for lack of transportation. 

 - It brought King, with his powerful rhetoric, to the forefront of the movement. In 1957 he helped to establish a new organization, the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC).

 - It showed the effectiveness of the NAACP strategy of working through the US legal system. 

     - It was only after the Supreme Court backed the federal district court in Browder v. Gayle (1956) that buses were desegregated. 

 - Historiography debate:\

    - David Garrow (1994) says the Montgomery Bus Boycott marked the start of the civil rights movement. 

    - In contrast, Mark Newman (2004) says it $\text{\textquotedblleft}$did not spark a mass movement".

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Montgomery Bus Boycott that took place for almost a year in 1955-1956 was highly significant for the civil rights movement. One of the main reasons for this was that it showed the importance of unity of the community to achieve success in direct and non-violent action ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$). In Montgomery, almost all 50,000 members of the African American community participated in the boycott of the city buses ($\checkmark$). Due to this, the boycott was able to prove the economic importance of the African American community since the boycott led to an estimated \$1 million in lost sales since people could not go downtown to shop ($\checkmark$). This economic power forced many white businessmen to start opposing segregation ($\checkmark$). 

According to historian David Garrow (1994), the Montgomery Bus Boycott was one of the most important events of the civil rights movement as he considers it marked the start of the movement ($\checkmark$). However, Mark Newman (2004) concludes it was not so significant since it $\text{\textquotedblleft}$did not spark a mass movement" as the next big mass action event was many years later, with the Freedom Rides of 1961 ($\checkmark$).
