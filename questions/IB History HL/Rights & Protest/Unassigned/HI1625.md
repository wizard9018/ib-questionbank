# HI1625

Explain **two** political impacts of the Voting Rights Act (1965) on American society.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any combination of **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- Enfranchisement of Black Americans in the South
    - Removed barriers to voting by ending literacy tests and poll taxes, making it easier for African Americans to register and vote.
    - Greatly increased Black voter registration in the South, with Mississippi's registration rate rising to 59% by 1968 and the gap between Black and White voter registration narrowing to just 7% by 1980.
- The rise of Black political power
    - Increased Black political representation, as the number of African Americans elected to office in the South increased six-fold between 1965 and 1969 and doubled again by 1980.
- Party realignment
    - Shifted American political alignments dramatically, with the South transitioning away from the Democratic Party and towards the Republican Party. The transformation reshaped both parties' ideological identities and formed the foundation of US politics to today.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One impact of the Voting Rights Act (1965) was that it removed barriers to voting by abolishing literacy tests and poll taxes, making it easier for African Americans to vote ($\checkmark$). Another impact was that Black voter registration increased significantly across the South, with Mississippi reaching 59% registration by 1968 ($\checkmark$). A further impact was the increase in Black political representation, as the number of African Americans elected to office in the South increased six-fold between 1965 and 1969 ($\checkmark$). The Civil Rights Movement reshaped American political loyalties, as many white Southern voters moved from the Democratic to the Republican Party. This shift contributed to the parties' modern ideological identities shaping American politics even in modern times ($\checkmark$).
