# HI1194

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source O for a historian studying the significance of Bantustans.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source O** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Nelson Mandela's article on Verwoerd's proposal of Bantu self-government, published in Liberation magazine N° 36.. |

:box[There is no sovereignty. No autonomy. No democracy. No self-government. Nothing but a crude, empty fraud, to bluff the people, and to serve as a pretext for accumulating yet more hardships and injustices upon the African people. Politically, the talk about self-government for the reserves is a trick. Economically, it is an absurdity.\
\
The few scattered African reserves in the Union, comprising approximately 13 percent of the least desirable land, represent the last remnants of land ownership left to the African people in their original ancestral home. After the depredations of generations of Europeans, the outrageous Land Acts from 1913 onwards had turned the once free and independent Tswana, Sotho, Xhosa, Zulu, and other peasant farmers of this country into a nation of landless outcasts and roving beggars, humble "work-seekers" on the mines and the farms where yesterday they had been masters of the land. And now the new White masters of the country generously "presented" the few miserable areas that were left to remain as reservoirs and breeding-grounds for black labor. These are the reserves.\
\
The facts are that the reserves are congested, distressed areas, completely unable to sustain their present populations. People are on the verge of starvation. The White Paper [Verwoerd's proposal] discusses teaching Africans about soil conservation and agriculture. No amount of agricultural instruction will ever enable 13 percent of the land to sustain 66 percent of the population.]

[Source: Mandela, N. (1959, May). *Verwoerd's Grim Plot.* South African History Online. Retrieved October 11, 2025, from https://sahistory.org.za/archive/verwoerds-grim-plot-nelson-mandela. CC-BY.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose, and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation, or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- Written by Nelson Mandela, offering a firsthand political critique of apartheid and Bantustan policies.
- Aims to expose Bantustans as unjust and mobilize resistance through passionate, accusatory language.
- Describes harsh conditions like poverty, land scarcity, and forced labor migration.
- Links apartheid land laws to colonial exploitation and African liberation struggles.

For limitations, any of the following:
- The source is politically charged and reflects Mandela's personal stance rather than a neutral viewpoint.
- It overlooks diverse African perspectives, especially those from rural communities and traditional leaders.
- Emotive language may obscure practical details about how Bantustans were administered.
- Lacks empirical data and official records, limiting its value for policy or quantitative analysis.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Written by Nelson Mandela, the source's origin is valuable because it represents a firsthand political critique of apartheid ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). Mandela seeks to expose the injustices of the Bantustan policies by explicitly addressing their impacts on black South Africans, describing poverty, land scarcity, and the forced migration of laborers, painting a grim picture of life under systemic oppression ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The content of the source links these conditions to the broader legacy of colonial exploitation, framing apartheid land laws as a continuation of imperialist control and situating the struggle against Bantustans within the larger context of African liberation movements ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

Because the source's explicit purpose is to critique the Bantustan system, it cannot be considered an objective account of its significance ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). The emotive language used by Mandela further evidences this. Though effective in conveying urgency and injustice, it can obscure practical details about the actual administration of Bantustans ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
