## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The protests were specifically directed at the extension of pass laws to women, as evidenced by the signs mentioning "women" and the fact that all protesters visible are women.
- The protests were peaceful and non-threatening, as shown by the presence of a baby, smiling women, and the absence of any weapons or aggressive behavior.
- The protests were organized and planned, as demonstrated by the prepared signs with consistent messaging and coordinated slogans.
- Pass laws were viewed very negatively by protesters, who equated them with slavery, as shown by the sign $\text{\textquotedblleft}$WITH PASSES WE ARE SLAVES."
- The protests involved ordinary community members rather than just political activists, as evidenced by the participation of women of different ages, including mothers with children.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source B suggests that the protests against pass laws in 1956 were peaceful and non-violent ($\checkmark$), as evidenced by the presence of women smiling and a mother carrying her baby, showing they did not expect or intend confrontation. The source also suggests the protests were well-organized ($\checkmark$), as demonstrated by the prepared signs with clear, consistent messages opposing the pass laws. Finally, Source B suggests that black South African women felt that pass laws made them feel like “slaves” ($\checkmark$).
