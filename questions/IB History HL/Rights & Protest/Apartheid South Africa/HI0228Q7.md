# HI0228Q7

Explain the significance of the Sharpeville Massacre to the anti-apartheid movement.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - Marked the shift from peaceful, non-violent protest to armed struggle

     - In response to the events in Sharpeville, both the African National Congress (ANC) and Pan Africanist Congress (PAC) adopted an armed struggle creating uMkhonto weSizwe and Poqo, respectively.

 - Led to government crackdowns and further repression

     - The South African government declared a state of emergency and implemented mass arrests of anti-apartheid leadership. Major anti-apartheid groups including the ANC and PAC were banned, forcing them underground. The South African government implemented even more repressive laws including the Sabotage Act and the 90-day Retention Law.  This response from the government largely suppressed the broader anti-apartheid movement until the 1970s, essentially destroying the PAC.

 - Increased international attention and anti-apartheid activism

     - International outcry resulted in increased diplomatic pressure on the South African government. The United Nations passed a resolution condemning the South African government’s actions. Originally known as the “Boycott Movement” in the UK, the organization rebranded as the Anti-Apartheid Movement (AAM) and expanded the scope of its work. Anti-apartheid activism also grew in other countries through economic sanctions, arms embargoes, cutting diplomatic ties and grassroots domestic protest campaigns 

 - Inspired a new generation of domestic anti-apartheid activism

    - While the initial crackdown on and repression of anti-apartheid activism after Sharpeville initially had a dampening effect on the movement, the massacre served as a point of inspiration for continued activism.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One reason the Sharpeville Massacre was significant in the anti-apartheid movement was because it became the catalyst for increased resistance for many activists ($\checkmark$). More specifically, it helped inspire the birth of the military wing of the African National Congress, uMkhonte weSizwe, which signaled the transition toward violent resistance ($\checkmark$). 

However, in response, the massacre also sparked an uptick in violent, repressive responses from the government as they became concerned such protests would continue and threaten their control ($\checkmark$). In the short time following the massacre, the ANC and Pan Africanist Congress (PAC) were both banned and more restrictive laws were implemented, such as the 90-day Retention Law, which allowed the government to jail and hold arrested activists almost indefinitely ($\checkmark$).
