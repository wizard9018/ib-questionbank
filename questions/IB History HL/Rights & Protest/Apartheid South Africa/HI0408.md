:box[The Defiance Campaign in 1952 was the first large-scale, multi-racial political mobilization against apartheid laws under a common leadership - by the African National Congress, South African Indian Congress, and the Coloured People's Congress. More than 8,000 trained volunteers went to jail for $\text{\textquotedblleft}$defying unjust laws,” laws that had grown worse since the National Party came to power in 1948. Volunteers were jailed for failing to carry passes, violating the curfew on Africans, and entering locations and public facilities designated for one race only.

\

\

In early 1953, the government imposing [sic] stiff penalties for protesting discriminatory laws, including heavy fines and prison sentences of up to five years. It then enacted the Public Safety Act, allowing declaration of a State of Emergency to override existing laws and oversight by courts. Although the Defiance Campaign did not achieve its goals, it demonstrated large-scale and growing opposition to apartheid. Furthermore, the use of non-violent civil disobedience was part of an important international tradition - from the independence movement in Indian two decades before to sit-ins and other non-violent protests in the United States civil rights movement more than a decade later.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Sources C and D largely agree about the significance of the 1952 Defiance Campaign in South Africa.

Both sources agree that the campaign did not achieve its overarching goal of overturning apartheid ($\checkmark$ similarity). Source C says, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the campaign did not achieve the desired aim of overturning the apartheid laws” and Source D simply says the campaign $\text{\textquotedblleft}$did not achieve its goals” ($\checkmark$ evidence).

However, both sources also agree that the campaign was still significant in other ways ($\checkmark$ similarity). Both sources agree that the campaign was the first notable example of cooperative resistance to apartheid between race-based organizations. Source C says $\text{\textquotedblleft}$this period marked the beginning of non-racial co-operation in the resistance to apartheid” while Source D says the campaign was $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the first large-scale, multi-racial political mobilization against apartheid laws under a common leadership” ($\checkmark$ evidence).

From there, however, they diverge in their emphasis on the campaign's significance, emphasizing different successes ($\checkmark$difference ). Source C mentions two major impacts on the African National Congress including its increased membership from the campaign and that it signalled a move $\text{\textquotedblleft}$from moderation to militancy” ($\checkmark$ evidence) in the organization while Source D does not mention this. Instead, Source D emphasizes the campaign's significance as an example of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$international tradition” of “non-violent civil disobedience” along with the Indian independence movement and the US civil rights movement ($\checkmark$ evidence). 

Source D also mentions that the campaign drove an increased legal response from the South African government ($\checkmark$difference ) with the passage of the Public Safety Act, which would allow it to declare a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$State of Emergency to override existing laws and oversight by courts” ($\checkmark$ evidence) while Source C does not mention this.
