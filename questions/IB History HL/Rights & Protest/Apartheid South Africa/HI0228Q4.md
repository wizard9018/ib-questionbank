|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |Excerpt from $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) in exile” published in the academic website *South African History Online* in 2019.

:box[Initially\, South African liberation movements were committed to a policy of non-violence and constructive engagement with the government. The increasing use of security forces to brutally suppress resistance and torture those [who] fought against the government's racially biased [policies] convinced the ANC that [the] military option could succeed where non-violence had failed. Consequently, the armed wing of the ANC uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) was formed in 1961 jointly by the ANC and SACP to spearhead the armed struggle.

\

\

A positive development for the ANC was an increasing tide of Independent African states in the 1960s. Coupled with the growing influence of Pan Africanism and African nationalism in the foreign policy of these states. Most of them were committed to the liberation of Africa as a whole from colonial rule and apartheid. Thus, they supported the liberation struggle through the institutions such as the Organisation of African Unity. The ANC was supported by a number of African states which hosted ANC leaders fleeing from increasing repression by the Apartheid state. In some countries they were allowed to stay as [refugees] but not allowed to establish military bases while in others they could establish military training camps. From these countries MK operated and co-ordinated the liberation struggle abroad and in South Africa.

\

\

Though committed to the principle of supporting liberation movements in African colonies, efforts of some African countries were limited by their economic weakness and dependence on their former colonial masters. Aware of this weakness, South Africa adopted what it called a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$new policy" to entice African states into closer co-operation and friendly relations with her in return for financial aid, mainly in the form of technical aid. This was on condition that they did not support the ANC and MK activities within their borders. In instances where the desired outcomes by the South African government were not met, a deliberate policy of aggression was adopted. For instance the South African government embarked a deliberate policy of destabilisation by funding armed groups and assassination of political activists. Neighbouring countries like Botswana, Lesotho, Mozambique and Swaziland were especially vulnerable.

\

\

Despite these threats these countries and others such as Tanzania, Angola, Zambia and Zimbabwe played an important role in supporting the South African liberation struggle. Furthermore, the MK campaign abroad extended to other countries in Europe and the Soviet Union where MK cadres received military training.]

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Sources B and D agree that there was a wide, significant international response to the apartheid government and its policies ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$)

. Source B describes support for the anti-apartheid movement from the Organisation of African Unity and Source D mentioned that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$twenty-nine [UN] Member States” signed the resolution condemning the actions of the apartheid government ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, while the sources both mention the importance of international responses, they differ in the examples of these responses they emphasize with Source B primarily discussing the material and training support provided to the ANC by African allies while Source D only represents a diplomatic condemnation of the regime and its actions ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source B says that the ANC's African allies “hosted ANC leaders fleeing from increasing repression” and, in some cases, allowed the ANC to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$establish military training camps ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Source D, in contrast, is the official language of the UN's condemnation of the Sharpeville massacre saying that is “deplores the policies and actions” taken by the South African government, calling upon it to change its behavior in alignment with the “principles of the [UN] Charter” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

While both sources suggest a broad condemnation by the international community ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$), Source B specifies the countries like $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Tanzania, Angola, Zambia and Zimbabwe” that provided material support $\text{\textquotedblleft}$liberation struggle” and “the Soviet Union where MK cadres received military training ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source D simply mentions $\text{\textquotedblleft}$twenty-nine Member States” who signed the resolution and that the resolution was adopted by nine of the eleven members of the UN Security Council at the time (and specifically names France and the UK/Northern Ireland as having abstained from the vote) ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
