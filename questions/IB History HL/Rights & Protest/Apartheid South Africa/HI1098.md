# HI1098

Analyze the role of the Group Areas Act (1954) and the Native Resettlement Act (1954) in the implementation of “grand apartheid” measures.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any combination of the following:
- Reshaping South African society ensured that whites maintained unquestioned power.
- Assigned racial groups to distinct residential and commercial areas in urban regions as part of a system of urban apartheid (Group Areas Act).
- Exclude people of color from living in the most developed areas, which were restricted to Whites (Group Areas Act).
- Allowed the removal of Black people from areas within and near the magisterial district of Johannesburg by the South African government. (Native Resettlement Act).
- The forced removals under the Group Areas Act and Native Resettlement Act uprooted non-white communities, relocating them to deprived areas. This led to enduring social dislocation and persistent poverty.
- These laws provided the legal foundation for the apartheid government to systematically enforce spatial segregation in cities, setting a precedent for further discriminatory legislation and policies throughout the apartheid era.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Group Areas Act and the Native Resettlement Act of 1954 are considered part of the “grand apartheid” measures because they were designed to fundamentally reshape South African society by enforcing strict racial segregation. The Group Areas Act assigned racial groups to separate residential and business districts in urban areas, creating a system of urban apartheid that ensured whites would maintain unquestioned power in the most developed and economically prosperous locations ($\checkmark$).

The Natives Resettlement Act allowed the government to forcibly remove Black South Africans, enabling the displacement of these communities and strengthening white control over urban centers. These laws formalized urban apartheid, systematically marginalizing and disenfranchising non-white populations, and supporting the goal of maintaining white dominance, reflecting the core aims of “grand apartheid” ($\checkmark$). In addition, the implementation of the Group Areas Act and the Natives Resettlement Act had profound social and economic effects. Entire communities were uprooted, often with little to no compensation, and relocated to areas with fewer resources, limited infrastructure, and poor access to employment opportunities. The forced removals also caused overcrowding and poverty in the designated non-white areas ($\checkmark$).

These laws provided the legal basis for the apartheid government to systematically plan and carry out the spatial segregation of South African cities, establishing a precedent for additional discriminatory laws and policies throughout the apartheid era ($\checkmark$).
