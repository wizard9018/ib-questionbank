# HI0228Q5

Describe **two** examples of increasing repression and/or violence by the government of South Africa between 1960 and 1965.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - The Sharpeville Massacre (1960) and other examples of state violence/repression

     - March 21, 1960 - police opened fire on a crowd of initially unarmed protestors outside of Sharpeville Police Station in a suburb of Johannesburg. Reports vary about the numbers killed and injured, but most reports are at least 60 and at least 180, respectively.

     - The Pondoland Rebellion/Revolts (began as early as 1951, but escalated in June 1960) is another notable example of increasing state violence/repression.

 - The Unlawful Organizations Act (1960)

     - April 1960 - the South African government declared both the Pan Africanist Congress (PAC) and African National Congress (ANC) illegal, banning them. Shortly after, the Indemnity Act of 1961 protected the government and police from any legal action being taken against them connected to the violence of the Sharpeville Massacre.

 - The Sabotage Act (General Law Amendment Act No. 76 of 1962)

     - June 1962 - lessened the burden of proof on those accused of sabotage and presumed guilty (needed to prove their innocence) and widened the definition of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$sabotage” to include strikes and other crimes typically seen as petty, such as writing graffiti/messages on the walls of buildings. The law also implemented a minimum sentence of five years and a maximum sentence of execution by hanging.

 - The 90-Day Detention Law (General Law Amendment Act, number 37 of 1963)

     - May 1963 - allowed police to detain people accused of political crimes almost indefinitely without access to an attorney as the initial 90-day detention could be immediately re-applied upon release. This law also added an element known as the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Sobukwe Clause” that would allow retroactive application of the law to individuals who had already been arrested/accused prior to the law’s existence. 

 - The Rivonia Trial, life sentences for ANC leadership (1963-1964)

     - October 1963-June 1964 - signalled a shift in the seriousness of the consequences for anti-apartheid action. While previous non-violent direct action campaigns had led to the arrests and detention of activists, the outcome of the Rivonia Trial for many of the accused was life imprisonment.

\

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The most notable example of increasing violence by the South African government between 1960 and 1965 was the Sharpeville Massacre ($\checkmark$). In March of 1960, police officers opened fire on a large, unarmed gathering of protestors outside the Shapeville Police Station, killing at least 60 people and injuring many more ($\checkmark$). To make matters worse, in the heightened tensions after the massacre, the South African government pushed through even more repressive legislation targeting the anti-apartheid movement ($\checkmark$). One notable example of this increasing repression was the Unlawful Organizations Act of 1960, which banned both the African National Congress (ANC) and Pan Africanist Congress (PAC,) who were forced to continue their work underground ($\checkmark$).
