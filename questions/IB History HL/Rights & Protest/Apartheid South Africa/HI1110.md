# HI1110

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source A for a historian studying the racial classification of apartheid's legislation.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpt from The Population Registration Act (Act No. 30 of 1950). The act was published and came into effect on July 7, 1950. |

[Source: Parliament of South Africa (1950, June). *Population Registration Act.* wikisource.org. Retrieved October 31, 2025, from https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Population_Registration_Act_1950. Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- As a primary source, the Population Registration Act is a piece of legislation enacted by the apartheid-era South African government in 1950.
- The Act aimed to categorize every South African into rigid racial groups with further subdivisions for “coloured” and “native” groups.
- Section 5 illustrates how race was not self-identified but imposed by the state, often arbitrarily and bureaucratically.

For limitations, any of the following:
- As an official apartheid-era law, the source reflects the perspective and intentions of the ruling National Party. It does not offer a neutral or critical view of the legislation.
- The Act was designed to enforce racial categorization, not to justify or explain the broader apartheid system. It omits the ideological motivations behind racial segregation and the political goals of the regime.
- The language is formal and administrative, which may obscure the social consequences of the law. It focuses on procedures rather than outcomes.
- The Act does not explain why these classifications were deemed necessary or how they would be used to enforce other apartheid laws.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
As a piece of official legislation enacted by the apartheid-era South African government in 1950, the Population Registration Act serves as a direct primary source. Its origin provides historians with unfiltered insight into the legal mechanisms used by the National Party to institutionalize racial segregation ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$).  The Act's explicit aim to classify all South Africans into racial categories such as “white,” “coloured,” or “native,” reveals the foundational role of racial categorization in the apartheid system. The further subdivisions within “coloured” and “native” groups underscore the regime's intent to fragment society along ethnic lines ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).  Section 5, which outlines how racial identity was determined by the state rather than self-identified, highlights the arbitrary and bureaucratic nature of apartheid enforcement, providing evidence of how racial identity was imposed by law, rather than based on lived experience or self-perception ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

As an official government document, the Act reflects the ideology and intentions of the ruling National Party. It does not offer a critical or oppositional view, nor does it acknowledge the human cost or moral implications of its policies ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).  The Act is concerned solely with the mechanics of racial classification. It does not explain the broader ideological motivations behind apartheid or how this classification system was used to enforce other discriminatory laws, such as those governing land ownership, education, or movement ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
