# HI0807

Discuss the perspectives around the ANC's decision to adopt the armed struggle in 1961.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Students should provide a discussion that considers both perspectives on the ANC's decision. To achieve full marks, students should include perspectives from both sides, including at least one point from each side, but a minimum of four points in total:

Perspective supporting the decision to adopt an armed struggle:
- Decades of non-violent resistance (including the Defiance Campaign and the 1956 Women's anti-pass protest) had failed to achieve meaningful change, proving peaceful methods were ineffective against the apartheid government
- The government's increasingly repressive policies throughout the 1950s had closed all legitimate political space for opposition
- The government's violent response to peaceful protests (for example, at Cato Manor in 1959 and Sharpeville in 1960) demonstrated it was willing to use lethal force against civilians
- The banning of the ANC and other political organizations in 1960 eliminated legal avenues for political activity, making underground resistance an inevitable option

Perspectives questioning the decision to adopt an armed struggle:
- The ANC had a history of non-violent protest and had built a reputation and following based on the principle of non-violent resistance. In fact, it was just three months before the launch of Umkhonto we Sizwe that the ANC’s President-General was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize based on this non-violent history
- Although the armed struggle aimed to avoid human casualties, there was a risk of escalating violence and potentially harming innocent civilians
- Non-violent resistance had not been fully exhausted, as other forms of peaceful protest could have been attempted
- The decision to adopt an armed struggle led to the government becoming more repressive, with increased security legislation including the Sabotage Act (1962) and the General Laws Amendment Act (1963), which gave police more power to arrest and detain opposition and imposed the death penalty for acts deemed as treason
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The ANC's decision to adopt armed struggle in 1961 can be understood from multiple perspectives. The adoption of the armed struggle came after decades of unsuccessful peaceful protest. Large protest actions, like the 1956 Defiance Campaign, had been unable to end apartheid laws ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, the government's violent response to peaceful demonstrations, notably the Sharpeville massacre in 1960, where 69 unarmed protesters were killed, and the banning of the ANC shortly thereafter, limited the options for effective peaceful protest ($\checkmark$).

On the other hand, the decision to adopt an armed struggle directly resulted in the government passing laws like the General Laws Amendment Act in 1963, which allowed for wider police powers to arrest opponents, further weakening resistance ($\checkmark$). The adoption of the armed struggle also ran counter to the image and philosophy that the ANC had cultivated since its inception, which had even led to the ANC's President-General being awarded the Nobel Peace Prize just three months before the start of the armed struggle ($\checkmark$).
