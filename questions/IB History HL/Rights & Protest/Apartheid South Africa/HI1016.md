# HI1016

According to Source C, what were the main differences between Afrikaans Churches and English-speaking Churches in their approach to Black participation?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** | $\hspace{3em}$ | This source is taken from the 1972 SPRO-CAS Church Commission Report, which examined how apartheid shaped church membership and racial divisions. It highlights why many Black Christians left mainstream churches and turned to independent African churches. |

[Source: Randall, P. (Ed.). ## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Afrikaans Churches created structures for separate Black participation
- English-speaking Churches failed to implement integration for Black members
- Afrikaans Churches had few breakaways, but many Blacks left English Churches for independent churches.
- Afrikaans Churches allowed some African culture, while English Churches offered almost none.
- Both struggled to attract Black members, but for different reasons: separation vs. unequal treatment.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
According to Source C, Afrikaans Churches “provided the structures for blacks to have… separate independence,” creating segregated but organised spaces for Black worship, while English-speaking Churches “failed to implement their official policy of integration” and did not give Black members equal participation, keeping Black members in unequal, subordinate roles ($\checkmark$).

The source also says there were “fewer separatist churches and sects splitting off” from Afrikaans Churches, but many Black Christians left English-speaking Churches to join independent churches where “self-respect and Africanisation” were priorities ($\checkmark$).

It also notes that Afrikaans Churches allowed “some degree of self-expression in their own cultural forms,” so African music, language or ritual could appear in a limited way, while English-speaking Churches offered almost no space for African culture and remained strongly European in style ($\checkmark$).
