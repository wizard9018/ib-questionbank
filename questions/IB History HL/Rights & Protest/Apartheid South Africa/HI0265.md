# HI0265

What, according to Source A, was the rationale behind apartheid? 

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | The following extract is from a speech titled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Case for Apartheid” made by A.L. Geyer, apartheid supporter and South African High Commissioner in Great Britain, to the Rotary Club in London in 1953. 

[Source: Geyer, A.L. (1953, August 19). *The Case for Apartheid*. Internet Modern History Sourcebook. Retrieved January 19, 2025 from https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/mod/1953geyer.asp. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - Apartheid is described as a means of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$self-preservation" for white South Africans, portrayed as a natural and necessary urge.

 - The source claims that apartheid would enable the Bantu to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$develop fully as a separate people" in their designated areas.

 - Whites are justified in maintaining political power because the Bantu are deemed $\text{\textquotedblleft}$immature" and not ready to govern.

 - The source references the Dutch Reformed Church's statement that no people would accept being excluded indefinitely from decision-making, implying a moral obligation to gradually give the Bantu limited autonomy.

 - The immediate aim is to enforce racial separation outside Bantu areas and improve living standards for the Bantu.

 - The long-term aim is to develop Bantu areas agriculturally and industrially, making them the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$national home" for the Bantu, where they could eventually gain autonomy.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

According to Source A, the rationale behind apartheid was framed as a policy of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$self-preservation" for white South Africans ($\checkmark$). The source also claims that apartheid would enable the Bantu people to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$develop fully as a separate people" in their designated areas, where they would progressively gain greater autonomy ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, it emphasises that political power should remain with whites due to the perceived $\text{\textquotedblleft}$immaturity" of the “Bantu population,” suggesting that apartheid was in their "best interest" ($\checkmark$).
