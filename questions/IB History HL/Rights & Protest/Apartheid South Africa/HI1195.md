# HI1195

Compare and contrast what Sources O and P reveal about the impacts of the Bantustan system.
[6 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source O** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Nelson Mandela's article on Verwoerd's proposal of Bantu self-government, published in Liberation magazine N° 36.. |

:box[There is no sovereignty. No autonomy. No democracy. No self-government. Nothing but a crude, empty fraud, to bluff the people, and to serve as a pretext for accumulating yet more hardships and injustices upon the African people. Politically, the talk about self-government for the reserves is a trick. Economically, it is an absurdity.\
\
The few scattered African reserves in the Union, comprising approximately 13 percent of the least desirable land, represent the last remnants of land ownership left to the African people in their original ancestral home. After the depredations of generations of Europeans, the outrageous Land Acts from 1913 onwards had turned the once free and independent Tswana, Sotho, Xhosa, Zulu, and other peasant farmers of this country into a nation of landless outcasts and roving beggars, humble "work-seekers" on the mines and the farms where yesterday they had been masters of the land. And now the new White masters of the country generously "presented" the few miserable areas that were left to remain as reservoirs and breeding-grounds for black labor. These are the reserves.\
\
The facts are that the reserves are congested, distressed areas, completely unable to sustain their present populations. People are on the verge of starvation. The White Paper [Verwoerd¨s proposal] discusses teaching Africans about soil conservation and agriculture. No amount of agricultural instruction will ever enable 13 percent of the land to sustain 66 percent of the population.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |
| :-- | :-- |
|Includes clear points of comparison| ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) |
|Includes clear points of contrast| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |
|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence** from the texts.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both state that forced removals and displacement stripped Africans of their fundamental rights.
- Both sources show how the system disrupted African life and identity.
- Both express that African ethnic groups became impoverished after losing their land, causing trauma and loss of their communities.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Source O focuses more on the political and economic impacts, while P focuses more on the social consequences.
- Source O focuses on the immediate impacts of the implementation of the system, while Source P, written much later, explores the long-term consequences of the Bantustan system
- Source O critiques the Bantustan system's impacts, while Source P takes a more neutral stance, emphasizing its practical consequences.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources reveal the damaging impacts of the Bantustan system ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). In Source O, Mandela says the system created “no sovereignty. No autonomy. No democracy. No self-government” and was a “crude, empty fraud” that only served to further white domination in South Africa. Source P says black “communities… were destroyed” while “White people made enormous profits” from the system ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, their specific emphases on the damage to black communities differ ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). In Source O, Mandela focuses primarily on economic and political disenfranchisement, saying black populations were forced to become "work-seekers" rather than land owners, and that they now simply supplied “black labor” rather than holding real economic power ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). In contrast, Source P only briefly mentions economic impacts ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$), describing the homelands as “under-resourced and overwhelmed” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) and instead focuses on the social and cultural destruction of black communities, saying the system gave “little opportunity [for black South Africans] to re-establish their former relationships” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Finally, the sources differ in the tone of their analyses ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source O, in the immediate wake of the policy and written by someone seeing the impacts directly, takes a passionate and condemnatory tone that reveals the moral implications of the system saying sarcastically that the Bantustands were “generously $\lq$presented'” by “the new White masters of the country,” and that they were “miserable areas” which simply served as “breeding-grounds for black labor.” Source P, as an academic source, takes a more neutral tone, describing the practical impacts of the system without explicitly condemning or criticizing it ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
