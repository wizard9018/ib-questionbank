# HI1011

Explain the contributions of **two** female leaders in the anti-apartheid movement during the 1950s and 1960s.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following (or others not mentioned):
- Winnie Mandela: Active in the, she mobilised women at the grassroots and became an enduring symbol of resistance, especially after Mandela's imprisonment, showing women's central role in sustaining the struggle.
- Ruth First was an investigative journalist who exposed apartheid abuses and gave voice to the oppressed. She was active in the SACP, supported the Freedom Charter, and was charged in the 1956 Treason Trial. In 1963 she was detained under the 90-Day Detention Law. As an academic scholar, she linked her research to activism, raising awareness both inside South Africa and internationally.
- Lilian Ngoyi: first woman elected to the ANC's National Executive Committee who helped bridge grassroots movements with national political organizations. Also a leader in FEDSAW.
- Helen Joseph: helped draft the Freedom Charter (1955) and played a key role in FEDSAW.
- Other FEDSAW leaders (e.g., Sophia Williams-De Bruyn, Rahima Moosa): Organised the 1956 Women's March, uniting 20,000 women of all races in a landmark act of defiance.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The leadership of Winnie Mandela showed the importance of women in sustaining the anti-apartheid struggle. She organised through the ANC Women's League, encouraging women to join resistance campaigns ($\checkmark$),
and later stood as a public symbol of resilience when Nelson Mandela was jailed, proving that women could lead from the front ($\checkmark$).

Similarly, Ruth First offered intellectual and activist leadership. Through her role as a journalist for New Age and as an academic, she revealed the injustices of apartheid, supported the Freedom Charter, and helped broaden the movement's vision ($\checkmark$). Her involvement in the 1956 Treason Trial and later detention in 1963 showed how central she was to the struggle ($\checkmark$).
