## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** or a combination of the following:
- The sign explicitly threatens violence against $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Natives, Indians & Coloureds," showing how brutality was used as a tool of racial control.
- The language is dehumanizing, depicting targeted groups as intruders whose lives are considered disposable.
- The fact that such signs could be publicly displayed indicates that White landowners had the right to use deadly force without consequences.
- The sign serves as both a literal and symbolic barrier, enforcing segregation through fear.
- This shows that apartheid was about creating fear and forcing submission.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
This source shows how racial violence was normalized during apartheid in South Africa by highlighting explicit threats of brutality like shooting on sight and releasing savage dogs against marginalized groups such as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Natives, Indians & Coloureds" ($\checkmark$). The dehumanizing language on the sign portrays these communities as unwanted intruders whose lives are considered disposable, reflecting an ideology that denied non-White South Africans any legal or moral protection ($\checkmark$).
