# HI0268

What does Source C suggest about the actions of the police during the Sharpeville Massacre?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | The following extract comes from an article written by a journalist, Humphrey Tyler, who was present during the Sharpeville Massacre in 1960. He was the assistant editor of the anti-apartheid publication Drum magazine at the time of the shooting. 

[Source: Tyler, H. (1960, March 21) *Eyewitness accounts of the Sharpeville Massacre 1960*. Retrieved January 18, 2025 from https://sahistory.org.za/article/eyewitness-accounts-sharpeville-massacre-1960. CC-BY-SA 4.0.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

The following:

 - The actions of the police were excessive and unjustified, challenging the police's claim of self-defence by highlighting the imbalance between the number of police who were injured by stones and the number of unarmed protestors who were shot by police. 

 - The source suggests that police actions were brutal, as they fired $\text{\textquotedblleft}$without warning” and continued shooting until “there was no living thing” in the compound

 - The source also suggests that the police force was dishonest in recounting its version of the events, with the journalist questioning the claim that the crowd was armed by reporting that he saw no weapons, and just personal belongings amongst the bodies. 

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source C suggests that the actions of the police during the Sharpeville Massacre were excessive and unjustified, contradicting the police's claim that they were acting in self-defence with the assertion that only three policemen had been injured with stones, while many more unarmed protestors had been shot. ($\checkmark$

). The source states that the police fired without warning and continued shooting until $\text{\textquotedblleft}$there was no living thing" in the compound, implying a deliberate and brutal attack ($\checkmark$

). It also challenges the police's claim of self-defence by highlighting that only three officers were hit by stones while over 200 protestors were shot, many of whom were unarmed ($\checkmark$ ).
