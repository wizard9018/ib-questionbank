# HI0266

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source A for an historian studying apartheid policies.

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | The following extract is from a speech titled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Case for Apartheid” made by A.L. Geyer, apartheid supporter and South African High Commissioner in Great Britain, to the Rotary Club in London in 1953. 

[Source: Geyer, A.L. (1953, August 19). *The Case for Apartheid*. Internet Modern History Sourcebook. Retrieved January 19, 2025 from https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/mod/1953geyer.asp. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:

 - The origin of the source provides a firsthand perspective of an apartheid supporter, giving insight into the mindset, justifications and rhetoric of proponents of apartheid in the 1950s

 - The purpose of the source is to defend apartheid policies,  revealing how apartheid supporters rationalised their policies and attempted to gain international acceptance.

 - The content of the source reveals the apartheid government’s immediate goals for racial segregation and paternalistic view toward black South Africans as "immature" and in need of guidance by the white minority.

 - The content reveals that the apartheid government used the Dutch Reformed Church's perspective that black South Africans would rise up against the system unless they received some autonomy as a justification for the homelands policy, which could be presented as a degree of that autonomy.

For limitations, any combination of the following: 

 - The origin of the source is a representative of the apartheid government in Britain, and therefore the source is unlikely to be critical of the National Party regime

 - The purpose of the source is to defend apartheid, persuade an international audience of its benefits, and gain sympathy therefore it downplays the regime's harsh realities.

 - The content of the source fails to acknowledge the perspective and experiences of oppression and exploitation faced by black, coloured and Indian South Africans.  

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The origin of the source, a speech by the South African High Commissioner in Britain, A.L. Geyer, is valuable as it provides insight into the views and justifications of apartheid supporters in the 1950s, reflecting their rhetoric and ideology ($\checkmark$ VO). However, as a propaganda tool created by an apartheid supporter, it is inherently one-sided and fails to provide the perspectives of those who opposed the system ($\checkmark$ LO). The purpose of the speech, aimed at defending apartheid to an international audience, is valuable because it shows how the South African government framed apartheid as a policy of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$self-preservation" to sanitise its image abroad ($\checkmark$ VP). A value of the content is that it provides examples of the justifications used for apartheid policies, the homelands policies in particular ($\checkmark$ VC).
