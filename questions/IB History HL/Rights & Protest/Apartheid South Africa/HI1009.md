# HI1009

Explain the significance of the Women's March on Pretoria in 1956
[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Mass protest: Around 20,000 women marched peacefully against pass laws, one of the largest demonstrations in South African history.
- Unity: Demonstrated solidarity across racial and class lines, showing the potential of mass non-racial resistance.
- Women's leadership: Highlighted the role of women such as Lilian Ngoyi, Rahima Moosa, Sophia Williams-De Bruyn, and Helen Joseph.
- Challenged stereotypes: Undermined the view that women were passive or politically uninvolved.
- National symbol: Became an iconic moment of resistance, remembered annually as Women's Day (9 August).
- Grassroots power: Reflected successful local organising by the Federation of South African Women (FEDSAW).
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Women's March of 1956 was significant because it showed unity across racial and class lines. Women from different communities came together to protest the pass laws, proving that non-racial cooperation was possible in the fight against apartheid ($\checkmark$).

It also challenged stereotypes that women were passive in politics. By taking a leading role in such a large protest, women demonstrated that they could be powerful organisers and leaders in the resistance movement ($\checkmark$).
