# HI1014

With reference to the origin, purpose and content, assess the value of source A for a historian studying resistance to apartheid pass laws.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  An extract from a speech delivered by Dora Tamana in Langa Township on January 4, 1953, during a protest against the new pass laws. Tamana was a member of the ANC Women’s League and later the Federation of South African Women (FSAW). |

[Source: South African History Online. (2015, May 13). Found in the article *The 1956 Women's March in Pretoria.* South African History Online. Retrieved February 10, 2025, from https://sahistory.org.za/article/1956-womens-march-pretoria  Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

Values:
- Because the source is a speech by Dora Tamana, an ANC Women's League member later active in the Federation of South African Women, it provides a direct voice from a female leader involved in early resistance to apartheid pass laws.
- The purpose of the speech, to mobilise African women and men in Langa township in 1953, shows how activists tried to inspire solidarity and direct action against the new pass regulations.
- The content of the speech, which describes unemployment, lack of housing, family break-ups, and imprisonment caused by the passes, also demonstrates how women framed the laws as destructive to family life.

Limitations:
- Because the source is a public political speech, it is inherently biased towards the opposition and does not provide a balanced perspective.
- The purpose was to inspire resistance, so it emphasises only the negative effects of the passes without acknowledging any government justification or wider social context.
- The content reflects one leader's passionate appeal, but may not capture the diversity of experiences among African women, nor the views of men or other community groups.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
This source is valuable because its origin is a speech by Dora Tamana at a protest in Langa in 1953, showing the views of a woman leader in the ANC Women's League who was actively resisting and organizing resistance to the pass laws at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). Tamana says, “we women will never carry these passes,” suggesting that the laws were impacting all sectors of society and inspiring resistance within them ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The content highlights the damage of pass laws, mentioning “unemployment, lack of accommodation and families broken,” which helps us understand why women opposed them ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). However, as a rally speech, it is one-sided and leaves out the government's perspective ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).
