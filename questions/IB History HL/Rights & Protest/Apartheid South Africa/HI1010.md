# HI1010

Describe **three** ways women's organisations strengthened grassroots resistance in the 1950s
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Mass mobilisation and protest:
    - The Federation of South African Women (FEDSAW) united women across racial and class lines.
    - Organised the 1956 Women's March, with 20,000 women protesting against pass laws, one of the largest demonstrations of the era.
- Local and community organising:
    - Women worked street by street in townships, linking local struggles to national campaigns.
    - Mobilised through trade unions and neighbourhood networks.
- Broadening the anti-apartheid struggle:
    - Framed resistance as both a fight for racial equality and for gender rights, giving the movement wider legitimacy.
    - Challenged stereotypes that women were passive or politically uninvolved.
- Support structures and alliances:
    - The Black Sash, led mainly by white women, supported grassroots struggles with vigils, petitions, and legal aid centres.
    - Provided moral visibility and practical assistance for Africans targeted by apartheid laws.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Women played a central role by organising at grassroots level, connecting people in their streets and workplaces so that local actions could strengthen larger campaigns ($\checkmark$). They also reshaped the resistance, insisting it included women's rights and overturning the belief that women should stay out of politics ($\checkmark$). Meanwhile, the Black Sash, an organisation for liberal white women, showed solidarity through vigils and petitions and gave Black South Africans legal advice, offering both moral pressure on the state and concrete assistance to those under apartheid laws ($\checkmark$).
