# HI0228Q3

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source A for an historian studying the anti-apartheid movement.

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Nelson Mandela's statement at the opening of the defense case in the Rivonia Trial (Pretoria Supreme Court, April 20, 1964).

:box[I have already mentioned that I was one of the persons who helped to form Umkhonto. I, and the others who started the organization, did so for two reasons. Firstly, we believed that as a result of Government policy, violence by the African people had become inevitable, and that unless responsible leadership was given to canalize [direct] and control the feelings of our people, there would be outbreaks of terrorism which would produce an intensity of bitterness and hostility between the various races of this country which is not produced even by war. Secondly, we felt that without violence there would be no way open to the African people to succeed in their struggle against the principle of white supremacy. All lawful modes of expressing opposition to this principle had been closed by legislation, and we were placed in a position in which we had either to accept a permanent state of inferiority, or to defy the Government. We chose to defy the law. We first broke the law in a way which avoided any recourse to violence; when this form was legislated against, and then the Government resorted to a show of force to crush opposition to its policies, only then did we decide to answer violence with violence.

\

\

But the violence which we chose to adopt was not terrorism. We who formed Umkhonto were all members of the African National Congress, and had behind us the ANC tradition of non-violence and negotiation as a means of solving political disputes. We believe that South Africa belongs to all the people who live in it, and not to one group, be it black or white. We did not want an interracial war, and tried to avoid it to the last minute. If the Court is in doubt about this, it will be seen that the whole history of our organization bears out what I have said, and what I will subsequently say, when I describe the tactics which Umkhonto decided to adopt. (...)]

[Source: Mandela, N. (1964, April 20). *I Am Prepared to Die*. Pretoria Supreme Court. Retrieved March 11, 2025 from https://law2.umkc.edu/faculty/projects/ftrials/mandela/mandelaspeech.html. Public domain. ]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following: 

 - The source provides insight as a firsthand account from Nelson Mandela, a significant anti-apartheid activist and a founder of Umkhonto we Sizwe.

 - The source provides details about the motivations for the strategic shift from non-violence to violence in anti-apartheid resistance.

 - The content of the source sheds light on the details of the more controlled form of violence the MK pursued in the broader struggle.

For limitations, any combination of the following: 

 - The source is from Mandela's courtroom testimony after his arrest in 1964 and thus cannot provide further insight into the anti-apartheid movement after that date.

 - Mandela seeks to justify the turn to violence and downplay the accusations of terrorism, thus framing the decision as necessary and omitting details that might contradict that framing.

 - The source lacks the perspective of other anti-apartheid groups with both more and less violent methods of resistance or the international response to the decision. It also does not fully address the consequences of violent resistance.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source A is valuable to an historian studying the anti-apartheid movement because it represents a contemporary accounting of Nelson Mandela's perspective as the most prominent anti-apartheid leader ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$)

. The purpose of the source is also valuable to the study of the anti-apartheid movement because Mandela seeks to explicitly state the reasoning for the shift from non-violence to violence in the resistance and the tactics that the MK used ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).

One limitation of the source's origin, however, is that it comes from 1964 just before Mandela was imprisoned and thus cannot provide insight into the continuation of the anti-apartheid movement after his (and many other prominent leaders' arrests) ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). Furthermore, as this statement is made at the beginning of a defense trial, its purpose is somewhat limited as Mandela seeks to justify the turn to violence and reframe their actions not as terrorism, but rather as necessity, downplaying the consequences of their methods ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). Finally, the source's content is limited as it only provides the perspective of Mandela, a leader of the ANC/MK and cannot include the perspectives or actions of other anti-apartheid organizations, especially those that chose to maintain non-violent resistance ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
