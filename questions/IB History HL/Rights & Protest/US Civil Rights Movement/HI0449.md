# HI0449

Describe the significance of the Brown v. Board of Education decision of 1954.

[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any combination of **three** of the following:

 - Overturned the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$separate but equal” doctrine enshrined by the 1896 Plessy v Ferguson Supreme Court decision on the grounds that it was a violation of the Equal Protection clause of the 14th amendment

 - Outlawed segregation in public schools in the US, aiming to ensure black students had the same educational opportunities as white students

 - Signalled a major victory for the US Civil Rights movement and catalyzed further actions to fight for racial equality in the US

 - Provided a legal basis to challenge other forms of institutional racism and segregation in the United States using the 14th amendment

 - Argued that desegregation was not just a legal, educational or practical issue, but a moral one as well, citing the negative social impacts of segregated schools on black children

 - Sparked resistance to the decision in communities across the US including notable examples such as the challenge integration faced in Little Rock, Arkansas in 1957

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka, Kansas US Supreme Court decision of 1954 ended the long-standing concept of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$separate but equal” facilities in the United States that had been established nearly 60 years early by the Plessy v. Ferguson Supreme Court decision in 1896 ($\checkmark$). The decision served as a significant boon to the broader Civil Rights movement and encouraged further legal challenges to segregation such as the NAACP's work to desegregate public transportation through the Browder v. Gayle decision in 1956 ($\checkmark$). However, this decision was highly controversial and also sparked substantial resistance such as Virginia Senator Harry F. Byrd's $\text{\textquotedblleft}$massive resistance” laws that sought to prevent the integration of Virginia's public schools ($\checkmark$).
