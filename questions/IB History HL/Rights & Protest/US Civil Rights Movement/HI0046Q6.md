# HI0046Q6

Describe the significance of President Lyndon B Johnson to the civil rights movement in the United States.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any combination of **three** of the following:
- President Johnson signed key civil rights legislation including the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.
- Johnson created a broader domestic legislative agenda known as the "Great Society" which aimed to reduce poverty and inequality, which disproportionately affected African Americans.
- Key pieces of legislation within the Great Society which addressed social and racial inequality  included: the laws focused on the “War on Poverty” such as the Economic Opportunity Act of 1964, Medicare and Medicaid (1965) and the Food Stamp Act (1964) as well as the Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965) and the Higher Education Act (1965).
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
President Johnson pushed for and signed the two most important pieces of civil rights legislation of the period, the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965 ($\checkmark$). Along with those two key laws, Johnson also worked to implement his Great Society reforms ($\checkmark$) which included the creation of Medicaid to address the health inequalities for people living in poverty in the US ($\checkmark$).
