# HI1088

Outline **four** reasons why Birmingham was targeted as a place for civil rights activism in 1963.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following:
- Though the city's population was 40% black, there were no black police officers, firefights, sales clerks, bus drivers or store cashiers
- Only 10% of the city's black population was registered to vote in 1960
- Jobs available to African Americans were limited to manual labour, yard work or work in black neighbourhoods
- The unemployment rate for blacks was more than twice that for whites
- The average income for black employees in the city was half that for white employees
- Racial segregation of public and commercial facilities was legally required and rigidly enforced
- 50 unsolved racially motivated bombings between 1945 and 1962 had earned the city the nickname Bombingham
- Economic deprivation as the city shifted from white to blue collar work meant that white workers were afraid of black workers taking their limited employment opportunities if equal employment rights were enforced
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Birmingham was an extremely segregated city and an ideal place for civil rights activism, as according to voter registration in 1960, only 10% of the city's African American population could vote ($\checkmark$). The city was suffering from economic challenges as white collar work declined and white citizens were afraid that African Americans might take their jobs  ($\checkmark$). The unemployment rate was twice as high for African Americans as for whites in the city, so there was an apparent injustice that could be tackled ($\checkmark$). The city followed a strong policy of racial segregation in all public and commercial facilities ($\checkmark$).
