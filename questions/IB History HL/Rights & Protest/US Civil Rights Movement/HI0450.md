# HI0450

Define the term $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Jim Crow law” and provide **two** examples.

[1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For $\text{\textquotedblleft}$define," the following:

 - State and/or local laws common in the southern United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries which mandated and enforced racial segregation in a variety of aspects of life.

For $\text{\textquotedblleft}$example," any combination of the following OR other valid examples:

 - Public facilities and transportation including schools, railroads, buses/bus stations, hospitals, waiting rooms, parks, restrooms and water fountains

 - Privately owned restaurants - states and localities made it illegal for restaurants to serve black and white customers in the same spaces

 - Voting access and political participation including unequal application of literacy tests, disproportionate impacts of poll taxes and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$grandfather clauses” that outlawed voting unless the individual was a descendant of an eligible voter

 - Marriage and family including restrictions on interracial marriage and adoption

 - Employment restrictions that prevented blacks and whites from working in the same facilities or parts of the same facilities (e.g. segregated hospitals and/hospital wards)

 - Entertainment services including theaters that maintained separate seating areas for black and white patrons

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Jim Crow laws were laws in southern states of the US that required segregation between races in many different day-to-day activities ($\checkmark$). 

One common example of Jim Crow laws was segregated transportation systems including city buses, interstate buses and railroads (as well as their waiting rooms). Buses were often internally segregated with black and white areas while railroads had separate cars for black and white passengers ($\checkmark$).

Another example of Jim Crow laws were those that prevented full political participation by black residents such as poll taxes. While outwardly they were applied equally as black and white residents were both required to pay the fee, they disproportionately limited black voter participation because, on average, black residents tended to have much lower incomes than white residents ($\checkmark$).
