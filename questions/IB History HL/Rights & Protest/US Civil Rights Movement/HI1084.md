# HI1084

Explain how voting rights for African Americans were limited in Southern United States after the Civil War.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following plus the corresponding evidence/explanation:
- The grandfather clause was created to allow poor whites to vote despite other restrictions that had been created.
    - The clause stated that anyone whose grandfather or great-grandfather had been eligible to vote was also able to vote. This ensured that whites could continue to vote, but blacks were excluded.
- Widespread tampering with votes.
    - African-American votes were marginalised by the stuffing of ballot boxes or the removal of African-American votes. At times, African-American votes were even recast into boxes of white candidates.
- Implementation of poll taxes
    - The poll tax ensured that poor African-American citizens could not actually place their vote, as they were unable to afford the tax
- Implementation of literacy tests
    - As many African-American citizens remained illiterate the introduction of a literacy test excluded them from the vote. Biased voting officials also discounted African-American citizens who could read from voting.
- The actions of the US Supreme Court hindered African-American voting rights
    - The decision of the Supreme Court in the US v. Reese decision of 1875 limited the federal government’s ability to enforce proper voting practices in states
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Practices were introduced designed to limit African-American voting rights, such as poll taxes ($\checkmark$). These had to be paid for a person to be eligible to place their vote, and as many African-American citizens remained impoverished, this was an effective hindrance to their voting ($\checkmark$). Similarly, literacy tests were introduced for citizens to be able to vote ($\checkmark$). This hindered African-American citizens, as around half were illiterate, and even those who could read were often denied the right to vote by biased electoral officials ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, the introduction of a Grandfather Clause limited African American voting rights ($\checkmark$). This clause gave the right to vote to anyone whose grandfather or great-grandfather had had the right to vote, which enabled poor whites to bypass restrictions against voting that might have applied to them (such as literacy tests), whilst keeping African Americans unable to vote ($\checkmark$).
