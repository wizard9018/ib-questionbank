# HI0951

Evaluate the social and economic effects of Jim Crow laws on African Americans in the 1950s.
[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following with an evaluation. Responses may include two points for each side (2/2), or be weighted (3/1 or 1/3), as long as both social and economic effects are addressed.

Social effects:
- Legally enforced racial segregation in all public facilities.
- Systemic humiliation and exclusion from mainstream society.
- Barriers to quality education, health care, and public services.

Economic effects:
- Limited access to skilled jobs or unionized labor.
- African Americans were often confined to menial, low-paying work.
- Denied equal access to home loans, property ownership, and economic mobility (e.g., redlining).
\
[4 marks]

Evaluation:
- Jim Crow laws entrenched a racial caste system, reinforcing white supremacy.
- Although challenged by legal and grassroots activism, the legacy persisted beyond formal repeal.
\
[1 marks]
\
[5 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Socially, the Jim Crow Laws created a system of exclusion and humiliation, where African Americans were denied access to the same facilities as white citizens and forced into a separate and inferior public existence ($\checkmark$). This legalised inequality reinforced the notion of Black inferiority and discouraged social mobility ($\checkmark$).

Economically, Jim Crow laws limited African Americans' opportunities for advancement. They were routinely excluded from skilled jobs and often confined to low-paying, insecure employment ($\checkmark$). Discriminatory housing policies and redlining practices restricted where African Americans could live and blocked access to home ownership, one of the main pathways to building wealth ($\checkmark$).

While these laws were increasingly challenged by legal activism and grassroots protest in the 1950s, their effects remained deeply entrenched. Overall, Jim Crow laws played a central role in preserving systemic racism and ensuring that African Americans remained socially and economically disadvantaged, even as the civil rights movement began to gain momentum ($\checkmark$).
