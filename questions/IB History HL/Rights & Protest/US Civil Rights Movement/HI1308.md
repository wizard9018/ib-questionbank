# HI1308

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for an historian studying the 1961 Freedom Rides.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Statement by the President John F. Kennedy concerning Interference With the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Freedom Riders" in Alabama, 1961. |

[Source: Kennedy, J. F. (1961, May 20). *Statement by the President concerning interference with the “Freedom Riders” in Alabama.* The American Presidency Project. ## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
**A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:**
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

**For value, any of the following:**

Origin (President John F. Kennedy, 1961)
- Statement issued by the sitting U.S. President gives insight into official federal attitudes during the Freedom Rides.
- Contemporary source created during the events, making it valuable as a first-hand governmental response.
- Reflects the perspective of the highest level of federal authority, useful for understanding national policy-making.

Purpose (public appeal for order, federal reassurance)
- Intended to calm public tensions and assert federal responsibility, showing how the government sought to manage the crisis.
- Reveals federal attempts to pressure state officials into preventing violence, showing the dynamic between federal and state powers.
- Provides insight into how the administration wanted its actions perceived domestically and internationally during the Cold War.
- Helps historians understand what the federal government was publicly willing to say, indicating political priorities and sensitivities.

Content (description of disorder, call for restraint)
- Acknowledges “outbreaks of violence,” showing recognition of the dangers faced by Freedom Riders.
- Demonstrates federal concern and the limits of local enforcement, highlighting systemic challenges to civil rights.
- Shows the government urging both sides not to “provoke further outbreaks,” indicating the tension between maintaining order and supporting civil rights activism.
- Provides evidence of the moderate, cautious federal stance, useful for analysing federal reluctance and gradual intervention.

**For limitations, any of the following:**

Origin (official government position)
- Represents the executive branch only, not the views of activists, local authorities, or segregationist groups.
- As a public statement, it reflects politically calculated language, limiting its frankness about federal anxieties or shortcomings.

Purpose (to manage crisis and maintain stability)
- Intended to reassure rather than reveal internal debates or failures, so it may downplay federal inaction.
- Purposefully avoids blaming the federal government, making it less reliable for understanding federal reluctance before intervention.
- Crafted to avoid alienating Southern states, so it may soften criticism of segregationist authorities.

Content (selective and constrained)
- Focuses on “law and order” rather than the moral dimensions of segregation or the broader goals of the Freedom Riders.
- Does not acknowledge the systemic nature of Southern resistance, so the depth of the challenge is understated.
- Omits reference to violence by segregationist mobs or police complicity, limiting its usefulness for understanding local hostility.
- Provides no insight into activist motivations, strategy, or lived experience.
- Presents the federal government as acting decisively (“take all necessary steps”), which historians know was partially rhetorical at this point.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source has value because its origin as a public statement by President John F. Kennedy in May 1961 provides a contemporary federal perspective on the Freedom Rides during the crisis itself ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$).  Its purpose, to calm tensions and urge state authorities to prevent further violence, reveals how the federal government publicly framed events as issues of “law and order,” which is useful for understanding how Washington sought to manage the situation ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).

However, the source also has limitations. The origin as an official presidential statement means it reflects a politically cautious and diplomatic stance, not the full range of debates within the administration ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).  Furthermore, the content is selective, focusing on appeals for restraint rather than describing the severity of mob violence or police complicity, which restricts its usefulness for understanding the full extent of the challenges faced by non-violent protesters ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
