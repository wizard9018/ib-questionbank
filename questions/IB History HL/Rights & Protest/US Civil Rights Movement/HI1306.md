# HI1306

What, according to Source D, were the responses to the Freedom Riders' non-violent protest by local authorities?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source D** | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpt from an article titled “Freedom Riders pose a problem for the South.” Published in *The Daily News*, Jackson, Mississippi, 28 May 1961 (Associated Press). |

[Source: The Associated Press. (28 May 1961). *Freedom Riders Pose Problem for the South.* The Daily News, Jackson, Mississippi. Accessed via Trove. Retrieved January 24, 2026) https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/103082711. Public Domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Immediate and organised arrests of Freedom Riders (“quick,” “orderly,” “efficient”).
- A pre-planned system to detain riders on arrival (police wagon waiting).
- A refusal to change tactics, showing entrenched opposition to desegregation.
- A cycle of arrests and imprisonment, including transfers to Parchman penitentiary.
- A “stalemate,” indicating that protest did not shift state behaviour.
- A determination by white authorities to preserve segregation, despite the non-violent nature of the protest.
- A coordinated, large-scale effort to contain or suppress the movement.
- Mississippi authorities adopted a different enforcement tactic compared to the violent mob attacks in Alabama. Instead of mob violence, Mississippi used swift, systematic arrests to contain the Riders.
- This suggests segregationists in Mississippi preferred legalistic suppression to uncontrolled violence. However, this was not a softening of resistance, just a different method of maintaining segregation.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The repeated arrests and the description of a “stalemate” suggest that Mississippi authorities responded to the Freedom Riders' non-violent actions with consistent and organised resistance, rather than attempts to negotiate or change segregation policies ($\checkmark$). The article also describes how riders were “quickly” and “efficiently” arrested each time they arrived, implying a systematic effort to neutralise the protest ($\checkmark$). Finally, the source also implies that segregationist authorities in Mississippi relied on efficient legal suppression, mass arrests, rather than mob violence, suggesting a different tactical approach but not a reduction in opposition ($\checkmark$).
