## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |

|Includes clear points of contrast| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |
|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

Points of comparison and contrast must **both** be included to reach the 5-6 markband.

For **comparison**, any combination of the following similarities:
-   Both sources mention the creation of the MFDP and its goal to challenge the all-white Democratic Party of Mississippi at the Democratic National Convention.
-   Both sources mention the creation of the “Freedom Schools”, the reason they were needed, their goals and their connection to the Head Start program.
-   Both sources mention voter registration as a focus of the campaign.
-   Both sources mention the national attention drawn by the campaign.

For **contrast**, any combination of the following differences:
-   Source D mentions both the successes and failures of the MFDP whereas Source C simply mentions its creation.
-   Sources C and D disagree on the quantities of Freedom Schools and their student numbers. Source C says that 40 Freedom Schools served 2000 students whereas Source D says that 30 Freedom Schools served 3000 students.
-   Source C cites specific quantities of attempts to register voters (17000) and their frequent refusal whereas Source D simply states that the drives drew attention to black disenfranchisement.
-   Source D mentions the role the Freedom Summer played in the passage of the Voting Rights Act of 1965 whereas Source C makes no mention of it.
-   Source C mentions the violent backlash against the Freedom Summer whereas Source D makes no mention of it beyond its title.

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Both Sources C and D mention the creation of Freedom Schools and voter registration campaigns as significant impacts of the Freedom Summer ($\checkmark \text{similarity}$). About Freedom Schools, Source C mentions that “40 Freedom Schools… served 2000 students, including adults who sought literacy education” and Source D says that the Freedom Schools offered “black history, the philosophy of the Civil Rights Movement and leadership development in addition to remedial instruction in reading and arithmetic.” ($\checkmark \text{evidence}$) One minor contrast on this point is that the sources disagree about the quantities of Freedom Schools and their number of students ($\checkmark \text{difference}$). Source C says that 40 Freedom Schools served 2000 students whereas Source D says that 30 Freedom Schools served 3000 students ($\checkmark \text{evidence}$).

Both Sources C and D mention the creation of the MFDP to challenge the traditional, all-white Democratic Party of Mississippi ($\checkmark \text{similarity}$), but Source D also goes further to discuss the successes and failures of the MFDP whereas Source C only mentions its creation ($\checkmark \text{difference}$).

Finally, two major contrasts between Sources C and D is their discussion of the overall outcomes of the Freedom Summer and their emphasis on the violent backlash ($\checkmark \text{difference}$). Source D credits the Freedom Summer in the eventual passage of the Voting Rights Act in 1965 while Source C makes no mention of this ($\checkmark \text{evidence}$). On the contrary, Source C explicitly discusses the violent response by the local community to the Freedom Summer while Source D makes no clear comment on this besides its title “Three CORE members murdered in Mississippi.” ($\checkmark \text{difference and \checkmark\ evidence}$ )
