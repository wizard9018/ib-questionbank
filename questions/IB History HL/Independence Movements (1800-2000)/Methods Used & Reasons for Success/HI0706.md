# HI0706

According to Source B, what was the impact of Gandhi's campaign against the British government's monopoly of salt?
[3 marks]

$\\$

[Source: Goshal, K.. (1944). *The People of India.* Sheridan House (New York). Retrieved April 18, 2025 from https://hdl.handle.net/2027/mdp.39015027043804. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The people enthusiastically responded by boycotting and picketing legal salt stores, and manufacturing illegal salt. There were strikes, mass demonstrations and refusal to pay rent. Women played an important part in these demonstrations.
- The armory in Chittagong was raided by a crowd of young people.
- Towns such as Peshawar and Sholapur were controlled solely by the people for many days.
- The Hindu soldiers of the 18th Garhwali Rifles refused orders to fire on a crowd of predominantly Muslim agitators in Peshawar and Peshawar remained under the control of the people from April 25th to May 4th as the British withdrew all military police from the city.
- Gandhi was  arrested on May 5th and the Congress and its subsidiary organizations were declared illegal. This increased public protests and demonstrations.
- The Viceroy issued a number of ordinances that created a martial law like situation and the boycott impacted British business.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Gandhi's campaign against the British government's monopoly of salt manufacturing and sale found huge support from the people who proceeded to boycott and picket stores selling salt and also proceeded to manufacture salt themselves; an act that was illegal. Women were a major part of these demonstrations ($\checkmark$). The British also faced the threat of mutiny among the Indian soldiers in their army when Hindu soldiers of the 18th Garhwali Rifles refused orders to fire on a predominantly Muslim crowd of agitators. The British lost control of the city and withdrew all military police, leaving the city to be controlled by the people from April 25th to May 4th ($\checkmark$). Finally, the source suggests that after the British arrested Gandhi and declared the Congress and its subsidiary organisations illegal, public protests and demonstrations increased ($\checkmark$).
