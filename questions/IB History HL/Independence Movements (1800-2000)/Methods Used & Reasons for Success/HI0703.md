# HI0703

Outline the reasons for and impact of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Quit India" Movement on the Indian struggle for independence. 

[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For reasons, any **two** of the following:

 - In September 1939, Viceroy Linlithgow of India declared that India was at war with Germany without consulting Indian politicians. The provincial Congress governments resigned in protest. 

 - The British Prime Minister, Churchill sent the Cripps Mission to India in March 1942 to negotiate with the nationalist leaders in an attempt at conciliation, as the Japanese threatened India's eastern borders. Cripps made a commitment to grant India independence after the war was over. Congress rejected the offer that Gandhi referred to as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$a post-dated cheque on a failing bank". 

 - In August 1942, the Congress adopted the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Quit India" resolution drafted by Gandhi and called for a mass campaign of civil disobedience to force Britain to leave India. 

\

[2 marks]

For impacts, any **three** of the following:

 - The British imprisoned Congress leaders including Gandhi and Nehru. Closed Congress offices and printing press. 

 - In the absence of the major Congress leaders, the movement continued and included many acts of violence. Strikes by factory workers, attacks on police stations, rural uprisings and acts of sabotage that targeted railway lines and bridges all occurred during the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$August Revolution". 

 - The British suppression was ruthless, with the imposition of martial law in areas, machine guns used on demonstrators and entire villages destroyed. 1000 people were killed, 3000 wounded and 100000 Indians detained without trial. 

 - While the uprising was controlled in six weeks, the demonstration of grass-roots activism played an important role in the British decision to leave India at the end of the war.  

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Quit India Movement was launched by the Indian National Congress in August 1942 and called for a mass campaign of civil disobedience to force Britain to leave India ($\checkmark$). This followed the declaration by the Viceroy in September 1939 of Indian participation in the Second World War, which was made without consulting Indian politicians. The provincial Congress governments resigned in protest and declared their lack of support for the British war effort ($\checkmark$). 

The British responded by arresting Congress leaders, including Gandhi and Nehru. They also closed Congress offices and the printing press. In the absence of the major Congress leaders, the movement continued and included acts of violence with strikes by factory workers, attacks on police stations, rural uprisings and acts of sabotage that targeted railway lines and bridges that all occurred during the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$August Revolution" ($\checkmark$). The British responded with the imposition of martial law, detaining Indians without trial, using machine guns on demonstrators, and destroying entire villages ($\checkmark$). 

Despite the British bringing the Quit India movement under control in six weeks, it played an important role in their eventual decision to leave India at the end of the war. The mass demonstrations that continued despite the imprisonment of major leaders were a clear indication of grassroots activism that would now make it impossible for the British to suppress the nationalist movement and retain control of the country. This would prove an important consideration in their eventual decision to withdraw from the country in 1947 ($\checkmark$).
