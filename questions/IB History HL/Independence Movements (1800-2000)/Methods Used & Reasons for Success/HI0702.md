# HI0702

Describe the methods of the Civil Disobedience Campaign launched by the Indian National Congress between 1930 and 1934 and explain their impacts.

[1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For methods, the following or similar:

 - The lack of response from the British government to Gandhi's list of demands to the Viceroy which included the abolition of the salt tax and the government's monopoly on the production and sale of salt prompted Gandhi to launch his second satyagraha campaign with the Salt March in March 1930. 

 - Gandhi and a select group of 78 volunteers walked from his ashram in Ahmedabad to Dandi on the Gujarat coast. A journey of 400 kms covered by local and international press,where they were joined by thousands on the way. 

 - The symbolic action of picking up dried salt from the beach was a public breach of law and followed by people in other parts of the country where the boycott extended to alcohol and foreign cloth. 

\

[1 marks]

For impacts, any four of the following:

 - Gandhi was arrested before the next step of the campaign; the march to the Dharasana salt works near Dandi. The march to Dharasana in May 1930 resulted in nonviolent protestors being brutally attacked by Indian soldiers under British command and turned public opinion on the world stage against British rule in India. 

 - Gandhi's arrest led to nationwide strikes and riots resulting in the arrest of 90,000 people and many deaths. This further damaged the credibility of the British ability to govern India. 

 - Britain was alarmed by the economic impact of the campaign as foreign cloth imports had halved, there was reduced income from taxes on salt and alcohol and loss of land revenue. 

 - In March 1931, Gandhi and the British authorities reached an agreement and signed the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, with Gandhi suspending the Civil Disobedience campaign. 

 - Gandhi calls for a resumption of the campaign following the lack of progress at the Second Round Table Conference in London. However, the campaign failed to regain its earlier momentum as a result of increasingly repressive measures used by the British along with Gandhi's arrest on his arrival in India in January 1932. The campaign was formally called off in April 1934. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Gandhi launched the Civil Disobedience Campaign following a lack of response from the British government to his list of demands which included the abolition of the tax on salt along with the government's monopoly on the production and sale of salt. His second satyagraha campaign began with the Salt March in March 1930, when Gandhi and a group of 78 volunteers walked 400 kms from his ashram in Ahmedabad to Dandi on the Gujarat coast and proceeded to symbolically break the law prohibiting the production of salt. This was to be the start of an India wide campaign of opposition to British rule that extended to the boycott of alcohol and foreign cloth ($\checkmark$).

The British arrested Gandhi before the next step of the campaign - the march to the Dharasana salt works near Dandi. The march itself went ahead in May 1930 and the brutal attacks by Indian soldiers under British command, turned public opinion on the world stage against British rule in India ($\checkmark$). 

Gandhi's arrest led to nationwide strikes and riots put down by the British with force and mass arrests of 90000 Indians. These disturbances and deaths called into question the British ability to govern India ($\checkmark$). 

The economic impact of the campaign resulted in reduced income for the British as foreign cloth imports were halved and the British were alarmed by  the reduced income from taxes on salt and alcohol along with the loss of land revenue ($\checkmark$). 

Gandhi suspended the Civil Disobedience Movement in March 1931 following an agreement with the British and the signing of the Gandhi-Irwin Pact. The campaign was formally called off in April 1934, following Gandhi's arrest on his arrival in India following the failure of the Second Round Table Conference held in London ($\checkmark$).
