# HI0225

Explain **two** reasons why some black people decided to fight for the royalists against the patriots in the Latin American wars of independence.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:

 - They believed the Spanish Crown gave them a better chance to achieve their political ends.

    - A reason is some black people considered the authority of the Spanish Crown conducive to achieve their political ends. In previous years, communities of black freed people, called cimarrones, were working towards their political recognition utilizing their knowledge of the colonial legal framework. 

 - They believed the king of Spain would better protect them from their creole masters.

     - A reason some enslaved black people pledged loyalty to the king of Spain and opposed revolutionary insurgents is they saw the Crown as a protector from their creole masters. This is true, for example, in areas like Cali and Popayán in New Granada. More evidence of this can be seen in the 1789 slave law issued by Spain, which aimed to improve the living and working conditions of enslaved people. In contrast, Creole slave owners resisted these reforms, leading to the law's suspension in 1794 across much of the Spanish Caribbean.

 - They thought the Spanish Crown would grant them greater economic freedoms. 

     - Freedom was closely tied to aspirations for land ownership and recognition as members of society with rights. This pattern was evident not just in Popayán, New Granada, and the Caribbean, where enslaved people were often granted land or legal rights as a reward for their involvement in conflicts. In Esmeraldas, New Granada, enslaved workers reached agreements with royalist forces (aligned with the Spanish Crown), allowing them to retain control over mining operations in exchange for paying tribute.

 - Spain promised them freedom in exchange for their service.

     - A reason blacks supported the royalist faction was that Spanish authorities promised enslaved black people that if they fought for the royalist armies, they would be freed. This is the case, for example, in Popayán, where the governors promised enslaved people freedom and armed them to fight for the royalist side.

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One reason why some Black people decided to fight for the royalists was that Spanish authorities promised enslaved Black people freedom in exchange for their service in the royalist armies ($\checkmark$). This was the case, for example, in Popayán, New Granada, where the governor promised freedom to enslaved individuals and armed them to fight for the royalist cause. In such cases, the backlash from their Creole owners against the governor reinforced the belief that the revolutionary (patriot) faction was unlikely to grant them freedom ($\checkmark$). A second reason was economic. For black people, freedom was closely tied to aspirations for land ownership ($\checkmark$). This was evident not just only in New Granada, but in the Caribbean, where enslaved people were often granted land as a reward for their involvement in conflicts. In Esmeraldas, New Granada, enslaved workers reached agreements with royalist forces, allowing them to retain control over mining operations in exchange for paying tribute ($\checkmark$).
