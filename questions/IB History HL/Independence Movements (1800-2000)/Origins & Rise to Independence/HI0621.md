# HI0621

Describe **three** ways India's involvement in the First World War influenced the growth of Indian Nationalism.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Most nationalist leaders supported Britain's war effort and viewed substantial Indian involvement as an opportunity to press for greater independence. The leaders hoped that the considerable Indian support for Britain in the war would result in Britain conceding to their demand for self-government and working towards a Dominion status.
- The war caused a conflict of loyalties for Muslims in India as Britain was fighting the Ottoman Empire, whose Sultan was regarded as the Caliph of Sunni Islam, to which most Indian Muslims belonged. This resulted in opposition to the British amongst the Muslim community.
- Heavy wartime taxation and increased recruitment of soldiers caused dissatisfaction as the war continued. The war resulted in rising food prices and fuel shortages. The failure of the monsoons in large areas in 1918 resulted in drought, causing food shortages and famine. This coincided with the spread of the Spanish flu brought by the returning troops, which killed 12 to 13 million people within a year. These were factors that increased anger against continued British control.
- Indians realised how dependent Britain had been on their help to secure victory, as one and a half million Indians served in the British army. This was the largest volunteer army for the British Empire that fought on the Western Front in Gallipoli, Palestine, and North and East Africa. This increased their confidence that Britain would now work to meet the demands of the Indian nationalist movement for self-government to retain continued control of India.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
India's involvement in the First World War in support of the British war effort,  despite having the backing of most Indian nationalists, caused a conflict for Indian Muslims. As Britain was now fighting the Ottoman Empire, whose Sultan was the Caliph or leader of the Sunni sect of Islam to which most Indian Muslims belonged, opposition amongst Indian Muslims grew and fueled the nationalist movement ($\checkmark$).

Dissatisfaction among Indians increased as the war progressed due to heavy taxation imposed by the British as well as, at times, forced recruitment of soldiers. This anger and opposition to the British served to strengthen the nationalist movement ($\checkmark$).

Indian nationalists were further strengthened with the realisation that Britain's eventual victory relied, to a large extent, on Indian contribution. One and a half million Indians had served in the British army as the largest volunteer force and played a crucial role in the battles on the Western Front, Gallipoli, Palestine and North and East Africa. British dependence on Indian resources gave the nationalist movement confidence that they could now press for their demands to be accepted as they hoped the British would satisfy nationalist demands in order to retain control over the country ($\checkmark$).
