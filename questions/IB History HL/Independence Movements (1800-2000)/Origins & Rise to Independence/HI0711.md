# HI0711

Explain **two** impacts of the Amritsar Massacre of 1919 on the Nationalist movement in India. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- Increase in British suppression of dissent:
    - The massacre was followed by public flogging and punishments such as the ‘Crawling Order’, which forced any Indian that passed down the street where a British woman missionary had been attacked, to crawl on their stomachs.
- Conflicting official British viewpoints resulting in a decline of British legitimacy:
    - The Hunter Commission, appointed by the British government, censured Dyer's actions, forcing him to resign from the army. However, British officials in India and London expressed approval, and the House of Lords passed a motion approving his actions. This increased the anger in India and strengthened the nationalist movement because it was seen as a disregard for Indian lives.
- Increasing Nationalist support for complete independence
    - The news of the massacre and the British reaction to it caused widespread anger and support for the Congress, which demanded a call for an end to British rule rather than just self-governance. One of the Congress leaders who condemned the massacre was Mohandas Gandhi, who now emerged as a dominant figure in the nationalist movement.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Amritsar Massacre of 1919 was followed by an increased British suppression, particularly in Punjab. The British resorted to public floggings and other harsh punishments that aimed to humiliate, such as the $\lq$Crawling Act', that forced Indians to crawl on the stomachs to get to their homes located on a street that had witnessed an attack on a British woman. This served to stiffen resistance against British rule and further strengthened the Nationalist movement ($\checkmark$).

The massacre caused widespread revulsion not just in India but also in Britain. This led the British government to appoint the Hunter Commission that proceeded to censure General Dyer as a result of which he was forced to resign from the British army. However, the General found support amongst some British officials both in India and London including the House of Lords that passed a motion approving his actions; a signal of Britain's intentions to continue to retain control of India ($\checkmark$).
