# HI0627

Compare and contrast what Sources B and C reveal about the Partition of Bengal.
[6 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Bipin Chandra Pal, Indian nationalist, journalist and social reformer in his book, $\lq$The Spirit of Indian Nationalism'. Published by *The Hind Nationalist Agency* (London), 1910. |

:box[On the 16th of October, 1905, the old Province of Bengal was divided into two administrative units by Lord Curzon. The old unit comprised three provinces, Bengal proper, Behar, and Orissa. It was under one Lieutenant-Governor, who was assisted in legislative work by a Legislative Council, and in revenue work by a Board of Revenue, composed of the senior members of the Civil Service. The plea for a redistribution of the administrative area was that the burden of responsibility had become too heavy for one Lieutenant-Governor.\
\
But why should a mere redistribution of administrative area be so strongly resented by the people? The reason of it is that the people of Bengal saw in it an attempt to weaken the solidarity of the Bengalee-speaking people, and, by depriving Calcutta of the support of Eastern Bengal, to curb and cripple the increasing political strength and influence of Bengal.[...]the most strong objection, was that it struck at the sense of unity of the Bengalee race. The Eastern and Western districts had always formed an integral social, literary and commercial, as well as an integral political and administrative unit. This redistribution destroyed to some extent, or at least sought to destroy, this sense of national unity. It was interpreted as a strategic move, and the interpretation came from the general  policy of Lord Curzon, whose one aim was, it seemed to the thinking and intelligent classes in the country, to kill the growing political power of the educated classes.\
\
The proposal for thus dividing Bengal at once called forth an agitation, the like of which had never been seen before in British India. The classes and the masses stood shoulder to shoulder to protest against what they all regarded as a wanton outrage on their cherished sentiments. Mass meetings were held in every part of the province, and people came to these in their thousands and tens of thousands. Petitions were sent to the Government. The Hindus and the Mahomedans both joined together to support these. The protest, especially from Eastern Bengal, was absolutely unanimous.[...]\
\
When the final decision of the Government of India on the Partition of Bengal was published, people saw that their suspicions of the scheme were fully justified. In this document, Lord Curzon gave a new reason for it; and this was to institute a special Mahomedan Province in British India. In the eastern districts of Bengal there is a very great preponderance of Mahomedan populations. But still there never had been any Hindu-Mahomedan disturbance there within living memory. The two communities had always lived together in peace and amity.
]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources state that the partition of Bengal resulted in intense opposition and a wave of nationalist opposition.
    - Source B: $\lq$The proposal for thus dividing Bengal at once called forth an agitation, the like of which had never been seen before in British India.'
    - Source C:  $\lq$1905 marked the beginning of the first wave of nationalist activity in India.'
- Both sources state that Bengal was a strong centre of the nationalist movement.
    - Source B: $\lq$to curb and cripple the increasing political strength and influence of Bengal'
    - Source C: $\lq$Bengal was the center of revolutionary political activity in India.'
- Both sources state that the aim of the partition was to create an eastern province with a Muslim majority.
    - Source B: $\lq$this was to institute a special Mahomedan Province in British India'
    - Source C: $\lq$decision to create a new province in Eastern Bengal in which the Moslems. would form a clear majority of the population, was deeply resented.'

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Source B mentions the opposition to the partition came from both Hindus and the Muslims unlike Source C which states the opposition was led by the Hindus.
    - Source B: $\lq$Petitions were sent to the Government. The Hindus and the Mahomedans both joined together to support these.'
    - Source C: $\lq$was deeply resented by the educated middle class (predominantly Hindu) on the grounds that it was designed to weaken political activity and subject nationalist Hindus to Moslem control.'
- The sources also differ in their discussion regarding the nature of the agitation e.g. violence versus petitions and meetings.
    - Source C: $\lq$In protest, a boycott of foreign goods was proclaimed on August 7, 1905, and there was a rapid spread of revolutionary and terrorist activity in Bengal.'
    - Source B: $\lq$Mass meetings were held in every part of the province, and... Petitions were sent to the Government.
- Source C mentions that the plan for partition was devised by Lord Curzon but the implementation was left to his successor Lord Minto, while Source B only credits Lord Curzon with the partition of Bengal.
    - Source C: $\lq$the Partition of Bengal, a plan devised by Lord Curzon and carried out under his successor, Lord Minto.'
    - Source B: $\lq$the old Province of Bengal was divided into two administrative units by Lord Curzon.'
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
When comparing Sources B and C, both sources state  that the partition of Bengal resulted in a wave of nationalist opposition and aroused  intense opposition ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source B states $\lq$The proposal for thus dividing Bengal at once called forth an agitation, the like of which had never been seen before in British India while Source C mentions, $\lq$1905 marked the beginning of the first wave of nationalist activity in India' ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Both Sources also state that Bengal was a major centre of the nationalist movement ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). This is evident in Source B where the partition was said $\lq$to curb and cripple the increasing political strength and influence of Bengal' which is also reflected in Source C  that states, $\lq$Bengal was the center of revolutionary political activity in India' ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Both Sources also have points of contrast. Source B mentions that both Hindus and Muslims opposed partition unlike Source C which states the opposition was led by the Hindus ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). This is evident in Source B which states, $\lq$Petitions were sent to the Government. The Hindus and the Mahomedans both joined together to support these' while Source C states the partition, $\lq$was deeply resented by the educated middle class (predominantly Hindu) on the grounds that it was designed to weaken political activity and subject nationalist Hindus to Moslem control' ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Both sources differ in their discussion regarding the nature of the agitation ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source B mentions its revolutionary nature, $\lq$In protest, a boycott of foreign goods was proclaimed on August 7, 1905, and there was a rapid spread of revolutionary and terrorist activity in Bengal.' while Source C emphasises its moderate and disciplined nature, $\lq$Mass meetings were held in every part of the province, and... Petitions were sent to the Government ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Finally, in contrast, Source C mentions that the plan for partition was devised by Lord Curzon but the implementation was left to his successor Lord Minto, while Source B only credits Lord Curzon with the partition of Bengal. This is evident in Source C which states, 'the Partition of Bengal, a plan devised by Lord Curzon and carried out under his successor, Lord Minto.' while Source B only mentions,
$\lq$the old Province of Bengal was divided into two administrative units by Lord Curzon' ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
