# HI0713

Explain the impacts of **two** actions taken by the British government in India between 1918 and 1939 on the Nationalist Movement.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- $\underline{\textrm{Government of India Act 1919}}$ introduced a form of $\lq$dyarchy' (dual government), where some power over local affairs in agriculture, education, and health was given to Indian legislatures in the provinces, while the British retained firm control over the central government, police, justice system and retained the right to veto legislation. The Act also extended the right to vote for provincial legislatures to 10% of the adult male population based on property qualifications, and separate electorates for minorities were established.
    - The initial reaction from the moderate section of the nationalists was cooperation with the British regime, which seemed to be taking steps towards self-government. However, with the release of the Hunter Report into the Amritsar Massacre of 1919, together with the realisation that the British retained control as a result of the right to veto legislation, nationalist attitudes hardened with Gandhi and the Congress leaders urging voters to boycott the first elections of November 1920.
    - It reinforced separate identities and created divisions, encouraging communalism and further perpetuating a policy of $\lq$divide and rule'.
- $\underline{\textrm{The Simon Commission}}$ appointed in 1927, recommended further constitutional reforms. The commission did not include Indians.
    - Members of the Commission faced angry public demonstrations in India, and political organisations, including the Indian National Congress and Muslim League, refused to give evidence before it.
    - In 1928, a series of All-Parties Conferences released the $\lq$Nehru Report,' calling for dominion status with full self-government for India and a federal constitution.
    - Attempting to avoid a showdown between the nationalists and the British authorities, the Viceroy, Lord Irwin, published the $\lq$Irwin Declaration' in October 1929. It confirmed British support for dominion status and proposed a Round Table conference in London to draw up a constitutional framework for India.
    - The $\lq$Nehru Report', together with the ‘Irwin Declaration', established the irrelevance of any report produced by the Simon Commission.
- $\underline{\textrm{The Government of India Act 1935}}$ was a set of constitutional reforms that gave elected Indian ministers in the provinces more control. However, Britain retained control through emergency powers that could be imposed whenever necessary. The system of $\lq$dyarchy' was abolished, and the provisional governments controlled everything except defense and foreign affairs. The electorate was separated into religious, racial, and $\lq$special interest' constituencies.
    - Both the Congress and the League condemned the measures as inadequate but decided to participate in the provincial elections held in 1937.
    - Congress emerged with a landslide victory with 70% of the popular vote. The Muslim League got barely 5% of the total Muslim vote.
    - The Congress turned down Jinnah, the leader of the Muslim League's offer of cooperation in provinces with large Muslim minorities, which caused the League to strengthen its efforts to gain a mass following.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Government of India Act of 1919 formalised the reforms outlined in the Montagu-Chelmsford report of 1918 and aimed to calm the opposition to British rule resulting from the Amritsar Massacre. It introduced a system of $\lq$dyarchy' (dual government), with some power over local affairs in agriculture, education, and health given to the provincial Indian legislatures. In contrast, the central government remained under British control ($\checkmark$). The moderate nationalists initially viewed the Act as evidence that the British government was taking steps towards self-government ($\checkmark$). However, nationalist attitudes hardened with the realisation that the British ultimately retained control due to the power to veto legislation. This together with the release of the Hunter Report into the Amritsar Massacre of 1919 prompted Gandhi and the Congress leaders to urge voters to boycott the first elections of November 1920 ($\checkmark$).

Continuing protests led Britain to take further action and appoint the Simon Commission in 1927. The Commission visited India to explore the possibility of  further constitutional reforms but the visit caused anger as it failed to include any Indians ($\checkmark$). As a result, the Commission faced angry public demonstrations in India, and political organisations, including the Congress and Muslim League, refused to meet the members of the Commission ($\checkmark$). The mandate of the Commission to recommend constitutional reforms was further undermined by the decision taken in 1928 by all the Indian political parties to demand full self-government, rather than agree to a few reforms.   This demand was outlined in the release of the Nehru Report following a series of All-Parties Conferences in 1928, where dominion status with full self-government for India and a federal constitution was highlighted. The increasing conflict between the nationalists and the British authorities resulting from the Nehru Report led to the publication by the Viceroy Lord Irwin of the $\lq$Irwin Declaration' in 1929.  The Declaration now confirmed British support for the Indian demand for dominian status.  The agitation against the Simon Commission by the nationalists also led the Viceroy Lord Irwin to propose a Round Table conference in London to draw up a constitutional framework for India, furthering the goal of the nationalist movement ($\checkmark$).
