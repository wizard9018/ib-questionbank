# HI0478

Explain how the Saint-Domingue Slave Rebellion (Haiti, 1791) delayed Cuban independence.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following impacts with relevant explanation from below:

 - The rebellion in Haiti frightened the Cuban plantation owners. 

 - The Spanish authorities tightened control over the enslaved population.

 - The sugar plantations were expanded in Cuba. 

 - Slavery was expanded in Cuba by the Spanish authorities.

 - Cuban elites/plantation owners became even wealthier.

Any **two** of the following explanations to support each impact:

 - Spain increased its military presence in Cuba to cope with more enslaved people and quell any possible rebellions.

 - The sugar industry in Saint-Domingue was destroyed, which opened up the opportunity for Cuban plantations to expand their sugar industry.

 - Fear of slave rebellions and protection of the Spanish authorities made the Cuban elites even less likely to support a rebellion against the Spanish authorities.

 - Expanded slavery in Cuba created a paradoxical situation - more slaves created more fear in the Cuban elite, but also made them very rich.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

First, the destruction of Saint-Domingue's economy as a result of the rebellion created an opportunity for Cuba to expand its sugar industry, leading to a surge in plantation profits ($\checkmark$). As a result, the Cuban elite became even wealthier and had little incentive to support a rebellion against Spanish rule ($\checkmark$). Additionally, after the rebellion, Spain increased its military presence in Cuba to control the growing enslaved population and prevent uprisings similar to the one in Haiti ($\checkmark$). This reinforced Spanish dominance and delayed any significant movement toward Cuban independence ($\checkmark$).
