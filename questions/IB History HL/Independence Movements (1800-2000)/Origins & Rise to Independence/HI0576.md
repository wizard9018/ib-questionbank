# HI0576

Compare and contrast the Cartagena and Bogotá uprisings in 1810. 

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |

| :-- | :-- |

|Includes clear points of comparison (similarity)|($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$)|

|Includes clear points of contrast (difference)|($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$)|

|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments|($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$)|

For **comparison**, any of the following **similarities**:

 - Both rejected Spanish colonial authority and set up local juntas.

 - Both first claimed loyalty to King Ferdinand VII to avoid being seen as rebels.

 - Both inspired other provinces in New Granada to form their own juntas.

 - Both movements sought political autonomy, which later moved toward full independence.

For **contrast**, any of the following **differences**:

 - In Bogotá, the uprising started with a planned argument (the Flower Vase Incident), but in Cartagena, leaders acted directly without planning a public fight.

 - Creoles in Bogotá were more careful and talked with Spanish officials, while in Cartagena they quickly removed Spanish leaders without much discussion.

 - Bogotá's leaders stayed loyal to the Spanish king longer, but Cartagena moved faster and declared independence in 1811.

 - Arguments between different political groups happened sooner in Bogotá, while in Cartagena, the leaders stayed united at first.

 - In Bogotá, crowds of people helped push for change, but in Cartagena, the leaders made the decisions without needing big protests.

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

In 1810, growing tensions in New Granada between Creoles and Spanish authorities led to uprisings in Bogotá and Cartagena marking a regional shift towards emancipation. 

The uprisings were alike in that both encouraged other provinces in New Granada to form their own juntas and start breaking away from Spanish control. ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) For example,after Cartagena, the creation of similar juntas all over the viceroyalty were Cali on July 3, Pamplona the next day, and Socorro on July 10. Meanwhile, after Bogotá's events on July 20, cities such as Tunja, Socorro, and Popayán soon followed by establishing their own juntas in the following weeks. ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) 

Furthermore, in both cases, the first goal was to get more local power, by replacing Spanish-born officials with American-born leaders while still claiming loyalty to King Ferdinand VII, who was a prisoner of Napoleon at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). However, as political debates intensified and it became clear that Spain might reassert its authority, both cities gradually shifted from seeking autonomy to demanding full independence. ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$)

However, there were some notable differences between the uprisings as well. In Bogotá, the uprising began with a planned incident or trigger, called the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Flower Vase Incident” (florero de Llorente), to get people angry and start protests. ($\checkmark\,\text{evidence}$) In contrast, the uprising in Cartagena was more organic in that leaders acted immediately without creating a public fight.($\checkmark\, \text{difference and evidence}$) Also, in Bogotá, large crowds of citizens gathered in the main square and were key in putting pressure on the viceroy, showing the importance of public involvement in the uprising, whereas in Cartagena, the process was more controlled by the elite, who made the key decisions without needing support from mass protests. ($\checkmark\, \text{difference and evidence}$)
