# HI0224

Explain **four** reasons why the following statement is inaccurate: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$New Granadians fought from 1810 to 1819 against the Spanish for their Independence.”

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **four** of the following:

 - In 1810 independence was declared in Bogotá, the capital of the Viceroyalty of New Granada, but different cities declared their independence at different moments. 

 - In the patriots faction, who fought against Spanish rule, there were also combatants from other countries such as England and France. 

 - The act of independence of July 20, 1810, rejected French-dominated Spain but maintained allegiance to the king of Spain.

 - No cohesive New Granadian identity existed, and the declaration in Bogotá did not represent all regions.

 - Some regions, like Pasto and Santa Marta, supported Spanish rule rather than independence.

 - Indigenous people, Black people, mestizos, and Creoles fought on both sides, often switching allegiances based on personal interests.

 - In 1819 independence from Spain was gained, but in the center of New Granada, not the peripheral regions, continuing in wars until 1823.

 \

 [4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The statement is inaccurate as it does not reflect the complex process of independence in the Viceroyalty of New Granada. To begin, although on July 20, 1810—known as the Grito de Independencia ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$the cry for independence”)—an act was signed by Creoles in Bogotá, the capital of New Granada, declaring independence from Spain, this act specifically repudiated French-dominated Spain while maintaining fidelity to the king of Spain ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, at the time, a cohesive New Granadian national identity did not yet exist. As a result, the declaration in Bogotá did not necessarily represent other cities and regions, many of which had declared their own independence from Spain already or did so afterwards, such as Cartagena, Tunja, and El Socorro, among others ($\checkmark$). Additionally, some regions and cities supported Spanish rule over New Granada; in other words, there were New Granadians who were royalists, such as those in Pasto and Santa Marta ($\checkmark$). Lastly, Indigenous people, Black people, mestizos, and even Creoles born in New Granada fought on both the patriot and royalist sides, often switching allegiances based on economic and political interests ($\checkmark$).
