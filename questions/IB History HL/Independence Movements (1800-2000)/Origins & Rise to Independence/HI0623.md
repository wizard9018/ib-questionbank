# HI0623

Describe **two** causes of the Amritsar Massacre of 1919.
[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- The Rowlatt Acts alienated a wide section of society, including politically moderate Indians, who saw this as an insult after India’s contribution to the war effort.
- The Rowlatt Acts became a major source of widespread opposition, with protest marches in major cities accompanied by violence and disorder.
In Amritsar, there were incidents of uncontrolled unrest. Banks and buildings were torched, and Europeans attacked.
- The governor of the Punjab province sent troops commanded by General Reginald Dyer to control the situation. Dyer issued prohibitory orders on all public meetings and imposed a curfew, which he proceeded to implement using force. These actions increased anger and caused opposition and protest amongst the people, increasing tension in the lead-up to the massacre.
- On 13th April 1919, which was Baisakhi, an important Sikh festival, a large gathering of people from the surrounding villages gathered at Jallianwala Bagh in the center of Amritsar to protest against the Rowlatt Act and the arrest of pro-independence activists Saifuddin Kitchlew and Satyapal. The Bagh (a had only one entrance/exit and was enclosed on the other three sides.  Dyer ordered troops to open fire on the unarmed crowd, killing 379 people and wounding 1200. Dyer and the troops then departed, leaving the wounded and dead, many of whom were women and children.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Amritsar Massacre of 1919 was a pivotal point of the nationalist movement. The passing of the Rowlatt Acts in 1919, following the considerable Indian contribution to the First World War and the promise of the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms, was met with widespread outrage and anger all across India. This opposition was expressed in protest marches accompanied by violence and disorder in many major cities, including Amritsar ($\checkmark$
).

The Governor of Punjab ordered General Reginald Dyer to control the situation, who proceeded to issue prohibitory orders on public meetings and imposed a curfew on the area. General Dyer, on April 13, 1919, decided to order troops to fire on a large gathering of people at Jallianwala Bagh, which had only one exit, resulting in the massacre of 379 people, primarily women and children. His actions and the fact that Dyer and the troops left the dead and wounded unattended resulted in outrage and anger that fuelled the nationalist movement ($\checkmark$).
