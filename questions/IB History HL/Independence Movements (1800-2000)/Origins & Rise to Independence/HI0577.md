# HI0577

What, according to Source A, were the main threats to those pursuing independence from Spain?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  An excerpt from a supplement to Issue 5 (1811) of “La Bagatela,” a political criticism newspaper founded by Antonio Nariño, a politician, soldier, and hero of Independence. |

[Source: Adapted from *La Bagatela - Suplemento al número 5.* (1811). Biblioteca Virtual de la Republica Retrieved May 11, 2025 from https://babel.banrepcultural.org/digital/collection/p17054coll26/id/1507. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Spanish deception with promises: Spain uses false promises of equality to pacify colonies and keep them under control using words such as $\lq$fraternity' or $\lq$integral parts'
- Hypocrisy about liberty and independence: Spain allows freedom at home but calls it treason in America, showing double standards where Liberty in Europe is a virtue though a crime in the Americas.
- Internal corruption and ambition: it shows the  worry that Spanish offers of honors, jobs, and titles would corrupt them so it warns against Americans accepting titles or rewards from Spain, as this weakens the independence cause and encourages betrayal
- Violence and broken trust: the massacre in Quito shows Spain's violent repression and broken promises, warning colonists that trusting Spain is dangerous.
- Religious manipulation: it accuses clergy members and elites of being co-opted by Spain to legitimize colonial rule using religious arguments like the Bull of Alexander VI, that is invoked to justify Spanish dominance.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source A shows that the patriots feared many threats. First, there is the danger of Spanish lies and false promises. The text says the words “fraternity,” “equality,” “integral parts” are nothing more than snares laid for your credulity.” This means Spain used nice words to trick the colonies into trusting them, but they never planned to give real freedom ($\checkmark$).

Second, there is hypocrisy about liberty. The author explains that “in the former, the words $\lq$liberty' and $\lq$independence' are virtues; in the latter, they are $\lq$insurrection' and $\lq$crime.’” This shows Spain treated freedom as good in Spain but as a crime in America, so equality was impossible ($\checkmark$).

Finally, there is the threat of violence and broken trust. The text warns: “Keep your gaze turned ever toward Quito: let the bloody scene of August 2 constantly remind you of the trust you should place in the promises of your former masters.” This refers to the massacre in Quito, showing that Spain kills those who resist and cannot be trusted ($\checkmark$).
