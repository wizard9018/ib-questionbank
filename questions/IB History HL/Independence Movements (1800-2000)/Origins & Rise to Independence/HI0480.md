# HI0480

Explain in detail **two** ways Spain economically exploited Cuba in the late-19th and early-20th centuries.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting detailed explanation: 

 - Spain held a monopoly over Cuban trade

     - Spain imposed the mercantilist system, which restricted Cuba to trade only with Spain and its approved merchants.

     - Cuban ports were closed to most foreign ships; this limited access to competitive markets.

     - Spain retained tight control over import-export licenses, often favoring peninsulares merchants (Spanish born merchants living in the Spanish colonial territories)

 - Spain placed heavy taxes and financial controls over Cuba

     - Spain imposed royal monopolies on products like tobacco and salt,forcing Cubans to buy and sell through the state

     - The $\text{\textquotedblleft}$situado” system required Cuban tax revenues to be sent to other parts of the empire, such as Florida and Venezuela, rather than being reinvested locally

     - Cuba's growing economy was heavily taxed to fund Spain's military and administrative presence on the island 

 - Cuba's industry was dominated by sugar which Spain controlled

     - By the mid-19th century, Cuba had become the world's largest sugar producer controlled by Spain

     - The economy became deeply dependent on slave labor.

     - The expansion of sugar was driven by the Haitian Revolution, which collapsed Haiti's sugar economy, opening space for Cuba to dominate the global market.

 - Spain restricted industry and manufacturing in Cuba

     - Spain discouraged industrial development in Cuba to prevent competition with Spanish goods.

     - Laws restricted the growth of textile mills, ironworks, and shipbuilding, forcing Cuba to import manufactured goods from Spain

     - Even modest proposals to industrialize the sugar industry with modern machinery faced bureaucratic hurdles or were blocked by colonial authorities.

 - Spain exploited Cuba's land ownership by owning most of the land in Cuba

     - Large tracts of fertile land were owned by peninsulares (Spaniards born in Spain), who were favored in land grants and legal disputes

     - Many Creole (Cuban-born) elites were marginalized from high-ranking offices or denied land unless fully loyal to Spain

     - Spanish-owned latifundios (large estates) dominated the countryside, and land was often cultivated by enslaved Africans, not smallholders

     - Land ownership patterns reinforced a rigid racial and class hierarchy, and fueled resentment among Creoles seeking autonomy or reform

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Spain maintained a colonial economic system in Cuba, holding a monopoly on trade ($\checkmark$) and forcing Cubans to trade only with Spain or with Spanish-approved merchants ($\checkmark$).  Additionally, any new licences to trade would be given to peninsular merchants who were Spanish-born rather than Cuban-born criollos ($\checkmark$). 

In addition to controlling trade, Spain also maintained control over the land through large estates called latifundios ($\checkmark$). Production on these estates was maintained through slave labor, not through Cuban farmers, helping the Spanish maintain control ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, it was virtually impossible for the Creole (Cuban-born elites) to buy land or become wealthy landowners, as they were often denied the right to purchase land in favor of the Spanish-born elite ($\checkmark$).
