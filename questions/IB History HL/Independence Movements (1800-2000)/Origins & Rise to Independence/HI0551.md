# HI0551

In what ways did tension over race and class impact the Cuban independence movement?
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following tensions with supporting explanation:
- Most white Creole elites did not embrace social change although the push for independence was initially led by wealthy white Cuban-born elites (Creoles)
    - Carlos Manuel de Céspedes, who sought political autonomy, was reluctant to challenge the existing racial and class hierarchy which made unity difficult.
- Afro-Cuban participation was large but devalued by other Cubans.
    - While Afro-Cubans made up a large portion of the rebel forces, they were often excluded from high-level leadership roles, reflecting ongoing racial inequality within the movement which led to resentment and internal conflict within the movement.
- Antoinio Maceo's participation focused heavily on the inequality of the participation of Afro-Cubans which added to the tensions
    - Maceo, an Afro-Cuban general, also highlighted the broader lack of racial equality which again led to resentment and internal conflict.
- Resistance to embrace change by elites (mostly Creoles)
    - Elites in the leadership hesitated to support the redistribution of land, fearing it would upset the social hierarchy and economic system and were therefore often against the independence movement or for more US or international elite participation in the movement
- Resistance to the abolition of slavery
    - Many land-owning elite leaders were worried that the abolition of slavery would upset the economic system which made them unlikely to work with the rebels seeking independence.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
There were significant tensions over race and class which made the independence movement difficult. There was a difference between Creoles who were often for independence ($\checkmark$) but did not want to challenge class and social differences which led to an inability to work with other members of the independence movement ($\checkmark$).  Antonio Maceo was an Afro-Cuban who became very prominent and he brought a clear focus on the inequality of Afro-Cubans ($\checkmark$) which made working together with other factions difficult as the Afro-Cubans were angry at the lack of recognition for their participation ($\checkmark$).  Lastly, there was on the part of some elites a resistance to changing the economic system by reorganising land ($\checkmark$) as this meant that the social hierarchy might be upset and therefore this fear made working together as a unit with the working class or the Afro-Cubans more difficult. Many elites moved towards favoring the participation of the US to achieve independence ($\checkmark$).
