# HI0497

To what extent did Marti's concerns, summarized in Source C, become a reality according to Source A?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Text of the Platt Amendment, initially introduced in the Army Appropriations Bill of 1901 and approved by the US Congress on May 22, 1903 and signed as a permanent treaty between the United States and Cuba.|

[Source: Adapted from *Platt Amendment* (1903). Milestone Documents. National Archives. Retrieved February 14th, 2025 from https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/platt-amendment. Public domain.]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpt from “Cuba in 1898” by Jose M. Hernandez. Published by the Library of Congress as a part of the presentation *World of 1898: International Perspectives on the Spanish American War.* |

:box[He [Marti] feared that if Cuba's struggle for independence continued indefinitely without the imminent prospect of success it would create conditions leading to U.S. intervention and ultimately to the annexation of the island. At one point he even came to believe, rightly or wrongly, that there existed an $\text{\textquotedblleft}$iniquitous plan to put pressure on the island and drive it to war so as to fabricate a pretext to intervene in its affairs and with the credit earned as guarantor and mediator keep it as its own." For this reason, he thought that Cubans had to achieve a quick victory and then present Washington with their political emancipation as an accomplished fact. Otherwise, they could very well shed their blood merely to exchange one master for another.]

[Source: Hernandez, J.M. (2023, February 28). *Cuba in 1898.* Library of Congress. Retrieved June 10, 2025 from https://guides.loc.gov/world-of-1898/cuba-overview. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any combination of **three** of the following concerns paired with appropriate evidence from Source C:
- Fear of U.S. intervention if Cuban independence was delayed or uncertain.
- Suspicion of an "iniquitous plan" by the U.S. to engineer a pretext for intervention.
- Worry that Cuba might exchange one colonial master (Spain) for another (the U.S.).
- Urgency to win independence quickly and present it as a fait accompli to avoid domination.

Possible evidence from Source C (The Platt Amendment):
- U.S. retains intervention rights (Clause III):
 “The United States may exercise the right to intervene for the preservation of Cuban independence...”
- Limits on Cuba's foreign policy and sovereignty (Clause I):
 “Cuba shall never enter into any treaty... with any foreign power...”
- Requirement to lease naval bases to the U.S. (Clause VII):
 “The government of Cuba will sell or lease to the United States lands necessary for... naval stations...
- Control over Cuban public debt and finances (Clause II)
- U.S. oversight of internal Cuban affairs (sanitation, governance, law validation)
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source C, the Platt Amendment, legally limited Cuban Independence by limiting its ability to interact with other countries and retaining a right to intervene, which Marti predicted would happen ($\checkmark$). The Platt Amendment also limited Cuban sovereignty by promising to $\lq$protect' Cuba, which Marti alluded to as a pretext for controlling Cuba ($\checkmark$).  Finally, Marti suggested that the US would replace Spain as its colonizer and the control of Cuba outlined in the the Platt Amendment suggested the US would maintain a similar control oer Cuba that Spain did ($\checkmark$).
