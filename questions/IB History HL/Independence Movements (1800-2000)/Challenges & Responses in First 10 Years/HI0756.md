# HI0756

Explain **two** responses of the Indian state to the social challenges faced after independence.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- The new government was faced with the challenge of improving access to education as India’s literacy rate was very low at 16%. The government focused on universal primary education. While there was an increase in the number of children attending school, compulsory education for all children was not achieved. Centres of higher education with the establishment of national institutions like institutes of technology and research establishments and universities helped support economic goals of industrialization. By 2010 literacy rates had risen substantially to 61%.
- Despite the Constitutional guarantee of equality (Articles 14–16) women faced legal, social, and economic discrimination.New laws gave women equal rights in inheritance and ownership of property and greater rights in marriage. Societal attitudes especially in the rural areas proved an obstacle and educational opportunities for girls here lagged far behind that for boys.
- The entrenched caste system marginalized Dalits and Scheduled Castes/Tribes, denying them education, employment, and social dignity. The 1950 Constitution specifically abolished untouchability. Affirmative action with reservations in education, employment, and legislatures aimed to promote social justice and representation.However social discrimination and caste oppression was still common in rural areas and at times linked to resentment over the preferential treatment of the government policies.
- Linguistic and Regional Diversity was a challenge as India had over a dozen major languages and strong regional identities. The government responded with reorganization of states on linguistic lines through the States Reorganisation Act (1956). There was the adoption of a three-language formula in education with the inclusion of the state language along with English and Hindi.
- Health services were another challenge in a country with a population of 350 million, facing regular epidemics of smallpox, plague, cholera and malaria. The government funded health services to train doctors, nurses and build hospitals and clinics. While life expectancy improved, it resulted in rising population growth that put pressure on land and resources. Population control focused on offering incentives for smaller families and a campaign promoting sterilisation which faced considerable resistance. Between 1947 and 2010 average life expectancy had risen to sixty -six years and the population tripled to nearly 1.2 billion people.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One of the main challenges facing the new government in India was improving access to education, as India's literacy rate was very low at 16% ($\checkmark$). The government focused on both implementing access to universal primary education and ensuring the establishment and expansion of post-secondary technical education. This increased the number of children attending school; however, the goal of compulsory education for all children was not achieved ($\checkmark$).

The Indian state also faced the challenge of ensuring access to health services for all its citizens ($\checkmark$). The country faced regular epidemics of smallpox, plague, cholera, and malaria with a sizable population of 350 million. Health services were funded by the government to train doctors and nurses, as well as build hospitals and clinics. The results were tangible, with a dramatic improvement in life expectancy; however, the rising population growth put pressure on land and resources. Between 1947 and 2010, the average life expectancy had risen to sixty-six years, and the population tripled to nearly 1.2 billion people ($\checkmark$).
