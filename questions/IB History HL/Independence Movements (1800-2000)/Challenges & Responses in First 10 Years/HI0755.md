# HI0755

Describe **three** responses to economic challenges faced by the newly independent Indian state.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The government adopted a mixed economy model, combining state-led planning with a regulated private sector.
- India launched a series of Five-Year Plans starting in 1951. The First Five-Year Plan (1951–56) focused on Agricultural development while the Second Five Year Plan emphasised industrialization and public sector development focusing on heavy industries, steel and coal.
- There was a shift from industry to agriculture in the late 1960s to make India self-sufficient in food production. The $\lq$Green Revolution' with the use of high-yielding seed varieties, irrigation schemes, and the use of chemical fertilisers led to an impressive increase in agricultural production.
- Government controls over the economy were relaxed in the 1980s, and India sought to become part of the world capitalist system. This led to an exponential growth of the economy in the 1990s.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The newly independent Indian state adopted a mixed economy model that combined state-led planning along with a regulated private sector, in order to meet the economic challenges they faced ($\checkmark$).

A series of Five-Year Plans were launched in 1951, the first of which focused on Agricultural development in the period 1951-1956, while the Second Five Year Plan emphasised industrialization and public sector development focusing on heavy industries, steel and coal ($\checkmark$).

The late 1960s witnessed a shift from industry to agriculture with the $\lq$Green Revolution'. In order to make India self-sufficient in food production, there was a focus on  the use of high-yielding seed varieties, irrigation schemes and the use of chemical fertilisers, all of which led to an impressive increase in agricultural production ($\checkmark$).
