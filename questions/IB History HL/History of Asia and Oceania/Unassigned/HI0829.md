# HI0829

Using evidence from Source B, describe how Ho Chi Minh uses foreign political ideals to justify Vietnam's independence.
[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Reference to the U.S. Declaration of Independence (1776): Ho quotes “All men are created equal... Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness” to appeal to American democratic values and show that Vietnamese independence is based on the same universal principles.
- Reference to the French Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789): Ho cites “All men are born free and with equal rights...” to highlight the hypocrisy of France’s actions in Vietnam, contrasting French ideals with their colonial oppression.
- Appeal to post-World War II Allied principles (Tehran and San Francisco Conferences): Ho emphasizes that Vietnam’s struggle aligns with Allied declarations supporting self-determination and equality of nations, suggesting that Vietnam deserves international recognition just like other decolonizing countries.
- Alignment with anti-fascist efforts: He claims that the Vietnamese people fought “side by side with the Allies against the Fascists,” arguing that their contribution to the global struggle for freedom entitles them to national sovereignty.
- Use of Enlightenment values to delegitimize colonialism: By grounding his argument in widely respected Western political ideals, Ho frames colonialism as outdated and unjust, reinforcing Vietnam's moral right to independence.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Ho Chi Minh uses foreign political ideals to justify Vietnam's independence by aligning his arguments with well-known democratic principles. Firstly, he references the U.S. Declaration of Independence (1776), quoting that “All men are created equal... Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness,” to appeal to American values and emphasize that the Vietnamese desire for independence is rooted in the same ideals ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, he draws on the French Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789), citing that “All men are born free and with equal rights” to expose the contradiction between France's professed ideals and its colonial rule over Vietnam ($\checkmark$).

Finally, Ho refers to post-World War II Allied commitments made “at Tehran and San Francisco”, a reference to the Tehran and San Francisco Conferences, highlighting principles of self-determination and equality among nations. In doing so, he argues that Vietnam's independence is consistent with international agreements and should therefore be recognized globally ($\checkmark$).
