# HI1157

Outline **two** impacts of Khrushchev's $\lq$Secret Speech' (1956) on Mao Zedong.
[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:

Ideological Shock and Threat to Mao's Legitimacy
- Khrushchev's criticism of Stalin's cult of personality challenged Mao's own leadership style and authority.
- Mao viewed de-Stalinisation as “revisionist” and feared it would weaken revolutionary ideology in China.

Deterioration of Sino-Soviet Relations
- Mao resented Khrushchev's actions as disrespectful and saw the speech as a betrayal of true Marxism.
- This ideological rift deepened tensions, paving the way for the Sino-Soviet split (1959-1960) and the withdrawal of Soviet aid.

Domestic Consequences in China
- The speech encouraged Mao to launch the Hundred Flowers Campaign (1956) to show China's ideological unity.
- When criticism grew, Mao reversed course with the Anti-Rightist Campaign (1957) to reassert control and loyalty to his leadership.

Additional Impacts
- Motivated the Great Leap Forward (1958) as Mao sought to prove China's revolutionary superiority.
- Reinforced Mao's emphasis on continuous revolution and resistance to Soviet-style reform or liberalisation.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Khrushchev's denunciation of Stalin's cult of personality directly undermined Mao's leadership style, which also centred on personal authority and ideological purity ($\checkmark$). Secondly, Mao saw de-Stalinisation as an attack on Marxist-Leninist orthodoxy and revolutionary principles, laying the foundation for the later Sino-Soviet split (1959-1960) ($\checkmark$).
