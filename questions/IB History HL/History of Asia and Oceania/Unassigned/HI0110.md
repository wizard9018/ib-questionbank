# HI0110

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyze the value and limitations of the source for a historian studying Sino-American relations.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from the Joint Statement Following Discussions With Leaders of the People's Republic of China (also known as the Shanghai Communiqué). February 27th, 1972.

[Source: Joint Statement Following Discussion With Leaders of the People's Republic of China  (1972, February 27th). Office of the Historian. Retrieved October 6th, 2024 from https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v17/d203. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
If only values or limitations are discussed, the student cannot get more than **two** marks. There must be at least one reference to origin, purpose and content in either value or limitations to reach **four** marks.
$\\$
 |  | $\ce{VALUE}$ |$\ce{LIMITATIONS}$|
 | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| ORIGIN | The source is made in 1973 when Sino-American relations are starting to change so it has insight into their development. | It lacks the benefit of hindsight so it does not know the impact of this meeting in future events.|
| PURPOSE| To inform what the powers agree on. Since the objective is to inform, we can expect the information to be at least in part accurate.| |
| CONTENT | Demonstrates the willingness to have a diplomatic relationship and some of the agreements achieved.|It ignores the impact of this agreement both on the relations between the US and the 2 Chinas and on other countries.|

[4 marks]
$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Given that the source is a primary source that explicitly outlines the intentions of the US and the PRC ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$), it might be assumed that the source is of great use to a historian studying Sino-American relations. Not only does it provide a first-hand account of what the powers agree on ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$) (the need for peaceful coexistence for example) it also demonstrates the willingness to have a diplomatic relationship ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

However, on its own, the extent of the source's value to the historian is limited. It is described as an excerpt hence it is likely just a snapshot of the power`s intentions ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). Also, given the fact that it is the first document signed to reestablish Sino-American relations, it is not helpful to the historian trying to uncover the success of this new relationship ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). It is also only the decision of the two signatories and does not  indicate how other powers perceived this new relationship, for example, the USSR ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$). To fully appreciate its value, this source cannot be used in isolation.
