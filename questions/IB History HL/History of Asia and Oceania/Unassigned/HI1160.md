# HI1160

Explain **three** ways Mao's intervention in the Korean War (1950-1953) influenced Cold War superpower tensions.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with explanation:

Escalation of Superpower Conflict
- The intervention reversed U.N./U.S. advances and prolonged the war, demonstrating that communist forces could effectively challenge Western military power.
- This marked the first direct military confrontation between communist and Western forces, intensifying U.S.-Soviet rivalry.
- Mao's willingness to confront the U.S. convinced Western leaders that the Cold War had become a truly global and militarised conflict, not confined to Europe.

Impact on U.S. Foreign and Military Policy
- The Chinese intervention shocked the U.S., prompting a major military buildup and the globalisation of containment.
- The U.S. quadrupled defence spending (NSC-68) and accelerated rearmament, framing communism as a monolithic/unified, expansionist threat directed by Moscow and Beijing.
- Truman's administration expanded alliances and bases across Asia (e.g., ANZUS 1951, SEATO 1954).
- Mao's actions also led to the U.S. commitment to defend Taiwan, increasing tensions in the Taiwan Strait.

Impact on Sino-Soviet Relations
- China's intervention elevated its status within the communist bloc as a powerful revolutionary force.
- Stalin initially encouraged Mao's involvement indirectly, but was cautious about provoking direct Soviet confrontation with the U.S.
- Mao's sacrifices in the war gave China a sense of independence and equal standing with the USSR, but also exposed unequal alliance dynamics with the USSR provided limited support and demanded repayment for aid. As such, the war laid the groundwork for future Sino-Soviet tensions, as Mao began to resent Soviet hesitancy and dominance.

Militarisation and Division of the Cold War
- The Korean War, sustained by China's intervention, entrenched the division of Korea at the 38th parallel, symbolising the permanent bipolar structure of the Cold War.
- It justified the long-term U.S. military presence in Asia and NATO's expansion in Europe.
- Both superpowers increased nuclear development and readiness, pushing the Cold War into an arms race.
- China's role solidified Asia as a central theatre of Cold War rivalry.

Long-Term Significance
- Mao's actions positioned China as a key Cold War actor and the leading communist power in Asia.
- The conflict hardened ideological lines between capitalism and communism, ending hopes of U.S.-China cooperation.
- It marked the beginning of the U.S. policy of containment in Asia, later extended to Vietnam and beyond.
- The war also legitimised Mao's domestic authority, reinforcing his revolutionary credentials and aligning China's identity with global anti-imperial struggle.
