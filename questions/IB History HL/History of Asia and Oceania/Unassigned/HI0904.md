# HI0904

Evaluate the limitations of the military strategies of the United States in the Vietnam War (1956-1975).
[7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with supporting explanation and a reasoned conclusion:

Limited effectiveness in the short term or tactically:
- Operation Rolling Thunder (1965-1968): Intended to weaken North Vietnamese morale and supply lines. Destroyed some infrastructure but failed to stop supplies along the Ho Chi Minh Trail. Gave North Vietnam time to strengthen air defenses.
- Superior firepower and air mobility: Use of helicopters (e.g., UH-1 Huey) allowed fast deployment in jungle terrain. Enabled rapid troop movement and medical evacuation. Provided temporary tactical advantages in battles like Ia Drang (1965).
- Search and destroy missions: Aimed to kill Viet Cong forces and reduce enemy presence. Occasionally successful in eliminating enemy units. Produced high body counts, seen as a success metric by U.S. military leadership.

Largely ineffective or counterproductive in the long term:
- Overreliance on attrition warfare: Strategy focused on body counts rather than holding territory. Misjudged the Viet Cong's and North Vietnamese Army's (NVA) willingness to endure losses. Failed to break enemy morale.
- Guerrilla warfare by the Viet Cong undermined U.S. strategy: The Viet Cong avoided direct confrontations and used ambushes and booby traps. U.S. troops were unprepared for asymmetric warfare in dense jungle terrain. U.S. technology and firepower were often ineffective against elusive guerrilla tactics.
- Alienation of the civilian population: Bombing campaigns and destruction of villages pushed civilians toward supporting the Viet Cong. Tactics like the use of napalm and Agent Orange caused long-term environmental and human harm. Events like the My Lai Massacre (1968) damaged U.S. credibility and intensified anti-war sentiment.
- Failure to cut off the Ho Chi Minh Trail: Despite intense bombing, the supply route through Laos and Cambodia remained operational. North Vietnam adapted with effective camouflage and constant rebuilding efforts.
- Tet Offensive (1968): Militarily, the U.S. and South Vietnamese forces repelled the attacks. Strategically, it was a major propaganda victory for the North. Shattered public perception of U.S. progress in the war and eroded political will.
- Vietnamization policy (1969 onwards): Attempted to train and equip South Vietnamese forces to take over combat roles. Lacked long-term effectiveness due to corruption, poor morale, and inadequate training in the ARVN (Army of the Republic of Vietnam). Viewed as a way for the U.S. to exit rather than achieve victory.
- Strategic Hamlet Program (1962-1964): U.S.-backed counterinsurgency initiative to isolate civilians from Viet Cong influence and build loyalty to the South Vietnamese regime. Often involved forced relocations, generating resentment among peasants. The Viet Cong were often able to infiltrate the hamlets or exploit them for propaganda purposes. Reflected broader failures in U.S. understanding of Vietnamese society and nationalism.

Broader strategic failures:
- Lack of understanding of Vietnamese nationalism and culture: U.S. policy framed the conflict as a Cold War battle against communism. Underestimated the strength of nationalist motivations among North Vietnamese and the Viet Cong. Failed to win the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$hearts and minds" of the South Vietnamese population.
- Limited domestic support over time: Initial support eroded due to rising casualties, media coverage, and lack of clear progress. Anti-war protests and the credibility gap pressured politicians to seek withdrawal. Strategy was unsustainable without domestic political backing.
\
[7 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, U.S. strategies such as Operation Rolling Thunder and helicopter deployment offered short-term tactical gains but failed to achieve strategic objectives. ($\checkmark$). Bombing campaigns did not stop supply routes like the Ho Chi Minh Trail, and although helicopters provided mobility, they could not secure lasting control over territory or defeat guerrilla tactics ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the U.S. strategy of attrition, focused on body counts, misjudged the determination of the Viet Cong and North Vietnamese Army ($\checkmark$) Despite high casualties, enemy morale remained strong. This approach ignored the nature of guerrilla warfare and resulted in costly battles that failed to produce meaningful long-term outcomes ($\checkmark$).

Finally, tactics such as search-and-destroy missions, napalm, and Agent Orange alienated civilians and boosted Viet Cong support ($\checkmark$). Events like the My Lai Massacre further damaged U.S. credibility. Later, Vietnamization failed due to ARVN weakness, becoming a withdrawal strategy rather than a path to sustainable victory ($\checkmark$).

In conclusion, despite technological advantages, U.S. military strategies were ultimately ineffective. They failed to adapt to guerrilla warfare, alienated the civilian population, and could not build a stable South Vietnamese force. This lack of strategic flexibility and cultural understanding contributed directly to the U.S. failure in Vietnam ($\checkmark$).
