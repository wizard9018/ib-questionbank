# HI0768

Using evidence from Source A, explain Mao's concerns about the direction of the Chinese Communist Party.

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- | 

|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |Excerpt from $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$A Letter To The Red Guards Of Tsinghua University Middle School,” sent August 1, 1966, by Mao Zedong. Originally published in *Long Live Mao Tse-tung Thought*, a Red Guard publication. 

[Source: Mao\, Z. (1966, August 1). *A Letter To The Red Guards Of Tsinghua University Middle School.* Retrieved May 12, 2025 from https://marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-9/mswv9_60.htm. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - Implies internal threats; $\text{\textquotedblleft}$those who have made serious mistakes.” Suggesting Mao feared corruption and complacency among cadres.

 - The message is directed at students, suggesting Mao saw youth as more ideologically pure and less corrupted.

 - Mao encourages the students in their role of confronting and correcting perceived Party deviations.

 - Calls for rectification and re-education rather than purges alone.

 - Emphasizes ongoing revolutionary struggle to prevent revisionism.

 - Shows Mao's belief that the Party needed to stay connected to the masses.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source A implies that Mao believed there were internal threats within the CPP. His reference to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$those who have made serious mistakes” suggests that he was concerned about the ideological weakness, corruption, or complacency of certain party members. This indicates a fear that some cadres were drifting away from revolutionary principles and embracing more bureaucratic or capitalist tendencies. ($\checkmark$) Secondly, rather than calling for their immediate removal or punishment, Mao emphasizes the need for rectification and re-education, showing that he hoped to ideologically reform these individuals instead of relying solely on purges. This reflects a broader concern that the party was losing its revolutionary spirit and needed to return to its Marxist-Leninist roots through mass mobilization and ideological struggle. ($\checkmark$) Finally, the fact that Mao directs this message to the Red Guards rather than party officials further reveals his mistrust of the existing party leadership and his reliance on youth to reinvigorate the revolution. ($\checkmark$)
