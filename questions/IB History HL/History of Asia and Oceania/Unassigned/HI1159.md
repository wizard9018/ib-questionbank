# HI1159

Explain **three** ways Nixon's visit to China in 1972 affected relations between the United States and the Soviet Union.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with relevant explanation:
- Nixon's visit to China heightened Soviet fears of U.S.-China cooperation and strategic encirclement, pressuring Moscow to improve relations with Washington.
- It triggered a triangular diplomacy dynamic, where the U.S. could exploit tensions between Beijing and Moscow for diplomatic advantage.
- The USSR, worried about isolation, accelerated efforts to negotiate arms control and détente with the U.S.
- The visit contributed directly to the signing of the SALT I Treaty (1972) and the Anti-Ballistic Missile (ABM) Treaty, as the Soviets sought to stabilise relations.
- Soviet leaders saw Nixon's opening to China as evidence of a shifting balance of power and sought to counter it through renewed engagement in Europe and the developing world.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The visit alarmed Soviet leaders, who feared a potential U.S.-China alliance and the risk of strategic encirclement ($\checkmark$). As a result, Moscow reassessed its foreign policy and sought improved relations with Washington. ($\checkmark$). Second, it created a new triangular balance of power ($\checkmark$). The US was able to exploit tensions between Beijing and Moscow to its own advantage. By engaging separately with the communist powers, the U.S. gained greater diplomatic leverage in the Cold War ($\checkmark$). Finally, the USSR, to avoid isolation, moved quickly to strengthen ties with the United States ($\checkmark$). This drove renewed arms control negotiations and the formalisation of détente through agreements such as the SALT I Treaty (1972) ($\checkmark$).
