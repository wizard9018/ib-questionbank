In the place of the old youth order came the new disorder-the Red Guards. Much has been written in the Western press about the activities of these bands of fanatical secondary-school and university students the attacks on persons regarded as bourgeois, the beating of aged persons in the streets, the seizure and destruction of works of art, the attacks on both Christian churches and Buddhist temples, the forced extortion of ancestral tablets from terrorized peasants, the desecration of graveyards, and so on.

Finally, in September, there were some cautionary notes. The Red Guards were told not to attack factories or communes, and many of them were sent into the countryside to help the communes with the harvesting. At the same time, their organization was greatly tightened, and the formation of committees in the provinces to supervise their activities was accelerated, with party and PLA [People's Liberation Army] representatives both playing key roles. Classes in schools and universities remained suspended, however, and foreign students were sent home for a year, suggesting that Red Guard activities will dominate school life for many months to come.

Mao's Thought was the holy book of the religion and the associated symbols of communism - the hammer and sickle were now the symbols of the religion linked to Mao in the Cultural Revolution.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The destabilizing impact of the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution was felt by all sections of society. Source C discusses the impact of the Revolution on the Communist Party and states that there was a purge of the senior Communist party membership with Communist party Chairman Liu Shao-ch'i [Liu Shaoqi] and Secretary-General Teng Hsiao-p'ing [Deng Xiaoping] being removed from power as a result of their resistance to the directives aimed at public humiliation of important members ($\checkmark$). It also mentions the consolidation of Lin Piao's position as Mao's presumed successor with Prime Minister Chou Enlai being the only senior official who retained his position through this chaotic period ($\checkmark$).

 

Further evidence of the destabilizing impact of the Revolution is evident in Source A which portrays the complete control that Mao achieved as a result of the Cultural Revolution suggesting that Mao was raised to the position of a national deity to be worshiped ($\checkmark$). $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Mao's Thought” had now assumed the status of the holy book of the religion associated with him. The symbols of communism, the hammer and sickle, were now the symbols of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$religion” linked to Mao in the Cultural Revolution ($\checkmark$).

 

In addition, as mentioned in Source C, all sections of society faced the terror unleashed by the Red Guards as they attacked anyone regarded as bourgeoisie and destroyed places of worship and artifacts linked to culture ($\checkmark$). Source C further mentions the destabilizing impact on education as schools and universities remained closed for the entire period of the Revolution, depriving an entire generation of education in order to emphasize $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Red Guard activities” instead ($\checkmark$). 

The chaos unleashed by Mao by launching the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution to further his doctrine of continuous revolution and to regain his power and position within the Communist Party caused waves of disruption that would bring upheaval to the lives of a generation of people in China ($\checkmark$).
