# HI1156

Identify **three** key differences between Maoism and traditional Marxist-Leninism.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:

Revolutionary Base
- Maoism: Revolution led by the peasants in rural areas.
- Marxist-Leninism: Revolution led by the urban working class (proletariat).

Nature of Revolution
- Maoism: Believed in continuous revolution to prevent corruption.
- Marxist-Leninism: Focused on seizing power once and consolidating socialism.

Role of the Masses
- Maoism: Emphasised mass participation and the “mass line” (listening to the people).
- Marxist-Leninism: Relied on a centralised vanguard party to lead the people.

Economic Development
- Maoism: Favoured self-reliance and small-scale rural industry.
- Marxist-Leninism: Focused on state-led industrialisation and central planning.

Attitude Toward Peasantry
- Maoism: Viewed peasants as the main revolutionary force.
- Marxist-Leninism: Considered peasants less revolutionary and sought rapid urbanisation.

International Outlook
- Maoism: Promoted national independence and Third World revolutions.
- Marxist-Leninism: Promoted Soviet-led international communism.

Cultural and Ideological Emphasis
- Maoism: Saw culture and thought reform as central to class struggle.
- Marxist-Leninism: Prioritised economic structure over cultural change.

Relationship with the USSR
- Maoism: Criticised the USSR for “revisionism” and dependence on the West.
- Marxist-Leninism: Accepted Soviet leadership and peaceful coexistence with the West (under Khrushchev).

Adaptation to Context
- Maoism: Adapted Marxism to agrarian, developing societies.
- Marxist-Leninism: Applied mainly to industrial, urban societies.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Maoism saw the rural peasantry as the main revolutionary force capable of overthrowing feudal and capitalist systems. In contrast, Marxist-Leninism centred on the urban working class (proletariat) as the revolutionary vanguard ($\checkmark$). Secondly, Maoism advocated mass mobilisation and political participation as the source of revolutionary energy. Marxist-Leninism stressed centralised control by a disciplined vanguard party of professional revolutionaries ($\checkmark$). Finally, Maoism favoured small-scale, labour-intensive industry (e.g., “backyard furnaces” during the Great Leap Forward). In contrast, Marxist-Leninism favoured state-led industrialisation and heavy industry as the foundation of socialism ($\checkmark$).
