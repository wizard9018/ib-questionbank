# HI0769

What is the message of Source B?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$  $`$Two Whatevers' Do Not Accord With Marxism” written by Deng Xiaoping, May 24, 1977. An excerpt from a talk with two leading comrades of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China.

[Source: Deng, X. (1977, May 24) *`Two Whatevers' Do Not Accord With Marxism*. Retrieved May 14, 2025 from https://marxists.org/reference/archive/deng-xiaoping/1977/124.htm. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - Rejection of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Whatevers". 

     - Deng rejects blind loyalty to Mao's words and policies, arguing that it blocks reform and the fair reassessment of events like the 1976 Tiananmen Incident and his own rehabilitation.

 - Support for reassessing Mao's legacy. 

     - Deng encourages an honest evaluation of Mao, noting that even Mao admitted his mistakes, and suggesting the party should weigh both his successes and failures.

 - Promotion of true marxist principles. 

     - Deng stresses that genuine Marxism (historical materialism) requires flexibility and critical analysis, not rigid adherence to past statements.

 - Justification for reform. 

     - By opposing the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Whatevers," Deng lays the theoretical groundwork for his shift away from Maoist ideology and toward pragmatic reform.

 - Implicit criticism of Mao's rule. 

     - Without directly attacking Mao, Deng subtly challenges the excesses of Mao's later years by referencing his errors and fallibility.

 - Reassertion of Deng's authority. 

     - Deng uses this ideological stance to challenge Hua Guofeng's conservative leadership and position himself as the leader of post-Mao China.

 - Alignment with popular sentiment. 

     - In defending the 1976 Tiananmen protests, Deng shows support for public discontent with Maoist extremism and connects with the broader mood of the people.

 - Call to move beyond Mao's cult of personality. 

     - Deng urges the party to abandon idolization of Mao and adopt a more thoughtful form of Marxism based on reason, not reverence.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Source is a clear rejection of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Whatevers" policy, which stated that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Whatever Chairman Mao said must be followed; whatever instructions he gave must be upheld.” Deng argues this principle is unacceptable and counterproductive, especially in the context of reform and the reevaluation of past events like the Tiananmen Incident of 1976 and his own political rehabilitation. ($\checkmark$

) Secondly, the Source expresses support for a critical reassessment of Mao's legacy. Deng emphasizes that Mao himself acknowledged making mistakes, and that no historical figure, including Marx, Engels, Lenin, or Stalin, was infallible. He invokes Mao's own saying that a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$70 percent achievements, 30 percent mistakes” judgment was fair, suggesting the party must honestly assess both Mao's successes and errors. ($\checkmark$

)  Finally, the Source contains an implicit criticism of Mao's later rule. While Deng maintains a respectful tone, he undermines the idea of Mao's infallibility, particularly by referencing Mao's own admission of mistakes. This subtly challenges the Cultural Revolution and other radical policies of Mao's later years, without outright condemning Mao as a whole. ($\checkmark$)
