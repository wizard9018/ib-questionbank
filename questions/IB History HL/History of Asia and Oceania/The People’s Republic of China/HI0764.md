# HI0764

Describe **three** ways the status and rights of women changed in the People's Republic of China between 1949 and 1976.

[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - The Marriage Law of 1950 was a landmark reform. It outlawed arranged, forced, and child marriages, as well as concubinage, and gave women the right to divorce, radically challenging traditional Confucian family structures.

 - The CCP promoted gender equality in law, encouraging women to participate equally in work and society. Women were legally recognized as equal to men in the workplace and within the family. This was evident especially during the Great Leap Forward (1958-62), where their labor in agriculture and industry was seen as vital to economic growth.

 - The government invested in female literacy and education, but enrollment and progression for girls lagged behind boys, especially in less developed regions.

 - In practice, patriarchal norms persisted, particularly in rural areas. While women worked outside the home, they were still expected to perform traditional domestic roles, which was particularly evident during the Great Leap Forward.

 - During the Great Leap Forward, communal living was promoted through communal kitchens and childcare facilities. These were supposed to liberate women from duties at home so women could work in fields or factories. However, in practice, these systems often increased the burden on women rather than alleviating it:

     - Communal kitchens were often poorly managed and at times lacked sufficient food, especially during the famine that followed. Women had to stand in long lines to get meager food rations, then sometimes still cook or find food for their families due to shortages. Rather than relieving the burden, this added time and stress, especially when kitchens were far from home.

     - The childcare services were understaffed and poorly maintained. Infants and toddlers were often neglected or got sick easily, so mothers often had to check in frequently or take care of children after long workdays. This reduced the intended $\text{\textquotedblleft}$freedom" to work and added emotional and physical strain.

 - Women were expected to match men's labour output in agriculture and construction without adjustments for physical differences or family responsibilities. This $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equality" in labour demands often meant double workloads, doing hard labor and then managing home tasks inadequately supported by the commune.

 - During the Cultural Revolution (1966-76), women were depicted as revolutionary comrades, often shown in propaganda wielding rifles or working in factories, but this was more symbolic than systemic, as women still faced structural limitations.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

A key milestone in the advancement of status and rights for women was the Marriage Law of 1950, which fundamentally challenged traditional Confucian values that had long ruled family life. The law banned arranged, forced, and child marriages, as well as concubinage, and granted women the right to seek divorce. These reforms gave women unprecedented legal autonomy and aimed to redefine marriage as a partnership based on equality and consent. ($\checkmark$) 

The CCP strongly promoted gender equality as part of its broader revolutionary goals. Women were encouraged to take on roles outside the home and participate fully in the workforce and public life. Legally, they were granted equal rights to men in the workplace and within the family. ($\checkmark$)

However, despite these legal and ideological improvements, deeply rooted patriarchal attitudes continued to shape women’s daily lives. In rural areas, especially, traditional expectations remained strong. Women who worked full-time outside the home were still expected to fulfill domestic responsibilities, effectively leaving them with a double burden. As a result, the promise of equality often translated into heavier demands rather than genuine empowerment. ($\checkmark$)
