# HI0762

Explain **three** reasons Mao launched the Cultural Revolution in 1966.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following with supporting explanation:

 - Reassert authority after the Great Leap Forward. 

     - The catastrophic failure of the Great Leap Forward (1958-62) had damaged Mao's credibility. He was sidelined by more pragmatic leaders like Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping. Mao complained that he was being treated as a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$dead ancestor" (respected but ignored). The Cultural Revolution was a way for him to return to the political centre.

 - Purge $\text{\textquotedblleft}$revisionists” and restore ideological purity. 

     - Mao believed the CCP was becoming too bureaucratic and moving away from revolutionary principles. He feared the Soviet-style shift toward a ruling class.

 - Revive revolutionary enthusiasm among the youth. 

     - Mao saw the youth as politically pure and revolutionary. By mobilising students, he bypassed the Party apparatus and built a mass movement loyal to himself.

 - Protect against capitalist and traditional influences. 

     - Mao wanted to destroy Confucian values, capitalist behaviours, and bourgeois thinking, which he viewed as threats to socialism, and which he thought had crept back into Chinese society.

 - Support from radicals like Jiang Qing and the Gang of Four. 

     - These allies encouraged Mao's cultural and ideological goals and helped him orchestrate attacks on his political opponents through mass campaigns.

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One of the key motivations in launching the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution (GPCR) was Mao's desire to reassert his authority after the disastrous outcomes of the Great Leap Forward (1958-1962), which had severely undermined his credibility within the CCP. ($\checkmark$) In the aftermath, moderate leaders like Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping took on greater roles, pushing Mao to the sidelines. Feeling marginalized and reportedly likening himself to a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$dead ancestor" (revered but no longer influential), Mao initiated the Cultural Revolution to regain control and reaffirm his dominance at the top of the Party hierarchy. ($\checkmark$)

Another major factor behind the movement was Mao's determination to purge the Party of so-called $\text{\textquotedblleft}$revisionists" and to restore what he viewed as the ideological purity of the revolution. ($\checkmark$) He grew increasingly concerned that the CCP was drifting to the right and adopting a Soviet-style government, which he believed betrayed the original revolutionary spirit. To counter this, Mao sought to eliminate those within the Party who he thought were deviating from Marxist-Leninist principles. ($\checkmark$)

In addition, Mao aimed to rekindle revolutionary fervor among the population, particularly the youth. ($\checkmark$) He regarded young people as untainted by the compromises and corruption of older generations and saw them as ideal agents of radical change. By encouraging students to challenge authority and embrace revolutionary ideals, Mao was able to sidestep the established Party structure and create a mass movement that was personally loyal to him. ($\checkmark$)
