# HI0766

Analyse **three** reasons Deng Xiaoping emerged as the paramount leader after Mao's death.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following with supporting evidence:

 - Removal of the Gang of Four. The Gang of Four were arrested in October 1976 by Hua Guofeng, with backing from senior military leaders like Ye Jianying. This action removed the most radical faction within the CCP, ended the Cultural Revolution, and created space for moderates and pragmatists like Deng to return, as the party rejected the chaos and ideological extremism associated with radical Maoism.

 - Deng was officially restored to his positions in July 1977, including Vice Premier and later Vice Chairman of the CCP. His rehabilitation marked the beginning of his political comeback.

 - Deng returned to power after being purged twice during the Cultural Revolution. He worked behind the scenes to build alliances with influential party officials, military leaders, and reform-minded bureaucrats, positioning himself as a unifier and pragmatic leader.

 - Deng's famous motto, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$It doesn't matter whether a cat is black or white, as long as it catches mice," reflected his practical approach and ideological flexibility. He moved away from class struggle and ideological purity, focusing instead on economic growth and national rejuvenation.

 - Hua, Mao's designated successor, lacked personal authority and vision. His $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Whatevers” policy (upholding whatever Mao said and did) became increasingly unpopular, and Deng used this to argue for a break from the past. Deng gradually pushed Hua into the background by gaining control over key party bodies, like the Central Military Commission and the Organization Department, which oversees personnel appointments.

 - Deng championed the Four Modernizations (agriculture, industry, defense, and science/technology), which offered a clear alternative to the chaos of the Cultural Revolution. These policies helped win popular support and legitimacy among the elite.

 - Although Deng did not initially take top formal positions, he gradually took control of the military, the Central Committee, and the Organization Department. This gave him de facto power over party appointments and direction.

 - Deng reinstated many competent officials who had been removed during the Cultural Revolution, creating a loyal support base and restoring experienced leadership to the CCP.

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Deng Xiaoping's emergence as the paramount leader of China was facilitated initially by the removal of the Gang of Four. ($\checkmark$) Their arrest in October 1976 eliminated the most radical faction within the CCP and marked the end of the Cultural Revolution. This allowed for the return of more moderate figures like Deng, as the party and the country moved away from the chaos and extremism associated with radical Maoism. ($\checkmark$) 

Deng, who had been purged twice during the Cultural Revolution, carefully built alliances with influential party officials, military leaders, and reform-minded bureaucrats. ($\checkmark$) His behind-the-scenes efforts positioned him as a unifying figure, with the political skills to gain support and ultimately reclaim power. ($\checkmark$) 

Another important factor was the weak leadership of Hua Guofeng. ($\checkmark$) Hua, Mao's designated successor, struggled with a lack of personal authority and vision. His policy of adhering to Mao's legacy, the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Whatevers," became unpopular, and Deng capitalized on this by gaining control over key party institutions, gradually sidelining Hua and asserting his own leadership. ($\checkmark$)
