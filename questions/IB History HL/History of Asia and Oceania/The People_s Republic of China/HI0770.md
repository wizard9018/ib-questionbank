# HI0770

What does Source B suggest about ideological tensions within the CCP after Mao's death?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$  $`$Two Whatevers' Do Not Accord With Marxism” written by Deng Xiaoping, May 24, 1977. An excerpt from a talk with two leading comrades of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China.

[Source: Deng, X. (1977, May 24) *`Two Whatevers' Do Not Accord With Marxism*. Retrieved May 14, 2025 from https://marxists.org/reference/archive/deng-xiaoping/1977/124.htm. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following

 - Reveals a divide between conservatives (pro-$\textit{\textquotedblleft}$Two Whatevers") and reformists.

 - Speaker represents the reformist side, promoting critical reassessment of Maoism.

 - Suggests the ideological struggle is also about political legitimacy (e.g., Tiananmen 1976 protests).

 - Shows that control over Mao's legacy was central to the post-Mao power struggle.

 - Reveals debates about historical interpretation and leadership accountability.

 - Implies internal debate within the Party leadership, not unified consensus.

 - Emphasizes the role of Marxist theory (historical materialism) as a battleground for legitimacy.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source B reveals a clear ideological divide within the CCP between conservatives, who supported the $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$*Two Whatevers"* and continued strict adherence to Mao's teachings, and reformists like Deng Xiaoping, who promoted a more critical and flexible approach to Mao's legacy. Deng's rejection of the $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$*Two Whatevers”* shows his belief that dogmatic loyalty was holding back progress and reform. ($\checkmark$) Secondly, the Source suggests that these ideological debates were tied to political legitimacy. Deng defends the 1976 Tiananmen protests, previously condemned by Maoists, as justified, using this to legitimize his own political comeback and challenge the verdicts of the Cultural Revolution. ($\checkmark$) Finally, the source shows that control over Mao's legacy was central to the power struggle. Deng is not rejecting Mao entirely but is arguing for a more nuanced, realistic view, which helped pave the way for post-Mao reform and modernization. ($\checkmark$)
