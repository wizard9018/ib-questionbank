# HI0765

Describe **three** ways the Chinese Communist Party attempted to improve health care in rural areas between 1949 and 1976.

[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - The barefoot doctor program, introduced in the 1960s, trained peasants in basic medicine and sent them to rural areas. These workers provided primary health services, first aid, and hygiene education, reflecting Mao's goal of practical, community-based care.

 - Health care was decentralized and organized around communes. Each commune often had a clinic or cooperative health station, integrating health into collective farming life.

 - Mass public health campaigns were launched to combat widespread diseases. These included vaccinations, pest control (*e.g., the $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$Four Pests Campaign”*), and improved sanitation, contributing to major reductions in infant mortality and infectious disease.

 - The state emphasized preventive care and hygiene, educating people on sanitation, nutrition, and disease prevention through posters, operas, and local propaganda.

 - Access to health care expanded significantly in rural regions where no services previously existed, improving life expectancy and basic health indicators.

Despite improvements, the system lacked trained medical staff and modern equipment, especially outside urban centers. Most barefoot doctors received only a few months of training.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One of the most influential initiatives to improve rural health was the barefoot doctor program, introduced in the 1960s. Under this scheme, local peasants received short-term medical training and were sent to villages to provide essential health services. These $\text{\textquotedblleft}$barefoot doctors" offered basic treatments, administered first aid, and educated communities on hygiene and disease prevention. The program was a practical, low-cost solution rooted in Mao Zedong's vision of accessible, community-based care, emphasizing prevention over specialization. ($\checkmark$)

In addition, health care delivery was decentralized and integrated into the commune system, which structured rural life during this period. Most communes had small clinics or cooperative health stations serving the collective population. This approach embedded medical services directly into the daily lives of rural workers, making health care more readily available in otherwise underserved areas. ($\checkmark$)

The CCP also launched nationwide public health campaigns to tackle major health threats. These mass mobilizations focused on improving sanitation, controlling pests, and vaccinating against common infectious diseases. Notable efforts included the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Four Pests Campaign,” which targeted disease-carrying animals like rats and mosquitoes. These campaigns played a crucial role in reducing rates of infectious diseases and infant mortality, laying the foundation for long-term improvements in public health. ($\checkmark$)
