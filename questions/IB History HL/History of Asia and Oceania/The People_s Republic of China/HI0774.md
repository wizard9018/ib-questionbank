# HI0774

Explain **two** consequences of land reform on China's countryside during the 1st and 2nd 5-year Plans.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - Destruction of the landlords

     - The Agrarian Reform Law was passed, in which land formerly owned by landlords was distributed to peasants.

     - Landlords and rich peasants were denounced in public trials and some were summarily executed in the countryside by party cadres.

 - Collectivisation

     - Peasants were put into agricultural producers cooperatives in which peasants pooled resources and worked together on the same land.

     - Large cooperative farms under state control were constructed, in which all peasants collectively own the land.

     - By the end of 1956 over 96$\%$ of peasant households were in collective farms. 

 - People's Communes 

     - The People's Communes were created as part of the government's attempt to centralise the control of resources for industrialisation.

     - By 1958, over 98$\%$ of the farming population resided in communes. 

     - The People's Communes administration controlled the commune's agricultural and industrial resources.

     - The communes provided welfare for the peasants and workers by operating schools, nurseries and public kitchens. 

     - The production teams within the commune were organized in a military fashion to raise output. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The first and foremost impact was the destruction of the landlord class that dominated China for over a millennium. This was achieved by the Agrarian Reform Law, which confiscated the landlord's land and redistributed it to peasants ($\checkmark$). Also, when the Communist Party took over the countryside, the party cadres organized public trials to denounce landlords and rich peasants, in which some of them were executed ($\checkmark$). 

After the destruction of the landlords and the land redistributed, the government put peasants into large cooperative farms that were under state control, and by the end of 1956, over 96$\%$ of the peasant population were in collective farms ($\checkmark$). Inside the state-owned collective farms were the People's Communes, responsible for managing the resources and labor along military lines to raise agricultural output to meet the state quota ($\checkmark$).
