# HI0763

Describe the role of the Red Guards in the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution.

[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - Red Guards were often teenagers from urban schools and universities who were mobilised by Mao as revolutionary agents. He directly encouraged students to rise against $\text{\textquotedblleft}$capitalist roaders” within the Party. 

 - Red Guards attacked the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Four Olds”, which included old customs, culture, habits, and ideas. They destroyed temples, burned books, and renamed streets, aiming to erase traditional Chinese culture.

 - Red Guards led the violent persecution of perceived enemies, including teachers, intellectuals, former landlords, and anyone accused of being anti-revolutionary. They were humiliated, beaten, imprisoned, or killed.

 - Red guards led public denunciations and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$struggle sessions”. Victims were paraded, forced to confess, and publicly shamed. These events instilled fear and reinforced Maoist ideology.

 - Brought up on Mao's cult of personality, the Red Guards worshipped Mao, carrying his Little Red Book and treating his words as absolute truth. This helped cement Mao's supremacy over the CCP.

 - As the GPCR progressed, rival groups of Red Guards began to clash, leading to chaos and civil strife. Mao eventually had to intervene. As order broke down, Mao turned to the army to restore control. Many Red Guards were sent to the countryside to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$learn from the peasants", effectively ending their political role. 

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Red Guards played a central and highly influential role during the early years of the Cultural Revolution, acting as Mao Zedong's chosen agents to carry out his radical ideas. Composed mostly of teenagers and young adults from urban schools and universities, the Red Guards were mobilized by Mao to challenge and uproot perceived $\text{\textquotedblleft}$capitalist roaders" and enemies within the Chinese Communist Party. Mao's direct endorsement gave them the authority and zeal to confront established figures and institutions in the name of revolutionary purity. ($\checkmark$)

One of their key missions was to attack the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Four Olds": old customs, old culture, old habits, and old ideas. In pursuit of this goal, Red Guards launched campaigns to erase traditional Chinese heritage, including vandalizing temples, burning historical texts, destroying cultural relics, and renaming streets and institutions. These actions were driven by the belief that old cultural elements were obstacles to the progress of socialism and needed to be eradicated. ($\checkmark$)

By 1969, the situation had grown increasingly unstable. As a result, Mao, along with key figures like Premier Zhou Enlai and military leaders such as Lin Biao, turned to the People's Liberation Army (PLA) to suppress the Red Guards and restore order. Many of the youth were sent to the countryside in the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Down to the Countryside Movement" to be $\text{\textquotedblleft}$re-educated" by peasants, effectively removing them from urban political life and ending their revolutionary activism. ($\checkmark$)
