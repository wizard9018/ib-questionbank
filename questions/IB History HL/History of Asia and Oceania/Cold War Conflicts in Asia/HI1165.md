# HI1165

Explain **three** political effects of the USSR's invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with explanation:
- Collapse of Détente and Renewed Cold War Tensions
    - The invasion ended détente, prompting U.S. condemnation and sanctions.
    - The Carter Doctrine (1980) and Western rearmament signalled a return to confrontation. Tthe “Second Cold War.”
- Soviet Diplomatic Isolation.
    - The USSR lost support among non-aligned and Muslim-majority nations, facing repeated UN condemnation.
    - Even allies like India and Yugoslavia distanced themselves; China denounced the invasion as imperialist.
- U.S. Proxy War Response
    - The U.S. launched Operation Cyclone, arming Afghan Mujahideen through Pakistan.
    - Afghanistan became a major Cold War battleground, worsening global tensions and Soviet losses.
- Domestic Political Impact in the USSR
    - The war became unpopular as casualties mounted and censorship failed to hide the truth.
    - It divided Party leaders, undermined faith in the regime, and contributed to leadership instability in the 1980s.
- Weakening of the Communist Bloc
    - The invasion strained relations with China and Eastern Europe, revealing cracks in the Soviet alliance system.
    - The USSR's moral and ideological authority declined as the war dragged on without victory.
- Gorbachev's Rise and Policy Shift
    - The war's failure pushed Gorbachev to pursue reform and withdraw Soviet troops (1989).
    - Marked a turn away from military interventionism toward diplomacy and openness with the West.
- Contribution to the USSR's Collapse
    - The conflict exposed Soviet weakness and undermined communist legitimacy.
    - Together with economic strain, it accelerated the decline and dissolution of the USSR (1991).
- Broader Regional Effects
    - Left Afghanistan unstable, empowering Mujahideen fighters who later formed the Taliban and al-Qaeda.
    - Strengthened Pakistan's role as a U.S. ally and reshaped power dynamics in the Middle East and South Asia.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The invasion led to the end of détente and a renewal of the Cold War ($\checkmark$). The U.S. and its allies condemned the Soviet action as aggressive and expansionist, ending the period detente. The Carter Doctrine (1980) promised to defend the Persian Gulf from the Soviets, while the U.S. also imposed economic sanctions, cancelled SALT II, and boycotted the 1980 Moscow Olympics. Western nations also increased defence spending, marking the start of the “Second Cold War”($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the invasion left the USSR politically isolated ($\checkmark$). Many non-aligned and Muslim-majority countries condemned the intervention, and the United Nations repeatedly criticised Soviet actions. Long-standing partners like India and Yugoslavia were also uneasy, while China denounced the move as imperialist ($\checkmark$).

Finally, the war's failure contributed to significant political change within the USSR ($\checkmark$). By 1985, Gorbachev recognised the conflict as a political disaster and introduced glasnost (openness) and perestroika (restructuring) to reform Soviet politics and foreign policy. His decision to withdraw Soviet troops in 1989 marked a shift away from military interventionism toward diplomacy. The war accelerated the eventual collapse of the USSR in 1991 ($\checkmark$).
