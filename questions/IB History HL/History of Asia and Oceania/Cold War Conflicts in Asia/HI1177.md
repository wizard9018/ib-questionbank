# HI1177

Identify **one** reason the Soviet Union boycotted the United Nations vote on Korea in June 1950 and explain **two** ways this affected the outcome of the UN decision to intervene in the Korean conflict.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **one** reason and any **two** effects:

Reason for the Soviet boycott
- In January 1950, the Soviet Union began boycotting the United Nations to protest the continued recognition of the Republic of China (ROC) (Chiang Kai-shek's government in Taiwan) instead of the People's Republic of China (PRC) (Mao Zedong's communist regime).
- The USSR argued that the PRC was the legitimate representative of China and that the ROC had lost control of the mainland after the Chinese Civil War (1949).
- The boycott was intended to pressure the UN to transfer China's seat to the PRC.
- As a result, the Soviet delegate was absent when the UN Security Council convened on 25 June 1950, following North Korea's invasion of South Korea.

Impact on the UN Security Council decision
- With the USSR absent, the Security Council lacked a communist veto power, which normally could have blocked Western-led resolutions.
- The UN Security Council passed Resolution 82 (June 25, 1950), condemning North Korea's invasion and calling for an immediate withdrawal.
- Two days later, it passed Resolution 83, recommending that UN member states provide military assistance to South Korea to repel the attack.
- Because of the boycott, the Soviet veto was not exercised, allowing the United States to secure UN authorisation for collective military intervention.
- The decision effectively made the Korean War the first UN-backed military operation in history.

Consequences for the Korean War
- The US led the UN coalition, contributing the majority of troops, resources, and command leadership under General Douglas MacArthur.
- The absence of the USSR gave the US-led forces international legitimacy, framing intervention as a collective action to uphold international peace, rather than unilateral American aggression.
- The UN's backing allowed the US to act decisively and swiftly, stabilising the South and later advancing beyond the 38th parallel.
- The Soviet miscalculation was widely seen as a major diplomatic blunder, since their presence could have prevented UN approval of military intervention.

Broader Cold War significance
- The episode demonstrated how Soviet diplomacy could inadvertently strengthen Western influence in international institutions.
- The Korean War solidified the US role as leader of the “Free World”, using the UN as a tool for collective security.
- It prompted the USSR to return to the UN Security Council in August 1950, determined never to miss another vote.
- The crisis intensified East–West confrontation, setting the tone for future proxy wars under UN auspices.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Soviet Union boycotted the United Nations in early 1950 to protest the UN's continued recognition of the Republic of China (ROC). Moscow argued that the People's Republic of China (PRC), not the Nationalists on Taiwan, should hold China's UN seat after the Communist victory in 1949 ($\checkmark$).

This boycott had two key effects on the outcome of the UN decision. First, the UN Security Council passed Resolution 82, condemning North Korea's aggression and demanding withdrawal. Soon after, Resolution 83 authorised member states to provide military support to South Korea under UN command ($\checkmark$). Second, the decision strengthened the U.S. position in East Asia by demonstrating its leadership within the UN and its commitment to containing communism. This, in turn, increased the strategic importance of other regional allies like Taiwan, Japan, and the Philippines within the broader Cold War security network ($\checkmark$).
