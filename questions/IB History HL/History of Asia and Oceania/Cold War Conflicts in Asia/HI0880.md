# HI0880

Identify **two** key failures of Prince Sihanouk's rule before the rise of the Khmer Rouge.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- Economic mismanagement: Sihanouk's regime suffered from growing corruption and inefficiency, especially in state-run enterprises. Misuse of resources and lack of economic reform led to stagnation, worsening inequality and weakening popular support, particularly among rural populations.
- Neutrality policy issues: Sihanouk's attempt to remain neutral during the Vietnam War backfired. His tolerance of North Vietnamese activity in Cambodia angered the U.S., while communists at home viewed him as compromising, increasing domestic and external tensions.
- Political repression: Sihanouk tightly controlled political life, banning opposition parties and suppressing dissent. This fostered resentment, especially among students and leftist groups, some of whom later joined or supported the Khmer Rouge movement.
- Over-reliance on personal rule/authoritarianism: Sihanouk concentrated power on himself, sidelining institutions and creating a patronage-based system. His rule became increasingly out of touch with everyday Cambodians, eroding legitimacy and leaving the country vulnerable to radical alternatives.
- Failure to anticipate rural discontent: Sihanouk underestimated the depth of rural grievances, particularly over land inequality and government neglect. His regime's failure to address these issues allowed the Khmer Rouge to gain support by promising justice and reform.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, under Sihanouk, widespread corruption and poorly run state enterprises damaged the economy ($\checkmark$). The lack of reform and uneven wealth distribution led to economic stagnation and growing dissatisfaction, especially among Cambodia's rural majority ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, Sihanouk limited political freedoms by outlawing opposition groups and cracking down on dissent ($\checkmark$). This alienated many, particularly urban students and leftists, some of whom later supported or joined the Khmer Rouge ($\checkmark$).
