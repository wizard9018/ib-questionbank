# HI0909

Outline the internal political developments in Afghanistan between 1978 and 1979 that prompted Soviet intervention.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Saur Revolution (April 1978): The communist People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA) overthrew President Mohammed Daoud Khan. Daoud and his family were executed, ending the Republic of Afghanistan and replacing it with a Marxist regime.
- Emergence of PDPA rule under Nur Muhammad Taraki: Taraki became President and implemented radical socialist reforms (e.g. land redistribution, women's rights, secularisation). These reforms clashed with traditional tribal, Islamic, and rural values, causing widespread resentment.
- Factionalism within the PDPA: PDPA was split into two rival factions: Khalq (dominant, led by Taraki and later Amin) and Parcham (led by Babrak Karmal). Internal purges and repression targeted Parcham members and other opponents.
- Brutal repression of dissent: Mass arrests, torture, and executions by the secret police (AGSA). Tens of thousands of political prisoners were taken; many were religious leaders and tribal elders.
- Growing armed resistance and instability: Anti-communist insurgencies (mujahideen) began to emerge across rural areas. Civil unrest spread rapidly, threatening the PDPA's control of the countryside.
- Taraki-Amin power struggle (1979): Deputy Prime Minister Hafizullah Amin gained increasing control and eventually overthrew and killed Taraki in October 1979. Amin's leadership was viewed as erratic, brutal, and unpredictable, even by the Soviets.
- Soviet distrust of Amin: The USSR feared Amin might turn to the West or lose control of the country to Islamist forces. Reports suggested Amin had secret contacts with the U.S., deepening Soviet paranoia.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, the Saur Revolution in April 1978 brought the communist People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA) to power. The new regime, led by Nur Muhammad Taraki, introduced radical reforms such as land redistribution and the promotion of women's rights, which sparked widespread resistance from conservative and rural communities ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, growing internal divisions within the PDPA, especially between the Khalq and Parcham factions, led to political instability and purges, weakening the government's legitimacy and control ($\checkmark$).

Finally, the rise of Hafizullah Amin following his overthrow and killing of Taraki in 1979 alarmed the Soviet leadership. They viewed Amin as unpredictable and feared he might seek support from the West or fail to contain the rising Islamist insurgency. These developments convinced the USSR that intervention was necessary to preserve a friendly regime on its southern border ($\checkmark$).
