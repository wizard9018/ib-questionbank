# HI0883

How does Pol Pot present the role of the Communist Party of Kampuchea in shaping society in Source A?
[3 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from a speech by Pol Pot, leader of Cambodia at the time, titled “Long Live the 17th Anniversary of the Communist Party of Kampuchea,” September 29, 1977. |

[Source: Pot. P. (1977, September 29) *Long Live the 17th Anniversary of the Communist Party of Kampuchea.* Marxists.org. Retrieved June 19, 2025 from https://www.marxists.org/archive/pol-pot/1977/cpk-17th-aniv.pdf. Public Domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The Party is presented as the supreme guiding force: Pol Pot frames the Party as the central authority in both governance and moral development. Its leadership is portrayed as essential to managing contradictions in society and guiding the revolutionary path; “under the leadership of the Party.”
- The Party leads the educational and revolutionary transformation process: Education is not portrayed as neutral or optional, but as a tool for ideological transformation. The Party directs efforts to reshape individuals' thinking and behaviour to align with revolutionary goals. “It is important to consistently carry out thorough-going educational work.”
- The Party supervises criticism and self-criticism, implying control over personal beliefs and behaviour. Individuals are expected to monitor and correct themselves with the Party's help. This system of “criticism and self-criticism” is a form of surveillance and ideological discipline.
- The Party demands total ideological and social obedience to its goals: Pol Pot stresses the need for the people to adopt a revolutionary lifestyle, eliminating personal desires in favour of collective progress as defined by the Party; “gradually eliminating the idea of private ownership”, “each of us becomes a revolutionary of the new Kampuchea”.
- The Party assumes responsibility for personal transformation. Pol Pot suggests that the Party's role is not only to shape society but also to reshape the very character of its people, turning them into ideal socialist citizens; “to continue to build the revolutionary strength of the people.”
- The Party integrates itself into every aspect of national life (education, lifestyle, and national reconstruction): There is no distinction between state, society, and individual development; the Party permeates all layers of daily existence, from labour to thought.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, Pol Pot depicts the Party as the supreme guiding authority, responsible not only for political leadership but also for the moral direction of the people. He emphasises that societal contradictions can only be resolved “under the leadership of the Party,” indicating that no progress or unity is possible without its control ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, Pol Pot highlights the Party's role in leading the ideological transformation of individuals. He does not view education as neutral, but rather as a means to reshape citizens' beliefs and behaviours in line with revolutionary values. This is reflected in his insistence that “it is important to consistently carry out thorough-going educational work,” making it clear that the Party oversees both learning and re-education as tools of social control ($\checkmark$).

Thirdly, Pol Pot calls for complete ideological and social conformity to the Party's goals. He outlines the need to “gradually eliminate the idea of private ownership,” showing how collective ideals must replace individual identity and desires. He describes the ideal citizen as someone who “becomes a revolutionary of the new Kampuchea,” suggesting that personal worth is measured entirely by loyalty to the Party's vision ($\checkmark$).
