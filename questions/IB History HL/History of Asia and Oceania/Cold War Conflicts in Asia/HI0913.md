# HI0913

Describe economic and political impacts of the Korean War on North Korea (DPRK).
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following. Responses may include two points for each side (2/2), or be weighted (3/1 or 1/3), as long as both political and economic impacts are addressed.

Political:
- Solidification of Kim Il-Sung's regime. War enabled the purging of rivals and consolidation of absolute power.
Increased dependence on Soviet and Chinese support. USSR and China helped rebuild the North and entrenched its communist orientation.
- Adoption of strict totalitarian control. Surveillance, repression, and cult of personality intensified after the war.
- Permanent division of Korea at the 38th parallel. No peace treaty signed; both states claimed to be the sole legitimate government of Korea.
- Ongoing militarization and hostility. Heavily fortified DMZ created; tensions and propaganda persisted for decades.

Economic:
- Severe war destruction. U.S. bombings devastated nearly all major cities and infrastructure.
- High civilian and military losses, along with widespread displacement, placed a heavy burden on the economy. Massive human casualties(estimated at over 1 million) and widespread population displacement, severely strained the labor force and hindered post-war recovery.
- Post-war reconstruction supported by Soviet and Chinese aid. North Korea initially recovered faster than the South in the 1950s.
- State-controlled economy entrenched. Reconstruction focused on heavy industry under central planning.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Politically, the conflict enabled Kim Il-Sung to consolidate his authority by eliminating internal rivals and strengthening his grip on power ($\checkmark$). The war also deepened North Korea's dependence on the Soviet Union and China, reinforcing its alignment with communist ideology and entrenching a totalitarian regime marked by surveillance and repression ($\checkmark$).

Economically, North Korea suffered extensive destruction due to heavy U.S. bombing, which devastated cities, factories, and infrastructure ($\checkmark$). Despite these challenges, post-war reconstruction was made possible through substantial aid from the USSR and China, allowing the country to initially recover quickly by focusing on heavy industry under a centrally planned, state-controlled economy ($\checkmark$).
