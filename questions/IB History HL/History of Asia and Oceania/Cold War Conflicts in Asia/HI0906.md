# HI0906

Describe the role of Ho Chi Minh in the struggle for Vietnamese independence between 1946 and 1954.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:

Political Leadership and Vision
- Founder and leader of the Viet Minh (1941): Created the League for the Independence of Vietnam to fight Japanese occupation and later French colonialism.
- Symbol of national unity and anti-colonial resistance: Embodied the struggle for independence and appealed to Vietnamese nationalism. Seen by many as the legitimate leader of a unified Vietnam.
- Clear ideological framework: Combined Vietnamese nationalism with Marxist-Leninist ideology. Positioned the struggle as both anti-imperialist and revolutionary.

Diplomatic Efforts
- Declaration of Independence (1945): Proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi after Japan's surrender. Quoted the U.S. Declaration of Independence and the French Rights of Man to appeal for international support.
- Attempted negotiations with France (1946): Initially sought a peaceful resolution with French leaders at the Fontainebleau Conference. Efforts failed, leading to the outbreak of war in December 1946.
- Maintained international relations with key allies: Gained recognition and support from the Soviet Union and China, strengthening the Viet Minh's position.

Military and Strategic Direction
- Oversaw the Viet Minh's military resistance against the French: Provided leadership and direction for the armed struggle during the First Indochina War. Promoted a strategy of protracted guerrilla warfare to wear down French forces.
- Encouraged political and military mobilisation: Inspired mass participation from peasants and workers.
- Supported the spread of revolutionary education and land reform in Viet Minh-controlled areas.
- Backed key military leaders like General Vo Nguyen Giap: Allowed capable subordinates to lead military operations, such as the planning of Dien Bien Phu.

Propaganda and Ideological Influence
- Effective use of propaganda: Portrayed the Viet Minh as a liberation force fighting for the people. Depicted the French as oppressive imperialists and rallied support for the resistance.
- Promoted the idea of an independent, unified Vietnam: Framed the conflict as a continuation of Vietnam's long history of resisting foreign domination (e.g., Chinese, French, Japanese).

Contribution to Victory and Legacy
- Key figure behind the eventual Viet Minh victory at Dien Bien Phu (1954): Though not a military commander, his leadership enabled the long-term strategy that led to victory. The defeat forced the French to negotiate and led to the Geneva Accords.
- Influenced the Geneva negotiations: Ho's government gained partial international recognition. Although Vietnam was temporarily divided, the North was under Viet Minh control, which laid the foundation for further conflict and eventual reunification.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Ho Chi Minh played a vital political role as the founder of the Viet Minh in 1941 and later as the symbolic leader of Vietnamese resistance during the First Indochina War (1946-1954). He represented national unity and anti-colonialism, gaining widespread support as the legitimate leader of a future independent Vietnam ($\checkmark$),

Secondly, although not a military commander, Ho Chi Minh guided the overall direction of the Viet Minh's armed struggle. He advocated a strategy of prolonged guerrilla warfare to weaken French forces and supported capable military leaders, especially General Vo Nguyen Giap, who led key campaigns against the French ($\checkmark$).

Finally, Ho's long-term leadership contributed directly to the Viet Minh's success at Dien Bien Phu in 1954, a turning point in the war. This defeat forced the French to the negotiating table, resulting in the Geneva Accords, which marked a major step toward Vietnamese independence and secured the North under Viet Minh control ($\checkmark$).
