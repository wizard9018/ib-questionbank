# HI0828

According to Source A, what justification does the United States give for military action in Southeast Asia?
[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Defense against attack: U.S. naval vessels were allegedly “deliberately and repeatedly attacked” by North Vietnamese forces while “lawfully present in international waters.”
- Violation of international law: The source claims these attacks violated the Charter of the United Nations and international law, framing the response as a defense of international norms.
- Threat to international peace: The attacks are described as a “serious threat to international peace,” suggesting U.S. action is required to maintain global stability.
- Collective defense: The resolution emphasizes that the U.S. is assisting “nations joined with them in the collective defense of their freedom,” linking the action to alliances like SEATO.
- Support for freedom: It states that the U.S. desires that the “peoples [of Southeast Asia] should be left in peace to work out their own destinies,” portraying U.S. involvement as defending self-determination.
- No territorial ambitions: The U.S. claims to have “no territorial, military or political ambitions” in the region, presenting its actions as altruistic.
- Presidential authority: The resolution supports the President's power to “take all necessary measures” to repel attacks and prevent further aggression, reinforcing executive responsibility to act swiftly in defense.
- Vital national interest: The U.S. declares the region to be of “vital national interest and to world peace,” justifying intervention on strategic and global stability grounds.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, the United States claims that its naval vessels were “deliberately and repeatedly attacked” by North Vietnamese forces while “lawfully present in international waters.” This suggests that military action was justified as a direct act of self-defense ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the resolution argues that these attacks violated “the principles of the Charter of the United Nations and of international law.” This positions U.S. involvement as a legitimate response aimed at upholding international legal norms ($\checkmark$).

Finally, the source describes the attacks as “a serious threat to international peace,” implying that U.S. intervention was necessary to maintain global stability and prevent further aggression in the region ($\checkmark$).
