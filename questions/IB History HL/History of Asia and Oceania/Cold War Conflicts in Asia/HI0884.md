# HI0884

Explain **two** ways Source A could help a historian understand the ideology behind the Khmer Rouge's policies.
[4 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from a speech by Pol Pot, leader of Cambodia at the time, titled “Long Live the 17th Anniversary of the Communist Party of Kampuchea,” September 29, 1977. |

[Source: Pot. P. (1977, September 29) *Long Live the 17th Anniversary of the Communist Party of Kampuchea.* Marxists.org. Retrieved June 19, 2025 from https://www.marxists.org/archive/pol-pot/1977/cpk-17th-aniv.pdf. Public Domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** points with supporting explanation:
- Maoist influence and ongoing revolution: The source reflects Maoist ideology, particularly the concept of permanent revolution. Pol Pot describes “contradictions among the people” caused by lingering class traits, which must be corrected through “criticism and self-criticism.” This illustrates the regime's emphasis on ongoing re-education to maintain ideological purity and revolutionary discipline.
- Justification for eliminating class enemies: Pol Pot's focus on eradicating “old class character” explains why the Khmer Rouge targeted intellectuals, minorities, and religious groups. The regime saw such individuals as incapable of transformation and threats to revolutionary unity. The speech implies social cleansing was necessary to create a truly classless, obedient society.
- Ideological purity over individual identity: The source emphasises total submission to collective ideology. Pol Pot urges citizens to “become a revolutionary of the new Kampuchea,” abandoning personal ambition for the revolution. The promotion of “collectivist and socialist ownership” reveals the regime's demand that individual identity be replaced with loyalty to the Party's vision.
- Radical restructuring of society: Pol Pot's call to “eliminate the idea of private ownership” helps explain radical Khmer Rouge policies like abolishing money, destroying religious institutions, and relocating city dwellers. The goal was to erase all remnants of the old society and build a self-sufficient, ideologically pure agrarian utopia under Party control.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, Pol Pot's emphasis on ongoing ideological transformation reflects the Maoist belief in continuous revolution and mass re-education ($\checkmark$). The source speaks of “contradictions among the people” due to lingering “vestiges of our old class character,” which he argues must be corrected through “education, study, criticism and self-criticism.” This reflects Maoist methods of ideological purification and social engineering, where people are constantly pushed to re-align their thoughts and behaviours to fit the revolutionary ideal ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the speech demonstrates a worldview in which those representing pre-revolutionary social status or thinking are seen as threats ($\checkmark$). Pol Pot suggests that all Cambodians must undergo ideological transformation, but those who cannot or will not, such as intellectuals, religious leaders, and ethnic minorities, were often labelled enemies of the revolution. The mention of “deep-rooted” class characteristics and the need to “eliminate the idea of private ownership” supports the view that the Khmer Rouge sought to remove all elements associated with class hierarchy and capitalist values, often through violent means ($\checkmark$).
