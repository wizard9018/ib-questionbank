# HI0666

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the values and limitations of Source C for an historian studying Truman's decision to involve the USA in Korea in 1951.

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from a radio address titled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Report to the American People on Korea” by US President Harry Truman on April 11, 1951.

\

\

It is right for us to be in Korea now. It was right last June. It is right today.

\

\

I want to remind you why this is true.

\

\

The Communists in the Kremlin are engaged in a monstrous conspiracy to stamp out freedom all over the world. If they were to succeed, the United States would be numbered among their principal victims. It must be clear to everyone that the United States cannot—and will not—sit idly by and await foreign conquest. The only question is: What is the best time to meet the threat and how is the best way to meet it?

\

\

The best time to meet the threat is in the beginning. It is easier to put out a fire in the beginning when it is small than after it has become a roaring blaze. And the best way to meet the threat of aggression is for the peace-loving nations to act together. If they don't act together, they are likely to be picked off, one by one. [...]

\

\

If history has taught us anything, it is that aggression anywhere in the world is a threat to peace everywhere in the world. When that aggression is supported by the cruel and selfish rulers of a powerful nation who are bent on conquest, it becomes a dear and present danger to the security and independence of every free nation. [...]

\

\

The aggression against Korea is the boldest and most dangerous move the Communists have yet made.

\

\

The attack on Korea was part of a greater plan for conquering all of Asia. [...]

\

\

]

[Source: Truman\, H. (1951, April 11). *Report to the American People on Korea.* US National Archives via the Miller Center. Retrieved May 11, 2025 from https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-speeches/april-11-1951-report-american-people-korea. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:

 - It is a speech by President Truman that gives us a direct insight into the political perspective of the time.

 - It aims to reassure the American public that the US will take action against aggressive countries and provides justification for US involvement in Korea.

 - The source shows the public the potential danger if the US does not act. There could be long-term consequences for their security, and it might lead to World War III.

For limitations, any of the following: 

 - The source is a speech to the American people and shows only the US perspective of the threat.

 - The source was written during the crisis by the person who created the containment policy, which was being applied to Korea, so it seeks to support that policy.

 - The source uses emotive language that portrays the threat as dangerous to America's freedom.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The origin of this source can be considered both a value and a limitation to an historian studying Truman's decision to involve the USA in Korea. The source is a speech by President Truman, so it's value lies in that it gives us a direct insight into the political perspective that existed in America at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The limitations of the origin are because it is addressed solely to the American people and shows only a US perspective of the threat ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). 

The content of the source shows the public perception of the potential danger to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the security and independence of every free nation" and that America needs to act to prevent a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$third World War" ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). However, the language of the source is very emotive. It portrays the situation as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$monstrous" and that the blame lies with the communists, which indicates a clear bias toward the US perspective ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$). 

The purpose is to reassure the American public that the US will act quickly and protect the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$threat to peace" that the crisis poses ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The limitation of purpose is that it is stated by Truman, who, as the creator of the containment policy, is also looking to support and justify that policy ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).
