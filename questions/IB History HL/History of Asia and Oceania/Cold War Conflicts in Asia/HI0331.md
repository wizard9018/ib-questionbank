# HI0331

What, according to Source B, does the war in Vietnam reveal about the nature of the United States' involvement in the Cold War?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | John F. Kerry. Statement before the U.S. Senate Committee on Foreign Relations, 22 April 1971. |

[Source: Kerry, J. F. (1971, April 22). *Statement before the U.S. Senate Committee on Foreign Relations.* Retrieved October 25, 2025 from: https://voicesofdemocracy.umd.edu/kerry-statement-before-the-senate-committee-speech-text/. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- U.S. involvement in the Cold War was based on distortion and misinformation, as veterans describe the official justification as a “terrible distortion” and a “mystical war against communism.”
- It portrays the war as morally corrupt, claiming America “lost her sense of morality” and accepted atrocities like My Lai.
- Implies that U.S. leaders misunderstood the conflict, calling Vietnam “a civil war” and “an effort by a people… seeking their liberation,” not a front in a global fight against communism.
- The U.S. is depicted as hypocritical, since linking the deaths of soldiers to “the preservation of freedom” is called “the height of criminal hypocrisy.”
- The war is shown to have damaged the United States internally, with such hypocrisy having “torn this country apart.”
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
According to Source B, the war in Vietnam revealed that the United States' involvement in the Cold War in Asia was based on distortion and false justification. The veterans describe official claims of a “mystical war against communism” as a “terrible distortion,” suggesting U.S. leaders misrepresented the conflict ($\checkmark$).

They also imply that America had become morally corrupted, saying the country “lost her sense of morality” and accepted atrocities like My Lai without question ($\checkmark$).

Finally, the source portrays U.S. actions as hypocritical and self-destructive, condemning attempts to link soldiers' deaths to “the preservation of freedom” as “the height of criminal hypocrisy,” which had “torn this country apart" ($\checkmark$).
