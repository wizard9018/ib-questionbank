# HI0912

Describe economic and political impacts of the Korean War on South Korea (ROK).
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following. Responses may include two points for each side (2/2), or be weighted (3/1 or 1/3), as long as both political and economic impacts are addressed.

Political:
- Strengthened authoritarian leadership under President Syngman Rhee. Wartime chaos allowed Rhee to consolidate power and suppress political opposition.
- Increased dependence on the United States for security and governance. U.S. military and financial aid tied South Korea closely to Western interests.
- Establishment of a pro-Western, anti-communist regime, shaping Cold War alignment.
- Democratic reforms delayed as national security concerns dominated politics.
- Permanent division of Korea at the 38th parallel. No peace treaty signed; both states claimed to be the sole legitimate government of Korea.
- Ongoing militarization and hostility. Heavily fortified DMZ created; tensions and propaganda persisted for decades.

Economic:
- Widespread destruction of infrastructure and industry. Seoul changed hands four times; cities, railways, and factories were heavily damaged.
- High civilian casualties and displacement strained the economy. Over 1 million South Koreans killed and millions more displaced.
- Initial economic stagnation due to war damage and instability.
- Massive U.S. economic aid led to post-war recovery and eventual growth. The U.S. funded reconstruction and provided food, fuel, and equipment.
- Militarization of the economy began, laying the foundations for later industrialization.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Politically, the war strengthened the authoritarian leadership of President Syngman Rhee, who used the conflict to suppress opposition and consolidate power ($\checkmark$). Additionally, South Korea became heavily reliant on the United States for military and economic support, solidifying its alignment with the West and delaying democratic reforms due to ongoing security concerns ($\checkmark$).

Economically, the war caused widespread destruction across South Korea, with cities like Seoul changing hands multiple times and infrastructure left in ruins ($\checkmark$). However, the large-scale U.S. aid that followed enabled reconstruction and eventually contributed to economic recovery, laying the groundwork for South Korea's later industrial development ($\checkmark$).
