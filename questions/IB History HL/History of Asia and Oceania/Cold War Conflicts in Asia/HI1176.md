# HI1176

Explain **three** ways the Korean War (1950-53) affected Taiwan's strategic importance to the United States.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with supporting explanation:

The Korean War's outbreak and regional implications:
- The North Korean invasion of South Korea (June 1950), supported by the USSR and later China, transformed US perceptions of communist expansion in Asia.
- The war appeared to confirm the “domino theory”, that communism was spreading aggressively across Asia.
- The US concluded that Taiwan could be the next target in this pattern of communist aggression.

Shift in US policy toward Taiwan:
- President Harry Truman ordered the US Seventh Fleet into the Taiwan Strait (June 1950) to prevent PRC invasion and ROC attacks on the mainland.
- This marked a decisive reversal of earlier non-intervention policy, placing Taiwan under de facto US protection.
- The Korean War thus elevated Taiwan from a Chinese civil war remnant to a strategic Cold War asset.

Strategic importance within the containment policy
- Taiwan became a crucial link in the US containment network in East Asia, alongside Japan, South Korea, and the Philippines.
- Its geographical position (off China's coast, near major sea lanes) made it a forward base for intelligence, naval operations, and surveillance of mainland China.
- The ROC regime was now portrayed as part of the “Free World”, defending Asia from communist encroachment.
- The island's defence became symbolic of US resolve to confront communism after the perceived failure to “save” China in 1949.

Diplomatic and military outcomes
- The US began supplying economic and military aid to the ROC, strengthening Chiang Kai-shek's regime.
- In 1954, the Sino-American Mutual Defense Treaty formalised US commitment to defend Taiwan and the Pescadores Islands.
- Taiwan's inclusion in the US-led alliance system reflected the post-Korean War expansion of American global commitments.

Broader Cold War implications
- The Korean War reinforced the bipolar division of East Asia. Communist mainland China vs US-supported Taiwan.
- It entrenched Taiwan's position as a long-term flashpoint between communist and capitalist blocs.
- It contributed to the militarisation of the Pacific, with a permanent US naval and air presence in the region.
- The war also hardened China's hostility toward both Taiwan and the United States, setting the stage for the First Taiwan Strait Crisis (1954-55).
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Korean War (1950-53) greatly increased Taiwan's strategic importance as the war convinced U.S. policymakers that communist expansion in Asia was coordinated, raising fears that Taiwan might be the next target of aggression after Korea ($\checkmark$). This shifted Taiwan from a regional issue to the frontline of the global Cold War ($\checkmark$).

Second, Taiwan became a critical link in the U.S. containment network in East Asia, along with Japan, South Korea, and the Philippines. It formed part of a defensive “island chain” against the communists ($\checkmark$). This integration reinforced the U.S. strategy of encircling communist powers and building allies along the Pacific rim ($\checkmark$).

Finally, Taiwan's geographic position made it an ideal forward base for monitoring China and projecting American military power across the Western Pacific, reinforcing U.S. strategic dominance in the region ($\checkmark$).  The U.S. could observe the Chinese Communists and maintain a rapid response capability in future crises ($\checkmark$).
