# HI0885

In Source B, how does Truman justify US involvement in Korea?
[3 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Preventing a third World War: Truman argues that the U.S. is acting in Korea to avoid a larger global conflict: “In the simplest terms, what we are doing in Korea is this: We are trying to prevent a third world war.” Suggesting early intervention stops wider wars before they escalate.
- Containment of communism: Truman frames Communism as a global threat that must be stopped: “The Communists in the Kremlin are engaged in a monstrous conspiracy to stamp out freedom all over the world.” “They want to control all Asia from the Kremlin.” Describes Korea as one front in a global ideological struggle.
- Lessons from History: Truman uses historical analogy to justify early action: “It is easier to put out a fire in the beginning... than after it has become a roaring blaze.” Compares current inaction to the failures of the 1930s: “If they had acted in the beginning... there probably would have been no World War II.”
- Collective security & UN cooperation: Justifies U.S. action as part of a broader international responsibility: “The best way to meet the threat of aggression is for the peace-loving nations to act together.” “This is the basic reason why we joined in creating the United Nations.” Implies that failing to act would undermine the UN and global security.
- Precedent from other Cold War conflicts: Cites successful past interventions to show Korea fits a pattern: “That is what we did in Greece... The United Nations supported Greek resistance.” “Another big Communist threat... was the Berlin blockade... it was settled because free men would not back down.”
- Direct Communist threat to Asia: Argues Korea is part of a larger Communist plan for conquest. Cites secret Communist speech: “Java, Indochina, Malaya, India, Tibet, Thailand, Philippines, and Japan are our ultimate targets... The United States is the only obstacle.” Claims the goal is “the long-awaited world revolution.”
- Defence of freedom and democracy: Positions the U.S. as protector of free nations: “Aggression anywhere... is a threat to the peace everywhere in the world.” Suggests America has a moral duty to resist tyranny and oppression.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, Truman justifies U.S. involvement in Korea by presenting it as a necessary step to prevent another global conflict. He argues that intervention is meant to stop the outbreak of a larger war, stating, “In the simplest terms, . . . We are trying to prevent a third world war"($\checkmark$).

Secondly, he portrays communism as a global threat that must be contained. Truman claims the Soviet Union is leading a coordinated effort to destroy freedom worldwide, saying “The Communists in the Kremlin are engaged in a monstrous conspiracy.” Thus, Korea is just one part of a wider plan to “control all Asia from the Kremlin”($\checkmark$).

Finally, Truman draws historical parallels. He refers to the failures of the 1930s to justify early intervention, arguing that if democratic nations had confronted dictators sooner, “there probably would have been no World War II.” By invoking this example, he presents U.S. action in Korea as both urgent and informed by the mistakes of the past ($\checkmark$).
