# HI0910

Outline the short- and long-term consequences of the Soviet invasion for Afghanistan's government.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following. Responses may include two points for each side (2/2), or be weighted (3/1 or 1/3), as long as both the short- and long-term consequences are addressed.

Short-Term Consequences (1979-1989)
- Overthrow of Hafizullah Amin: Soviet forces assassinated President Hafizullah Amin in December 1979 and replaced him with Babrak Karmal, a member of the rival Parcham faction of the PDPA.
- Installation of a Soviet-backed puppet regime: Babrak Karmal's government lacked popular legitimacy and was heavily reliant on Soviet military and political support. Many Afghans saw the government as a foreign-imposed regime.
- Escalating civil conflict and growing opposition: The PDPA government faced widespread resistance from mujahideen fighters, tribal leaders, and Islamic groups. The government's control was largely limited to urban centres, while rural areas fell under insurgent influence.
- Expansion of secret police and repression: The regime intensified surveillance, imprisonment, and torture of suspected dissidents, fuelling further unrest and resistance.
- Dependence on Soviet military presence: The survival of the Afghan government became tied to the presence of approximately 100,000 Soviet troops deployed during the war. Without Soviet backing, the regime lacked the capacity to maintain control.

Long-Term Consequences (Post-1989)
- Collapse of the PDPA regime (1992): Following the Soviet withdrawal in 1989, the Afghan government, under President Mohammad Najibullah (Karmal's successor), survived briefly but collapsed in 1992 when Soviet aid ceased.
- Power vacuum and civil war: The fall of the PDPA regime led to a violent power struggle among mujahideen factions, plunging Afghanistan into a brutal civil war.
- Loss of centralised government authority: The idea of a strong, centralised Afghan government eroded, with warlords and militias dominating various regions.
- Discrediting of communist ideology: The association of the PDPA with foreign occupation and internal repression led to a long-term rejection of communist politics in Afghan governance.
- Foundation for Taliban rise (1990s): The chaos and governance vacuum left in the aftermath of the PDPA's collapse created conditions for the Taliban's emergence and eventual takeover in 1996.
- Enduring political instability: The invasion and its consequences triggered a decades-long cycle of conflict, weakening Afghanistan's political institutions and fostering chronic instability.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
In the short term, the Soviets removed President Hafizullah Amin and installed Babrak Karmal, a more compliant leader from the rival Parcham faction of the PDPA ($\checkmark$). However, Karmal's regime lacked popular support and quickly became dependent on Soviet military and economic aid to stay in power ($\checkmark$).

In the long term, after the Soviet withdrawal in 1989, the Afghan government under Najibullah collapsed by 1992, leading to a violent civil war between mujahideen factions ($\checkmark$). This prolonged instability undermined central authority and created conditions that ultimately enabled the rise of the Taliban in the mid-1990s ($\checkmark$).
