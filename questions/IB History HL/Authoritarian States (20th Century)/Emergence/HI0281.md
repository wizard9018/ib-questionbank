# HI0281

Describe **four** characteristics of the period of 1930 to 1943 in Argentina that gave it the nickname $\text{\textquotedblleft}$La Década Infame” (The Infamous Decade).

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any combination of **four** of the following characteristics of the period:

 - Social Inequality

     - Argentina was marked by a stark divide between wealthy landowners and the working class, with widespread poverty and limited opportunities for social mobility.

 - Corruption and Fraud

     - Corruption and the practice known as "fraude patriótico" (patriotic fraud) were widespread as political elites, particularly the conservative oligarchy, manipulated elections to maintain power and prevent the rise of opposition groups using vote-rigging and coercion.

 - Political Instability 

     - The combination of military interference since the 1930 Coup and fraudulent elections eroded political stability, fostered social unrest, and laid the groundwork for further political crises, eventually leading to the rise of Peronism in the 1940s.

 - Economic Dependence on Britain and foreign influence

     - Argentina relied heavily on Britain for trade and investment, giving foreigners significant control over the economy like Roca-Runciman Treaty. 

 - Repression of Opposition

     - Political dissenters, including socialists, labor unions, and members of the Radical Civic Union (UCR), were targeted through censorship, arrests, and the suppression of protests.

     Strikes faced violent crackdowns, socialist leaders were imprisoned or exiled, and repression silenced opposition, fueling resentment that contributed to the rise of Peronism.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The political context in Argentina until the time of the Revolución del 43 on June 4  was characterized by the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Década Infame" (Infamous Decade), which spanned from 1930 to 1943 because it was a period of political instability ($\checkmark$) and one of corruption and electoral fraud ($\checkmark$). Moreover, Argentina's economy was heavily influenced by Britain, with treaties like Roca-Runciman granting significant control to foreign interests ($\checkmark$). Meanwhile, opposition groups, including socialists and labor unions, faced harsh repression through censorship, arrests, and violent crackdowns, fueling discontent that paved the way for Peronism.($\checkmark$).
