For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources emphasise the importance of the dictatorship of the proletariat in the revolution. Source D: “emancipate humanity” and Source E: “historic struggle.”
- Both sources emphasise that the capitalists and non-proletariat were the main threat. Source D: “ the oppression of capital” and Source E: “except the class against which the historic struggle of the proletariat is directed.”
- Both sources targeted the rich as a threat: Source D, “Democracy for the Rich,” and Source E, “Rich Capitalists.”

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Source D argues that the dictatorship of the proletariat should eradicate all the bourgeois elements from society in order to “emancipate humanity.” Source E suggests that some “healthy elements” of society can still be retained, except for the “rich capitalists.”
- Source D states that oppression is a political problem arising from “bourgeois democracy” and “democracy for the rich.” Source E states that the threat is primarily the “rich capitalists.”
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources emphasised the importance of the dictatorship of the proletariat in the Russian Revolution ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source D says the dictatorship of the proletariat can “emancipate humanity from oppression…” and Source E says it is engaged in a “historical struggle” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Another similarity is that both sources emphasised capitalists and non-proletariats being the main threat to the revolution ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source D references “the oppression of capital,” and Source E says the struggle of the proletariat is directed at “the $\lq$rich capitalists.'” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, the sources differ in their description of the role of the dictatorship of the proletariat ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source D thinks that the dictatorship of the proletariat should eradicate all the bourgeois elements from society to “emancipate humanity.” In contrast, Source E thinks that some “healthy elements” of society can still be retained, except for the “rich capitalists” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Another difference between the sources is that they differ in their description of the nature of the oppression or the threats they faced ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source D states that oppression is a political problem that comes from “bourgeois democracy” and “democracy for the rich.” In contrast, Source E states that the threat is primarily the “rich capitalists” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
