# HI0287

What does Source C suggest about the communist perspective of Perón and the events of October 17, 1945?

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | A cartoon published by the communist publication Orientación that depicts its view of the demonstrations of October 17, 1945 in the Plaza de Mayo, Buenos Aires. On October 24, he following image appeared on its cover. The caption of the image reads: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The colonel displayed his cast of thugs and criminals, already familiar to the country from the events of the 17th and 18th. The most regrettable thing is that, alongside this cast, he managed to mislead a few honest workers lacking experience and political insight." The fishing rod is labled “Borlenghi” referring to a General Confederation of Labor (Confederación General del Trabajo or CGT) union leader, Ángel Borlenghi, drawing in workers labeled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$tough guys.” Also it can be seen Perón holding a sword with the initials $\text{\textquotedblleft}$T y P" (referring to the Secretaría de Trabajo y Previsión).

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - It portrays Perón's supporters as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$maleantes" (criminals) and aims to discredit the movement and Perón's leadership, suggesting that it lacked legitimacy and was supported by undesirable sectors of society.

 - Workers are shown as tricked, symbolized by a fishing rod labeled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Borlenghi," referring to the General Confederation of Labor (CGT) leader who backed Perón. The communist press suggests that these workers lacked political experience and were unable to recognize Perón's true intentions, portraying them as victims of false promises.

 - Perón is drawn in a dictator-like pose, while the police are blamed for organizing the event. Perón holding a sword with the initials $\text{\textquotedblleft}$T y P" (referring to the Secretaría de Trabajo y Previsión). This visual suggests that his leadership style is authoritarian and militaristic. The sword symbolizes control and power, reinforcing the communist press's criticism of Perón as a manipulative leader who seeks to dominate the working class.

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The communist press's perspective in Source C shows Perón's supporters as criminals and outsiders, making the demonstration look disorganized and illegitimate ($\checkmark$). It also suggests that workers were misled, using a fishing rod labeled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Borlenghi" to show they were tricked by false promises ($\checkmark$).
