# HI1150

Explain **three** methods Fidel Castro used to establish authoritarian control in Cuba.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with supporting explanation:
- Creation of a one-party state:
    - Castro banned opposing political parties, so there was no political competition.
- Eliminated opposition
    - He placed loyal revolutionaries in positions of power and command - political opponents were imprisoned or executed, and independent labour unions and human rights organisations were not allowed.
- Established the Committees for the Defense of the Revolution in 1960
    - This was a system of neighborhood surveillance that encouraged people to monitor others and report acts of disloyalty. Created an environment of fear and distrust.
- Censorship and propaganda:
    - All news outlets were state-controlled, and the media was used to glorify the revolution and Castro's leadership. Access to foreign media and information was also restricted.
- Ideological indoctrination
    - Educational programmes were used as a way of promoting the ideas of the revolution.
- State control of the economy
    - Nationalisation of industries and agriculture gave the state increased power.
- Use of fear
    - Public arrests, censorship, and the disappearance of opposition increased the public's fear and enabled greater governmental control.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
To establish authoritarian control over Cuba, Castro had to gain political and economic control, and also control over Cuban culture and the media. The establishment of a one-party state was central to his success ($\checkmark$). The banning of political opposition helped him achieve control and was a key factor in allowing further measures to be implemented ($\checkmark$). Castro placed loyal revolutionaries in positions of power and command, which enabled him to control any opponents ($\checkmark$). Political opponents were imprisoned or executed, and independent labor unions and human rights organisations were not allowed ($\checkmark$). Ideological indoctrination played a key role in establishing his ideas ($\checkmark$). By creating educational programs, Castro was able to promote the ideas of the revolution, and success here was also supported by increasing literacy rates among the population ($\checkmark$).
