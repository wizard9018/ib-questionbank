# HI0196

According to Source D, what role does Mao believe the peasant movement will play in the Chinese revolution?

[3 marks]

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source D** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from a 1927 report by Mao Tse-Tung about the role of peasants within the Communist Party.  

[Source: Mao Tse-Tung. (March 1927). *Report on an Investigation of the Peasant Movement in Hunan, March 1927, in Selected Works of Mao Tse-tung, vol. I* (3rd printing; Peking: Foreign Languages Press, 1975), pp. 23-29. Retrieved January 13, 2025 from https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/mod/1927mao.asp. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - Mao sees the peasant movement as the central driving force of the revolution, describing it as a "mighty storm" that will reshape Chinese society.

 - Mao believes the movement will destroy the power of imperialists, warlords, corrupt officials, and local tyrants, paving the way for liberation

 - He highlights the peasant associations as the new centers of authority, replacing the feudal structures that had dominated rural areas for centuries.

 - Mao states that the movement will challenge every revolutionary party and individual, forcing them to either lead, follow, or oppose it, with clear consequences.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

According to Source D, Mao portrays the peasant movement as the driving force behind the Chinese revolution, likening it to a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mighty storm" that will dismantle the oppressive structures of feudalism and imperialism ($\checkmark$). He also emphasizes the critical role of peasant associations, which have become the new centers of authority in rural areas, symbolizing the collapse of the old gentry-dominated order ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, Mao describes the movement as a test for revolutionaries, compelling them to take a clear stance by leading, supporting, or opposing it, with significant consequences for their revolutionary legitimacy ($\checkmark$).
