# HI1128

Explain **two** ways the economic problems caused by the Great Depression contributed to Hitler's rise to power.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- The Nazis promised to solve unemployment.
    - The Great Depression caused an economic crisis in Germany. In Germany, there were wage cuts, short-time working, unemployment, homelessness, and poverty on a scale never seen before. In desperation, voters changed support to the party that promised full employment, the Nazis.
- Wealthy industrialists supported Hitler.
    - The economic depression also led to an increase in support for the Communists. They argued that the root cause of Germany's problems was capitalism, and this frightened the middle and upper-class Germans. Wealthy industrialists began to support the Nazi Party and gave financial support to the Nazis to help them in their election campaigns.
- The Nazis blamed the Weimar Republic.
    - The Depression caused massive unemployment and hardship in Germany in the early 1930s. The main political parties, like the Social Democrats, were blamed for this, and people began to turn to the Nazis, who had always criticised the Weimar Republic and its governments.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Great Depression caused an economic crisis in Germany. By January 1932, unemployment stood at 6.1 million. For those who lost their job, there was poverty, hunger, and homelessness. Many people lost faith in the Weimar Republic ($\checkmark$), which seemed to offer no solutions to the problems of rising unemployment and growing poverty, and began to see salvation in the solutions offered by the Nazi Party. Nazi Party members delivered messages that appealed to the unemployed and industrial workers by aiming to overcome the depression and providing work on road building and public works ($\checkmark$). As the economic crisis deepened, support for the Communists also rose. In the general election of September 1930, the Communists polled more than 4.5 million votes, making them the third-largest party in the Reichstag. Business leaders feared the Communists due to their plans to introduce nationalization and state control of businesses ($\checkmark$). The Nazis turned this to their advantage by playing on the fears of communism. They portrayed the Communists as scheming revolutionaries. Business leaders felt the Nazis would combat this threat, and some began to put money into Nazi election campaigns ($\checkmark$).
