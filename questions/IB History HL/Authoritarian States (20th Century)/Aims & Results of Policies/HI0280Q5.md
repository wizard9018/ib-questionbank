# HI0280Q5

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyze the value and limitations of Source A for an historian studying the domestic policies of the Soviet Union.

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$ Constitution (Fundamental law) of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics,” Kremlin, Moscow, December 5, 1936.

[Source: Stalin, J.V. (1936, December 5). *Works Vol. 14. Red Start Press Ltd, 1978*. Transcribed by Salil Sen for Marxists Internet Archive, 2008. Retrieved December 14th, 2024 from  https://marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1936/12/05.htm.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full four marks.

For value, any of the following:

 - The source is an official document from the 1936 Constitution of the USSR, providing a primary perspective on the legal framework and state ideology under Stalin's rule.

 - The document's purpose is to articulate the rights and responsibilities of Soviet citizens and present the state as a guarantor of social welfare, equality, and progress.

 - The content of the source indicates a clear enumeration of rights (e.g. education, gender equality and equality amongst various races and cultures) as well as the secular nature of the Soviet government. 

For limitations, any of the following:

 - The document's propagandistic nature may exaggerate or misrepresent the extent to which these rights were implemented in practice. Its purpose to glorify the USSR undermines its reliability as an objective representation of living conditions or individual freedoms.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source A, being an official document from the 1936 Soviet Constitution, offers valuable insights for historians as an official text  ($\checkmark VO$),  it provides a primary perspective on the legal framework and state ideology, directly reflecting the goals and policies of the Soviet government  ($\checkmark VP$). Its endorsement by the Soviet state ensures that it represents the regime's vision for the USSR, particularly its emphasis on social welfare, equality, and progress ($\checkmark VC$). The constitution enumerates specific rights such as the right to work, rest, and education, signalling the state's prioritization of these areas  ($\checkmark VC$). 

However, the source also has significant limitations. Authored under Stalin's regime, it is inherently biased, serving as propaganda to legitimize the government and promote the image of the USSR as a utopian socialist state  ($\checkmark LO$). This propagandistic nature means the document likely exaggerates or misrepresents the actual implementation of these rights, presenting a rosy picture of Soviet life that was far from reality  ($\checkmark LP$). The highly controlled political environment in which the document was created also means it doesn't reflect genuine public opinion or opposition views, limiting its usefulness as a complete or objective representation of Soviet domestic policies  ($\checkmark LC$).
