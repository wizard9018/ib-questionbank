# HI0219

Explain the successes and failures of Fidel Castro's social policies in Cuba.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
For successes, any of the following:
- Improved literacy: The 1961 National Literacy Campaign reduced illiteracy from 23% to 4%, earning recognition from UNESCO.
- Improved public health: The creation of a universal healthcare system made Cuba a model in Latin America, achieving a life expectancy comparable to developed nations by 1980.
- Increased social equality: The government implemented housing reforms, providing subsidized or free housing to many citizens. By the 1980s, homelessness was virtually eliminated, and access to basic utilities like water and electricity improved significantly.

For failure, any of the following:
- Resource shortages: The economic embargo and excessive centralization led to shortages of essential goods, especially during the Special Period in the 1990s following the collapse of the USSR.
- Restrictions on political freedom and speech: Social policies were accompanied by censorship and repression, including the imprisonment of dissenters during the 1960s and 1970s.
- Economic dependency: Cuba's reliance on the USSR for subsidies and trade made its economy vulnerable. When the USSR collapsed, the resulting economic crisis in the 1990s severely impacted social programs, reducing their quality and accessibility.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Fidel Castro's social policies in Cuba achieved notable successes. The 1961 National Literacy Campaign dramatically reduced illiteracy from 23% to 4%, earning global recognition ($\checkmark$). Universal healthcare established Cuba as a model in Latin America, with life expectancy rivaling that of developed nations by 1980 ($\checkmark$). Additionally, housing reforms provided subsidized or free housing, eliminating homelessness and improving access to essential utilities like water and electricity by the 1980s ($\checkmark$).

However, these achievements were accompanied by significant failures. The economic embargo and centralization caused severe resource shortages, particularly during the Special Period in the 1990s ($\checkmark$). Social policies also came at the cost of political freedoms , as censorship and repression targeted dissenters, especially in the 1960s and 1970s ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, Cuba's dependence on the USSR made its economy fragile, and the Soviet collapse undermined the quality and sustainability of its social programs ($\checkmark$).
