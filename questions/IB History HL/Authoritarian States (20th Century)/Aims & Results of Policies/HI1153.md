# HI1153

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source A for historians studying the causes of conflict between Cuba and the United States in 1959-1960
[4 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A**| $\hspace{3em}$ | An excerpt from an interview by Fidel Castro, in 1959, discussing early revolutionary reforms and U.S. opposition. |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- The source is an extract from a speech by Fidel Castro, who played a key role in the politics of this time and had direct knowledge of the motivations behind Cuban policies
- The source provides insight into events from a Cuban leadership perspective and explains the Cuban revolution to a wider audience.
- The source allows Castro to justify his actions, especially the confiscation of US assets, as a defensive measure, suggesting that the cause of the conflict is due to aggressive action from the US.
- It gives specific examples of reforms that were implemented and the US response to these, which led to the confiscation of US assets.
- Identifies the actions of the US, for example, the economic blockade, which highlight the reasons for Castro's response.

For limitations, any of the following:
- The author of the source is Castro, and so offers only a Cuban perspective of events.
- Castro is using the speech to justify the increasing conflict with the US, defend his actions and policies, and gain sympathy for his cause.
- The source looks to portray Cuba as the victim and the US as the aggressor in these events - the aim is to elicit sympathy from the public, and so it oversimplifies the situation.
- IT makes no mention of US concerns about the influence of communism, which they saw as a legitimate cause for intervention as well as the talk of an alliance with the USSR
- The source frames the causes as being unavoidable actions rather than ideological choices.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is an extract from a speech by Fidel Castro, who played a pivotal role in the politics of this era, so its value lies in the fact that he had direct knowledge of the motivations behind Cuban policies of that time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$).  The limitations of the origin are that the source is written by Castro, so it only offers the reader a Cuban perspective of events ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). The purpose of the source is a value as it allows one to understand Castro's justification for his actions, especially the confiscation of US assets. It suggests that they are a defensive measure and that the cause of the conflict is due to aggressive action from the US ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).  The limitation of the source's content is that the justifications provided as causes do not mention US concerns about the influence of communism, which they saw as a legitimate cause for intervention. This omission limits the greater context for the conflict between the US and Cuba ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
