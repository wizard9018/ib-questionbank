# HI0696

Contrast **two** policies toward women in the German (1933-1939) and Cuban (1959-1975) authoritarian states.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following differences and supporting explanation/evidence:
- Women in Germany should stay at home, whereas women in Cuba were able to engage in wider social activities.
    - Germany: KKK (Children, Kitchen and Church) principles.
    - Cuba: Creation of Federation of Cuban Women (FMC) in 1960, led by revolutionary leader Vilma Espín.
- Emotional condition of women in Germany and political militancy of women in Cuba.
    - Germany: Women's specific role in self-sacrifice and suffering in Adolf Hitler's speech before the National Socialist Women's League (September 1934).
    - Cuba: The Family Code (1975) enshrined the equal civil and political standing of women with respect to men.
- Reproductive roles of women aligned with the Aryan race in Germany and significant contributions of women to education policies in Cuba.
    - Germany: Lebensborn program (December 1935) through which SS members were to have children with racially Aryan pure women. Further, the creation of the Cross of Honour of the German Mother (December 1938) incentivised women to have children against rewards in the form of bronze, silver and gold crosses.
    - Cuba: Women's active role as teachers in Cuba's Literacy Campaign (1961).
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Although women in both authoritarian states played a significant role in their societies, they were relegated in Germany to childbearing of the Aryan population through propaganda and incentive schemes between 1935 and 1938 ($\checkmark$). By contrast, women were integral to the Cuban Revolution and maintained an active role in the political realm after Fidel Castro's rise to power in 1959. Led by Vilma Espín, the creation of the Federation of Cuban Women in 1960 further enhanced their status as political equals with men and allowed them to uphold their rights ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, women in Germany were subject to sex-related indoctrination with reference to deeply ingrained roles of housewives and emotional beings responsible for raising children and cooking ($\checkmark$). For their part, women in Cuba were integrated into social policies by serving as teachers in the nationwide literacy and numeracy campaign in 1961, which paved the way for strengthening the capabilities of youngsters and workers ($\checkmark$).
