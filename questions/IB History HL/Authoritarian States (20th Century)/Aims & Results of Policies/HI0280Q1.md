# HI0280Q1

Explain **two** ways in which national minorities were impacted by Stalin's leadership and/or policies. 

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following: 

 - Russification/Korenizatsiya ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$Indigenization") 

     - Stalin promoted the Russian language and culture as a unifying force across the USSR. In the 1920s and early 1930s, Stalin initially supported policies that encouraged the development of local languages, cultures, and leadership within minority regions. This was intended to gain the loyalty of national minorities and integrate them into the Soviet system. 

 - Deportation: 

     - Stalin used mass deportations to weaken ethnic groups he considered disloyal or a security threat. During World War II, groups like the Crimean Tatars, Chechens, Ingush, and Volga Germans were accused of collaborating with Nazi Germany and forcibly relocated to remote areas such as Siberia and Central Asia. These deportations often involved brutal conditions, leading to widespread death and suffering.

 - Purges: 

     - Stalin targeted minority elites and intellectuals through purges, accusing them of nationalism or counter-revolutionary activity. Prominent examples include the purges of Ukrainian intellectuals during the 1930s and the repression of leaders in other minority regions.

 - Creation of National Republics: 

     - Stalin formally recognized the USSR's multi-ethnic composition by establishing national republics and granting them nominal autonomy. Each republic had its own constitution and was allowed to promote local languages and cultures, but this autonomy was tightly controlled by Moscow.

 - Collectivization and Industrialization: 

     - Stalin's economic policies often disproportionately affected minority regions. Collectivization, for example, caused widespread famine in Ukraine during the 1932-1933 Holodomor, which many scholars view as a deliberate act of repression against Ukrainian nationalism.

 - Infrastructure Development: 

     - While Stalin's industrialization projects brought economic development to some minority regions and the accompanying infrastructure needed to support industrialisation, they often prioritized the interests of the central government over local populations, further marginalizing minority groups.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One way national minorities were dealt with under Stalin was through forced deportations ($\checkmark$). Stalin targeted ethnic groups he deemed disloyal or a threat to Soviet unity, particularly during World War II. Entire populations, such as the Crimean Tatars, Chechens, Ingush, and Volga Germans, were accused of collaborating with enemy forces and forcibly relocated to remote areas like Siberia and Central Asia ($\checkmark$). 

Stalin also eliminated minority ethnic intellectuals branding them as nationals or enemies of the State ($\checkmark$). For example, in Ukraine there was a crackdown on intellectuals in the 1930s driven by his belief that they might undermine the Soviet regime or prevent the assimilation of various ethnic groups into Russian culture ($\checkmark$).
