$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The message of Source B aligns closely with Nazi policies and expectations toward women, particularly in reinforcing traditional gender roles. The source emphasizes that “the woman's is a smaller world” ($\checkmark\, \text{source}$) centered around “her husband, her family, her children, and her home” ($\checkmark\, \text{source}$), which mirrors the Nazi ideal of Kinder, Küche, Kirche (children, kitchen, church) ($\checkmark\, \text{OK}$). Nazi propaganda glorified motherhood and domesticity, promoting women as the backbone of the Volksgemeinschaft (people's community). For example, policies such as the Law for the Encouragement of Marriage (1933) gave loans to newlywed Aryan couples, with portions forgiven for each child born, incentivising domestic motherhood ($\checkmark\, \text{OK}$).

However, inconsistencies also emerged, especially during the Second World War. Despite the initial emphasis on domesticity, the regime was forced to mobilize women into the workforce and auxiliary roles due to labour shortages. For example, by 1943, conscription of women into wartime industries had begun, contradicting the earlier vision of the woman's “smaller world” ($\checkmark\, \text{OK}$). Similarly, the Nazi regime sometimes allowed women to serve in organizations like the NS-Frauenschaft (Nazi Women's League) to spread ideology, showing that the public-private divide was not always rigid in practice ($\checkmark\, \text{OK}$).

In conclusion, while Source B strongly reflects the ideological stance of the Nazi regime toward women in the 1930s, particularly regarding their domestic roles, practical demands during wartime led to contradictions, showing the regime's policies were not entirely consistent with the idealized vision expressed in the source ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
