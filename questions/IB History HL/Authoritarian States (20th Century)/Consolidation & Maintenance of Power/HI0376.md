$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence** from the texts.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources examine the role and significance of different institutions under the Cuban regime in consolidating the power of the authoritarian state (e.g., the Communist Party's Political Bureau and the Ministry of Public Health).
- Both sources suggest that the Castro regime was influential amongst different social groups, such as the national and international audiences and people in rural areas.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- While Source C emphasises the Cuban state's consolidation of power through the regime's control of the media, Source E analyses the extent of the regime's control through social policies tailored to vulnerable groups in rural areas.
- Source C highlights the significance of foreign policy in the Cuban state's consolidation of power, facilitated by the support of international media networks. In contrast, Source E emphasises that securing social support for the implementation of social policies in the country was crucial for strengthening the regime's legitimacy domestically.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both Sources C and E highlight the significance of a strong institutional apparatus in the consolidation of power of the Cuban authoritarian state ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source C argues that the Communist Party's Political Bureau played a key role in controlling the information released by media outlets. Source E illustrates the Cuban state's power by analysing the lasting impact of the Ministry of Public Health's social policies ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, both sources differ on the extent of the influence of the state apparatus ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). While Source C addresses the influence of Cuban media on public audiences, Source E highlights the positive impact of health measures on vulnerable groups in rural areas ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Additionally, Source C focuses on the Cuban state's consolidation of power from an international perspective, whereas Source E underscores the importance of strengthening societal relations at the local level ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source C emphasises the interplay between the Cuban regime's international networks and the spread of an anti-US nationalist ideology as key to consolidating the state's power among the broader public. By contrast, Source E highlights the strong bond public health doctors established with peasants as essential in increasing the legitimacy of the state ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
