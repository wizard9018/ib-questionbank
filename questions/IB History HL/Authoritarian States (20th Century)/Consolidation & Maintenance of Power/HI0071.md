# HI0071

Explain **three** legal methods used by Mao Zedong to consolidate his power after 1949.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following **two** marks each:

- Mao relied on legal frameworks, such as the 1954 Constitution, which centralised power under the Communist Party and legally established a one-party state.

- Mao used legal methods to outlaw opposition parties and create a one-party state, ensuring that the Communist Party had absolute control over China's political system.

- Mao enacted land reform laws to legally redistribute land from landlords to peasants, which gained him significant support from rural populations.

- The Marriage Law introduced social reforms, such as rights for women and easier access to divorce, aligning the Communist Party with social progress, which increased popular support and helped Mao integrate the Party's influence into daily life.

- Mao used legal structures like the judicial system to suppress opposition initially, but increasingly relied on extra-legal means such as violent repression, arbitrary arrests, and executions during purges.

- Nationalising industries allowed Mao to bring economic control under state authority, reducing the influence of private business owners and centralising economic power within the Communist Party.

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One legal method Mao used to consolidate his power was the 1954 Constitution, which centralised authority under the Communist Party ($\checkmark$). This allowed Mao to shape the political and legal systems according to the Party's needs, ensuring that no other groups could challenge his control ($\checkmark$). Another method was the implementation of land reform laws, which redistributed land from landlords to peasants ($\checkmark$). This weakened the power of the landowning class while building loyalty among rural populations, securing Mao's influence in the countryside ($\checkmark$). Lastly, by creating a one-party state, Mao eliminated any political opposition ($\checkmark$). This meant that all political decisions were controlled by the Communist Party, making it easier for Mao to maintain his dominance without facing any organised resistance ($\checkmark$).
