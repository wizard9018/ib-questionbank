## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The **strongest answers** will identify and develop at least one similarity and one difference, followed by a further point of either similarity or difference. Each point must be supported by clear evidence from the texts.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources show that Mao believed intellectuals needed ideological reform to become loyal supporters of socialism and communism.
- Both sources indicate that intellectual reform requires an active and continuing process.
- Both sources imply that intellectuals cannot remain detached from the wider population.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Source A emphasises reforming intellectuals through physical labour and integration with workers and peasants, whereas Source B stresses criticism and ideological debate.
- Source A presents intellectual reform in a positive and constructive manner, whereas Source B adopts a more hostile and threatening attitude towards ideas associated with intellectuals.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Sources A and B both reveal that Mao was suspicious of intellectuals and believed they needed to be transformed to become true supporters of socialism ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A indicates that intellectuals should be $\text{\textquotedblleft}$rooted into the masses of workers and peasants," and in Source B, Mao says, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$We still have to wage a protracted struggle against bourgeois and petty-bourgeois ideology” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Both sources indicate that intellectual reform requires an active and continuing process($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A instructs intellectuals to participate in “practical struggles” and become $\text{\textquotedblleft}$toughened", presenting true reform as something achieved through sustained experience and effort, while Source B describes ideological struggle as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$protracted” because bourgeois and anti-Marxist ideas would $\text{\textquotedblleft}$continue to exist for a long time" ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Source A presents intellectual reform in a positive and constructive manner, whereas Source B adopts a more hostile and threatening attitude towards ideas associated with intellectuals ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source A portrays the intellectual actively learning and working alongside a peasant. The instruction is to become $\text{\textquotedblleft}$toughened” and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$rooted", suggesting that intellectuals could be successfully remoulded and incorporated into socialist society. Whereas in Source B hostile expressions are used, such as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$poisonous weeds" and “ghosts and monsters", and there are warnings that such ideas must not be allowed to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$spread unchecked". This reveals a more hostile attitude and presents certain intellectual ideas as a continuing danger to socialism ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
