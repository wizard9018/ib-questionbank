## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |

|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence** from the texts.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources emphasise the significant influence of Castro on Cuba's political development after the Cuban Revolution.
- Both sources suggest that Castro's leadership allowed him to establish specific policies on the use of media.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- While Source C stresses Castro's control of all media outlets in Cuba, Source D highlights the government's openness to creating other publishing companies.
- Source C underscores the detrimental effects of Castro's leadership in spreading anti-US sentiments within Cuba and beyond, whereas Source D states that the Cuban government promotes freedom of expression in light of revolutionary values.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources agree that Castro's leadership was significantly influential in the development of Cuban institutions after he rose to power ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). For instance, Source C highlights the leading role of the Communist Party's Political Bureau in structuring different news outlets. Similarly, Source D describes the Cuban National Council as an overarching institution responsible for administering and overseeing media outlets ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, the sources differ on the extent of the function of the Cuban institutions responsible for controlling the media. While Source C stresses that Castro had absolute control over the news outlets, Source D highlights that the government was committed to diversifying the media ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). For example, Source C illustrates that Castro frequently reviewed and approved of the Granma's editorials before their publication. By contrast, Source D describes the legislative options for expanding the printing companies as evidence of openness in the media ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Moreover, both sources disagree on how the Cuban government used the media outlets as propaganda platforms. Source C underscores Castro's consolidation of power through anti-US sentiments both nationally and internationally, whereas Source D states that freedom of expression was at the core of the Cuban state under the revolution ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source C describes a wide range of news outlets in Cuba and international networks which served as platforms for Castro's campaigning against the USA and consolidation of his power. Meanwhile, Source D underscores the importance of promoting the artistic spirit in writers and artists ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
