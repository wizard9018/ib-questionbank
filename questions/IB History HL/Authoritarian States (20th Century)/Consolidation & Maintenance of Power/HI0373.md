$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |

|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence** from the texts.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources examine the significance of centralisation of state functions in the consolidation of power of the Chinese and Cuban authoritarian states.
- Both sources stress that the control of individuals through the reinforcement of ideological principles was essential in strengthening the regimes' influence on Chinese and Cuban societies.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- While Source A focuses on the significance of political control through the consolidation of party structures in China, Source C suggests that the media was at the core of the consolidation of the Cuban authoritarian state.
- Source A outlines the importance of domestic policy to the consolidation of the Chinese regime. In contrast, Source C emphasises the interplay of ideology, propaganda and foreign policy as a primary factor of the consolidation of Castro's regime in Cuba.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both Sources A and C argue that the centralisation of state functions under Mao's and Castro's regimes was paramount for the consolidation of power from above ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A emphasises the importance of establishing a hierarchical party system that reflects the Chinese Communist Party's (CCP) principles. Similarly, Source C underscores the influence of the state-controlled media outlets on Cuban society ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Moreover, both sources stress that ideological principles were at the core of the measures the Chinese and Cuban states implemented to consolidate their power ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). While Source A illustrates this through the references to national unity under communism, Source C describes the anti-US views of Castro's regime framed in nationalism ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, both sources differ in their levels of analysis regarding the consolidation of power in the Chinese and Cuban authoritarian states. While Source A refers to the domestic policies in China, Source C examines the relationship between Cuba's foreign policy and the regime's influence on society ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source A is primarily focused on domestic policies targeting CCP units across cities and rural towns, engaging the industrial workforce and former party members, respectively. By contrast, Source C examines the effects of Cuban nationalist anti-US ideology in media propaganda within the framework of Cuba's foreign policy toward the USA ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
