# HI0315

Describe the US intervention in Cuba known as Operation Mongoose (1960-1963) and explain **two** of its impacts.
[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
For description, the following or similar:
- Operation Mongoose 1960-1963 was a covert U.S. operation led by the CIA
- Aimed at destabilizing and overthrowing Castro's government through sabotage, propaganda, and assassination attempts
\
[1 marks]

Any **two** of the following impacts
- Legitimizing the narrative of an external threat: U.S. actions allowed Castro to consolidate his control by justifying the need to protect the revolution against a hostile foreign power.
- Justifying internal repression: The perceived threat enabled the implementation of strict security measures, such as the creation of the secret police, which facilitated the elimination of opponents.
- Strengthening Cuban nationalism: U.S. interventions, seen as a direct attack on Cuban sovereignty, rallied the population around Castro as the defender of the nation.
- Distracting from internal issues: The narrative of an external threat helped Castro shift focus away from internal economic and social difficulties, reinforcing his legitimacy.
- Discrediting opposition: U.S. interventions allowed Castro to associate internal opposition with imperialism, reducing their popular support.
- Increasing dependence on the Soviet Union: Operation Mongoose and other U.S. aggressions pushed Cuba into closer alignment with the USSR, bolstering its economic and military capacity.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Operation Mongoose (1960-1963) was a covert operation led by the U.S. government and the CIA, aimed at destabilizing and ultimately overthrowing Fidel Castro's government. The operation involved sabotage, economic disruption, propaganda campaigns, and even assassination attempts ($\checkmark$).

One key impact of Operation Mongoose was the strengthening of Cuban nationalism ($\checkmark$). U.S. actions were portrayed by the Cuban government as direct attacks on Cuban sovereignty, which allowed Castro to rally popular support by positioning himself as the defender of the revolution and the nation. This narrative united the population against a common enemy and reinforced national pride ($\checkmark$).

A second impact was the increased dependence on the Soviet Union ($\checkmark$). Faced with repeated U.S. aggression, Castro sought closer ties with the USSR for protection and support. This led to stronger economic and military alliances, including the placement of Soviet missiles on Cuban soil, ultimately intensifying Cold War tensions and deepening Cuba's alignment with the communist bloc ($\checkmark$).
