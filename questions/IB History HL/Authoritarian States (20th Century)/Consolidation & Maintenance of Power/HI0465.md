# HI0465

According to Source C, what role did U.S. foreign policy play in shaping Castro's strategy for consolidating and maintaining power?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Fidel Castro's October 28, 1962, declaration to US President John F. Kennedy during the Cuban Missile Crisis. 

[Source: Castro\, F. (1962, October 29). *Los Cinco Puntos (The Five Points)*. Translation by S. Garces. Published in Revolución, Monday, October 29, 1962. Cited in Granma, December 30, 2011. Retrieved from https://granma.cu/granmad/2011/12/30/pdf/pagina06.pdf. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any combination of the following: 

 - The source presents the U.S. as an ongoing threat, justifying Castro's authoritarian measures under the guise of national defense.

 - Castro portrays the U.S. as an imperialist aggressor, rallying nationalist support and uniting Cubans behind the revolution.

 - By listing multiple hostile actions from the U.S., Castro emphasizes the necessity of Soviet support, helping to maintain his regime.

 - The source's demand for the return of Guantánamo Bay reinforces Castro's narrative of resisting foreign control, strengthening his nationalist legitimacy.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source C suggests that U.S. foreign policy was an ongoing threat through actions such as the economic blockade and military interventions, which allowed Castro to justify his authoritarian control under the guise of national defense ($\checkmark$). By emphasizing external aggression by the United States, he could suppress dissent and maintain strict political control. Additionally, Castro strategically used anti-U.S. sentiment to unite the Cuban population behind his leadership. The source frames the U.S. as an imperialist aggressor, reinforcing the idea that the revolution was necessary to defend Cuban independence. This helped Castro maintain public support and solidify his position ($\checkmark$). Finally, the demand for the return of Guantánamo Bay underscores Castro's commitment to Cuban sovereignty, further legitimizing his rule. By positioning himself as the leader fighting against foreign domination, he strengthened nationalist pride and reinforced his control over the state ($\checkmark$).
