$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of two marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full four marks.

For value, any of the following:

 - The source is a primary document, a personal letter from Ernesto $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Che" Guevara, a key revolutionary figure and close ally of Fidel Castro. As such, it provides firsthand insight into the internal dynamics of the revolutionary leadership. The letter was read during a period when Castro was consolidating his power by eliminating internal opposition and securing ideological control, making it valuable for understanding the role of political loyalty in this process.

 - The letter, publicly read by Castro, was intended to demonstrate unwavering loyalty among top revolutionary figures, reinforcing the legitimacy of his rule and the ideological commitment of the Cuban government.

 - Guevara's statements emphasize his dedication to the revolution and Cuba's foreign policy, portraying Castro's leadership as inspirational and morally justified, which helps explain how Castro maintained ideological unity.

For limitations, any of the following: 

 - As a personal letter from a loyal revolutionary, it is highly biased in favor of Castro, omitting any criticism or internal conflicts within the revolutionary leadership.

 - Although written as a private letter, Castro's public reading suggests it was used as propaganda, meaning it was likely framed to strengthen his leadership rather than offer an objective reflection of events.

 - The source does not include the perspectives of those who opposed Castro's leadership, limiting its usefulness for understanding broader political struggles during consolidation.

 - The letter focuses on Guevara's personal commitment and ideological stance but does not provide concrete details on policies or specific mechanisms Castro used to maintain power.

\

Source B is valuable for studying Castro's consolidation of power because it is a primary source from Che Guevara ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$), a key revolutionary, offering firsthand insight into the loyalty within the leadership. Additionally, its purpose, being publicly read by Castro, suggests it was used to reinforce ideological unity and legitimize his rule ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).
