# HI0270

Describe **three** impacts of Mao's implementation of $`$collective ownership of the means of production' on the agricultural sector of China from 1950-1957. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following with supporting details:

- June 1950 Land Reform Programme: 

    - Mao launched a programme of land reform with the Agrarian Reform Law, which confiscated and redistributed the property of rural landlords. Land ownership remained private, but the former landlords who survived the attacks of the $`$speak bitterness campaign', which encouraged peasants to turn on their landlord, now held only as much land as the poorest peasant.  

- 1951-1953 cooperative farming:

    - The Communist party cadres encouraged peasants to farm the land cooperatively, with profits distributed according to the land, tools, animals and work contributed by individual peasants. The land was still individually owned while resources were pooled for better grain production. By 1953, almost 40% of peasant farms belonged to mutual aid teams or cooperatives. From 1953 on, the state was the sole purchaser of grain.    

- 1954-1955 Agricultural Producers' Co-operatives (APCs):

    - The peasants were encouraged to create higher-level Agricultural Producers' Co-operatives. Land was now pooled and no longer individually owned with profits being distributed according to work contributed by each peasant. Individual peasant farmers were unable to obtain bank loans, buy seed and fertilizer or get help during harvest time. 

    - By 1957 over 96% of peasant families were part of Agricultural Producers' Co-operatives and peasants were obliged to sell fixed quotas to the state. 

- 1958: Agricultural Producers' Cooperatives become $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Communes”.

    - APCs were amalgamated into much larger units called Communes where there were no more private farms. 

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

In June 1950, Mao launched his land reform programme with the passing of the Agrarian Reform Law. This implemented the confiscation of property from rural landlords and its redistribution amongst the landless peasants ($\checkmark$). This was accompanied by the $`$speak bitterness campaign', in which party cadres encouraged the peasants to subject the landlords to a $`$people's trial', where many lost their lives as they were subject to the anger of the mob ($\checkmark$). 

The next step in Mao's agrarian reforms was from 1951-1953, when the Communist party cadres encouraged peasants to farm their land cooperatively, with profits being distributed according to the land, tools, animals and work contributed by individual peasants ($\checkmark$). Land was still individually owned while resources were pooled for better grain production ($\checkmark$). 

By 1954-1955, the peasants were now encouraged to create higher-level Agricultural Producers' Co-operatives. Land was now pooled and no longer individually owned with profits being distributed according to work contributed by each peasant ($\checkmark$). Individual peasant farmers who held out were unable to obtain bank loans, buy seed and fertilizer or get help during harvest time ($\checkmark$).
