# HI0070

Explain how the use of force differed between Hitler's regime and Mao Zedong's regime in maintaining power.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **four** of the following:

- Hitler used highly organised paramilitary groups like the SA and SS to eliminate political opponents and enforce Nazi policies, while Mao relied on loosely organised, ideologically driven Red Guards to target perceived enemies during the chaotic Cultural Revolution.

- Hitler's force was aimed at eliminating external political opponents like Communists and Jews, while Mao used force primarily against internal enemies within the Communist Party and intellectuals.

- Hitler's use of force extended beyond Germany in military conflicts and expansionism, while Mao focused on internal consolidation through purges and land reforms.

- Hitler framed violent actions as legal measures for national security, using laws like the Reichstag Fire Decree, whereas Mao bypassed formal legal processes, using mass mobilisation to carry out violence.

- Hitler's use of force maintained strict control and stability through fear, while Mao's use of force during the Cultural Revolution caused long-term social upheaval and instability.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Hitler used highly organised paramilitary forces like the SS and SA to suppress opposition and enforce Nazi policies, whereas Mao relied on the chaotic Red Guards during the Cultural Revolution, who targeted intellectuals and internal enemies with less centralised control ($\checkmark$). Hitler's use of force primarily focused on eliminating external political enemies such as Communists and Jews, while Mao concentrated on internal ideological control, aiming to purify the Communist Party and eliminate counter-revolutionary elements ($\checkmark$). Additionally, Hitler's use of force contributed to maintaining a stable, if repressive, political order in Germany, while Mao's reliance on mass mobilisation and violence during the Cultural Revolution led to social chaos and long-term instability in Chinese society ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, Hitler framed many of his violent actions as legally justified through measures like the Reichstag Fire Decree, whereas Mao's actions often bypassed formal legal processes, especially during the Cultural Revolution when mass violence was encouraged outside of legal channels($\checkmark$).
