# HI0272

Describe **two** impacts of the Great Leap Forward on China. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting details:

- Failure of agricultural systems:

    - Communal farming failed as Chinese farmers were not able to adapt to the demands of large scale farming. Farmers left their fields fallow as the focus of propaganda was on keeping the backyard furnaces running. Many farmers melted their tools to produce steel, much of which was unusable. 

    - The $`$four pests campaign' endorsed by Mao further damaged agriculture. The four pests campaign $`$outlawed' and was aimed at the eradication of flies, mosquitoes, rats and sparrows. The killing of sparrows led to an explosion of caterpillars and other insects that destroyed crops.

    - The focus on producing crude steel in the backyard furnaces led to neglect of animal husbandry and growing of vegetables. 

- Famine and mass death

    - Government increased the share of grain collected from the communes from 17% to 28%, leaving the communes with insufficient food for its members and leading to starvation. 

    - 1959 to 1961 (the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Three Bitter Years”) witnessed the death of 20 to 50 million Chinese as a result of the policies implemented during the Great Leap Forward and were exacerbated by the drought that affected northern and central China in 1960 and floods in Southern China. 

- Loss of some power for Mao and new Chinese leadership

    - The failure of the Great Leap Forward resulted in Mao stepping down as the Chairman of the People's Republic of China while retaining his position as Head of the Communist Party. 

    - Liu Shaoqi, as the new Head of State along with Deng Xiaoping, now introduce elements of private ownership in order to stabilise the situation. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Great Leap Forward failed to meet its agriculture or steel production targets ($\checkmark$). Communal farming failed as Chinese farmers could not adapt to the demands of large-scale farming. Farmers left their fields fallow as propaganda focused on keeping the backyard furnaces running. The $`$four pests campaign', endorsed by Mao, aimed to eradicate flies, mosquitoes, rats and sparrows. The killing of sparrows led to an explosion of caterpillars and other insects that destroyed crops ($\checkmark$).

Focusing on producing crude steel in backyard furnaces to increase steel production in China was also a failure of the Great Leap Forward ($\checkmark$). As peasants were told they would get rich quickly if they concentrated on iron and steel production, many farmers melted their tools to produce steel, much of which was unusable. This further impacted agricultural production and, at the same time, failed to contribute to an increase in steel production ($\checkmark$).

This resulted in $`$three bitter years' from 1959 to 1961, which witnessed the death of 20 to 50 million Chinese from the policies implemented during the Great Leap Forward and from the natural disasters that hit China at the same time - a drought that affected northern and central China in 1960 and floods in Southern China ($\checkmark$). In the end, the failure of the Great Leap Forward resulted in Mao stepping down as the Chairman of the People's Republic of China while retaining his position as Head of the Communist Party ($\checkmark$).
