# HI0651

Describe **four** impacts of World War I on civilians in Germany.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Any **four** of the following:
- The German civilians were rationing their food like the other countries and experienced a massive food shortage on the homefront. This led to what was called the Turnip Winter, where most people were forced to eat only turnips.
- The German government used propaganda to convince the people at home to support the war effort. This propaganda came in the form of posters and cartoons that portrayed the allies as evil or greedy.
- Germany had a compulsory military service procedure in place even before the war, though the service time was extended during the war. This led to less men on the homefront and in the factories and farms.
- Women were expected to work in the factories and on the farms in order to produce food and weapons for the war.
- Food shortages and devastating military losses eventually led to political upheaval. The Kaiser was overthrown and a new government was created.
- Social unrest and decline in civilian morale occurred due to military losses and general dissatisfaction with the war effort.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Germany, like most other countries during World War I, was in a total war. Every aspect of civilian lives revolved around the war effort. This could be seen most clearly through the policy of compulsory military service for men. While many men served voluntarily due to the nationalist and militaristic attitude of the country at the time, all men between 20 and 45 were expected to serve ($\checkmark$). Large numbers of men enlisting in the German military led to an increased number of women in the workforce. These women were responsible for working in agriculture and the factories, producing food and weapons for the war effort ($\checkmark$). The German civilians were also forced to ration their food while experiencing food shortages due to a British naval blockade. This led to many people dying of starvation and a loss of support for the war effort ($\checkmark$). All of these problems led to political upheaval at home in the last few months of the war. After some devastating military losses, civilians and members of the military revolted against the leadership leading to the abdication of Kaiser Wilhelm II and the eventual exit from the war ($\checkmark$).
