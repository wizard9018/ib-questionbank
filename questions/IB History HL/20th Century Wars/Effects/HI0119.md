# HI0119

Evaluate the impact of WWI on the role of women.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Since the command term is “evaluate”, the response must contain **both positive and negative** results to reach more than four marks. To reach full marks, there must be a **conclusion.**

Positive results:
- The war allowed women to take up occupations traditionally monopolised by men, especially in the war industries.
- Women gained the right to vote in Germany, Russia, Austria, Britain, Czechoslovakia, the Netherlands, Poland, Sweden, the US and Belgium
- Their wages were higher than traditional women's work.
- In Britain, some professions opened up to women after the war; they could now train to become architects and lawyers and were allowed to serve on a jury.

Negative results:
- Women were supposed to receive equal wages to men for similar jobs, but rarely did.
- With demobilisation and the smaller demand for goods that characterised the post war years, most women lost the jobs they had done during the war.
\
[5 marks]

Concluding remark:
- Responses are likely to conclude that the overall results of WWI were positive for the role of women in opening up new possibilities in the economic and political spheres.
- The strongest responses will mention the overall positive results while acknowledging the negative.
\
[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
WWI had many positive impacts on the role of women although many of them were short lived. Due to the lack of men available because of mobilisation and the increased demand caused by the war, women were needed in industry to perform what were previously viewed as men's jobs. This opened up new possibilities for employment and getting better wages than in traditional “women's jobs” ($\checkmark\, \text{positive}$). However, in their new jobs, they were paid less than men even though they performed the same tasks ($\checkmark\, \text{negative}$). The end of the war meant the return to “home life” for most women, so new job opportunities were not a lasting impact of WWI on women ($\checkmark\, \text{negative}$).

On the positive side, even though new job opportunities did not last long for most positions, there were some exceptions. Women in Great Britain were allowed to train to become architects and lawyers after the war ($\checkmark\, \text{positive}$) and in most countries, their contribution to war effort made it evident women should have a say in government. That is why, in many countries such as the UK and the US, the right to vote was extended to women right after the war ($\checkmark\, \text{positive}$).

Overall, WWI had a positive impact in opening up new possibilities for women in the economic and political spheres. Their participation in the war effort and in the world of work made it hard to sustain the idea that women should be limited to home life. That is why, even though the economic opportunities were short lived, they paved the way for women to get the right to vote which had been denied to them previously ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
