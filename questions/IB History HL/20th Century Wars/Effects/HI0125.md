# HI0125

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyze the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying the situation of Germany after WWII. [4 marks]

| |  | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpt from the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Speech of Hope" by James F. Byrnes, United States Secretary of State (September 6, 1946). This speech was given in Stuttgart, Germany.|

 

:box[When the ruthless Nazi dictatorship was forced to surrender unconditionally, there was no German government with which the Allies could deal. The Allies had temporarily to take over the responsibilities of the shattered German state, which the Nazi dictatorship had cut off from any genuine accountability to the German people. The Allies could not leave the leaders or minions of Nazism in key positions, ready to reassert their evil influence at first opportunity. They had to go.![Screenshot 2025-02-17 at 20.42.35](<images/Screenshot 2025-02-17 at 20.42.35.png>)

But it never was the intention of the American Government to deny to the German people the right to manage their own internal affairs as soon as they were able to do so in a democratic way, with genuine respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms.(...) The Potsdam Agreement wisely provided that administration of the affairs of Germany should be directed toward decentralization of the political structure and the development of local responsibility. This was not intended to prevent progress toward a central government with the powers necessary to deal with matters which would be dealt with on a nation-wide basis. But it was intended to prevent the establishment of a strong central government dominating the German people instead of being responsible to their democratic will. ]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source B is valuable in its origin since it is The source was written in 1946 at the end of WWII, so it has insight into the situation ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). It was also written by one of the men that was in charge of the future of Germany at the end of the war, so we can assume a high degree of knowledge and expertise on the topic ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). However, precisely because it is from 1946, it lacks hindsight and does not yet know about, for example, the establishment of a dictatorship in Eastern Germany ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).

The purpose is limited since it seeks to persuade the Germans that Germany will have democracy eventually even if it does not seem like that in 1946. ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).

The content is valuable since it identifies what the US wants to do with Germany at the end of WWII ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). However, the content is also limited as Byrnes gives his own interpretation of what the Allies intended for Germany but the Allies also included countries like the USSR that did not have the same objectives as the Americans ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
