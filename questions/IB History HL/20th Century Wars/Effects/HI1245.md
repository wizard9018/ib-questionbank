Guidance:
- All wars have political consequences. Students must choose two wars from different world regions and clearly explain the connections between the wars and the political situation that followed.

- Some possible examples include:
    -  **First World War (1914-1918):** The First World War caused the collapse of the German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman, and Russian empires. These empires broke up into independent republics. Due to the harsh realities of the war and the punishments imposed by the peace treaties, many of these republics, including the Russian and German ones, went through a period of civil war and political instability.
    - **Second World War (1939-1945):** The Second World War led to the occupation of Germany and Japan, dismantling their existing political systems. In the case of Germany and Korea, they were divided into two new countries, each representing the opposing sides of the Cold War. In Japan, its political system was modified by limiting the emperor's power to a symbolic role.
    - **Vietnam War (1955-1975):** The Vietnam War resulted in the collapse of South Vietnam and the establishment of a unified communist state. In the United States, the war caused immediate political division and declining public trust in government leadership.
    - **Spanish Civil War (1936-1939):** The Spanish Civil War ended with the victory of Francisco Franco over the Second Spanish Republic and the establishment of an authoritarian dictatorship. Political opponents were repressed or exiled.
    - **Iran-Iraq War (1980-1988):** In Iran, the conflict strengthened the Islamic Republic by consolidating the power of Ayatollah Khomeini, who used the conflict as an excuse to eliminate opposition to his rule. The same can be said for Saddam Hussein in Iraq and his increased repression of dissenters.
    - **Chinese Civil War (1927-1949):** The war led to the control by the Chinese Communist Party, led by Mao Zedong, over the People's Republic of China.  The Guomindang escaped to the island of Taiwan, where it established a one-party state under the leadership of Chiang Kai-shek. The two parties would seek recognition as the legitimate government of all of China. This was a key issue in Cold War tensions with the Communist bloc recognizing the government set up in Beijing and the anti-communist countries recognizing the government from Taipei.

**Indicative content for the Falklands/Malvinas War (1982) and the Russian Civil War (1917-1922):**

Falklands/Malvinas War
- For Argentina…
    - The collapse of the dictatorship of the military Junta and a return to democracy. The defeat was the last straw for a regime that entered the fight to distract the population from the government's other failings.
    - The war would motivate a peaceful resolution of the territorial disputes with Chile in 1984.
    - The war ended decades of the Argentine Armed Forces` supervision of or direct control over the government.
- For the United Kingdom…
    - The strengthening of the government of Margaret Thatcher, who boosted her popularity through her handling of the war. This allowed her to be reelected as Prime Minister in 1983.
- For both: Diplomatic relations between the two countries remained severed until the Madrid Accords of 1989 and 1990.

Russian Civil War
- Victory of the Bolsheviks over rival political parties. All other parties were eliminated, and the USSR became a one-party state.
- Creation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR) to give the appearance of self-determination to some of the different nations within, but in reality as a means of centralizing all power in Moscow.
- The war legitimized the use of violence and repression to eliminate opposition.
- Trotsky's role in the war put him in a favorable position for the struggle for power that followed Lenin's death.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Wars are constants in history that affect most, if not all, aspects of a nation's development. This essay will focus on the political effects of two wars, the  Malvinas War (1982) between Argentina and the United Kingdom, and the Russian Civil War (1917-1922) between the Bolsheviks and the Whites (a loose alliance of all anti-Bolshevik forces in Russia)  ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). While the defeat of Argentina in the war accelerated the collapse of its military dictatorship and facilitated a return to democratic rule, the Russian Civil War enabled the Bolsheviks to consolidate their power and establish a one-party state based on centralization and repression of dissidents ($\checkmark\, \text{organisation}$). Together, these cases demonstrate how wars can act either as catalysts for political liberalization or as foundations for long-term authoritarian control ($\checkmark\, \text{perspectives}$).

In Argentina, the Malvinas War led to the collapse of the military dictatorship and a return to democracy. The ruling Junta had launched the war partly as a nationalist distraction from economic crisis, repression, and declining legitimacy ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). However, defeat by the United Kingdom in June 1982 destroyed the regime's credibility and triggered widespread public protests like the ones that took place on June 15, a day after Argentina surrendered ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This loss of legitimacy forced the resignation of de facto president Galtieri, who was even removed from the Armed Forces. His successor, Bignone, decreed that democratic and clean elections would be held in October of the following year ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). These elections ended seven years of military dictatorship.

However, unlike other times in Argentina's history when a military dictatorship would be followed by civilian rule supervised by the armed forces, the Malvinas War would have such a negative impact on their reputation that the military never again had an accepted role in government ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). This was especially so because of the report, commissioned by the same armed forces to investigate what had gone wrong in the conflict. Known as the Rattenbach Report, it established that the military junta was guilty of driving the nation to a war for which it was not prepared and of handling it in an unprofessional manner. In short, it blamed the armed forces themselves for the defeat and the negative consequences it had on the economic and social fabric of the nation ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This was the last straw for a population that had already seen their terrible handling of the economy and continued human rights abuses ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). Evidence of the end of the power of the Argentine military was the trial of the junta under the government of Alfonsín, where the leaders were sentenced to prison terms ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). President Alfonsín faced three military uprisings while attempting to hold Argentina's military leaders accountable for their crimes. However, the wide support of the Argentine population for this accountability proved that there was no going back to military control. Defeat in war ended up having positive consequences for Argentina's political stability while hurting the power of the institution that started it ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

On the other hand, in the case of the Russian Civil War, the Bolshevik victory consolidated the Party's power, leading to political stability but also to the end of democracy in the country until the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991. Since other political parties joined the Whites, the Bolsheviks successfully sought to eliminate them. This was first achieved in 1918 with the closure of the Constituent Assembly ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This democratically elected assembly was supposed to organize the future government of Russia and had the Socialist Revolutionaries in a clear majority. However, it was only allowed to meet for one day. The Civil War was a perfect excuse to portray all opposition to the Bolsheviks as an opposition to the Revolution. For example, when the Kronstadt Rebellion of 1921 demanded a return to a “workers' democracy” as in the first months of 1917, Trotsky labelled it as a plot by the Whites and proceeded to suppress it ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This rebellion forced Lenin to realize the fragility of the regime's support among the peasantry and workers and thus the need to centralize decision-making even further ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). The little democracy that existed inside the Bolshevik party itself was also eliminated with the 1921 ban on factions decided by the 10th Party Congress ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This ensured that decisions would not be discussed by anyone outside the Party's top leadership positions, solidifying Bolshevik power.

The Russian Civil War further centralized control over the country, culminating in the creation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR) in 1922. This was a way to appear to grant self-determination to the various nationalities within the former Russian Empire while, at the same time, centralizing real authority in Moscow ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). At the start of the Civil War, to garner the support of the different nationalities, the Bolsheviks had promised the right of the peoples of Russia to self-determination, including secession ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). However, part of the Southern front during the civil war saw the Bolsheviks fighting in the Ukrainian War of Independence, denying the Ukrainian People's Republic's right to secede ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). In practice, the federal system established in 1922 was designed to provide a feeling of autonomy (constitutions, flags, local ministries) that concealed the absolute power of the centralized Communist Party in Moscow ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Moreover, the experience of civil war legitimized violence and repression. The Red Terror and its persecution of perceived opponents through the Cheka (secret police), counted 200,000 victims by 1921 ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$).Censorship and executions were tools to win the civil war, but they became accepted practices of the Soviet state in the long term.  The experiences of the Civil War either forced or allowed the Bolsheviks to centralize control over the people of Russia for years to come ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

In conclusion, the Malvinas War and the Russian Civil War illustrate how war can act as a turning point for a country's government system. In Argentina, military defeat shattered the legitimacy of an already weakened authoritarian regime, permanently discrediting the Armed Forces and enabling a sustained transition to democracy. By contrast, Bolshevik victory in the Russian Civil War allowed the regime to equate political opposition with counterrevolution, using the conditions of war to justify repression, eliminate pluralism, and institutionalize centralized one-party rule ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). While both wars brought political stability, the form that stability took differed fundamentally: democratic consolidation in Argentina versus authoritarian entrenchment in Soviet Russia ($\checkmark\, \text{perspectives}$). These cases demonstrate that whoever wins the war will strengthen their political position and the loser will see it weakened, both in the short and in the long term ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
