# HI0743

Describe changes to the role and status of women in **two** countries as a result of World War Two.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following. Changes to both role and status must be described. 

 - United States: WWII labour shortages drew over 19 million American women into industrial jobs, from shipyards to aircraft factories, symbolized by $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Rosie the Riveter.” This expanded workforce role gave women more independence and made them more visible in public life, fueling calls for gender equality. However, after the war ended, most women were pressured back into domestic life, which slowed progress but planted the seeds for future women's rights movements.

 - Soviet Union: Under total war mobilization, Soviet women served as snipers, pilots (the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Night Witches”), medics, and factory and farm workers. Women's work in combat and industry during the war briefly raised their social status and showed that gender equality was possible. After the war, many were pushed back into traditional roles, but their efforts were praised as patriotic, supporting government messages about shared sacrifice.

 - Britain: With many men conscripted, British women joined the Auxiliary Territorial Service, Women's Land Army, civil defense units, and worked in munitions and transport industries. Their important contributions made them more visible in society and showed they could succeed in non-traditional roles. These wartime experiences helped speed up social changes, including the 1944 Education Act and the development of the welfare state, cementing women's position in post-war society.

 - Germany: The Nazis promoted the idea that women were $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equal but different,” elevating their status as complementary partners in the Volksgemeinschaft. Initially confined by Nazi ideology to “Kinder, Küche, Kirche,” German women were later drafted into weapons factories, auxiliary services, and labour battalions as manpower decreased. This expansion of roles when against the original expectations set by the state. 

 - France: During occupation, French women engaged in resistance work, acting as couriers, intelligence agents, and sabotage operatives, and worked in factories to sustain the war economy. Their bravery in the Liberation elevated their symbolic status. In 1944, France granted women the vote, directly recognizing their contributions. Post-war, these political rights and increased respect marked a lasting shift in women's social status.

 - Japan: Mobilized for total war, Japanese women worked in munitions factories and served as nurses, though many also endured forced sexual slavery as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$comfort women.” Despite this expanded role, pre-war gender norms largely persisted. After surrender, U.S. occupation reforms introduced a new constitution granting women legal equality and political rights, initiating democratic participation. However, traditional views still shaped their status in society. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Nazis advanced the belief that women were $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equal but different,” portraying them as essential, complementary figures within the Volksgemeinschaft (people's community). ($\checkmark$) Under Nazi ideology, women were initially encouraged to remain at home, fulfilling the roles of wife, mother, and homemaker (Kinder, Küche, Kirche), a change for many who had previously been employed outside the home. However, as the war intensified and male enlistment drained the workforce, the regime was forced to conscript women into war industries, auxiliary military services, and labour roles. While women's roles expanded during the war, Nazi policies conflicted with these wartime necessities, as they emphasized traditional gender roles. Women's participation in the workforce was begrudgingly accepted but seen as temporary. ($\checkmark$)

During the German occupation, many French women played a critical role in resistance networks, serving as couriers, intelligence agents, and taking up industrial work. The need for resistance forced women into public roles, which was a shift from their pre-war positions ($\checkmark$). The French Resistance elevated the symbolic status of women, as their contributions to the liberation of France were recognized after the war. In 1944, French women were granted the right to vote, marking a significant political change. This was a major milestone in raising the status of women in French society ($\checkmark$).
