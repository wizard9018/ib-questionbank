For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both leaders were dissatisfied with the outcome of the war:
    -  Rhee (C): Opposed the armistice as it meant accepting division and the continued presence of communists in the North; “a death sentence without protest.”
    - Kim (B): Acknowledged that reunification had not been achieved; “we have not yet reunified the country.”
- Both frame the war as part of a larger ideological struggle:
    - Rhee (C): Viewed the conflict as a front in the fight against communism, part of global Cold War dynamics.
    - Kim (B): Described the war as a battle against “US imperialism” and claimed it exposed the “brutal nature of imperialism” worldwide.
- Both used the war to promote national pride and justify their leadership:
    - Rhee (C): Presented South Korea's resistance as morally correct and rooted in justice.
    - Kim (B): Claimed the armistice was a “great victory,” enhancing the prestige of the Korean people, the army, and the party.
- Both maintained claims to legitimacy over the entire Korean peninsula:
    - Neither leader accepted the legitimacy of the other side's government.
    - Both saw the armistice as a temporary condition, not a permanent settlement.

For contrast, any of the following differences
- Attitude Toward the Armistice
    - Rhee (C): Strongly opposed the armistice; saw it as appeasement and betrayal.
    - Kim (B): Publicly embraced the armistice as a strategic victory, even though military goals were unmet.
- Perceived Outcome of the War
    - Rhee (C): Believed the war had not achieved its goals, particularly Korean reunification under the South.
    - Kim (B): Claimed a political and ideological victory by resisting the U.S. and “forcing” the enemy to sign the agreement.
- Framing of the Enemy
    - Rhee (C): Focused on the threat of communist expansion and Chinese military presence in Korea.
    - Kim (B): Focused on U.S. imperialism as the main aggressor, calling it the “ringleader” of global oppression.
- Vision for the Future
    - Rhee (C): Urged continued resistance to communism and advocated for Korean reunification through stronger action.
    - Kim (B): Positioned the post-war state as stronger, more unified, and ready to continue defending socialism.

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both leaders were unhappy that the war ended without reunification ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Rhee refused to accept any peace that left North Korea intact, calling such a deal “a death sentence without protest” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Similarly, Kim acknowledged that “we have not yet reunified the country,” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) suggesting that complete victory was not achieved. This reveals a shared goal of national reunification, although under very different regimes.

Also, Rhee and Kim both presented the war as part of a broader ideological struggle, tied to Cold War rivalries. ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) Rhee saw the armistice as appeasement in the face of communist expansion, while Kim celebrated the war as a blow against “US imperialism,” stating that it “shattered the myth of the might of U.S. imperialism” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Furthermore, neither Rhee nor Kim accepted the legitimacy of the other Korean government ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Rhee argued that settling the war without unifying Korea would be “a death sentence without protest,” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) implying that anything short of reunification under his government was unacceptable. Kim similarly described the outcome as unfinished, stating, “we have not yet reunified the country,” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) suggesting that the DPRK still aimed to assert control over the entire peninsula. Both treated the armistice as a temporary arrangement rather than a lasting peace ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).

However, the sources also differ. Rhee viewed the proposed armistice as a betrayal of Korean independence and openly rejected it. He feared it would legitimise continued foreign occupation and communist rule in the North. In contrast, Kim portrayed the armistice as a diplomatic and military success, claiming that North Korea had forced the U.S. to sign on its terms, calling it “a great victory” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Rhee saw defeat; Kim declared triumph ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$).
