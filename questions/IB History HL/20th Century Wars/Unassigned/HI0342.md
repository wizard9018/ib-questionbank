# HI0342

$\text{\textquotedblleft}$Nuclear weapons were NOT necessary for the US to win the war with Japan." Provide **three** arguments that support this perspective.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following possible arguments with supporting evidence:
- The US was keen on showing the USSR its superior armament as the alliance created to fight in WWII was turning into the rivalry that would characterize the Cold War
    - At Potsdam there were already many disagreements with Stalin regarding the new world order. Truman did not want Soviet entry into the war against Japan since that would allow the Soviet Union to increase its its influence in East Asia just like it had done in Eastern Europe
- The Japanese were willing to negotiate the peace as long as their Emperor was not made to pay the price for Japan's involvement in WWII.
    - The US demanded an “unconditional surrender” and the Japanese feared this would mean an end to their constitutional monarchy. In fact, after the war, the US allowed the Emperor Hirohito to remain as sovereign, however, it had never clarified this when asking for peace.
- The USSR had promised to join the war against Japan at the Yalta Conference and that was enough to bring the Japanese to their knees.
- Japan was virtually defeated by August of 1945. Military advisers told Truman that Japan that the US naval blockade of the home islands meant that its war effort would wane quickly. Thanks to the blockade and the continued conventional bombing, Japan was expected to surrender soon.
    - The Japanese had sent Stalin a telegram in July 1945 asking for peace.
- What the US really wanted was vengeance for the attack on Pearl Harbour and for the horrible treatment of Japanese prisoners of war.
    - Following the liberation of the Allied prisoner-of-war camps in 1944 and 1945, accounts of horrific conditions and barbaric treatment by the Japanese guards fed a desire for vengeance that has started in 1941
- Making the bomb had cost thousands of hours of research and development as well as billions of dollars. The government had to show the resources had been well spent
    - The Manhattan Project cost approximately \$2 billion by 1945 (or over \$30 billion in 2023 USD)
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Since the dropping of the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, there has been considerable debate on whether these horrific events were really necessary for the US to win the war with Japan. Many arguments support the notion that it was actually not necessary. In fact, many of Truman's military advisers, like General Eisenhower, told him that nuclear weapons were not needed since Japan's surrender was imminent and that it could not hold out much longer due to the US`s blockade of the Japanese home islands ($\checkmark$). This blockade prevented the empire's soldiers from returning home and accessing the resources necessary to keep up the war effort. In fact, in July of 1945, Japan had already requested the USSR to act as a mediator for peace negotiations ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

The main issue that prevented a Japanese surrender was the future of Emperor Hirohito, the political and religious leader of the nation. If the US had guaranteed his position, as it did once the war was over, the Japanese government might have been willing to surrender sooner ($\checkmark$). However, the US insisted on demanding an “unconditional surrender” even in the Potsdam Declaration of July 1945, and this scared the Japanese, who saw their emperor as a living god ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Many historians point to the fact that the US wanted to use its atomic weapons not so much to end the war but to prove its superiority to the USSR since the end of WWII meant the conclusion of the alliance against the Axis and the start of the rivalry that would characterize the Cold War ($\checkmark$).  At the Potsdam Conference, disagreements between Truman and Stalin over the post-war world order were already evident. Truman wanted to end the war as quickly as possible so that the Soviets would not have to set foot on Japan, allowing them to extend Soviet influence in East Asia, much like it had in Eastern Europe ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
