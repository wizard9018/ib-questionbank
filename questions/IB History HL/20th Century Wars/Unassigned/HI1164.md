# HI1164

Discuss **three** economic effects of the USSR's invasion of Afghanistan.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Massive Military Expenditure
    - The war cost the USSR an estimated US $8-10 billion per year, diverting funds from civilian sectors.
    - Sustained defence spending (up to $30\%$ of GDP) deepened economic stagnation and weakened domestic growth.
- Resource Drain and Opportunity Costs
    - Huge quantities of weapons, fuel, and materials were consumed, reducing industrial output and export capacity.
    - Investment in modernisation and consumer goods was neglected, worsening inefficiency in the command economy.
- Human and Labour Impact
    - Around 15,000 soldiers died, and many more were injured or traumatised, increasing welfare costs.
    - The loss of young workers and veterans' reintegration challenges further strained productivity and the economy.
- Impact on the Soviet Bloc Economy
    - The USSR sent large-scale economic aid to Afghanistan, burdening the wider communist economic system (COMECON).
    - Eastern European allies faced reduced trade and resources as Soviet spending prioritised the war effort.
- Decline in Oil Revenue and Global Economic Pressure
    - Falling oil prices and Western sanctions (trade and grain embargoes) reduced Soviet export income.
    - International isolation, symbolised by the 1980 Olympic boycott, limited investment and economic engagement with the West.
- Impact on Global Economic Relations
    - The invasion ended détente and reignited the Cold War arms race, forcing further unsustainable military spending.
    - The USSR's competition with the U.S. under Reagan's rearmament policies deepened its financial crisis.
- Long-Term Domestic Economic Consequences
    - The war exposed the inefficiency of central planning and increased public discontent.
    - Gorbachev labelled the war an “economic and political burden,” using it to justify reforms, but the damage was lasting.
    - By the late 1980s, the financial strain and loss of legitimacy contributed to the collapse of the Soviet economy and the USSR (1991).
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979-1989) was one of the most costly foreign interventions in Soviet history, imposing enormous financial costs on the Soviet Union, estimated between US \$ 8-10 billion per year throughout the 1980s ($\checkmark$). Occurring at a time of stagnation in the Soviet economy, it contributed to structural weaknesses and to the USSR's decline, with military operations diverting funds from civilian industries, agriculture, and welfare programs. This aggravated public dissatisfaction and revealing severe limits to central planning ($\checkmark$). Withdrawal in 1989 did not reverse the damage as the war had weakened state finances, eroded public trust, and undermined the legitimacy of Soviet leadership ($\checkmark$).
