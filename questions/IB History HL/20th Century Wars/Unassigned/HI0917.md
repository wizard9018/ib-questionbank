# HI0917

Using Source C and your own knowledge, discuss why Syngman Rhee was dissatisfied with the 1953 armistice negotiations.
[5 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An excerpt from a letter from the President of the Republic of Korea, Syngman Rhee, to US President Eisenhower with a “top secret” classification. May 30, 1953. |

[Source: Rhee, S. (1953, May 30). *The President of the Republic of Korea (Rhee) to President Eisenhower.* Office of the Historian. Retrieved June 25, 2025, from https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p1/d565. Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with a conclusion. Both the source and own knowledge must be addressed.

From the source:
- Belief that the armistice amounted to surrender: Rhee viewed the U.S./UN proposal as “appeasing” and warned it “cannot avoid the appearance of surrender,” suggesting it rewarded aggression rather than punished it.
- Fear of permanent division of Korea: He opposed any agreement that would leave Chinese forces in the North, which he believed would solidify Korea's division and undermine national reunification.
- Described the armistice as a “death sentence” for Korea: Rhee warned that accepting the armistice would mean “an acceptance of a death sentence without protest,” showing he feared long-term vulnerability or abandonment.
- Excluded from meaningful participation in negotiations: Rhee complained that the Republic of Korea (ROK) had been “patiently waiting” for others to discuss proposals while South Korea had not had a real say in its own future.
- Emphasised justice and punishment for aggressors: He insisted that the war should end with the punishment of North Korea and Communist China, and that only reunification would ensure peace and collective security.

Own Knowledge:
- Deep mistrust of Communist China and North Korea: Rhee believed any truce leaving communist forces in Korea would only delay future aggression and allow them time to regroup.
- Uncompromising commitment to reunification under ROK rule: Rhee's ultimate goal was a unified Korea led by the South; he saw the armistice as abandoning this objective.
- Strong nationalist ideology and anti-communism: Rhee’s political identity was rooted in fierce anti-communism and Korean nationalism, making him unwilling to accept any solution that preserved communist rule in the North.
- Frustration with U.S. priorities: Rhee felt the U.S. was prioritising global Cold War stability over Korean national interests, especially after Eisenhower showed willingness to end the war even with Korea divided.
- Attempted to sabotage armistice talks: In June 1953, Rhee released 27,000 North Korean POWs unilaterally to disrupt negotiations and pressure the U.S. into making stronger commitments.
- Used the crisis to demand a U.S.–ROK mutual defense treaty: Rhee's resistance to the armistice was also a negotiation tactic to secure long-term military guarantees from the U.S., which he eventually achieved in October 1953.
\

- Provide a clear summary judgment that accurately reflects the evidence provided about Rhee's dissatisfaction/opposition to the armistice.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
First, Rhee rejected any agreement that would allow Chinese Communist forces to remain in Korea, warning it would be “an acceptance of a death sentence without protest.” Second, he criticised the United Nations' proposal as overly conciliatory, stating it appeared to be an act of “appeasement” that could lead to disaster ($\checkmark$).

Beyond the source, Rhee's broader frustration stemmed from being sidelined in negotiations that directly affected his country's future. Despite leading South Korea, he was excluded from key decisions made by the U.S. and UN ($\checkmark$).

Additionally, his goal of unifying Korea under Southern leadership was completely undermined by a ceasefire that left the peninsula divided. Rhee feared this division would become permanent, and he saw the armistice as a betrayal of Korean sovereignty ($\checkmark$).

In conclusion, Rhee opposed the armistice because it not only failed to secure Korean reunification but also left his country vulnerable to future threats, all while excluding him from shaping the outcome ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
