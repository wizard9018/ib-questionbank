# HI0649

Describe the First Moroccan Crisis (1905-1906) and explain how it increased tensions among the European powers.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
The following description or similar:
- The First Moroccan Crisis occurred in 1905 after Kaiser Wilhelm II publicly recognized the sultan of Morocco as a sovereign leader in an attempt to lessen France's imperial influence.
\
[1 marks]

Any **three** of the following to explain how it increased tensions:
- Morocco was under the French sphere of influence in the early 1900s (later becoming a protectorate of France). By publicly recognizing the sultan as a sovereign leader, Germany was undermining French influence in the country.
- When the Kaiser declared the sultan sovereign, he was directly challenging France's efforts to build an empire and the Entente Cordiale between Great Britain and France, forcing Great Britain to take a stand in support of France.
- The German chancellor demanded an international conference to settle the dispute between France and Germany. When France said there was no need, the chancellor threatened war.
- When the conference was held, only Austria-Hungary backed Germany. This greatly increased tensions between the alliances in Europe.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The First Moroccan Crisis was an attempt by Kaiser Wilhelm to weaken France's influence in Morocco ($\checkmark$). Though Morocco was an independent state with a sultan leading it, France held a lot of power there. Kaiser Wilhelm was looking to weaken France while also testing Britain and France's new entente ($\checkmark$). In 1905, Kaiser Wilhelm publicly recognized the sultan as the leader of Morocco. This undermined French influence in the country. When France criticized the move, the Kaiser demanded a conference to settle the dispute. France refused a conference, and Germany threatened war ($\checkmark$). When the conference was finally held, Britain and all other European countries except Austria-Hungary backed France's claims ($\checkmark$).
