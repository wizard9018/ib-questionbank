# HI0303

Describe **two** political causes of the Korean War.

[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - The division of Korea after WWII led to the country being split along the 38th parallel, with North Korea led by Kim Il Sung, a communist leader, and South Korea led by Syngman Rhee, an anti-communist leader. Both claimed to be the legitimate government, leading to increasing tensions and border conflicts

 - Cold War tensions between the United States and the Soviet Union turned Korea into a proxy battleground, as both superpowers sought to expand their influence and ideologies in Asia, making war more likely.

 - The Soviet Union and China encouraged North Korea's leader, Kim Il Sung, to invade the South, providing military aid and believing the United States would not intervene, increasing North Korea's willingness to launch an attack.

 - The United States' policy of containing communism led to its commitment to defending South Korea, ensuring that any invasion by the North would result in direct American involvement, increasing the scale of the conflict.

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One political cause of the Korean War was the division of Korea at the 38th parallel after World War II, which resulted in two opposing governments. Kim Il Sung led the communist North, while Syngman Rhee led the anti-communist South, and both claimed to be the rightful rulers of all Korea, leading to rising tensions ($\checkmark$). Another cause was the broader Cold War rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union. Korea became a key battleground in their ideological struggle, with each superpower supporting its respective Korean ally, making conflict increasingly inevitable ($\checkmark$).
