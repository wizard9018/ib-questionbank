# HI0143

Explain the impact of British appeasement policies on Hitler's foreign policy. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Any combination of **four** of the following:

 - Britain's acceptance of Hitler's initial foreign policies in 1934-1935 such as revising the terms of the Treaty of Versailles, a declaration of rearmament and implementing conscription gave Hitler the initial confidence that he would not be opposed.  

 - The signing the Anglo-German Naval pact of 1935 approving the expansion of the German Navy which would be limited to 35 percent of the size of the British navy; hence condoning the violation of the terms of the Treaty of Versailles. This permitted German rearmament. 

 - Permitting Hitler's remilitarization of the Rhineland, another violation of the terms of the Treaty of Versailles, strengthened Hiter's growing confidence that he would face no opposition from major European powers. 

 - Britain's acceptance of Anschluss, the annexation of Austria, in March of 1938 and which was forbidden by the Treaty of Versailles, gave Hiter the confidence to expand into Czechoslovakia next. 

 - The signing of the Munich Agreement in September 1938 permitted Hitler to occupy the Sudeten area of Czechoslovakia making it clear that he would be unopposed in his quest to expand Germany's existing borders.

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

British policy of appeasement helped Hitler embark on his expansionist foreign policy. Lack of opposition to initial German rearmament and expansion of the German army permitted Hitler to strengthen his defence forces ($\checkmark$). Germany rearmament was further validated with the signing of the Anglo-German Naval pact in 1935  that approved the expansion of the German Navy which would be limited to 35 percent of the size of the British navy. This British action condoned the violation of the terms of the Treaty of Versailles and officially permitted German rearmament ($\checkmark$). The lack of response from Britain (and France) when Hitler sent troops to take over Austria in March 1938, resulted in acceptance of Anschluss that was forbidden by the Treaty of Versailles. This gave Hiter the confidence to expand into Czechoslovakia next and increased his demands for Sudetenland, the German speaking area of Czechoslovakia ($\checkmark$). The  highlight of the appeasement policy was the signing of the Munich Agreement in September 1938, permitting Hiter to occupy the Sudeten area of Czechoslovakia. This further convinced Hitler that he would be unopposed in his quest to continually expand Germany's existing borders ($\checkmark$).
