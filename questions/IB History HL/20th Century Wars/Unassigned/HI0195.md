# HI0195

What does Source C suggest about Nazi foreign policy in the late 1930s?

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | A political cartoon by cartoonist Clifford Berryman titled “It's A Good Act But It's Hard On The Spectators” published in the Washington Evening Star in August of 1939. It shows Hitler balancing the world on the tip of a rifle balanced on the tip of his nose as spectators, including Uncle Sam, John Bull, and a caricature of France, watch. 

![HI0195(i)](<images/HI0195(i).jpg>)

[Source: Berryman, C. K. (August 20, 1939). *It's A Good Act But It's Hard On The Spectators*. Washington Evening Star. Retrieved on January 12, 2025 from https://catalog.archives.gov/id/6012197. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - The source suggests that Nazi foreign policy was aggressive and aimed at destabilizing the global order, as symbolized by Hitler balancing the world on a rifle.

 - The cartoon portrays Nazi foreign policy as reckless and precarious, symbolized by the delicate act of balancing the world, highlighting the risks inherent in Hitler's strategies.

 - The nervous spectators, including Uncle Sam, John Bull (Britain), and France, reflect widespread international concern about Hitler's aggressive and unpredictable foreign policy.

 - By placing the world literally at the tip of a rifle, the cartoon suggests that Nazi foreign policy directly endangered global peace and stability.

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The source suggests that Nazi foreign policy in the late 1930s was aggressive and destabilizing ($\checkmark$). This is depicted by Hitler balancing the world precariously on the tip of a rifle, symbolizing the recklessness of his actions, while the anxious spectators, representing nations like the U.S., Britain, and France, reflect widespread fear of the consequences of his policies ($\checkmark$).
