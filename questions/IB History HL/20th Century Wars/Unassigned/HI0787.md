# HI0787

What does Source A suggest about Belgium's relationship with the Congo Free State?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | A political cartoon originally published in 1906 in the British magazine Punch. The cartoon shows a Congolese worker tangled with a snake that has the face of King Leopold II of Belgium. The first caption reads $\text{\textquotedblleft}$In the rubber coils” and the second, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$SCENE - The Congo $`$Free' State.”

![HI0787_A](images/HI0787_A.png)

[Source: Sambourne\, E. L. (1906). *In the Rubber Coils*. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved on May 15, 2025 from  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Punch_congo_rubber_cartoon.jpg. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - By putting free in quotation marks, the artist is implying that the Congo is not actually free under Belgian rule.

 - The crown and face on the head of the snake suggests that King Leopold of Belgium II is the main person harming the people of the Congo.

 - The snake is wrapped around a person and the person is struggling to keep the snake from harming him. This suggests that Belgium is not considering the wellness of the people of the Congo.

 - People are watching and running away in horror which implies that they see the bad things happening but couldn't do anything to stop it.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source A suggests that Belgium is abusive to the people who live in their colonies. This is implied by the snake being wrapped around the Congolese figure ($\checkmark$). People are looking on in horror and that implies that there was not much they could do to defend themselves against the Belgians ($\checkmark$). Also, the title of the cartoon has the word free in quotation marks and this gives the impression that the artist does not believe the people of the Congo are free at all ($\checkmark$).
