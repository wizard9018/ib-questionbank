# HI0532

What does Source A suggest about General Francisco Franco's influence during the war?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- | 

|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | A propaganda poster titled El Generalisimo by Pedero, a poster artist for a socialist trade union during the Spanish Civil War. The title depicts Francisco Franco as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$*el generalisimo*” or supreme commander whose cape is being carried by three characters representing the military, business owners and the Catholic Church

![HI0532](images/HI0532.png)

[Source: Pedero. (1937). *El Generalisimo.* Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved February 21, 2025 from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_General%C3%ADsimo.jpg. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any three of the following

 - Franco is larger than other characters, which implies that he is considered to be extremely powerful.

 - Franco is shown with a skull face, suggesting that he is considered deadly or to be feared.

 - The swastika on his uniform suggests that his power is backed by Germany.

 - The men trailing behind him, holding his cape and representing the military, the capitalists, and the Catholic Church, suggest that all three groups supported Franco and his policies.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

In this poster, General Franco is larger than the men following him, suggesting that he is the most powerful among them ($\checkmark$) The swastika on his chest indicates that his power might come from foreign sources like the Nazis ($\checkmark$). The three men trailing behind him look like they represent the Church, the military and the wealthy and that they are backing him and riding his coattails to gain more power ($\checkmark$).
