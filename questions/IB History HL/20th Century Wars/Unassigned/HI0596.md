# HI0596

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying the internment of Japanese Americans during WWII.

[5 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Paul Sparrow, Director of the FDR Library, in an introduction to the exhibition *Images of Internment* held at the Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum (2017).

[Source: Sparrow, P. M. (2017, February 17). *Images of Internment*. Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum. National Archives. Retrieved February 10, 2025  from https://fdr.blogs.archives.gov/2017/02/17/images-of-internment/.  Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following: 

 - The source contains hindsight as it was made in 2017 so the author is able to analyze events detached from the passions of WWII. He is also the Director of the FDR Library so he is likely an expert on FDR's actions.  

 - The text seeks to give a short explanation of the internment of Japanese Americans since it is the introduction of an exhibit on the topic that was organized by the Library and Museum (purpose). 

 - From the source we can learn the reasons why Roosevelt gave the executive order as well as some information on those who supported and those who opposed the measure (content).

For limitations, any combination of the following: 

 - The source lacks some insight as it was made by someone who did not live the events and was not privy to the feelings at the time. Moreover, since the author is the Director of the FDR Library and Museum, he is biased in favor of Roosevelt (origin).

 - The source seeks to give only a brief introduction so the details given are few. It also wants to portray FDR in a positive light stressing that it was not really his fault that the executive order was given (purpose).

 - The source's nature as a brief introduction means it has a limited amount of information about the internment of Japanese Americans during WWII. Furthermore, it contains some exaggerations regarding Roosevelt calling him $\text{\textquotedblleft}$one of our greatest champions of human rights” (content).

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source B is valuable for a historian studying the internment of Japanese Americans during WWII. Since it was created in 2017, the origin contains hindsight and is thus able to analyze events without the emotional intensity of World War II. Additionally, the author is the Director of the FDR Library, so he should be an expert on Roosevelt's actions ($\checkmark$ VO). The purpose of the source is valuable since it provides a brief explanation of the internment of Japanese Americans, as it is part of an exhibit organized by the Library and Museum ($\checkmark$ VP). The content allows us to learn some of the reasons Roosevelt issued Executive Order 9066, as well as information on those who supported and opposed the measure ($\checkmark$ VC).

However, the source also has limitations. The origin is limited since the author did not experience the events firsthand, it lacks insight into the emotions of the time. Furthermore, as he is the Director of the FDR Library, the author has a bias in favor of Roosevelt ($\checkmark$ LO). This is apparent in the content of the source where the author includes some exaggerations such as calling Roosevelt $\text{\textquotedblleft}$one of our greatest champions of human rights” ($\checkmark$ LC).The purpose is also limited since the brevity required for an introduction to an exhibition necessarily means that a lot of information must be left out. We can also see from the text that the Director of the FDR Library understandably seeks to portray Roosevelt in a positive light, downplaying his responsibility for the executive order ($\checkmark$ LP).
