## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any combination of **two** or more of the following with supporting discussion for **four** marks maximum:

 - Source B states the British attempt at appeasing Mussolini as proposed by the Hoare Laval Pact was undermined by the British public who believed that the League should follow its principles and stop Italian aggression in Abyssinia. Mussolini hence feared that public opinion in England might demand the closing of the Suez Canal and deeply resented the negativity of public opinion shown in the British press.

 - Source C states that the British policy towards Mussolini was hugely influenced by public opinion. Hence in the General Election in November 1935, the National Government of Britain was reelected for showing their perceived support of the League. However, the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$floating vote”, a group of people whose support was unpredictable, shifted their opinion and this public opposition forced the Government to reject the Hoare-Laval plan and its author for appeasing Italy and Mussolini's aggression. . 

 - Source C also states that after the rejection of the Hoare-Laval Plan, those that believed in sanctions were unable to follow it up as they no longer had the support of the large mass of middle opinion (the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$floating vote”, who now were beginning to be “bored” with the situation and thus making it difficult for the League to follow through with effective sanctions on Italy. 

[4 marks]

Concluding statement for **one** mark that aligns with the evidence and discussion presented:

The best responses will include evidence from both Sources B and C and provide specific evidence of the impacts of British public opinion on its policies toward Mussolini.

[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

British policy towards Mussolini's demands and their attempt at appeasement as put forward by the Hoare-Laval pact was $\text{\textquotedblleft}$undermined by the honest British people who believed that the principles of the League should be carried out” as stated in Source B ($\checkmark$). Mussolini hence feared that public opinion in England might demand the closing of the Suez Canal as stated in the source and deeply resented the reprimands of public opinion in the British press ($\checkmark$). This impact of British public opinion was also apparent when $\text{\textquotedblleft}$at the General Election in November 1935, the government was elected as a reward for their perceived support of the League” (Source C). The source states that the group of undecided voters (the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$floating vote”) had been cast for the National Government, as a “reward for its support of the League'. However the government loses this support “in December with the revelation of the Hoare-Laval pact and public opinion swung violently away and forced the Government to repudiate the Hoare-Laval plan and its author” ($\checkmark$).

Source C also states that those who supported sanctions found that they failed to keep the pressure on Mussolini because after the rejection of the Hoare-Laval Plan, the British public became less interested in the crisis in Abyssinia. This lack of interest made it difficult for the League to follow through with effective sanctions ($\checkmark$). 

Sources B and C display the power of British public opinion which put pressure on the British government to support the League's sanctions against Italy ($\checkmark$).
