 - They both identify racism as a cause of why people of Japanese descent were targeted

     - Source B: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$entrenched and widespread racism of that time”

     - Source B: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The 'dastardly' surprise attack on Pearl Harbor, and Japan's brutal military expansion throughout Asia, enflamed the entrenched and widespread racism of that time.”

     - Source B: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$There was sincere concern about espionage and sabotage, and fear that the Japanese Empire could attack”

For contrast, any one to two of the following differences:

 - The sources differ on who was calling for internment. 

     - Source B speaks of military leaders, Congress, newspapers, and the military: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The President was under tremendous pressure from military commanders, members of congress … Leading newspaper columnists demanded action.”

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Sources B and C give similar reasons for Roosevelt's Executive Order confining people of Japanese descent to internment camps. First, they both identify racism as a factor in the targeting of Japanese Americans ($\checkmark$ similarity), with Source B referring to the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$entrenched and widespread racism of that time” and Source C describing how $\text{\textquotedblleft}$long-standing anti-Asian racism turned into disaster for Japanese Americans” ($\checkmark$ evidence). Both also mention the attack on Pearl Harbor as the catalyst for Executive Order 9066 (✓ similarity), with Source B emphasizing how the attack $\text{\textquotedblleft}$enflamed the entrenched and widespread racism of that time,” while Source C states that the attack $\text{\textquotedblleft}$launched a rash of fear about national security” ($\checkmark$ evidence). Furthermore, both sources highlight the fear for national security as one of the reasons for the order ($\checkmark$ similarity). Source B mentions $\text{\textquotedblleft}$sincere concern about espionage and sabotage, and fear that the Japanese Empire could attack”  while Source C says that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the attack launched a rash of fear about national security” ($\checkmark$ evidence). 

However, the sources differ on what groups were calling for the internment of people of Japanese descent ($\checkmark$ contrast). Source B mentions pressure from military leaders, Congress and how $\text{\textquotedblleft}$leading newspaper columnists demanded action.” In contrast, Source C highlights how $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Lobbyists from western states, many representing competing economic interests or nativist groups, pressured Congress and the President” ($\checkmark$ evidence). The role of lobbyists helps explain why Source C includes “economic competition, distrust over cultural separateness, and long-standing anti-Asian racism” as reasons for internment, while Source B stays focused on the military aspect, saying,  $\text{\textquotedblleft}$There was sincere concern about espionage and sabotage, and fear that the Japanese Empire could attack or even invade the West Coast” ($\checkmark$ contrast and evidence).
