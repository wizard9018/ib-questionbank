# HI0293

Explain the impact of WWII on minorities in the United States.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with its corresponding evidence:

 - Around 1.5 million African Americans moved in search of the jobs that opened up in the war industries. In their new homes, they had substandard housing, and experienced discrimination from neighbouring communities. This occasionally led to race riots such as the one that occurred in Detroit in 1943

 - Roosevelt, by decree, prohibited war industries from discriminating when hiring employees. This was the first time race discrimination was outlawed. Thanks to this, while African Americans suffered around 50$\%$ unemployment in 1940, and usually found work only in low-level positions, by 1945 African Americans made up 8$\%$ of all war production workers.

 - The National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP) was an organization that pressed for racial equality. It successfully pressured the US government to allow African American men to participate in combat, although in segregated units. 

 - NAACP membership went from 50,000 members in 1940 to 450,000 in 1946. Many of the new members were returning servicemen determined to fight for their civil rights. 

 - In 1948, Truman prohibited segregation in the Armed Forces.

 - 1942-Executive order to remove people of Japanese descent from the West coast. About 120,000 were sent to internment camps even though ⅔ were American citizens. They were ordered to dispose of their property in very little time=lost their jobs and businesses

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

There was an improvement in the access to equal rights for some minorities in the United States. In great part, this was due to the need to increase their contribution to the war effort. President Roosevelt issued a decree prohibiting war industries from discrimination based on race when hiring employees. This is the first time race discrimination was outlawed in the US. Thanks to this, by 1945, African Americans made up 8$\%$ of all war production workers which helped them improve their economic situation ($\checkmark$). Other roles were also opened up for African Americans, most notably in the Armed Forces. The National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP) played a key role in successfully advocating to convince the government to allow African American men to participate in combat, even if they did so in segregated units ($\checkmark$).

Other minorities, however, were greatly disadvantaged by the war situation. The clearest example is that of Japanese Americans who lived on the West Coast who, due to an executive order of 1942, were sent to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$internment camps” ($\checkmark$).  Approximately 120,000 persons, two-thirds of whom were American citizens, were sent to internment camps having to leave their jobs and business, losing their freedom until 1945 and their economic stability for many more years ($\checkmark$).
