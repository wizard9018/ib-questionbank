# HI0080

Evaluate the significance of the Long March for the Chinese Communist Party (CCP).
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Since the command term is “evaluate”, the response must contain both positive and negative results to reach more than **four** marks. To reach full marks, there must be a conclusion.

The following ideas may be present in the answer to address significance:

Positive results:
- It allowed the CCP to survive the first phase of the Chinese Civil War.
- It brought the CCP close to the areas subject to the Japanese invasion.
- It brought the CCP to an area with limited GMD presence so they could start implementing their policies in the areas under their control.
- It left Mao as the only surviving leader of the CCP.
- It put an end to Jiang Jieshi`s encirclement campaigns.
- It served as great propaganda material having covered 9,600km of China's most inhospitable territory in little over a year.

Negative results:
- The CCP had to retreat in the face of GMD pressure.
- It led to the loss of more than 90% of the Communists that had been fighting the GMD in the Jiangxi Soviet.
\
[5 marks]

Concluding remark:
- Responses are likely to conclude that the overall results of the Long March were positive for the CCP especially in the long-term.
- The strongest responses will mention the overall positive results while acknowledging the negative.
\
[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Long March was a threshold moment in the history of the CCP as it allowed the CCP to survive ($\checkmark$) and start implementing the reforms that would bring them the peasants' support ($\checkmark$). Going north put them in a position where they would have to fight the Japanese and thus be perceived as saviors of the nation ($\checkmark$) especially as the GMD was not viewed as doing its job of defending the country from foreign aggression. Meanwhile, the GMD now had to think of a new strategy to fight the CCP since the encirclement campaigns had failed ($\checkmark$). The Long March also cemented Mao's position within the CCP as most of his rivals died during the trek ($\checkmark$) and, in later years, it would serve as the basis of propaganda that portrayed Mao as its hero ($\checkmark$).

However, the CCP was accepting that it could not defeat the GMD in the south and was essentially running away with the Long March ($\checkmark$). Moreover, it caused considerable damage to CCP's forces with nearly 90% lost to either death or abandonment ($\checkmark$).

Even though the Long March was the result of initial weakness and it meant a huge loss of life for the CCP  it still had an overall positive impact. The March placed the Party on its path to victory under the undisputed leadership of Mao and in a new territory where they could implement their policies as well as defend the nation from Japanese aggression ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
