# HI0430

Describe **two** reasons the Catholic Church supported Franco and the Nationalists during the Spanish Civil War. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

- Opposition to Republican anti-clerical reforms

    - The Second Republic (1931-1936) introduced secular laws that weakened the Catholic Church's influence, such as banning religious education, confiscating Church property, and legalising divorce.

    - These measures were viewed as attacks on traditional values. Many churches were burned and priests assaulted, deepening hostility toward the Republican government.

    - The Church saw Franco as someone who would restore its authority and reverse secularisation.

- Fear of left-wing violence and persecution

    - The Republican side included radical left-wing groups such as anarchists and communists who promoted atheism and were involved in violent acts against the Church.

    - Religious sites were desecrated, clergy were executed, and symbols of Catholicism were destroyed.

    - The Church aligned itself with the Nationalists as a means of survival and protection against what it saw as a campaign to eliminate religion.

- Franco's pro-Catholic image and policies

    - Franco portrayed himself as a staunch defender of Catholicism and traditional moral values.

    - His regime restored religious privileges, reintroduced Catholic education, and elevated Catholicism as the moral foundation of Spanish identity.

    - The Church supported Franco in return for the promise of social and political restoration.

- Religious framing of the war as a crusade

    - The Nationalists used the language of a religious crusade, presenting the war as a battle to save Spain from atheism and moral decay.

    - This appealed to the Church's sense of spiritual mission and helped frame support for Franco as a moral imperative.

    - The Church gave ideological and moral backing to the Nationalists, contributing to their strong support base.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One reason the Catholic Church supported Franco and the Nationalists was its opposition to the anti-clerical policies of the Second Republic ($\checkmark$). The Republican government had introduced reforms that restricted the Church's influence, such as banning religious education in schools, confiscating Church property, and legalising divorce. Many priests and religious figures were persecuted, leading the Church to align itself with Franco, who promised to restore its authority($\checkmark$).

Another reason was the Nationalists' portrayal of the war as a religious crusade against communism and atheism ($\checkmark$). Left-wing groups within the Republican side, including anarchists and communists, viewed the Church as a symbol of oppression and attacked clergy members, burning churches and monasteries. Franco and the Nationalists positioned themselves as defenders of Catholicism, gaining strong Church support in return ($\checkmark$).
