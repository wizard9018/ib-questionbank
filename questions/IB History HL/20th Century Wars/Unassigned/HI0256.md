$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources agree that one reason to use nuclear weapons was to end the war quickly and avoid further American casualties ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source B explains that “an amphibious invasion of the Japanese mainland was unthinkable. Neither were the estimated millions of American lives that would be lost if mainland Japan was invaded” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) and Source C gives a similar motivation when it says: “the evidence does indicate that the view of the bomb as a potential way to end the war quickly--in the hope of (1) avoiding the need for an invasion with resulting casualties that by any standard would be of intimidating proportions” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, the sources also contain many contrasts regarding the decision to drop the atomic bombs on Japan. While Source B stresses Truman´s desire to show the unity of the Allies against Japan by saying that “He hoped the ultimatum would coincide with a successful test of the atomic bomb to demonstrate the resolve of the Allies to Japan,” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) Source C identifies the USSR as a rival of the US saying that the decision had to do with the desire to reduce “the USSR's postwar leverage” and there is not mention of a joint decision regarding the use of the bomb ($\checkmark\, \text{difference }$$\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

They also disagree on who made the decision ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). While Source B states, “No doubt the weight of the world was on Truman's shoulders, and the final decision was not easy. Finally, he concluded it was his decision, alone.” In contrast, Source C speaks of “US leadership” as a group of many who participated in the decision-making ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

A final difference between the sources is that Source B only talks about a desire to avoid casualties as the motivation behind the use of the atomic bombs, while Source C identifies many other motivations ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) such as,  “(2) minimizing the USSR's postwar leverage, and (3) not having to confront debate over concessions on the terms of unconditional surrender” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). This helps explain another difference, the fact that Source B says that Truman had “no alternative,” while Source C explores the possibility of obtaining the surrender of Japan with the entry of the USSR to the war ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$).
