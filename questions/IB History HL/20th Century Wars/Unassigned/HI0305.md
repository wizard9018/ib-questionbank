# HI0305

Describe **two** political causes of the First Chinese Civil War (1927-1937).

[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - The collapse of the First United Front (1924-1927) led to tensions between the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). Chiang Kai-shek turned against the Communists in the Shanghai Massacre of 1927, leading to open conflict.

 - The authoritarian leadership of Chiang Kai-shek alienated various groups, including warlords and left-wing factions, leading to internal opposition within China.

 - The failure of the Nationalist government to implement social and economic reforms weakened its legitimacy, pushing peasants and workers to support the Communists.

 - The influence of Soviet and foreign ideologies contributed to tensions, as the USSR supported the CCP while the KMT sought Western backing, deepening ideological divisions.

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One political cause of the First Chinese Civil War was the breakdown of cooperation between the Kuomintang and the Chinese Communist Party, which culminated in Chiang Kai-shek's violent purge of Communists in 1927, known as the Shanghai Massacre ($\checkmark$). Another cause was the inability of the Nationalist government to address key social and economic issues, which led many disillusioned citizens, particularly peasants, to turn toward Communist leadership as an alternative ($\checkmark$).
