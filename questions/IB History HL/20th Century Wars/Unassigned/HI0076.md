# HI0076

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the values and limitations of this source for a historian studying Britain's policy of appeasement.

[4 marks]

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Extract from a letter from Nevile Henderson, British Ambassador in Germany, September 6th, 1938. Henderson has been described by historians as a supporter of appeasement.

[Source: Henderson, Nevile. (6 September 1938) *European situation: Sudeten-German problem*. Code 18 File 1941 (papers 9619 - 9884). (FO 371/21737). Retrieved from https://nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/chamberlain-and-hitler. UK Open Government Licence.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:

 - The source's origin is valuable because it comes from Nevile Henderson, the British Ambassador to Germany, who had direct contact with Nazi officials and firsthand insight into British-German relations. His position allows historians to understand the perspectives and recommendations of a senior diplomat actively involved in Britains policy of appeasement.

 - The purpose of the source, to advise the British government on handling Hitler through positive media portrayal, provides valuable insight into the strategies employed in appeasement. It reveals a preference for influencing Hitler diplomatically rather than through confrontation, which is crucial for understanding British efforts to avoid war.

 - The content of the source, suggesting that the British press avoid criticizing Hitler and instead focus on presenting him positively, is valuable as it captures the British diplomatic mindset at the time. It illustrates the hope that diplomatic gestures might encourage Hitler toward peace, offering insight into appeasement strategies.

For limitations, any combination of the following:

 - As an ambassador, Henderson's perspective may reflect his personal biases and diplomatic objectives, which could skew his assessment. His views might not represent the entire British government's stance, making it a limited source for understanding broader British policy beyond his own pro-appeasement viewpoint.

 - The purpose is also a limitation, as it reflects an attempt to sway government and press strategy rather than an objective analysis of Hitler's intentions. Henderson's goal to portray Hitler as a potential $\text{\textquotedblleft}$apostle of Peace" may reveal wishful thinking rather than a realistic assessment, thus potentially misrepresenting the Nazi threat.

 - The content is limited as it reflects only Henderson's optimistic perspective on influencing Hitler through appeasement. His suggestion to avoid criticizing Hitler directly may overlook the true extent of Hitler's aggression, thus potentially downplaying the risks of appeasement for contemporary historians.

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The origin of this source, written by Nevile Henderson, the British Ambassador to Germany, is valuable because Henderson had firsthand experience with Nazi officials and was deeply involved in British-German relations. His perspective provides a unique window into the British diplomatic approach at the time, especially as he was a senior figure actively trying to influence the course of events ($\checkmark$ VO). The purpose of the document—to recommend that the British press portray Hitler positively—reveals a strategic attempt to use media as a tool to steer Hitler toward peace, offering insight into the broader rationale behind appeasement policies ($\checkmark$ VP). Furthermore, the content, which suggests avoiding direct criticism of Hitler and instead presenting him as an $\text{\textquotedblleft}$apostle of Peace,” reflects British hopes that diplomacy and public messaging might discourage Nazi aggression, shedding light on Britain's cautious approach during this period ($\checkmark$ VC). 

However, the source has limitations due to its origin, as Henderson's personal position and pro-appeasement stance may color his views, meaning this source might not fully represent other, perhaps more skeptical, voices within the British government ($\checkmark$ LO). The purpose of advising the government to use the press to influence Hitler could be seen as overly optimistic, as it reflects Henderson's belief that media portrayal could sway Hitler's actions, which might not align with the reality of Hitler's ambitions ($\checkmark$ LP). Lastly, the content's suggestion to avoid criticizing Hitler directly could underestimate the true threat of Nazi Germany, as Henderson's approach may reflect wishful thinking about Hitler's potential for peace rather than a realistic assessment, thereby presenting a potentially misleading view of the risks of appeasement ($\checkmark$ LC).
