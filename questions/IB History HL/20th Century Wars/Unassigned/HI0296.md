# HI0296

What does Source A suggest about the negotiations of the Treaty of Brest Litovsk? 

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Cartoon by Clifford Berryman, June 11, 1918. It refers to the negotiations that ended with the signing of the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk. The German character has a paper that reads $\text{\textquotedblleft}$deed to Russian lands” and the small character, identified as Lenine, says $\text{\textquotedblleft}$That's right. The barrel is his”.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - That Russia was giving up a lot of land as symbolized by paper labeled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Deed to Russian lands”

 - That Russia was being left poor as symbolized by how skinny the character labeled as Russia looks and by the fact that he does not even have any clothes

 - That Russia no longer had the capacity to fight, while the German character did

 - Lenin participated in the negotiations, and he was on the German side as he even wants Russia to give up the barrel

 - The Germans kept demanding more from Russia. 

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

From Source A we can learn that Russia had to give up a lot of land to Germany under the Treaty of Brest Litovsk. This is shown by the German character holding a paper labeled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Deed to Russian lands” ($\checkmark$). We can also learn that Lenin participated in the negotiations and that he was on the German side of the debate as he even wanted Russia to give up the barrel, which seems to be the only thing Russia had left ($\checkmark$).
