# HI0429

Explain **two** economic causes of the Spanish Civil War. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following causes with supporting details/explanation:

- Agrarian inequality and rural poverty deepened social divisions

    - Spain's economy remained dominated by agriculture, with land ownership concentrated in latifundios, leaving most rural workers landless and in poverty.

   - The Second Republic's land reforms (1931-1936) were limited and slow, failing to meet peasants' expectations while alienating conservative landowners.

    - Rural uprisings and land seizures led to violent confrontations, pushing many landowners to support Franco and the military to protect their interests.

- Economic instability and the Great Depression increased political polarisation 

    - Pre-existing structural weakness and low industrialisation were worsened by the Great Depression, causing soaring unemployment and widespread poverty.

    The government’s poor economic management frustrated both the working class (who saw reforms as insufficient) and the elites (who saw them as threatening).

    - By 1936, economic hardship and class tensions had eroded faith in democratic compromise, contributing directly to support for armed uprising.

- Government mismanagement of reforms and finances undermined economic stability.

    - Republican reforms in land, labour, education, and the military were seen as too moderate by the left and too radical by the right, satisfying neither.

    - Inconsistent implementation and internal divisions within the government created a perception of incompetence and increased frustration.

    - Widespread dissatisfaction with reform efforts led to declining confidence in the Republic, making it vulnerable to authoritarian alternatives.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One economic cause of the Spanish Civil War was agrarian inequality  ($\checkmark$). A significant portion of Spain's land was concentrated in the hands of a small elite, particularly in the south, where large estates (latifundios) left many peasants landless and in poverty. Attempts at land reform by the Second Republic (1931-1936) were slow and ineffective, increasing tensions between rural workers and wealthy landowners, leading to strikes and violence, which ultimately led to many landowners supporting Franco and the military in order to protect their interests ($\checkmark$).

Another economic cause was the economic instability caused by the Great Depression ($\checkmark$). Spain's already fragile economy suffered further decline, resulting in high unemployment and reduced wages for both industrial and agricultural workers. This deepened class divisions, with the working class supporting left-wing movements advocating for economic reform, while business elites and landowners backed right-wing groups resisting change. The economic crisis radicalised both sides, increasing polarisation and contributing to the outbreak of war in 1936 ($\checkmark$).
