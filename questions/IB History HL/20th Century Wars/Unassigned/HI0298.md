# HI0298

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for a historian studying the results of the Paris Peace Conference. 

[4 marks]

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Economic Consequences of the Peace” by John Maynard Keynes, 1920. The author was part of the British delegation to the Versailles Peace Conference. He believed the demands on Germany were too harsh so he resigned his government position and wrote this book explaining his reasons.

[Source: Keynes, J. M. (1920). *The Economic Consequences of the Peace*. Retrieved December 16 10, 2024 from https://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/keynes-the-economic-consequences-of-the-peace. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following: 

 - The source contains insight as it was made in 1920 by a man who participated in the Paris Peace Conference as part of the British delegation (origin).

 - The source wants to explain the economic consequences of the Paris Peace Conference and why he thinks the treaty's impacts will be negative (purpose).

 - The source describes the real motivations of the negotiators and the reasons why the author thinks the results of the Paris Peace Conference are negative (content).

For limitations, any of the following:

 - The source lacks hindsight as it was made in 1920 and does not know the actual consequences of the peace treaties (origin).

 - The source seeks to defend his position against the results of the Paris Peace Conference (purpose)

 - The source does not provide the perspective of other people involved in the negotiations. It also contains some exaggerations such as when he refers to Clemenceau's $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Carthaginian Peace” (content).

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Because Source C was created in 1920 by a man who was part of the British delegation at the Paris Peace Conference, it provides first hand insight into what happened at the conference ($\checkmark$ VO). The purpose of the source is also valuable since Keynes seeks to explain the economic consequences of the Paris Peace Conference and give his view on why he thinks the impacts of the Treaty will be negative ($\checkmark$ VP). From the source we can learn what the author saw as the real motivations of the negotiators and this makes the content valuable ($\checkmark$ VC). 

However, the source also has limitations for a historian studying the results of the Paris Peace Conference. Regarding the origin, the source lacks hindsight as it was made in 1920 when the consequences of the peace treaties were still unknown ($\checkmark$ LO). Moreover, the author wants to defend his position against the results of the Paris Peace Conference and justify his having resigned as a member of the British delegation therefore, he says only critical things about the Conference ($\checkmark$ LP). This also makes the content limited since it makes the author use some exaggerations to prove his point, like when he refers to Clemenceau´s  “Carthaginian Peace” ($\checkmark$ LC).
