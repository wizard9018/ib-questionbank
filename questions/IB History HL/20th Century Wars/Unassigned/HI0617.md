# HI0617

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for a historian studying the impact of the Balkan Wars on the European powers. [4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An excerpt from a 1914 journal article titled “The Effect of the Balkan Wars on European Alliance and the Future of the Ottoman Empire” by Norman Dwight Harris, a professor of European History at Northwestern University. |

[Source: Harris, N. D. (1914). *The Effect of the Balkan Wars on European Alliances and the Future of the Ottoman Empire.* The American Political Science Review, 8(1), 105-116. Retrieved March 11, 2025 from http://www.jstor.org/stable/4617011. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:

- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For **value**, any of the following:

- The source is secondary as it was written by an American professor who was removed from the wars and was written within a few years of the wars.

- The source connects the growing militarism in Europe to the wars in the Balkans, specifically connecting it to the need for a strong Austro-Hungarian military to respond to Russian and Pro-Slavic militaries near their Southern borders.

- The purpose of the source is to highlight how European powers responded to the wars in the Balkans. 

For **limitations**, any of the following:

- While the source is secondary, it was written before World War I broke out and cannot analyze how these events led to the war.

- The source briefly mentions the need for the Ottoman Empire but does not go into detail on how the Balkan Wars strengthened or weakened the empire.

- The source refers to a “status quo” in the Mediterranean but does not go into detail about what that means.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source C is valuable to a historian as a secondary source that informs the reader about how the European powers were affected by the Balkan Wars ($\checkmark$ VO). However, the source was written in 1914 and is limited in how deeply the effects were analyzed as they could not consider World War I nor its aftermath ($\checkmark$ LO). The source is valuable for making connections between the problems in the Balkans and the growing militarism in Europe ($\checkmark$ VP) but is limited in its treatment of the Ottoman Empire. The document mentions that the Ottoman Empire was struggling but does not detail how the Balkan Wars affected the stability of the empire ($\checkmark$ LC).
