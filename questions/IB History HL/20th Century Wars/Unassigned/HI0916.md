# HI0916

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for a historian studying South Korean attitudes to the armistice of 1953.
[4 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An excerpt from a letter from the President of the Republic of Korea, Syngman Rhee, to US President Eisenhower with a “top secret” classification. May 30, 1953. |

[Source: Rhee, S. (1953, May 30). *The President of the Republic of Korea (Rhee) to President Eisenhower.* Office of the Historian. Retrieved June 25, 2025, from https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p1/d565. Public Domain.]

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For **value**, any of the following:\
\
Origin
- Written by Syngman Rhee, President of South Korea, on May 30, 1953, addressed to U.S. President Dwight D. Eisenhower. The Source offers a direct insight into the official South Korean leadership's position on the armistice.
- Since it is addressed to Eisenhower and not intended for public consumption, it likely reflects Rhee's true diplomatic concerns and political strategy, rather than propaganda.
- Demonstrates the tensions between South Korea and the U.S., even as allies, highlighting differing priorities regarding peace and reunification.

Purpose
- Intended to convince or pressure the United States to reconsider or adjust its approach to armistice negotiations. Reveals South Korea's diplomatic posture and negotiation strategy, especially Rhee's strong emphasis on reunification and anti-communism.
- Aimed to assert South Korea's sovereignty and raise objections to any peace that would leave the North Korean regime and Chinese troops intact. Highlights the political stakes and frustrations felt by the South Korean leadership at being sidelined in decisions affecting Korean sovereignty.

Content
- Rich in detail about the official South Korean narrative of the war and its aftermath.
- Shows the ideological dimension of South Korean leadership, strong nationalism, anti-communism, and fear of long-term division.
- Useful for understanding the reasons behind Rhee's resistance to the armistice, including his willingness to act unilaterally (e.g., releasing POWs).

For **limitations**, any of the following:\
\
Origin:
- Reflects Rhee's personal views, not necessarily representative of the broader South Korean population or all political groups.
- As a diplomatic message, it may still be somewhat calculated to influence American policy, rather than purely expressing national sentiment.
- Limited in scope; it does not reveal how ordinary South Koreans, opposition politicians, or civil society viewed the armistice.

Purpose
- The persuasive tone of the letter means some arguments may be exaggerated to achieve diplomatic aims.
- The purpose may have influenced the way Rhee frames the situation, possibly downplaying compromises South Korea might have been willing to accept privately.

Content
- Does not provide statistical data, social context, or public opinion, limiting its usefulness for understanding broader societal views.
- Focused mainly on high-level diplomatic concerns, leaving out discussion of how the armistice might impact ordinary citizens.
- Offers a one-sided perspective, there's no engagement with counter arguments or internal dissent.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
As a top-secret letter from South Korea's president to his U.S. counterpart, the origin increases its value. It was not intended for public release, which likely makes it more candid and reflective of Rhee's true diplomatic stance. This gives historians rare access to his personal reasoning and frustrations, especially his strong rejection of an armistice ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). However, the origin also has limitations as it represents only Rhee's views, not those of South Korean citizens or opposition voices ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).

The purpose of the letter was to persuade the United States to change its approach to the armistice, pushing for stronger action against communism and reunification of Korea. This has value as it clearly demonstrates South Korea's frustration at being sidelined in key negotiations. For historians, this purpose helps explain diplomatic tensions between allies and shows how deeply South Korea valued sovereignty and reunification ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).

In terms of content, Rhee criticises the proposed ceasefire as a form of surrender, arguing it would be “a death sentence without protest.” He stresses the importance of punishing aggression, unifying the country, and protecting the security of free nations. This is valuable for understanding the ideological basis of Rhee's policies, including his fierce anti-communism and nationalism ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).
