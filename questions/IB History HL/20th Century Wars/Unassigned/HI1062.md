# HI1062

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, assess the value and limitations of Source A for a historian studying the annexation of the Sudetenland in 1938.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from the diary of Wehrmacht soldier Herbert Wetzig, October 10, 1938. Wetzig describes the takeover of the Sudetenland in Czechoslovakia. |

[Source: Wetzig, H. (1938, October 10) *Soldier's Diary Entries on the Takeover of the Sudetenland (1938)*, published in German History in Documents and Images. Retrieved 21 October 2025 from https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/soldiers-diary-entries-on-the-takeover-of-the-sudetenland-1938 Public Domain]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:

- Origin:
    - Written by a German soldier (Herbert Wetzig) in October 1938, it provides immediate insight into the annexation.
    - A primary source created during the event, offering direct observation and lived experience.
- Purpose:
    - Intended as a private diary, not propaganda, which may increase its reliability.
    - Reflects personal emotions and experiences, giving historians a sense of how soldiers perceived the annexation.
- Content:
    - Describes cheering crowds, people offering gifts, and a celebratory atmosphere, which shows popular support among Sudeten Germans.
    - Emphasizes unity and emotional connection between German troops and local populations (“brothers and sisters”), revealing how the annexation was framed.

For limitations, any combination of the following:

- Origin:
    - The author was a soldier in the Nazi military, likely influenced by Nazi ideology or expectations.
    - Only reflects the German perspective; it excludes the views of Czech citizens or Sudeten Germans opposed to the annexation.
- Purpose:
    - Although private, the diary may reflect internalized propaganda or selective memory shaped by nationalist thinking.
    - The emotional tone may exaggerate or romanticize events, reducing objectivity.
- Content:
    - Omits any signs of resistance, fear, or opposition and presents only one, celebratory narrative.
    - Lacks a broader political or strategic context; focuses on feelings rather than factual analysis of the annexation's causes or consequences.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is valuable because it is a primary account written by a German soldier who participated in the 1938 annexation of the Sudetenland, giving historians direct insight into the perceptions of those involved at the time  ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$).  Since it is a private diary, not an official propaganda piece, it is more likely to express genuine emotions and personal views, which may make it more reliable in reflecting the soldier's true experience ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The content vividly describes cheering crowds, people giving gifts to soldiers, and a sense of emotional unity between the German army and the local population. This shows how at least some Sudeten Germans reacted positively to the annexation and felt a connection to Germany ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

However, the source is limited because it only presents the perspective of a German soldier and does not include any views from Czech citizens or Sudeten Germans who may have opposed the annexation ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). Even though it is a diary, the author may have internalized Nazi propaganda or held nationalistic views, which could distort how he interpreted local reactions ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). The content only describes enthusiastic support and does not mention fear, resistance, or the political context of the annexation, which limits its usefulness in understanding the full picture of how the event was experienced by all communities involved ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
