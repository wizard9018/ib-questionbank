# HI0192

According to Source A, what were the United States' goals in Asia in 1949?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from a US National Security Council document NSC 48/2 entitled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Position of the United States With Respect to Asia.” Written in 1949.

[Source: National Security Council. (December 23, 1949). *NSC 48/2 - The Position of the United States with Respect to Asia*. Office of the US Historian. Retrieved December 3, 2024 from https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1949v07p2/d387. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - The U.S. aimed to help Asian nations develop into stable, self-sustaining states aligned with the United Nations Charter.

 - The U.S. sought to develop sufficient military power in non-Communist Asian nations like South Korea to prevent Communist expansion.

 - A key goal was to eliminate or significantly reduce Soviet power in Asia, ensuring the USSR could not threaten regional peace or U.S. security.

 - The U.S. planned to provide military, economic, and political aid to strategic areas, particularly South Korea, to bolster non-Communist regimes.

 - The U.S. committed to resisting Communist encroachment by strengthening the forces of freedom and maintaining the independence of allied nations.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

According to Source A, the United States pursued multiple objectives in Asia in 1949. One key goal was to support the creation of stable, independent nations that aligned with the principles of the United Nations Charter ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the U.S. aimed to strengthen the military capabilities of non-Communist countries, such as South Korea, to act as a barrier against Communist expansion ($\checkmark$). Another major objective was to curb Soviet influence in the region, preventing the USSR from disrupting security and stability in Asia ($\checkmark$).
