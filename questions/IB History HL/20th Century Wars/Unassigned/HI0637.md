# HI0637

Describe **three** reasons the hostilities between the CCP and KMT recommenced in 1946.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Deep-rooted ideological conflict between the CCP's communism and the KMT's nationalism made long-term cooperation impossible.
- With Japan defeated in 1945, the shared threat that had united the CCP and KMT during the Second United Front was gone, reigniting hostilites.
- Both parties sought to control post-war China, each claiming to represent the true government, leading to competition for power.
- US-mediated negotiations (e.g. Marshall Mission) failed due to mutual distrust and incompatible political goals.
- Skirmishes broke out in contested regions like Manchuria, where both sides rushed to occupy former Japanese territory.
- The U.S. supported the KMT with weapons and aid, while the Soviets allowed CCP access to Japanese arms in Manchuria, escalating tensions.
- Agreements made during the war, including political cooperation and troop withdrawal, were frequently violated by both sides.
- The KMT's declining popularity and internal issues made the CCP more confident in pursuing its goal of controlling China.
- The CCP had grown in numbers and support during the war, giving greater cofidence them to challenge the KMT militarily.
- After Japan's surrender there was a political vacuum in previously occupied rigions, the rush to control these areas led to direct military confrontations between CCP and KMT forces.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Hostilities between the CCP and KMT recommenced in 1946 due to entrenched ideological differences, with the CCP promoting communism and the KMT defending Chinese nationalism, making long-term cooperation unlikely. Attempts at peaceful resolution, such as the US-led Marshall Mission, failed due to mutual distrust and ideologically incompatible political goals. ($\checkmark$) Secondly, the defeat of Japan in 1945 removed the common enemy that had temporarily united them under the Second United Front, reigniting their antagonism. ($\checkmark$) Finally, the KMT's internal corruption, declining public support, and poor governance further weakened its position, giving Mao and the CCP greater confidence in pushing for control of China. ($\checkmark$)
