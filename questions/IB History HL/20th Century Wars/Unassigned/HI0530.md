# HI0530

Discuss the significance of foreign aid in the Spanish Civil War.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **four** of the following arguments/examples of foreign aid:

 - Italy and Germany sent a substantial number of troops,weapons, and money to assist the Nationalists, which led to effective bombings of Republican-controlled cities.

 - Portugal supported Franco and allowed open borders which aided the nationalists when they needed new supplies.

 - Stalin gave financial support and some weapons to the Republic. However, Stalin used this support to gain power among the Spanish communists and did not communicate well with the Republican leadership, making this aid less effective.

 - The International Brigade, a group of volunteer soldiers, fought for the Republicans but were hampered by communication issues and a lack of strong leadership.

 - France and Britain were determined not to intervene in Spain. Their influence was enough to ensure that the Republicans did not receive financial support from nations that otherwise may have supported their effort.  However, they were unable to deter Germany and Italy from intervening on behalf of the Nationalists.

\

[4 marks]

Concluding remark:

 - Responses should consider the role of foreign aid from various countries and in support of different sides of the conflict.

 - The strongest responses are likely to conclude that some foreign aid played a more important role in the conflict and/or that particular sides of the conflict benefited more from foreign aid.

 \

[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Foreign aid had a positive impact on the Nationalists and a negative impact on the Republicans during the Spanish Civil War. The Nationalists received financial and military aid from Italy and Germany. The military aid came in the form of organized troops and new advanced weaponry. This allowed the Nationalists to lead several successful campaigns, including the bombing of Guernica ($\checkmark$). While Portugal did not offer soldiers, they were sympathetic to Franco’s cause and allowed the Nationalists to cross their borders. This was helpful to the Nationalists in that it allowed them to resupply when needed ($\checkmark$). The Republicans did receive help from international allies, like the Soviet Union. However, this help was not as substantial. Stalin used his help to try to gain control over the Spanish communists, causing distrust with Republican leadership ($\checkmark$). Even more detrimental to the Republicans was the aid they did not receive. France and Great Britain were determined not to get involved in the Spanish Civil War and stopped most of the support that the Republicans could have received from outside sources, such as the International Brigade. However, because Italy and Germany ignored the League of Nations' Non-Intervention Committee, the Nationalists still received aid ($\checkmark$). Foreign aid from Germany and Italy played a significant role in helping the Nationalists, while foreign aid for the Republicans did little to support their efforts ($\checkmark$ conclusion).
