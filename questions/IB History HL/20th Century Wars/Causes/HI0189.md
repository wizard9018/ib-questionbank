# HI0189

Explain **two** factors that led to the escalation of the Vietnam War.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- The belief in the domino theory suggested that if one country in Southeast Asia fell to communism, others in the region would follow. This perspective convinced U.S. policymakers that Vietnam was critical to stopping the spread of communism, leading to increased American military and financial support to South Vietnam to resist the communist North.
- In 1964, U.S. naval vessels were allegedly attacked by North Vietnamese forces in the Gulf of Tonkin. This incident led to the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, which granted President Johnson the authority to escalate military involvement in Vietnam, resulting in the deployment of large numbers of U.S. troops and airstrikes.
- The Geneva Accords divided Vietnam into North and South at the 17th parallel and called for elections to reunify the country. These elections were never held, as the U.S. and South Vietnam feared a communist victory. This failure deepened tensions and led to a protracted conflict between the communist North and U.S.-backed South.North Vietnam received significant military and logistical aid from the Soviet Union and China, allowing it to sustain prolonged resistance against South Vietnam and the United States. This external support encouraged the U.S. to escalate its military operations to counter the influence of global communism.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One factor that escalated the Vietnam War was the domino theory, which suggested that if one country in Southeast Asia fell to communism, neighbouring nations would follow ($\checkmark$). This belief drove the United States to intensify its involvement to prevent North Vietnam's communist regime from overtaking South Vietnam. The theory heightened Cold War tensions and justified increased military action, including troop deployments and airstrikes, to contain communism in the region ($\checkmark$).

Another factor was the Gulf of Tonkin Incident in 1964, where a supposed attack on U.S. naval vessels by North Vietnamese forces led to the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution. This gave President Lyndon B. Johnson the authority to escalate U.S. military operations in Vietnam without a formal declaration of war ($\checkmark$). The resolution marked the beginning of full-scale American intervention, dramatically increasing troop numbers and intensifying the conflict ($\checkmark$).
