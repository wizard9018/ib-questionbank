# HI0191

Explain **two** economic causes of the Spanish Civil War.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Land inequality: The concentration of land ownership in the hands of a small elite left rural peasants impoverished and without opportunities for economic advancement. This created widespread discontent, particularly in southern Spain, where landless labourers faced poor living conditions and were drawn to leftist ideologies advocating for land reform.
- High unemployment: Economic hardships during the Great Depression worsened already high unemployment rates, particularly among the urban working class. This led to growing support for socialist and anarchist movements, which clashed with the conservative government and landowning elite, further polarising Spanish society.
- Economic divide between rural and urban areas: The economic divide between rural and urban areas in Spain created significant social tensions. Rural populations remained trapped in poverty due to reliance on traditional agriculture and lack of investment, while urban areas experienced limited industrialization that benefited a small working class. This divide deepened resentment among rural communities, who felt excluded from economic progress, and urban workers, who demanded better wages and conditions.
- Economic polarisation between regions: Wealthier regions like Catalonia and the Basque Country, which were more industrialised, resented the central government for syphoning resources without providing adequate autonomy. This regional economic disparity fueled separatist sentiments, adding to the divisions that contributed to the war.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One economic cause of the Spanish Civil War was the extreme inequality in land ownership. In southern Spain, much of the land was controlled by wealthy elites, leaving peasants landless and in poverty. This inequality created significant unrest among rural workers, many of whom turned to left-wing movements that promised land reform and social justice ($\checkmark$). Another cause was Spain's slow industrial development, which created stark economic disparities between rural and urban areas. Workers in industrialised regions demanded better wages and conditions, while rural populations felt neglected, increasing tensions between different social groups and regions ($\checkmark$).
