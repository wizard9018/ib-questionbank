# HI0190

Describe **two** factors that contributed to the outbreak of the Falklands War in 1982.
[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Argentina's long-standing territorial claim over the Falkland Islands (known as Islas Malvinas in Argentina), which it argued had been illegitimately occupied by Britain since the early 19th century, created a historical grievance.
- Economic and political instability in Argentina under the military junta. Facing widespread unrest and a declining economy, the junta sought to rally national support and distract from domestic issues by reclaiming the Falklands.
- Britain's reduced military presence in the South Atlantic. The withdrawal of the HMS Endurance gave Argentina the impression that Britain was not committed to defending the islands, encouraging the decision to invade.
- The failure of diplomatic negotiations over sovereignty. Years of inconclusive talks between Britain and Argentina exacerbated frustrations and pushed Argentina to take military action.
- Nationalism in Argentina. The junta fueled nationalist fervour, portraying the Falklands as a matter of national pride, which pressured leaders to act decisively.
- Miscalculation by Argentina. The Argentine government believed that Britain would not respond militarily to an invasion and assumed a diplomatic resolution could be achieved after taking the islands.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One factor that contributed to the outbreak of the Falklands War was Argentina's historical claim to the Falkland Islands, which it referred to as the Islas Malvinas. The Argentine government believed that the islands had been unjustly taken by Britain in the 19th century, and this territorial dispute remained a source of tension for decades ($\checkmark$). Another factor was the political instability in Argentina during the rule of the military junta, which faced widespread public discontent due to a struggling economy and human rights abuses. The junta sought to use the reclamation of the islands as a way to divert attention from domestic issues and bolster nationalist sentiment among the population ($\checkmark$).
