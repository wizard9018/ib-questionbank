# HI0188

Describe **two** ideological differences between the Nationalists and Communists that contributed to the Chinese Civil War.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with detailed description:
- Political Structure and Ideology: The Nationalists (Guomindang) advocated for a centralized government with a focus on national unity and stability through a capitalist framework. They sought to modernize China based on nationalist principles and opposed radical social reforms. In contrast, the Communists envisioned a revolutionary transformation of society, aiming for a classless, socialist state based on Marxist-Leninist ideology, with an emphasis on equality and anti-imperialism.
- Social Support Base: The Nationalists relied heavily on the support of urban elites, including landowners, industrialists, and intellectuals who benefited from capitalist development. Conversely, the Communists drew their base from rural peasants and the working class, promising land reforms and social equality, which attracted widespread support among those disenfranchised by the existing social order.
- Foreign Influence and Alliances: The Nationalists sought to establish China as an independent, unified nation without direct alignment with foreign powers, resisting Soviet influence in particular. By contrast, the Communists aligned with the Soviet Union, adopting aspects of Soviet-style governance and economic policy, which included receiving material and ideological support from the USSR.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Nationalists promoted a centralized government based on capitalist ideals, appealing to urban elites and landowners who supported a stable, hierarchical society ($\checkmark$). In contrast, the Communists pursued a socialist, classless society grounded in Marxist-Leninist ideology, attracting support from peasants and the working class who desired radical social change ($\checkmark$). Additionally, while the Nationalists sought to minimize foreign influence and maintain independence ($\checkmark$), the Communists aligned closely with the Soviet Union, gaining both ideological inspiration and material support, which strengthened their revolutionary goals ($\checkmark$).
