# HI0187

Define the term arms $\lq$race' and explain how the arms race contributed to the tensions leading up to the First World War.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Definition for **one** mark:
- A competition between nations to build up military power, often involving rapid advancements in weaponry and technology, with the goal of achieving superiority over rivals.
\
[1 marks]

Any **two** of the following explanations for **one** mark each
- The arms race heightened fear and mistrust between nations, particularly as Germany and Britain each sought to outmatch the other's naval and military capabilities. This growing hostility fostered a climate in which diplomacy was weakened and alliances were hardened, increasing the risk of war.
- It contributed to a sense of inevitability of conflict, as the unprecedented military buildup across Europe created an atmosphere where war seemed not just possible, but unavoidable. The sheer scale of military resources and preparedness made peaceful resolutions appear ineffective, as nations increasingly prioritized military solutions over diplomacy. This perception encouraged governments to adopt more aggressive foreign policies and defensive alliances, as they believed war was imminent and that being unprepared would leave them vulnerable to attack. Ultimately, the arms race transformed Europe into a heavily militarized and polarized continent, where even minor crises could escalate into full-scale conflict.
- The arms race accelerated alliances and deepened divisions, as countries felt pressured to align with others for security. This alliance system created a “domino effect,” where conflict between two countries could easily draw in their allies, turning a local dispute into a broader war as countries sought to uphold the obligations of their alliances.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
An arms race is a competition where countries increase their military strength to surpass potential adversaries ($\checkmark$). Leading up to World War I, the arms race fueled distrust between nations, especially between Germany and Britain, who viewed each other as escalating threats due to their naval buildup ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the massive investment in militaries made war feel inevitable, and nations adopted aggressive policies to protect their interests, which further destabilized Europe ($\checkmark$).
