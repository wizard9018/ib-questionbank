# HI0613

Describe Germany's Schlieffen Plan and explain why it failed.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

The following or similar description of the Schlieffen Plan:  

 - The Schlieffen Plan was Germany's plan for fighting a two front war against Russia and France. It was developed by the German military in 1905. The plan was dependent on invading France first, conquering them, and then moving across Germany to fight Russia in the east.

\

[1 marks]

Any **three** of the following:

 - The plan called for invading France through Belgium, a neutral nation. By invading Belgium, Germany violated the Treaty of London which guaranteed Belgian neutrality and drew the British into the war.

 - While the Belgians ultimately could not keep the Germans out of France, they did fight back harder than Germany expected. This delayed their advance, helped diminish their resources, and allowed the Russias to mobilize quicker.

 - Russia was able to mobilize much quicker than the Schlieffen Plan accounted for. Because of this, the Germans were forced to split their troops sooner than expected.

 - The plan was outdated. It did not accurately consider new forms of technology and the updated Russian infrastructure that allowed them quicker mobilization.

 - Revisions made to the plan between 1905 and 1914 diluted its effectiveness by sending fewer troops to the initial attacks in Belgium and France.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Schlieffen plan was designed by the German military in 1905 in case they had to fight a two front war against France and Russia. The plan called for the Germans using most of their forces to defeat France before Russia could mobilize ($\checkmark$). There were several issues with the plan in practice. For one, by invading Belgium, they broke a neutrality agreement with Great Britain. This is the action that brought Great Britain into the war ($\checkmark$). In addition, they underestimated the amount of time they would need to get through Belgium and conquer France. The Belgians fought harder than they were expecting and by the time they reached France, they had fully mobilized and stopped the German progression ($\checkmark$). Finally, the Russian military was able to mobilize much more quickly than the Germans expected. This forced the Germans to split their troops sooner, resulting in the two front war they were hoping to avoid ($\checkmark$).
