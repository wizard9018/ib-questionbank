# HI0643

Evaluate the reasons for the adoption of guerrilla warfare and its significance in the outcome of **one** 20th-century war.
[15 marks]

Candidates must demonstrate a clear understanding of the question's requirements. In this case, candidates should evaluate the reasons why guerilla warfare was adopted and assess its significance for the war's outcome.

A high-scoring response should:
- Clearly define guerrilla warfare and explain why it was adopted in the selected war.
- Evaluate the reasons for its adoption, considering military, political, and/or geographical factors.
- Assess its significance in the war's outcome (whether it led to victory, prolonged resistance, or ultimately led to defeat).
- Use a well-structured argument with relevant historical examples and differing perspectives integrated into the answer.

Possible suitable examples include:
- **Chinese Civil War (1927-1949)**: The CCP lacked conventional military strength compared to the KMT; guerrilla warfare allowed them to survive, mobilize peasant support, and counter superior KMT forces. Guerrilla tactics secured rural support and facilitated the CCP's transition to conventional warfare, culminating in victory by 1949.
- **Vietnam War (1955-1975)**: The Viet Cong (NLF) lacked resources compared to U.S. and South Vietnamese forces; dense jungle terrain favoured guerrilla tactics. Guerrilla warfare weakened U.S. resolve, prolonged the war, and ultimately played a very significant role in American withdrawal and Communist victory in 1975.
- **Cuban Revolution (1956-1959)**: Castro's forces were outnumbered by Batista's army; mountainous terrain allowed for guerrilla warfare and small-scale ambushes. Guerrilla tactics, combined with propaganda and peasant support, undermined Batista's rule, leading to the revolution's success.
- **Algerian War of Independence (1954-1962)**: The FLN (National Liberation Front) lacked conventional military strength; urban and rural guerrilla warfare countered superior French forces. Guerrilla resistance, coupled with international pressure, led to French withdrawal and Algerian independence in 1962.
- **Afghan-Soviet War (1979-1989)**: The Mujahideen lacked advanced weaponry against Soviet forces; Afghanistan's rugged terrain was ideal for guerrilla tactics. Guerrilla resistance, supported by U.S. funding and arms, prolonged the war and contributed to the Soviet withdrawal in 1989.
- **Spanish Civil War (1936-1939)**: Republican forces, especially in rural areas, used guerrilla tactics against Franco's better-equipped Nationalists. Guerrilla warfare helped prolong resistance, but ultimately, lack of external support and military disadvantages led to Republican defeat.
- **World War II in Europe (1939-1945)**: Resistance movements across Nazi-occupied Europe, such as the French Resistance, Yugoslav Partisans, and Soviet Partisans, lacked conventional military strength against the German army. Guerrilla warfare was necessary to disrupt Axis supply lines, sabotage infrastructure, and gather intelligence. Partisan warfare significantly weakened Nazi control in occupied territories, diverted German military resources, and supported Allied advances. In Yugoslavia, Tito's Partisans played a key role in liberating the country without direct Allied military intervention. In the Soviet Union, partisan warfare hindered German advances, particularly during the Battle of Stalingrad.

The above material is an indication of what candidates may elect to write about in their responses; however, it is neither prescriptive nor exhaustive and no set answer is required. Credit should be awarded for other possible examples of guerrilla warfare when relevant and used appropriately to answer the question.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Chinese Civil War (CCW) (1927-1949) was fought between the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the Kuomintang (KMT). While conventional military strategies played a decisive role in the final stages of CCP victory, guerrilla warfare proved crucial in shifting the balance of power towards the CCP ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). Through guerrilla warfare, the CCP could at first survive KMT onslaughts and eventually thrive by mobilizing the rural populace to create a strategic advantage. Nevertheless, the Sino-Japanese War (1937-1945), Second United Front, and KMT mismanagement were also vital in the outcome of the war, as they alleviated pressure on the communists, allowing them to consolidate their power in key areas ($\checkmark\, \text{organization}$).

Guerrilla warfare is an unconventional form of warfare reliant on fast-moving mobile forces, ambush tactics, and the support of the local populace ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). Drawing on his study of historical Chinese and Marxist-Leninist theories, Mao adapted these principles to suit Chinese conditions. He particularly emphasized the mobilization of the peasantry. Mao famously stated, "The enemy advances, we retreat; the enemy camps, we harass; the enemy tires, we attack" ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$).

The adoption of guerrilla warfare by the CCP was a crucial strategic response to military pressure exerted by Chiang Kai-shek and played a decisive role in the party's survival and eventual success ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). The CCP first adopted guerrilla warfare on a large scale during the Jiangxi Soviet (1931-1934). This followed Chiang Kai-shek's brutal suppression of suspected communists from 1927 onwards. ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$) Chiang's campaigns highlighted the weaknesses of the CCP in urban areas and forced their strategic shift towards rural strongholds ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Initially, in Jiangxi, the CCP successfully employed ambushes, sabotage, and large-scale peasant mobilization ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). However, following a tactical shift by Chiang and a power shift away from Mao in the Jiangxi Soviet, guerrilla warfare was briefly abandoned. This led to devastating defeats for the CCP, culminating in the Long March (1934-35). During the march, Mao regained his influence over the party and re-established the primacy of guerrilla warfare ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). This change in tactics allowed the CCP to survive and establish a new soviet in Yan'an ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$).

Yan'an became a key military and ideological training center for the CCP; however, it is unlikely it would have lasted without the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937-1945) ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). The war diverted KMT attention, allowing the CCP to expand its rural influence without interference ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). The Sino-Japanese War was to be hugely beneficial for the CCP. Through guerrilla warfare, the CCP avoided large-scale battles, maintained and grew their forces, and portrayed themselves as national defenders ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). This strengthened their appeal to the rural population ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). Through successful propaganda campaigns and popular policies, they gained widespread support from much of the Chinese peasantry (about 90% of the population) ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). In contrast, the KMT focused on traditional methods of warfare, leading to huge drains on their resources. By the end of the war, the CCP's numbers swelled to over a million, while KMT forces declined to around 3 million ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$).

The CCP's strategic use of guerrilla warfare laid the foundation for the transition to large-scale conventional offensives, which proved critical in securing victory. With the end of the Sino-Japanese War and the full resumption of civil war, the KMT continued its reliance on conventional warfare. This proved highly ineffective in rural China, where the CCP had garnered peasant support ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Serious logistical and communication issues further overstretched the KMT ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). As a result of the CCP's growth and confidence, by 1946, Mao shifted towards more conventional large-scale offensives. Guerrilla warfare was still used in areas of CCP weakness, but increasingly, the CCP launched coordinated troop movements, strategic encirclements, and full-scale battles against major KMT strongholds ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). This turned the KMT stronghold strategy against them using siege tactics ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). By 1948, with over 4 million troops, the CCP won victories in the Liaoshen, Huaihai, and Pingjin Campaigns, leading to final victory in 1949 ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$).

Historians such as Rana Mitter argue that rather than guerrilla warfare, KMT internal weaknesses, corruption, and mismanagement were more important in the war's outcome. However, this underestimates the importance of guerrilla warfare in CCP success. Guerrilla warfare enabled CCP survival, allowing them to transition to conventional offensives. Without this, the CCP would not have built its military strength or secured rural support ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

Guerrilla warfare was crucial to CCP victory, as it neutralized KMT firepower and secured popular support. However, it was not the sole factor. The Sino-Japanese War, the Second United Front, and KMT mismanagement also played a significant role. Nevertheless, guerrilla warfare laid the foundation for CCP survival, allowing later conventional offensives to secure final victory ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
