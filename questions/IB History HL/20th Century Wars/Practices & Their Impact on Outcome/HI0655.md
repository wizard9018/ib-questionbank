# HI0655

How, according to Source B, did Russian public opinion lead to them leaving World War I?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An excerpt from “About Russia, Its Revolutions, Its Development, and Its Present,” written by Michal Reiman, a Czech historian, in 2016. |

[Source: Reiman, M. (2016). *About Russia, Its Revolutions, Its Development and Its Present* (pp. 13-24). Peter Lang AG. Retrieved March 25, 2025 from  http://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv2t4dn7.4. Open Access.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The Bolsheviks received financial support from Germany because the Bolsheviks did not want to continue the war. When the Bolsheviks won, it was natural they sought peace with Germany.
    - Evidence: “Even liberal and left-wing historiography that was not outright Bolshevik, and that emphasized the spontaneity of the revolutionary outburst itself, attributed its subsequent radicalization to Bolshevik actions. Some pointed to the Germans and ‘German’ money supplied to the Bolsheviks.”
- Food shortages made the war unpopular so the people supported the side that was more likely to leave the war.
    - Evidence: “As noted, government policy gave rise to the grudges of workers and soldiers that served as the flashpoint for the revolution; so did the despotic attitude of the owners of Putilov, St Petersburg’s largest machine works, and the shortage of bread in the stores of the city’s working-class districts."
- The public supported leaving the war because they did not see the Russians gaining anything even if there was an Allied victory.
    - Evidence: “In addition to the aforementioned causes, the unsuccessful conduct of the war had sapped the country's capacity. By 1917, a major segment of Russian society knew that Russia had already lost the war, and that even a potential victory by the Allies would not bring the promised profit nor see Russia restored to its pre-war geographic extent.”
- The Allies only wanted to accept unconditional defeat and rejected peace negotiations. This convinced the Russians that their best way out was an armistice.
    - Evidence: “Russians became convinced that only the rapid conclusion of an armistice and peace would save the country from catastrophe. This gives some insight into why the Russian provisional government resisted separate peace negotiations with Germany. The Allies rejected peace with Germany and the Triple Alliance on any basis other than the latter's defeat.”
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source B suggests that most Russians and especially the Russians who supported the Bolsheviks wanted to leave the war effort. The Bolsheviks and their supporters received financial aid from Germany, making it natural that they would look for a settlement with them following a victory in the revolution ($\checkmark$). Many workers and soldiers were frustrated by the food shortages at home and on the front lines, making them support an armistice ($\checkmark$). In addition, many Russians did not see the benefit in staying in the fight much longer, as they were not winning any land and they were unlikely to benefit from continuing in the war ($\checkmark$).
