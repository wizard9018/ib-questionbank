# HI0249

Describe **two** technological developments in weaponry and their impact on the outcome of WWI.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following mentioning both description and impact:

**Poison gas**: gas used in the First World War including tearing agents, asphyxiants, and blistering agents. Chlorine was the first common gas used by both sides. Mustard gas was the most common blistering agent. Impacts included:
- Death or debilitation of soldiers. Death might only come some days or even weeks after inhalation, but it removed soldiers from many battles indefinitely.
- Ability to clear trenches easily
- Slowed advances even for the side using the gas as they could not advance into it.
- Psychological impacts on soldiers
- Pushed the development of gas masks, making poison gas less effective.
- It did not help break the stalemate because it was dependent on the wind for distribution.

**Tanks**: A vehicle that could withstand machine gun fire and travel through the rough terrain of no man's land (the territory between enemy trenches) could be the perfect solution to the stalemate. The British first developed them and used them during the Battle of the Somme in 1916. The Germans produced only 20 tanks while the French, on the other hand, produced over 4,000 of various models and the British about 2,600. The impact was limited because:
- Since they could only travel 6km/hr and broke down frequently.
- The Allies used them mostly as shields for advancing soldiers but since they were so slow and broke down often, they slowed the general advance giving the enemy time to counter-áttack.
- Their armour plating was not strong enough to resist artillery.

**Submarines**: The submarine, or U-boat, was useful for the Germans since they did not have the numerical advantage of the British Royal Navy. The German Navy initially had about 30 U-boats which they used against the Royal Navy and against merchant ships that could supply the Allies. Impacts on the outcome of the war included:
 - “Unrestricted”  submarine warfare hurt the Allies` supplies for their armed forces and the home front. The German submarines sank over 15 million tonnes of shipping throughout the war and, at one point in 1917, reduced Britain to only six weeks’ reserve of food for the whole country.
- Unrestricted submarine warfare was diplomatically dangerous. Some of the ships that sank belonged to neutral countries. The sinking of the US ship Lusitania by torpedo attack and the loss of 1,000 lives, including 128 Americans, led to strong protests from the USA. This is one of the reasons the US ended up declaring war on the Central Powers.
- By 1918, sonar had been developed, and the French also used echo ranging, which allowed U-boat detection. In 1918, the Germans lost 69 U-boats which they were unable to replace.
\

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Poison gas was first used by the Germans in 1915. There were different types of poison gases used during the war. They could either be tearing agents, asphyxiants or blistering agents. The most common one used was chlorine ($\checkmark$). While poison gas did have a psychological impact and caused about 1 million casualties, since it depended on the wind to be carried it was not a reliable weapon and could often backfire if the wind blew in the direction of the army using the gas. Its effects were not very important for the outcome of the war ($\checkmark$).

Tanks were another technological development that, when first used, carried with them a lot of hope to break the stalemate of the war since the tank could, in theory, move easily through “no man's land” and invade the enemy trench without fear of bullets or barbed wire . They were first introduced by the British during the Battle of the Somme in 1916 ($\checkmark$). However, since they were very slow travelling at no more than 6 km/hr and broke down frequently, they gave the enemy plenty of time to prepare and even aim at the tanks which were not strong enough to resist artillery fire. At the Battle of the Somme, their only real impact was the panic they initially caused on the Germans ($\checkmark$).
