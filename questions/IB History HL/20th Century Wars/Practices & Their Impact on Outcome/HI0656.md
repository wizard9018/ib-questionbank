# HI0656

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source A for a historian studying the end of World War I.
[4 marks]

| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  A cartoon titled “Kerensky to the Rescue” by American political cartoonist Clifford Berryman in 1918. |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- This primary source was published in The Evening Star, a Washington, D.C. newspaper in 1917 during the war. The artist was Clifford Berryman, a well-known cartoonist from this time period.
- The cartoon's purpose is to inform The Evening Star readers about how the revolution in Russia was influencing the war effort. This is shown by the Bolsheviks pulling the bear labeled “Russia” away from the front.
- The cartoon is explicitly labeled to make the content clear for a historian studying the end of the war and gives a clear picture of how the Russian Revolution would impact World War I.

For limitations, any of the following:
- While the source is a primary source from a trusted publication, it was published in the United States and is naturally sympathetic to their allies.
- The cartoon's purpose may also be to persuade U.S. public opinion against the Bolsheviks by making a connection between them and the Germans.
- The cartoon only focuses on the Russian Revolution's impact on the end of the war while there are many other factors that will contribute to the armistice.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The origin of the source is valuable as it is a primary source published during the war in a reputable newspaper ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The purpose may be limited as it is from an American newspaper and is catering to the American perspective, which sides with the allies and does not consider other factors ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). The purpose of the cartoon is valuable to historians as it seeks to inform readers about how the revolution in Russia may affect the war in Europe, giving a historian a good idea of how the allies viewed this event ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). However, the content is limited as it only shows the possible impact of the Russian Revolution when there were many other factors that could have contributed to the end of the war ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
