# HI0907

Describe **three** examples of guerrilla tactics used by the Viet Minh against French forces during the French Indochina War (1946-1954).
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:

Nature of Guerrilla Warfare
- Avoidance of conventional battles: Viet Minh rarely engaged French forces in large-scale open combat unless the odds were favorable. Focused on wearing down the enemy over time.
- Small, mobile combat units: Operated in small, flexible groups that could strike quickly and retreat before French forces could respond.
- Hit-and-run attacks: Targeted French patrols, outposts, and convoys to cause continuous disruption and psychological stress.
- Ambushes and surprise raids: Took advantage of terrain and local knowledge to ambush isolated or vulnerable French units. Often occurred in remote or jungle-covered areas.
- Use of booby traps and mines: Simple yet effective traps slowed down French movement and demoralized troops.

Use of Terrain and Local Support
- Familiarity with the terrain: Viet Minh fighters used Vietnam's dense forests, mountains, and river systems to their advantage.
- Extensive tunnel networks and hidden supply routes: Enabled fighters to hide, store weapons, and launch attacks undetected.
- Blending in with local populations: Fighters wore no uniforms and relied on the support of rural villagers for shelter, supplies, and information. Made it difficult for the French to distinguish between combatants and civilians.
- Use of local militias ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$people's war"): Mobilized ordinary citizens to assist in guerrilla operations and create a climate of constant resistance.

Tactics of Attrition and Psychological Warfare
- Gradual weakening of French forces (war of attrition): Persistent guerrilla attacks slowly wore down French morale and resources. Forced the French to spread their troops thin across large rural areas.
- Psychological impact on French soldiers: Constant threat of ambush or booby traps led to fatigue and fear. Contributed to a growing sense that the war was unwinnable.
- Disruption of French supply lines and communication: Guerrilla fighters often attacked roads, bridges, and convoys to isolate French outposts and restrict movement

Evolution Over Time and Strategic Impact
- Gradual shift to more conventional tactics: By the early 1950s, especially under General Giap, Viet Minh forces began incorporating larger, more coordinated attacks (e.g., Dien Bien Phu), but guerrilla tactics remained foundational.
- Enabled long-term resistance: Guerrilla warfare allowed the Viet Minh to sustain their campaign over eight years despite being outgunned and out-financed.
- Forced the French to overextend: The French were drawn into remote areas where they were vulnerable and lacked local support.
- Helped pave the way for key victories: Constant pressure from guerrilla warfare contributed to weakening French positions leading up to the decisive Battle of Dien Bien Phu in 1954.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, the Viet Minh relied on guerrilla tactics that deliberately avoided large-scale battles unless they had a clear advantage. Instead, they operated in small, mobile units capable of launching hit-and-run attacks and surprise raids. These tactics kept French forces constantly off-balance, making it difficult for them to engage the enemy directly or maintain territorial control ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the Viet Minh exploited their terrain and local support. Familiarity with Vietnam's challenging geography, its forests, rivers, and mountains, allowed the Viet Minh to launch attacks and disappear quickly. They used simple tunnel systems and hidden supply routes to store arms and regroup in secret. By blending in with local villagers and relying on their support for supplies and intelligence, they made it nearly impossible for the French to distinguish between fighters and civilians ($\checkmark$).

Finally, the Viet Minh aimed to wear down French strength over time. Frequent attacks on French patrols, infrastructure, and supply routes strained resources and forced the French to spread their troops across vulnerable areas. The constant threat of ambushes and traps had a powerful psychological effect, lowering morale and contributing to growing doubts about the war's viability($\checkmark$).
