# HI0248

Explain **three** similarities in the impact of the airplane on the outcome of WWI and WWII.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any combination of at least **three** of the below with supporting details, examples and/or explanation:
- In both conflicts, new technology was developed which increased the importance of aircraft to the war's outcome.
    - WWI: interrupter gear, which allowed  machine guns to be fired forward through the propeller
    - WWII: Camera technology was greatly improved, making photo-reconnaissance aircraft more effective.
- In both conflicts, airplanes were used for surveillance
    -  WWI: airplanes were vital because they could both prevent enemy reconnaissance and gather information about the enemy.
    - WWII: Aircraft were used to identify troop movements and targets for bombing.
- Aircraft were used extensively by all main actors in both conflicts.
    - During WWI, this included the British Royal Flying Corps, the Imperial German Army Air Service, and the French Air Service, which many considered to be the world's first air force.
    - The Royal Air Force and the Luffwaffe being the most important in the European theater and the Imperial Japanese Army Air Force and the  United States Army Air Forces in the Pacific theatre during WWII.
- In both wars, strategic bombing was used against infrastructure.
    - In WWI, strategic bombing targeted railroads and, eventually, factories in the enemy’s rear from 1915.
    - During WWII on the European front, the allies used strategic bombing to force Germany to prioritize anti-aircraft strategy.  In the Pacific theater, strategic bombing, specifically incendiary bombs, was used during 1945 to devastate Japan’s urban areas which were often made of wood.
- In both wars, strategic bombing was used to terrorize civilians.
    - During WWI, the Germans heavily bombed southeastern England while the British responded by attacking German cities.
    - During WWII the Germans were responsible for the eight month bombing campaign against British urban centres known as “the Blitz.”
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
In both wars, technological innovations were necessary to make airplanes more useful for victory in war ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). In the case of WWI this is true of the interrupter gear, which allowed machine guns to be fired forward through the propeller, allowing aircraft to go beyond the task of reconnaissance. In the case of WWII, technological developments in the camera allowed airplanes to increase their preciseness in gathering information about the enemy ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).  This highlights one of the main uses of airplanes in both wars: gathering information about the enemy's position ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). A great example of this during WWI is when a French aviator spotted the German First Army wheeling in front of Paris - and not around Paris as the Schlieffen Plan had intended, giving the French army an opportunity for a counteroffensive. This caused the failure of the Schlieffen Plan and stopped the German advance on the Western Front in 1914. During WWII, the reconnaissance aircraft Straight Flush flew over Hiroshima to analyse weather conditions an hour before the Enola Gay dropped the atomic bomb on Hiroshima ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Both wars also used the airplane for strategic bombing to destroy enemy infrastructure and morale ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). During WWI, bombers like Germany's Gotha targeted railroads and factories, causing civilian casualties in cities such as London. However, due to technological limitations, the impact of such bombers was very limited. Gotha bombs caused only about 3,000 casualties in raids against London and southeast England ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Germany's Blitz over British industrial centers during WWII had the same objectives, and while the air raids failed to crush civilian morale or push the British toward peace negotiations, they were far deadlier ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
