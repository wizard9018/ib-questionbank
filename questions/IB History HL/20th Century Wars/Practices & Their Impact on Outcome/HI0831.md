# HI0831

To what extent did the goals outlined in Source C (NSDD-166) influence the outcome of the Soviet-Afghan War?
[4 marks]

$\\$

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

[Source: Reagan, R. (1985, March 27). *National Security Decision Directive 166: U.S. Policy Toward Afghanistan.* Ronald Reagan Presidential Library. Retrieved June 14, 2025 from https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/archives/reference/scanned-nsdds/nsdd166.pdf Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with a concluding sentence:

Goals that were largely achieved (high influence):
- Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan (1989): NSDD-166's ultimate goal was the removal of Soviet forces, which was achieved after sustained Mujahideen resistance and mounting costs for the USSR. U.S. covert support (weapons, funding, intelligence) played a critical role in weakening Soviet military efforts.
- Soviet military and political strain: The directive aimed to “demonstrate to the Soviet Union that its long-term strategy is not working”. Prolonged conflict and rising casualties demoralized Soviet troops and weakened domestic support. The war became known as the “Soviet Union’s Vietnam,” contributing to internal dissent and disillusionment with leadership.
- Support for indigenous resistance (Mujahideen): NSDD-166 emphasized preventing the defeat of the Afghan resistance, U.S. and Pakistani support helped the Mujahideen sustain a prolonged insurgency. Stinger missiles provided by the CIA in the mid-1980s significantly improved Mujahideen capacity to neutralize Soviet air superiority.
- Soviet international isolation: The directive aimed to “promote Soviet isolation in the Third and Islamic worlds”, many Muslim-majority countries condemned the occupation and supported the Afghan cause diplomatically and materially. The conflict damaged Soviet relationships in the Non-Aligned Movement and broader Islamic world.
- Denying Afghanistan as a Soviet base: The resistance and chaos meant Afghanistan never became a stable Soviet ally or base of operations.

Goals partially achieved or with mixed results:
- Demonstrating Soviet imperialism to the Third World: The U.S. succeeded in framing the USSR as an occupying force, shifting some countries' perspectives away from viewing the USSR as a natural ally. Still, some regimes continued to support or remain neutral toward the Soviets due to shared political or economic interests.

Goals not fully achieved or with long-term unintended consequences:
- Afghanistan's stability and independence: Although the Soviets withdrew, Afghanistan descended into civil war among Mujahideen factions. The rise of the Taliban and the harbouring of al-Qaeda in the 1990s were long-term outcomes contrary to U.S. interests.
- U.S. long-term control over outcomes: NSDD-166 focused on defeating the USSR, but lacked a coherent post-withdrawal plan; the resulting power vacuum led to instability. U.S. disengagement after 1989 meant little support for post-Soviet reconstruction or political settlement.
- Blowback and radicalization: Some Mujahideen fighters later became involved in global jihadist movements, including al-Qaeda. U.S. funding and training of extremists during the war had long-term unintended consequences.

Concluding sentence:
- Should provide a clear summary judgment that reflects the overall extent to which the US achieved their goals, linking the goals to regional developments.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, the directive's primary objective, the removal of Soviet forces from Afghanistan, was met to a high degree. The Soviet withdrawal in 1989 followed years of sustained resistance by the Mujahideen, supported by U.S. covert aid in the form of weapons, funding, and intelligence. This significantly weakened Soviet military effectiveness and made continued occupation unsustainable ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, NSDD-166 successfully aimed to “demonstrate to the Soviet Union that its long-term strategy is not working.” The protracted conflict placed a major strain on Soviet military resources and morale, contributing to public disillusionment and internal dissent. The war earned the nickname “the Soviet Union's Vietnam,” underscoring the extent to which it undermined Soviet confidence and political cohesion ($\checkmark$).

However, the goal of restoring Afghan independence and stability was met to a lesser degree. Although Soviet forces left the country, Afghanistan soon descended into civil war between rival Mujahideen factions. The eventual rise of the Taliban and the emergence of al-Qaeda in the 1990s represented long-term consequences that ultimately ran counter to U.S. interests in the region ($\checkmark$).

In conclusion, while NSDD-166 played a decisive role in achieving key U.S. objectives, namely, the Soviet withdrawal and the weakening of Soviet geopolitical influence, its longer-term aim of fostering a stable and independent Afghanistan was not fully realised. The directive reflected Cold War priorities, but its legacy highlights the complexity of foreign intervention and the unintended consequences that can follow even seemingly successful policy outcomes ($\checkmark$).
