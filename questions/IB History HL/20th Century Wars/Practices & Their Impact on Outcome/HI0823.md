# HI0823

Analyse **three** reasons for international involvement in the Vietnam War.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following with explanation:
- U.S. containment policy and the domino theory: The United States was committed to preventing the spread of communism in Asia, especially after the Chinese Communist Revolution (1949) and the Korean War (1950-1953). The Domino Theory suggested that if one country in Southeast Asia fell to communism, others would quickly follow. Vietnam was seen as the critical first domino.
- Support from the USSR and China for North Vietnam. Both the Soviet Union and the People's Republic of China provided economic aid, weapons, and training to the North Vietnamese as part of Cold War rivalry. Their support stemmed from a desire to promote global communism and challenge U.S. influence.
- The USSR also used Vietnam to assert its leadership in the communist world, particularly during the Sino-Soviet split, creating competition with China over influence in Hanoi.
- U.S. strategic interests in Southeast Asia. The U.S. saw Vietnam as strategically vital for maintaining influence over Southeast Asia, which included proximity to key allies (e.g., the Philippines, Thailand) and control of trade routes. Establishing a military presence through bases and alliances helped counterbalance growing communist influence in Asia. Vietnam was also seen as a test of American credibility; failure to act would weaken U.S. standing globally.
- SEATO commitments. The Southeast Asia Treaty Organisation (SEATO), formed in 1954, was intended to block further communist gains in Southeast Asia. Although South Vietnam was not a member, the U.S. and its allies used SEATO as a justification for intervention under the principle of collective defence. Australia, New Zealand, and the Philippines were among SEATO members who contributed troops, citing treaty obligations.
- French colonial legacy. Vietnam was a former French colony (part of French Indochina), and France's defeat at Dien Bien Phu (1954) drew international attention to the region. The Geneva Accords (1954), which temporarily divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel, internationalised the conflict by creating a fragile and contested peace settlement. The U.S. stepped in to support South Vietnam after France's withdrawal, viewing the conflict as part of the broader fight against communism.
- Global superpower rivalry and Cold War alignments. The Vietnam War became a proxy war in the broader Cold War context, with countries aligning based on ideological and strategic interests. Australia and South Korea, for example, supported the U.S. due to alliance obligations and anti-communist alignment. The Soviet Union and China, meanwhile, supported North Vietnam to counter U.S. hegemony and extend their influence in the developing world.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, international involvement in the Vietnam War was driven by the United States' containment policy and belief in the Domino Theory ($\checkmark$). After the rise of communism in China and the outbreak of the Korean War, the U.S. feared that if Vietnam fell to communism, other Southeast Asian countries would follow. As a result, Vietnam became a key battleground in America's broader Cold War strategy to prevent the global spread of communism ($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the Soviet Union and China supported North Vietnam to advance their ideological interests and to counter U.S. influence in Asia ($\checkmark$). Both provided military and economic aid to Hanoi, aiming to promote global communism. This support was also shaped by Cold War competition, with the USSR and China each trying to assert their influence in the region, particularly during the Sino-Soviet split ($\checkmark$).

Finally, U.S. involvement was also justified through the Southeast Asia Treaty Organization (SEATO) ($\checkmark$). Although South Vietnam was not a formal member, SEATO allowed countries like the U.S., Australia, and the Philippines to frame their military intervention as part of a collective defense strategy against communist expansion in the region ($\checkmark$).
