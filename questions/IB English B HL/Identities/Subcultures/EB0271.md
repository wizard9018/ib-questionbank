**Maximum mark: 5**

**Why Teens Don't Pick Up the Phone Anymore**

Okay, let's be real—most of us hate answering phone calls. It's not that we're $\underline{\textrm{rude}}$, it's just... $\underline{\textrm{weird}}$. Like, why call when you can text, send a voice note, or DM?

Talking on the phone feels $\underline{\textrm{intense}}$. You have to pick up and talk *right away*, and sometimes you don't know what to say. With texting, you get time to think, chill, and reply when you're ready, and you can delete a message before it is read if needed! Another plus? You can use emojis or stickers to show how you feel.

Some adults think not picking up is bad manners…well, they did have landlines, but for us, it's just normal. We're not ignoring people, we're setting boundaries. If it's super urgent, sure, call. But most of the time, a quick message works better.

Another good option is texting before calling. It's polite and gives people a heads-up. No one likes surprise calls!

So yeah, phone calls aren't dead, but for teens, they reserved for serious conversations and grandma calls. We're just changing how we communicate—and that's totally okay.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- *Impolite* and *rude* mean not showing good manners. \

**Option D**, *mean*, could be confusing. However, *mean* implies someone is being intentionally rude or hurtful.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$   [1 mark]

- The correct replacement word for *weird* is *uncomfortable*. They both describe a feeling of awkwardness or tension, in this case related to picking up the phone.\

**Option A**, *crazy*, is not correct. Though *weird* and *crazy* could be similar, in this case, *crazy* is too strong.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- *Stressful* is the correct replacement word for *intense*. Both words express that phone calls are a source of stress and discomfort.\

**Option E**, *too much*, could be a good replacement phrase. However, in the context of the sentence the full phrase should be $\text{\textquotedblleft}$feels $\underline{\textrm{like}}$ too much” so the phrase does not fit grammatically.
