**Maximum mark: 5**

$\Large{\textrm{Goth subculture: A GUIDE TO THE GOTHIC}}$

I remember telling my father I was getting my Ph.D. in Gothic literature and he replied, confused, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Gothic like Goths? The people who wear all black?” While technically true, this definitely wasn't my area of study and as I tried to explain I realized how difficult it can be to define what is gothic. Historically, the Goths were a group of Germanic tribes that toppled the Roman Empire and plunged Europe into the Dark Ages. The term $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Gothic” was first used to describe the architecture that developed during this period which was considered barbaric and garish by Renaissance artists. Borrowing the term's association with darkness and superstition, Horace Walpole used it to describe his novel The Castle of Otranto: A Gothic Story (1765). In the 1970s, bands like Joy Division and The Cure adopted the genre's macabre elements to create Gothic rock, a genre of atmospheric post-punk rock with a thriving Goth subculture. At each stage in its evolution, the Gothic has exceeded existing boundaries to influence literature, film, popular culture and fashion to the point that a pair of black Crocs festooned in metal spikes and chains can now be described as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$gothic”.

[Source:Laredo, J. A. (n.d.). Guide to Gothic literature. Pressbooks. https://pressbooks.pub/guidetogothic/front-matter/introduction/ (Accessed November 3, 2024). CC BY 4.0. ]
