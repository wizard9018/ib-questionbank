**Maximum mark: 5**

$\Large{\textrm{Subcultures and Countercultures}}$

Groups in societies known as subcultures and countercultures play crucial roles in shaping personal and collective identity. Such groups are characterized by shared values, beliefs, styles, and behaviors that set them apart from the mainstream. 

Subcultures share interests in  music, fashion, interests, activities among others. Examples include skateboarders, Goths, and hip-hop lovers with different ways of dressing, speaking, and thinking. Some subcultures are based on the members' experience; biker culture and  the body modification subculture are some examples. Subcultures help people to develop individual identities, yet the individuals continue to be a part of the wider culture.

While countercultures are different from the dominant culture they go against it and develop opposite values and beliefs that are usually seen as a threat to society. For instance, the Hippie subculture of the 1960s protested against materialism, militarism and the conventional norms of society, thus embracing peace, love and saving the environment. Countercultures give people an opportunity to think out of the box and challenge the existing paradigms which in the long run may bring changes in the society. Some countercultures form societies that work outside the norms of the society.

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ 

- a.$\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{music, fashion, interests, activities among others}$ [1 mark]

- The text mentions that “subcultures share interests” which is another way of saying that they have these in common.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ 

- a.$\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{subcultures continue to be part of the mainstream.}$  [1 mark]

- The word *mainstream* refers to ideas and attitudes that are shared by most people. In this sense, subcultures continue to follow society's rules and conventions.

- b.$\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{countercultures work outside the norms of society.}$  [1 mark]

- As opposed to subcultures, countercultures, do not follow conventional rules.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{challenge}$

- *challenge* the existing paradigms means to resist the rules, therefore, defy (the paradigms) is a good replacement word. [1 mark]

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{existing paradigms}$

- *status quo* refers to the present state of affairs (things $\text{\textquotedblleft}$as they are”), the text uses the existing paradigms to describe this concept.  [1 mark]
