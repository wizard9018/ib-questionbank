# EB0373

**Maximum mark: 6**

**The Last Whistlers**

Hey guys! If you've been following my posts, you'll know I've been spending a lot of time with my grandmother lately. What you may not know is that my grandmother is one of the last people in our village who still speaks our $\underline{\textrm{traditional}}$ whistled language. I think she can only $`$talk' to three or four neighbors now and she's worried about it completely disappearing - so I asked her to tell me more about it.

She said it was once $\underline{\textrm{vital}}$ to daily life in the $\underline{\textrm{steep}}$ mountain valleys around our village.  People communicated with each other from very far away - and a simple whistled melody could contain a lot of information. Farmer's wives could tell $\underline{\textrm{distant}}$ husbands that sheep were missing, and mothers could call children to dinner from kilometers away! Each village had their own language and proudly passed it down from generation to generation, which created a really strong sense of community.

But here's the really exciting part - a new project has been started to document and teach $\underline{\textrm{it}}$ to younger villagers. And $\underline{\textrm{we}}$'re both going to take part!! Isn't that amazing?

Do you have family stories about languages? Leave a comment! Love ya! \

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$our traditional whistled language"\

*Indigenous* means something that belongs to a community. It is a good replacement word for *traditional*, as it means that the language has been part of the community for a long time.\

\

**Option G** is the distractor. *Hereditary* refers to something passed down through generations, but it is usually associated with genetics.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{H}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$it was once vital to daily life" \

*Vital* describes something that is absolutely necessary. *Essential* has the same meaning - something that cannot be done without.\

\

**Option D** does not work here. Although *internal* can mean the same thing as *vital* in some uses (vital organs, for example), the text uses the word *vital* to show the importance of language to daily life.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the steep mountain valleys around our village" \

*Steep* describes land that rises or drops at a sharp angle. *Sharp* has the same meaning when describing a sudden change in height or direction.\

\

**Option B** is the distractor. Although mountain valleys might seem *quiet*, the text describes a different physical aspect of the area - its steepness - and does not tell us if it is also quiet.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Farmer's wives could tell distant husbands that sheep were missing" \

*Distant* describes someone or something that is far away. *Remote* is a close synonym, showing that they were located a long way from their homes.\

\

**Option C** does not work here. Although farmers working in mountain valleys might be *tired*, the text says nothing about how the husbands felt, only about how far away they were.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{the (traditional whistled) language}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$a new project has been started to document and teach it to younger villagers" \

The pronoun *it* refers back to the whistled language mentioned throughout the blog post.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{the blogger and her grandmother}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$And we're both going to take part!!" \

*We* refers to the two people discussed in the final paragraph, the blogger and her grandmother, who have both joined the project.
