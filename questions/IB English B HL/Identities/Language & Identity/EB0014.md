$\Large{\textrm{The Lost Word}}$

In a small village, there lived an elderly woman named Elena, known for her rich storytelling. She spoke a unique dialect that combined words from her native language with phrases borrowed from neighbouring cultures. This dialect was a tapestry of her life experiences, connecting her to her heritage and the world around her.

One day, a group of young students visited Elena to learn about her stories. They were fascinated by the colourful language she used, which held meanings that went beyond mere words. However, they struggled to understand some of the terms, as they were unfamiliar with the nuances of her dialect.

Elena noticed this and decided to teach them a special word from her language: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$sukari,” which meant both $\text{\textquotedblleft}$sweetness” and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$belonging.” She explained how language shapes identity; every word carries emotions and memories. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$When you understand a word, you connect to the heart of the culture it comes from,” she said.

Inspired, the students began to explore their own languages, discovering the richness of their identities. They realised that language isn't just about communication; it's a key to understanding themselves and their place in the world.

While **Option E**, *and*, may seem like an option, it implies addition rather than integration and misses the nuance of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mixing."

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- *To* shows the relationship between language and cultural heritage, suggesting a link or connection and specifying that language acts as the pathway connecting cultural heritage and identity.\

While *with* (**Option C**) can seem like an option, it would not be grammatically correct, as the correct collocation is *link to*.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$   [1 mark]

- *Of* expresses the connection between understanding and different perspectives, indicating that understanding implies or is related to various viewpoints.\

*For* might seem like a possible option. However, if we used *for* (**Option H**) instead of *of*, as in $\text{\textquotedblleft}$an understanding for different perspectives," it would imply that the understanding is intended for the benefit of different perspectives.
