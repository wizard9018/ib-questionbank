## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{away}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$My job is to take care of pets when their owners are away.”\

The text mentions that the pet sitter looks after pets when the owners are away, not home.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{walks}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I give them the right food, play with them, and take them for walks or other activities”\

The text says the pet sitter takes care of different aspects of the pet's routine, like food and exercise. The preposition *for* indicates that the activity must be something you $\text{\textquotedblleft}$go for”

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{comfortable}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I want them to feel calm and comfortable.”\

The word *calm* is similar to *relaxed*, so the word *comfortable* fits this space.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{(fun) games}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Others need fun games and exercise.”\

The text mentions different needs for different pets and compares the options ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$soft beds and quiet music” and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$fun games and exercise”) In the exercise the word *whether* introduces two possible options, just like in the original text.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{deserve}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$If you want someone who is careful, kind, and professional, I am here to help. Your pets deserve the best”\

The word *expert* summarizes the idea of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$someone who is careful, kind, and professional,” and the word *deserve* completes the idea from the original sentence.
