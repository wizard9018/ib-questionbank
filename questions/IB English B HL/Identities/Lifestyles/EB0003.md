$\Large{\textrm{Rushing or delaying decisions is linked to anxiety and depression in young people}}$

Every day, young people make  $\underline{\textrm{countless}}$ decisions, from what to wear to choosing their future careers. However, how they make these decisions can significantly impact their mental health. Research indicates that decision-making styles contribute to anxiety and depression among young adults.

In a study involving 1,411 young South Africans aged 15-25, findings revealed that those who adopt a $\underline{\textrm{vigilant}}$ decision-making style—evaluating options thoroughly—tend to experience lower levels of anxiety and depression. Conversely, those who rush their decisions or delay making them are at higher risk for mental health issues. Alarmingly, 37.3% of participants were at $\underline{\textrm{risk}}$ for major depressive disorder, while 74.2% faced anxiety disorders.

To support better mental health, young people are encouraged to take their time when making decisions. This can involve identifying the issue, brainstorming solutions, weighing pros and cons, and consulting trusted individuals. By $\underline{\textrm{adopting}}$ a careful approach, they can enhance their emotional well-being and improve decision outcomes.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$   [1 mark]

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{G}$   [1 mark]

- *Risk* refers to the potential for harm or negative outcomes, which is synonymous with *possibility* in this context.

- **Options F and C**, *danger* and *harm*, could be confusing here as they are related to risk, but their meaning is fundamentally different. (*Danger* poses an immediate risk. *Harm* refers to actual damage)

- **Option E**, *possibility*, is not a good match for any of the words.
