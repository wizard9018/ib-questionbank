# EB0268

**Maximum mark: 6**

December 23rd  

Today was Festivus, and honestly, the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Airing of Grievances” might just be my new favorite wellness ritual. I gathered my friends and family, and instead of bottling up the small irritations, the grievances from the year, we set them free- openly, but with humor. It felt strange at first, putting words to frustrations I usually swallow, but once we started, I realized how refreshing it was.  

There's something deeply freeing in voicing one's complaints this way. Saying, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I was upset when you canceled on me last minute,” in a safe, structured setting eased the tension I didn't even know was there. We laughed at the petty complaints, like my brother never responding to texts, but we also acknowledged deeper feelings that needed communicating. My chest felt lighter, my jaw less tight, as if my body was releasing stress. 

When it was my turn to listen, I practiced not taking things personally. Instead, I heard them as invitations to reflect and improve. By the end, the room felt lighter too, there was less resentment and more connection. Who knew that Festivus could be therapeutic? I'm convinced: releasing grievances, instead of burying them, is a true medicine for the mind and body.  

**Choose the three correct statements.** [3 marks]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$The author spent Festivus with his loved ones. 

- B.$\hspace{1em}$It felt natural to talk about the frustrations. 

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Telling people about their complaints made the author tense.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$The event included both playful and serious complaints.

- E.$\hspace{1em}$Most complaints were about the writer's brother.

- F.$\hspace{1em}$The writer felt emotionally better after talking about year-long irritations.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1- 3.$\hspace{1em}$*The answers can be written in any order.*

- $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A, D, F}$        [3 marks]

- **A**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I gathered my friends and family..."\

The writer states they were together with friends and family, so his loved ones, for Festivus.

- **D**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$We laughed at the petty complaints, but we also acknowledged deeper feelings that needed airing."\

The author notes that both minor (*petty* - small, unimportant) complaints and more serious issues were discussed.

- **F**:  $\text{\textquotedblleft}$My chest felt lighter, my jaw less tight, as if my body was releasing stress."\

The author felt physical and emotional relief following the airing of grievances.

- *Incorrect options:*  B, C, E

- **B**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$It felt strange at first”\

The author says it felt strange to air grievances at first, not natural. 

- **C**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Saying... eased the tension”\

The author mentions that telling people what had made them angry *eased the tension*, which means it made them feel less tense.

- **E**: The writer mentions one of the complaints was about their brother but they were not only about him.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Instead, I heard them as invitations to reflect and improve.”\

Though *invitations* and *possibilities* are not synonyms, they both express that the writer felt what was said to them offered a chance to change and improve their attitude or behavior.\

\

**Option A**, *complaints*, does not express the idea that change is possible; instead, it implies that something is wrong and needs to be fixed. This will make the sentence negative and critical.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$there was less resentment and more connection”\

*Tension* is a good replacement word for *resentment* in this context, as it expresses that, after communicating their grievances, the room felt less tense.\

\

**Option C** is the distractor here, as *anger* is a stronger, more violent emotion, whereas *resentment* implies a subtler feeling of bitterness.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I'm convinced: releasing grievances, instead of burying them, is a true medicine for the mind and body.” \

*Remedy* and *medicine* are similar in meaning. They both convey that this process works, metaphorically, heals $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the mind and body.”\

\

**Option B**, *solution*, could sound possible. However, a solution does not imply healing, which is a process.
