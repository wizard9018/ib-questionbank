# EB0129

**Maximum mark: 5**

**You are going to hear a pianist talk about the impact that her piano teacher had on her career.**

:audio{#965b5ea6-1025-4bab-96dc-7016cf0c2704 aid="965b5ea6-1025-4bab-96dc-7016cf0c2704"}

**Answer the following questions.**

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ Why did the speaker's mother think she might be interested in music? [1 mark]

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ How did the speaker's mother know Mr. Vernuchi?  [1 mark]

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ How did Mr. Vernuchi describe the speaker's natural talent at the piano? [1 mark]

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ At first, what was the obstacle to the speaker having a piano at her house? [1 mark]

**Choose the correct answer.**

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$At the pianist's first solo concert, Mr Vernuchi… [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$gave her technical instructions during the performance.
- B.$\hspace{1em}$looked distressed.
- C.$\hspace{1em}$gave the speaker emotional support and encouragement.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{She found her playing a simple tune by ear }$  [1 mark]

- *Also accept*: She found her playing a simple tune by ear at the piano (at his grandmother's).
- *Accept other wording with the same meaning*. The correct answer must include the words “by ear.”

- “I was eight when my mother was surprised to find me playing a simple tune by ear at the piano at my grandmother's.”

- To play a tune *by ear* means to play the piano without reading any sheet music. It is not common to find a child playing a simple melody by ear at the piano without prior knowledge, which suggests the speaker as a child showed she had a musical ear.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{her childhood piano teacher}$  [1 mark]

- *Also accept*: Mr. Vernuchi had been her childhood piano teacher.
- *Accept other wording with the same meaning*.

- “She asked Mr. Vernuchi, who had been her childhood piano teacher…”
- Since she used to take piano lessons with Mr. Vernuchi when she was a child, she sent the speaker to take lessons with him.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A real prodigy.}$  [1 mark]

- “Soon, he said to my parents I was “a real prodigy” at the piano…”
- The word *prodigy* refers to a person, especially a child, who has an extraordinary talent; in the speaker's case, she was a musical prodigy.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{Her parents couldn't afford it.}$  [1 mark]

- *Accept other wording with the same meaning*. The correct answer should include the idea of money as a problem.

- The speaker says, “...he said to my parents (...) and that I should have one to play at home. But they couldn't afford it.” *To afford something* means to have the right amount of money to pay for something.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$   [1 mark]

- The speaker says, “At my first solo piano concert, he was sitting in the first row, constantly giving me a reassuring nod.” The word *reassuring* means the speaker's mentor was trying to make her feel confident by smiling at the speaker during the performance; this is a sign of support and encouragement.

- *Incorrect options:* A, B

- **Option A**: is incorrect because the speaker never mentions that the mentor was talking to her during the concert; he was sitting in the audience during the performance.
- **Option B**: is not correct because the word *distressed* means worried or anxious. According to the speaker, the piano teacher was smiling during the performance.
