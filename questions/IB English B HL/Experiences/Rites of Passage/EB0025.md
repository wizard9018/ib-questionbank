$\Large{\textrm{Rites of passage}}$

**[-1-]**

A rite of passage is a particular type of ritual, conducted to mark an important transition in somebody’s life. These rituals most commonly follow people from the cradle to the grave. An initiation ritual transforms a person from their current state to a new one.

**[-2-]**

Rites of passage have multi-layered meanings. The purpose and intent of the ritual can be social or psychological as well as spiritual or religious. Certain rites of passage represent first and foremost transformations in the religious status or circumstances of the initiated. Religious initiation rituals primarily have a spiritual meaning.

**[-3-]**

Initiation rituals are not limited to religions and form part of every society around the globe. Within our great cultural variety, we find many commonalities in the way $\underline{\textrm{these events}}$ are structured. French anthropologist Arnold van Gennep (1873-1957) is famous for   $\underline{\textrm{his}}$ analysis of Rites of Passage. In his book *Les Rites de Passage* (1909), he shows that these rites include three phases: 

- Separation

- Transition

- Reincorporation.

**[-4-]**

Each initiation ritual has three stages: the pre-liminal, liminal, and post-liminal. The Latin word $\text{\textquotedblleft}$limen” means a threshold, and the liminal world forms the threshold between the old and the new.
