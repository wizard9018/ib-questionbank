# EB0397

**Maximum mark: 6**

**The Queue**\

*Thursday evening*

People call me a professional queuer. I prefer *line consultant* - it looks more distinguished on a business card. The work is simple: demanding clients pay me to wait so they don't have to. They are busy Londoners with money to spare. I stand in line. They get the prize.

This week: three jobs.

The O2, Monday. A sold-out concert. A client needed the wristbands for her two front-row tickets. I joined the queue at dawn, got the bands by mid-morning and was home by lunch. She messaged yesterday to say the show was unforgettable. I was glad for her.

Wembley, Tuesday. Champions League. I held a client's place in line for twelve hours and handed over my spot 30 minutes before the opening ceremony. He messaged at midnight - apparently the match was incredible. I was happy for him.

Carnaby Street, Wednesday. A limited-edition trainer release. I queued for four hours, entered the store, and secured the last pair in my client's size, narrowly ahead of a very determined sixteen-year-old. My client was delighted. The teenager was not.

My phone just buzzed.  Another job tomorrow. Crufts tickets. Great - I love dogs.

**Find the word or phrase in paragraph 1 which means the following:** 

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$impressive    [1 mark] 

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$difficult    [1 mark] 

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$burn    [1 mark] 

**The following statements are either true or false. Write True or False, then justify it using words as they appear in the text. Both parts are required for [1 mark].** 

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$The speaker waited all day to get the concert wristbands.    [1 mark] 

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$The speaker's client contacted them after the match.    [1 mark] 

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$The speaker was unable to get the trainers in the client's size.    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{distinguished}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$it looks more distinguished on a business card"\

*Distinguished* describes something that appears impressive or of high social status. The speaker prefers a title that sounds more professional and elevated to clients.

 

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{demanding}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$demanding clients pay me to wait"\

*Demanding* describes people who have high expectations and are not easy to please. This tells us that the speaker's clients are rich and used to getting exactly what they want.

 

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{spare}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$busy Londoners with money to spare"\

*Burn* works because it fits the same idiomatic structure (“money to + verb”) and conveys excess wealth. *Burn* is used metaphorically, meaning they have extra money. 

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{False}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$was home by lunch"\

The speaker joined the queue at dawn but had completed the job by midday, which is significantly less than a full day.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{True}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$He messaged at midnight"\

The client sent a message after the match had ended to share their happiness reaction with the speaker.

 

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{False}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$secured the last pair in my client's size"\

The speaker DID successfully obtain the correct size by beating another customer (a teenager) to the last pair.
