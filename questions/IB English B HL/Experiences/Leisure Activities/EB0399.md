**Night Shift at the Bounce Palace**

I told you I just started working nights at an indoor trampoline park. It's a simple job: guard the building and make sure nobody breaks in. During the day it is loud and full of customers of all ages. When I'm there, it is completely different.

 

My shift starts at midnight. The whole building is quiet and the lights are low. There are about forty trampolines in the main hall, and they just sit there in the dark, waiting. I do my rounds. I drink my coffee. I try not to look at them too much.

 

I lasted three weeks before I tried one.

I told myself it was just a quick test to make sure the equipment was safe. I jumped. And bounced. And then I spent the next forty minutes doing something that I can only describe as the best fun I have had since I was ten years old.

 

I am a 34-year-old man. I am responsible for the security of this building. I bounced off the walls. I laughed so hard my sides hurt. Now I bounce every night, and I have never told anyone about it before - except you.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1 - 3.$ $\hspace{1em}$*The answers can be written in any order.*

- $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B, D, E}$        [3 marks]

- **B**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Guard the building and make sure nobody breaks in"\

The guard describes his job as keeping the building safe, which tells us that security is his main responsibility.

- **D**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$My shift starts at midnight"\

Midnight is the middle of the night, confirming that the guard begins work at this time.

- **E**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$There are about forty trampolines in the main hall"\

Forty trampolines is a large number, telling the reader that the hall contains many trampolines.

- *Incorrect options:*  A, C, F

- **A**: The guard says he $`$just' started working a month ago, which is a short time, not a long time. The word $`$just' also implies something that has happened recently.

- **C**: The text mentions $\text{\textquotedblleft}$customers of all ages” using the park, not only children.

- **F**: The guard says he tries not to look at the trampolines too much, which suggests he finds it difficult to ignore them.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I lasted it out three weeks before I tried one" \

*Lasted it out* means to continue for a period of time without giving up. The guard resisted trying the trampolines for three weeks before giving in.\

\

**Option H** is not correct. Although $\text{\textquotedblleft}$worked it out" is a common phrase in English, it means to find a solution to a problem, which does not fit the meaning here.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I jumped. And bounced." \

The guard describes getting onto the trampoline energetically. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Jumped onto one" fits the action described in the original text.\

\

**Option B** is not correct. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Climbed onto one" is grammatically correct but the text describes a sudden, energetic action, not a careful or difficult movement.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{G}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I spent the next forty minutes doing something that I can only describe as the best fun I have had since I was ten years old" \

*Spent* describes passing a period of time doing an activity. The guard bounced around for nearly forty minutes.\

\

**Option C** is not correct. *Endured* suggests suffering through something unpleasant, which contradicts the joyful description of this moment.

$7.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I laughed so hard my sides hurt" \

The guard was laughing with such intensity that it caused physical discomfort - but in this case, it shows how much fun he was having.\

\

**Option A** is not correct. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Bounced so hard" makes sense in a trampoline context, but the text makes clear it is laughing, not bouncing, that caused the pain.
