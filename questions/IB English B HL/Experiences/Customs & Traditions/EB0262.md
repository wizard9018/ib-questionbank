# EB0262

**Maximum mark: 5** 

Dear Tom,

How are you? I hope you are fine. I have so much to tell you about my trip to England that I don't know where to begin! Visiting England has been amazing so far!

I am staying with the Thompsons; the mom is called Susan, the dad is Paul, and Jenny is their 12-year-old girl. I am living just five blocks from Buckingham Palace. It is fantastic since I can visit it almost every day. One day, I saw King Charles' car from the window of my bedroom. You know, I'm a Royal fan, and I feel :u[thrilled] at the simple idea of :u[catching a glimpse] of the King or Queen. 

I learnt a lot about traditions and customs, too! One day, Susan fixed dinner, and she prepared a :u[mouthwatering] Shepherd's pie. Let me tell you that it was the best dish I have ever tasted, and when we get together, we are going to try to do it. Susan told me that the recipe had been passed down through generations and that her mom had taught her the “secret” ingredient. First, you have to make the mashed potato, and then you prepare the filling with onions, carrots, minced lamb, and then… you add the “secret” ingredient: tomato paste. Making it sounds easy, but it is not! 

As soon as I get back, I'll phone you to tell you more about the recipe and my trip. Take care!

With love, Susan.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$   [1 mark]

- In the phrase “I hope you are fine,” the replacement word *well* fits naturally and is used in greetings.

- **Option H** is confusing, but *healthy* is more specific and not usually used in greetings.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- *Wonderful* matches the positive meaning of *fantastic* in describing the experience.

- **Option C** is incorrect. *Joyful* describes a feeling of great happiness.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- *Excited* clearly matches the emotion expressed when thinking about seeing the King or Queen.

- **Option E** is confusing. *Enthusiastic* describes attitude or interest rather than emotion.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{... through generations}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$... the recipe had been passed down through generations”. \

\

The *instructions* refer to the *recipe*. The phrase *were transmitted* is similar in meaning to the phrasal verb *passed down* used in the text.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{... easy, (but it is not!)}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Making it sounds easy, but it is not!”\

\

The phrase *making it* refers to *preparing the recipe* and the word *seems* can be replaced by the word *sounds* in the context of this text. The sentence “but it is not!” is optional because it does not change the original idea.
