# EB0032

**Maximum mark: 6**

Many professionals face difficult choices when conflicts $\underline{\textrm{arise}}$ in their countries.  The choice of staying or trying to find a new country to live in - to emigrate -  is extremely tough to make, but many people choose to leave, believing that their families will be safer and that there will be new opportunities - for themselves and their children - in a conflict-free country.

Many countries welcome professionals and their families, assisting them in finding new homes and work, providing medical care, and $\underline{\textrm{ensuring}}$ that children go to school.  But for some, the process of moving and finding good work is not that simple.  Doctors often find that there are licensing $\underline{\textrm{barriers}}$ for them: despite their skills and years of experience, they find that they are not qualified to practice medicine in their new country.  Many must make another painful choice: to take a lower-paying job in a $\underline{\textrm{field}}$ outside of medicine to support their family immediately or go through the sometimes very difficult and expensive process of requalifying as a doctor.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{G}$   [1 mark]

- While **Option C**, *get up*, is another meaning of the word *arise*, it does not fit in the context of the text (conflicts happening in a country). **Option G**, *Occur*, means *to happen*, which is the exact meaning in the text.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- **Option B.** To *guarantee*, means to be sure that something happens, making it the correct choice.  The word *ensure* may be easily confused with the word *insure*, which means to make safe.   **Option E** is, therefore, incorrect.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$   [1 mark]

- The word *barriers* has several meanings, one of which is *fences* (**Option H**). However, in the context of this text, the meaning of the word barrier is implied to be negative ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$...$\underline{\textrm{despite}}$ their skills and years of experience they find they are not qualified to practice medicine…”).  Therefore, the correct choice is *obstacles*.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- A *field* of expertise is the *area* in which a person has a lot of experience or skills, which makes it the correct choice. An alternative meaning for *field* is a large open area, usually covered with grass or some other plants - a field of corn, for example - however, **Option A**, *grassland*, does not fit in the context of the text.  

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{True}$   [1 mark]

- **Justification:**  Many countries welcome professionals and their families

- The text states that being accepted into a country as an immigrant can be a very positive experience, as shown in the sentence above.   

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{False}$   [1 mark]

- **Justification:** But for some, the process of moving and finding good work is not that simple.

- The main theme of the text is that although immigrants may be welcomed into a new country, there are some professions - specifically related to medicine - in which even highly qualified and experienced doctors may not be able to work in their field.
