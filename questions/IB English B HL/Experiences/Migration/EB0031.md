# EB0031

**Maximum mark: 7**

Immigrant farmworkers are vital to U.S. agriculture, making up an estimated 73% of the agricultural workforce. $\underline{\textrm{This}}$ labor-intensive work is essential to the economy, with a total value of over \$1 trillion, yet it faces an ongoing labor shortage that was made worse by the COVID-19 pandemic. Farmworkers often work long hours in all types of weather, and $\underline{\textrm{some}}$ face dangerous conditions - like exposure to chemicals and pesticides - without access to medical care, which means fewer Americans are applying for these jobs.

In California, 56\% of farmers reported struggling to find enough labor over the last five years. This shortage leads to increased imports, food waste, and economic losses. The dairy industry, for example, could see doubled retail milk prices if these workers were not permitted to work in the U.S.  The inability to find reliable sources of agricultural labor leads to increased imports of fruit and vegetables, raising consumer prices. Without immigrant farmworkers, crops are not gathered or harvested, which contributes to food waste. 

**To whom or to what do the following words refer?  Answer using words as they appear in the text.**

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{This}}$ labor-intensive work...    [1 mark]

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$and $\underline{\textrm{some}}$ face dangerous conditions...    [1 mark]

**Answer the following questions.**

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$Why are fewer Americans applying for jobs as farmworkers?  Give **two** examples.    [2 marks]

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$What economic impact does the lack of agricultural workers have on consumers? Give **two** examples.    [2 marks]
