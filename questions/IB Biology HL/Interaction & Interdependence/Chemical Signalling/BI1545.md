# BI1545

**Maximum mark: 4**

Compare and contrast how peptide and steroid hormones bring about a response once they have reached their target cells.    [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **four** of the following (*response must include at least one similarity and one difference to gain full marks*):

    **Similarities:** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** control enzymes OR regulate cellular activity                    [1]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** bind to specific receptors                                [1]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** amplify the initial signal                                [1]

    **Differences:** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide hormones are hydrophilic **OR** polar and do not enter the cell **WHEREAS** steroid hormones are hydrophobic **OR** non-polar so can enter the cell                 [1]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide hormones bind to receptors on the cell surface membrane **WHEREAS** steroid hormones bind to intracellular receptors **OR** receptors in the cytoplasm **OR** nucleus     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide hormones activate a second messenger **OR** cyclic AMP  **WHEREAS** steroid hormones do not activate a second messenger    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide hormones initiate cascade of reactions **OR** signal transduction **WHEREAS** steroid hormones directly activate gene expression    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide hormones bring about a more rapid **OR** short-lived response **WHEREAS** steroid hormones bring about a slower **OR** longer-lasting response         [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Both** peptide and steroid hormones **regulate cellular activities** ($\checkmark$) and **bind to specific receptors** ($\checkmark$). **Peptide** hormones are **hydrophilic** so **cannot enter** the **cell** and instead **bind** to **cell surface receptors**, **activating** a **second messenger**, such as **cAMP** which brings about a **cascade response**.  In contrast, **steroid** hormones are **hydrophobic** and so can diffuse **through** the cell **membrane** ($\checkmark$). Once inside the cell, they bind to **intracellular receptors** ($\checkmark$), directly affecting **gene transcription** ($\checkmark$).
