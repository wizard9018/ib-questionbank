# BI2954

The image shows a transmembrane acetylcholine receptor on the postsynaptic membrane.

![2954Ed](images/2954Ed.png)

Which row correctly describes what binds to the receptor, the substance that flows through it, and the effect on the postsynaptic membrane potential?

| | What binds to the receptor? | Substance that flows through the receptor channel | Effect on the membrane potential |
| --- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Acetylcholine | Sodium ions | Depolarisation |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Acetylcholine | Chloride ions | Hyperpolarisation |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Acetylcholinesterase | Sodium ions | Depolarisation |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Calcium ions | Potassium ions | Hyperpolarisation |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
When an action potential reaches the presynaptic membrane, acetylcholine is released, which diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to both binding sites on the receptor. This causes a conformational change in the receptor, opening the channel, allowing sodium ions to flow into the postsynaptic neuron down their electrochemical gradient, thereby depolarising the membrane. **Choice A** is correct.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor is an excitatory channel; chloride ions flowing in and hyperpolarisation describe an inhibitory synapse. \
**Choice C** is incorrect because acetylcholinesterase does not bind to the receptor; it is an enzyme in the synaptic cleft that breaks down acetylcholine. \
**Choice D** is incorrect because calcium ions do not bind to this receptor; their entry into the presynaptic knob triggers acetylcholine release. Also, the main movement through the channel is Na$^+$ diffusing into the cell, which depolarises the membrane rather than hyperpolarising it.
