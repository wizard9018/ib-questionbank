# BI0485

Below is a diagram showing feedback mechanisms, labelled I, II and III, involving sex hormones.

![BI0485](images/BI0485.png)

Which row in the table correctly names the type of feedback shown by I, II and III?

| | I. | II. | III. |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1em}$A.$\hspace{1em}$| Negative | Positive | Negative |
|$\hspace{1em}$B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Positive | Positive | Negative |
| $\hspace{1em}$C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Positive | Negative | Negative |
| $\hspace{1em}$D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Negative | Positive | Positive |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
FSH brings about follicle growth in the ovaries at the beginning of the menstrual cycle. The increase in follicle growth leads to increased levels of oestradiol (oestrogen). Normally, oestradiol exerts negative feedback on the anterior pituitary and hypothalamus to reduce the release of FSH and LH. However, when oestradiol levels reach a high threshold in the middle of the cycle, just before ovulation, the feedback on the anterior pituitary and hypothalamus becomes positive, leading to a surge in LH (and to some extent FSH). This LH surge triggers ovulation. Feedback loop **I** therefore shows positive feedback.

After ovulation, the ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, an endocrine gland in its own right. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone and some oestradiol. These hormones exert negative feedback on the anterior pituitary and hypothalamus, leading to decreased secretion of GnRH, LH, and FSH. The reduction in LH and FSH prevents the maturation of additional follicles during the luteal phase and is a classic example of a negative feedback loop. Therefore **II** and **III** are both examples of negative feedback mechanisms

**Choice C** is, therefore, correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because I is positive feedback, and II is negative feedback.
**Choice B** is incorrect as II is negative feedback.
**Choice D** is incorrect as the types of feedback shown for I, II, and III are all incorrect.
