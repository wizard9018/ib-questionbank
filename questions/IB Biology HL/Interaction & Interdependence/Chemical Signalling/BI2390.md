# BI2390

What is the likely time taken for an adrenergic neurotransmitter to perform its function in chemical signalling?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ 60s

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ 1s

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ 1ms

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ 1$\mu$s

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because there are usually open ligand-gated channels on the postsynaptic membrane, ready to bind to the neurotransmitter. The synapse is very narrow (typically 30nm) so diffusion across the synaptic gap is rapid. Adrenergic and cholinergic neurotransmitters are believed to have equal or near-equal transmission speeds.

**Choice A** and **B** are incorrect because a transmission time measured in whole seconds would be unrealistically slow, given the overall speed of nervous transmission, even when an impulse has to pass through several synapses as it is directed to its target organ. For example, nervous transmission through myelinated neurons in humans can be as fast as 120 ms$^{-1}$ (432 kilometres per hour!); whilst the synapses cause some delay, voluntary reaction times to stimuli can be measured in split-seconds, which shows that the synaptic transmission must be in that order of magnitude of speed.

**Choice D** is incorrect because this is too little time for a neurotransmitter to diffuse across the synaptic gap.
