# BI1419

**Maximum mark: 7**

Organisms communicate in a variety of ways. The nervous system is a communication system found in animals. Below is a diagram of a motor neuron from a person suffering from multiple sclerosis. Multiple sclerosis disrupts the transmission of the electrical impulses along neurons.

![BI1419](images/BI1419.png)

1. Outline saltatory conduction and suggest how multiple sclerosis affects this process.[3 marks]

2. Bacteria cells carry out a form of intercellular communication, called quorum sensing, which enables the control of the colony. Outline the mechanism of quorum sensing. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Must include following to gain full marks:

 $\ce{ -> }$ damage to myelin sheath reduces rate of transduction as axon no longer insulated **OR** saltatory conduction can no longer occur [1 mark]

 Then any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ myelin prevents changes in membrane potential where it is located [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ nodes of Ranvier between Schwann cells where ion channels concentrated [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ depolarisation at one node of Ranvier causes local current through axon to next node [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ stimulates depolarisation **OR** action potential at next node [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ action potential jumps from node to node **OR** saltatory conduction [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 There are **many ion channels** at the **nodes of Ranvier** between the Schwann cells ($\checkmark$). The depolarisation of the membrane, therefore, occurs rapidly at these nodes, and the rush of sodium ions into the cell causes a **local current** to flow passively through the **intracellular fluid** of the **axon** ($\checkmark$). This current travels to the next node of Ranvier, **generating** a **new action potential** ($\checkmark$). The action potential effectively jumps **from node to node**, a process known as **saltatory conduction** ($\checkmark$). If there the **myelin sheath** is **degraded in Multiple Sclerosis**, saltatory conduction cannot happen and the **rate of transduction** of the impulse is **reduced** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **four** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ release of ligands **OR** autoinducer **OR** signalling molecules into environment by individual cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as colony increases in size concentration of ligands **OR** autoinducer **OR** signalling molecules increases [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ bacteria detect increase in concentration of autoinducers OR ligands through cell surface receptors [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ once autoinducer **OR** ligand **OR** signalling molecule threshold reached binding of ligand **OR** autoinducer **OR** signalling molecules to receptor causes transduction cascade inside a bacterium [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ leads to change in gene expression **OR** activates autotransducer genes **OR** represses transcription of genes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ further increase in autoinducer **OR** ligand **OR** signalling molecules concentration example of positive feedback loop [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ controls virulence **OR** biofilm formation **OR** bioluminescence **OR** production of secondary metabolites **OR** sporulation [1 mark]
 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 In quorum sensing, **individual bacteria release autoinducers** into the environment ($\checkmark$). The **larger** the **colony**, the **more autoinducers** are released and these **bind to receptors** on the **cell surface** of each bacterial cell ($\checkmark$). Once a **threshold autoinducer concentration** is reached, it causes a **transduction cascade** ($\checkmark$) inside the cell leading to a **change in gene expression** ($\checkmark$). This causes a further increase in the **autoinducer concentration** and so this is a **positive feedback** loop ($\checkmark$). Quorum sensing is involved in the control of a variety of **functions**, such as **virulence** and **bioluminescence** ($\checkmark$).
