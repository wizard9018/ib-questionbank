# BI1465

**Maximum mark: 4**

Oestradiol, shown below, is a hormone involved in human reproduction.

![BI1465 fig1](<images/BI1465 fig1.png>)

Explain how oestradiol brings about a response in its target cells. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Any **four** of the following

$\ce{ -> }$ lipid-soluble, so diffuses through the lipid bilayer of the cell membrane [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ binds to intracellular receptors **OR**, once inside cell, binds to specific oestrogen receptors in cytoplasm **OR** nucleus. [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ binding activates receptors [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ oestradiol-receptor complex acts as transcription factor and enters nucleus [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ oestradiol-receptor complex **OR** transcription factor binds to promoter region [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ this binding regulates the transcription of genes **OR** upregulates gene expression **OR** downregulates gene expression [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ altered levels of mRNA lead to changes in protein synthesis [1 mark]

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Oestradiol, a **steroid hormone**, **diffuses through** the **plasma membrane** of its target cells due to its **lipid-soluble** nature ($\checkmark$). **Inside** the cell, it **binds to specific oestradiol receptors** located either **in** the **cytoplasm** or directly in the **nucleus** ($\checkmark$). This **hormone-receptor complex** then acts as a **transcription factor** by **moving into** the nucleus ($\checkmark$) (if not already there), where it binds to **promoter regions** on the DNA ($\checkmark$). This binding **alters** the **transcription of certain genes**, either **upregulating or downregulating** their **expression** ($\checkmark$). This change in gene expression **determines** whether certain **proteins** are **synthesised** ($\checkmark$) or not.
