# BI0972

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast the structure of transmembrane and intracellular receptors. [3 marks]

2. Explain why a variety of chemically different substances are used as hormones and neurotransmitters in humans. [5 marks]

3. Describe how adrenaline (epinephrine) brings about glycogen breakdown in liver cells and how progesterone acts on endometrial cells. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following (*must include at least **one** similarity and at least **one** difference to gain full marks; differences must refer to both receptor types*):

    ***Similarities***

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are proteins   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** have binding sites for specific chemicals **OR** ligands   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can undergo conformational changes when their ligand binds   [1 mark]

    ***Differences***

    $\ce{ -> }$ binding site of transmembrane receptors is on the extracellular region **OR** faces the outside of the cell **WHEREAS** binding site of intracellular receptors is inside cell   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transmembrane receptors span the plasma membrane **OR** have hydrophobic amino acid side chains in the membrane-spanning region **WHEREAS** intracellular receptors do not span the membrane **OR** have predominantly hydrophilic amino acid side chains exposed on their surfaces   [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
**Both** transmembrane and intracellular receptors are **proteins** ($\checkmark$) with **specific binding sites** for their ligands ($\checkmark$). **Transmembrane** receptors have **hydrophobic amino acid side chains** exposed in their **membrane-spanning regions**, whereas **intracellular** receptors have predominantly **hydrophilic amino acid side chains** exposed on their surfaces ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*answer must link each reason to a type of chemical or named example*):

    $\ce{ -> }$ steroid hormones/named steroid hormone are lipid soluble so diffuse through the plasma membrane to bind intracellular receptors   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide/protein hormones/catecholamines/suitable named molecule are water soluble so bind transmembrane receptors in the plasma membrane   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ steroids alter gene expression so responses are slower/longer-lasting   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water-soluble hormones act through transmembrane receptors that trigger intracellular signalling pathways, so responses are rapid   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water-soluble hormones dissolve in plasma **WHEREAS** steroids need carrier proteins **OR** this affects duration of action   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemically different signalling molecules bind to specific receptors, allowing target cells to distinguish between different signals   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different neurotransmitters can produce excitatory **OR** inhibitory effects depending on the receptors activated, e.g. glutamate is usually excitatory **WHEREAS** GABA **OR** glycine is usually inhibitory   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different neurotransmitters are removed from the synaptic cleft by enzymatic breakdown **OR** reuptake so signals can be terminated quickly   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neuropeptides/endorphins have slower/longer-lasting/modulatory effects   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nitric oxide/NO is a gas so diffuses freely across membranes **OR** is not stored in vesicles **OR** acts locally on nearby cells, whereas hormones travel in the blood to distant target cells, allowing communication over different distances   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different signalling molecules are synthesised from different precursors **OR** by different pathways, allowing their production and regulation in different tissues   [1 mark]

    *(adrenaline / noradrenaline may be used as a hormone or neurotransmitter example, but credit the same idea only once)*

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
**Steroid** hormones such as **testosterone** are **lipid soluble**, allowing them to **diffuse through** the plasma **membrane** and bind to **intracellular receptors** ($\checkmark$). In contrast, **protein hormones** such as **insulin** are **water soluble** and bind **transmembrane receptors** ($\checkmark$). **Steroids** alter **gene expression**, so their effects are slower but **longer-lasting** ($\checkmark$). Different **neurotransmitters** can produce **excitatory** or **inhibitory** effects depending on the **receptors** activated; for example, **glutamate** is usually excitatory while **GABA** is usually inhibitory ($\checkmark$). **Nitric oxide** is a **gas**, so it **diffuses** freely **across membranes** and is **not stored in vesicles** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following (*maximum four marks for each hormone*):

    ***Epinephrine/Adrenaline: G-protein-coupled receptor***

    $\ce{ -> }$ adrenaline/epinephrine binds to a G-protein-coupled receptor/GPCR in plasma membrane (of liver cells)   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ binding activates a G protein   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ activated G protein activates adenylyl/adenylate cyclase   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adenylyl/adenylate cyclase converts ATP to cAMP, which acts as a second messenger   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cAMP activates protein kinase A/PKA, which activates phosphorylase kinase **OR** initiates a phosphorylation cascade **OR** amplifies the signal   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen phosphorylase/enzymes involved in glycogen breakdown are activated, increasing glucose availability in liver cells   [1 mark]

    ***Progesterone: intracellular receptor***

    $\ce{ -> }$ progesterone is lipid soluble **OR** hydrophobic and diffuses through the plasma membrane of endometrial cells   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ progesterone binds to a specific intracellular receptor in the cytoplasm **OR** nucleus, activating the receptor   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the receptor–hormone complex enters the nucleus if the receptor was in the cytoplasm   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the complex binds to specific DNA sequences **OR** hormone response elements   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the complex regulates **OR** promotes transcription of specific genes   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA is translated into proteins that contribute to the maintenance **OR** development of the endometrium/uterine lining   [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
**Epinephrine** binds to a **G-protein-coupled receptor** (GPCR) in the **plasma membrane** of liver cells ($\checkmark$). This **activates** a **G protein** ($\checkmark$), which activates **adenylate cyclase**. Adenylate cyclase converts **ATP into cAMP**, which acts as a **second messenger** ($\checkmark$). cAMP activates **protein kinase A**, starting a **phosphorylation cascade** that activates **glycogen phosphorylase**, resulting in the **breakdown of glycogen** and increasing the **availability of glucose** ($\checkmark$).

    **Progesterone** is **lipid soluble**, so it **diffuses through the plasma membrane** of endometrial cells ($\checkmark$). It binds to a specific **intracellular receptor**, **activating** the **receptor** ($\checkmark$). The **receptor–hormone complex** binds to **specific DNA sequences** in the nucleus, promoting **transcription** of specific genes ($\checkmark$). The **mRNA** is **translated** into **proteins** that help **maintain the endometrium** ($\checkmark$).
