# BI2921

Ecologists are comparing the population distribution of two different plant species, X and Y, using random quadrat sampling.  They calculate the mean number of individuals per quadrat and the standard deviation for both species.

| | Density (plants/quadrat) |Standard Deviation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Species X** | 15 | 2 |
| **Species Y** | 15 | 10 |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X has a larger total population size than Species Y.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Species Y is more evenly distributed throughout the sampled area than Species X.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X shows less variation in population density over the sampled area compared to Species Y.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Both species have the same population density, so their distribution patterns must be identical.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because standard deviation measures how spread out the data are around the mean. A smaller standard deviation indicates that the quadrat counts are more tightly clustered around the mean, suggesting that Species X has a more uniform distribution of individuals across the sampled area. In contrast, Species Y has a larger standard deviation, meaning the counts are more spread out and show greater variation, implying a less uniform or more clumped distribution.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the mean population density for both species is the same (15 plants per quadrat), so this does not indicate any difference in total population size between the two species.

**Choice B** is incorrect because Species Y has a larger standard deviation than Species X, which actually indicates greater variation in its distribution, meaning it is less evenly spread out.

**Choice D** is incorrect because even though the two species have the same mean density, their distribution patterns can differ, as reflected in the different standard deviations. A species can have the same mean but different patterns of distribution (e.g., clumped vs. uniform).
