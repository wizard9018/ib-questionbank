# BI2926

Mexican petunia (*Ruellia simplex*), introduced from Mexico in the 1930s as an ornamental flower, is a favourite among Florida gardeners. Its hardiness, rapid growth, and attractive purple flowers make it a popular choice. Gardeners appreciate its ability to outcompete weeds due to its fast growth, dense clusters, and vast root structure, which effectively suppresses other, less desirable plants in a garden setting.

Based on this description, how would Ruellia simplex be classified?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Endemic

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Native

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Opportunistic

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Invasive

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because the Mexican petunia was introduced from Mexico, and it suppresses the growth of other plants.  These characteristics describe it as outcompeting other plants for resources.  Being introduced to a new region and outcompeting native and other species are the two defining features of an invasive species.

**Choice A** is incorrect because endemic species are native species found only in one geographic region.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because the Mexican petunia was introduced to Florida and is therefore not native.\
**Choice C**  is incorrect because the description goes beyond that of an opportunistic species, which may take advantage of a disturbed environment, and it highlights aggressive, competitive behaviour that actively suppresses the growth of other plants.
