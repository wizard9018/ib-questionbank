 $\ce{ -> }$ number of marked individuals in the second sample is counted [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 A **first sample** is **captured** and the number of individuals is **counted** ($\checkmark$). Each individual is **marked** in a way that **does not harm** it or make it **more visible** to predators, and then **released** ($\checkmark$). Time is allowed for the **marked individuals** to **mix back** into the population ($\checkmark$). A **second sample** is then **captured**, and the **total number** is **counted**, along with the **number** of **marked individuals** ($\checkmark$). To improve **reliability**, the process is **repeated** two more times, and the **mean is calculated** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** statement and **one** example for **both** competition **and** cooperation to gain full marks*)
:

 ***Competition:*** \
 $\ce{ -> }$ organisms compete for a limited resource such as food / space / light / water / mates [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ competition is a cost to one or both organisms because a resource used by one is not available to the other **OR** because it reduces survival / reproduction [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ intraspecific competition is between members of the same species **OR** interspecific competition is between members of different species [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ named example of interspecific competition with the resource stated, e.g. grey squirrels compete with red squirrels for food and are more successful, so red squirrel populations decline **OR** any other named example [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ named example of intraspecific competition with the resource stated, e.g. plants of the same species competing for light **OR** male red deer competing for mates [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ competition limits population size **OR** acts as a density-dependent factor as the population nears carrying capacity [1 mark]

 ***Cooperation:*** \
 $\ce{ -> }$ cooperation is when organisms work together so that they benefit [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ cooperation increases the chance of survival **OR** reproduction [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ named example of intraspecific cooperation, e.g. wolves hunting in packs to catch larger prey **OR** worker bees caring for the colony's young [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mutualism is an interspecific relationship where two species live in close association **AND** both benefit [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ named example of mutualism with the benefit to both partners stated, e.g. *zooxanthellae* provide hard corals with products of photosynthesis and the coral provides a protected habitat **OR** *Rhizobium* in the root nodules of legumes fixes nitrogen and receives sugars **OR** mycorrhizal fungi supply minerals to orchids and receive sugars [1 mark]

 [7 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Competition happens when organisms need the same **limited resource**, such as **food, space or mates** ($\checkmark$). It is a **cost** to one or both organisms, because resources used by one are not available to the other, which reduces survival or reproduction ($\checkmark$). Competition can be **intraspecific**, between members of the **same species**, or **interspecific**, between members of **different species** ($\checkmark$). For example, grey squirrels **compete with red squirrels for food**. Grey squirrels are more successful, so red squirrel populations have declined where grey squirrels have been introduced ($\checkmark$).

 Cooperation is when organisms **work together** so that they **benefit** ($\checkmark$). Mutualism is when organisms of **two species** live in **close association** and **both benefit** ($\checkmark$). For example, ***zooxanthellae*** live inside **hard corals** and provide them with **nutrients** made by photosynthesis, while the coral provides the *zooxanthellae* with a **protected habitat** ($\checkmark$).
