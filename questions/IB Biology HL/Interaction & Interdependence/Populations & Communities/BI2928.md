# BI2928

The graph below illustrates the population dynamics of Species X and Species Y over several years in a stable ecosystem.

![2928](images/2928.png)

Y as represented in the graph?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X is the prey, species Y is the predator.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Both Species X and Species Y are experiencing density-independent population control.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X and Species Y are engaged in competitive exclusion.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X is the parasite, and Species Y is its host.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because the graph illustrates the characteristic predator-prey cycle, where the population peak of Species Y (the predator) follows the peak of Species X (the prey). In a predator-prey dynamic, an increase in the prey population leads to a rise in the predator population. As the predator population grows, it exerts pressure on the prey, causing a subsequent decline in the prey population. This, in turn, leads to a decline in the predator population. This cyclic relationship is driven by density-dependent factors, specifically predation.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the cyclical nature of the graph is indicative of density-dependent factors, such as predation, where one population directly influences the other. Density-independent factors (e.g., weather, natural disasters) would not produce such predictable fluctuations.

**Choice C** is incorrect because competitive exclusion refers to one species outcompeting and driving the other to extinction, which is not represented in the graph. Instead, both species are fluctuating in a cyclical manner without one being eliminated.

**Choice D** is incorrect because if Species X were a parasite, its population would typically follow the decline of the host species. In a parasite-host relationship, the host population (in this case, Species Y) would typically be larger and experience a decline only after the parasite population increases, which is not reflected in the graph.
