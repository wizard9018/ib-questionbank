# BI0109

Which of the following can be determined using the chi-squared test?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The biodiversity found in an area of a forest.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The range of values obtained for heights of trees in an area.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The probability of finding certain species of plants in adjoining plots based on observations made for one area.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Testing for a statistically significant association between the occurrences of two plant species

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The presence or absence of two plant species can be recorded in a series of sampling quadrats. A chi-squared test compares the observed frequencies with the expected frequencies if the species occur independently. This allows scientists to test for a statistically significant association between the occurrences of the two species. Therefore, **Choice D** is correct.

C**Choice A** is incorrect because a chi-squared test does not calculate biodiversity. Species diversity can instead be quantified using an index, such as Simpson’s diversity index, which takes account of the number of species and their relative abundances.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the range of tree heights is calculated by subtracting the smallest recorded height from the largest recorded height. A chi-squared test is not needed for this calculation.

**Choice C** is incorrect because a chi-squared test does not directly predict the probability of finding plant species in unsampled adjoining plots. It evaluates observed frequency data against the frequencies expected under a null hypothesis, such as the hypothesis that two species occur independently.
