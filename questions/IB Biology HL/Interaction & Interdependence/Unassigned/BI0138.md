# BI0138

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how decomposers contribute to energy transformations in an ecosystem. [3 marks]

2. Compare and contrast primary production by autotrophs and secondary production by consumers in an ecosystem. [5 marks]

3. Nutrients are cycled in all ecosystems.  Draw and label the carbon cycle showing at least four stores or sinks and at least four flows or processes. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ break down organic matter **OR** dead organisms **OR** waste material **OR** prevent accumulation of dead material     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ release nutrients into the environment **OR** release inorganic nutrients **OR** named inorganic nutrient back into the soil **OR** water for uptake by autotrophs **OR** increase soil fertility **OR**  enhance soil composition so supporting plant growth **OR** primary production   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ facilitate detritivore activity **OR** make it easier for detritivores **OR** named detritivore to consume **OR** process organic matter  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ convert organic carbon to inorganic carbon **OR** release carbon dioxide during respiration which re-enters the atmosphere     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ release energy as heat **OR** heat is lost as a byproduct of respiration   [1 mark]

[3 marks]

 :u[Sample answer]:

    Decomposers **break down dead organisms** and **waste material**, **preventing** the **accumulation** of organic matter ($\checkmark$). They **release nutrients** such as **nitrogen** and **phosphorus** into the **soil**, increasing **fertility** and **supporting plant growth** ($\checkmark$). During **respiration**, they release **heat energy** as a by-product ($\checkmark$) and **carbon dioxide**, which re-enters the atmosphere and can be used by autotrophs in **photosynthesis**($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to gain full marks*):

***Similarities***:

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** involve the accumulation of carbon compounds in biomass        [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ In **BOTH** energy is lost as biomass is transferred to the next trophic level       [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are typically measured in grammes per metre cubed per year OR  g m⁻² yr⁻¹     [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** contribute to biomass availability in an ecosystem     [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** depend on energy input [1 mark]

***Differences***:

$\ce{ -> }$ primary production occurs in autotrophs **WHEREAS** secondary production occurs in heterotrophs      [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ primary production is greater **THAN** secondary production   [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ primary production occurs at the first trophic level **WHEREAS** secondary production occurs at higher trophic levels      [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ primary production usually depends on sunlight for energy  **WHEREAS** for secondary production depends on consuming other organisms  [1 mark]
[5 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:

    **Both** primary and secondary production involve the **accumulation** of **carbon compounds in biomass** ($\checkmark$). In **both** cases, **energy** is **lost** as **biomass** is **transferred** to the next **trophic level** ($\checkmark$). However, **primary production** occurs in **autotrophs**, which **convert energy** from **light** or **chemical** sources into **organic molecules**, whereas **secondary** production occurs in **heterotrophs** ($\checkmark$), which obtain **energy** by **consuming** other **organisms** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, primary production is greater than secondary production due to energy losses at each trophic level ($\checkmark$).
