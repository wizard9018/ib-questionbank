1. Any **one** of the following:

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The enzyme is **extracted from the turnip root cells** ($\checkmark$) by grinding them and filtering to remove the parts of the plant cells that are not soluble.

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The dependent variable is the **volume of oxygen produced in 1 minute** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ different volumes of enzyme extract and water were used in each sample to provide different concentrations of enzyme [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ volume of water was added to get a final volume of 10ml for all concentrations of enzyme [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The different volumes of enzyme extract and water added were used **to produce different concentrations of enzyme** ($\checkmark$).

4. **Two** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ values shown correctly, 1.6 for volume of O:sub[2] gas /ml min:sup[-1] for 0.5% H:sub[2]O:sub[2] and 3.2 for volume of O:sub[2] gas /ml min:sup[-1] for 1% H:sub[2]O:sub[2] [1 mark]

5. **two** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ rate of reaction, **OR** volume of gas, increases with increased substrate concentration (up to 1.7%) **BECAUSE** more substrate molecules means more chances of successful collisions **OR** more enzyme-substrate-complexes form per unit time [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ at higher concentrations gas production plateaus **BECAUSE** concentration of enzyme, **OR** number of enzyme molecules, become limiting **OR** at higher substrate concentrations the active sites on the enzyme are saturated [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 As more substrate is added, the rate of reaction **increases** ($\checkmark$) up to 1.7% concentration of hydrogen peroxide. At higher concentrations of substrate, the enzyme concentration becomes limiting and therefore the curve **plateaus** ($\checkmark$).

6. Any **two** of the following (one for stating inhibitor is competitive)

 $\ce{ -> }$ inhibitor is competitive [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ competitive inhibitor competes with the substrate for the active site, so as substrate concentration increases, the activity of the enzyme **OR** rate of reaction, increases [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ with a non-competitive inhibitor, even at high substrate concentrations the enzyme activity, **OR** rate of reaction, is always lower than when there is no inhibitor. [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ at highest substrate concentrations enzyme activity rate, **OR** rate of reaction, is restored to, **OR** is the same as, levels without inhibitor [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ a graph sketched and annotated to show how each point above can be awarded the appropriate marks [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

 ![BIO785G](images/BIO785G.png)

 At higher concentrations of substrate **the rate of enzyme activity is nearly as high as without an inhibitor** ($\checkmark$) which shows that the inhibitor is competitive. With a non-competitive inhibitor, even at higher concentrations of substrate , **the enzyme activity rate is lower** ($\checkmark$) than when there is no inhibitor.

7. Any **one** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ temperatures either side of the optimum temperature **OR** exposure to extreme temperatures [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ pH away from the optimum [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ surface area if in solid form [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Varying the pH **away from the optimum** ($\checkmark$) (by adding acid or alkali to the enzyme extract) can lower the activity of the enzyme.
