# BI0774

**Maximum mark: 10**

The white clover (*Trifolium repens*) is native to the fields around Cornell University. The image shows a 10m x 15m area of the park, where quadrats (1m:sup[2]) were used to measure the number of white clover in the fields. The results from 10 of the quadrats are also shown below.

![BI0774A](images/BI0774A.png)
[Source: adapted from: NY State IPM Program at Cornell University, N. Y. S. I. P. M. P. at C. U. (2014, June 19). *White Clover in soccer field*. Flickr. Retrieved March 8, 2023, from https://www.flickr.com/photos/99758165@N06/14273941729 Copyright under CC BY 4.0]

1.
    1. Describe how quadrats could be used to estimate the mean number of white clover in the field. [3 marks]

    2. alculate the mean number of white clover in the field, showing your working. [1 mark]

    3. Explain why this type of sampling can only be used for sessile or immobile organisms. [1 mark]

    4. Suggest two limitations of quadrat sampling. [2 marks]

2. State the statistical test that could be conducted to check if white clover are associated with another organism in the field. [1 mark]

3. There are nitrogen fixing bacteria in the root nodules of the white clover. State two ways that the white clover uses the nitrogen provided by the bacteria. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ divide the field into a grid using a measuring tape **OR** other suitable apparatus [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ use a random number generator **OR** other suitable apparatus to generate ten coordinates     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ place quadrats at the ten coordinates **AND** count the number of white clovers in each quadrat    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calculate the mean number of white clover per quadrat    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ multiply the mean number of white clover by 150        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    To estimate the number of white clover in the field, the field should be **divided into a grid** using a measuring tape ($\checkmark$) and each grid should be assigned a coordinate. Next, using a **random number generator** ($\checkmark$), you should generate **10 coordinates** and then place a quadrat at one coordinate and count the number of white clovers at that coordinate. Then place the quadrat at another coordinate and **count the number of white clover** ($\checkmark$) there, this should be repeated for all of the coordinates. Finally,  **the mean number** ($\checkmark$) of white clover per quadrat should be calculated and the mean should be **multiplied by 150** ($\checkmark$).

1.
    2. any **one** of the following:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ 4290     [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ Mean number of white clover per 1 m:sup[2] quadrat=  **(13 + 44 + 33 + 42 + 10 + 18 + 34 + 29 + 7 + 56)  ÷ 10 = 28.6**

    $\hspace{1em}$ Area of field = 10 m x 15 m  = **150** m:sup[2]

    $\hspace{1em}$ Mean number of white clovers per field = 28.6 x 150 = **4290** ($\checkmark$) white clover

1.
    3. any **one** of the following:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ immobile organisms do not move out of the sample area making counting accurate **OR**  highly mobile organisms can move out of the sample area making counting inaccurate     [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ Quadrats are mostly used for counting sessile or immobile organisms, as they **do not move**  out of the sample area, so counting is **accurate** ($\checkmark$).

1.
    4. any **two** of the following:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ the number of organisms may be underrepresented **OR** overrepresented **OR** size of quadrat can greatly influence the results    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ researcher can make mistakes when counting    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ if a white clover is on the edge of a quadrat, then it might be difficult to know whether to include it    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ there is only a small sample size    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ Two limitations of quadrat sampling are that the number of organisms may be **underrepresented** ($\checkmark$) or the researcher can make **mistakes when counting** ($\checkmark$).
