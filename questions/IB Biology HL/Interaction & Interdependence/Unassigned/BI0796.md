# BI0796

**Maximum mark: 10**

Students were investigating metabolic reactions in the classroom and were given an experiment which demonstrated the effect of temperature on a catabolic reaction.

A sample of fat was added to a beaker, which contained 150ml of a 1% sodium chloride solution.

![BI0796A](images/BI0796A.png)

The beaker and its contents were then added to a water bath, which was set at 15 :sup[o]C. The beaker was given 5 minutes to equalise with the temperature of the water bath. After 5 minutes, 5 ml of a 2 % lipase solution, at the same temperature as the water bath, was added to the beaker.

![BI0796B](images/BI0796B.png)

5 ml of 2 % sodium hydroxide (NaOH), pH 8.4, was added to a test tube and phenolphthalein, a colourless solution which turns pink in basic environments, was then added. The intensity of the pink colour is proportionate to the strength of the basic solution.

At exactly 20 minutes, a 5ml sample was taken from the beaker incubated in the water bath and added to the test tube.

![BI0796C](images/BI0796C.png)

The contents of the test tube were then immediately transferred into a cuvette and placed in a spectrophotometer set at 420 nm. The percentage transmission of light able to pass through the sample was measured and recorded.

![BI0796D](images/BI0796D.png)

The procedure was then repeated using water baths set at a range of different temperatures. The measurements were recorded in a table and presented in a graph.

![BI0796E](images/BI0796E.png)

![BI0796F](images/BI0796F.png)

1.
 1. State the independent variable in this experiment. [1 mark]

 2. Identify two factors, not already mentioned, that would need to be controlled in this experiment. [1 mark]

2.
 1. Explain how the spectrophotometer is able to measure the breakdown of fat. [3 marks]

 2. Explain the effect of increasing the temperature from 15 :sup[o]C to 35 :sup[o]C on the rate of the catabolic reaction. [2 marks]

3. Predict the percentage transmission for a sample incubated at 20 :sup[o]C. [1 mark]

4. Evaluate the claim that based on the data, the optimum temperature for lipase activity is 35 :sup[o]C. [2 marks]

2.
 1. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ enzyme lipase breaks down fat into fatty acids and glycerol [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ fatty acids are acidic [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ phenolphthalein is pink in basic environments **OR** is pink when added to sodium hydroxide [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the intensity of the pink colour reduces as the pH of the solution decreases [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the pH of the solution can be used as a measure of the concentration of fatty acids in a sample **OR** a lower pH can indicate a higher concentration of fatty acids [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the intensity of the pink colour **OR** the percentage of light transmission can be measured using the spectrophotometer [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ a higher percentage of % of light passes through the sample **OR** a higher light transmission result **OR** a lower colour intensity, correlates to a higher concentration of fatty acids in the sample [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The enzyme lipase breaks down fat into **fatty acids and glycerol** ($\checkmark$). Fatty acids are **acidic** ($\checkmark$) because they have a carboxyl group. When the sample containing the fatty acids is added to the solution with NaOH and phenolphthalein, the intensity of the pink colour will decrease depending on the concentration of fatty acids in the sample. The higher the percentage of **light transmission**, which is measured using the **spectrophotometer** ($\checkmark$), the greater the **concentration of fatty acids** ($\checkmark$).

 2. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ as the temperature increases (from 15 :sup[o]C to 35 :sup[o]C) the kinetic energy of the molecules increases [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as kinetic energy increases there are more successful collisions between the enzyme and the substrate **OR** between lipase and fat [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as temperature increases (from 15 :sup[o]C to 35 :sup[o]C), the rate of reaction increases **OR** rate of fat digestion increases [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ more enzyme-substrate complexes form [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 As the temperature increased from 15 :sup[o]C to 35 :sup[o]C, the **kinetic energy** of the molecules **increased** ($\checkmark$). An increase in the kinetic energy resulted in an increase in the number of **successful collisions** between the enzyme lipase and its substrate fat ($\checkmark$), which in turn, **increased** the rate of reaction ($\checkmark$).

3. (units are required for the mark to be awarded)

 $\ce{ -> }$ 31.5% [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

 20 :sup[o]C is half way between 15 :sup[o]C and 25 :sup[o]C

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ **HOWEVER** the optimum temperature may be between 25 :sup[o]C and 35 :sup[o]C **OR** 35 :sup[o]C and 55 :sup[o]C [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ to find the optimal temperature, more experiments would need to be carried out at narrower increments of temperature around 35 :sup[o]C **OR** to get closer to finding the optimum temperature repeat the experiment at temperatures of 30, 32, 34, 36, 38, 40 :sup[o]C [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ 35 :sup[o]C may be the optimum temperature for lipase activity because in the experiment it yielded the **highest percentage transmission** ($\checkmark$). However, because temperatures between 25 :sup[o]C - 35 :sup[o]C and 35 :sup[o]C - 55 :sup[o]C **were not tested** ($\checkmark$), the optimum temperature could lie between these temperatures.
