# BI0784

**Maximum mark: 3**

Dissolved CO:sub[2] is increasing in standing waters of lakes and ponds. Research has shown this will impact the nutritional quality of phytoplankton (primary producers) and may further affect zooplankton (consumers) that feed on the phytoplankton.

Mesocosms were used to monitor three species of zooplankton, the water flea (*Daphnia magna*), the seed shrimp (*Heterocypris incongruens*) and the rotifer (*Brachionus calyciflorus*) under three different conditions of CO:sub[2] in ponds and shallow lakes:

- Treatment C - present day levels of CO:sub[2], (control)
- Treatment 1 - moderate increase of CO:sub[2] (possible future levels)
- Treatment 2 - high increase of CO:sub[2]  (more extreme future levels)

Glass jars filled with filtered pond water were injected with both carbon dioxide and air under pressure, and the representative species of zooplankton, water flea, seed shrimp or rotifer were added to the jars. They were monitored for survival, growth rate and body size over the next several days.

1. Giving a reason, predict which zooplankton is the least resilient to the effects of increased carbon dioxide.    [1 mark]

2. Describe how using mesocosms for research is both an advantage and a disadvantage in predicting the effects of increasing carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.     [2 marks]

1. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the water flea, because it could not survive treatment T2 or extreme future levels of CO:sub[2] and therefore was the most sensitive  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ both seed shrimp and rotifer could survive treatment T2 for more days and therefore the    water flea is the most sensitive to elevated levels of CO:sub[2]  [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Unlike the seed shrimp and rotifer, **the water flea could not survive treatment T2** ($\checkmark$)  after the first two days and is therefore the most sensitive to future elevated levels of CO:sub[2].

2. Any **one** from each section

    **Advantages:**

    $\ce{ -> }$ with a closed system it is easier to increase levels of CO:sub[2]     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mesocosms can be monitored to see the effects of elevated CO2    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mesocosms will yield results in a short period of time     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mesocosms allow small scale experiments to be set up    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reproducibility - can do repeats more easily to collect more data    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ costs - can be cheaper to set up [1 mark]

    **Disadvantages:**

    $\ce{ -> }$ it is not possible to simulate all that happens in the environment     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ it is unethical to add larger organisms to a mesocosm    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ it can be difficult to set up mesocosms and monitor the changes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ often short term experiments do not capture the long term effects of changes in carbon dioxide [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    An advantage is that it is **easier to increase levels of CO:sub[2] in a closed system** ($\checkmark$) like a mesocosm to study the effects. A disadvantage is that it is **not possible to simulate** ($\checkmark$) exactly all that happens in the environment.
