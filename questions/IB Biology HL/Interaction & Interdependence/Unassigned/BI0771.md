4. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ at a higher concentration of protein (25mM), initially peptides are made at a faster rate as there is a greater chance of successful collisions between substrate and enzyme **OR** at a lower concentration of protein (5mM), initially peptides are made at a comparably slower rate as there is a lower chance of successful collisions between enzyme and substrate [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ for all concentrations of protein, there is a faster rate of reaction within the first 6 minutes compared to the latter half of the reaction, as there is a higher concentration of substrate available to bind to the enzyme [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ for all concentrations of protein, the reaction rate slows down as the substrate reduces in concentration and the concentration of product increases [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ The highest concentration of protein (25mM) produces the highest concentration of peptides because there is higher initial concentration of substrate so more product can be made **OR** the lowest concentration of protein (5mM) produces the lowest concentration of peptides because there is a lower initial concentration of substrate so less product can be made [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 With the **highest concentration** of protein (25mM), peptides are initially made at a **faster rate** compared to the lower concentrations, as there is a great chance of **successful collisions** ($\checkmark$) between substrate and enzyme. For all concentrations of protein, there is a **faster rate of reaction within the first 6 minutes** compared to the latter half of the reaction, as there is **more substrate available to bind to the enzyme** ($\checkmark$). However, as all of the reactions progress, the reaction rate begins to **slow down** and eventually become stops, as the concentration of **substrate reduces** and the concentration of **product increases** ($\checkmark$).
