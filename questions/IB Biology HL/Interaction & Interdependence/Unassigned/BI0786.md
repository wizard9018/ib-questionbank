# BI0786

**Maximum mark: 10**

Cancer studies have shown that many tumours have abnormal patterns of aerobic respiration, showing increased levels of glycolysis and lower levels of oxidative phosphorylation, even in the presence of high oxygen levels. A breast cancer cell line (MDA-MB-231) shows variable protein expression of complex III in the electron transport chain compared to normal breast tissue.

![BI0786](images/BI0786.png)
[Source: Adapted from : Owens, K. M., Kulawiec, M., Desouki, M. M., Vanniarajan, A., & Singh, K. K. (2011). *Impaired Oxphos Complex III in breast cancer. PLoS ONE, 6(8)*. Retrieved April 1, 2023, from https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023846 Copyright free]

![BI0786CHGCOR](images/BI0786CHGCOR.png)

1. State the independent variable in this study. [1 mark]

2. Outline the role of the complexes in the electron transport chain. [2 marks]

3. Suggest one advantage and one disadvantage of breast cancer cells using glycolysis to generate ATP rather than oxidative phosphorylation. [2 marks]

4. Predict, with a reason, what would happen to the levels of oxidative phosphorylation in the non-cancerous cells if they were treated with an inhibitor of glycolysis. [1 mark]
5. Explain why non-cancerous breast cancer cells were used as a control in this study. [1 mark]

6. The researchers concluded that downregulation of complex III in breast cancer cells is a cause of breast cancer. Evaluate this conclusion. [2 marks]

7. Outline one measurement that could be taken to determine the activity of Complex III in this study. [1 mark]

1. any **one** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ the type of cell [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The independent variable is whether the **cell type** ($\checkmark$) is cancerous or not.

2. any **two** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ oxidises reduced NAD/NADH **OR** oxidises reduced FAD/FADH:sub[2] [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ releases electrons [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ electrons move from complex to complex, releasing energy **OR** electrons are passed along complexes by redox reactions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ energy released is used to pump protons from the matrix to the intermembrane space [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ protons pass through ATP synthase down a concentration gradient **OR** using facilitated diffusion releasing energy, which is used to phosphorylate ADP [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ last complex **OR** complex IV, passes electrons onto oxygen **OR** reduces oxygen, so more electrons can enter electron transport chain [1 mark]
[2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The complexes **oxidise reduced NAD** ($\checkmark$), which **releases electrons** ($\checkmark$). The electrons move from complex to complex, **releasing energy** ($\checkmark$) which is used to **pump protons** from the matrix into the **intermembrane space** ($\checkmark$). This creates a **concentration gradient**, so protons move down the concentration gradient, through ATP synthase releasing energy which is used to **phosphorylate ADP to ATP** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **two** of the following (*answer must include **one** disadvantage and **one** advantage to gain full marks*):

 ***Advantage***

 $\ce{ -> }$ glycolysis releases ATP more quickly than oxidative phosphorylation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ glycolysis is able to occur in the presence and absence of oxygen, whereas oxidative phosphorylation requires oxygen [1 mark]

 ***Disadvantage***

 $\ce{ -> }$ glycolysis releases less energy per glucose molecule than oxidative phosphorylation **OR** glycolysis releases 2 ATP per glucose molecule whereas oxidative phosphorylation releases 36-38 ATP per glucose molecule [1 mark]
[2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 One advantage of breast cancer cells using glycolysis rather than oxidative phosphorylation to generate ATP is that **glycolysis generates ATP faster** than oxidative phosphorylation ($\checkmark$). One disadvantage is that **glycolysis releases less ATP per glucose molecule** than oxidative phosphorylation ($\checkmark$).

4. any **one** of the following (*answer must include a reason to gain one mark*):

 $\ce{ -> }$ decrease **BECAUSE** oxidative phosphorylation relies on the production of pyruvate from glycolysis [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The levels of oxidative phosphorylation would decrease, because oxidative phosphorylation **relies on the production of pyruvate** from glycolysis ($\checkmark$).

5. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ indicates that any differences between the two cell types are only due to cancer [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 Non-cancerous breast cancer cells were used as a control in this study as this indicates that any **differences between the two cell types are only due to cancer** ($\checkmark$).

6. any **two** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** reason for and **one** against to gain full marks*):

 ***Reasons for***

 $\ce{ -> }$ there is a clear difference between expression of complex III in the breast cancer cell line compared to the non-cancerous cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ altered respiration in the cancer cell line can contribute to uncontrolled tumour growth [1 mark]

 ***Reasons against***

 $\ce{ -> }$ study shows correlation not causation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ study was only conducted on one cell line [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ there may be other causes of breast cancer [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ there is no information on what other variables are being controlled [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The study shows the downregulation of complex III may be a cause of breast cancer as **altered respiration** in the cancer cell line can contribute to **uncontrolled tumour growth** ($\checkmark$), however, there may be **another factor** which is causing uncontrolled cell division ($\checkmark$). The study was conducted on only **one cell line** ($\checkmark$) so it is unknown whether other cancer cell lines also have reduced levels of complex III.
