3. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ during the summer (in the northern hemisphere), **OR** spring (in the northern hemisphere) **OR** growing season, an increase in vegetation **OR** biomass **OR** temperature causes an increase in photosynthesis which results in increased fixation of carbon dioxide, causing a decrease in atmospheric concentration     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ during the autumn (in the northern hemisphere) **OR** winter (in the northern hemisphere), there is a decrease in photosynthesis and an increase in respiration due to cooler temperatures which results in an increase of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vegetation decay  during the dormant season **OR** autumn **OR** winter (in the northern hemisphere) and carbon dioxide is released        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fluctuations in ocean carbon exchange in response to temperature changes **OR** cooler temperatures more carbon dioxide absorbed by oceans, reducing atmospheric carbon dioxide **OR** warmer temperatures ocean releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The annual fluctuations in atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations are primarily driven by the **seasonal patterns** of **photosynthesis** and **respiration in plants**.  In the northern hemisphere, there is an **increase** in **plant growth** in the **summer**, which **increases carbon dioxide fixation** by **photosynthesis** ($\checkmark$). In **winter**, there is a **decrease in photosynthesis**, so **reduced carbon dioxide uptake** by plants ($\checkmark$).
