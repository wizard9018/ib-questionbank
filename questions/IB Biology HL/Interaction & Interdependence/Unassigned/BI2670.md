# BI2670

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how other organic molecules are formed using glucose produced in the Calvin cycle and substances absorbed from the soil.        [4 marks]

2. Describe the overall role of ATP and NADPH in the Calvin cycle.            [4 marks]

3. Explain how light energy is used to produce the molecules required in the light-independent reactions.            [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ fatty acids  **OR** amino acids can be synthesised from glycerate-3-phosphate **OR** triose phosphate         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mineral ions from the soil are required for the synthesis of some organic molecules    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phosphorus for synthesis of  phospholipids **OR** nucleotide **OR** ATP **OR** nucleic acids **OR** DNA **OR** RNA comes from phosphate ions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nitrogen for synthesis of amino acids **OR** proteins comes from nitrate **OR** ammonium ions [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sulfur for synthesis of some amino acids **OR** proteins comes from sulfate ions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ carbon fixed during the Calvin cycle provides the carbon backbone for biosynthetic pathways [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Triose phosphate** from the Calvin cycle can be used to make **fatty acids** or **amino acids** ($\checkmark$). **Phosphate ions** absorbed from the **soil** are needed to make **ATP** and other **nucleotides** ($\checkmark$). **Nitrate** from the soil provides **nitrogen** for making **amino acids** and **proteins** ($\checkmark$). The **carbon backbone** for these molecules comes from **carbon dioxide fixed** during the Calvin cycle ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP provides energy for converting glycerate 3-phosphate **OR** GP to triose phosphate **OR** TP            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ NADPH reduces glycerate 3-phosphate **OR** GP to triose phosphate **OR** TP by donating electrons AND a hydrogen ion    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP is also used to regenerate ribulose bisphosphate **OR** RuBP from triose phosphate **OR** TP            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ for each $\ce{CO_2}$ fixed, 3 ATP and 2 NADPH are used        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ to make one glucose molecule, 18 ATP and 12 NADPH are required [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ resulting ADP and $\ce{NADP^+}$ return to the light-dependent reactions so more ATP and NADPH can be produced        [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **ATP** provides **energy** to convert **glycerate 3-phosphate** (GP) into **triose phosphate** (TP) ($\checkmark$). **NADPH reduces GP to TP** by donating two **electrons** and a **hydrogen ion** ($\checkmark$). **ATP** is also required to **regenerate ribulose bisphosphate** (RuBP) **from TP** ($\checkmark$). For each molecule of **carbon dioxide fixed, 3 ATP** and **2 NADPH** are **used** in the Calvin cycle ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ light energy is absorbed by pigments **OR** chlorophyll **OR** accessory pigments in photosystems **OR** photosystems I **OR** II        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ photosystems are photosynthetic pigment complexes **OR** protein-pigment complexes embedded  in thylakoid membranes            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ absorption of light excites electrons in the reaction centre of photosystem **OR** excites electrons in chlorophyll a    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ excited electrons from Photosystem II are transferred through an electron transport chain [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy released from electrons is used to pump hydrogen ions **OR** protons **OR**  $\ce{H^+}$  into thylakoid space                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ an electrochemical gradient **OR** proton **OR** hydrogen ions **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ gradient is built up across thylakoid membrane                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ protons **OR** hydrogen ions **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ diffuse back into stroma down their concentration gradient **OR** electrochemical gradient through ATP synthase **OR** by chemiosmosis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ flow of protons **OR** hydrogen ions **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ **OR** chemiosmosis brings about the phosphorylation of ADP to form ATP **OR** brings about the production of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ photolysis splits water into electrons **AND** protons **OR** hydrogen ions  **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ **AND** oxygen                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electrons from photolysis replace those lost from Photosystem II         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ protons **OR** hydrogen ions **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ from photolysis are used in chemiosmosis **OR** in the reduction of $\ce{NADP^+}$ **OR** the production of NADPH            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ light re-excites **OR** also excites electrons in Photosystem I     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electrons from Photosystem I are used to reduce $\ce{NADP^+}$ to NADPH [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ $\ce{NADP^+}$ accepts two electrons **AND** one proton **OR** hydrogen ion **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ to form NADPH    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP and NADPH are the molecules required for the light-independent reactions    [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Light energy** is **absorbed** by **pigments** in **photosystems I and II** embedded in the thylakoid membranes ($\checkmark$).  This **energy excites electrons** in **chlorophyll a** at the **reaction centre** of each photosystem  ($\checkmark$).

    **Electrons** from **Photosystem II** are transferred through an **electron transport chain**  ($\checkmark$). The **energy released** is used to **pump protons** ($\ce{H^+}$) into the **thylakoid space**, creating a **proton gradient** ($\checkmark$). **Protons** move **back into the stroma** through **ATP synthase**, driving the production of **ATP from ADP** and **inorganic phosphate**  ($\checkmark$). **Water** is **split** in **photolysis**, producing **electrons, protons** ($\ce{H^+}$), and **oxygen**  ($\checkmark$). The **electrons** from photolysis **replace** those lost from **Photosystem II**  ($\checkmark$). **Light** also **excites electrons** in **Photosystem I**  ($\checkmark$).  These electrons are used to **reduce** $\mathbf{NADP^+}$ **to NADPH**  ($\checkmark$).
