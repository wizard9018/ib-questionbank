# BI0062

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast anaerobic respiration in muscle and yeast cells. [3 marks]

2. Outline the role of ATP in cells. [5 marks]

3. Explain how a respirometer could be used to investigate the effect of temperature on the rate of respiration.     [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference*):

   ***Similarities***

   $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** processes occur in the cytoplasm and do not require oxygen [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** involve glycolysis, producing a net yield of two ATP molecules per glucose molecule [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** regenerate NAD from reduced NAD, allowing glycolysis to continue [1 mark]

   ***Differences***

   $\ce{ -> }$ in muscle cells, lactate is produced, **WHEREAS** in yeast cells, ethanol and carbon dioxide are produced **OR** in muscle cells, pyruvate is directly reduced to lactate, **WHEREAS** in yeast cells, pyruvate is first decarboxylated (to ethanal) then reduced to ethanol [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ in muscle cells, pyruvate accepts hydrogen from reduced NAD, **WHEREAS** in yeast cells, ethanal accepts hydrogen from reduced NAD [1 mark]

[3 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:

Both muscle and yeast cells carry out anaerobic respiration **in the cytoplasm without requiring oxygen** ($\checkmark$). Both processes involve **glycolysis, producing a net yield of two ATP molecules per glucose molecule** ($\checkmark$). In both cells, **reduced NAD is oxidised to regenerate NAD, allowing glycolysis to continue** ($\checkmark$). However, in muscle cells, **pyruvate is reduced directly to lactate, whereas in yeast cells, pyruvate is first decarboxylated to ethanal and then reduced to ethanol, releasing carbon dioxide** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

   $\ce{ -> }$ ATP is the molecule that distributes energy within cells [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and inorganic phosphate / Pi releases a small / usable amount of energy [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ ATP can transfer a phosphate group to another molecule, changing its properties / reactivity / stability, enabling cellular processes [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ ATP is regenerated from ADP and Pi using energy released by cellular respiration [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ most ATP during aerobic respiration is produced by ATP synthase in the inner mitochondrial membrane **OR** by chemiosmosis [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ protons flow through ATP synthase down their concentration gradient, providing energy to phosphorylate ADP [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ used for active transport across membranes **OR** suitable example [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ used for synthesis of macromolecules **OR** anabolism **OR** suitable example [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ used for movement of the whole cell **OR** cell components **OR** suitable example [1 mark]

[5 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:

**Hydrolysis of ATP** to **ADP** and **inorganic phosphate** releases a small, **usable amount of energy** ($\checkmark$). ATP is **regenerated** from **ADP and Pi** during cellular respiration ($\checkmark$), mostly by **ATP synthase** in the **inner mitochondrial membrane** ($\checkmark$), using energy from the **flow of protons down their concentration gradient (chemiosmosis)** ($\checkmark$). ATP provides energy for **active transport** ($\checkmark$) and **anabolic reactions** such as **protein synthesis** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

   $\ce{ -> }$ respirometer containing germinating seeds **OR** invertebrates with an alkali / soda lime / potassium hydroxide to absorb carbon dioxide [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ oxygen uptake reduces the volume / pressure of gas so the coloured liquid in the manometer moves towards the organisms [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ temperature is the independent variable, set using water baths [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ a range of at least five temperatures is used (eg. 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 °C) [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ apparatus left to equilibrate at each temperature before readings are taken [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ distance moved by the liquid is measured over a fixed time [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ volume of oxygen consumed is calculated using the cross-sectional area of the capillary tube multiplied by the distance moved / using $\pi r^2h$ [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ rate of oxygen uptake is calculated by dividing the volume of oxygen consumed by the time taken **OR** rate expressed per unit mass (eg. cm³ O₂ g⁻¹ min⁻¹) [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ controlled variables / named controlled variable kept constant [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ control respirometer with glass beads / dead seeds of equal volume at each temperature to account for changes in gas pressure unrelated to respiration [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ at least three repeats at each temperature and mean calculated [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ mean respiration rate is plotted against temperature to identify the relationship between temperature and respiration rate [1 mark]

[8 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:

A **respirometer** is set up containing **germinating seeds** and an **alkali such as soda lime to absorb carbon dioxide** produced during respiration ($\checkmark$). As the seeds respire, **oxygen is taken up**, so the **gas pressure in the tube decreases and the coloured liquid in the manometer moves towards the seeds** ($\checkmark$). **Temperature is the independent variable** and is controlled using **thermostatically controlled water baths** ($\checkmark$). A **range of five temperatures** is used, eg. 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 °C ($\checkmark$), and at each temperature the apparatus is left to **equilibrate** before readings are taken ($\checkmark$). The **distance moved by the liquid in a fixed time** is measured ($\checkmark$), and the **volume of oxygen consumed is calculated using the cross-sectional area of the capillary tube** ($\checkmark$). A **control tube containing glass beads** is used at each temperature to correct for changes in gas volume caused by temperature ($\checkmark$). Measurements are **repeated at each temperature three times**, the **mean calculated** ($\checkmark$), and the **results are plotted against temperature** ($\checkmark$).
