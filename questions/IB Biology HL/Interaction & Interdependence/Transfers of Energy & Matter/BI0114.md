# BI0114

Which of the following statements is **NOT** a **TRUE** description of energy flow in terrestrial ecosystems?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Trophic relationships between plants and animals do not exist as a simple chain, but tend to be more elaborate and interwoven as a food web.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ In food webs, different herbivores may feed on the same plant. Also, the same species of herbivore may feed on different plants.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Some consumers that feed on the dead remains of plants and animals are called detritivores.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ In food webs, a typical food chain consists of approximately 6 organisms.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is not a true statement as typically, food chains in ecosystems tend not to be very long due to energy loss at each trophic level. Approximately 90% of the energy is lost through metabolic processes and as heat at each level, leaving only about 10% available to the next level. This energy loss limits the number of trophic levels (and hence the number of organisms in a straightforward food chain) to usually fewer than 6. Most food chains have between 3 and 5 trophic levels.

**Choice A** is a true statement. In nature, trophic relationships (i.e., feeding relationships between organisms) often form complex networks rather than simple, linear chains. These networks are termed food webs, and they illustrate the multiple feeding connections (who eats whom) in ecosystems.

**Choice B** is a true statement. Food webs demonstrate the diversity of feeding relationships in an ecosystem. It is common for multiple herbivores to feed on the same plant species and for individual herbivores to consume various types of plants. This flexibility increases the stability and resilience of the ecosystem.

**Choice C** is a true statement. Detritivores, such as earthworms and certain insects, play a critical role in ecosystems by breaking down dead organic material. This process helps in nutrient cycling and energy flow through the ecosystem, facilitating decomposition and the release of nutrients back into the soil for use by plants.
