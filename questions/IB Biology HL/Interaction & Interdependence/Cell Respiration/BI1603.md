# BI1603

Metabolic reactions can be exothermic, releasing energy to their surroundings, or endothermic, requiring an energy supply.

Which one of the following is an endothermic reaction?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The conversion of triose phosphate to pyruvate

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The conversion of glucose to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The oxidation of glyceraldehyde by NAD:sup[+], in glycolysis

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The decarboxylation of citrate

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The conversion of glucose to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate occurs during glycolysis and involves two phosphorylation steps that require the input of ATP. Glucose is first converted to glucose-6-phosphate and then to fructose-6-phosphate before being phosphorylated again to form fructose-1,6-bisphosphate. These steps require energy in the form of ATP; **Choice B** is, therefore, correct.

The conversion of triose phosphate to pyruvate also occurs during glycolysis. Each triose phosphate molecule undergoes a series of enzyme-catalysed reactions, producing ATP and NADH while being oxidised to form pyruvate. This reaction is exothermic so **Choice A** is not the correct answer to this question.

The oxidation of glyceraldehyde by NAD+, in glycolysis is exothermic. This step also results in the reduction of NAD+ to NADH, which stores some of the energy released during the reaction; **Choice C** is not the correct answer to this question.

The decarboxylation of citrate occurs in the Krebs cycle, where citrate is converted to a 5-carbon molecule, producing carbon dioxide and forming NADH by transferring high-energy electrons to $\ce{NAD^+}$. The breaking of bonds and the formation of NADH release more energy than is required, resulting in a net release of energy. This reaction is exothermic, so **Choice D** is not the correct answer to this question.
