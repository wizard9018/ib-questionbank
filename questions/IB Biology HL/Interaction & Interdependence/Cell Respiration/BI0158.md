# BI0158

The diagram shows part of the electron transport chain.

![BI0158](images/BI0158.jpg)

What explains the role of oxygen?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Oxygen loses electrons and is oxidised to form water.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ As oxygen is reduced by electrons protons also bind, forming water and maintaining a proton gradient.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Oxygen stops the electron transport for ATP production.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Oxygen allows protons to diffuse into the matrix.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Electrons are carried by the electron carriers coenzyme NADH (and FADH:sub[2]) from the Krebs cycle, which occurs in the matrix, to the cristae of the mitochondrion, where the electron transport chain takes place. NADH (and FADH:sub[2]) reduce protein complexes on the cristae by adding electrons to them.

Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor. Oxygen gains electrons, and this is known as reduction, not oxidation (**Choice A**).

The chemical potential energy supplied by the proton gradient helps the protons to diffuse back to the matrix from the intermembrane space via ATP synthase and not through oxygen (**Choice D**).

When oxygen is available, a high yield of ATP is produced (**Choice C**). Hence, electron transport only stops in the absence of oxygen.
