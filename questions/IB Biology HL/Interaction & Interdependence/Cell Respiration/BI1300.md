# BI1300

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how the structure of ATP is related to its function. [5 marks]

2. Explain how temperature affects the rate of respiration, with reference to enzymes.    [4 marks]

3. Compare and contrast anaerobic respiration in mice (*Mus musculus*) and yeast.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following (*answer must link structure to function to gain full marks*):

    $\ce{ -> }$ nucleotide made up of adenine **OR** nitrogenous base, **AND** a ribose **OR** pentose sugar, **AND** three phosphate groups    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cannot pass freely through the plasma membrane **SO** is retained inside the cell where it is needed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis of the terminal phosphate group releases energy (approximately 30 kJ per mole) **SO** energy is available immediately when needed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ small relative to macromolecules **AND** soluble **SO** can diffuse freely within the cytoplasm to where energy is needed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ soluble **OR** highly reactive **SO** can interact readily with other molecules **OR** participate in chemical reactions **OR** can easily be utilised by cells **OR** can react with other metabolites    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can lose a phosphate to make ADP, **AND** ADP can be phosphorylated to make ATP    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis and phosphorylation means it is reusable **OR** recycled    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can transfer a phosphate group to other molecules (phosphorylation) **SO** they become more reactive **OR** less stable    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    ATP is a **nucleotide** consisting of a nitrogenous **base (adenine)**, a **ribose** and **three phosphate groups** ($\checkmark$). It can easily **lose a phosphate group** via **hydrolysis** to **release energy** ($\checkmark$), and **ADP** can be **phosphorylated** back to **ATP**, making it **reusable** ($\checkmark$). The **polar** phosphate groups make ATP **soluble** in water, allowing it to **diffuse freely** ($\checkmark$) within cells and enabling it to readily **participate in chemical reactions** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ as temperature rises, the rate of respiration increases as enzymes **AND** substrates have more kinetic energy **OR** at lower temperatures, the rate of respiration is slower as enzymes **AND** substrates have less kinetic energy    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ so there is a higher chance of successful collisions between enzymes and substrates    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ until an optimum temperature is reached, at which the enzyme exhibits its maximum activity    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at optimum temperature, the kinetic energy of the molecules is sufficiently high to promote a high level of enzyme-substrate interaction **BUT** not so high to cause extensive denaturation of enzyme    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when the temperature is higher than the optimum, the rate of respiration begins to decrease as enzymes involved become denatured **OR** the active site changes shape so the substrate no longer fits    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    At **lower temperatures**, the rate of respiration is **slower** because the **enzymes** involved have **less kinetic energy** ($\checkmark$), so there is a **lower chance** of successful **collisions** with **substrates** ($\checkmark$). As **temperatures rise**, the **rate** of respiration **increases**, as the enzymes involved have **more kinetic energy** ($\checkmark$) until an **optimum** temperature is reached, where the rate of respiration is **highest** ($\checkmark$). **Above** this **optimum** temperature, the **respiration rate decreases** as the **enzymes** start to become **denatured** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to gain full marks*):

    *Differences*\
    $\ce{ -> }$ the product of anaerobic respiration in mice is lactic acid **OR** lactate **WHEREAS**, in yeast, it is ethanol and carbon dioxide    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ yeast is better adapted to long-term anaerobic respiration **WHEREAS** mouse cells can perform anaerobic respiration but are limited in endurance    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ yeast converts pyruvate to ethanal, releasing carbon dioxide, then ethanal to ethanol **WHEREAS** mice convert pyruvate directly to lactate with no carbon dioxide released    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lactate can be converted back to pyruvate **OR** glucose when oxygen is available **WHEREAS** ethanol and carbon dioxide are released from yeast cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ products of yeast are used commercially, ethanol in brewing **AND** carbon dioxide in baking, **WHEREAS** lactate in mice is produced in muscles during vigorous activity    [1 mark]

    *Similarities* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** use glucose as a substrate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** occur in the cytoplasm    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** occur in the absence **OR** shortage of oxygen    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the products of anaerobic respiration in **BOTH** mice and yeast can be toxic if they build up    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can occur alongside aerobic respiration    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** produce a small amount of ATP **OR** 2 ATPs    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anaerobic respiration in **BOTH** mice and yeast allows ATP production to continue when oxygen is insufficient    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** begin with glycolysis, in which glucose is converted to pyruvate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in **BOTH**, reduced NAD is oxidised to regenerate NAD for glycolysis    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The product of anaerobic respiration in mice is **lactate**, whereas in yeast, it is **ethanol and carbon dioxide** ($\checkmark$). However, in both the anaerobic respiration of mice and yeast, **glucose** is used as a **substrate** ($\checkmark$), it occurs in the **cytoplasm** ($\checkmark$), produces a **small** amount of **ATP** ($\checkmark$) and in the **absence of oxygen** ($\checkmark$). Also, in both mice and yeast, if the **products** of anaerobic respiration **build up**, they can be **toxic** to the cell ($\checkmark$), and anaerobic respiration can occur **alongside aerobic respiration** ($\checkmark$).
