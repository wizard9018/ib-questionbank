# BI1636

**Maximum mark: 5**

The diagram shows the structure responsible for ATP production toward the end of aerobic respiration in humans.

![BI1636](images/BI1636.png)

1. State where the ATP synthase, represented in the diagram, is located within a cell. [1 mark]

2. Describe how a proton gradient is established in the electron transport chain. [2 marks]

3. Outline what happens to protons after they have been used to generate ATP. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. \
 $\ce{ -> }$ inner mitochondrial membrane **OR** cristae of mitochondria [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The ATP synthase involved in aerobic respiration is found in the **inner mitochondrial membrane** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ electrons from reduced NAD/FAD transferred to protein carriers in the electron transport chain [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as electrons are transferred between carriers, energy is released [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ carriers act as proton pumps [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ protons **OR** hydrogen ions **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ transferred from matrix to intermembrane space [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ using active transport **OR** against the concentration gradient [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ high concentration of hydrogen ions **OR** protons maintained in intermembrane space **OR** electrochemical gradient established between intermembrane space and matrix [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Electrons** from **reduced NAD** are **transferred between electron carriers** in the inner **membrane, releasing energy** ($\checkmark$). The energy is used for the **active transport** ($\checkmark$) of **protons** from the mitochondrial **matrix** to the **intermembrane space** ($\checkmark$), generating a proton gradient.

3. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ protons **OR** hydrogen ions **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ transferred back into **OR** accumulate in the matrix [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ oxygen functions as the final electron acceptor [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ oxygen binds with hydrogen ions **OR** protons **OR** $\ce{H^+}$ and electrons, forming water [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Hydrogen ions** that have passed through ATP synthase **build up** in the mitochondrial **matrix** ($\checkmark$). **Oxygen**, the **final electron acceptor** ($\checkmark$) of the electron transport chain, **binds to hydrogen ions** and **electrons**, **forming water** ($\checkmark$).
