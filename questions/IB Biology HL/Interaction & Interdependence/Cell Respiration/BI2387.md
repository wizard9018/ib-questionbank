# BI2387

Which term best describes the chemical conversion step undergone by pyruvate prior to the Krebs cycle?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Reductive carboxylation

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Reductive decarboxylation

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Oxidative carboxylation

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Oxidative decarboxylation

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA in the link reaction involves both oxidation (removal of electrons) and decarboxylation (loss of a carbon atom as $\ce{CO_2}$). This step aligns with the overall oxidative nature of cellular respiration, where glucose and its intermediates are oxidised progressively. Pyruvate (3 carbons) is oxidised and decarboxylated to form acetate (2 carbons), which then combines with coenzyme A to produce acetyl-CoA.

**Choice A** and **B** are incorrect because the process is oxidative, not reductive. While the reduction of $\ce{NAD^+}$ to NADH occurs alongside pyruvate oxidation (as part of the redox process), pyruvate itself undergoes oxidation, not reduction.

**Choice C** is incorrect because pyruvate is decarboxylated, meaning it loses a carbon atom as $\ce{CO_2}$. Carboxylation refers to the addition of a carbon atom, which does not occur in this step.
