# BI0160

In respiration which process does **not** involve the conversion of ADP to ATP?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Reformation of oxaloacetate from a 5-carbon compound in the Krebs cycle

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA in the link reaction

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Glycolysis

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Rotation of ATP synthase

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
ADP combines with inorganic phosphate to form ATP, which is the usable form of energy. This conversion takes place during three stages of cellular respiration; glycolysis, Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

In glycolysis, glucose is converted into two pyruvate molecules (**Choice C**). This  involves a net production of 2 molecules of ATP.

The next stage after glycolysis is the link reaction  (**Choice B**). Here the decarboxylation of pyruvate happens, producing acetyl-CoA and reduced NADH. This reaction does not involve any conversion of ADP to ATP.

In part of the Krebs cycle, a 5-carbon compound is decarboxylated and, through a series of reactions, the 4-carbon compound oxaloacetate is reformed (**Choice A**). This involves the production of 1 molecule of ATP.

On the cristae of mitochondria, ATP synthase rotates (**Choice D**) as protons diffuse from the intermembrane space to the matrix of the mitochondria (or from the thylakoids to the stroma in chloroplasts) and converts ADP to ATP.
