# BI0522

The diagram shows the pathway that converts threonine to isoleucine.

![BI0522FC](images/BI0522FC.png)

What is the name and mechanism of the process through which isoleucine interacts with threonine dehydratase?

| | Name | Mechanism |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Competitive inhibition | Isoleucine binds to the active site on the enzyme. |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Competitive inhibition | Isoleucine binds to an allosteric site on the enzyme. |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | End-product inhibition | Isoleucine binds to the active site on the enzyme. |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | End-product inhibition | Isoleucine binds to an allosteric site on the enzyme. |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The diagram shows that threonine undergoes a series of enzyme-catalyzed reactions to produce isoleucine. At the end of this pathway, if isoleucine accumulates it inhibits threonine dehydratase. So, the product of the pathway inhibits the conversion of threonine to isoleucine. This is an example of end-product inhibition. Isoleucine inhibits the enzyme in the pathway by binding to an allosteric site on the enzyme, not the active site. The binding of isoleucine changes the conformation of the enzyme such that the active site is no longer able to bind to threonine. **Choice D** is correct.

In competitive inhibition (**Choices A** and **B**), a substance binds to the active site on the enzyme to prevent the substrate from binding. In end-product inhibition, the product binds to the allosteric site, not the active site (**Choice C**).
