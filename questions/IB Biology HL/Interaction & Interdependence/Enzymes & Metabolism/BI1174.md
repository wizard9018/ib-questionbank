# BI1174

Below are four graphs with the solid lines showing the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction with increasing substrate concentrations, in the absence of an inhibitor. The dashed lines show possible rates of reaction in the presence of a non-competitive inhibitor. Which graph correctly shows the effect of a non-competitive inhibitor on reaction rate?

![BI1174](images/BI1174.png)

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A non-competitive inhibitor has a dissimilar shape to the substrate and binds to the allosteric site of an enzyme, which causes a conformational change in the enzyme so that the substrate can no longer bind. This means that some of the enzyme molecules are unable to bind to the substrate regardless of the substrate concentration, so the reaction rate will remain below the uninhibited enzyme; **Choice B** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect as the relationship between substrate concentration and rate of reaction is not linear, and the rate of reaction with a non-competitive inhibitor will not reach the rate of an uninhibited enzyme. A competitive inhibitor is an inhibitor with a similar shape to a substrate that reversibly binds to the active site of an enzyme, decreasing the rate of reaction. However, increasing the concentration of substrate will overcome the effect of a competitive inhibitor. The dashed line in graph C represents a competitive inhibitor; **Choice C** is incorrect. Graph D suggests that an inhibitor increases the rate of reaction compared to an uninhibited enzyme; **Choice D** is incorrect.
