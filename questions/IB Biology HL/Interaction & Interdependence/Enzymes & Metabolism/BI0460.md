# BI0460

Malate is converted to oxaloacetate (OAA) by the enzyme malate dehydrogenase. It is an example of metabolic reaction in the mitochondria.

![BI0460FC](images/BI0460FC.png)
[Huai, K. (2017, February 18). *File: Krebs Cycle 8 L-Malate to oxaloacetate.svg*. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved May 15, 2022. From https ://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Krebs_cycle_8_l-malate_to_oxaloacetate.svg Copyright under CC-BY-SA.]

Which of the following is applicable to this reaction?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ The enzyme is not affected by higher temperatures and extreme pH conditions.
- B. $\hspace{1em}$    The enzyme can catalyze both forward and backward reactions.
- C. $\hspace{1em}$    With increasing enzyme concentration in the presence of sufficient substrate, there is no change in the reaction rate.

- D. $\hspace{1em}$    The active site for the forward reaction is different from the one for the backward reaction.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Most enzymes can catalyze both forward and backward reactions with the process being dependent on the concentration of substrates, products and other factors. \
Both pH and temperature affect the rate of enzyme activity (**Choice A**). \
Increasing enzyme concentration will increase the rate of reaction (**Choice C**). \
The active site is where the substrate binds and is also where the product is released.  The same site is used in the forward and reverse reactions (**Choice D**).
