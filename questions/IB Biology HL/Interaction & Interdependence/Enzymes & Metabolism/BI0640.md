# BI0640

The Calvin cycle is a series of enzyme catalysed reactions.  A diagram outlining the Calvin cycle is shown below.

![BI0640](images/BI0640.png)
[Source: Adapted from Jones, M. (2010, February 17). *File: Calvin-cycle4.svg*. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved August 8, 2022, from https:// commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Calvin-cycle4.svg. Copyright under CC BY-SA 3.0.]

What is true about the Calvin cycle and most other metabolic pathways?

-    A. $\hspace{1em}$ It\'s usually the same enzyme for all steps in one metabolic pathway.

-    B. $\hspace{1em}$ The product of one step cannot be the substrate for subsequent steps.
-    C. $\hspace{1em}$ Each step of the pathway is catalysed by a different enzyme.
-    D. $\hspace{1em}$ All the enzymes are regulated by competitive inhibitors.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Metabolic pathways consist of either a chain or a series of cyclical reactions where each reaction is catalysed by a different enzyme. \
The same enzyme **is not** used for each reaction in a metabolic pathway (**Choice A**). \
The product of one reaction or step **is** the substrate for the next or subsequent step in the pathway (**Choice B**). \
Although some enzymes are regulated by competitive inhibitors, this is not true for all enzymes in a metabolic pathway (**Choice D**).
