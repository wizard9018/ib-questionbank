# BI1760

**Maximum mark: 5**

The enzyme catalase breaks down hydrogen peroxide, a metabolic waste product, into water and oxygen, preventing potential harm to the cell. One catalase molecule can catabolise six million hydrogen peroxide molecules per minute. Catalase is located within membrane-bound organelles called peroxisomes, where it breaks down hydrogen peroxide.

Students conducted an experiment to investigate how increasing substrate concentration affects catalase activity. They ground 1 g of liver tissue (a tissue rich in peroxisomes) with 5 ml of water and soaked a filter paper disc in this mixture for 5 seconds to absorb catalase. The disc was then placed at the bottom of a tube with 10 ml of 1% hydrogen peroxide solution. The time it took for the disc to rise to the surface of the hydrogen peroxide was recorded. This process was repeated twice more, and the mean time was calculated. The experiment was then repeated with four different hydrogen peroxide concentrations using the same liver mixture. The results are shown in the table below.

| Hydrogen Peroxide Concentration / M| Mean time taken for disc to rise / s | Rate of reaction / s$^{-1}$ |

| :-: | :-: | :-: |

| 0.4 | 62 | 0.016 |

| 0.8 | 41 | 0.024 |

| 1.2 | 13 |  |

| 1.6 | 8 | 0.125 |

| 2.0 | 7 | 0.143 |

1.  Calculate the rate of reaction for the 1.2 M hydrogen peroxide solution.        [1 mark]

2.  In addition to the controlled variables specified in the method, identify **two** other variables that should be kept constant to ensure a fair test in this experiment.    [1 mark]

3.  Describe the relationship between increasing hydrogen peroxide concentration and catalase activity.        [1 mark]

4.  Suggest why catalase is held in peroxisomes and not released directly into the cytoplasm.    [2 marks]
