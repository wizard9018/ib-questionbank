# BI2386

Which of the following does penicillin inhibit the formation of?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Short cross-linking peptides between peptidoglycan polymers

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Transpeptidases

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Glycosidic bonds within peptidoglycan

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Enzyme-antibiotic complexes

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because the disruption of the formation of transpeptides that lie between peptidoglycan's polysaccharide chains weakens the bacterial cell wall and makes it susceptible to rupture via osmotic pressure.

**Choice B** is incorrect because transpeptidases are the enzymes that catalyse transpeptidation, the formation of peptide cross-links. Penicillin binds to and inhibits these enzymes but does not prevent their synthesis.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the main polysaccharide structure of peptidoglycan is unaffected by penicillin. Rather, penicillin disrupts the formation of the short peptides that link peptidoglycan strands together.

**Choice D** is incorrect because penicillin is an antibiotic that forms very effective complexes with the transpeptidase enzyme. The two molecules bond irreversibly, rendering the enzyme ineffective.
