# BI1092

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast intracellular and extracellular chemical reactions, given one example of each type of reaction.     [5 marks]

2. Enzymes are regulated by inhibitors that are classified as competitive or non-competitive.  Distinguish between these two types of inhibitors.     [4 marks]

3. Describe, using an example, how metabolic pathways can be regulated by end-product inhibition.         [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference **AND** **one** example of **each** type of reaction to gain full marks*):

    ***Similarities*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are controlled by enzymes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** types of reactions are tightly regulated    [1 mark]

    ***Differences*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ intracellular reactions occur inside cells **WHEREAS** extracellular reactions occur outside a cell        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ intracellular reactions are more focused on the specific needs of the cell, **WHEREAS** extracellular reactions often involve interactions between cells **OR**, tissues **OR** contribute to broader physiological processes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ intracellular reactions are primarily controlled by cellular mechanisms, **WHEREAS**  extracellular reactions might involve the integration of signals from multiple cells **OR** systems [1 mark]

    ***Examples*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ intracellular example is glycolysis **OR** Krebs cycle **OR** photosynthesis **OR** any other suitable example        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ extracellular example is chemical breakdown in digestive system **OR** any other suitable example [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Intracellular and extracellular chemical reactions are **both tightly regulated** ($\checkmark$) by **enzymes** ($\checkmark$), however, **intracellular** reactions go on **inside cells**, and **extracellular** reactions go on **outside** of cells ($\checkmark$). **Glycolysis** is an example of an **intracellular** reaction ($\checkmark$) as it occurs in the cytoplasm of cells. The **chemical breakdown of food** is an example of **extracellular** reactions ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ competitive inhibitors have a shape similar to that of the substrate **WHILE** non-competitive inhibitors have a completely different shape from the substrate        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ competitive inhibitors bind to the active site **WHILE** non-competitive inhibitors bind to a site other than the active site, **OR** allosteric site     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ competitive inhibitors have no effect on **OR** do not change the shape of the active site **WHILE** binding of non-competitive inhibitors changes the shape of the active site     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in competitive inhibition, increasing substrate concentration can overcome inhibition **WHILE** in non-competitive inhibition increasing substrate concentration will not have this effect     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ example of competitive inhibitor is disulfiram inhibition of alcohol dehydrogenase **WHILE** example of non-competitive inhibitor is isoleucine end-product inhibition of threonine deaminase, **OR** any other valid examples of corresponding inhibitors        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

    | competitive inhibition | non-competitive inhibition |
    | :-: | :-: |
    | substrate and inhibitor have a **similar** shape | substrate and inhibitor have **different** shapes ($\checkmark$) |
    | the inhibitor can **bind to the active** site | inhibitor **binds to an allosteric** site ($\checkmark$) |
    | inhibitor has **no effect on the shape** of the active site | inhibitor **changes the shape of the active site** after binding ($\checkmark$) |
    | increasing substrate concentration **reduces** inhibition | increasing substrate concentration **does not reduce** inhibition ($\checkmark$) |

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the product of the last reaction will inhibit an enzyme at the beginning of the pathway **OR** a clearly drawn and labelled diagram     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the end-product binds to the allosteric site of the enzyme        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the end-product acts as a non-competitive inhibitor        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when bound the inhibitor changes the shape of the active enzyme so the substrate can no longer bind        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ end-product inhibition is reversible         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as the concentration of the end-product decreases inhibition decreases as well, so the pathway can resume             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ end product inhibition is an example of negative feedback inhibition     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ intermediate products **OR** substrates do not build up if there is an excess of the end-product [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ threonine is converted to isoleucine in a series of enzyme-controlled steps **OR** any other valid example of end-product inhibition    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

    ![BI1092](images/BI1092.png)

    In some metabolic pathways, regulation is achieved by end-product inhibition. The product of the last reaction will **inhibit the first enzyme** ($\checkmark$) in the pathway. **Threonine is converted to isoleucine**, and this pathway is an example
($\checkmark$) of end-product inhibition

    The end-product (isoleucine) **binds to the allosteric site** ($\checkmark$) of the first enzyme and changes the shape of the active site of the enzyme so the **substrate can no longer bind** ($\checkmark$).

    Isoleucine acts as a **non-competitive inhibitor** ($\checkmark$). The binding of the **end product is reversible** ($\checkmark$). Once the end-product detaches from the enzyme, the enzyme can once again catalyse reactions.

    End-product inhibition is an **example of negative feedback inhibition** ($\checkmark$). If there is an excess of the end product, **intermediate products do not build up** ($\checkmark$).
