# BI1996

Which type of bonds are disrupted when an enzyme denatures?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Covalent bonds in the substrate.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Hydrogen bonds and ionic interactions in the enzyme.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Peptide bonds in the enzyme.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Disulfide bridges in the substrate.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Denaturation disrupts hydrogen bonds and ionic interactions within the enzyme that maintain its specific three-dimensional (tertiary and quaternary) structure. These changes render the enzyme non-functional as its active site loses its shape. **Choice B** is correct because it specifies the bonds and interactions disrupted during denaturation.

**Choice A** is incorrect as covalent bonds in the substrate are unrelated to enzyme denaturation. Denaturation involves changes in the enzyme, not the substrate.\
**Choice C** is incorrect because peptide bonds in the enzyme are not broken during denaturation; breaking peptide bonds would result in complete hydrolysis of the protein, not just structural changes.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because disulfide bridges in the substrate are irrelevant; the question is asking about the denaturation of enzymes.
