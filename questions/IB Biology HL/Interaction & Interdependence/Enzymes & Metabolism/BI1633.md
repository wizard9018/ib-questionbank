# BI1633

**Maximum mark: 8**

Gaucher disease is a genetic disorder characterised by a defective enzyme, glucocerebrosidase, which normally breaks down lipids in lysosomes. This leads to a buildup of lipids in some organs, including the liver and spleen.

A study was conducted on 12 patients with Gaucher disease who received enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) injections. The graph shows the effect of ERT on liver and spleen size, measured as a percentage of body mass, over 6 months.

![1633aqui](images/1633aqui.png)

1. Calculate the decrease in mean spleen size, as a percentage of body mass, between the beginning and end of the study. [1 mark]

2. Explain why ERT is not a cure for patients with Gaucher disease. [2 marks]

 Substrate reduction therapy (SRT), which uses oral medications to reduce the amount of the lipid glucocerebroside synthesised, can also be used in combination with ERT to treat Gaucher disease. Common side effects of SRT treatment include digestive problems and diarrhoea.

 The graph shows the results of a follow-up study in which four groups of patients were given no treatment, ERT treatment, SRT treatment, or a combination of both ERT and SRT.

 ![BI1633 graph 2](<images/BI1633 graph 2.png>)

3. Using all the data provided, discuss whether a combined ERT and SRT treatment is more effective than ERT or SRT alone. [3 marks]

4. Explain why patients receiving a combined ERT and SRT treatment would not likely benefit from very high doses of glucocerebrosidase. [2 marks]
