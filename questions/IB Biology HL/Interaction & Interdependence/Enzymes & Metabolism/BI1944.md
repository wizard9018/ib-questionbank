# BI1944

Which statements are true for denaturation?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | The product cannot separate from the active site|
    | II. | It is irreversible |
    | III. | It affects enzyme-substrate specificity |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Statement I** is not true because denaturation does not cause the product to remain bound to the active site. Instead, denaturation disrupts the enzyme's tertiary structure, leading to the active site losing its specific shape and function. This means the enzyme-substrate complex cannot occur, and no products are formed.

**Statement II** is true. It is important to distinguish between changes in enzyme activity and denaturation. When temperature increases within the enzyme’s normal working range, activity rises and falls in a reversible manner, so cooling the enzyme restores normal function. However, once the temperature becomes high enough to cause denaturation, the enzyme’s three-dimensional structure is permanently altered. The active site loses its specific shape, hydrophobic regions may aggregate, and the polypeptide cannot spontaneously refold under biological conditions. For almost all enzymes in living systems, heat-induced denaturation is irreversible. A few proteins can refold under special laboratory conditions, but NOT under normal physiological conditions.

**Statement III** is true because denaturation changes the shape of the active site, preventing the enzyme from binding to its specific substrate, which directly affects enzyme-substrate specificity.

**Choice B** is, therefore, correct as it includes only **Statements II** and **III**.
**Choices A, C**, and **D**  are incorrect because they all include **Statement I**.
