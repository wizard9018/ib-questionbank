1. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ measuring cylinder is used to measure equal volumes of substrate, **OR** starch, **AND** equal volumes of enzyme, **OR** amylase, into test tubes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$set up 5 different water baths at a range of at least 5 different temperatures **OR** set up water baths at 0, 20, 40, 60 and 80 :sup[o]C **OR** 5 sensible temperatures given [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ place test tubes containing starch and a test tube containing amylase in each of the water baths to equilibrate **OR** to get to the temperature of the water bath, before mixing together [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ enzyme **OR** amylase, and substrate **OR** starch, are mixed together in test tubes in the water bath for a fixed amount of time **OR** at least 5 minutes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ a variable that must be controlled is the pH **OR**, concentration of substrate **OR** starch **OR** substrate of enzyme **OR** amylase, **OR** the time that the substrate and enzyme are incubated together for in the water bath **OR** the volume of enzyme **OR** the volume of starch suspension [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the presence of starch can be tested for using iodine solution [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ a positive test for starch is iodine solution turning dark blue **OR** black [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 To study the effect of temperature on amylase activity, the **measuring cylinder** can be used to measure an **equal volume of starch suspension** and an **equal volume of amylase** ($\checkmark$) into individual test tubes. Five different water baths should be set up with a **range of at least five temperatures** ($\checkmark$) and a test tube containing starch and a test tube containing amylase, should be placed in each water bath to **equilibrate** before being mixed together ($\checkmark$). Next, the amylase and starch should be **mixed together** and incubated at different temperatures for a **fixed time** ($\checkmark$). The presence of starch can be tested for using **iodine solution** ($\checkmark$), which turns dark **blue or black** in the presence of starch ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ take samples from the mixtures in each water bath at regular intervals **AND** test the solution with iodine solution **OR** place samples of the mixtures on a spotting tile at regular time intervals **AND** test the solution with iodine solution [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ record time when the iodine solution stays orange **OR** record time when the iodine solution no longer changes colour **OR** record time when no starch remains [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ calculate the rate of starch breakdown by taking the reciprocal of the time interval for each sample **OR** the rate can be calculated using the formula; rate = 1/ time [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The rate of reaction of the breakdown of starch by amylase can be measured by taking samples of the **mixture** of amylase and starch, from **each temperature every minute, for up to 15 minutes** and can be tested with **iodine solution** ($\checkmark$). The **time taken for the iodine solution to no longer change colour** should be recorded ($\checkmark$) and the rate of starch breakdown can be calculated using the formula; **1/time** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ using the same apparatus **AND** volume of solution [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ replace amylase **OR** enzyme [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ at the same range of temperatures [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ there should be no change in the concentration of starch **OR** presence of starch can be confirmed by testing solution with iodine solution [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 A control experiment would be using the **same apparatus** and **volume of solutions** ($\checkmark$), but **replace amylase with water** ($\checkmark$) and conducting the same experiment with the **same range of temperatures** ($\checkmark$). There should be **no change in the concentration of starch** ($\checkmark$).

 To increase the reliability of the experiment, you could carry out **repeats** ($\checkmark$).
