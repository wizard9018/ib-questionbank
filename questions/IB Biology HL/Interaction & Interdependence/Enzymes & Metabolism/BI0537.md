# BI0537

An experiment tests the effect of changing substrate concentration on an enzymatic reaction. What could be the independent and dependent variables in this experiment?

| | Independent variable | Dependent variable |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | temperature | substrate concentration |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | substrate concentration | rate of product formation |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | enzyme concentration | temperature |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | rate of product formation | substrate concentration |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In a scientific experiment, the independent variable is the variable that is changed or controlled, while the dependent variable is the variable that is measured as an outcome, or response, to the changes in the independent variable.

In an experiment that tests the effect of changing substrate concentration on an enzymatic reaction, the substrate concentration would be varied by the experimenter. Thus, substrate concentration is the independent variable.

During an enzymatic reaction, the enzyme catalyzes the formation of a product from the substrate. The rate at which product forms varies based on the activity of the enzyme, meaning this rate gives a measure of enzymatic activity. So, the rate of product formation could be the dependent variable in the experiment.

In this experiment, the effect of substrate concentration on rate of reaction is being tested, so enzyme concentration and temperature are controlled variables.  (**Choice A** and **Choice C**). **Choice D** is the wrong way round.
