# BI2669

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how light energy is converted into chemical energy during photosynthesis.            [4 marks]

2. Describe the benefits and challenges of FACE experiments for investigating photosynthesis.    [5 marks]

3. Explain how valid results can be obtained when investigating the effect of varying light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ light energy is absorbed by photosynthetic pigments **OR** chlorophyll    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ absorbed light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ excited electrons provide energy to convert carbon dioxide into glucose **OR** carbohydrate [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electrons lost from chlorophyll are replaced by electrons from the splitting of water **OR** photolysis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ photolysis is the splitting of water molecules in the presence of light [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ photolysis produces electrons **AND** hydrogen ions **AND** oxygen     [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Light energy** is **absorbed** by **chlorophyll** in the chloroplasts ($\checkmark$). This **energy excites electrons** in the **chlorophyll** molecules ($\checkmark$). The excited electrons provide energy to **convert carbon dioxide into glucose** ($\checkmark$). The **electrons lost** from chlorophyll are **replaced** by electrons from the **splitting of water** in a process called **photolysis** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following *(must include at least **one** benefit and **one** challenge to gain full marks):*

    ***Benefits:***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ take place in natural ecosystems, increasing ecological validity    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allow observation of long-term plant responses to elevated $\ce{CO_2}$        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ simulate future atmospheric $\ce{CO_2}$ conditions predicted under climate change    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ plants experience natural interactions with pests, competitors, and pollinators, giving realistic outcomes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enable study of whole-ecosystem responses, not just individual plants    [1 mark]

    ***Challenges:***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ difficult to control environmental variables like temperature and rainfall        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ expensive **OR** requires large infrastructure    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ differences in the environmental conditions **OR** characteristics of different experimental plots may cause unintended variability in the results        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ wind or weather may disperse added $\ce{CO_2}$ unevenly        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ monitoring **OR** data collection over large areas is logistically demanding    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ harder to isolate the effect of $\ce{CO_2}$ alone due to multiple interacting variables        [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    FACE experiments are beneficial because they are conducted in **natural ecosystems**, so they provide **more valid data** about how **plants respond** to **elevated** $\mathbf{CO_2}$ levels ($\checkmark$).  They also allow **long-term observations** of plant **growth** and **photosynthesis** under **future climate** conditions ($\checkmark$).  However, a major challenge is that **environmental variables** like temperature and rainfall are **difficult to control** in open-air settings ($\checkmark$).  In addition, differences between plots, such as soil type or microclimate, can **introduce variation** in the **data** that is **unrelated** to $\ce{CO_2}$ levels ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ use a light meter to measure light intensity    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allow plant time to acclimatise at each light level before taking measurements        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ carry out multiple trials **OR** repeats **OR** collect at least three results at each light intensity    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ take mean of results **OR** remove any anomalous results [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ use  same plant **OR** same species **OR** size **OR** condition of plant            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ maintain a constant $\ce{CO_2}$ concentration, **OR** use a fixed mass **OR** concentration of sodium hydrogen carbonate        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ keep temperature constant **OR** use a water bath to maintain stable **OR** suitable temperature [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ record data in a consistent **OR** quantifiable way **OR** count  bubbles of oxygen per minute **OR** collect volume of oxygen in same set time            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ carry out the experiment in a darkened room to eliminate interference from other light sources [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calibrate equipment before use to reduce systematic error    [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    To ensure valid results a **light meter** should be used to **accurately measure** light intensity ($\checkmark$). The **plant** should be allowed to **acclimatise** at **each** light **intensity before recording data** ($\checkmark$). Multiple trials should be carried out at **each light intensity** ($\checkmark$), and a **mean** should be **calculated** to provide a more reliable estimate of the true photosynthetic rate ($\checkmark$). The **same species** and **size of plant** should be used in **all trials** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, **carbon dioxide concentration** must be **kept constant**, for example by using the **same mass** of **sodium hydrogen carbonate** for each trial ($\checkmark$). **Temperature** should also be **kept constant**, possibly using a **water bath** to maintain a suitable temperature for the plant's enzymes and to prevent heat from the lamp from affecting results ($\checkmark$).
