# BI1041

Algae commonly live in water. In what form do they obtain carbon dioxide from their surroundings, and what do they use this carbon dioxide for?

| | Form carbon dioxide is in | Role of Carbon Dioxide |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Carbon dioxide gas | Binds to RuBP, catalysed by Rubisco |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Hydrogen carbonate ions | Fixed into an organic molecule, catalysed by RuBisCO |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Carbon dioxide gas | Fixed into an organic molecule, catalysed by RuBisCO |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Hydrogen carbonate ions | Binds to Rubisco, catalysed by RuBP |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Aquatic algae obtain their carbon dioxide from the water in the form of dissolved carbon dioxide or hydrogen carbonate ions. Once in the stroma, the carbon dioxide enters the Calvin cycle and binds to RuBP, a reaction catalysed by the enzyme RuBisCO; **Choice B**  is correct.

When carbon dioxide enters the water it is no longer in a gaseous state. It forms dissolved carbon dioxide and carbonic acid, which dissociates into hydrogen carbonate ions and to a lesser extent carbonate ions.  Once in the stroma, the carbon dioxide binds to RuBP, not RuBisCO, and the carbon dioxide is fixed into glucose and other organic molecules. **Choices A, C** and **D** are therefore incorrect.
