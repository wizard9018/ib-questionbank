# BI0165

What is used to generate a proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane in the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Triose phosphate

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Electrons from Photosystem II

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ ATP synthase

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Reduced NADP

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Excited electrons from Photosystem II release energy as they pass along the electron transport chain. This energy is used to pump protons (H⁺ ions) from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen, generating a proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane. **Choice B** is the correct answer.

ATP synthase does not generate the proton gradient. Instead, it uses the proton gradient to synthesise ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate via chemiosmosis. **Choice C** is incorrect.

Excited electrons from Photosystem I are used to reduce NADP⁺, which is not involved in generating the proton gradient. Instead, NADPH provides hydrogen atoms to reduce glycerate 3-phosphate (GP) to triose phosphate (TP) in the Calvin cycle. **Choice D** is incorrect.

Triose phosphate is produced in the Calvin cycle and is used to regenerate ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP) and to synthesise carbohydrates. It plays no role in the light-dependent reactions or in establishing the proton gradient. **Choice A** is incorrect.
