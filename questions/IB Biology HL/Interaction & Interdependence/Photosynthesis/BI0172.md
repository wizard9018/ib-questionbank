# BI0172

What is the process that takes place before excited electrons are transferred from photosystem II to photosystem I?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Chemiosmosis

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Photophosphorylation

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Photoactivation of photosystem II

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Reduction of NADP

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Photoactivation of photosystem II needs to take place first before the transfer of excited electrons from photosystem II to photosystem I. Light energy needs to be absorbed by chlorophyll in photosystem II for the electrons to be excited to high-energy electrons.

Chemiosmosis (**Choice A**), photophosphorylation (**Choice B**) and reduction of NADP (**Choice D**) are processes that take place after, or during, the transfer of excited electrons from photosystem II to photosystem I.

The transfer of excited electrons from photosystem II to photosystem I causes a release of energy which is used to move protons into the thylakoid space. The movement of protons generates a proton gradient. Chemiosmosis then occurs, which leads to the synthesis of ATP, a process known as photophosphorylation. These excited electrons from photosystem I are then used to reduce NADP.
