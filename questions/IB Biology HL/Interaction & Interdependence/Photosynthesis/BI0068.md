# BI0068

The graph shows the action spectrum for photosynthesis.

![BI0068](images/BI0068.png)

What can be directly inferred from the graph?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The highest rate of photosynthesis is in the presence of red light.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The highest rate of photosynthesis is in the presence of blue light.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The peaks mean that there are two types of chlorophyll pigments.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Different amounts of light are absorbed by chlorophyll at different wavelengths.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The graph shows a clear peak between ~430–480 nm, which corresponds to blue light. This is the highest point on the entire curve, and therefore, the highest photosynthesis rate occurs under blue wavelengths. **Choice B** is correct.

There is a peak in the red region (~650–680 nm), it is lower than the peak in blue light. Therefore red light does not give the highest photosynthesis rate in this graph, so **Choice A** is incorrect.

The action spectrum only shows photosynthetic rate, not pigment identity. Peaks indicate wavelengths effective for photosynthesis, but do not prove how many pigments are involved. The presence of multiple pigments (chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, carotenoids) is a biological explanation, but it cannot be directly inferred from the action spectrum alone. **Choice C** is, therefore, incorrect.

**Choice D** is incorrect because it describes the absorption spectrum, not the action spectrum. Although absorption and action spectra are related, the graph does not directly measure absorption, so we cannot infer this statement directly.
