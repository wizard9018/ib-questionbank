# BI2002

**Maximum mark: 10**

Plant growth chambers are contained and controllable environments used to strictly control growth conditions. A researcher planted *Arabidopsis thaliana* in several growth chambers at six different carbon dioxide concentrations. After 3 weeks of growth, the plants were dried and massed.

![BI2002A](images/BI2002A.png)

1. State the independent variable. [1 mark]

2. Suggest **one** reason for reporting biomass units as grams per 10 plants. [1 mark]

3. Explain the trend seen in the graph. [3 marks]

4. Discuss a probable result if the $\ce{CO_2}$ concentration were raised above
2,000 mg m$^{-3}$. [2 marks]

5. Explain the use of dried biomass in the experiment. [2 marks]

6. State **one** light variable that can be adjusted in the growth chamber to potentially change the biomass produced by the plant. [1 mark]

3. any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ as $\ce{CO_2}$ concentration increases, dry biomass also increases, indicating a possible positive correlation **OR** indicating a positive relationship [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ $\ce{CO_2}$ is a substrate for photosynthesis, so increasing its availability can enhance rate of photosynthesis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ photosynthesis reactions are catalysed by enzymes, and higher $\ce{CO_2}$ concentrations can initially increase the rate of these reactions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ increase in biomass suggests increase in $\ce{CO_2}$ concentration increases rate of photosynthesis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ at higher $\ce{CO_2}$ concentrations, the biomass plateaus, suggesting limiting factor **OR** enzyme concentration **OR** light intensity **OR** temperature is limiting [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 From 600 to 1,200 mg m$^{-3}$ $\ce{CO_2}$, biomass increases, indicating a **positive relationship** ($\checkmark$). This occurs because **CO**$_2$ is a **substrate for photosynthesis**, and its availability **enhances** the **rate of photosynthesis** ($\checkmark$). However, **beyond 1,200 mg m**$^{-3}$, the biomass **plateaus**, suggesting that **enzymes** are **saturated** or another **factor**, such as **light intensity**, has become **limiting** ($\checkmark$).

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The biomass measurements would stay at the **plateau** ($\checkmark$) because **carbon dioxide** is **no longer** the **limiting factor** once the photosynthetic **enzymes** are **saturated** ($\checkmark$) with the substrate.

5. any **two** of the following:

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Dried biomass is an **indirect measure of photosynthetic rate** ($\checkmark$), because it reflects the organic molecules produced by the plant. Drying **ensures results** are based only on **organic molecules** by **removing variability** caused by **water content** ($\checkmark$).
