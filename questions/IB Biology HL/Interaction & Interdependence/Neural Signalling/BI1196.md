# BI1196

**Maximum mark: 9**

Alpha-methyltyrosine inhibits the synthesis of dopamine, which is a neurotransmitter.  Alpha-methyltyrosine is also structurally similar to tyrosine, a substrate involved in dopamine synthesis.

1. Based on the information above, deduce, with a reason, what type of inhibitor alpha-methyltyrosine is.         [1 mark]

2. Describe the events in the presynaptic neuron that trigger the release of neurotransmitters.            [3 marks]

3. Dopamine is an excitatory neurotransmitter, which means that it increases the chances of an action potential occurring. Describe what happens to the neurotransmitter after it has been released from the presynaptic knob.      [3 marks]

4. If alpha-methyltyrosine concentration increases significantly, the resulting decreasing synthesis of dopamine can cause negative health effects. Suggest how dopamine synthesis could be returned to normal levels even if alpha-methyltyrosine concentrations remain elevated.  [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ competitive inhibitor **BECAUSE** similar shape so can bind to same receptor        [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Alpha-methyltyrosine is a **competitive inhibitor** and binds to the enzyme at the tyrosine (substrate) binding site ($\checkmark$) as it is a similar shape.

2. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ nerve impulse reaches the presynaptic membrane **OR** presynaptic knob **OR** bulb, OR depolarisation occurs in the presynaptic membrane  **OR** presynaptic knob **OR** bulb    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ causes calcium channels to open **OR** voltage-gated calcium ions open    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions enter presynaptic knob **OR** bulb        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ causes vesicles containing the neurotransmitter to move towards the presynaptic membrane **AND** fuse with it **OR** vesicles move towards the presynaptic membrane, and  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neurotransmitter is released by exocytosis        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When a **nerve impulse** reaches the presynaptic membrane ($\checkmark$), it causes **calcium ions** to diffuse through **channels** into the neuron ($\checkmark$). Subsequently, this causes **vesicles** containing the neurotransmitters to **move towards the presynaptic membrane** and **fuse** with it ($\checkmark$), releasing neurotransmitters into the synapse via **exocytosis** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ dopamine diffuses across synaptic cleft    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ dopamine binds to receptors on the post-synaptic membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sodium channels open, and sodium ions    enter post-synaptic neuron    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ entry of sodium ions causes an action potential [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enzymatic inactivation of the neurotransmitter **OR** neurotransmitter is broken down by enzymes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reformation of neurotransmitter in the presynaptic neuron    [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When dopamine is secreted, it **diffuses across the synaptic cleft** ($\checkmark$) and binds to receptors on the **post-synaptic membrane** ($\checkmark$). This opens sodium ion channels, allowing the **entry** of **sodium ions** ($\checkmark$), which **trigger** an **action potential** ($\checkmark$). Further, the **neurotransmitter** is **broken down** by enzymes ($\checkmark$), diffuses back to the presynaptic neuron, and is **reformed** as a neurotransmitter ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ as alpha-methyltyrosine is a competitive inhibitor it binds reversibly at the substrate binding site            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if the concentration of substrate is increased, rate of dopamine production will increase **OR** increasing concentrations of tyrosine will reverse the effects of alpha-methyltyrosine **OR** if enough of the substrate is provided, eventually it may outcompete inhibitor **OR** may restore the original production rate (of dopamine) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ administration of substrate **OR** tyrosine **OR** dopamine therapeutically could relieve symptoms [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Alpha-methyltyrosine is a **competitive inhibitor**, and so **binds reversibly** at the **substrate binding site** ($\checkmark$) in place of substrate tyrosine, thereby preventing the synthesis of dopamine. If **more of the substrate tyrosine** is added, it will **reverse** the **effects** of the **alpha-methyltyrosine** ($\checkmark$) since the **chances** of the **substrate binding** at the active site are **more than** the chances of **inhibitor binding** ($\checkmark$).
