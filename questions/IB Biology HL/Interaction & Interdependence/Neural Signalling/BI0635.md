    2. any **four** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ neonicotinoids bind to acetylcholine receptors [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ in the postsynaptic membrane [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ in cholinergic synapses [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ they bind irreversibly, so they cannot be broken down by acetylcholinesterase [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ so acetylcholine cannot bind    [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$  synaptic transmission is blocked [1 mark]

        [4 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
        Neonicotinoids **bind to acetylcholine receptors** ($\checkmark$) in the **postsynaptic membranes** ($\checkmark$) of **cholinergic synapses** ($\checkmark$). They **bind irreversibly**, so they cannot be broken down by the enzyme acetylcholinesterase ($\checkmark$), which normally breaks down acetylcholine. Because the neonicotinoids are bound to the receptor, the neurotransmitter **acetylcholine cannot bind** ($\checkmark$); therefore, **synaptic transmission is blocked** ($\checkmark$) and the insect gets paralysed and dies.

    3.    any **one** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$   acetylcholine will bind to receptors after the pesticide is broken down by acetylcholinesterase [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$   synaptic transmission will occur **OR** take place **OR** not be blocked [1 mark]

        [1 mark]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
If the pesticides used by farmers were broken down by acetylcholinesterase, they would be less effective and **synaptic transmission would occur** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ insects have proportionally more cholinergic synapses than mammal [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neonicotinoids bind more strongly to acetylcholine receptors in insects than in mammals [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Pesticides containing neonicotinoids are less toxic to mammals because neonicotinoids **bind more strongly** to acetylcholine receptors in insects than in mammals ($\checkmark$), preventing synaptic transmission.
