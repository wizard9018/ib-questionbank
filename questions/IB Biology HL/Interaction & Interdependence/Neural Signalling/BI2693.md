# BI2693

Which processes are responsible for sodium channels adjacent to an area of depolarisation reaching threshold potential?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Diffusion of sodium and potassium ions inside the axon

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Diffusion of sodium ions inside and outside the axon

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Diffusion of sodium ions inside the axon and potassium ions outside the axon

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Diffusion of potassium ions inside and outside the axon

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because, when sodium ions ($\ce{Na^+}$) enter the axon during depolarisation, they increase the concentration of $\ce{Na^+}$ inside that region. These sodium ions diffuse along the inside of the axon towards the next resting region, reducing the membrane potential there. Meanwhile, outside the axon, sodium ions move back toward the previously depolarised region to replace the sodium ions that moved into the axon. These movements, known as local currents, help propagate the action potential by bringing the adjacent section of the axon to the threshold potential.

**Choice A** is incorrect because only sodium ions, not potassium ions, are involved in generating local currents.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the action potential is propagated by the diffusion of sodium ions inside and outside the neuron, not potassium ions.

**Choice D** is incorrect because local currents of sodium ions propagate the impulse, not the diffusion of potassium ions.
