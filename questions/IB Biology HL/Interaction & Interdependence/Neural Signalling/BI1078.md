**Maximum mark: 10**

1. Describe the events in the presynaptic neuron that trigger the release of neurotransmitters.        [3 marks]

2.
    1. State two things that happen to neurotransmitters once they reach the postsynaptic membrane.                [2 marks]

    2. Predict what would happen to the postsynaptic neuron if the neurotransmitter did not do what you described in part (i).        [2 marks]

3. Outline one possible effect on neuronal transmission if a compound similar in shape to the neurotransmitter was added to the synaptic cleft.    [1 mark]

4. Explain why a nervous impulse can only be conducted in one direction across the synaptic cleft.    [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ nerve impulse reaches the presynaptic membrane **OR** presynaptic knob **OR** bulb, **OR** depolarisation occurs in the presynaptic membrane  **OR** presynaptic knob **OR** bulb [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ causes calcium channels to open **OR** voltage-gated calcium channels open    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions enter presynaptic knob **OR** bulb        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ causes vesicles containing the neurotransmitter to move towards the presynaptic membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles fuse with presynaptic membrane        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neurotransmitter released by exocytosis        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When a **nerve impulse** reaches the presynaptic membrane ($\checkmark$), it causes **calcium ions** to diffuse through **channels** into the neuron ($\checkmark$). Subsequently, this causes **vesicles** containing the neurotransmitters to **move towards the presynaptic membrane** ($\checkmark$) and **fuse** with it ($\checkmark$), **releasing the neurotransmitter** into the synapse via **exocytosis** ($\checkmark$).

2.
    1. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ binds to receptors                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ broken down **OR** enzymatic degradation        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reabsorbed into vesicles **OR** reuptake        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ diffuse away from the synaptic cleft **OR** gap    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Once a neurotransmitter reaches the postsynaptic membrane, it **binds** to specific **receptors** ($\checkmark$) and then the signal is terminated by the neurotransmitter being **broken down** ($\checkmark$) by an enzyme.

2.
    2. Any **two** of the following (must include **one** result for **each** statement given in **(bi)**):

    *If it didn't bind to receptors:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ action potential **OR** nerve impulse would not be generated in next axon     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ postsynaptic neuron wouldn't be able to process and integrate signals from multiple sources effectively                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ disruptions in the flow of information within neural circuits, affecting brain functions and behaviours                    [1 mark]

    *If it did't break down, get reabsorbed back into vesicles, or diffuse away:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ postsynaptic neuron will be continuously activated, **OR** an action potential will be continuously fired            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ postsynaptic receptors could become desensitised **OR** downregulated, resulting is less responsiveness to the neurotransmitter over time **OR** reducing the
effectiveness of future signalling at that synapse        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ accumulation of neurotransmitters in the synaptic cleft  could interfere with subsequent signals **OR** result in overstimulation **OR** crosstalk between neighbouring synapses        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the ability to distinguish between different synaptic inputs could be compromised, leading to a loss of specificity in neuronal communication     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ synapses may not be able to repeatedly transmit signals, as the accumulation of neurotransmitters might prevent the proper resetting of the synapse for subsequent signalling            [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    If the neurotransmitter did not bind to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, then an **action potential would not be generated** ($\checkmark$) in the postsynaptic neuron. If the neurotransmitter wasn't broken down in the synaptic cleft, the postsynaptic neuron would **continuously fire an action potential** ($\checkmark$) without returning to rest.

3. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ slow down **OR** stop **BECAUSE** compound would bind to receptors on postsynaptic membrane blocking the receptor **OR** preventing the neurotransmitter from binding **OR** it could act as a competitive inhibitor **OR** could bind to a different site on the receptor, causing allosteric changes that inhibit the receptor's ability to bind        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ agonistic effect **OR** compound could activate the receptors and produce a response similar to the actual neurotransmitter            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the compound might bind to the receptors with low efficacy **OR** affinity compared to actual neurotransmitter, triggering a partial response, which might result in a weaker **OR** modified transmission                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ compound might not be able to bind effectively **OR** activate the receptors, and so may have no significant impact                    [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    If a compound similar in shape was added to the synaptic cleft, then neuronal transmission would **slow down** because some of the **receptors** on the postsynaptic membrane would be **blocked** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles of neurotransmitter only found in presynaptic bulb **OR** knob **OR** neurotransmitter only released from presynaptic membrane        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ receptors for neurotransmitter are only found on postsynaptic membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ voltage-gated calcium channels are concentrated in the presynaptic knob **OR** bulb and trigger release of neurotransmitter        [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Due to the **refractory** period, nerve impulses only travel in one direction, and they stimulate the release of neurotransmitters ($\checkmark$).  Additionally, the **receptors** for neurotransmitters are only found on the **postsynaptic membrane** ($\checkmark$).
