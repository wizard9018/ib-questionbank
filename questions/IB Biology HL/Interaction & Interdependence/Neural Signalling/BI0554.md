# BI0554

The diagram shows a process that occurs in the membranes of neurons during a stage of the action potential. What process does the diagram depict?

![554_6.5](images/554_6.5.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Open sodium channels initiating depolarisation.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The sodium/potassium pump initiating depolarisation.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Open sodium channels restoring the resting potential.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The sodium/potassium pump restoring the resting potential.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The four labelled steps in the diagram show the following:

$\hspace{0.5em}$ 1. The binding of three sodium ions (Na:sup[+]) on the inside of the neuron to the protein pump embedded in the membrane of the neuron

$\hspace{0.5em}$ 2. The hydrolysis of ATP to ADP, releasing energy which allows the change in conformation of the protein to transport the three sodium ions out of the neuron

$\hspace{0.5em}$ 3. The binding of two potassium ions (K:sup[+]) on the outside of the neuron to the protein pump embedded in the membrane of the neuron

$\hspace{0.5em}$ 4. The release of the two potassium ions into the inside of the neuron

The diagram shows the active transport of three sodium ions out of the neuron and the transport of two potassium ions into the neuron. Therefore, this diagram shows the sodium/potassium pump establishing the resting potential; **Choice D** is correct.

During depolarisation sodium ions enter the neuron through sodium channels, which is not being shown by this diagram; **Choice A** is incorrect.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the sodium/potassium pump is responsible for establishing the resting potential, not initiating depolarisation

**Choice C** is incorrect because the diagram shows the sodium/potassium pump, not open sodium channels. Furthermore, open sodium channels depolarise the cell, they do not restore the resting membrane potential.
