# BI1074

Which statement is **not**  a reason that a signal normally passes along a neuron or across a cholinergic synapse in one direction?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Specific neurotransmitter receptors are only present on the postsynaptic membrane

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Straight after an action potential, there is a refractory period during which it is difficult or impossible to fire another action potential

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ There is a higher concentration of neurotransmitters released at the postsynaptic membrane

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Vesicles are formed in, and neurotransmitters are released from, the presynaptic knob

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
There is a higher concentration of neurotransmitters released at the presynaptic membrane, rather than the postsynaptic membrane, which then diffuse towards the postsynaptic membrane and bind to receptors, **Choice C** is an untrue statement, and so is the correct answer.

Neurotransmitters that are released from the presynaptic membrane bind to specific receptors on the postsynaptic membrane. These receptors receive the neurotransmitters and initiate a response in the postsynaptic membrane. The signal transmission is unidirectional since the specific receptors are present only on the postsynaptic membrane, **Choice A**  is a true statement and, therefore, not a correct answer to this question. Electrical impulses can only travel in one direction through a neuron due to hyperpolarisation and the refractory period, **Choice B**  is a true statement and, therefore, not a correct answer to this question. Vesicles are only formed in and released from the presynaptic knob meaning that the impulse can only be transmitted in one direction, **Choice D**  is a true statement and, therefore, not a correct answer to this question.
