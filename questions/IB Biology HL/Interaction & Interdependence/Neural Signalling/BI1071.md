# BI1071

Which are correct reasons for a negative resting potential in a neuron?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | The action of sodium-potassium pumps |
    | II. | The permeability of the membrane to potassium ions |
    | III. | Opening of sodium channels in the membrane |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A neuron that is not transmitting a signal will have a negative resting potential due to an imbalance of charges across the membrane. The resting potential is maintained by the action of sodium-potassium pumps, which pump  3 Na:sup[+] ions out for every 2 K:sup[+] ions (**Statement I**) that they pump in and the membrane being 50$\times$ more permeable to K:sup[+] ions than  Na:sup[+] ions (**Statement II**), causing  K:sup[+] ions to leak back out across the membrane, increasing the concentration gradient, **Choice B**  is correct.

**Statement III**  is incorrect, as the opening of sodium channels begins the depolarisation process and raises the membrane potential to a positive value, **Choices C** and **D**  are incorrect. **Choice A**  is incorrect as it only includes **Statement I**.
