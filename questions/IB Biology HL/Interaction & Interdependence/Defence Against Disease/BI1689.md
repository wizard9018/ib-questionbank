# BI1689

Which of the following is an example of an innate immune response?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Antibody production

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Agglutination

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Phagocytosis

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Neutralisation

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

The innate immune response is the body's first line of defence against pathogens, characterised by a rapid, non$-$specific reaction through physical barriers, immune cells, and various proteins and molecules. Phagocytosis is an innate immune response in which phagocytic cells engulf and digest pathogens and debris. It is a non$-$specific and immediate defence mechanism that does not require previous exposure to the pathogen. **Choice C** is the correct answer. 

Antibody production is part of the adaptive immune response. It involves plasma B cells producing specific antibodies against antigens after being exposed to them. **Choice A** is incorrect.

Agglutination refers to the clumping of particles, such as pathogens, which is usually mediated by antibodies binding to antigens. Since it involves antibodies, it is part of the adaptive immune response. **Choice B** is incorrect.

Neutralisation is also an adaptive immune response where antibodies bind to pathogens or toxins, neutralising their harmful effects. **Choice D** is incorrect.
