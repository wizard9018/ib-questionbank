# BI1182

The diagram shows a red blood cell with the A antigen. What is the likely result if someone with blood group A is transfused with blood group AB?

![BI1182](images/BI1182.png)

| | Result | Reason |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Rejection | An immune response will be triggered in the recipient, where they will make anti-A antibodies |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Rejection | An immune response will be triggered in the recipient, where they will make anti-B antibodies |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Acceptance | Both the recipient and donor have A antigens present on their red blood cells |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Acceptance | Half of the red blood cells from the donor have A antigens on their surface |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Red blood cells from people who have blood group A only have A antigens on their surface so the immune system recognises any other antigens as foreign. Red blood cells from people who have blood group AB, have both A and B antigens on their surface, therefore the B antigens are recognised as foreign, triggering an immune response and the recipient makes anti-B antibodies. **Choice B** is correct.

The recipient does not make anti-A antibodies, as the A antigen is not recognised as foreign; **Choice A** is incorrect. **Choices C** and **D** are incorrect, as blood group AB would not be accepted.
