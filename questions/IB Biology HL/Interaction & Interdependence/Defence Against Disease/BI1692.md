# BI1692

Below is a table showing the differences between the innate and adaptive immune systems

| Feature | Innate Immune System | Adaptive Immune System |

| :-: | :-: | :-: |

| Response Time | Fast | Slow |

| Specificity | Broad | Specific |

| Memory | None | Present |

| Primary Cells | I | II |

Primary cells are the main immune cells that are present in the body under normal conditions, ready to respond to infections. What primary cells do "I" and "II" represent in the table above?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I: T and B cells, II: Phagocytes

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I: Phagocytes, II: Lymphocytes

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ I: Plasma cells, II: Phagocytes

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I: Macrophages, II: Plasma cells

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Phagocytes are the primary cells in the innate immune system, while T and B lymphocytes are the primary cells in the adaptive immune system. **Choice B** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because they are reversed; T and B lymphocytes are not part of the innate immune system, and phagocytes are not the primary cells of the adaptive immune system.

**Choice C** is incorrect because plasma cells are not part of the innate immune system; they are differentiated cells derived from B lymphocytes in the adaptive immune system, and phagocytes are not primary cells of the adaptive immune system.

Although macrophages are primary cells of the innate immune system, **Choice D**  is incorrect because plasma cells are not considered primary cells of the adaptive immune system. T and B lymphocytes are the primary cells of the adaptive immune system. Plasma cells are differentiated effector cells derived from B lymphocytes after the B lymphocytes have been activated.
