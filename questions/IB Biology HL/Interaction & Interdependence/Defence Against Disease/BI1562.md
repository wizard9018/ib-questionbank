# BI1562

Upon injury, blood platelets release clotting factors, which lead to the production of thrombin. Thrombin is an enzyme that converts the protein fibrinogen, present in the blood, to fibrin, resulting in blood clotting. The graph shows the relative concentrations of these substances following an injury.

![BI1562iii](images/BI1562iii.png)

What property allows these proteins to perform their functions?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ All three substances are hydrophobic.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Thrombin functions optimally at a pH of 4.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Fibrinogen and thrombin, but not fibrin, are water-soluble.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Hydrogen bonding between these three substances leads to blood clotting.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because both thrombin and fibrinogen are water-soluble proteins. This allows fibrinogen to be present in the blood and easily transported to the injured site when needed. As thrombin is released, it catalyses the conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin. Since fibrin is an insoluble, fibrous protein, it creates a physical barrier over the wound, which then facilitates clotting.

**Choice A** is incorrect because fibrinogen is hydrophilic while thrombin and fibrin are somewhat hydrophobic. In essence, fibrinogen and thrombin are hydrophilic and remain soluble in the blood plasma, while fibrin, once formed, is a fibrous protein with hydrophobic characteristics. **Choice B** is incorrect since thrombin is optimally functional at a neutral pH similar to that of blood plasma and would likely denature at low pH values. Although hydrogen bonding between fibrin molecules does occur, **Choice D** is incorrect since these three substances do not all interact with each other simultaneously to form hydrogen bonds. Fibrinogen interacts with thrombin to form an enzyme-substrate complex, which involves hydrogen bonds among other interactions. Once fibrin is produced, it is released from thrombin and does not form hydrogen bonds with it. Instead, fibrin molecules interact with each other, including forming hydrogen bonds, to create a stable clot.
