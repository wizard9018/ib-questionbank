# BI0300

The graph shows how the concentration of antibodies changed in an individual\'s blood over time.

![BI0300i](images/BI0300i.png)

What is the most likely cause of the change in antibody levels at point X?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Primary infection

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Secondary infection by the same pathogen
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Infection by a different pathogen
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Antibiotics administered

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The graph shows two antibody peaks. The first smaller peak after the first arrow (pointing to the initial rise in antibody concentration) is typical of a primary immune response. The second, much larger and faster peak (at point X) is characteristic of a secondary immune response. When the same pathogen enters the body again, memory B cells created during the primary infection are rapidly activated. This causes a faster and more intense antibody production, which explains the sharp and high peak at point X. **Choice B** is the correct answer.

**Choice A** is incorrect because a primary infection would result in a slower and lower rise in antibodies, as seen at the first peak, not the second.

**Choice C** is incorrect because if infected by a different pathogen, the immune system would treat it as a new primary infection, and the antibody response would again be slow and small, not rapid and large.

**Choice D**is incorrect because antibiotics do not directly cause an increase in antibody concentration. They target bacteria but do not stimulate antibody production, which is the job of the immune system
