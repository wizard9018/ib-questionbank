# BI1357

Which factor does **not** contribute to the transmission of zoonotic diseases between animal and human species?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Destruction of animal habitats

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Humans and animals being bitten by infected mosquitoes

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Humans and animals being supplied with preventative antibiotics

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Humans eating animal byproducts

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Zoonotic diseases are diseases that can be transmitted from animals to humans. They may be bacterial, viral, or parasitic. Although antibiotics do not affect viral or parasitic infections, providing preventative antibiotics to humans and animals can help reduce the spread of bacterial zoonotic diseases. This practice can be beneficial in controlling disease transmission, especially in high-risk settings; **Choice C**  is correct.

Destruction of animal habitats can result in animals being in closer proximity to humans, which can increase the risk of transmission of zoonotic diseases between animals and humans; **Choice A** is incorrect. Infected mosquitoes can transmit diseases like malaria, Zika, and West Nile virus from animals to humans. Mosquitoes can act as vectors, carrying the pathogens from one host to another during their blood-feeding process.; **Choice B**  is incorrect. Humans eating infected animal byproducts in raw forms e.g. unpasteurised milk or undercooked meat, can spread zoonotic diseases between animals and humans; **Choice D**  is incorrect.
