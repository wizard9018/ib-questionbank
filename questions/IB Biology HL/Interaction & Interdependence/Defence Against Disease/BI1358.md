# BI1358

Which of the following can contribute to increasing the chance of herd immunity within a population?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ A high proportion of the population being vaccinated

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Quarantining people who are infected with the disease 

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Providing free preventive drugs to the whole population

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Closing international borders so that no infected people can travel between countries

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Herd immunity is achieved when a large proportion of a population is immune to a disease, either through vaccination or previous infection. This means that there is a much lower risk of someone who is susceptible to the disease becoming infected, as the chances of disease transmission are lowered. Vaccinating a large proportion of the population increases the chance of achieving herd immunity; **Choice A**  is correct.

**Choice B**  is incorrect  because quarantine is a containment strategy. While it reduces the spread of the pathogen, it does not increase the number of immune individuals in the population.

**Choice C**  is incorrect because preventive drugs (like antivirals or antibiotics) treat or prevent the symptoms or infection, but do not typically trigger the production of memory cells and antibodies required for long-term immunity.

**Choice D**  is incorrect because closing international borders is a "containment" or "mitigation" strategy. While it limits the movement of a pathogen between populations, it does not change the biological immune status of the individuals within those populations. Herd immunity can only be built when individuals develop an active immune response (memory cells), which border closures do not provide.
