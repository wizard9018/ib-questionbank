# BI2888

Plant shoots detect and respond to light. What membrane protein is involved in phototropism, and what mechanism does it control?

| | Protein | Mechanism |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Efflux carriers | Movement of  auxin into cells |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Protein channels  | Movement of hydrogen ions into cell wall |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Efflux carriers | Movement of auxin out of cells |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ |  Protein channels | Movement of cytokinin from roots to shoots |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In phototropism, auxin is redistributed to the shaded side of the plant shoot in response to light. The efflux carriers (such as PIN proteins) are membrane proteins that actively transport auxin out of the cells. The auxin accumulates on the shaded side of the shoot, where it promotes cell elongation, causing the plant to bend towards the light. Efflux carriers are necessary because, once auxin enters the cell, it becomes negatively charged (as it loses a proton), and cannot diffuse out passively. Therefore, efflux carriers are essential for moving auxin out of the cell and contributing to the phototropic response. **Choice C** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because efflux carriers are responsible for moving auxin out of cells, not into them. Auxin diffuses freely into cells.

**Choice B** is incorrect because hydrogen ion (H⁺) movement into the cell wall is mediated by proton pumps, not protein channels. Proton pumps actively transport hydrogen ions from inside the cell into the apoplast (the cell wall region), which acidifies the wall and facilitates cell elongation.

**Choice D** is incorrect because cytokinin is not involved in phototropism. Cytokinin is produced in the roots and travels to the shoots via the xylem to promote cell division and growth. It is unrelated to the movement of auxin or the mechanism of phototropism, where the key hormone is auxin.
