# BI2581

A scientist treated a plant shoot with an inhibitor that blocked auxin efflux carriers. What is the most likely effect on the plant's growth?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The shoot will bend towards the light more quickly.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The shoot will fail to bend towards the light.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The shoot will bend away from the light.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The shoot will grow at the same rate but without directional bending.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because auxin efflux carriers are necessary for redistributing auxin to the shaded side of the shoot, where auxin stimulates cell elongation. If these carriers are blocked, auxin remains evenly distributed, and no differential growth occurs, meaning the shoot grows straight instead of bending toward the light.

**Choice A** is incorrect because blocking auxin efflux carriers prevents the normal redistribution of auxin to the shaded side of the shoot. If auxin is not concentrated on one side, differential cell elongation cannot occur, meaning phototropic bending will not happen.

**Choice C** is incorrect because blocking auxin efflux carriers disrupts auxin distribution but does not cause an inversion of the response (i.e., bending away from light).

**Choice D** is incorrect because if auxin cannot be redistributed, the plant may still grow, but without bending (which makes this option seem plausible). However, auxin cannot be efficiently transported down the shoot, which leads to auxin accumulation in the shoot tip rather than being redistributed to the elongation zone. With less auxin reaching the elongation zone, less cell expansion and slower growth occur.
