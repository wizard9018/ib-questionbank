# BI0680

Below is a photograph of a lily flower (*Lilium candidum*), which begins to flower as days get longer in summer. Gibberellins, a type of phytohormone, trigger the flowering in meristem tissue.

![lilly petal anther stigma style](<images/lilly petal anther stigma style.jpg>)

How do gibberellins bring about flowering in lily plants?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ They bring about differentiation in the lateral meristem.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ They cause changes in gene expression in the apical meristem.

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ They stimulate the production of cytokinins.
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ They bring about gene mutations in the apical meristem.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because gibberellins activate genes in the shoot apical meristem, promoting the transition from vegetative growth to flowering.

**Choice A** is Incorrect as gibberellins act on the shoot apical meristem, not the lateral meristem, which is responsible for secondary growth and not flowering.

**Choice C** is incorrect as gibberellins do not directly stimulate cytokinins. Cytokinins interact with auxins and are involved in processes like cell division, but they are not central to flowering in long-day plants.

**Choice D** is incorrect because gibberellins do not cause gene mutations; they regulate gene expression to activate flowering-related genes.
