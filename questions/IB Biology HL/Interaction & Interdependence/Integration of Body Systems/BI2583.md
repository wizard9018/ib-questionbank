# BI2583

A plant which has a mutation that inhibits auxin production is treated with a synthetic auxin-like compound. This compound, which is much larger than natural auxin molecules, is applied to the extracellular spaces in a plant stem. Which of the following diagrams best represents the most likely result?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because the question states that the synthetic compound is much larger than natural auxin. If it is too large to pass through auxin transporters, it will remain in the extracellular spaces in a plant stem (the apoplast) and cannot enter. Since auxin must be transported inside cells to activate proton pumps and trigger the acid-growth mechanism, this synthetic compound will be unable to induce cell elongation or phototropic bending, so the stem will grow without bending. Other phytohormones, such as gibberellins and cytokinins, will still promote some growth even in the absence of auxin.

**Choice A** is incorrect because this would happen only if auxin successfully entered cells and promoted elongation. Since this compound is large and stays outside the cells of the stem (in the apoplast), it won't induce bending. This could happen if the synthetic compound was able to bind extracellularly, but there is nothing in the question to indicate this.

**Choice B** is incorrect because if the synthetic auxin compound was able to enter cells on the treated side, then we would see bending in the other direction (away from the treated side).

**Choice D** is incorrect because the plant may still have other hormones (e.g., gibberellins, cytokinins) that promote some growth, even without auxin. Growth would be stunted but not completely halted.
