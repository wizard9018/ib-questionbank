# BI2579

The sweet pea plant (*Lathyrus oleraceus*) coils around other plants or structures for support.

![BI2579](images/BI2579.png)

When a tendril touches another plant or support, a phytohormone is transported and accumulates on the opposite side (the side not in contact). This causes cell elongation on the non-touching side and less growth on the touching side, leading to the coiling effect.

Which hypotheses could be inferred from the information given?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
 | | |
 | :-- | :-- |
 | I. | The phytohormone is likely to be an auxin. |
 | II. | The coiling effect is likely a form of tropism. |
 | III. | The phytohormone is likely to move to the opposite side of the plant via diffusion. |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Statement I** could be inferred from the information. Auxin is the primary phytohormone involved in directional growth responses such as phototropism and gravitropism. The description states that a phytohormone accumulates on the opposite side and causes differential growth, which matches the known action of auxin.

**Statement II** could be inferred from the information. Tropisms are directional growth responses in plants that occur in response to external stimuli. In this example, the tendril grows in response to touch, which is characteristic of thigmotropism (a growth response to mechanical stimulation).

**Statement III** cannot be inferred from the information. Although the description states that the phytohormone moves to the opposite side of the tendril, it does not specify the mechanism of transport. Auxin transport occurs through specialised transport proteins and active transport mechanisms rather than simple diffusion.

**Choice B** is correct because **Statements I** and **II** are supported by the information given, whereas **Statement III** is not.
