# BI2336

Chemoreceptors in the body detect and monitor various blood variables to regulate ventilation rate. Which of the following is the factor that most directly drives changes in ventilation rate under normal conditions?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Blood oxygen concentration

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Blood pH

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Blood carbon dioxide concentration

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Blood osmolarity

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Chemoreceptors primarily monitor blood pH to regulate ventilation rate. Since carbon dioxide dissolves in blood to form carbonic acid, which dissociates into H⁺ and HCO₃⁻ ions, an increase in carbon dioxide lowers blood pH. This results in an increase in ventilation rate to excrete carbon dioxide and restore pH balance. **Choice B** is the correct answer.

Blood oxygen concentration plays a secondary role, with chemoreceptors only responding to severe hypoxia (very low oxygen levels). Variations in oxygen concentration in the blood are, therefore, not involved in the main feedback control of ventilation rate; **Choice A** is incorrect.

While blood carbon dioxide concentration indirectly affects ventilation, chemoreceptors detect pH changes caused by variations in carbon dioxide concentrations, rather than carbon dioxide directly; **Choice C** is incorrect.

 Blood osmolarity is not directly involved in ventilation control; **Choice D** is incorrect.
