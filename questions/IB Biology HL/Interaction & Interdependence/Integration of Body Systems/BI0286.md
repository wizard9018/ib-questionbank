# BI0286

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how the enteric nervous system ensures the coordinated movement of food through the digestive tract. [5 marks]

2. Explain how emergent properties arise from the integration of subsystems in a multicellular organism.                [4 marks]

3. Compare and contrast how the nervous system controls conscious and reflex responses. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ controls involuntary peristalsis from mid **OR** lower oesophagus to rectum  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peristalsis is the wave-like contraction of circular and longitudinal muscles in the gut wall                     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ coordinates contraction and relaxation of smooth muscle of gut walls [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ contraction of circular muscles behind bolus of food to push food along **OR** stops food moving backwards  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ contraction of longitudinal muscles in front of a bolus of food shortens gut, moving food along [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ acts independently of central nervous system **OR** CNS **OR** is influenced by autonomic nervous system **OR** ANS     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ modulates peristalsis speed based on food composition and digestive needs [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ responds to stretch receptors in the gut walls [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
The enteric nervous system (ENS) controls **involuntary peristalsis** from the **lower oesophagus** to the **rectum** ($\checkmark$) . It **coordinates** the **contraction** of **circular muscles behind** the **food** to **prevent backflow** ($\checkmark$) and **longitudinal muscles ahead** of it, pushing the **food forward** ($\checkmark$) . Neural **signals** between different **regions** of the **gut** ensure that this **movement** is **synchronised** throughout the digestive tract ($\checkmark$) . This coordination ensures that food moves in **one direction** and at a **speed** that allows for proper **digestion** and **absorption** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ emergent properties are characteristics **OR** abilities that arise from the interaction of parts within a system                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in multicellular organisms, cells are organised into tissues, tissues into organs, and organs into systems    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each higher level has properties that emerge from the interactions within the levels below    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the organisms systems must communicate and coordinate to perform higher-level functions **OR** Integration between systems enables functions that individual systems cannot perform alone                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ emergent properties depend on timing **OR** coordination between systems     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ macromolecular **OR** genetic interactions within cells contribute to emergent properties at the cellular level        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ whole organism relies on coordination between all levels of biological organisation     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ failure of integration at any level disrupts the organism's function         [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Emergent properties are **characteristics** that **arise** from the **interaction** of **parts** in a system ($\checkmark$). In **multicellular organisms**, **cells** are organised into **tissues**, which form **organs**, and organs into **systems** ($\checkmark$). Each level in this hierarchy has **new properties** that are **not found** at the **lower** levels ($\checkmark$). **Failure** of integration at **any level disrupts** the organism's **function**, leading to disease or dysfunction ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following (at least **two** rsimilarities and **two** differences are needed to achieve full marks)

    ***Similarities***

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** involve sensory neurons conveying input from receptors to the central nervous system, **OR** CNS    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** result in a response via motor neurons, either as muscle contraction **OR** glandular secretion    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** rely on synaptic transmission **OR** neurotransmitter release    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can involve the spinal cord as part of the neural pathway        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are coordinated by the nervous system to produce appropriate responses to stimuli     [1 mark]

    ***Differences***

    $\ce{ -> }$ conscious responses are always controlled by the brain **OR** cerebral hemispheres, **WHEREAS** reflex responses are often integrated in the spinal cord **OR** do not always pass through the brain        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ conscious responses involve voluntary control **WHEREAS** reflex responses are involuntary **OR** automatic [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reflex responses are faster because they involve fewer synapses **WHEREAS** conscious responses are slower due to more complex processing    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reflex responses cannot be consciously initiated **WHEREAS** conscious responses require awareness **OR** decision-making    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ conscious responses can be modified by learning **OR** memory **WHEREAS** reflex responses are usually fixed **OR** innate        [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    **Both** conscious and reflex responses **begin with sensory neurons** detecting a stimulus and sending **signals** to the **central nervous system (CNS)** ($\checkmark$). In **both** cases, **motor neurons** carry **signals to effectors**, which may be **muscles or glands** ($\checkmark$). **Synapses and neurotransmitters** are involved in **transmitting impulses** between **neurons** in both types of response ($\checkmark$).

    However, **reflex** responses are usually **integrated** in the **spinal cord**, while **conscious** responses are processed in the **cerebral hemispheres** ($\checkmark$). **Reflexes** are **involuntary** and occur **automatically**, whereas **conscious** responses involve **voluntary** control ($\checkmark$). **Reflexes** are typically **faster** because they involve **fewer neurons** and **synapses**, while **conscious** responses are **slower** due to more complex **processing** ($\checkmark$). **Conscious** responses can also be **modified by learning** or past **experience**, but **reflexes** are generally **fixed** and not influenced by memory ($\checkmark$).
