# BI1104

Reindeer feed on mouse-ear chickweed in the Arctic. What is likely to result from a trophic mismatch in the Arctic?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ More Arctic mouse-ear chickweed seeds germinate.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Fewer reindeer calves are born.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Greater reproductive success of migrating reindeer.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Fewer Arctic mouse-ear chickweed seeds germinate.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A trophic mismatch can occur when climate change causes shifts in the timing of plant growth. If, for example, the Arctic mouse-ear chickweed, a key food source for reindeer, experiences a change in its growth cycle due to warming temperatures, it might peak earlier or later than the period when reindeer need it most for calving. If the timing is not synchronised with the needs of pregnant reindeer, it can reduce food availability for the mothers and their calves, potentially leading to fewer reindeer calves being born.
More seeds may develop if conditions improve, such as temperatures increase and there is enough precipitation, however, this is not a result of a trophic mismatch, so **Choice A** is incorrect.

A trophic mismatch usually leads to challenges in finding sufficient food, which can negatively impact reproductive success, therefore, **Choice C** is incorrect.

Fewer seeds may germinate if conditions are not right to break the dormancy of the seeds; a lower germination success is not to do with a trophic mismatch, therefore, **Choice D** is incorrect.
