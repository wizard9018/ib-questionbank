# BI1625

Which set of conditions gives the best combination of nutrients for oceanic food chains?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" }

    | | $\text{Temperature of deep water}$ | $\text{Salinity of water}$ | $\text{Direction of movement of water}$ |

    | :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: |

    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Cold | Low  | Down |

    | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Cold | High | Up |

    | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Warm | High | Down |

    | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Warm | Low  | Up |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

**Choice B** is correct because the ideal combination is for cold water with high salinity to move upwards in the process of upwelling. Generally, cold water is more nutrient-rich because it can hold more dissolved gases, including oxygen and nutrients, which are crucial for marine life. Water with high salinity tends to be denser and so often sinks, bringing nutrients from the surface down to deeper layers. The movement of water upwards, known as upwelling, will bring the nutrients from the deep ocean to the surface. These nutrients often come from decaying carcasses of dead sea organisms or minerals that enter the water from hydrothermal vents on the deep ocean floor. These nutrients are used by phytoplankton in photosynthesis, which act as producers for oceanic food chains. 

**Choices A** and **C** are incorrect because nutrients need to be channelled upwards towards the surface of the ocean by water movement. This enables photosynthetic organisms to utilise them. 

**Choice D** is incorrect because cold water is better than warm water for nutrient supply.
