3.  $\ce{→}$ D, B, E, A, C [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

    ![1628](images/1628.png)

The Earth's rotation and wind patterns cause surface water to move away from the coast **(D)**, which is the initial event that triggers the entire sequence. As surface water is pushed away, it is replaced by cold, nutrient-rich water from the deep ocean in a process known as upwelling **(B)**. The upwelled deep water brings essential nutrients (like nitrates and phosphates) to the surface, enriching the nutrient-poor surface waters **(E)**. The influx of nutrients supports the growth of phytoplankton, which photosynthesise and form the base of the oceanic food chain **(A)**. As phytoplankton populations grow, they provide a food source for zooplankton and small fish, supporting larger fish populations and increasing fish stocks **(C)**.

4.  any **three** of the following (must include at least **one** statement in favour and **one** statement against the phrase)\

    ***Statements in favour of the phrase:***\

    $\ce{→}$ climate change **OR** global warming is causing upslope range shifts **OR** warmer temperatures are pushing certain species to higher altitudes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ the higher up the mountain, the less available habitat area [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ higher elevations often have fragmented habitats, so it is harder for species to find areas large enough to support viable populations        [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ upward migration of species can disrupt existing ecological communities and interactions [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ species that are less competitive **OR** have more specialised habitat requirements may be displaced **OR** outcompeted by more adaptable species, pushing them closer to extinction    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ changes in precipitation **OR** rain patterns can alter the availability of water resources    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ lack of niches will prevent the species from reproduction, hence extinction    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ some evidence exists to support the effect in certain species only        [1 mark]

    ***Statements against the phrase:***\

    $\ce{→}$ there is only a small body of evidence **OR** very few examples exist to prove the integrity of the phrase        [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ the species could exist in other countries **OR** mountain ranges so might not necessarily become extinct        [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ some species have adaptive potential to help them survive even as their environment changes **OR** species may evolve quickly enough to cope with changing conditions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ mountains can have a variety of microclimates where species can find suitable conditions even as the broader climate changes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ conservation efforts by humans can help mitigate the impacts of climate change    [1 mark]

    $\ce{→}$ species with mutualistic relationships may be better able to survive    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    This phrase illustrates the phenomenon of **upslope range shift**, in which species occupy niches at higher and higher elevations as **climate changes** ($\checkmark$). As such, it can be likened to an elevator in that the species themselves are getting higher. The higher up the mountain, the **less available habitat area** there is because the circumference of the mountain decreases with altitude. The species will have **reduced availability of locations** that can provide a niche ($\checkmark$), and so they will become extinct.\

    In countering the phrase, the species in question **could live perfectly well in another country** or mountain range in which the **effects of climate change are not so severe**; this will not lead to ‘extinction' because the **species will remain viable** in the more suitable habitat ($\checkmark$). Also, **conservation** work by humans may have recognised the threat and proved an **alternative habitat** for the species ($\checkmark$).
