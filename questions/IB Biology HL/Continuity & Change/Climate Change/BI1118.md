# BI1118

The spruce bark beetle (*Ips typographus*) is an invasive pest species of spruce and pine trees in Europe. They are one of many species that are impacted by climate change. Females burrow through the bark into phloem tissue, where they lay eggs. Once hatched, the developing larvae feed on phloem sap and burrow through the bark as they emerge as adults.

This would lead to an increased population of spruce bark beetles or more outbreaks per year.

Over many generations, these traits become more common in the population, which leads to **evolutionary changes** ($\checkmark$).

    4.
    $\ce{ -> }$ named technique, e.g. afforestation, forest regeneration, and peat-forming wetland restoration   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ description of the technique, e.g. afforestation is the process where trees are planted in deforested areas  [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Afforestation** ($\checkmark$) and **forest regeneration** ($\checkmark$) are two techniques that can be used to sequester carbon, which can help mitigate the impacts of climate change. Afforestation is when trees are planted in areas that have suffered from deforestation. The newly planted trees act as **carbon sinks** ($\checkmark$), which can help to reduce atmospheric carbon dioxide levels. Forest regeneration is somewhat similar to afforestation, but trees were removed due to forest fires or disease. These trees are replaced with new trees and also act as **carbon sinks** ($\checkmark$).
