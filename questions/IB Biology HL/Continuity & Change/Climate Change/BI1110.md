# BI1110

Which change contributes to a positive feedback cycle that amplifies global warming?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased absorption of atmospheric carbon dioxide by oceans as water temperature rises

- B.$\hspace{1em}$
Increased reflection of solar radiation as ice cover decreases

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased decomposition of previously frozen organic matter as permafrost thaws

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ ncreased carbon storage in forest vegetation as the frequency of forest fires rises

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C**  is correct because rising temperatures cause permafrost to thaw, allowing microorganisms to decompose previously frozen organic matter. This releases carbon dioxide and, under suitable anaerobic conditions, methane. These greenhouse gases strengthen the greenhouse effect, causing further warming and additional permafrost thawing. This is positive feedback because the initial temperature increase is amplified.

**Choice A** is incorrect because increased absorption of atmospheric carbon dioxide would tend to oppose warming rather than amplify it. Furthermore, increasing water temperature reduces carbon dioxide solubility, so warming alone does not increase the ocean's capacity to absorb carbon dioxide.

**Choice B** is incorrect because decreasing ice cover reduces the reflection of solar radiation. The darker surfaces exposed absorb more radiation, contributing to further warming.

**Choice D** is incorrect because forest fires release carbon stored in vegetation into the atmosphere rather than increasing carbon storage in that vegetation. Greater carbon dioxide emissions from fires can contribute to further warming.
