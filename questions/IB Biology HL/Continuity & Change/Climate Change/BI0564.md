3. The bar-tailed godwit (*Limosa lapponica*) nests and breeds on Arctic coasts and tundra and overwinters in the Southern Hemisphere. Predict how the increase in surface temperatures in the Northern Hemisphere may affect the survival of such a migratory bird. [1 mark]

2. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$ earth's surface emits longer wavelength radiation **OR** infrared radiation as heat [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ longer wavelength radiation **OR** infrared radiation is absorbed by greenhouse gases [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ longer wave **OR** infrared radiation is reradiated by greenhouse gases  [1 mark]
[2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The Earth's surface receives shorter wavelength radiation (electromagnetic radiation) from the sun and **emits longer wavelength radiation** ($\checkmark$) (infrared radiation) as heat. **Longer wavelength radiation is absorbed by greenhouse gases** ($\checkmark$) in Earth's atmosphere and is **reradiated** back to the Earth's surface ($\checkmark$). These processes mean heat is retained rather than released to space, which increases the Earth's temperature.

3. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$ survival decreases because of loss of resources **OR** loss of food **OR** loss of habitat  [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ survival decreases because of reduced reproductive success caused by changes in resource availability **OR** loss of food **OR** loss of habitat  [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ survival decreases because of mistiming of migration **OR** godwits arriving in Arctic breeding ground after key food sources have emerged   [1 mark]
[1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    As temperatures increase in the Northern Hemisphere, the availability of food and habitat may change.  The **survival** of godwits will **decrease** if these changes result in a **loss of food or habitat** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, global temperature increases may result in the start of spring/summer in the Arctic occurring earlier in the year. If godwits do not change the timing of their migration back to their breeding grounds to keep pace with the start of spring, they may miss the **emergence** of their **food sources** ($\checkmark$), meaning less food would be available for breeding birds and their chicks. This **change in the food availability** for breeding birds and their offspring would **lower reproductive success** ($\checkmark$) and therefore threaten the survival of the bird population.
