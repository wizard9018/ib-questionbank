1.  Deduce, with a reason, the biological sex of the foetus.    [1 mark]

2.  The biological father of the foetus has haemophilia, and the biological mother is a carrier of the disease. Using a Punnett square, calculate the probability that the foetus has haemophilia.    [3 marks]

3.  Haemophilia occurs in approximately 1 in 6000 biological males and 1 in 100,000 biological females. Suggest a reason for this difference.    [1 mark]

4.  Much research has been conducted into the use of stem cells to treat genetic diseases. Outline the advantages and disadvantages of the use of adult stem cells over embryonic ones in stem cell therapy.    [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  any **one** of the following\

    $\ce{ -> }$ biologically male **BECAUSE** they have a Y chromosome **OR** they have 22 pairs of homologous chromosomes **AND** 1 pair of non-homologous sex chromosomes        [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    The foetus is biologically **male** because they have a **Y chromosome** ($\checkmark$).

2.  any **three** of the following\

    $\ce{ -> }$ parental genotypes identified in the Punnett square; biological father: $\ce{X^hY}$ **AND** biological mother: $\ce{X^HX^h}$    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ offspring genotypes identified as $\ce{X^HY, X^hY}$    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ 0.50 **OR** 50% **OR** $\frac{1}{2}$    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

    ![1630](images/1630.png)

    The biological sex of the foetus is male, so it must inherit the Y chromosome from its biological father. The probability of a biologically male foetus having haemophilia is **50 %** ($\checkmark$).

3.  any **one** of the following\

    $\ce{ -> }$ it is a sex-linked recessive disease, **OR** the gene is carried on the X chromosome **AND** biological males only have one X chromosome, so if they have the recessive allele, they will have the disease, whereas biological females with one copy would be carriers    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ for a biological female to have haemophilia, they would need to have two copies of the recessive allele, whereas biological males only need one copy of the recessive allele to have haemophilia    [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    Haemophilia is more common in biological males because it is a **sex-linked disease** on the **X chromosome**. **Biological males** have only **one X chromosome**, so inheriting the **recessive allele results** in the **disease**, while **biological females** with **two X chromosomes** can be **carriers without symptoms** ($\checkmark$).

4.  any **three** of the following (*at least* ***one*** *advantage and* ***one*** *disadvantage must be given to gain full marks*)\

    ***advantages:***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cells can be obtained from the individual needing treatment, reducing the risk of immune rejection **WHILE** embryonic stem cells may be rejected by the patient's immune system [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ harvesting adult stem cells is generally less controversial **OR** has fewer ethical implications compared to embryonic stem cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cells have a lower risk of producing tumours compared to embryonic stem cells  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cell therapies generally face fewer regulatory hurdles and greater public acceptance compared to therapies involving embryonic stem cells    [1 mark]

    ***disadvantages:***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cells are multipotent **OR** are often restricted in the number of cell types they can produce **WHILE** embryonic stem cells are pluripotent **OR** can differentiate into any bpdy cell type [1 mark]    

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cells are limited in number within adult tissues **OR** can be challenging to obtain **OR** culture **WHILE** embryonic stem cells are more numerous OR easier to harvest    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cells have less potential for long-term proliferation **OR** durability in culture **COMPARED TO** embryonic stem cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adult stem cells are derived from fully developed tissues so typically more differentiated, **WHEREAS** embryonic stem cells are derived from a very early developmental stage so less differentiated    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    One **advantage** of using adult stem cells in therapy is that they can be obtained from the patient, **reducing the risk of immune rejection** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, harvesting adult stem cells is **less controversial** and has **fewer ethical implications** ($\checkmark$) compared to embryonic stem cells. However, adult stem cells are **multipotent** and **limited** in the **types of cells**($\checkmark$)  they can produce, unlike pluripotent embryonic stem cells. Moreover, adult stem cells are **less numerous** and **harder to harvest** ($\checkmark$) than embryonic stem cells.
