# BI1221

**Maximum mark: 16**

DNA and RNA are nucleic acids that are essential to all living organisms. ATP is a type of nucleotide.

1. Distinguish between ATP and the monomers of DNA in humans. [3 marks]

2. Outline the roles of the different types of RNA involved in translation.     [5 marks]

3. Describe the structure of the DNA double helix.  [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP contains three phosphate groups **WHEREAS** each DNA nucleotide contains only one phosphate group         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP has adenine for a nitrogenous base **WHEREAS** a DNA nucleotide can contain adenine, cytosine, guanine or thymine    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a component of ATP is ribose **WHEREAS** DNA nucleotides have deoxyribose instead        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP stores energy **OR** provides energy, **WHEREAS** DNA carries **OR** transmits genetic information                          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP can perform its function as a single monomer **WHEREAS** DNA can only perform its function, **OR** carry genetic material as a polymer            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP is found throughout the cell **OR** primarily in the cytoplasm **OR** mitochondria **WHEREAS** DNA is found primarily in the nucleus **OR** only found in the nucleus AND mitochondria            [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Both ATP and DNA molecules are nucleic acids which contain a phosphate group, a sugar and a base. The structural difference between a molecule of ATP and a molecule of DNA is that one ATP molecule contains **three phosphate groups** whereas one DNA nucleotide contains **one phosphate group** ($\checkmark$). In addition, the nitrogenous base in ATP is **adenine** whereas there are four possible nitrogenous bases in one nucleotide of DNA which are **adenine, cytosine, guanine and thymine** ($\checkmark$). The functional difference is that ATP **stores energy**, whereas DNA **carries genetic material** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (there must be at least one statement for each of mRNA, tRNA and rRNA to achieve maximum marks):

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA carries the genetic information from DNA to the ribosome            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA serves as a template for protein synthesis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ codons of mRNA  specify positioning of  amino acids along polypeptide **OR** codons guide the assembly of amino acids into a polypeptide         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tRNA molecules carry amino acids to mRNA **OR** ribosome    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anticodons on tRNA complementary to codons on mRNA allow amino acids to be positioned correctly along the polypeptide **OR** anticodons bind to codons to bring corresponding amino acid to the ribosome             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rRNA provides the ribosome's structural framework **OR** forms ribosomes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rRNA catalyses the formation of peptide bonds between adjacent amino acids     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rRNA facilitates binding of mRNA and tRNA        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA orchestrate protein synthesis during translation    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Together, mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA** orchestrate the process of **protein synthesis** ($\checkmark$) during translation. **mRNA carries** the genetic **information** from **DNA** to the **ribosome**  ($\checkmark$). **Codons** on the mRNA **specify the positioning** of **amino acids** along the **polypeptide** chain  ($\checkmark$). Each **tRNA** molecule has a specific **anticodon** sequence that is **complementary** to a **codon** on the mRNA  ($\checkmark$). This base-pairing specificity allows tRNA to bring the **corresponding amino acid** to the **correct position** to form the polypeptide  ($\checkmark$). The rRNA **catalyses** the formation of **peptide bonds** between adjacent amino acids  ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following: \
    *(appropriately labelled and correctly drawn DNA segment can be used in the answer)*

    $\ce{ -> }$ each strand of DNA is a polymer of nucleotides         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each strand has a sugar-phosphate backbone            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ four nitrogenous bases A **OR** adenine **AND** T **OR** thymine **AND** C **OR** cytosine **AND** G **OR** guanine              [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ two strands are anti-parallel **OR** parallel but in opposite directions **OR** one strand is in the 5' to 3' direction and the other strand is in the 3' to 5' direction    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ strands are twisted **OR** right-handed and turn into a double helix with around 10 base pairs per turn          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nucleotides consist of one phosphate group, one deoxyribose sugar and one nitrogenous base                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phosphate group is bound to the 5' carbon of the sugar **OR** deoxyribose **OR** pentose                           [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phosphate group is bound to the 3' carbon OH group of deoxyribose (of the next nucleotide)                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ covalent **OR** sugar-phosphate bond between phosphate and sugar        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nitrogenous base **OR** base is bound to the 1' carbon of the sugar **OR** deoxyribose **OR** pentose sugar             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ covalent bond between nitrogenous base and deoxyribose **OR** sugar **OR** pentose sugar                     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ two strands are held together by hydrogen bonds between nitrogen bases of two nucleotides **OR** 3 hydrogen bonds between guanine and cytosine **OR** 2 hydrogen bonds between adenine and thymine    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ complementary base pairing between nitrogenous bases    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ A pairs up with T AND C pairs up with G        [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    DNA is a nucleic acid, also known as a **polymer of nucleotides** ($\checkmark$) . Many nucleotides are joined together to form it. **One nucleotide** consists of three parts; a **phosphate group, deoxyribose sugar and a nitrogenous base** ($\checkmark$).

    One end of the **phosphate group** is bonded to the **5' carbon** of the **deoxyribose sugar** ($\checkmark$). On the other side of the nucleotide, **the phosphate group is bonded to the 3' carbon sugar** ($\checkmark$) of another nucleotide. Both bonds are covalent **sugar-phosphate bonds** ($\checkmark$). Repeated alternating sugar-phosphate bonds form the **sugar-phosphate backbone** ($\checkmark$).  The **1' carbon of the sugar is covalently bonded to the nitrogenous base** ($\checkmark$) also through a condensation reaction to make up one complete nucleotide.

    **DNA is a double helix** ($\checkmark$) in which nitrogenous bases form the centre of the structure, and **both strands are held together by hydrogen bonds** ($\checkmark$) between the complementary bases from each strand. **A** pairs up **with T**, and **C** always pairs up **with G** ($\checkmark$).
