# BI2446

**Maximum mark: 10**

Monozygotic twin studies have been used to investigate the effect of environmental factors on epigenetic tags.

The table below summarises data from three twin studies.

![BI2446](images/BI2446.png)

1. Define the term epigenesis. [1 mark]

2. Explain why monozygotic twins are suitable to investigate the effect of the environment on methylation patterns. [3 marks]

3. Describe how methylation affects gene expression. [2 marks]

4. Evaluate the validity of the following statement:
“Stress has the most significant impact on methylation in humans.” [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ development of differentiation patterns in cells of multicellular organisms [1 mark]
[1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ Epigenesis is the process by which **cells** in a **multicellular organism** develop **different functions** through controlled **gene expression**, influenced by **epigenetic modifications** without changing the DNA sequence ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ monozygotic twins share 100% of their DNA [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows comparison of identical DNA **OR** DNA is a control variable [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ environmental factors are the independent factors [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ differences between the methylation patterns are the dependent variable **OR** can be attributed to the environment [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ methylation differences can be quantified **OR** extent of DNA methylation at specific genes can be measured [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Monozygotic twins have **identical DNA** ($\checkmark$), making genetic differences **a controlled variable**, and **environmental factors** the **independent variable** ($\checkmark$). This means that differences between the methylation patterns of the twins can be **attributed to the environment** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ methyl tags attach to cytosine in gene promoters [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ methylation of promoters reduces transcription [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ methyl tags attach to amino acids in histones [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ methylation of histones can repress **OR** activate gene expression [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Methyl tags **attach** to **cytosine** in **promoters** ($\checkmark$) and **amino acids** in **histones** ($\checkmark$). Methylation of promoters **reduces transcription** ($\checkmark$).

4. any **four** of the following:

 ***Results could be valid because:***\
 $\ce{ -> }$ difference in methylation between the low- and high-stress environment is highest compared to the other variables **OR** the difference in methylation between the low and high-stress environment is 25%, but it is only 10% **AND** 15% for exercise and pollution respectively [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ experiments used monozygotic twins wo share one hundred percent of their DNA **OR** are suitable to be used as a control [1 mark]

 ***Results may not be valid because:***\
 $\ce{ -> }$ no test for significance of results **OR** statistical analysis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ small data set means there is limited confidence in results **OR** results may not be generalisable **OR** fit the wider population [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ only three environmental factors were investigated **OR** other factors not investigated may have more of an impact [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ uncontrolled variables may have influenced results **OR** it is difficult to control all the environmental variables to ensure the validity of results [1 mark]
 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 There is a **greater difference** in **% methylation** between the **high-stress** and **low-stress** environmental conditions compared to the other variables tested ($\checkmark$). The data could be valid because **monozygotic twins share 100% of their DNA** so they are a suitable control ($\checkmark$). The results may not be valid because there is **no statistical analysis** ($\checkmark$) and **uncontrolled variables may have influenced the data** ($\checkmark$).
