2. any **four** of the following (answer can be in a table format):

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** occur during meiosis I    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** promote variation      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are random processes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Crossing over occurs during prophase I **WHEREAS** random orientation occurs during metaphase I [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ During crossing over, there is an exchange of genetic information **OR** alleles **WHEREAS** during random orientation pairs of homologous chromosomes align randomly along the equator         [1 mark]
[4 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Both random orientation and crossing over occur during **meiosis I** ($\checkmark$), and are **random processes** ($\checkmark$) that promote **variation** ($\checkmark$), as they encourage the formation of genetically distinct gametes at the end of meiosis. However, crossing over occurs during **prophase I** and random orientation occurs during **metaphase I** ($\checkmark$). During crossing over there is an **exchange** of genetic information between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes, whereas during random orientation homologous chromosomes **independently and randomly align** on either side of the equator ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ 4 stages in meiosis I and meiosis II; prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prophase I: homologous chromosomes condense **OR** become visible       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prophase I: crossing over occurs between homologous chromosomes **OR** non-sister chromatids              [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ metaphase I: homologous chromosomes align on the equator     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ metaphase I:  random orientation **OR** random alignment of homologous chromosomes     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anaphase I: spindle fibres attach to the centromeres of each chromosome        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anaphase I: each chromosome in a pair **OR** each pair of sister chromatids, is pulled to either side of the cell      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ telophase I: cytokinesis occurs **OR** two nuclear envelopes form **OR** cells go from diploid to haploid      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prophase II: chromosomes condense and become visible [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ metaphase II: chromosomes **OR** pairs of sister chromatids, align on the equator of the haploid cells     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anaphase II: spindle fibres attach to the centromeres of each chromosome     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anaphase II: sister chromatids are pulled apart (now chromosomes in own right)         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anaphase II: chromosomes are pulled to either pole of the cell         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ telophase II: cytokinesis occurs **OR**  four nuclear envelopes are formed         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ telophase II: four haploid cells are formed         [1 mark]
[8 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Meiosis is a form of cell division that results in the production of four haploid gametes. It is split into meiosis I and II, which both consist of four stages; **prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase** ($\checkmark$).

    **Meiosis I**

    In prophase I, homologous chromosomes **condense** ($\checkmark$) and become visible, they form tetrads and **crossing over** ($\checkmark$) can occur between non-sister chromatids. In metaphase I, the homologous chromosomes align on the **equator** ($\checkmark$) and are subject to **random orientation** ($\checkmark$). In anaphase I, the chromosomes, each composed of a pair of sister chromatids, are pulled to **opposite sides** ($\checkmark$) of the cell and then in telophase I the nuclear membrane reforms and the cytoplasm divides during **cytokinesis** ($\checkmark$).

    **Meiosis II**

    In prophase II, chromosomes **condense** ($\checkmark$) and become visible, they then align at the **equator** ($\checkmark$) during metaphase II. In anaphase II the **sister chromatids are pulled apart** ($\checkmark$) and now, as chromsomes in their own right, move to **opposite sides** of the cell ($\checkmark$) and finally in telophase, the nuclear membrane **reforms** in each of the haploid cells ($\checkmark$), resulting in the production of four **haploid gametes** ($\checkmark$).
