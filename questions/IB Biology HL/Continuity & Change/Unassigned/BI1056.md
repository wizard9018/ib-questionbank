# BI1056

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how gel electrophoresis separates molecules and suggest a use for this separation technique. [3 marks]

2. Explain how compartmentalisation is advantageous to eukaryotic cells. [5 marks]

3. In eukaryotic cells, transcription occurs in the nucleus and translation occurs in the cytoplasm. Explain the benefits to the cell of this physical separation.     [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ separates molecules by passing them through a gel using an electrical charge    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ separates molecules by size **OR** charge, **OR** smaller **OR** shorter molecules move further than larger **OR** longer ones                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ separating DNA **OR** protein **OR** RNA,  **OR** DNA profiling **OR** DNA fingerprinting     [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Gel electrophoresis separates molecules by **size or charge** ($\checkmark$). The molecules are placed into wells in the gel, and an **electrical charge** is applied ($\checkmark$). This process can be used to **separate DNA fragments**, in DNA profiling ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows for division of labour    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enzymes and substrates involved in the same processes can be in close proximity to each other          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$   reactions can happen faster **OR** more efficiently        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ optimal pH can be maintained in areas of the cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ substances that can cause damage to the cell can be safely stored **OR** reduces risk of damage from digestive **OR** hydrolytic enzymes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organelles can be moved around the cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ post-transcriptional modifications such as splicing can take place    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lower chance of non-specific interactions    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Eukaryotes having a compartmentalised structure means that enzymes and substrates involved in the same processes can be in close **proximity** ($\checkmark$) to each other. **Optimal pH** ($\checkmark$) can be maintained in certain areas of the cell so reactions can happen **faster** or more efficiently ($\checkmark$) . It also means that substances that can damage the cell such as digestive enzymes can be **safely stored** ($\checkmark$) , and organelles such as ribosomes can **move** around the cell ($\checkmark$). It also allows important processes to occur such as **splicing** of pre-mRNA ($\checkmark$), which increases the variety of polypeptides that can be made.

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ nucleus provides a controlled environment for transcription        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA protected within nucleus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transcription factors **OR** regulatory proteins can enter nucleus     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this separation allows for precise control over when and where genes are transcribed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosomes are located in the cytoplasm        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ newly transcribed RNA can be processed and modified in the nucleus before it is transported to the cytoplasm (for translation)         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this spatial separation prevents potential conflicts between transcription and translation machinery    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ modifications of mRNA in nucleus are essential for the maturation and stability of the RNA before it is transported to the cytoplasm     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosomes can simultaneously synthesise multiple polypeptides from a single mRNA molecule, increasing efficiency             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA molecules that are not correctly processed **OR** modified in the nucleus can be prevented from entering the cytoplasm for translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ helps maintain the accuracy of gene expression        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ regulatory proteins **OR** other factors can interact with mRNA molecules in the cytoplasm to influence their translation        [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The DNA is **protected inside the nucleus** ($\checkmark$) and the nucleus provides a **controlled environment** for transcription ($\checkmark$) to occur. **Transcription factors** can **enter** the nucleus ($\checkmark$) but DNA cannot leave, so this provides **precise control** over where and when the genes are **transcribed** ($\checkmark$). The **mRNA** is **modified** ($\checkmark$) before leaving the nucleus, and this is essential for its **stability** ($\checkmark$) and use in translation. **Ribosomes** are located in the **cytoplasm** ($\checkmark$) and this **spatial separation prevents conflict** ($\checkmark$) between transcription and translation machinery. Cytoplasmic ribosomes can **simultaneously synthesise multiple polypeptide** chains ($\checkmark$) from one mRNA strand, **increasing efficiency** ($\checkmark$). mRNA that has **not been properly processed** can be **prevented from leaving** the nucleus ($\checkmark$) **preventing incorrect** proteins from being formed by **translation** ($\checkmark$).
