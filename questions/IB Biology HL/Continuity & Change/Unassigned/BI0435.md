# BI0435

Which of the following contain(s) covalent bonds between monomers?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}

  | | |

  | :-- | :-- |

  | I. | Dipeptides |

  | II. | Amino acids |

  | III. | Triglycerides |

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Dipeptides (**molecule I**) contain covalent bonds between monomers. The condensation reaction between two amino acid molecules results in the formation of a peptide bond, which is a type of covalent bond. Since amino acids act as monomers in protein structures, this bond qualifies as a covalent bond between monomers.

Amino acids (**molecule II**) themselves do not contain covalent bonds between monomers. Each amino acid is a single monomer, composed of covalently bonded atoms within its structure. However, it lacks a linkage to another monomer, meaning it does not satisfy the condition of having covalent bonds between monomers.

Triglycerides (**molecule III**) are formed when one glycerol molecule reacts with three fatty acid molecules, creating ester bonds. Although ester bonds are covalent, glycerol and fatty acids are not identical monomers. Since polymers consist of repeating monomer units, triglycerides do not qualify as polymers, meaning the bonds in their structure do not count as covalent bonds between monomers.

Only dipeptides contain covalent bonds between monomers, so the correct answer to this question is **Choice A**.

**Choices B, C** and **D** are incorrect because they include incorrect molecules.
