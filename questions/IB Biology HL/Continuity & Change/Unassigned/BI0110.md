# BI0110

**Maximum mark: 7**

A student wants to set up an aquatic mesocosm using seawater to simulate conditions in the food web (shown in the image below) and test its stability. He used seawater and sand from the area to set up the system and added some small organisms to the mesocosm.

![BI0110fw](images/BI0110fw.png)
[Source: Adapted from Cmglee. (2003, October). *File: Arctic Food Web.svg - Wikimedia Commons*. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved April 14, 2022, from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Arctic_food_web.svg. Copyright under CC BY-SA 3.0]

1. In the food web shown, identify an autotroph. [1 mark]

2. State a reason why phytoplankton shown in the food web would be a good addition to the mesocosm. [1 mark]

3. Discuss how saprotrophs perform a necessary function for the mesocosm. [3 marks]

4. Describe the disadvantages of using mesocosms in scientific investigations. [2 marks]

2. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ phytoplankton are autotrophs **OR** can add energy to the mesocosm through photosynthesis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ to provide energy continuously to the closed system [1 mark]
[1 mark]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    The phytoplankton **are autotrophs** ($\checkmark$). Autotrophs are necessary for all ecosystems because they produce organic compounds through photosynthesis.

3. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ saprotrophs secrete enzymes into the environment [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ saprotrophs break down food outside their bodies into simpler substances [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ saprotrophs are found in the water **OR**  sand [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ saprotrophs recycle nutrients in ecosystems [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ saprotrophs sometimes remove toxic compounds [1 mark]
[3 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Saprotrophs **recycle nutrients in ecosystems** ($\checkmark$) by **secreting enzymes into the environment** ($\checkmark$) which **break down organic material into simpler substances** ($\checkmark$) which they then absorb and use for food.  Saprotrophs can also **break down toxic compounds** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ large organisms may not fit in the mesocosms **OR** sometimes not all organisms from the ecosystem can be added to the mesocosm **OR** mesocosms are sometimes limited in size[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mesocosms are limited in their capacity to simulate natural conditions [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ small organisms added from the field to a mesocosm may be stressed [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mesocosms need to be monitored regularly [1 mark]
[2 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Mesocosms are experiments set up in laboratories that are **limited in their physical space** ($\checkmark$). Only small organisms can be used in the experiments and the organisms **can be stressed** once inside a mesocosm ($\checkmark$).
