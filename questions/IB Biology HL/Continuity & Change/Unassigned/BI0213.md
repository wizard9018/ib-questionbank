# BI0213

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the events which would lead to an individual being born with Down syndrome, which is characterised by the presence of three copies of chromosome 21. [4 marks]

2. Explain how meiosis increases variation.    [3 marks]

3. Outline the stages of meiosis. [9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ non-disjunction as the cause of trisomy **OR** having three copies of a chromosome **OR** an extra chromosome [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes do not separate properly during cell division     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ both chromosomes from a homologous pair move to the same pole **OR** end of the cell [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some gametes receive an extra chromosome    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the chances of non-disjunction increase with the mother's age **OR** there is a positive correlation between the mother's age and non-disjunction [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    **Nondisjunction** ($\checkmark$) is the reason individuals are born with an **extra chromosome**  which is the cause of the condition down syndrome. Nondisjunction can happen when the chromosomes **do not separate properly** during the meiosis. ($\checkmark$) For example, **homologous pair moves to the same end of the cell** ($\checkmark$). The likelihood of non-disjunction increases for individuals with **older mothers** ($\checkmark$).

2.    any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ random orientation of **homologous** pairs in metaphase I [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ independent assortment increases the number of potential combinations of alleles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over during prophase I **OR** exchange of sections of DNA between non-identical sister chromatids leads to new combinations of alleles **OR** recombinant chromatids     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduction division **OR** production of haploid cells allowing for fertilisation   [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Meiosis increases genetic variation through three primary mechanisms: **crossing over during prophase I**, where **non-identical chromatids** of homologous pairs exchange segments of genetic material ($\checkmark$); **independent assortment during metaphase I**, leading to a multitude of possible genetic combinations ($\checkmark$); and the formation of unique **haploid gametes** allowing for **fertilisation** during sexual reproduction ($\checkmark$).

3.    any **nine** of the following (accept marking points clearly shown in an annotated diagram):

    $\ce{ -> }$ meiosis has eight stages, prophase I, metaphase I, anaphase I and telophase I, and prophase II, metaphase II, anaphase II and telophase II    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ meiosis I is called a reduction division; it reduces the number of chromosomes by half **OR** produces haploid cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ meiosis II separates sister chromatids to produce cells with a single copy of one chromosome from each homologous pair [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ meiosis produces four unique haploid nuclei [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Prophase I}}$ nuclear membrane breaks down and condensed chromosomes are clearly visible [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Metaphase I}}$, homologous chromosomes line up on the equator [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Anaphase I}}$, spindle fibres contract and separate the chromosomes of each homologous pair **OR** homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite poles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Telophase I}}$ two haploid nuclei are formed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Prophase II}}$, chromosomes condense, and the nuclear membrane breaks down [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Metaphase II}}$, chromosomes line up on the equator **OR** each chromosome is attached to both poles (at the centromere)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Anaphase II}}$, spindle fibres contract and sister chromatids are separated and move to opposite poles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in $\underline{\textrm{Telophase II}}$ two haploid nuclei are formed from each cell, 4 haploid cells in total     [1 mark]

    [9 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    In meiosis, there are **two cycles of prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase** (meiosis I and meiosis II). Meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes and produces 2 haploid nuclei; this is called a **reduction division.** ($\checkmark$) Meiosis II separates **sister chromatids** ($\checkmark$) and produces **four haploid nuclei** ($\checkmark$) in total.

    **Meiosis I**

    In **prophase I**, chromosomes are made of sister chromatids and are **visible** (e.g. 46 chromosomes with two strands in human cells). **The nuclear membrane breaks down** ($\checkmark$). In **metaphase I**, **homologous chromosomes line up on the equator** ($\checkmark$). The centromere of each chromosome is attached to one pole. In **anaphase I** spindle fibres contract and **homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite poles** ($\checkmark$). In **telophase I, two haploid nuclei form** ($\checkmark$).

    **Meiosis II**

    In **prophase II** the **nuclear membrane breaks down, and chromosomes condense** (haploid number, e.g. 23 in a human cell) ($\checkmark$). In **metaphase II, chromosomes are lined up on the equator** ($\checkmark$), and each centromere is attached to both poles. In **anaphase II, spindle fibres contract** and **sister chromatids are separated** and are pulled to opposite poles ($\checkmark$). **In telophase II**, two haploid nuclei form from each cell containing haploid number of chromosomes ($\checkmark$).
