# BI2894

**Maximum mark: 7**

A specific region of a DNA coding strand was investigated to determine the DNA sequence. PCR was used to amplify fragments of the template strand derived from this coding strand.

The electropherogram below shows the resulting DNA sequence of the DNA fragments.

![BI2894a](images/BI2894a.png)

1. Identify the DNA sequence of the coding strand based on the electropherogram. [1 mark]

2. Explain why PCR is used before the sequencing begins. [2 marks]

3. State the direction in which DNA replication occurs. [1 mark]

 The template DNA fragments were then run through gel electrophoresis, the results of which are shown below. The boxes at the top of the diagram represent the four bases.

 ![BI2894b](images/BI2894b.png)

4. The first box, located on the far left, has been identified as thymine. Based on this, identify the bases **X**, **Y** and **Z**. [1 mark]

5. Distinguish between the nucleic acid fragments circled in the diagram [1 mark]

6. Outline **one** reason why DNA sequencing is often preferred over protein sequencing in the study of evolutionary relationships between organisms. [1 mark]

 The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is important before sequencing to **produce multiple copies** ($\checkmark$) of DNA fragments in a **short period of time** ($\checkmark$) ready to be sequenced.

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA sequences include both coding and non-coding regions **WHEREAS** protein sequencing only reflects the expressed portion of genes [1 mark]
