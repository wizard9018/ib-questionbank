# BI0048

**Maximum mark: 5**

Gel electrophoresis is a technique often used to analyse DNA molecules. It includes a gel and an electric field. The diagram below shows the outcome of a gel electrophoresis run.

1. DNA is a type of nucleic acid. State one location where DNA is found in cells. [1 mark]

2. Deduce, with a reason, which direction the DNA fragment moves through the gel. [2 marks]

3. Distinguish between the functions of DNA and RNA in unicellular organisms. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
$\hspace{1.5em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ nucleus **OR** nucleoid **OR** mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts **OR** plasmids [1 mark]

[1 mark]

$\hspace{1.5em}$:u[Sample answer]:

$\hspace{1.5em}$ One place where DNA is found in the cell is in the **nucleus**($\checkmark$).

2. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ towards the positive electrode **OR** from negative to positive electrode **OR** downwards [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is negatively charged **OR** phosphate group of DNA is negatively charged **OR** DNA is an acid [1 mark]
[2 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid; therefore, as an acid, it is **negatively charged** ($\checkmark$). The phosphate group present in nucleotides also gives DNA a negative charge.  In an electric field, opposites attract; therefore, negatively charged DNA would be travelling **towards the positively charged electrode** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA stores genetic information **WHILST** RNA carries this information from DNA to ribosomes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA replicates before cell division so genetic information can be passed to next generation **WHILST** RNA does not replicate, it carries information within the cell, not between generations [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is used as a template for RNA synthesis, **WHILST** RNA has a variety of functions such as carrying information to the ribosome for translation (any described role for tRNA, rRNA or any other correct function is acceptable) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is transcribed **WHILST** RNA is translated [1 mark]
[2 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    DNA is the cell's long-term **storage of genetic information** and it **replicates** before cell division, to allow it to transmit this information from **one generation to the next**. RNA does not transmit information from one generation to the next but instead carries the genetic information from the **DNA to the ribosomes** ($\checkmark$) of the **same cell** ($\checkmark$).  DNA is the **template for RNA** and the RNA is made during **transcription**, This RNA  is then used during the process of **translation** ($\checkmark$). Translation involves ribosomes, containing **rRNA**, that read the **code**, and **tRNA** which brings in the **amino acids** to form the polypeptide chain ($\checkmark$).
