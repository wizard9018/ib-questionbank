# BI0467

The figure below illustrates the results of an experiment based on the methods used by Meselson and Stahl. Initially, bacteria were cultured in a medium containing only the heavy isotope of nitrogen ($^{15}$N) over multiple generations. The DNA extracted from these bacteria formed the band shown in the centrifuge tube labelled as Generation 0. Subsequently, these bacteria were transferred to a medium with only the light isotope of nitrogen ($^{14}$N). After undergoing one round of binary fission, some of the bacteria were removed, their DNA was extracted, and this underwent centrifugation. The results of the centrifugation are shown as Generation 1. The process was repeated after another round of binary fission in the $^{14}$N medium, with the results displayed as Generation 2.

![meselson & stahl semi-conservative centrifugation results](<images/meselson & stahl semi-conservative centrifugation results.png>)

What evidence in Generation 2 supports the theory that DNA replication is semi-conservative?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ There are two bands; a band for the heavy $^{15}$N and a band for the light $^{14}$N

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ There are two bands; both bands contain half $^{14}$N and half $^{15}$N

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ There are two bands; a band for a hybrid of $^{15}$N - $^{14}$N DNA and a band for the light $^{14}$N molecule

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ There are two bands; both bands only contain $^{14}$N

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
This experiment refers to Meselson and Stahl's investigation that demonstrated that DNA replication is semi-conservative. Based on the diagram, there are two distinct bands in the second generation. The relative weight of this band pattern indicates that DNA replication is semi-conservative.

If replication were conservative, then the two bands would have consisted of one band with only heavy $^{15}$N and one band with only light $^{14}$N (**Choice A**).

Semi-conservative replication results in two bands where one band contains only light $^{14}$N and the other contains both $^{14}$N and $^{15}$N. The band containing both $^{14}$N and $^{15}$N can be seen in the diagram between $^{14}$N and $^{15}$N (**Choice C**).

If replication were dispersive, there would be only one band which would consist of both $^{14}$N and $^{15}$N (**Choice B**).

It is not possible to have two bands containing only $^{14}$N at generation 2 because they would have the same mass which would result in only one band of DNA (**Choice D**).
