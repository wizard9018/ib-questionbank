# BI0468

The table below shows the percentage of nitrogenous bases found in the DNA of *Escherichia coli* bacteria.

| Adenine | Cytosine | Guanine | Thymine |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 32\% | 18\% | 18\% | 32\% |

What conclusion can be made based on the data shown in the table above?

-     A. There is complementary base pairing between adenine and thymine, and between cytosine and guanine

-    B. The total amount of cytosine and guanine is less because there are fewer hydrogen bonds between cytosine and guanine

-    C. The bacteria contain a circular chromosome which prevents errors when nitrogenous bases pair up

-    D. The percentage composition of base pairs found in chromosomes and plasmids is different

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The percentage of adenine bases equals that of thymine, and the percentage of guanine equals that of cytosine. These percentages support the rules of complementary base pairing where adenine pairs with thymine and cytosine pairs with guanine.

Even though the total amount of cytosine and guanine is less than the total amount of adenine and thymine, this does not mean that the hydrogen bonds between cytosine and guanine are fewer. There are three hydrogen bonds between cytosine and guanine compared to two hydrogen bonds between adenine and thymine (**Choice B**).

Bacterial chromosomes are circular. However, this does not mean that there are fewer errors in base pairing compared to linear chromosomes (**Choice C**).

Bacteria are prokaryotes which generally have DNA in the form of a single chromosome, and also plasmids. The base pair rules apply to both forms of DNA, but this cannot be concluded from the table (**Choice D**).
