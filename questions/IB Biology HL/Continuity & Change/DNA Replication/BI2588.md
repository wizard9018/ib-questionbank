**Maximum mark: 10**

The diagram below shows a DNA molecule at the replication fork. The dotted lines help to visualise the sugar-phosphate backbone as the two strands are separated.

2. State the name of the enzyme responsible for breaking the hydrogen bonds between the two DNA strands. [1 mark]

3. Describe the semiconservative nature of DNA replication which results in each daughter DNA molecule being identical to the original. [2 marks]

4. An RNA primer has been added to each strand of DNA. Label the nitrogenous base in the RNA primer by writing the correct letter in the shaded rectangle. [1 mark]

5. Draw the next nucleotides that will be incorporated into each strand by the enzyme DNA polymerase III. [2 marks]

6. State the role of DNA primase in DNA replication. [1 mark]

7. Compare and contrast DNA replication on the leading and lagging strands. [2 marks]

7. any **two** of the following *(must include **one** similarity and **one** difference to achieve full marks):*

 ***Similarities***\
 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** leading and lagging strands have DNA nucleotides added in 5’ → 3’ direction **OR** have DNA nucleotides added to 3’ carbon of pentose sugar [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ on **BOTH** leading and lagging strands DNA polymerase III adds new DNA nucleotides [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** strands require RNA primers to initiate replication [1 mark]

 ***Differences***\
 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is synthesised continuously on leading strand **WHEREAS** occurs discontinuously **OR** in Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ RNA primer is only needed once on leading strand **WHEREAS** primers are required repeatedly on lagging strand. [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ lagging strand requires DNA ligase to join fragments **WHEREAS** leading strand does not require DNA ligase [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ leading strand synthesis occurs toward the replication fork **WHEREAS** lagging strand synthesis occurs away from the replication fork [1 mark]

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 On both the leading and lagging strands, **DNA polymerase III** ($\checkmark$) adds **nucleotides** in the **5’ to 3’ direction** ($\checkmark$). However, DNA is synthesised **continuously** on the **leading strand**, whereas it is synthesised **discontinuously in Okazaki fragments** on the **lagging strand** ($\checkmark$).
