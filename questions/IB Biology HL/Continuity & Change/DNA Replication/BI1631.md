# BI1631

**Maximum mark: 6**

Using evidence collected from a crime scene, genetic profiles were amassed to aid in identifying the assailant (the person who committed the crime).

![BI1631 fig1](<images/BI1631 fig1.png>)

1. Deduce, with a reason, who is most likely to have committed the crime. [1 mark]

2. The genetic profiles from the crime scene were created using short tandem repeats (STRs). Explain why STRs are often used in genetic profiling. [2 marks]

3. Explain why a control sample from someone who was not involved in the crime was included in the genetic profiles. [1 mark]

4.

 1. Forensic scientists were only able to recover a small volume of the suspect's DNA from the victim's clothing. State which technique was used to make many copies of the DNA to create the genetic profiles. [1 mark]

 2. The technique you named in part (i) uses a specialised DNA polymerase, Taq polymerase, to make many copies of the DNA sample. State what property of Taq polymerase makes it suitable for this technique. [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

 ![1631](images/1631.png)

 **Suspect 1** is most likely to have committed the crime because the genetic profile from the victim's clothing contains **all of the fragments** from **suspect 1** and the **victim** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **two** of the following\

 $\ce{ -> }$ STRs vary in the number of times the sequence is repeated **OR** vary in length [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ STRS are inherited from one generation to the next so can be used to infer relationships between individuals [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ STRs are distributed throughout the human genome, making them readily available for analysis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ STR regions are quite short so are easily amplified using polymerase chain reaction **OR** PCR techniques [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ combination of STRs is unique **OR** almost unique to each person [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the size of the STR determines how far it migrates **OR** its position on a genetic profile [1 mark]

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

 STRs are used to create genetic profiles because they **vary** in the number of times the **sequence is repeated** in **individuals** ($\checkmark$). E**veryone**, except for monozygotic twins, has a **unique combination of STRs, giving them a unique DNA profile** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **one** of the following\

 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

 A control sample was used in the genetic profiles to **minimise the risk of false positives** ($\checkmark$).
