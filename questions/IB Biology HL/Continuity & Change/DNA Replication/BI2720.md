# BI2720

During DNA replication, which enzyme primarily proofreads and corrects mismatched bases before continuing elongation?

![BI2720](images/BI2720.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ DNA primase

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ DNA ligase

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ DNA polymerase I

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ DNA polymerase III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because DNA polymerase III can proofread and correct mismatched bases by removing errors immediately after adding nucleotides to the growing DNA strand during replication.

**Choice A** is incorrect because DNA primase is an enzyme that synthesises short RNA primers to provide a starting point for DNA polymerase during replication. \
**Choice B** is incorrect because DNA ligase is an enzyme that seals nicks (gaps) in the sugar-phosphate backbone of the newly synthesised DNA strand. \
**Choice C** is incorrect because DNA polymerase I removes RNA primers and replaces them with DNA. While it also has exonuclease activity, DNA polymerase III is the primary enzyme responsible for correcting mismatched bases as they occur.
