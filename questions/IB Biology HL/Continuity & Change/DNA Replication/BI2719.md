# BI2719

The diagram below shows DNA replication at a replication bubble. Identify the templates lagging strand(s).

![bidirectional DNA replication in replication bubble BR](<images/bidirectional DNA replication in replication bubble BR.png>)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ II only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ III only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I and IV

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In the diagram, DNA replication is occurring in both directions from a replication origin, forming a replication bubble. Each fork has two template strands, and DNA is being synthesised on both.
In DNA replication, new DNA is always synthesised in the 5' → 3' direction, because DNA polymerase reads the template strand in the 3' → 5' direction. The leading strand is synthesised continuously towards the replication fork, whereas the lagging strand is synthesised discontinuously away from the fork in Okazaki fragments.

![bidirectional DNA replication explainer diagram](<images/bidirectional DNA replication explainer diagram.png>)

If we apply this to the diagram provided: at the left-hand replication fork, the upper template strand (**I**) runs in the 5' → 3' direction, so polymerase synthesises the new strand right to left, which is towards the fork. This makes **I** a leading strand template. The lower template strand (**III**) runs 3' → 5', so polymerase synthesises the new DNA strand left to right, away from the fork using Okazaki fragments. This makes **III** a lagging strand template.
At the right-hand fork on the upper template (**II**), polymerase is still synthesising right to left, but now this means it is synthesising the new DNA away from the replication fork. This makes **II** a lagging strand template. On the lower template (**IV**), the new DNA strand is still being formed from left to right, but now this is towards the replication fork. This makes **IV** a leading strand template.

**Choice C** is correct because both **II** and **III** label sections of DNA that are templates for lagging strand synthesis.

**Choice A** is incorrect because it includes only strand **II** and omits strand **III**, which is also lagging. **Choice B** is incorrect because it includes only strand **III** and omits strand **II**. **Choice D** is incorrect because strands **I** and **IV** are synthesised toward the replication fork and are therefore leading strands.
