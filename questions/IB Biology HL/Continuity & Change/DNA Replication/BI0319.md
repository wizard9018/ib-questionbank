# BI0319

Which event happens in the leading strand but not in the lagging strand?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ DNA polymerase I removes the RNA primer and replaces it with DNA. 

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ DNA polymerase III adds free nucleotides in the direction of the replication fork.

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Ligase joins DNA fragments together.

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Helicase unwinds the strands by breaking hydrogen bonds.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

On the leading strand, DNA polymerase III synthesises continuously because DNA polymerase III adds free nucleotides to the 3' end in the direction toward the replication fork. In contrast, on the lagging strand, DNA polymerase III synthesises in short segments (Okazaki fragments) away from the replication fork. **Choice B** is, therefore, the correct answer, as this continuous addition in the same direction as the replication fork occurs only on the leading strand.

RNA primers are initially laid down on both strands to initiate synthesis. DNA polymerase I then removes these RNA primers and replaces them with DNA nucleotides on both strands after synthesis by DNA polymerase III. **Choice A** is incorrect.

As the lagging strand is synthesised in fragments (Okazaki fragments), DNA ligase is needed to join these fragments together. **Choice C**  is therefore incorrect as it only occurs on the lagging strand.

Helicase unwinds the DNA strands by breaking hydrogen bonds between the base pairs, an essential process that occurs on both the leading and lagging strands. It does not selectively work on only one strand but rather unwinds both in preparation for replication. **Choice D** is incorrect.
