# BI2723

A scientist replicates a DNA strand in vitro with a high concentration of cytosine analogs that cannot hydrogen bond. What is the most likely result?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Only the lagging strand will replicate

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Guanine will fail to pair correctly, increasing error rate

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The sugar-phosphate backbone will be broken

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ DNA helicase will not be able to unwind the double helix

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Cytosine normally forms three hydrogen bonds with guanine. If a cytosine analogue cannot hydrogen bond, guanine nucleotides will lack a proper complementary base. As a result, guanine in the newly synthesised strand cannot form stable hydrogen bonds with the cytosine analogue on the template strand. This reduces the stability of the DNA molecule and may disrupt accurate replication or cause it to stop entirely. **Choice B** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because both leading and lagging strands require base pairing for replication. If cytosine analogues can't pair, both strands will be affected, not just the lagging strand.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the sugar-phosphate backbone is held together by covalent sugar-phosphate bonds between adjacent nucleotides and is not affected by the ability to hydrogen bond.

**Choice D** is incorrect because helicase unwinds DNA by breaking hydrogen bonds between bases, but it acts before replication begins. The presence of cytosine analogues that fail to hydrogen bond affects base pairing during elongation, not the unwinding step.
