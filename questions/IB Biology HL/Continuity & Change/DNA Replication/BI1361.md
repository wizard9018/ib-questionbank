# BI1361

**Maximum mark: 16**

1.  Explain why Okazaki fragments are formed during DNA replication. [4 marks]

2.  Compare and contrast DNA replication on the leading and lagging strands. [7 marks]

3.  Outline the functions of the enzymes involved in prokaryotic DNA replication. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase can only work in the 5'-3' direction **OR** can only add nucleotides to the 3' end of the growing DNA strand            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is made up of two complementary strands running in opposite directions **OR** antiparallel strands            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the lagging strand runs in the 5'-3' direction away from the replication fork **OR** DNA polymerase must move in the 5' to 3' direction away from the replication fork **OR** in the opposite direction to helicase            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this results in discontinuous replication **OR** as the fork moves forward, DNA polymerase must fall off **AND** reattach on the newly exposed DNA strand            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ primase **OR** DNA primase creates many RNA primers on the lagging strand, so many small DNA fragments **OR** Okazaki fragments are formed            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA ligase joins the individual fragments together **OR** catalyses formation of sugar-phosphate bonds between fragments to form continuous lagging strand            [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:

Okazaki fragments are formed during DNA replication because **DNA polymerase can only work** in the **5' to 3'** direction away **from the replication fork** ($\checkmark$); however, the **lagging** strand runs in the **5' to 3'** direction **from the replication fork** ($\checkmark$).**Primase** synthesises many **RNA primers** on the lagging strand($\checkmark$) because as the fork moves forward, DNA polymerase must **fall off and reattach** to the newly exposed DNA strand. **DNA ligase** then **joins** the individual **fragments** together ($\checkmark$). 

2. any **seven** of the following (*answer must include at least **two** similarities and **two** differences to gain full marks*):

***Similarities***\

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** use complementary base pairing [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** require primers **OR** RNA primers to initiate DNA replication    [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** require enzymes **OR** DNA polymerase [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ replication occurs in the 5' to 3' direction on BOTH strands [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$ Both have proofreading **OR** repair mechanisms [1 mark]

***Differences***\

$\ce{ -> }$

 Okazaki fragments are formed on the lagging strand **BUT** not on the leading strand [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$

 template for leading strand runs in the 3'$\ce{ -> }$ 5' direction **WHEREAS** template for lagging strand runs in 5'$\ce{ -> }$ 3' direction [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$

 DNA replication is continuous on the leading strand **BUT** discontinuous on the lagging strand [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$

 the leading strand only requires one RNA primer to initiate replication, **BUT** the lagging strand requires many [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$

 DNA ligase is required on the lagging strand to stick many fragments together, **BUT** not on the leading strand [1 mark]

$\ce{ -> }$

 replication occurs in opposite directions **OR** towards the replication fork for the lagging strand **AND** away from the replication fork for the lagging strand [1 mark]

 [7 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:\

Both the leading and lagging strands use **complementary base pairing** ($\checkmark$), require **primers** for the initiation of replication ($\checkmark$), require **enzymes** ($\checkmark$) such as DNA polymerase and replication occurs in the **5' to 3' direction** on both strands($\checkmark$). However, the **leading strand** only requires **one RNA primer**, whilst the **lagging strand** requires **many** ($\checkmark$).  **Okazaki fragments** are formed on the **lagging strand** but not on the leading strand ($\checkmark$), so DNA replication is **continuous on the leading strand** but **discontinuous** on the **lagging** strand ($\checkmark$).

3. any **five** of the following:\

    $\ce{ -> }$

 helicase unwinds the DNA strand **OR** separates the strands **OR** breaks the hydrogen bonds between the strands [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$

 DNA gyrase **OR** gyrase prevents supercoiling of the DNA strands **OR** helps overcome the strains as helix unwinds [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$

 DNA primase **OR** primase synthesises RNA primers[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$

 DNA polymerase III forms the DNA strand starting at the RNA primer **OR** adds complementary nucleotides to the 3' end of the growing strand [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$

 DNA polymerase I replaces the RNA primer at the start of each Okazaki fragment with DNA nucleotides [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$

 ligase joins the DNA fragments **OR** Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand together **OR** catalyses the formation of sugar-phosphate bonds between Okazaki fragments **OR** DNA fragments [1 mark]

 [5 marks]

 :u[Sample answer]:\

 Several enzymes are involved in DNA replication, including **helicase**, which **unwinds the DNA** strand ($\checkmark$) to allow other enzymes to access the DNA and carry out replication. DNA **gyrase prevents supercoiling** of the DNA strands ($\checkmark$). **Primase** creates **RNA primers** ($\checkmark$), which **DNA polymerase III**  binds to in order to **start replication** ($\checkmark$) and **DNA polymerase I replaces the RNA primer** at the start of each Okazaki fragment with a DNA strand ($\checkmark$).
