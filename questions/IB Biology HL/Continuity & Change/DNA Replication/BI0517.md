# BI0517

A DNA profile was made using DNA from a crime scene. Locus TH01 was used to identify whether a suspect’s DNA matched the crime scene DNA. The suspect’s two alleles of the gene at Locus TH01 are shown below.

![BI0517A](images/BI0517A.png)

The alleles TH01-6 and TH01-4 were separated and visualised with gel electrophoresis. The resulting gel, with two bands of DNA, is shown below.

![BI0517B](images/BI0517B.png)

What is the DNA in Band B and why is this DNA at this position?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ TH01-4; smaller fragments travel faster and farther in the gel

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ TH01-4; smaller fragments are more attracted to the negative pole

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ TH01-6; larger fragments travel faster and farther in the gel

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ TH01-6; larger fragments are more attracted to the positive pole

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In gel electrophoresis, DNA is loaded into the gel at the negative pole and then an electric current is applied. Since DNA is negatively charged, it migrates through the gel to the positive pole. Shorter strands of DNA can move more quickly and thus travel farther, than longer strands of DNA.

All DNA fragments are equally attracted to the positive electrode (**Choices B** and **D**). In the gel electrophoresis results, Band B is shown as being closer to the positive pole. So, the DNA that made up Band B travelled farther in the gel than the DNA that made up Band A, meaning the fragment of DNA in Band B is shorter than the fragment in Band A, not larger (**Choice C**).

According to the image, TH01-6 has 6 TCAT repeats, while TH01-4 has 4 TCAT fragments. Therefore, TH01-4 is the shorter fragment of DNA. So, the DNA in Band B is TH01-4.
