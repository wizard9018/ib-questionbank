# BI1294

The table shows possible advantages and disadvantages of sexual reproduction via cross-pollination in flowering plants, compared to asexual reproduction. Which row of the table is correct?

| | Advantage of cross-pollination | Disadvantage of cross-pollination |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Reduces variation within a species | Limited ability to adapt to environment |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Promotes variation within a species | More pollen required |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ |Reduces variation within a species | More pollen required |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Promotes variation within a species | Limited ability to adapt to environment |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Cross-pollination is when pollen grains, the male gametes, are transferred from the anther of one flower to the stigma of another flower of the same species. When the pollen fertilises the ovules of another plant, it combines genetic material from two different individuals. This means that it promotes variation within a species so that plants are more likely to adapt to change in their environment, a great advantage. A disadvantage of cross-pollination is that, because it is brought about by animals or wind, there is a high risk that the pollen picked up from one flower, will not reach the stigma of another flower of the same species.  Therefore, plants need to produce a lot of pollen to increase their chances of cross-pollination. **Choice B**  is correct.

Cross-pollination is more likely to result in genetic diversity, which promotes variation and would increase the chances of plants being able to adapt to the environment; **Choice A** is incorrect. Although more pollen is required for cross-pollination, cross-pollination promotes variation within a species; **Choice C**  is incorrect. Although cross-pollination does promote variation within a species, this increases the chances of plants to adapt to changes in the environment; **Choice D**  is incorrect.
