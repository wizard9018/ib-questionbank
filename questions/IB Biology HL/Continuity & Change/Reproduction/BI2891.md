# BI2891

Which of the following is the primary biological reason for the difference in the number of gametes produced by males and females?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Females produce gametes only when fertilisation is imminent.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Males need to conserve energy by producing fewer gametes.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Females need to invest more in each gamete to provide nutrients for early development.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Males need to ensure they produce genetically identical offspring.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because female gametes (eggs) are large and contain significant cytoplasm, which provides the nutrients needed for the early development of the embryo after fertilisation. This requires more energy investment, so females produce fewer eggs over their lifetime.

**Choice A** is incorrect because males produce large numbers of small, mobile gametes (sperm) to increase the chances of fertilisation, not to wait for fertilisation to be imminent.

**Choice B** is incorrect because females do not only produce eggs when fertilisation is imminent. They produce eggs periodically throughout their reproductive life, with the timing of release being controlled by hormonal cycles.

**Choice D** is incorrect because males do not produce gametes to create genetically identical offspring. Instead, they produce large numbers of genetically diverse gametes to maximise the chance of successful fertilisation and increase genetic variation in offspring.
