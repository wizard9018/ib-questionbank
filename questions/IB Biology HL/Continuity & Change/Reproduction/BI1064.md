# BI1064

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how the sperm is adapted for its role of fertilising the egg. [4 marks]

2. Describe what happens when a sperm reaches the egg in the oviduct to when fertilisation is complete.    [5 marks]

3. Explain the role of hormones in the control of the ovarian and uterine cycles.     [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid nucleus **OR**  nucleus contains half of the genetic material to fuse with nucleus of egg during fertilisation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ acrosome contains enzymes that help penetrate the egg        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ midpiece packed with mitochondria **OR** lots of mitochondria to provide energy for movement [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tail **OR** flagellum propels the sperm towards the egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ streamlined shape **OR** very small to reduce resistance as it swims     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemotaxis **OR** can detect chemical signals released by the egg for navigation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ short lifespan ensures only most viable sperm have a chance to fertilise egg    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The sperm is very **small** and **streamlined** to **reduce** the **resistance** as it swims ($\checkmark$) through fluids. It has a **flagellum** to **propel** itself forward ($\checkmark$) and **many mitochondria** in the midpiece to provide the **energy**  ($\checkmark$) for swimming movements. The **acrosome** contains digestive **enzymes** to help the sperm **penetrate** the protective outer **layers of the egg** ($\checkmark$) and a **haploid nucleus** ($\checkmark$) to provide half the genetic material during fertilisation.

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ enzymes in sperm head help penetrate plasma membrane **OR** zona pellucida of egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm plasma membrane fuses with egg plasma membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles released from egg break down sperm flagellum **OR** mid-piece    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm haploid nucleus enters egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid nucleus of sperm forms pronucleus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA in pronucleus of egg and pronucleus of sperm **OR** pronuclei replicates    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pronuclei fuse **OR** nucleus of the sperm fuses with the nucleus of the egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ forms diploid zygote **OR** zygote contains complete set of chromosomes     [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When the sperm reaches the egg, it releases **enzymes** from the **acrosome** to help **penetrate** the egg’s cell **membrane** ($\checkmark$). The plasma **membrane** of the head of the **sperm fuses** with the plasma **membrane** of the **egg** ($\checkmark$), and the **haploid nucleus enters** the egg’s cytoplasm ($\checkmark$). **Vesicles** released from the egg **destroy** the sperm’s **flagellum** and **midpiece** ($\checkmark$). The sperm’s nucleus **forms** a **pronucleus** ($\checkmark$). The **DNA** in the pronucleus of sperm and pronucleus of egg **replicates** ready for mitosis ($\checkmark$), and then they **fuse** to form a **diploid zygote** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ hypothalamus secretes Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH)        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ GnRH stimulates the anterior pituitary gland to release gonadotropin hormones    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gonadotropin hormones are follicle-stimulating hormone **OR** FSH and luteinising hormone **OR** LH [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ LH  **OR** luteinising hormone triggers ovulation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ovarian cycle involves follicular phase **OR** development of follicles, ovulation, **AND** luteal phase **OR** corpus luteum    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ uterine cycle controls preparation and maintenance of uterine lining **OR** endometrium  ready for fertilised egg        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ FSH **OR** follicle-stimulating hormone stimulates follicular phase **OR** development of follicles
[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ follicles produce oestradiol as they grow    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oestradiol promotes the thickening **OR** increases blood vessel density of the uterine lining **OR** endometrium     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ positive feedback loop as oestradiol increases FSH and LH levels        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in levels of FSH and LH results in development of Graafian follicle     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ surge in LH (and FSH) leads to ovulation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oestradiol inhibits further release of FSH     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inhibiting FSH is a negative feedback loop **OR** prevents excessive follicular development    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ remaining follicular cells transform into the corpus luteum        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ corpus luteum produces progesterone (and oestradiol) to maintain the uterine lining    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high levels of progesterone and oestradiol prevent production of GnRH **OR** has negative feedback effect on GnRH **OR** hypothalamus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if pregnancy doesn't occur, corpus luteum degenerates, therefore decreasing progesterone and oestradiol levels, triggering menstruation        [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **gonadotrophin** hormones, **luteinising hormone** (LH) and **follicle-stimulating hormone** (FSH) ($\checkmark$) are released from the **pituitary** ($\checkmark$) when **GnRH** levels rise ($\checkmark$). **FSH** triggers the **development** of the **follicles** ($\checkmark$), and as they develop, they **release oestradiol** ($\checkmark$). The **rising** levels of **oestradiol** cause a further **increase** in **FSH** and **LH**, so this is an example of a **positive feedback** ($\checkmark$) loop. A **surge in LH**, and to a lesser extent, **FSH**, brings about **ovulation** ($\checkmark$). **Oestradiol** now **inhibits** further release of **FSH** ($\checkmark$) to prevent more follicles from developing, an example of a **negative feedback** loop ($\checkmark$). The remaining cells of the ruptured Graafian follicle now develop in the **corpus luteum**, an endocrine gland in its own right. This **secretes progesterone** and **oestradiol** to **maintain** the thickened **endometrium** ($\checkmark$), brought about by the **increase** in **oestradiol** before ovulation ($\checkmark$). If pregnancy does not occur, the **corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone** and **oestradiol levels drop**, and **menstruation** begins ($\checkmark$).
