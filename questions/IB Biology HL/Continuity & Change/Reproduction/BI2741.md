# BI2741

What is the correct chronological order of the events in oogenesis?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | Growth of primary oocyte|
    | II. | Mitosis of oogonia |
    | III. | Formation of secondary oocyte and polar body |
    | IV. | Migration of primordial germ cells and differentiation into oogonia |
    | V. | Completion of meiosis II after fertilisation |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ IV $\rarr$ II $\rarr$ I $\rarr$ III $\rarr$ V

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ II $\rarr$ IV $\rarr$ I $\rarr$ V $\rarr$ III

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ IV $\rarr$ I $\rarr$ II $\rarr$ III $\rarr$ V

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ II $\rarr$ IV $\rarr$ I $\rarr$ III $\rarr$ V

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because it reflects the correct chronological order of events in oogenesis:

-   1\. Migration of primordial germ cells and differentiation into oogonia (**Statement IV**): During early embryonic development, primordial germ cells migrate to the developing ovaries and differentiate into oogonia.

-   2\. Mitosis of oogonia (**Statement II**): The oogonia, which are diploid, undergo mitotic divisions to increase their number.

-   3\. Growth of primary oocyte (**Statement I**): Some oogonia enlarge and enter meiosis I, pausing in prophase I — they are now called primary oocytes.

-   4\. Formation of secondary oocyte and polar body (**Statement III**): At puberty, during each menstrual cycle, one primary oocyte completes meiosis I, producing a secondary oocyte and a polar body.

-   5\. Completion of meiosis II after fertilisation (**Statement V**): The secondary oocyte completes meiosis II only if fertilisation occurs, producing a mature ovum and a second polar body.

**Choice B** is incorrect because it places mitosis before the initial differentiation of germ cells into oogonia.

**Choice C** is incorrect because it places the growth of the oocyte before mitosis, which is biologically out of order.

**Choice D** is incorrect because it places mitosis before differentiation, which is inconsistent with embryonic development.
