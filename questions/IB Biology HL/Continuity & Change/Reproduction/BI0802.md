# BI0802

**Maximum mark: 7**

![BI0802A](images/BI0802A.png)

The following results were obtained when a researcher investigated the effect of temperature and pH on the germination of black-eyed peas. He placed the seeds in petri dishes with some cotton wool spread out evenly at the bottom. He then added water every day and monitored the growth by counting the seeds that had germinated.

The results below on the left show the cumulative percentage of seeds that germinated over a 28 day period. Different temperatures (5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 :sup[o]C) were investigated by placing the petri dishes in ovens at the appropriate temperature.
Again, pH was investigated by adding water (with pH adjusted to 2, 3.5, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 12.5) as shown in the results.

The results on the right show the final percentage of seeds that germinated at the different temperatures and pH.

![BI0802B](images/BI0802B.png)
[Source:Adapted from Cherrate, Mustapha, et al.( Mar,2023) *“Effects of Temperature, Ph, and Salinity on Seed Germination of Acinos Alpinus Subsp*. Meridionalis and FTIR Analysis of Molecular Composition Changes.” MDPI, Multidisciplinary Digital Publishing Institute, Retrieved May 5, 2023, from https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/6/4793, Copyright under CC0 4.0]

The percentage germination rate was calculated as follows, for the days the experiment was monitored.

Germination rate = (number of seeds that germinated / total number of seeds) x 100 %

A student wanted to set up an experiment to check the effect of salinity on germination and plant growth, using the data from above.

1. Based on the results shown,
 1. State the optimum temperature for germination of black-eyed peas. [1 mark]

 2. State the optimum pH for growth of black-eyed peas. [1 mark]

2. As an extension of the studies shown above, state what the student would
need to replace the pH solutions with if they wanted to investigate the effect of salinity on the germination of peas? [1 mark]

3. Other than pH and temperature, identify two controlled variables for setting up the experiment [2 marks]

4. Other than germination rate, state two other dependent variables that could be monitored to see the effect of salinity on the growth of black-eyed peas. [2 marks]

2. Any **one** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ by making equal volumes of aqueous solutions containing different masses of salt (NaCl) and adding this to the different petri dishes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ adding water to the control experiment, and adding at least five different concentrations of saline solutions to subsequent petri dishes [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The student can set up different petri dishes with the same numbers of black eyed peas. The student can then add water to the control plate, and **five different concentrations of saline solutions** ($\checkmark$) to the other petri dishes.

3. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ adding equal volumes of water and salt solutions (regularly) to all the petri dishes on a daily basis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ counting the germinated seeds for all the petri dishes at the same time of day [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ all the petri dishes are so placed that they receive the same light intensity on a daily basis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ number of seeds placed in the petri dishes **OR** placed same distance apart [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sizes of the petri dishes are the same [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ same mass of cotton wool [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ peas from the same source **OR** stored in similar conditions **OR** same age [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ accept any other valid control [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Other than pH and temperature, controlled variables are **the number of seeds** ($\checkmark$) placed in the petri dishes, and making sure **the germinated seeds are counted for all the petri dishes at the same time** ($\checkmark$) on a daily basis.

4. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ fresh **OR** dry weight (of plants at end of experiment) [1]

 $\ce{ -> }$ shoot length [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ root length [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ shoot to root ratio [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ number of root hairs [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ shoot **OR** root, width [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Other than germination rate, two dependent variables that could be measured are **shoot length** ($\checkmark$) and **root length** ($\checkmark$) at the end of the experiment.
