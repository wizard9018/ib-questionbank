# BI1095

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe the mutualistic relationship between insect-pollinated plants and pollinators.[4 marks]

2. Outline the self-incompatibility mechanisms that some plants have to prevent fertilisation resulting from self-pollination.    [4 marks]

3. Explain how insect-pollinated flowers are adapted to their function.    [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following (*must include at least **one** statement for insect-pollinated plants and at least **one** for pollinators*)

    $\ce{ -> }$ both benefit from the relationship        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ insect-pollinated plants need pollinators to transfer pollen from the anther to the stigma **OR** from the male reproductive structures to the female reproductive structures    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  insect-pollinated plants need pollinators for fertilisation **OR** seed production **OR** to reproduce        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  pollinators increase genetic variation of plant species through cross-polllination    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  pollinators obtain food from the nectar and pollen of flowers [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  nectar provides carbohydrate **OR** is an energy source        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  pollen provides proteins and other nutrients [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The relationship between insect-pollinated plants and pollinators is a mutualistic relationship because **both benefit** ($\checkmark$). Pollinators obtain **food** from the **nectar** and **pollen** ($\checkmark$) of flowers. Pollinators are necessary for insect-pollinated plants **to reproduce** ($\checkmark$). Pollinators **transfer pollen** from the **anther** to the **stigma** ($\checkmark$). Pollinators **increase** the **genetic variation** of the plant species through **cross-pollination** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ S-gene system **OR** genetic mechanisms control self-incompatibility in many flowering plants
 [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ S-locus contains many different alleles across a population [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each plant expresses specific S-allele **OR** S-alleles **OR** have specific alleles in BOTH pollen and stigma **OR** style **OR** carpel [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when pollen lands on a stigma, the plant compares the alleles **OR** S-allele of the pollen with the allele **OR** S-allele of stigma **OR** style **OR** carpel [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if  alleles **OR** S-alleles match **OR** are the same, the pollen is recognised as "self"  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gametophytic self-incompatibility **OR** GSI **OR** pollen tube growth inhibited within the style **OR** pollen tube is stopped during its growth towards ovule **OR** pollen tube forms but growth of it is arrested **OR** it does not grow down style **OR** carpel [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sporophytic self-incompatibility **OR** SSI **OR** pollen germination blocked [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ recognition of self-pollen activates biochemical pathways **OR** degradation of pollen RNA **OR** inhibition of pollen tube enzymes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ male gamete **OR** pollen nucleus breaks down before entering ovule **OR** ovary **OR** fusing with ovum [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ embryo formed but breaks down before seed formed [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Plants can prevent self-pollination through genetic mechanisms. If the pollen is recognised as ‘self’ by the plant the **germination** of the **pollen grain** to form a **pollen tube** can be **halted** ($\checkmark$). If the **pollen tube** does manage to germinate it is **prevented** from **growing down to the ovary** ($\checkmark$). The **pollen nucleus** may also **break down**, ($\checkmark$) preventing fertilisation if it reaches the ovary. If fertilisation is successful the **embryo** may be **prevented from developing** ($\checkmark$) into a seed.

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ flowers are used for sexual reproduction    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ insect-pollinated flowers have coloured petals to attract pollinators    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ insect-pollinated flowers have nectar, to attract **OR** reward, pollinators    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anthers produce pollen that is consumed by pollinators     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the nectary is inside the flower so that pollinators touch the anthers and stigma when feeding            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pollen grains have spines which stick to insects **OR** pollinator            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the ovule develops into the seed, which is used for dispersal            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the ovary develops into the fruit, which is used for dispersal **OR** attracts animals to eat it for seed dispersal        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a large amount of pollen is produced to increase the likelihood of pollination    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some species of flowers are structured so that only one species can pollinate them        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ flowers attract insects using smell **OR** gases         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sepal protects bud **OR** developing flower             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ filament holds anther in best position to touch pollinators [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ top of stigma is sticky so holds pollen in place for germination    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ flowering occurs at same time **OR** after certain night length for the same species to increase chance of cross-pollination        [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Flowers are adapted to their function in several ways. The structure and shape of flowers are adapted for reproduction. **Coloured petals** ($\checkmark$) and the **nectary** attract pollinators ($\checkmark$). When pollinators **feed** on nectar and **pollen** on one plant ($\checkmark$), some of the **pollen** from the anther **sticks** to the insect due to **spikes** on the pollen ($\checkmark$).  Then, when the same pollinator **feeds** on another plant's nectar or pollen, some of the pollen lands on the **stigma** ($\checkmark$) because the nectary is inside the flower. This is known as pollination and is used for **sexual reproduction** ($\checkmark$) in flowering plants. Both the **seed**, which develops **from the ovule** ($\checkmark$), and the **fruit**, which develops from the **ovary**, are used for seed dispersal ($\checkmark$).
