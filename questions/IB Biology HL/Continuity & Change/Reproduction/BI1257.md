# BI1257

Early epidemiological studies indicated that patients receiving hormone replacement therapy (HRT) had a reduced risk of coronary heart disease (CHD). Later studies conducted using randomised trials showed the opposite effect.

What could be deduced from these findings?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ There is a strong correlation between HRT and CHD.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ There is a strong causal link between HRT and CHD.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ There may be a correlation between HRT and CHD, but a causal link cannot be implied.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ There may be a causal link between HRT and CHD, but a correlation cannot be implied.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The information provided indicates that there might be some correlation between HRT and CHD, but the contradictory findings between early epidemiological studies and later randomised trials emphasise that causation cannot be assumed without further investigation or evidence. Later research showed that women’s socioeconomic status was the likely cause of the reduced risk of CHD, not HRT.

**Choice A** is incorrect, as a strong correlation should generally produce consistent results instead of the mixed results observed here. **Choice B** is incorrect as a cause cannot be determined based on this data alone. **Choice D** cannot be correct, as a causal link should always result in a correlation of some kind.
