# BI2742

Which of the following best explains why only one sperm typically fertilises the human egg?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The acrosome reaction modifies the sperm’s plasma membrane, allowing it to bind to the zona pellucida

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Sperm binding to  receptors blocks other sperm from recognising the zona pellucida

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The cortical reaction alters the zona pellucida to prevent additional sperm fusion after the first has entered

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Completion of meiosis II ensures only one sperm nucleus is capable of fertilising the ovum at any time

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
When the first sperm fuses with the egg membrane, the cortical granules inside the egg release their contents into the space around the egg. This causes biochemical changes that harden the zona pellucida and inactivate sperm-binding receptors, a process called the zona reaction. This mechanism prevents polyspermy, which would lead to an inviable zygote. **Choice C**  is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the acrosome reaction allows sperm to penetrate the zona pellucida, but multiple sperm can undergo this reaction simultaneously. It is the cortical reaction, not the acrosome reaction, that enforces the one-sperm rule.

**Choice B** is incorrect because binding to receptors initiates the acrosome reaction, but does not block other sperm. Only after one sperm fuses with the egg does the zona become impenetrable.

**Choice D** is incorrect because completion of meiosis II in the oocyte occurs after sperm entry, and has no role in limiting sperm fusion. It ensures the oocyte becomes haploid before the male and female nuclei fuse, but does not prevent polyspermy.
