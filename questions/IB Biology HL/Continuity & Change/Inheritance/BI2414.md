# BI2414

**Maximum mark: 10**

An ordered tetrad is a group of four haploid fungal spores contained within an ascus (a sac). These spores are arranged in a straight line, reflecting the exact order in which they were produced during meiosis.

![EditA](images/EditA.png)
[Source *File: Peziza nivalis (Port-neuf, Québec) 4.jpg*. (2022, April 8). Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved 03:33, January 15, 2025 from https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File: Peziza_nivalis_ (Port-neuf,_Qu%C3%A9bec)_4.jpg&oldid=647612529.]

The diagram below illustrates the process of crossing over and its effects on the arrangement of alleles within an ascus of a fungus. Scientists use biochemical markers of specific sequences or chromosomes to visualise the differences between spores.

![EditB](images/EditB.png)

1. Explain how the arrangement of spores in the ascus without crossing over reflects the segregation of alleles during meiosis. [1 mark]

2. Describe how the recombination event alters the spore arrangement by contrasting the diagrams. [1 mark]

3. In a study of meiosis in a fungal species, the following percentages of tetrad types were observed: 65% no crossing over, and 35% crossing over. Predict how these percentages would change if the gene locus was closer to the centromere. [1 mark]

4. Describe a method to determine the relative positions of three genes on a chromosome using this type of analysis and only testing one gene at a time. [2 marks]

5. Describe the key events in meiosis I and meiosis II that lead to the spore arrangements shown in the diagram. [3 marks]

6. Explain how a mutation affecting the recombination machinery in fungi might alter the ratio of the "crossing over" to "no crossing over" patterns in asci **AND** predict the consequences for genetic diversity. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes separate during meiosis I into separate nuclei [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the alleles are distributed into separate cells according to the law of segregation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ each haploid spore receives only one allele from each homologous pair [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 According to the law of segregation, only **one of the two copies of an allele is passed onto each daughter nucleus in meiosis** ($\checkmark$). Each spore has only one allele.

2. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over in prophase I results in a new allele arrangement [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the diagram on the right shows a different order of spores, resulting from a recombination [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ recombination between homologous chromosomes occurred during meiosis I in the diagram on the right [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ no recombination on the left diagram so the order does not change [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ genes near the centromere are physically constrained, limiting crossing-over events near the centromere [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 In the diagram on the right, the spores show a **different allele order** due to **recombination during meiosis I** ($\checkmark$). On the **left, no recombination occurred**, and the spores retained the **original parental allele order** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ genes closer to the centromere would have lower recombination frequencies [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the percentage of no crossing over would increase **OR** the percentage of crossing over would decrease [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 If the gene were closer to the centromere, the **recombination frequency** would **decrease**, and the percentage of **crossing over** would also **decrease** ($\checkmark$).

4. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ cross strains that differ at only one locus of interest each time [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ compare the recombination frequencies for each gene by measuring the proportion of asci showing crossing over versus no crossing over [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ genes with higher crossover frequency are farther from the centromere [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ compare the percentages to determine the relative positions of the 3 genes to each other and the centromere [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ results could be misleading if one of the genes is on the other side of the centromere [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The relative positions of the genes on the chromosome could be determined by doing one ascus analysis at a time. By **measuring the proportion of asci showing crossing over for each gene** ($\checkmark$), the frequencies of crossing over between each of the genes and the centromere can be determined, and then the **genes can be put in relative order based on those frequencies** ($\checkmark$).

5. any **three** of the following *(must include at least **one** statement for meiosis I and **one** for meiosis II)*

 $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes pair **OR** synapsis occurs in prophase I **OR** meiosis I [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes cross over in prophase I **OR** meiosis I [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes separate during anaphase I **OR** meiosis I [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ at the end of meiosis I the alleles **OR** homologous chromosomes are in two different cells **OR** spores [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sister chromatids separate during anaphase II **OR** meiosis II [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ at the end of meiosis II four haploid cells **OR** spores are produced [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the 4 haploid cells **OR** spores contain unique allele combinations [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 During **meiosis I, homologous pairs undergo crossing over** ($\checkmark$) and are separated into different cells. During **meiosis II, sister chromatids separate** into **different cells** ($\checkmark$), resulting in **four haploid cells** ($\checkmark$).

6. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ disrupt **OR** reduce frequency of crossing over in meiosis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ proportion of asci with crossing-over would decrease **OR** proportion of asci with no cross-over patterns would increase [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ fewer new combinations of alleles would be produced **OR** genetic diversity would decrease [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ no recombination could occur, leading to no independent assortment of alleles [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ without recombination offspring predominantly inherit only complete **OR** unchanged chromosomes from each parent [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 If the recombination machinery in fungi were mutated, the **number** of asci with no **crossing-over would increase** ($\checkmark$). This would **decrease genetic diversity** because there would be **fewer new combinations** of **alleles** ($\checkmark$).
