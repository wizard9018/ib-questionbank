# BI1245

A single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) at a particular location on a gene results in the production of a new allele. How many genotypes would be possible for this gene?

![BI1245](images/BI1245.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ 2

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ 3

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ 4

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ 6

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A gene with two alleles (A and B, for example) would have three possible genotypes (AA, BB, and AB).

**Choices A** and **C** are not possible for a gene with two alleles. **Choice D** could only be correct for a gene with three alleles (A, B, and C, for example), which would have six possible genotypes (AA, BB, CC, AB, AC, BC).
