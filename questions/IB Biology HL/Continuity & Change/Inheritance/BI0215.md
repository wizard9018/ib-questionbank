# BI0215

There are two alleles for flower colour in snapdragons. However, the result of a cross between two heterozygous plants yielded offspring with three different observed phenotypes, red, white and pink flowers. Which is the most likely explanation for this observation?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ One allele is dominant to the other.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$    There is codominance between alleles.
- C. $\hspace{1em}$    There is incomplete dominance between alleles.
- D. $\hspace{1em}$    The alleles are sex-linked.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
If there are two alleles for a gene, but there are three possible phenotypes, this could suggest incomplete dominance (**Choice C**). This is when the dominant allele doesn't completely suppress the effects of the recessive allele. **Choice A** is incorrect because if one allele was completely dominant over the other, then there would only be two possible phenotypes. **Choice B** is incorrect because if the alleles were codominant, then we would see both expressed in the phenotype. In this case, some petals would be white and others red, instead of the flower being pink. **Choice D** is incorrect because sex-linkage does not create an additional phenotype.
