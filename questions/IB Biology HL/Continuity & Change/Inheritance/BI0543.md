# BI0543

A biological male with haemophilia and a biological female without haemophilia have a daughter without haemophilia. What are the possible genotypes of the daughter?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | X:sup[H]X:sup[H] |
    | II. | X:sup[H]X:sup[h] |
    | III. | X:sup[h]X:sup[h] |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Haemophilia is a sex-linked recessive disorder carried by a recessive allele, X:sup[h], on the X chromosome. Since biological males only receive one copy of the X chromosome, a biological male with haemophilia must carry the recessive X:sup[h] allele. A biological female without haemophilia could have the genotypes X:sup[H]X:sup[H], meaning she does not carry any copies of the recessive allele, or X:sup[H]X:sup[h], meaning she carries one dominant and one recessive allele. However, the biological daughter must inherit her biological father's recessive allele, so the biological daughter will have one X:sup[h] allele (**Statement II** and **Statement III**). Since the biological daughter does not have haemophilia, she must have inherited the X:sup[H] allele from her biological mother (**Statement II**). Therefore, the only possible genotype for the biological daughter is X:sup[H]X:sup[h] (**Statement II**). **Choice B** is the correct answer to the question.

**Choices A, C** and **D** are all incorrect as they include **Statement I** and/or **Statement III**.
