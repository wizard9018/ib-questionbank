# BI1147

What can be concluded from the following karyogram?

![karyogram XYY](<images/karyogram XYY.png>)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ There was non-disjunction during meiosis in the mother.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ There was non-disjunction during meiosis in the father.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ There is one missing chromosome.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ There are two extra chromosomes.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The primary mechanism for the development of the XYY syndrome is a non-disjunction event during the sperm production process. Non-disjunction is when chromosomes fail to separate properly during nuclear division. In the case of XYY syndrome, during meiosis in the male, the two Y sister chromatids fail to separate properly during meiosis II. This results in a sperm cell ending up with two Y chromosomes. When this sperm (carrying YY) fertilises a normal egg with one X chromosome, the resulting zygote will have one X and two Y chromosomes (XYY). **Choice B** is correct.

The biological mother can only contribute X chromosomes. Non-disjunction in the mother could lead to other chromosomal abnormalities, such as Triple X syndrome (XXX)), but not to an increase in the number of Y chromosomes. **Choice A** is incorrect.

Missing chromosomes typically refer to conditions where a chromosome or a part of a chromosome is missing, such as Turner syndrome (XO), where an individual has only one X chromosome and no second sex chromosome. This individual has 48 chromosomes, not 45 so **Choice C** is incorrect.

While having 2 Y chromosomes in addition to an X chromosome does involve one extra chromosome, it does NOT involve two extra. **Choice D** is incorrect.
