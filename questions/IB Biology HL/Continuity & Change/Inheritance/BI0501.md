# BI0501

A biological female with blood type A had a child with blood type O. What are the possible genotypes of the biological father?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | ii |
    | II. | I:sup[A]i |
    | III. | I:sup[B]i |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The human blood typing system includes three alleles:
- I:sup[A]
- I:sup[B]
- i

I:sup[A] codes for blood type A, and I:sup[B] codes for blood type B. These alleles are codominant to each other (such that the genotype I:sup[A]I:sup[B] results in blood type AB), and dominant to the allele i (such that the genotype  I:sup[A]i results in blood type A and the genotype  I:sup[B]i results in blood type B). Only the genotype ii  codes for blood type O.

The genotype of the child must be ii. This child must have received one i allele from their mother (meaning the genotype of the mother must be I:sup[A]i) and one i allele from their father. So, the father could be of any genotype that contains at least one i allele. Therefore, the genotype of the father could be any of ii, I:sup[A]i, or I:sup[B]i.
