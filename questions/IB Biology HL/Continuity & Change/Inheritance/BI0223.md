# BI0223

What best describes the inheritance of the condition shown in the pedigree chart below?

![BI0223QB](images/BI0223QB.png)

- I. $\hspace{1em}$ Sex-linked trait
- II. $\hspace{.7em}$ Dominant trait
- III. $\hspace{.4em}$ Recessive trait

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I only
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ III only
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because it is an autosomal recessive trait. We know it is a recessive trait (**Statement III**) because the unaffected biological parents (individuals 5 and 6) have affected offspring (individual 8). That means both biological parents are carriers of the recessive allele.

It is not caused by a dominant allele (**Statement II**) because an affected child (individual 8) has unaffected biological parents. It is not a sex-linked trait (**Statement I**) because an unaffected biological mother (individual 6) and unaffected biological father (individual 5) have an affected daughter (individual 8). If this was a sex-linked condition, in order for her to be affected, she would have received the trait on both copies of the X chromosomes she inherited from her biological parents (if recessive sex-linked) or at least from one biological parent (if dominant sex-linked, but then at least one of the biological parents should have had the condition).
