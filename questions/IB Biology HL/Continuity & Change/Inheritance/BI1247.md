# BI1247

In domesticated horses (*Equus caballus*), coat colour is affected by a gene that has a cream (CR) allele. A chestnut horse with no cream allele has a reddish-brown coat. A horse with one copy of the cream allele is a palomino, with a golden coat and a pale mane and tail. A horse with two copies of the cream allele is a cremello, with a cream or off-white coat.

![BI1247](images/BI1247.png)

What can be deduced about the inheritance of coat colour controlled by this gene?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ It exhibits complete dominance.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ It exhibits incomplete dominance.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ It exhibits codominance.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ It does not follow known inheritance patterns.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A gene exhibiting incomplete dominance displays an intermediate phenotype, as seen here. The cream allele and non-cream alleles are both partially expressed in the heterozygous individual, resulting in a palomino horse.

**Choice A** cannot be correct because if the gene exhibited complete dominance then horses with one or two copies of the cream allele would display the same phenotype (cremello and palomino would be indistinguishable). **Choice C** is incorrect because with codominance both alleles are fully expressed leading to a combination effect (some chestnut and some cream hair, for example) instead of an intermediate phenotype. **Choice D** is incorrect because this example in the question shows incomplete dominance and this is a well-understood inheritance pattern.
