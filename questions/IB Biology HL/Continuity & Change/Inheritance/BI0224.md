# BI0224

**Maximum mark: 11**

1. Sickle cell anaemia is caused by the allele Hb$^S$. The normal allele is Hb$^A$.

 ![BI0224](images/BI0224.png)

 1. Identify the genotype of individuals 1 and 2. [1 mark]

 2. Predict the probability of individuals 5 and 6 having another child that is unaffected. Justify your answer. [2 marks]

 3. If individual 7 had a child with an affected biolgical female, identify the possible genotypes of the child. Justify your answer. [2 marks]

2. Deduce, **with evidence**, the pattern of inheritance of sickle cell anaemia that is shown in this pedigree chart. [3 marks]

3. Distinguish between sex-linked inheritance and autosomal inheritance in humans. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
 1.
 $\ce{ -> }$ Hb$^A$ Hb$^S$ **AND** Hb$^A$ Hb$^S$ [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
 The genotypes of individuals 1 and 2 must be **Hb$^A$ Hb$^S$** ($\checkmark$) (heterozygous) because they are unaffected, but have affected children.

 2. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ 50% **OR** 1:1 **OR** ½ [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ individual 8 is affected, so the unaffected parent must be heterozygous **OR** Punnett square showing the outcomes between heterozygous and homozygous recessive individuals [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
 The chances are **50%** ($\checkmark$). The **unaffected biological father** had an **affected biological daughter**, so he must be **heterozygous** ($\checkmark$). Hb$^A$ Hb$^S$ and Hb$^S$ Hb$^S$ parents have one out of two chances to have an affected child.

 3. Any **two** of the following (annotated Punnett square is acceptable for full marks):

 $\ce{ -> }$ possible genotypes are Hb$^A$ Hb$^S$ and Hb$^S$ Hb$^S$ **OR** offspring can be only heterozygous or homozygous recessive [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ individual 7 is heterozygous **OR** individual 7 can pass on either allele, Hb$^A$ or Hb$^S$ to their offspring [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ affected biological females will pass the recessive allele to all offspring **OR** biological mother would be Hb$^S$ Hb$^S$ genotype [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
The offspring could be **Hb$^A$ Hb$^S$ or Hb$^S$ Hb$^S$** ($\checkmark$) because individual 7 has an affected biological mother, but isn't affected themselves, so they must be **heterozygous** ($\checkmark$) for sickle cell anaemia. If they have a child with an affected biological female, the child would receive the **recessive allele** from the biological mother ($\checkmark$) but could receive the dominant or recessive allele from the biological father.

2. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ sickle cell anaemia is recessive [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BECAUSE** two unaffected **OR** heterozygous **OR** carrier parents had an affected child [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sickle cell anaemia is controlled by a gene on an autosome **OR** is an autosomal recessive trait **OR** is not a sex-linked trait [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BECAUSE** an affected bioloical mother can have an unaffected biological son [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
 This pedigree chart shows that **sickle cell anaemia is a recessive trait** ($\checkmark$). Evidence for this is that **individuals 1 and 2 are unaffected but can have an affected child** ($\checkmark$)
. This pedigree chart also shows that this is **not a sex-linked trait** ($\checkmark$)
. Evidence for this is that **an affected biological mother can have an unaffected biological son** ($\checkmark$); if it was a sex-linked trait, all of the biological sons of an affected biological female would be affected.

3. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ sex-linked genes are usually found on the X chromosome from the 23rd, sex chromosome, pair **WHILE** autosomal genes are found on chromosomes 1-22 [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ inheritance is dependent on the biological sex of the offspring in sex-linked BUT not in non-sex linked [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ biological males receive one allele for sex-linked characteristics, and biological females receive two alleles for sex-linked characteristics (when carried on the X chromosome), **BUT** biological males and females both receive two alleles for characteristics controlled by genes on autosomes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ biological males have a higher chance of having a sex-linked recessive characteristic than biological females, **WHILE** there is no difference in the inheritance of autosomal characteristics between biological males and females [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ if a biological female is affected by a recessive sex-linked trait, all of her biological sons will be affected, **BUT** for autosomal traits, some biological males of an affected biological mother may not be affected [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in sex-linked inheritance, biological females can be carriers for a trait, and biological males cannot be carriers, **WHILE** in autosomal inheritance, both biological sexes can be carriers [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
 Sex-linked inheritance refers to the inheritance of **genes typically located on the X chromosome** of the 23rd pair, whereas autosomal inheritance is the inheritance of genes found on **chromosomes 1-22** ($\checkmark$)
. **Biological males inherit a single allele** for X-linked characteristics from their biological mother, but **biological males and females inherit two alleles** ($\checkmark$)
 for characteristics controlled by genes on autosomes. **Biological males** have a **higher chance of sex-linked recessive** characteristics
than biological females, but there is **no difference** in the inheritance of **autosomal characteristics** between **biological males and females** ($\checkmark$).
