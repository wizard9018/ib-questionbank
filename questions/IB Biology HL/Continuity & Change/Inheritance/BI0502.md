# BI0502

What suggests that the inheritance of the disorder shown in the pedigree chart is X-linked recessive and not autosomal recessive?

![BI0502QB](images/BI0502QB.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Two unaffected individuals can have affected offspring.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ An unaffected biological female can produce unaffected biological sons.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Biological males are more likely to be affected than biological females.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Both biological males and biological females can be affected.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In the pedigree, only biological males are shown to inherit the disorder. The single biological female with the disorder is a mate of a biological male in generation II and not an offspring of the original parents. So, according to the pedigree, biological males are more likely than biological females to be affected, which suggests that the trait is X-linkage recessive.

Two unaffected individuals can have affected offspring (**Choice A**), an unaffected biological female can produce unaffected biological sons (**Choice B**), and both biological males and biological females can be affected (**Choice D**) when a trait is transmitted through either an X-linked recessive or autosomal recessive pattern of inheritance.
