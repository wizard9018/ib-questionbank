# BI0083

The diagram shows normal red blood cells on the left, and sickle-shaped red blood cells, caused by abnormal haemoglobin, on the right.

![BI0083](<images/Picture 1.png>)

Which of the following is the reason for the sickle-shaped red blood cells?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Physical cellular damage

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Being placed in a hypertonic solution

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ A base substitution mutation

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ An error in mitosis

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Sickle-shaped red blood cells result from a mutation in the HBB gene, which encodes the beta-globin subunit of haemoglobin. This mutation is specifically a point mutation (base substitution), where adenine (A) is replaced with thymine (T) in the DNA sequence. This single change alters the codon, leading to the substitution of valine instead of glutamic acid in the haemoglobin protein. **Choice C** is the correct answer.

While sickled red blood cells are fragile and prone to rupture, the cause of their abnormal shape is not physical damage. Instead, it originates from a genetic mutation affecting haemoglobin structure. **Choice A** is incorrect.

If red blood cells were placed in a hypertonic solution, they would shrink due to osmotic water loss, but they wouldn't take on the characteristic sickle shape. **Choice B** is incorrect.

Mitosis errors can cause chromosomal mutations, leading to abnormal cell division, but they do not directly result in sickle cell formation. **Choice D** is incorrect..
