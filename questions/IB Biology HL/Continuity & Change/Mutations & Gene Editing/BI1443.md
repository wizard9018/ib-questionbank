# BI1443

Which of the following can result in mutations in DNA?

- I. $\hspace{1em}$ Single base substitution
- II. $\hspace{.7em}$ Infection by a retrovirus
- III. $\hspace{.4em}$ Errors during DNA replication by DNA polymerase III

-    A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

-    B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only
-    C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only
-    D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A single base substitution is not a cause of mutations but a type of mutation itself. A single base substitution occurs when one nucleotide in the DNA sequence is replaced with another. This can be caused by errors in DNA replication (such as those made by DNA polymerases), chemical damage to DNA, or exposure to radiation.  **Statement I** is not true.

Retroviruses, such as HIV, integrate their genetic material into the host cell's DNA. This integration process can disrupt the host genome, potentially inactivating genes or causing mutations. Additionally, the process of reverse transcription (converting the retrovirus's RNA into DNA for integration) is prone to errors, which can introduce mutations into the viral DNA that is then integrated into the host genome. **Statement II** is true.

Errors made by DNA Polymerase III during DNA replication can cause mutations. One of the roles of DNA Polymerase III in prokaryotes is to proofread the newly formed strands and to correct any errors. However, if it incorporates a wrong nucleotide and the error escapes correction by its proofreading activity, this can lead to a mutation. **Statement III** is true.

**Choice C** is therefore the correct answer to this question. **Choices A, B** and **D** are all incorrect as they include **Statement I**.
