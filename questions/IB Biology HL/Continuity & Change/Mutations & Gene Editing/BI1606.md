# BI1606

Which of the following is why mutations are more likely to occur in regions with more CpG (cytosine next to guanine) sequences?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Both C and G pair with bases on the opposite strand with two hydrogen bonds, so the bonds can easily be broken.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ CpG regions are often acetylated, which is an easy epigenetic tag to remove.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The phosphate between C and G is very susceptible to environmental mutations.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ CpG regions form three hydrogen bonds between complementary bases, making the DNA more tightly bound and less accessible to proofreading.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Each C–G base pair is held by three hydrogen bonds, compared with two in A–T pairs.

Regions rich in C–G pairs are therefore more stable and tightly bound, making them less accessible to repair enzymes during proofreading. This can increase the chance that any replication errors remain uncorrecte

; **Choice D**  is correct.

**Choice A**  is incorrect as as both C and G pair with bases on the opposite strand with three hydrogen bonds, rather than two. This means they are more stable, not easier to break.

**Choice B**  is incorrect because acetylation is an epigenetic modification of histone proteins, not of DNA bases. CpG regions are commonly methylated, not acetylated. Acetylation affects DNA packaging and transcription, not mutation rate, so this statement is unrelated to CpG mutation frequency. 

**Choice C**  is incorrect, as the sugar-phosphate bond linking nucleotides in DNA is chemically stable and not typically affected by environmental mutagens.
