# BI1214

Which statement does **not** explain why fruit flies (*Drosophila melanogaster*)  are used as model organisms for humans in gene knockout studies?

![BI1214](images/BI1214.png)
[Source: Adapted from University, O. S. (2010, March 1). *Drosophila melanogaster*. Flickr. Retrieved 04 September 2023 from https://www.flickr.com/photos/oregonstateuniversity/4399088160. Copyright by CC 2.0]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Fruit flies share a high number of homologous genes with humans

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Fruit flies have a short life cycle

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Fruit flies are easy and inexpensive to culture in research laboratories

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Fruit flies have a different respiratory and cardiovascular system to humans

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D**  is correct, as fruit flies do have a different respiratory and cardiovascular system to humans so it is difficult to use them as a model when studying those systems.

Homologous genes are genes from the same genetic ancestor that have the same function in different organisms. Fruit flies and humans having a high number of shared homologous genes means that gene function can be studied in fruit flies as a model for human gene function; **Choice A**  is incorrect. Fruit flies having a short life cycle, means that the effect of gene knockouts can be studied relatively rapidly; **Choice B**  is incorrect. Fruit flies being easy and inexpensive to culture in research laboratories means that many can be bred and used in knockout studies; **Choice C**  is incorrect.
