# BI1240

What is the role of the Cas9 enzyme in the CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technique?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Acts as an endonuclease that cuts target DNA at a specific location

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ To cut target mRNA at a specific location

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Acts as an RNA guide for the CRISPR enzyme

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Prevents the wrong section of DNA from being cut

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct as Cas9 is an enzyme (endonuclease) that precisely cuts the double stranded DNA at a specific location. **Choice B** is incorrect as Cas9 cuts DNA, not mRNA. **Choice C**  is incorrect, as Cas9 itself is an enzyme and not an RNA molecule. Cas9 acts in conjunction with a guide RNA molecule, which is used to direct Cas9 to the specific DNA sequence that needs to be edited. **Choice D** is incorrect as Cas9 does not prevent the wrong section of DNA from being cut, and is instead guided to the correct sequence by an RNA guide. The specificity of the editing process relies on the guide RNA directing the Cas9 to the correct DNA sequence.
