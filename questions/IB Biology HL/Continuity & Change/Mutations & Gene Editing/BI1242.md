# BI1242

**Maximum mark: 10**

Myostatin is a protein with the function of limiting skeletal muscle growth in cattle, sheep, dogs, humans and mice. Research has been carried out to investigate what happens when the myostatin gene is removed, or knocked out, in mice. The photographs below show the results of the research. On the right is a mouse with the gene removed, known as a myostatin knockout mouse (Mstn:sup[−/−]). On the left is a mouse which still has the myostatin gene, known as a myostatin wild type (WT).

![BI1242](images/BI1242.png)
[Source: Adapted from Lee, S.-J. (2007, August 29). *File: Muscle Mouse 01.jpg* - Wikimedia Commons. Wikimedia commons. Retrieved 28 July 2023 https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Muscle_Mouse_01.jpg Copyright free]

1. State the phenotypic difference between the wild type and myostatin knockout mouse. [1 mark]

2. Suggest one advantage of using mice in this experiment instead of humans. [1 mark]

3. State one purpose for making knockout mice.    [1 mark]

4. Having seen the effect of knocking out the myostatin gene in mice, researchers hypothesised that knocking out this gene in humans could result in the restoration of muscle in humans with degenerative muscle diseases. Discuss to what extent you agree with this.        [4 marks]

5. The Myostatin knockout mouse has been added to an open-access knockout library. Suggest an advantage of this.[1 mark]

6.
    1. Myostatin is regulated by a repressor protein. Predict, with a reason, what would happen to the expression of myostatin if the repressor protein was removed rather than the myostatin gene.        [1 mark]

    2. Predict the phenotype of the mouse you referred to in part (i).     [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Using mice in experiments has significantly **less ethical considerations** than using human subjects ($\checkmark$).

    $\ce{ -> }$ study the function of genes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ study biological pathways     [1 mark]

4. Any **four** of the following: \
*Answer must include one point from agree and one point from disagree to gain full marks*

    **Agree because…** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ mice provide a good model for human disease    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myostatin has the same role in humans and mice    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could lead to improved muscle function in individuals with degenerative muscle disease, or improved quality of life        [1 mark]

    **Disagree because…** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ myostatin may not work in the same way in humans as in mice, **OR** loss of expression of  myostatin may have unexpected off-target effects     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ do not know how many mice were used in the study as only two shown in the photograph **OR** need more results **OR** more mice need testing as only one knockout mouse shown [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myostatin may not be involved in the progression of degenerative muscle diseases **OR** myostatin knockout mice do not have degenerative muscle diseases    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the phenotype of knockout myostatin mice is not enough evidence to begin human trials    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gene editing techniques raise ethical dilemmas in humans    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could lead to uncontrolled muscle growth which could have damaging effects **OR** could damage heart **OR** metabolism            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ effectiveness could vary between individuals        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Knocking out myostatin in humans with degenerative muscle diseases would not necessarily cure degenerative muscle diseases as the loss of expression of myostatin may have **unexpected off-target effects** ($\checkmark$) and myostatin may **not** be involved in the **progression of degenerative muscle diseases** ($\checkmark$). Also, the phenotype of knockout  myostatin mice is **not enough evidence** to begin **human trials** ($\checkmark$). However, myostatin plays the **same role in humans** and mice so it may help ($\checkmark$).

    If the repressor of myostatin expression was knocked down, then the myostatin gene would be **overexpressed** because the **normal role of the repressor is to downregulate** the expression of myostatin ($\checkmark$).
