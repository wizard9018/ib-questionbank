# BI0742

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how the glomerulus and Bowman’s capsule are adapted for their function.     [4 marks]

2. Explain the differences between the composition of blood in the renal artery and renal vein.         [4 marks]

3. Describe the processes in the nephron that leads to the production of urine. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ large surface area [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fenestrations **OR** large pores in the walls of the capillaries that allow ultrafiltration      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ podocytes allow fluid to pass through freely [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ basement membrane only allows molecules below a certain size to pass through    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ afferent arterioles are wider than efferent arterioles, so (hydrostatic) pressure is increased in the glomerulus    [1 mark]
[4 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    The glomerulus and Bowman’s capsule are adapted for their function because they have a **large surface area** ($\checkmark$). There are **fenestrations** ($\checkmark$) in the walls of the capillaries that allow ultrafiltration, the **basement membrane** only allow molecules below a certain size to pass through ($\checkmark$) and **podocytes** allow fluid to pass through freely ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following (difference and reason both need to be mentioned in order to get a marking point):

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood in the renal artery has a higher concentration of oxygen due to aerobic respiration in the cells of the kidney    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (blood in the renal artery has a) higher concentration of glucose due to cell respiration in the kidney     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood in the renal artery has a higher concentration of urea because urea is filtered out of the blood by the nephron     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood in the renal vein has a higher concentration of carbon dioxide because it is a waste product of aerobic respiration in the cells of the kidney    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood in the renal vein has balanced optimum levels of water and ions compared to the renal artery because the ultra-filtration and selective reabsorption in the nephron results in the optimum concentrations     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the renal artery may contain toxins and undigestible pigments that are not found in the renal vein due to ultrafiltration in the nephron         [1 mark]
[4 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Blood in the renal vein has lower concentrations of **oxygen** ($\checkmark$) and **glucose** ($\checkmark$) and a higher concentration of **carbon dioxide** due to **aerobic respiration** ($\checkmark$) in the kidney cells. Blood in the renal artery has a higher concentration of **urea** because urea is **filtered** out ($\checkmark$) of the blood in the glomerulus. Blood in the renal artery does not have **optimum water and ions concentrations** ($\checkmark$). Those get balanced out by the ultrafiltration and consecutive re-absorption of both. The renal artery may contain **toxins** ($\checkmark$) which are not found in the renal vein because they would be filtered out in the glomerulus and have no way of being re-absorbed.

3. any **eight** of the following (answers could be accepted in the form of an annotated diagram):

    $\ce{ -> }$ ultrafiltration happens in Bowman’s capsule  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glomerulus has structural features for ultrafiltration **OR** glomerulus has fenestrations, podocytes and basal membrane to increase permeability **OR** substances are filtered from the blood due to the high pressure in the glomerulus caused by afferent arteriole being wider than the efferent [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water, salts, glucose, urea, water and mineral ions are filtered out of the blood [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ substances from the filtrate are reabsorbed by osmosis, simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion and active transport  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in the proximal convoluted tubule, all of the glucose is reabsorbed (into the blood) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in the proximal convoluted tubule, most of the water, **OR** most of the salts, is, **OR** are, reabsorbed [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in the descending limb of the loop of Henle, water is reabsorbed  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in the ascending limb of the loop of Henle, sodium ions are reabsorbed by active transport **OR** are actively pumped out of the nephron **OR** glomerular filtrate     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the active transport of sodium ions in the ascending limb of the loop of Henle increases the solute concentration of the medulla, which increases water reabsorption in the descending limb and collecting duct  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in the distal convoluted tubule, mineral ions are reabsorbed into the blood, and blood pH and ion concentrations are balanced   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when blood water concentration falls, ADH is released from the pituitary gland **OR** when blood water potential decreases, osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect it and stimulate the release of ADH  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ADH results in more aquaporins in the plasma membrane of cells of the collecting duct wall, allowing more water to be reabsorbed         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a nephron consists of Bowman’s capsule and glomerulus, a proximal convoluted tubule, a loop of Henle, a distal convoluted tubule and a collecting duct (all have to be named and in the correct order for this marking point)     [1 mark]
[8 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    **Ultrafiltration** happens in Bowman’s capsule ($\checkmark$), and **reabsorption** of substances through osmosis, simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, and active transport happens in the rest of the nephron ($\checkmark$). The filtrate contains **water, salts, glucose, urea, water and mineral ions** ($\checkmark$). In the **proximal convoluted tubule**, most water and salt ($\checkmark$) are reabsorbed, as well as **all of the glucose** ($\checkmark$). In the **descending limb** of the loop of Henle, water is reabsorbed ($\checkmark$). In the **ascending limb** of the loop of Henle, sodium ions are reabsorbed by active transport ($\checkmark$). The active transport of salts **increases the solute concentration of the medulla** ($\checkmark$), which allows a higher volume of water to be reabsorbed in the descending limb and collecting duct. In the **distal convoluted tubule**, mineral ions are reabsorbed, and the pH and ion concentration of the blood is balanced ($\checkmark$).
