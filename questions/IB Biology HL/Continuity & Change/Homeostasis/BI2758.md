# BI2758

What happens to organ blood flow during prolonged vigorous physical activity?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | Decreases slightly to the kidneys |
    | II. | Increases to skeletal muscle |
    | III. | Decreases to the brain |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ III only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
During prolonged vigorous exercise, vasoconstriction of the renal arterioles occurs, reducing blood flow to the kidneys. This helps divert blood to more metabolically active organs, such as skeletal muscle. **Statement I** is true.

Skeletal muscle blood flow increases greatly during vigorous activity to supply more oxygen and nutrients for aerobic respiration and to remove waste products like carbon dioxide. Muscles may receive up to 80–85% of cardiac output during peak exertion. **Statement II** is true.

Brain blood flow is tightly autoregulated, meaning that despite changes in cardiac output or systemic vasoconstriction, cerebral perfusion remains relatively constant. It does not decrease during exercise; in fact, it may increase slightly due to elevated $\ce{CO_2}$ levels or local metabolic activity. **Statement III** is false.

**Choice C** is the correct answer to the question because it only includes the two true **Statements I** and **II**.\
**Choice A** is incorrect as it is missing the true **Statement II**.\
**Choices B** and **D** are incorrect because they include the false **Statement III**.
