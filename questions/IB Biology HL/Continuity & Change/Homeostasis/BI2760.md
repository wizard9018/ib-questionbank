# BI2760

The following table shows hormone levels and responses:

|Blood Glucose| Hormone Released | Body Response |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| High | Insulin | Glucose uptake by cells |
| Low | Glucagon | Glycogen breakdown |

Which process is an example of negative feedback?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Neither process

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Both processes

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Only insulin release

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Only glucagon release

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Negative feedback is a regulatory mechanism in which a change in a variable triggers a response that counteracts the original change, returning the system toward its set point.

When blood glucose concentration increases, the pancreas releases insulin. Insulin promotes glucose uptake by body cells and stimulates the conversion of glucose to glycogen in the liver and muscles. This reduces blood glucose levels, bringing them back to normal. This reversal of the initial rise in glucose clearly demonstrates negative feedback.

When blood glucose concentration decreases, the pancreas releases glucagon. Glucagon stimulates the breakdown of glycogen into glucose in the liver, raising blood glucose levels. This also restores glucose concentration to its normal range and is, therefore, another example of negative feedback.

Therefore, the correct answer is **Choice B**.

**Choice A**  is incorrect because both processes exhibit negative feedback mechanisms. **Choice C** is incorrect because it ignores the role of glucagon. **Choice D** is incorrect because it ignores insulin's role.
