# BI0817

**Maximum mark: 10**

Creatine is a naturally occurring chemical produced in the body which supplies energy to muscles. It’s also a supplement used widely in the fitness industry to reduce recovery times and improve athletic performance.

Creatine is broken down into creatinine which is toxic in high concentrations. A study was conducted on the effect of creatine supplementation on the creatinine concentration in the urine. Urine samples were collected from humans, and the creatinine concentration was measured.

![BI0817A](images/BI0817A.png)
[Source: Adapted from Yang, C-Y., Chen, F-A., Chen, C-F., Liu, W-S., Shih, C-J., Ou, S-M., et al. (2015) *Diagnostic Accuracy of Urine Protein/Creatinine Ratio Is Influenced by Urine Concentration*. PLoS ONE 10(9): e0137460. Retrieved May 29th, 2023 from https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0137460. Copyright free.]

The results from the study were used to make up corresponding creatine solutions to mimic the urine samples. These creatine solutions were then given to students to analyse. The specific gravity of each sample was measured using a hydrometer, a small graduated float used to compare the density of a liquid between two samples.

1.
    1. Describe how urea enters the urine from the blood.  [2 marks]

    2. The urine samples were collected from the subjects after the same intensity of exercise. Suggest how increased intensity of exercise could change creatinine concentration in the samples.      [2 marks]

2. Suggest a variable that would need to be controlled during the student’s experiment. [1 mark]

3. Suggest one advantage and one disadvantage of using model urine samples instead of human urine.         [2 marks]

4. Calculate, as a percentage, how much greater the urine sample specific gravity with 10g of supplemental creatine intake was compared to that with 5g of supplemental intake. [1 mark]

5. Describe **TWO** reasons why mice were used in this experiment and not humans.     [2 marks]

    1. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood enters the kidney through the afferent arteriole  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ afferent arteriole is wider than efferent arteriole so pressure in glomerulus is high        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high-pressure filtration **OR** ultrafiltration forces urea from blood **OR** plasma into Bowman’s capsule            [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Blood enters the nephron through the **afferent arteriole**, creating **high pressure** in the **glomerulus ** ($\checkmark$). **High-pressure filtration** forces urea from the blood plasma into the **Bowman’s capsule** ($\checkmark$).

    2. any **two** of the following:

    ***increased creatinine concentration***:

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased creatinine production **OR** increased breakdown of creatine in muscles due to more energy required for muscle activity    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ creatinine becomes more concentrated in nephron due to kidneys conserving water during exercise [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ exercise-induced muscle damage can increase creatine breakdown         [1 mark]

     ***decreased creatinine concentration***:

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced blood flow to the kidneys OR lower glomerular filtration rate during exercise  [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During exercise, the **increased breakdown of creatine** in muscles to meet **higher energy demands** leads to **more creatinine** production, which can increase its concentration in the urine ($\checkmark$). However, **reduced blood flow** to the **kidneys** during exercise **lowers the glomerular filtration rate**, which could **decrease** the **concentration** of creatinine in the urine ($\checkmark$).
