# BI0475

Ribosomes are reused many times for protein synthesis. What happens to a ribosome after completing translation of an mRNA molecule?

-    A. $\hspace{1em}$ It twists and folds proteins into a tertiary structure suited to perform a specific function

-    B. $\hspace{1em}$ It binds to another amino acid

-    C. $\hspace{1em}$ The large subunit and the small subunit of the ribosome separate to be reused again later

-    D. $\hspace{1em}$ It is destroyed by a lysosome

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
After a ribosome completes translation of an mRNA molecule, the large and small subunits separate. These subunits can then be reused to initiate translation of another mRNA. This recycling of ribosomes makes protein synthesis efficient and continuous in the cell. **Choice C** is the correct answer to the question.

**Choice A** is incorrect because ribosomes synthesise the primary structure (the polypeptide chain), but folding into a tertiary structure happens after translation and is not carried out by the ribosome.

**Choice B** is incorrect because it is tRNA, not the ribosome, that binds amino acids. Also, binding amino acids happens during translation, not after it's completed.

**Choice D** is incorrect because ribosomes are not broken down after translation. They are reused repeatedly, and only damaged or non-functional ones may be degraded.
