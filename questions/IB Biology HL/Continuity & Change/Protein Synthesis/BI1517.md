# BI1517

Which of the following would be possible reasons for polypeptides being modified after translation?

- I. $\hspace{1.6em}$ So that polypeptides are only functional at specific sites 

- II. $\hspace{1.3em}$ To stabilise the mRNA sequence

- III. $\hspace{1em}$ To mark them for degradation

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III 

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Modifications of polypeptides after translation, such as phosphorylation, methylation, acetylation, ubiquitination, and more, can regulate the activity of proteins, control their localisation within the cell, and determine their interactions with other molecules. These modifications ensure that proteins are active only in specific cellular contexts or locations, thereby facilitating precise control over cellular processes; **Statement I** is therefore true. The attachment of ubiquitin proteins to a lysine residue on a target protein often serves as a signal for the protein to be degraded by the proteasome, a protein complex responsible for degrading and recycling unneeded or damaged proteins within the cell. This process is crucial for regulating protein levels and removing malfunctioning proteins, thus maintaining cellular health and function; **Statement III** is also true. This means that **Choice C**  is correct.

Modifications such as adding 3' poly-A tails and 5' caps do function to stabilise mRNA sequences, but these are post-transcriptional modifications, not post-translational modifications. Therefore, **Statement II** is incorrect. **Choices A, B** and **D**  are incorrect as they include **Statement II**.
