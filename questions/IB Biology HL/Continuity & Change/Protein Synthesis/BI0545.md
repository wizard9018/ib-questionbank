# BI0545

Why can a eukaryote produce a larger number of mRNA transcripts than the number of genes in its genome?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Some mRNA transcripts are double-stranded.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ There are genes that produce multiple different mRNA transcripts.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Not all mRNA transcripts are produced by genes.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Some mRNA transcripts are produced by other mRNA transcripts.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Messenger RNA transcripts are produced as a result of transcription. During the transcription of RNA from DNA in eukaryotes, genes are transcribed to produce an RNA molecule that contains introns (non-coding regions) and exons (coding regions) of the gene. During post-transcriptional RNA processing, the introns are removed, and exons are spliced together to produce the mRNA transcript. However, the exons that make up the final transcript are not always the same: some genes can undergo alternative splicing, in which different combinations of exons from the gene are spliced to create mRNA. So, multiple mRNA transcripts can be produced by a single gene, which results in the number of possible RNA transcripts exceeding the number of genes in the genome.

mRNA is never double-stranded (**Choice A**).  All mRNA transcripts are produced by genes (**Choice C**).  One mRNA transcript cannot code for another mRNA transcript (**Choice D**).  mRNA is used in translation.
