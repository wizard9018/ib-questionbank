# BI1515

The image shows a mature mRNA sequence. What would be the likely result if the poly-A tail was missing?

![BI1515 fig1](<images/BI1515 fig1.png>)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ The 5' cap would not be added

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ The mRNA sequence would not be translated

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Splicing of introns would not occur

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Exonucleases would degrade the mRNA sequence

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

The poly-A tail is a long sequence of adenines added to the 3' end of mature mRNA by the enzyme poly-A polymerase. Adding the poly-A tail increases the stability of the mRNA and protects the mRNA from degradation by exonucleases. **Choice D** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the addition of the 5' cap to mRNA by the enzyme guanylyl transferase is independent of the polyadenylation of the 3' end. The 5' cap, which is a modified guanine nucleotide, is added to the 5' end of the mRNA precursor during the initial phases of transcription and before the mRNA is fully processed and exported to the cytoplasm. This process is not affected by the presence or absence of a poly-A tail. **Choice B** is incorrect because while the lack of a poly-A tail can lead to increased mRNA degradation and, thus, reduced translation, the actual process of translation itself does not directly require a poly(A) tail. If an mRNA lacking a poly(A) tail managed to evade degradation and reach a ribosome, it could still theoretically be translated. **Choice C** is incorrect because splicing is carried out by the spliceosome before the polyadenylation of the mRNA.
