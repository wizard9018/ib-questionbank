# BI1042

**Maximum mark: 10**

The table below shows estimates of the mean percentage similarity between monozygotic and dizygotic twins and also between brothers in terms of their genomes, transcriptomes and proteomes. Monozygotic twins result from the fertilisation of one egg by one sperm. The resulting embryo later divides into two embryos. Dizygotic twins result when two egg cells are fertilised by two different sperm cells.

![BI1042](images/BI1042.png)

1. Explain why it is important for different cells in multicellular organisms to have different proteomes. [2 marks]

2. Describe how methylation can influence gene expression. [3 marks]

3. Explain why the mean per cent similarity decreases from the most similarity between genomes to the least similarity between proteomes. [3 marks]

4. With reference to the data in the table, discuss the similarities and differences in mean per cent similarity between the dizygotic twins and the brothers. [2 marks]

2. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ methylation of DNA represses transcription **OR** methylation of cytosine represses transcription **OR** methylation of DNA in promoter represses transcription [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA methylation interferes with the functioning of RNA polymerase **OR** prevents RNA polymerase from transcribing genes **OR** prevents RNA polymerase **OR** transcription factors from binding [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ methylation of histone proteins **OR** amino acids in histone proteins can cause transcription to be repressed **OR** activated [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ during gamete formation, specific genes on either the maternal or paternal allele are methylated, leading to differential expression of these genes in offspring [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ may silence tumour suppressor genes, promoting uncontrolled cell growth and cancer progression [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Methylation of cytosine** ($\checkmark$) bases in the **promoter regions** ($\checkmark$) of genes **represses transcription** ($\checkmark$) by **interfering** with the functioning of **RNA polymerase** ($\checkmark$). The **methylation of histone** proteins can either **repress or activate genes** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ genomes change very little over the course of an individual's life [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ genomes only change as a result of mutation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ transcriptomes change as a result of environmental factors [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ transcriptomes change as a result of epigenetic changes to DNA **OR** histone proteins [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ transcriptomes **OR** proteomes change as a result of hormones [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ proteomes change as a result of environmental factors **OR** changes to the transcriptome [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ transcriptomes **OR** proteomes change as a result of ageing [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ transcriptomes OR proteomes change as a result of disease [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ proteomes change as a result of post-transcriptional modifications to mRNA [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ proteomes change as a result of protein-protein interactions [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 All types of siblings are born with a given per cent similarity in their **genomes which changes only as a result of mutation** ($\checkmark$), which amounts to **very little change** over the **course of an individual's life** ($\checkmark$). **Transcriptomes and proteomes**, on the other hand, **change** more frequently in response to **environmental factors** ($\checkmark$) and involve **epigenetic changes to DNA and histone proteins** ($\checkmark$). **Proteomes** can undergo further **changes** as a result of **post-transcriptional modification to mRNA** ($\checkmark$) and **post-translational modifications to polypeptides** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **two** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ the genomes are both 50% similar because both brothers and dizygotic twins are the results of a sperm fertilising an egg which becomes a fetus [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the transcriptomes **OR** proteomes are more similar in dizygotic twins because they share the same environment **OR** prenatal environment **OR** because brothers do not share the same prenatal environment [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as dizygotic twins develop in the womb together, they may experience similar early developmental cues, which can impact gene regulation and expression during critical periods of growth and organ formation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the transcriptomes **OR** proteomes of both brothers and dizygotic twins are more different than their genomes because of environmental factors **OR** epigenetic changes, **OR** any other valid example of environmental effects [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The **genomes** are both **50% the same** because both **brothers** and **dizygotic twins** are the result of a **sperm fertilising an egg** ($\checkmark$) and because they both receive **50%** of their **DNA** from **each parent**. The **transcriptomes and proteomes** of the **dizygotic twins are more similar** compared to the brothers because they **share the same prenatal environment** ($\checkmark$).
