# BI2298

A researcher observes that a gene is silenced despite being unmethylated in its promoter. What might explain this observation?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Methylation of histones near the gene

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Activation of RNA polymerase

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Lack of RNA splicing

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Absence of introns

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Methylation of histones, particularly at specific residues (e.g., H3K9 or H3K27), can lead to chromatin condensation, forming heterochromatin. This structure inhibits the binding of transcription factors and RNA polymerase to the promoter, silencing the gene even if the promoter itself is unmethylated. Histone methylation can repress gene expression without the need for DNA methylation in the promoter. **Choice A** is correct.

**Choice B** is incorrect because activation of RNA polymerase would lead to gene expression, not silencing. If the gene is silenced, RNA polymerase is likely unable to bind or initiate transcription due to chromatin structure or other regulatory mechanisms.

**Choice C** is incorrect because splicing occurs after transcription and does not influence whether a gene is transcribed. A lack of RNA splicing would affect the maturation of the mRNA, not the silencing of the gene.

**Choice D** is incorrect because, while introns can play regulatory roles, their absence does not inherently silence a gene. Many genes, such as bacterial genes, lack introns and are still actively expressed. The presence or absence of introns is unrelated to transcriptional regulation in this context.
