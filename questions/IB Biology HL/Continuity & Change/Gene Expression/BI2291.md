# BI2291

A scientist observes that an mRNA with a short poly-A tail is degraded rapidly, but when the poly-A tail is artificially lengthened, the mRNA persists longer. Which of the following experimental observations would provide the best evidence that the poly-A tail regulates translation efficiency in addition to stability?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ mRNA with a longer poly-A tail is less affected by nuclease activity.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ mRNA with a longer poly-A tail produces more stable proteins.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ mRNA with a longer poly-A tail is transcribed more efficiently.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ mRNA with a longer poly-A tail binds ribosomes more frequently.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The poly-A tail not only enhances mRNA stability by protecting it from degradation but also plays a role in translation efficiency. Ribosome binding is a critical step in translation initiation, and the poly-A tail promotes ribosome recruitment through interactions with proteins. Observing increased ribosome binding in mRNA with a longer poly-A tail directly links the tail to enhanced translation efficiency. **Choice D** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because while this observation explains the role of the poly-A tail in enhancing mRNA stability, it does not address translation efficiency. Stability and translation efficiency are separate functions, and the question specifically asks for evidence of the tail's role in translation.

**Choice B** is incorrect because protein stability depends on factors like post-translational modifications and protein degradation pathways, not directly on the length of the poly-A tail. This observation would not directly link the poly-A tail to translation efficiency.

**Choice C** is incorrect because transcription refers to the synthesis of mRNA from DNA and occurs in the nucleus. The poly-A tail is added post-transcriptionally and does not influence the efficiency of transcription itself.
