# BI2393

Which of the following describes the *lac* operon, as found in *Escherichia coli (E. coli*)?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Lactose interacts with a regulator protein, preventing it from binding with the promoter region of the gene.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The *lac* operon is activated in the presence or absence of lactose.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The *lac* operon operates by controlling the level of translation of mRNA.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The *lac* operon is also present in mammals to aid the digestion of lactose in milk.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because lactose interacts with the *lac* repressor protein, preventing it from binding to the operator region, located between the promoter and the structural genes for lactose-metabolising enzymes. If the operator is blocked, RNA is blocked from transcribing the gene. Once the operator is unblocked, RNA polymerase is free to transcribe the structural genes.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the *lac* operon is only activated when the bacterium is in the presence of lactose. This means that the bacterium does not waste cellular resources expressing a gene (for the metabolism of lactose) whose product would not be useful in the absence of lactose in the surroundings.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the *lac* operon works by controlling the level of transcription, not translation.

**Choice D** is incorrect because the *lac* operon is found exclusively in prokaryotes, such as *E. coli*. Gene regulation in eukaryotes is far more complex and does not involve operons like the *lac* operon.
