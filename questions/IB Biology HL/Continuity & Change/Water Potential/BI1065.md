# BI1065

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the stages of solvation when water acts as the solvent.        [5 marks]

2. Describe what you would see down a microscope if an onion cell is first put into pure water and then into a strong salt solution.    [4 marks]

3. Explain the movement of water across the cell surface membrane when an animal cell is placed in different solute concentrations.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ separation of solute particles **OR** molecules **OR** ions [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ breaking of intermolecular forces OR ionic OR hydrogen bonds holding solute particles together [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ disruption of hydrogen bonds between water molecules to accommodate solute particles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ attractive forces **OR** hydrogen bonds between solute and water molecules form  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water molecules surround solute particles **OR** "hydration shell" surrounds each solute particle, preventing  re-aggregating [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ over time, a dynamic equilibrium is established between solute particles leaving the solid and water molecules joining the hydration shells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at maximum solubility of the solute in the water, solution is saturated [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Solvation is the interaction of water molecules with **solute particles**. First, the **solute particles** must **separate** ($\checkmark$), which often involves the **breaking of intermolecular bonds** ($\checkmark$)  holding the solute particles together. Some **hydrogen bonds** between **water molecules** also **break**  ($\checkmark$) . The **water molecules** then form **hydrogen bonds** or electrostatic attractions with the **solute** ($\checkmark$). These **water molecules** surround the **solute** particles, forming **hydration shells** that **prevent** them from **re-joining** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following (*must include at least **one** from pure water and **one** from strong salt solution to gain full marks*):

    ***pure water:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves into the cell, **AND** the cell contents swell  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell membrane pushes against the cell wall [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the cell has become turgid, **OR** cell exerts turgor pressure [1 mark]

    ***strong salt solution:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves out of the cell, **AND** the cell contents begin to shrink **OR** reduce in volume [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell membrane pulls away from cell wall [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ plasmolysis occurs [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When a plant cell is put into **pure water** and is observed under a microscope, the **cell contents** can be seen to **swell** ($\checkmark$), and the **cell membrane pushes** out against the **cell wall** ($\checkmark$), exerting **turgor pressure** ($\checkmark$). If the plant cell is then put into a strong salt solution, the **cell contents shrink** ($\checkmark$), the **cell membrane pulls away** from the **cell wall** ($\checkmark$), and the cell is said to be **plasmolysed** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following (*must include at least **one** of the first 3 to gain full marks*):

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves across the membrane by osmosis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water molecules move by diffusion    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ membrane is selectively permeable    **OR** membrane is impermeable to some solutes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves through phospholipid bilayer **OR** membrane, which is not a barrier to water    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ net movement of water until equilibrium is reached **OR** until solute concentrations are the same    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water diffuses **OR** moves towards area of higher solute concentration    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ passive transport **OR** no ATP needed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves into cell if placed in hypotonic solution    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell swells, **OR** lysis occurs **OR** cell bursts in hypotonic solution    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves out of cell if placed in hypertonic solution    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell shrivels, **OR** crenation occurs in hypertonic solution    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ no net movement of water if cell is placed in isotonic solution     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell maintains normal shape in isotonic solutions                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rate of diffusion **OR** osmosis **OR** water movement is increased by water channels **OR** specific proteins in membranes **OR** aquaporins    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Water diffuses** ($\checkmark$) directly **across** the membrane **phospholipid bilayer** ($\checkmark$) in a process called **osmosis** ($\checkmark$).  When there is a difference in solute concentration, **water** will **move** to areas of **higher solute** concentration ($\checkmark$). **Water moves into** an animal **cell** if placed in a **hypotonic solution** ($\checkmark$), which may lead to **lysis** ($\checkmark$) of the cell. **Water moves out** of a cell if placed in a **hypertonic solution** ($\checkmark$), which may lead to **crenation** ($\checkmark$). There is **no net movement** of water if the cell is placed in an **isotonic solution** ($\checkmark$), and so the cell will maintain its **normal shape** ($\checkmark$). The movement of water is a type of **passive transport** ($\checkmark$) and does not involve ATP.
