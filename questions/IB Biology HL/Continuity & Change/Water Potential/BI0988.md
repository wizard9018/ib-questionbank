# BI0988

A solution of NaCl that is isotonic to the tissues of a donor's heart is used to store the heart prior to transplanting the heart into another person.  How might this affect the ability of myocytes, making up the heart tissue, to contract?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ There will be no effect on myocytes ability to contract.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Sodium ions will diffuse out of the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Binding sites on actin will be exposed less frequently.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Intercalated discs will lose their structural stability, reducing the heart rate.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because if the solution in which the heart is stored lacks Ca:sup[2+], there is a likelihood that  Ca:sup[2+]  will diffuse out of the myocytes. This diffusion of Ca:sup[2+] from the myocytes can lead to a decrease in the overall Ca:sup[2+] concentration within the heart. This reduced Ca:sup[2+] concentration may result in an insufficient amount available to effectively bind to troponin, and so tropomyosin will not be moved to expose the binding sites on actin filaments.

**Choice A** is incorrect because myocyte contractions will be affected by Ca:sup[2+] diffusing out of the myocytes.  **Choice B** is incorrect because sodium ions are not present in the sarcoplasmic reticulum to any significant extent and although they do play a role in action potentials in myocytes, they are not involved in myocyte contractions.  **Choice D** is incorrect because the structure of intercalated discs is unlikely to be affected by ion concentrations.
