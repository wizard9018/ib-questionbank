# BI0411

An artificial cell is placed in a beaker of sucrose solution, as shown below. In which direction will there be a net movement of water?

![0411i](images/0411i.png)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Water molecules will diffuse out of the cell.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Water molecules will diffuse into the cell.
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Water molecules will diffuse equally into and out of the cell.
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Water molecules will not move across the membrane.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The sucrose concentration inside the cell is 0.25 mol dm:sup[-3], making it hypotonic (lower solute concentration, higher water potential). The sucrose concentration of the solution surrounding the cell is 0.35 mol dmdm:sup[-3], making it hypertonic (higher solute concentration, lower water potential). Water molecules will move from the hypotonic region (inside the cell) to the hypertonic region (outside the cell) by osmosis. **Choice A** is correct.

Water molecules diffusing into the cell (**Choice B**) would occur if the inside of the cell had a higher solute concentration than the outside, which is not the case here.

Water molecules diffusing equally into and out of the cell (**ChoiceC**) would happen if the solute concentrations inside and outside the cell were equal, leading to no net movement of water. However, the solute concentrations are not the same in this scenario.

Water molecules will always diffuse across selectively permeable membranes, even in cases of dynamic equilibrium. When solute concentrations on each side are the same, water molecules move, but there is no overall net movement. **Choice D** is, therefore, not possible.
