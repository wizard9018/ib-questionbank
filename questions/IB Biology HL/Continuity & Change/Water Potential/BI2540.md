# BI2540

Which mechanism is used by both unicellular and multicellular organisms to regulate the direction of water movement across membranes?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Control the ion concentration of the tissues.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Use aquaporins to increase water movement.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Use contractile vacuoles to release water.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Use membrane proteins to control ion movement.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Water moves by osmosis, which depends on the concentration gradient of solutes, especially ions. Both unicellular and multicellular organisms use membrane proteins (such as ion pumps, ion channels, and co-transporters) to actively move ions across membranes. By controlling ion movement, cells establish osmotic gradients that regulate the direction of water flow. **Choice D** is the correct answer.

**Choice A** is incorrect because whilst altering ion concentrations can indirectly affect water movement (through osmotic gradients), this option refers to controlling ions in “tissues,” which is not applicable to unicellular organisms. Unicellular organisms do not have tissues and regulate water movement at the cellular level.

**Choice B** is incorrect because aquaporins increase the rate of water diffusion but do not control its direction. Water still moves passively through aquaporins in response to osmotic gradients, which are determined by ion transport.

**Choice C** is incorrect because contractile vacuoles are found primarily in freshwater unicellular organisms as a means to expel excess water that enters the cell by osmosis. Contractile vacuoles are not present in multicellular organisms, making this mechanism unsuitable as a universal solution for both groups.
