# BI2676

What is the water potential ($\psi_w$) of a plant cell with a pressure potential ($\psi_p$) of 778 kPa and solute potential ($\psi_s$) of -627 kPa?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ 1405 kPa

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ -151 kPa

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ 151 kPa

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ -1405 kPa

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because we use the equation $\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$.\
$\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$ = -627 + 778 = 151 kPa.\
This cell has an overall positive water potential, so it would be in a state of turgor, with a full vacuole and a well-hydrated cytoplasm. These will combine to exert pressure on the inside of the cell wall. It’s a common misconception that overall water potential is always negative. This is true of solute potential, but overall water potential has pressure potential contributing to it as well as solute potential, as indicated in the equation $\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$.

**Choices A, B** and **D** are incorrect because they contain calculation errors.
