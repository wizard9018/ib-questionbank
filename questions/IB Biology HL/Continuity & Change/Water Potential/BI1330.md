# BI1330

The diagram shows onion tissue in a hypertonic solution.

![BI1330](images/BI1330.jpg)

Which row correctly describes the initial net movement of water and the resulting condition of the onion cells?

| | Net movement of water | Condition of onion cells |

| :-: | :-: | :-: |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Out of onion cells | Turgid |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Out of onion cells | Plasmolysed |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Into onion cells | Lysed |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Into onion cells | Plasmolysed |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

**Choice B** is correct because the surrounding hypertonic solution initially has a lower water potential than the onion cells, so there is a net movement of water out of the cells by osmosis. As water is lost, the vacuoles shrink, and the cell contents decrease in volume, causing the cell-surface membranes to pull away from the cell walls. This condition is called plasmolysis.

**Choice A** is incorrect because, although water moves out of the cells, this reduces turgor pressure rather than making the cells turgid. Turgidity results from water entering plant cells and the cell contents pressing against the cell walls.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the net movement of water is out of the cells, not into them. Furthermore, the rigid cell walls normally prevent onion cells from bursting when water enters them in a hypotonic solution.

**Choice D** is incorrect because plasmolysis results from a net movement of water out of the cells, not into them.
