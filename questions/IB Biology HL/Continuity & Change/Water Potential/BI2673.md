# BI2673

Which statement(s) help(s) to explain why water potential ($\psi_w$) is typically expressed in units of pressure (kPa)?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | Because it is difficult to quantify potential energy in kJ $\ce{dm^{-3}}$ in a water-based system.  |
    | II. | Because the movement of water creates a pressure effect on cell membranes.  |
    | III. | Because pressure always evens out between cells when their osmotic values are ideally regulated.  |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Statement I** is true. Water potential is a form of potential energy possessed by water molecules. While energy can be expressed in units like J or kJ per $\ce{dm^3}$, it is difficult to measure directly in biological systems. Instead, pressure units (kPa) are used as a practical proxy, relative to the water potential of pure water at 20 $\degree$C, which is defined as 0 kPa.

**Statement II** is true because water movement across membranes generates pressure differences. For example, when water enters a plant cell, it increases the internal pressure (turgor pressure), which is resisted by the rigid cell wall. This pressure is a key component of the overall water potential.

**Statement III** is false because pressures do not always equalize between cells. In fact, water potential differences are necessary for water to move from cell to cell, and pressure may vary depending on environmental conditions, cell type, or stage in the cell cycle.

**Choice A** is correct because it includes the two true statements: **Statement I** and **Statement II**.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because it includes only one of the two correct statements.\
**Choice C** is incorrect because it includes only the false statement.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because it includes a false statement (**Statement III**).
