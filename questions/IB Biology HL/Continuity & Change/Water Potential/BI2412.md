A group of students was investigating the water potential of carrot cells. They cut ten cylinders of tissue from the same carrot, all the same diameter and length. They recorded the mass of each carrot cylinder and then placed each one into different solutions, with varying sucrose concentrations, as shown below.

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

 ![BI2412bms](images/BI2412bms.png)

 Line crosses x-axis at **0.47** mol $\ce{L^{-1}}$ ($\checkmark$) sucrose solution, indicating no change in mass at this concentration (equilibrium is reached) \
 At equilibrium water potential of cell (ψcell) equals water potential of external solution ($\psi_{\ce{solution}}$).\
 The pressure potential ($\psi_{\ce{p}}$) of the sucrose solution is 0, because it is in an open container \
 ψsolution = $\psi_{\ce{p}} + \psi_{\ce{s}} = 0 + \psi_{\ce{s}} = \psi_{\ce{s}}$ so, water potential of the sucrose solution (ψsolution) equals solute potential of the solution ($\psi_{\ce{s}}$)

 From table 0.5 mol $\ce{L^{-1}}$ sucrose solution = **-1242.11 kPa** \
 From table 0.4 mol $\ce{L^{-1}}$ sucrose solution = **-993.74 kPa**

 Difference = **1242.11 kPa - 993.74 kPa** = **248.37 kPa** ($\checkmark$)

 Calculating water potential of cell ($\psi_{\ce{cell}}$) using interpolation:\
 0.47 mol $\ce{L^{-1}}$ = **-993.74 + (0.7** $\times$ **248.37**) = **-1167.599 kPa**\
 = **1167.60 kPa** (to 2 d.p.) ($\checkmark$)

3. any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ as water moves in by osmosis, the increase in number of water molecules inside cell, increases pressure potential **OR** increase in water molecules increases turgor pressure inside the cell, leading to increase in pressure potential [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as water moves in by osmosis, number of solute molecules in cell decreases relative to water molecules, making solute potential less negative **OR** increasing solute potential [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ overall water potential of cell becomes less negative, **OR** increases, due to the combined effects of increased pressure potential and less negative **OR** increases solute potential [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 When immersed in hypotonic solutions, **water enters** the cells by **osmosis, turgor pressure increases**, and so there is an **increase in pressure potential** ($\psi_{\ce{p}}$) ($\checkmark$). The influx of water **dilutes** the **solutes** inside the cell, reducing their concentration and making the **solute potential** ($\psi_{\ce{s}}$) **less negative** ($\checkmark$). Overall, the **water potential** of the cell ($\psi_{\ce{cell}}$) becomes **less negative** due to the **combined effects** of **increased pressure potential** and **less negative solute potential** ($\checkmark$).
