# BI1207

The graph shows the changes in the population of pale (primarily white) and melanistic (mostly black) peppered moths (*Biston betularia*) before and after the industrial revolution. What selection pressure was the likely cause of the changes seen in the graph below?

![BI1207QB](images/BI1207QB.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased atmospheric carbon dioxide concentration

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased rates of pollution

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased number of predators of black peppered moths

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Differences in diet of the peppered moths

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Increased rates of pollution (particularly soot) during the Industrial Revolution caused white lichen and mosses on trees to turn black. This meant that light-coloured peppered moths were unable to camouflage and were at a higher risk of predation. This allowed black peppered moths to become the dominant species as they were better camouflaged; **Choice B** is correct.

Increased carbon dioxide concentration is not a likely cause for the increase in the number of black peppered moths. Increased carbon dioxide concentration primarily affects global climate and is not directly linked to the colouration of the moths; **Choice A** is incorrect. The increase in predators would not explain the rise in the black peppered moths. The rise in number of black peppered moths was due to the increased success of predators catching the lighter-coloured moths against the darkened background; **Choice C** is incorrect. While a change in diet may influence various aspects of the organism's biology, it is not the reason for the increase in the numbers of black peppered moth after the Industrial Revolution; **Choice D** is incorrect.
