# BI1312

**Maximum mark: 16**

Evolution by natural selection was first proposed by Charles Darwin based on his observations of traits in species.

1. Outline the requirements for evolution by natural selection.    [4 marks]

2. Distinguish between inherited and acquired characteristics.    [3 marks]

3. Describe the selection pressures acting on guppies using John Endler's experiments as an example.            [9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ overproduction of offspring        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ variation in populations        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ variations must be inheritable **OR** genetic for natural selection    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is a struggle for survival **OR** competition for resources    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ individuals that are best adapted to survive and reproduce **OR** fittest for the environment survive and reproduce     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ over long periods of time **OR** many generations, natural selection leads to genetic change in population    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ natural selection over long periods of time leads to evolution    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Darwin observed **variations** ($\checkmark$) within populations and that there is an **overproduction of offspring** ($\checkmark$). Darwin also observed that population sizes tended to remain constant over time, which led him to conclude that there is a **struggle for survival** ($\checkmark$). Individuals **best adapted to the environment survive, reproduce** ($\checkmark$), and pass on their genes. This **leads to evolution over long periods of time** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ inherited characteristics are controlled by genes **OR** encoded in DNA, **BUT** acquired characteristics are not controlled by genes **OR** encoded in DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inherited characteristics are not affected by the environment, **BUT** acquired characteristics are affected by the environment    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inherited characteristics cannot be learned, **BUT** acquired characteristics can be learned                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inherited characteristics can be passed onto the next generation **OR** offspring, **BUT** acquired characteristics cannot                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ an example of an inherited characteristic is blood group **OR** haemoglobin **OR** any other relevant example **OR** an example of an acquired characteristic is language **OR** dyed hair colour **OR** any other relevant example            [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Inherited characteristics are controlled by genes**, but acquired characteristics are not controlled by genes  ($\checkmark$). **Inherited** characteristics are **not affected** by the **environment**, but **acquired** characteristics **are affected by** the **environment** ($\checkmark$). **Inherited** characteristics are **not learned**, but **acquired** characteristics **can be learned**  ($\checkmark$).

3. any **nine** of the following:\
    Statements for **both** sexual selection **and** natural selection are required.

    $\ce{ -> }$ sexual selection affects mating and reproduction success, often at the cost of survival        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ natural selection is the process where individuals that are best adapted to their environment are most likely to survive and reproduce                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ male guppies express genes for bright colours                 [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Endler hypothesised that guppies in habitats protected from predators would show brighter colours **OR** brighter spots than those in habitats where they are exposed to predators  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Endler observed guppies in wild **AND** in man-made **OR** artificial ponds     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ independent variable was the presence of predators **OR** cichlids        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ guppies from the wild with varying colours **OR** spots were introduced to tanks OR guppies introduced to tanks and allowed to breed            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ one set of guppies was kept in ponds with predators **OR** cichlids present, **AND** one set with non-predatory **OR** weak-predatory fish present **OR** no predators        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ponds had different sized gravel, **OR** some ponds had fine gravel, **AND** some had course gravel        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in ponds with predators **OR** cichlids, guppies had duller colours **OR** duller spots        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in ponds with coarser gravel, guppies had larger spots **OR** in ponds with finer gravel, guppies had smaller spots     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ more brightly coloured males **OR** those with brighter spots **OR** the more conspicuous males **OR** those that have spots that do not match the gravel size tend to be eaten, so less likely to mate and pass on their alleles to the next generation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this is predatory selection pressure and leads to a decrease in frequency of alleles for bright colours **OR** spots in the population over time     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in ponds with no predators **OR** non-predatory fish, guppies had brighter colours **OR** brighter spots                     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ brighter coloured guppies **OR** those with brighter spots were more likely to mate and pass on their alleles to the next generation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this is sexual selection and leads to an increase in frequency of alleles for bright colours **OR** spots in the population over time    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ these experiments show the evolution of guppy colouration involves a dynamic interplay between sexual and natural selection     [1 mark]
    [9 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Endler hypothesised that **male guppies** would have **brighter colours** in areas where they were **protected from predators** compared to where predators were present ($\checkmark$). He observed guppies in **natural** and **artificial ponds** ($\checkmark$). **One set** of male guppies was kept in ponds with **predators**, and **one set** was kept in ponds **without predators** ($\checkmark$). In the ponds where **predators** were **present**, the **male guppies** were **duller** in colour ($\checkmark$), which means the males were more **camouflaged** and less likely to be eaten, and so **more likely to survive** to reproductive age and pass on their **alleles** to the **next generation** ($\checkmark$).  In ponds where there were **no**, or few, **predators**, male guppies were more **brightly coloured** ($\checkmark$), suggesting **female guppies** were **more attracted** to brightly coloured males, so they were **more likely** to **mate** and **pass on** their **alleles** to the next generation ($\checkmark$).  Over time, this increases the **frequency** of **alleles** for **bright colours** in these populations ($\checkmark$). These experiments provide **evidence** that the **evolution** of male guppie colouring is influenced by **natural** and **sexual selection** ($\checkmark$).
