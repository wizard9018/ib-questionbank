# BI0246

As pollution levels rise, the trees that the peppered moth (*Biston betularia*) lives on will become darker.

![BI0246](images/BI0246.png)
[Source: Phillips, A. (2010, June 21). *Peppered moth*. Flickr. Retrieved May 26, 2022, from https: //www .flickr.com/photos/wildhastings/4720082589. Copyright free]

What will happen to the frequency of the melanic (darker) form in the population?

- A.    $\hspace{1em}$ Decreases

- B.    $\hspace{1em}$ Increases
- C.    $\hspace{1em}$ Remains constant
- D.    $\hspace{1em}$ Fluctuates randomly

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because darker bark creates a selection pressure where melanic moths are less visible to predators, so they survive and reproduce more. Because the trait is heritable, the melanic allele becomes more common, increasing the frequency of the melanic form over generations.

**Choice A** is incorrect because when tree bark becomes darker, melanic moths are better camouflaged and are less likely to be eaten by birds, so their survival and reproduction increase rather than decrease.

**Choice C** is incorrect because if survival differs between forms (melanic is better camouflaged on dark bark), then reproductive success differs too, so allele/phenotype frequencies change over time rather than staying constant.

**Choice D** is incorrect because random fluctuation (genetic drift) is not the main expectation here because there is a directional selection pressure (predation based on visibility) that consistently favours the melanic form as pollution darkens trees.
