# BI0248

**Maximum mark: 11**

Darwin’s finches are a group of approximately 18 finches which share a common ancestor and are found on the Galapagos islands. Four of these finches are shown below.

1.
    1. Explain the differences in beak shape and size shown in the four individuals A, B, C, D. [4 marks]

    2. State two possible causes of genetic variation between finches found on the **same** island. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. Any **four** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ beak variation is due to natural selection [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ adaptive radiation [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ finches with different beaks have different food sources **OR** occupy different niches    [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ some individuals, **OR** finches, in a population are born with a random genetic mutation that affects beak size **OR** shape [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ finches with beaks better adapted to getting the food source are more likely to survive    [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ surviving individuals reproduce **OR** pass the trait onto their offspring [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ repeating selection over many generations leads to changes in beak size **OR** shape on the individual islands [1 mark]

        [4 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
        Differences in the beak shapes developed through the process of **natural $\hspace{1.5em}$ selection** ($\checkmark$). Natural selection is selecting the best-fitted traits over generations in a species. Populations of finches on different islands have **different food sources** ($\checkmark$). Some finches in a population are born with a **random genetic mutation** that affects their beak shape ($\checkmark$); if the beak shape makes them better adapted to the food on the island, then the birds are **more likely to survive** ($\checkmark$). The finches that survive breed and **pass the mutation onto their offspring** ($\checkmark$); therefore, the more advantageous beak shape gets enriched in the population, out-competing other beak shapes.

    2. Any **two**  of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ mutation [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ random fertilisation [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ random orientation of chromosomes in meiosis [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
        Genetic variation could be caused by **mutations** ($\checkmark$) or through **random fertilisation** ($\checkmark$)in sexual reproduction.

    3. Any **two** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ finches on the different islands look similar, so potentially could be the same species [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ to test the hypothesis, finches from different islands need to be bred together [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ members of the same species can breed to produce fertile offspring **OR** if finches cannot produce fertile offspring, then they are different species [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Finches found on different islands are **similar in looks**  ($\checkmark$) and behaviour, which could suggest that the hypothesis is correct. However, it can be tested to be sure. Organisms are considered the same species if they can interbreed and produce fertile offspring. To test this, we would have to **introduce males and females from different islands** and see if they **would breed** ($\checkmark$) **and produce fertile** offspring ($\checkmark$). So far, on the Galapagos islands, there are reported 13 different species of finches.

2. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ variation exists within a population [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ competition for resources due to more individuals than the environment can support    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ best adapted more likely to survive **OR** survival of the fittest [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ surviving organisms pass on characteristics to their offspring     [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    Species having more offspring than can be supported by the environment will lead to **competition for resources**, such as food or living spaces ($\checkmark$). All offspring produced will have a **variation in traits** ($\checkmark$)due to the mutations, sexual reproduction and meiosis. The individuals better adapted to the given environment will survive and pass on their **genes to the next generation** ($\checkmark$). Natural selection refers to the mechanism of evolution in which each succeeding generation has better adaptation to the environment than the previous one due to the **“survival of the fittest”**.
