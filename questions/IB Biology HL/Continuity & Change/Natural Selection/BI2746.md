# BI2746

**Maximum mark: 11**

Green skin colour in *Rana temporaria* (European common frog) is controlled by a dominant allele **G**, while brown skin is caused by a recessive allele **g**. Scientists set up an investigation in one population of *Rana temporaria* to see how allele and genotype frequencies in the gene pool changed over time in response to environmental factors. \
The Hardy-Weinberg equation (shown below) was used to calculate the expected allele and genotype frequencies.

$\ce{p^2 + 2pq + q^2}$ = 1

1. Define the term gene pool.        [1 mark]

2. The scientists observed that 10% of the frogs had brown skin.

    1. Assuming the population is in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, calculate the frequency of the recessive allele                 [1 mark]

    2. Use the equation provided to calculate the expected frequency of heterozygotes.                [2 marks]

    The actual observed frequency of heterozygous frogs in the population was 0.38.

    3. By comparing the observed and expected heterozygote frequencies, deduce, with a reason, whether this population is in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.    [1 mark]

3. Identify **one** condition required for a population to remain in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium. [1 mark]

In a different region, the scientists observed that brown frogs have a higher survival rate than green frogs.

4. Explain how this would lead to evolutionary change in the gene pool of that population.               [3 marks]

5. Distinguish between artificial selection and natural selection.       [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ complete set of genes and their different alleles in a population         [1 mark]

[1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ The gene pool is the **total set of genes** and their different **alleles** in a **population** ($\checkmark$).

2. 1.

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ 0.316 **OR** 0.32            [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{q = \sqrt{(gg)} = \sqrt{(0.10)} =}$ **0.316**  ($\checkmark$)

    $\hspace{1em}$ The frequency of the recessive allele is **0.32** ($\checkmark$)

    2. any **two** of the following:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ p = 0.684 **OR** 2pq = 2 $\times$ 0.684 $\times$ 0.316                [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ 0.432 **OR** 0.43        [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ p = 1 – q\
    $\hspace{1em}$ = 1 – 0.316 \
    $\hspace{1em}$ = **0.684** ($\checkmark$)

    $\hspace{1em}$ 2pq = 2 $\times$ 0.684 $\times$ 0.316 = **0.432** ($\checkmark$)

    3.

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ no **BECAUSE** observed heterozygous frequency is lower than expected         [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ yes **BECAUSE** the difference between observed frequency and expected is very small    [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ Since the **observed frequency** is **lower than expected**, this suggests the population is **not in** Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium ($\checkmark$)

3. any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ no mutation         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ no migration **OR** gene flow        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ no natural selection         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ random mating                 [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large population size         [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    There must be **random mating** in the population ($\checkmark$).

4. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ brown frogs have the recessive genotype (gg)        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ brown frogs survive and reproduce more than green frogs        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ g **OR** recessive allele becomes more frequent in the gene pool over generations     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ natural selection acts on heritable variation in the population     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ population undergoes directional selection favouring recessive **OR** g allele         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shift in allele frequencies in gene pool over time         [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Brown frogs have the **recessive genotype** (gg) ($\checkmark$). They **survive** and **reproduce** more, so the **g allele** becomes **more common** in the population ($\checkmark$). Over generations, **natural selection increases** the **frequency** of the **recessive allele**, leading to a **shift** in **allele frequencies** favouring brown frogs ($\checkmark$).

5. any **two** of the following *(must include **one** statement for artificial selection and **one** statement for natural selection to gain full marks)*

    ***Artificial selection***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ driven by human choice of which individuals reproduce        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ aims to increase traits desirable to humans                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ usually results in reduced genetic variation            [1 mark]

    ***Natural selection***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ driven by environmental pressures affecting survival and reproduction            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increases the frequency of traits that improve survival or reproductive success in the wild                                 [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ maintains **OR** shifts genetic variation based on fitness             [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Artificial selection is when **humans choose** individuals with a **desired trait** to **breed** ($\checkmark$). **Natural selection** happens when the **environmental pressures favour** traits that increase **survival** or **reproduction** ($\checkmark$).
