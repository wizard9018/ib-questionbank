# BI1181

The graph shows the relationship between the mean number of Forrest mice (*Leggadina forresti*) in the Tasmanian desert and fur colour. What type of natural selection is demonstrated on this graph?

![BI1181](images/BI1181.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Sexual selection

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Stabilising selection

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Directional selection

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Disruptive selection

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In stabilising selection, selection pressures act to remove extreme varieties. There are a larger number of mice with intermediate colours of fur, compared to the extremes. **Choice B** is correct.

In sexual selection, selection pressures act to make some mates more appealing to other members of the same species e.g. male mice with the darkest fur attract more mates; **Choice A** is incorrect. Directional selection describes natural selection where one extreme trait is favoured over others e.g.there are more darker mice in the population than any other fur colour; **Choice C** is incorrect. Disruptive selection describes when selection pressures act to remove intermediate varieties e.g. there would be a higher number of light and dark furred mice compared to the intermediate mice; **Choice D** is incorrect.
