# BI1485

The image shows the Galapagos marine iguana (*Amblyrhynchus cristatus*) and the Galapagos land iguana (*Conolophus subcristatus*). Marine iguanas have flatter tails and are thought to have diverged from land iguanas 8$-$10 million years ago.

What could explain the difference in tail morphology?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Random mutations in the marine iguana’s DNA

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Differences in diet

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ The need for the marine iguana to swim for food

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The marine iguana smashing its tail on rocks while swimming

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Evolution by natural selection acts on variations that arise due to random mutations in DNA. If organisms develop mutations that are beneficial to their survival, then they are more likely to survive and breed; **Choice A**  is correct.

Diet may affect health, but it does not change inherited traits like the tail structure of marine iguanas; **Choice B**  is incorrect. 

The need for the marine iguana to swim for food describes the selective pressure. However, "need" does not cause genetic change. Traits do not arise because they are needed - they arise through mutation and are selected if advantageous; **Choice C**  is incorrect. 

Flattening a tail by smashing it on rocks is an acquired characteristic, which is not inherited; **Choice D**  is incorrect.
