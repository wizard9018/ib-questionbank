# BI0242

The graph shows the number of female African elephants (*Loxodonta africana*) born without tusks in Gorongosa National Park. How does natural selection explain the trend shown in the graph?

![female elephants born tuskless graph](<images/female elephants born tuskless graph.png>)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Elephants broke their tusks while fighting, and their offspring were born without tusks.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Poachers hunted elephants with tusks, resulting in an increased survival rate for elephants without tusks.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Elephants born without tusks are better fighters and so survived better.
- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Mutations resulting in the absence of tusk formation began to occur more frequently.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because poaching removed tusked elephants from the breeding population, so females that were genetically tuskless had higher survival and reproductive success. Because tusklessness is heritable, the allele(s) for tusklessness were passed on more often, increasing the proportion born without tusks over generations, which is an example of natural selection in response to a selection pressure.

 **Choice A** is incorrect because broken tusks are an acquired change and are not inherited.

 **Choice C** is incorrect because the data are explained by poaching-driven selection rather than fighting ability.

 **Choice D** is incorrect because mutation is the source of variation, but the trend is best explained by selection changing frequencies, not a sudden rise in mutation rate.
