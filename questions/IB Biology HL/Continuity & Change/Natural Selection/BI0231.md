# BI0231

The peppered moth (*Biston betularia*) is a classic example of natural selection. During the Industrial Revolution in Britain, soot from factories darkened tree bark and reduced the amount of pale lichen. Over time, the frequency of the melanic (dark) form increased relative to the light form.

[Source: Sale, B.(2010, July 8). File:(1931) Peppered Moth (*Biston betularia*) - f.carbonaria (4777708667).jpg. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved October 30, 2022, from https:// commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:(1931)_Peppered_Moth_(Biston_betularia)_-_f.carbonaria_(4777708667).jpg. CC-BY-2.0.]

Which process best explains the increase in the frequency of the melanic form?

- I. $\hspace{1em}$ Increased mutation rate
- II. $\hspace{.7em}$    Natural selection
- III. $\hspace{.5em}$    Sexual selection

- A. $\hspace{1em}$    I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$    II only
- C. $\hspace{1em}$    I and II only
- D.$\hspace{1em}$     I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Statement I** is false because mutation only creates new alleles and does not make one form become common on its own; the rise in melanic moths happened because the melanic allele was favoured and selected for, not because mutations suddenly started happening more often.

**Statement II** is true because natural selection increased the frequency of the melanic allele. In polluted environments, dark moths were better camouflaged against soot-darkened bark, so they were less likely to be eaten and more likely to survive and reproduce, passing the melanic allele to their offspring. Increased mutation rate is not responsible because mutation mainly creates new variation and does not direct which phenotype becomes common; the change in frequency occurred because selection favoured the dark form, and sexual selection is unrelated because the change was driven by predation, not mate choice.

**Statement III** is false because sexual selection involves differences in mating success due to mate choice or competition, whereas industrial melanism was caused by selective predation (birds eating the more visible moths), not a preference for dark moths as mates.

**Choice B** is therefore the correct answer to the question as it only includes the one true **Statament II**.

**Choices A, C** and **D** are all incorrect as they include one or more of the false **Statements I** and **III**.
