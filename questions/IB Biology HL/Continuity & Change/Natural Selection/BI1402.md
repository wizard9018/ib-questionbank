# BI1402

Male peregrine falcons (*Falco peregrinus*) try to interest females with aerial manoeuvres and dives. If females prefer males that are faster and more athletic, what is this an example of?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Artificial selection

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Directional selection

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Stabilising selection

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Disruptive selection

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Although this question definitely describes sexual selection, it is also directional selection because only faster males are chosen. This would likely lead to an increase in the average speed of falcons over time as offspring inherit this trait. **Choice B** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because artificial selection involves humans selecting desired traits when breeding organisms. **Choice C** is incorrect because stabilising selection tends to favour individuals with average characteristics rather than those at the extremes (in this case, speed and athletic ability). Disruptive selection involves selection pressures that favour both extremes. Since only faster birds are favoured, **Choice D** cannot be correct.
