# BI2597

The frequency of a recessive phenotype in a population of dung beetles (*Onthophagus australis*) is higher than predicted by the Hardy-Weinberg equation. Which is the most likely reason?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Natural selection favours individuals with the recessive phenotype

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The frequency of dominant alleles is decreasing due to selection against heterozygotes

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Dung beetles with a dominant phenotype have a higher chance of survival

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Heterozygotes have a higher reproductive success rate than homozygotes

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
If natural selection favours individuals with the recessive phenotype, then these individuals are more likely to survive and reproduce. This will result in a higher-than-predicted frequency of the recessive phenotype; **Choice A**  is correct.

Heterozygotes will express both the dominant and recessive allele; therefore, if heterozygotes are being selected against, then the frequency of both dominant and recessive alleles will be affected. However, this alone does not explain why the frequency of recessive phenotypes is affected; **Choice B** is incorrect. If dung beetles with a dominant phenotype have a higher chance of survival, then the frequency of the dominant phenotype would be higher than expected rather than the recessive phenotype; **Choice C**  is incorrect. If heterozygotes have a higher reproductive success rate than homozygotes, then this would maintain the frequency of both alleles in the population, preventing either allele from having a higher-than-expected frequency; **Choice D**  is incorrect.
