# BI1101

Which of the following are the **same** for the processes of primary and secondary succession?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | The presence of pioneer species  |
    | II. | A varying rate of succession |
    | III. | The starting point of succession   |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Both primary and secondary succession involve pioneer species (**Statement I**). Pioneer species are the first organisms to colonise an area during succession, regardless of whether it's primary or secondary succession.

The rate of succession can vary in both primary and secondary succession (**Statement II**). The rate can depend on various factors, including the environmental conditions and the specific organisms involved. **Choice B** is therefore, correct, and **Choice A** is incorrect as it only includes one of the true statements.

Primary succession begins in barren, lifeless environments, for example, when glaciers expose bare rock, whereas secondary succession occurs in areas with existing communities that have been disturbed, such as after forest fires. **Statement III** is, not true, making **Choices C** and **D** incorrect.
