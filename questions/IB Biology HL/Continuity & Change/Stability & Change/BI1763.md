# BI1763

**Maximum mark: 3**

A study was conducted to investigate the relationship between water temperature and phytoplankton density in a bay along the southwest shoreline of Wales, U.K. This study was motivated by concerns about the potential effects of climate change on marine ecosystems, particularly how rising water temperatures could affect phytoplankton populations in the sea. Phytoplankton, which are the primary producers in marine food webs, play a crucial role in carbon cycling and are sensitive to changes in environmental conditions.
Each month the chlorophyll concentration of seawater samples was measured, using a spectrophotometer. The concentration of chlorophyll is an indicator of phytoplankton density.
In parallel with the in situ measurements, mesocosm experiments, like the one shown below, were conducted in the laboratory using water samples from the bay over the same time period.

![BI1763](images/BI1763.png)

Discuss the advantages and limitations of carrying out these laboratory mesocosm experiments compared to the monthly measurements of chlorophyll concentration in situ. [3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Mesocosm experiments offer the **advantage** of **controlled environmental conditions**, allowing for **precise manipulation** of **variables** like **temperature** ($\checkmark$), and they **minimise environmental impact** by **avoiding direct interference** with natural ecosystems ($\checkmark$). However, they may **not fully replicate** the complexity of **natural environments**, potentially leading to results that **lack ecological validity** ($\checkmark$), and they can be **resource-intensive**, requiring **specialised equipment**($\checkmark$).
