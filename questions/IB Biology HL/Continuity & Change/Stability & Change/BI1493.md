# BI1493

Which process(es) can trigger succession within a community?

- I. $\hspace{1.6em}$ New plants colonising an arid area

- II. $\hspace{1.3em}$ Recolonisation of an area by plants after a forest fire

- III. $\hspace{1em}$ Changes in rainfall patterns

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Succession is the progression of species that gradually replace one another over time within a community. It can be triggered by changes in both abiotic and biotic factors. New plants colonising an arid area (**Statement I**) would be an example of primary succession if the area was previously completely lifeless and soilless, such as bare rock or sand dunes, and plants begin to colonise it for the first time. The recolonisation of an area by plants after a forest fire (**Statement II**) is a classic example of secondary succession. After a forest fire, the existing community is destroyed or heavily damaged, but the soil remains, allowing plants and animals to recolonise the area. This process starts with pioneer species and gradually leads to the re-establishment of a forest ecosystem. Changes in rainfall patterns (**Statement III**) can directly trigger primary succession by altering previously uninhabitable areas, for example creating new ponds or wetlands or it can initiate secondary succession by changing the conditions of existing ecosystems so drastically that the current community composition cannot be maintained, leading to a significant shift in the species makeup over time. As all three statements are true, **Choice D** is the correct answer to the question.

**Choices A, B** and **C** are incorrect, as they do not include all three statements.
