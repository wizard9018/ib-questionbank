# BI1495

Ecologists studied plant communities on land exposed by a retreating glacier. They sampled sites that had been ice-free for different lengths of time, from 10 to 200 years. Which trend would be expected as the time since exposure increases?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Nitrogen-fixing pioneer species become more abundant as soil nitrogen content rises

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Soil pH increases as organic matter accumulates in the soil

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Total respiration by the community decreases as the community approaches the climax stage

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ A greater proportion of the ecosystem's nutrients is held in living biomass and recycled through decomposition

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

As primary succession progresses, the community's biomass increases. Larger, longer-lived plants such as shrubs and trees build up nutrients in their tissues. When leaf litter and dead organisms fall, decomposers break them down and return the nutrients to the soil, where plants take them up again. So more of the ecosystem's nutrients are held in living biomass, and nutrient cycling increases; **Choice D** is correct.

Nitrogen-fixing pioneer species, such as legumes and alder, grow well early on because the soil has very little nitrogen. As they add nitrogen to the soil, they make conditions suitable for other species. These later species then outcompete the pioneers, so nitrogen-fixers become less abundant over time. This is an example of facilitation; **Choice A** is incorrect.

Plant litter and humus build up as succession goes on. Their decomposition releases organic acids and carbon dioxide, so soil pH usually decreases over time; **Choice B** is incorrect.

The community's biomass increases during succession, so the total amount of respiration also increases. Near the climax stage, community respiration approaches gross primary production; **Choice C** is incorrect.
