# BI2711

The diagram shows the response of a chaparral ecosystem to periodic wildfires. What type of ecological succession does this illustrate?

![eDIT2711](images/eDIT2711.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Primary succession

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Secondary succession

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Arrested succession

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Cyclical succession

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because cyclical succession describes the recovery and renewal of ecosystems that rely on regular natural disturbances. Chaparral vegetation is specifically adapted to survive and regenerate after fires, which are essential to its long-term stability. \
**Choice A** is incorrect. Primary succession occurs in areas where no previous ecosystem existed, such as after a volcanic eruption or the retreat of a glacier. Since chaparral ecosystems recover after fire rather than forming from barren land, this is not primary succession.\
**Choice B** is incorrect. Secondary succession occurs after a disturbance removes vegetation but leaves the soil intact. While fire initiates regrowth, chaparral ecosystems are specifically adapted to recurring fire cycles, making this a better example of cyclical succession. \
**Choice C** is incorrect. Arrested succession occurs when environmental conditions prevent the further progression of succession, often due to factors such as poor soil quality or human intervention. Chaparral ecosystems continue to regenerate, so this does not apply.
