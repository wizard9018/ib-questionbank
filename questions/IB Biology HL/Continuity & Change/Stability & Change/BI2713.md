# BI2713

In which of the following environments is arrested succession most likely to occur?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ An area that has experienced a volcanic eruption and is now devoid of soil

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ A grassland with heavy grazing by herbivores, such as cattle

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ A wetland area that experiences seasonal flooding, allowing regular plant turnover

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ A forest recovering from a fire, progressing to a mature climax community

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because heavy grazing removes young woody plants, preventing the community from developing beyond the grassland stage. This halts the progression of succession, a phenomenon known as arrested succession. \
**Choice A** is incorrect. This is an example of primary succession, which starts from bare rock but is not necessarily arrested unless another disturbance occurs. \
**Choice C** is incorrect. This causes cyclic succession, where plant communities shift but are not permanently halted. \
**Choice D** is incorrect. This represents secondary succession, where the ecosystem gradually returns to a climax community rather than being stopped in an earlier stage.
