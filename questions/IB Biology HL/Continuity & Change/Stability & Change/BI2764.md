# BI2764

The graph below shows the change in total ecosystem biomass over time during primary succession on a newly formed volcanic island.

![BI2764](images/BI2764.png)

Which of the following best explains the pattern shown in the graph?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Pioneer species dominate early on, but biomass declines as conditions become less favourable.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Rapid colonisation by herbivores boosts biomass early, but plant growth later slows due to overgrazing.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Biomass increases quickly as simple organisms establish, then levels off as the ecosystem reaches a stable climax community.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Initial volcanic activity provides organic compounds for early growth, but the eventual cooling of lava reduces productivity.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because this accurately reflects primary succession, where pioneer species such as lichens and mosses colonise bare rock, contributing to soil formation. Over time, more complex plant species establish and animal species migrate, increasing biomass. As the community matures and reaches a climax community, the rate of increase slows, leading to a plateau in total biomass.\
**Choice A** is incorrect because while pioneer species do dominate early, conditions generally become more favourable over time due to improved soil and nutrient availability. Biomass doesn't decline — it increases as more species establish.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because herbivores do not initiate primary succession, and early biomass increase is driven primarily by plants, not animals. Overgrazing may affect succession in some ecosystems, but it's not a typical explanation for the plateau in biomass during primary succession.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because, despite the high mineral ion content of newly-formed volcanic rock, there is a lack of suitable complex organic compounds (e.g. amino acids) that contain those minerals. Only succession can allow those organic compounds to be built up and to be recycled over successive generations. The graph does not indicate a reduction in productivity.
