# BI1501

Which one of the following examples of disturbance is most likely to initiate secondary succession rather than primary succession?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ The retreat of a glacier, exposing bare rock. 

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ A landslide that removes all vegetation and soil in an area. 

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ A forest fire that burns down all the trees but leaves the soil intact. 

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The drying of a salt lake, resulting in a newly exposed land surface.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Secondary succession occurs in areas where there is already soil present, and some form of previous vegetation existed before the disturbance. The disturbance event, such as a fire, clears away the existing vegetation but doesn't destroy the soil, allowing for the relatively rapid regeneration of plant life from seeds, roots, or other surviving plant parts. **Choice C** is therefore the correct answer. 

The retreat of a glacier, exposing bare rock, would typically initiate primary succession. In this scenario, organisms would colonise the newly exposed rock and gradually break it down to form soil, eventually leading to the establishment of a community of plants and other organisms. **Choice A** is incorrect.

A landslide that removes all vegetation and soil in an area would also typically initiate primary succession. Similar to the retreat of a glacier, the area would start with bare rock or sterile soil, and the process of soil formation and colonisation by pioneer species would begin. **Choice B** is incorrect.

The drying of a salt lake can result in saline or sodic soils which may present inhospitable conditions for many plant species, indicating that primary succession, starting from sterile soil, is more likely. Secondary succession is possible if there are enough nutrients in the soil, however, this disturbance is not as likely to cause secondary succession as forest fires. **Choice D** is incorrect.
