# BI1492

The diagram shows an example of ecological succession. Which stage represents a pioneer community?

![BI1492FC](images/BI1492FC.png)

[Source: Adapted from Joshfn. (2014, May 19). File: AP Biology - primary succession drawing.svg. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved November 28 , 2023, from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: AP_Biology_-_Primary_Succession_Drawing.svg.  Copyright under CC BY 3.0]

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Bare rocks

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Mosses and lichens

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Annual weeds, mosses and lichens

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Beech and maple broadleaf forest

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

The pioneer community is the first stage of ecological succession and is characterised by species that are hardy, can colonise new areas quickly, and often can endure harsh environmental conditions. These characteristics enable them to establish a foothold in an area where other organisms cannot yet survive. In the diagram, these are the mosses and lichens. By breaking down rock into soil and retaining moisture, they help create conditions that allow other species to subsequently colonise the area; **Choice B**  is correct.

Bare rocks are the initial condition of an area before colonisation by a biological community, not a stage of the biological community itself. So, they are not a pioneer community but rather the substrate on which the pioneer community will establish; **Choice A**  is incorrect. Although lichens and mosses represent the pioneer community, annual weeds represent the next stage of succession; **Choice C**  is incorrect. Beech and maple broadleaf forests represent the climax community, as they represent the end point of ecological succession; **Choice D**  is incorrect.
