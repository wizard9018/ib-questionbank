# BI1498

Which process would **not** lead to arrested ecological succession?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Adding organic matter and fertilisers to the soil 

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Hunting species to extinction 

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Grazing by livestock

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Climate change resulting in increased drought

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Adding organic matter and fertilisers to the soil is likely to enhance soil quality and fertility, potentially accelerating succession by improving conditions for the growth of a wider range of plant species. This intervention can increase the rate at which a disturbed or degraded area recovers by providing the essential nutrients needed for plant growth, leading to quicker establishment of a stable ecosystem. In contrast, arrested succession is characterised by a halt or significant slowdown in the natural progression towards a more complex ecosystem, often due to persistent adverse conditions or disturbances.\

**Choice A is correct**.

**Choice B**  is incorrect as hunting species can remove key species from an ecosystem, potentially halting succession if those species play critical roles in ecological processes such as seed dispersal, pollination, or predation that maintain the balance of the ecosystem.\

**Choice C**  is incorrect as grazing by livestock lowers the chances of a community reaching a climax as native vegetation may be destroyed, which reduces the biodiversity in the community.\

**Choice D**  is incorrect as drought will lower the volume of water that an area receives which may prevent a community from reaching a climax.
