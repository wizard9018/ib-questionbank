# BI1500

Which statement best reflects the role of keystone species in succession?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Keystone species typically prevent succession by dominating the ecosystem.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Keystone species only appear in climax communities and maintain the community structure.

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Keystone species can influence the successional pathway by creating or modifying habitats.

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Keystone species have minimal impact on succession as they are not involved in ecosystem engineering

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Keystone species have a disproportionate effect on their environment relative to their abundance. They can modify the habitat in ways that affect which species are present during succession. For example, beavers are considered a keystone species because their dam-building activities create wetland habitats that support a diverse array of other species. This, in turn, can alter the trajectory of succession in the area. **Choice C** is therefore the correct answer. 

Keystone species do not typically prevent succession by dominating the ecosystem. In fact, they often facilitate succession by creating conditions that allow other species to thrive. **Choice A** is therefore incorrect. 

Keystone species are not exclusive to climax communities. They can be found in various stages of succession and can influence the community structure throughout the process, for example beavers creating new habitats that support an array of plants and animals. **Choice B** is therefore incorrect.

Keystone species do have a significant impact on succession as they often play roles in ecosystem engineering, which involves modifying the physical environment in ways that affect other organisms. This can include activities like building nests, digging burrows, or altering the availability of resources such as food or shelter. **Choice D** is therefore incorrect.
