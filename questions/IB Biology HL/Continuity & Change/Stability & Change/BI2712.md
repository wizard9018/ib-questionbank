# BI2712

The following data shows the percentage cover of annual weeds, grasses, and shrubs in an area over ten years. Which human activity best explains why this ecosystem has not progressed beyond the grass-shrub stage of succession over the past six years?

![BI2712](images/BI2712.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased levels of rainfall in the region

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Intensive livestock grazing and wetland drainage for agriculture

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Loss of permafrost due to global warming

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Pollution due to agrochemicals

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct. Intensive livestock grazing and wetland drainage for agriculture prevent succession by physically removing or suppressing tree seedlings. **Choice A** is incorrect. Increased levels of rainfall may actually promote growth and accelerate succession rather than prevent it. **Choice C** is incorrect. Loss of permafrost would not cause halted succession in a grassland/shrubland setting. **Choice D** is incorrect. Agrochemicals could harm plant life but wouldn't necessarily stabilise the ecosystem in a specific stage of succession.
