# BI1099

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Logging trees from forests and fishing from marine waters are two forms of income derived from natural resources. Outline the factors that need to be addressed to make both forms of income sustainable.    [5 marks]

2. Describe how choosing to consume locally grown foods can reduce the environmental impact of agriculture, making it more sustainable.    [3 marks]

3. Discuss the causes and impacts of eutrophication on water bodies due to agricultural practices.         [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:(*answer must includes at least **one** statement from each section*)

    ***Harvesting trees OR timber as natural income*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ sustainability of harvesting trees can be monitored by surveying soil characteristics of logged and unlogged sites in the forest     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ selective logging practices that only target mature trees **OR** target overpopulated trees **OR** ensure rate of logging does not exceed growth rate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ promote reforestation **OR** afforestation **OR** replacing **OR** replanting trees [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ protect **OR** conserve biodiversity within forest        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ leave enough trees to produce fruit **OR** seeds for next season    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ monitor **OR** collect data to compare volume of trees removed with volume of growth    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ encourage certification programmes like the Forest Stewardship Council [1 mark]

    ***Harvesting fish from marine waters as natural income*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ sustainability fishing can be achieved by catching fish that do not diminish the fish stock    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the population size of the fish can be monitored     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the number of juveniles in the population of fish can be monitored, **OR** population of fish can be monitored to see if it has more younger or more older fish           [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ more younger fish in the population indicates a growing population, so more sustainable **OR** more older fish in the population indicates a declining population, so not sustainable    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the increase in number of fish annually will provide an estimate of what can be caught        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ maximum sustainable yield (MSY) is the amount of harvest **OR** the maximum average catch without reducing the potential for growth of the fish in the future    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ harvesting at lower than the MSY or at the rate of the MSY is favourable [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ use fishing techniques **OR** equipment that minimise bycatch **OR** non-target species    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ designate marine protected areas **OR** breeding grounds    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enforce regulations to prevent overfishing **OR** illegal fishing practices    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ promote international cooperation in managing shared marine resources    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The sustainability of harvesting trees can be achieved by **replanting trees** ($\checkmark$) that are cut down. The **volume** or mass of **timber** can be **monitored** and replaced by **planting saplings** that will **replace** the timber when **fully grown** ($\checkmark$).

    Fishing practices need to be carried out so that by catching fish, the **original stock** is **not diminished** ($\checkmark$). **Maximum sustainable yield** is the amount of harvest or catch that **does not decrease** the **potential** for **growth** ($\checkmark$) in the future. **Harvesting fish** at a rate **lower than the maximum sustainable yield** ($\checkmark$) will make fishing sustainable.

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ agricultural crops are usually grown in rural areas and transported to the cities        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transportation uses fossil fuels, which release carbon dioxide    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ release of carbon dioxide causes global warming     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ release of carbon dioxide increases the carbon footprint of crops    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ consumption of locally grown crops reduces carbon footprint     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ demand for unseasonal foods increases carbon footprint    **OR** consumption of seasonal foods will reduce carbon footprint      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reducing carbon footprint will make crops more sustainable        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ locally grown produce minimises need for packaging, as it is not being transported long distances and will therefore reduce waste        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Transportation** of agricultural crops from rural areas to cities **uses fossil fuels** ($\checkmark$). This causes the release of carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, **increasing** the **carbon footprint** ($\checkmark$) of crops. **Locally grown produce** has a **smaller carbon footprint**, and because it is not being transported long distances, it needs **minimal packaging**, so there is **less waste** ($\checkmark$).

    Also, eating foods grown **seasonally will reduce the carbon footprint** ($\checkmark$) because some unseasonal foods, like strawberries, have to be brought in from where they are grown. In the long run, this is not sustainable.

3. any **eight** of the following (at least one from each section):

    ***Causes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ excessive fertiliser used **OR** applying fertiliser before heavy rainfall        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ excess nutrient runoff from fertilisers     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ improper management of manure can lead to its runoff into water bodies        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nitrogen **OR** phosphates carried in runoff    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ erosion from agricultural fields can carry sediment into water bodies, which carries nutrients        [1 mark]

    ***Impacts*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ excess nutrients to the water bodies will cause algae to grow rapidly **OR** algal blooms    [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ growth of algae will block sunlight to underwater plants, causing them to die    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ plant and algae die under surface due to lack of light        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in nutrients **OR** dead organic matter leads to more algal growth    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in dead organic matter decomposed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ aerobic bacteria are decomposers [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ greater oxygen demand **OR** biochemical oxygen demand **OR** BOD    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxygen-depleted water can lead to fish **OR** other aquatic organisms dying    [1 mark]

    ***As eutrophication takes place, leads to further effects*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in net productivity    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ decrease in biodiversity **OR** diversity of organisms    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shorter food chains    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ toxic cyanobacteria can take over    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ changes in fish communities        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some types of algae that thrive in eutrophic conditions can produce toxins harmful to humans and wildlife, which can enter the food chain and pose health risks    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ economic impacts on tourism **OR**, fisheries **OR** water treatment [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The runoff from using **fertilisers** ($\checkmark$) in agricultural fields can contaminate local water bodies, leading to an **increase** in the **growth of algae** ($\checkmark$) due to added nutrients. The algal growth will **block light** from penetrating the water bodies, so the **underwater plants** die and decompose ($\checkmark$). The death of underwater plants will cause an **increase** in **dead organic matter** ($\checkmark$), promoting the **growth** of **bacteria** ($\checkmark$). These aerobic bacteria will cause a **decrease** in **dissolved oxygen** ($\checkmark$) in the water as they respire and may lead to the **death** of the **fish** and other **organisms** ($\checkmark$) in the water.

    This can further lead to a **decrease in biodiversity** ($\checkmark$) and the **growth of toxic algae** or bacteria ($\checkmark$).
