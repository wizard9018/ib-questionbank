# BI1497

Which statement explains why climax communities are more stable than pioneer communities?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Climax communities are often less complex than pioneer communities

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Climax communities often have deeper and more nutrient-rich soils than pioneer communities

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Climax communities are often less species-rich than pioneer communities

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Climax communities provide less energy to support consumers

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

Climax communities are the last stage of ecological succession and are in equilibrium with natural environmental conditions. Pioneer communities are the first stage of ecological succession and are populated by hardy species that can withstand difficult environmental conditions. Climax communities often have deeper and more nutrient-rich soils than pioneer communities which means that they can support a range of organisms for a long period of time. **Choice B**  is correct.

**Choice A**  is incorrect as climax communities are often more complex than pioneer communities as they contain a larger range of organisms than pioneer communities. **Choice C**  is incorrect as climax communities are often more species-rich than pioneer communities. Climax communities, with their greater plant biomass and diversity, generally have a higher primary productivity, which translates into more energy available to support a wider array of consumers. The complex food webs in climax communities efficiently recycle nutrients and energy, supporting larger populations of consumers than is possible in less developed communities. **Choice D**  is incorrect.
