# BI2769

The table shows data collected from an investigation into the effects of grazing on vegetation types over time:

| Year | Undisturbed site | Grazed site |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 0 | Grass | Grass |
| 10 | Shrubs | Grass |
| 20 | Young trees | Grass |
| 30 | Mature trees | Grass |

Which conclusion is most likely to be formed from the data?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The grazed site reached its climax community earlier than the undisturbed site.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Succession has been delayed at the grazed site due to lower nutrient availability.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Grazing has limited species diversity, but not the rate of succession.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The grazed site shows arrested succession, preventing progression beyond early stages.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because it reflects that succession is not progressing in the grazed site — it remains grass-only.\
**Choice A** is incorrect because a grass-only system in this context isn't the natural climax community; the climax would be a forest.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because while overgrazing can affect nutrients, the main factor shown here is continued grazing pressure, not soil fertility. Plus, nutrients often increase in grazed systems from dung/urine, and we have no data on the nutrient availability. \
**Choice C** is incorrect because the site hasn't progressed beyond grass in 30 years, showing that succession is clearly being impacted.
