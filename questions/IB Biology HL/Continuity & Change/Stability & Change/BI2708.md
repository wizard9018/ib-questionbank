# BI2708

Why are sea otters and African elephants considered keystone species?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide" valign="top"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. $\hspace{0.8em}$ | Their activities, such as eating urchins and clearing trees, disproportionately shape the structure of the ecosystem, supporting other species |
    | II. $\hspace{0.4em}$ | If the species were to disappear from the ecosystem, no other species would be able to perform the same ecological functions or roles|
    | III. | They are apex predators that exert top-down control of populations in their communities |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Statement I** is true because keystone species have a disproportionate impact on community structure. **Statement II** is true because the ecological roles of keystone species are often unique, and their removal can lead to significant cascading effects in the ecosystem.\
**Statement III** is false because while some keystone species may be predators, being an apex predator is not a requirement for keystone status. Sea otters are mid-level predators, and African elephants are herbivores — neither is an apex predator.\
**Choice C** is, therefore, correct because **Statements I** and **II** are true. \
**Choices A** and **B** are incorrect as they are missing one of the true statements.\
**Choice D** is incorrect as it includes the false **Statement III**.
