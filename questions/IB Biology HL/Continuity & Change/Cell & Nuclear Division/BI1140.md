# BI1140

A human pancreatic cell divides three times by mitosis. What is the end result of these divisions?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Two genetically identical daughter cells.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Four genetically distinct daughter cells.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Eight genetically identical daughter cells.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Sixteen genetically distinct daughter cells.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because each successive round of mitosis doubles the number of cells, assuming all daughter cells divide in the next round. Each parent cell becomes two daughter cells, which can then divide themselves:

1 --> 2 --> 4 -->8

The number of cells therefore doubles in each round, rather than increasing by two. This gives \(2:sup[3]  = 8\) cells, not \(3 x 2 = 6\). The daughter cells are genetically identical to the original cell, assuming no mutations occur.

**Choice A** is incorrect because two genetically identical daughter cells would be produced after one round of mitosis, not three.

**Choice B** is incorrect because four daughter cells would be produced after two rounds of mitosis, not three. Additionally, mitosis produces genetically identical cells, not genetically distinct cells.

**Choice D** is incorrect because sixteen daughter cells would be produced after four rounds of mitosis, not three. These cells would also be genetically identical, not genetically distinct.
