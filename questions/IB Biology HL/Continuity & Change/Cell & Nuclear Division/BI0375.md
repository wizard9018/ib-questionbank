2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$ happens during prophase I     [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ homologous chromosomes pair up **OR** bivalents are formed     [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ non-sister chromatids form chiasmata **OR** crossing-over points         [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (during crossing-over) exchange of alleles, **OR** genetic information, **OR** DNA fragments between non-sister chromatids         [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ locations of chiasmata are random         [1 mark]
[3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Crossing over occurs during **Prophase I** ($\checkmark$). During this stage, the chromosomes condense and **homologous chromosomes** pair up to form **bivalents** ($\checkmark$).  Non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes form **chiasmata** ($\checkmark$) at **random** sections ($\checkmark$), **exchanging alleles** between chromosomes ($\checkmark$).

3. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$ crossing-over produces recombinant chromatids **OR** chromatids with new combinations of maternal and paternal alleles OR sister chromatids are no longer genetically identical         [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  random orientation of homologous chromosome pairs occurs in metaphase I     [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ independent segregation of chromatids in meiosis II increases the variety of allele combinations              [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  each gamete receives a different combination of recombinant and non-recombinant chromatids, resulting in genetically unique nuclei     [1 mark]

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Crossing over produces **recombinant chromatids** or **chromatids** with **new combinations of maternal and paternal alleles** ($\checkmark$). **Random orientation** of **homologous chromosome** pairs in **metaphase I** ensures that each gamete receives a **different combination of chromosomes** ($\checkmark$). **Independent segregation** of chromatids in **meiosis II** increases the **variety of allele combinations** in the gametes ($\checkmark$), resulting in genetically unique gametes.
