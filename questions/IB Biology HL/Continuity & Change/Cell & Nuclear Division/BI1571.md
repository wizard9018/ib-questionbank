# BI1571

The data in the table was obtained from a study on the causes of Down syndrome (trisomy 21). The researchers were interested in whether Down syndrome is associated with maternal age, errors originating from maternal or paternal sources, or different meiotic phases. 300 cases resulting in Down syndrome were compared to a control group of 410 unaffected individuals.

![BI1571](images/BI1571.png)

Based on the data in the table, what can be concluded regarding the risk of a child being born with Down syndrome?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ It decreases with maternal age.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ It increases with paternal age.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ It is more likely to be caused by non-disjunction in meiosis II.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ It is less likely to be caused by non-disjunction in male gametes.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because Down syndrome (trisomy 21) is caused by non-disjunction of chromosomes (a pair of chromosomes moving to the same pole) during meiosis. According to the data, paternal causes are less likely, suggesting that male gametes are less likely to contribute to cases of the disease.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the data indicates that older women are more likely to give birth to a child with Down syndrome. **Choice B** is incorrect because paternal age is not mentioned in the table. **Choice C** is incorrect because non-disjunction appears to occur more frequently in meiosis I rather than in meiosis II.
