# BI0393

Which of the following is the typical type of cell division seen in prokaryotic cells?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Mitosis

- B. $\hspace{1em}$    Meiosis
- C. $\hspace{1em}$    Binary fission
- D. $\hspace{1em}$    Budding

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct because prokaryotic cells divide through a process called binary fission. In binary fission, the DNA is replicated, and the cell divides into two genetically identical daughter cells. This is a simpler form of cell division compared to the processes in eukaryotic cells, and it does not involve a spindle apparatus or mitotic structures.

**Choice A** is incorrect because mitosis is the process of cell division in eukaryotic cells, where the cell divides into two genetically identical daughter cells. Prokaryotic cells, which lack membrane-bound organelles and a nucleus, do not undergo mitosis.

**Choice B** is incorrect because meiosis is a process of cell division that produces haploid gametes in eukaryotes. Prokaryotic cells do not undergo meiosis because they are not involved in sexual reproduction and do not have paired chromosomes like eukaryotic organisms.

**Choice D** is incorrect because budding is a form of asexual reproduction in certain organisms, such as yeast or some multicellular organisms, where a new individual forms from a small outgrowth (bud) from the parent organism. This is not the typical mode of division for prokaryotic cells.
