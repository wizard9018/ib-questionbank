2. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ stage C represents the G:sub[2] phase of the interphase [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ stage D represents the M phase **OR** mitosis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ during G:sub[2], the cell prepares for mitosis with duplication of organelles, **OR** other detail of G:sub[2] [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA was replicated in stage B, and its amount is not changed in G:sub[2] [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ during the M phase, **OR** mitosis, there is no reduction in DNA **OR** chromosome number [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ only during cytokinesis, **OR** stage F, does the cell split into two cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ cytokinesis, **OR** stage F, results in halving the DNA content of one cell [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

:u[Sample answer]:

Stage C represents **G:sub[2]** ($\checkmark$), and stage D represents **mitosis** ($\checkmark$). During this time, there is no reduction in the number of chromosomes. During G2, the cell **prepares for mitosis** ($\checkmark$) by doubling the amount of the organelles and producing additional protein. During mitosis, sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles of the cell, but they are still within the confines of the same cell. In anaphase, the amount of chromosomes doubles, but not the amount of the total DNA, as chromosomes are counted by the number of centromeres. The amount of DNA per cell is only reduced **(halved) during cytokinesis** ($\checkmark$), as this is the stage where the cytoplasm containing the two daughter nuclei is **divided into two new cells** ($\checkmark$).
