# BI0206

What is the most likely cause of the following human karyogram?

![BI0206](images/BI0206.png)

- A.    $\hspace{1em}$ Crossing over occurred during mitosis

- B.    $\hspace{1em}$ Crossing over occurred during meiosis
- C.    $\hspace{1em}$ Non-disjunction occurred during mitosis
- D.    $\hspace{1em}$ Non-disjunction occurred during meiosis

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In the karyogram there are 3 copies of chromosome 21, this is known as Trisomy 21. The most common cause of Trisomy 21 is nondisjunction during meiosis I or meiosis II, resulting in gametes that contain an abnormal number of chromosomes; **Choice D** is correct.

**Choices A** and **B** are incorrect as crossing over is not associated with trisomy. Although Trisomy 21 can also arise due to nondisjunction during mitosis in the developing zygote or foetus, it is much less likely; therefore **Choice C** is not the correct answer.
