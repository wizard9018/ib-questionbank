# BI0818

**Maximum mark: 10**

During a field day, students were asked to investigate the effect of soil salinity on the distribution of plant species. Students designed an experiment that measured the number of plant species found up the shore at varying distances from the high tide line. Three groups of students investigated three sites on the same shore. The distance from the high tide line was taken as an indication of the salinity levels. The results are shown in the table.

![BI0818A](images/BI0818A.png)

1.
    1. Identify the independent variable. [1 mark]

    2. Outline the steps students could take, and the equipment they could use, to collect their results.                                [3 marks]

2.
    1. Suggest a reason for the discrepancy in the number of species sampled in areas 1, 2, and 3.                                     [1 mark]

    2. Describe a way the students could reduce the impact of discrepancies on their collected data.                             [1 mark]

3. The different species of plants found in each area were recorded in a table.

![BI0818B](images/BI0818B.png)

Calculate how many times greater the number of plant species found in Area 1 was compared to Area 2.                                 [1 mark]

4. Evaluate the students' investigation into salinity's effect on plant species distribution.                                 [3 marks]

Record the **number** of different **plant species** found in **each quadrat** ($\checkmark$) in a results table.

4. Any **three** of the following (must include strengths and weaknesses):

    **Strengths**\
    $\ce{ -> }$ students sampled three areas **OR** more than one area, **WHICH MEANS** they can calculate a mean from their results **OR** increases the reliability of their results        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ all areas sampled were at the same beach, **WHICH** decreases the effects of confounding variables between trials        [1 mark]

    **Limitations**\
    $\ce{ -> }$ the salinity of the soil wasn't measured, **OR** distance from the high tide line was used as an indirect measurement, **WHICH** means the research question is not addressed, **OR** a conclusion cannot be reached        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large discrepancies in students' results **MEANS** that the data isn't reliable    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ more areas should be sampled to improve reliability    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lack of controlled variables, so students should identify variables such as soil moisture **OR** angle of slope **OR** soil pH **OR** light intensity, **OR** other valid controlled variable (example must be given to award mark)  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ all areas sampled were at the same beach, **WHICH** means the results may not be valid for other beaches            [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The students sampled **more than three** areas so a mean could be taken, and this increases the **reliability** ($\checkmark$) of the results. However, there were **large discrepancies** between student results ($\checkmark$), so **more areas need to be sampled** ($\checkmark$). Also, the **salinity** of the soil was **not measured**; instead, they relied on **indirect measurements**, which means a conclusion can only be supported to a limited degree ($\checkmark$).
