# BI1882

The micrograph shows a cross-section of a marram grass leaf (*Ammophila arenaria*), a xerophyte often found on sand dunes.

![BI1882](images/BI1882.png)
[Source: Adapted from Schools, S. and P. for. (2012, May 2). *Marram Grass*. Flickr. Retrieved 8 September, 2024 from https://www.flickr.com/photos/71183136@N08/6989003016. Copyright by CC 2.0]

Which row in the table correctly describes how this species is adapted to its environment?

| | Adaptation | Purpose |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Thick, waxy cuticle on inside of rolled leaf | To prevent water loss |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Rolled leaf structure | To trap moisture to create a humid microclimate near the surface of the leaf |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Rolled leaf structure | To allow the wind to remove sand from stomata on the outer surface of the leaf |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Bumpy inner surface of rolled leaf | To increase the surface area of the leaf for photosynthesis |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Marram grass leaves are rolled so that stomata are on the inner surface. The leaf's rolled structure creates a humid environment by trapping water vapour that exits the stomata, decreasing the water potential gradient between the stomata and the air. **Choice B** is correct.

Due to the rolling of the leaf, the thick waxy cuticle is on the outer surface of the leaf to prevent water loss; **Choice A** is incorrect. Although the leaf is rolled, this is to reduce water loss as the majority of stomata are on the inner surface of the leaf; **Choice C**  is incorrect. Although the inner surface of the leaf is bumpy due to the rolling of the leaf, this does not increase the rate of photosynthesis as the inside of the leaf is not fully exposed to the sun; **Choice D** is incorrect.
