# BI2777

Which set of conditions is the most suitable for coral reef formation?

| | Water depth | Temperature | Water pH |
| --- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Less than 20m | 23 $\degree$C to 29 $\degree$C | Above 7.0 |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | More than 20m | 20 $\degree$C to 23 $\degree$C  | Above 7.8 |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | More than 50m | Above 30 $\degree$C | 7.2 to 7.5 |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Less than 50m | 23 $\degree$C to 29 $\degree$C | Above 7.8 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because the conditions it describes are ideal for coral reef formation. Water depth less than 50 m allows sufficient sunlight to reach the reef, which is essential for photosynthesis by zooxanthellae, the symbiotic algae within coral tissues. A temperature range of 23 $\degree$C to 29 $\degree$C is ideal for coral growth and the activity of zooxanthellae. A pH above 7.8 is necessary to maintain alkaline conditions, which favour the deposition of calcium carbonate ($\ce{CaCO_3}$) for coral skeleton formation.

**Choice A** is incorrect because although the temperature range is suitable, the pH is too low. A pH of 7- 7.8 is not sufficient for coral calcification; values above 7.8 are required. The depth range is acceptable but narrower than necessary, as corals can thrive down to 50 m.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the temperature range is too low. Coral reefs require warmer water, typically 23 $\degree$C to 29 $\degree$C, for optimal growth. The depth of ‘more than 20 m’ is also unsuitable because, at greater than 50m, the light levels become too low.

**Choice C** is incorrect because all three factors fall outside the optimal range. A depth greater than 50 m limits light penetration, temperatures above 30 $\degree$C can lead to coral bleaching, and a pH between 7.2 and 7.5 is too acidic for calcium carbonate deposition.
