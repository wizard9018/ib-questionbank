# BI2774

Which adaptation is found in both grass species adapted to sand dunes and tree species found in mangroves?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Rolled leaves reduce transpiration.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Adaptations to increase water uptake by the roots.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Pneumatophores that grow vertically into the air.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Underground stems that grow deep into the sand or soil.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because both grass species in sand dunes and mangrove tree species have root adaptations that enhance water uptake in challenging conditions. These adaptations often involve the accumulation of solutes in root cells to increase osmotic potential, enabling more efficient water absorption despite low water availability in sandy, salty soils or high salinity in mangrove environments.\
**Choice A** is incorrect because rolled leaves are a xerophytic adaptation found in many sand dune grasses to reduce water loss through transpiration. However, mangrove trees typically have broad, flat leaves and use salt excretion or storage mechanisms, not rolled leaves, to manage water loss.\
**Choice C** is incorrect because pneumatophores are specialised aerial roots found in mangrove trees to facilitate gas exchange in anoxic, waterlogged soils. Sand dune grasses do not develop pneumatophores, as they grow in well-aerated sandy soils.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because rhizomes (horizontal underground stems) are found in some sand dune grasses to anchor the plant and access deep water. However, mangrove trees do not rely on rhizomes; they have other root adaptations like prop roots or pneumatophores to stabilise and function in muddy substrates.
