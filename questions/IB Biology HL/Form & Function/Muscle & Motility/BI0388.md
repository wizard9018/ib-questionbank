# BI0388

**Maximum mark: 6**

1. Outline how antagonistic muscles move joints, giving an example. [3 marks]

2. Describe how skeletal muscle fibres are adapted for their function.  [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ (antagonistic muscles) move bones in opposite directions **OR** work in pairs [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when one muscle contracts, the other relaxes[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ antagonistic muscle pair has to be around the same joint **OR** must be moving the same bone [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ biceps and triceps in the upper arm, **OR** hamstring and quadriceps, (**OR** any other valid example), are examples of antagonistic muscles     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when the biceps contracts the triceps relaxes and the lower arm is pulled upwards **OR** any other valid example of antagonistic muscle movement         [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Antagonistic muscles are muscles that move bones in **opposite directions** ($\checkmark$). When one muscle **contracts, the other muscle relaxes** ($\checkmark$). The contraction and relaxation of antagonistic muscles move the **same bone in joints** ($\checkmark$). The **biceps and triceps** in the upper arm are an example of antagonistic muscle pairs ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  long (multinucleated) fibres formed from fusin of cells, which support shortening and lengthening [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ many mitochondria to supply energy for the contraction [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcoplasmic reticulum stores calcium ions required for muscle contraction [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ thin and thick filaments, **OR** actin and myosin, slide past each other to shorten the sarcomere     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ T-tubules **OR** invaginations of sarcolemma allow action potential to be carried into the centre of muscle fibre [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ troponin and tropomyosin help control contraction [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Skeletal muscles have many adaptations that allow them to contract and relax to move bones around the joints. Muscle fibres are **long multinucleated cells** consisting of myofibrils. Myofibrils can **shorten or lengthen** ($\checkmark$) due to the presence of sarcomeres. Sarcomeres contain **myosin and actin** filaments that can **slide past each other** ($\checkmark$), causing the muscle fibre to shorten. This sliding action is dependent on the presence of ATP and Ca:sup[2+] ions. Muscle fibres contain a lot of **mitochondria** to supply **ATP** ($\checkmark$). **Ca:sup[2+] ions are stored** until they are needed in a specially adapted **sarcoplasmic reticulum** ($\checkmark$) inside the cell.
