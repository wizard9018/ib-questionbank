# BI2416

**Maximum mark: 9**

Premature infants are often treated with caffeine to prevent episodes of apnea, a condition characterised by a temporary cessation of breathing. Although the exact mechanism of action is not fully understood, scientists have observed that treatment with caffeine leads to increased intracellular calcium levels in the muscle cells of the diaphragm.

2. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ more calcium binding to troponin to allow actin-myosin connections increases diaphragm muscle contraction [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ increased calcium increases the amount of neurotransmitter **OR** acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 More calcium in the diaphragm cells means more calcium is available to **bind troponin, which moves tropomyosin away from myosin binding sites on the actin filament, allowing more contractions** ($\checkmark$) and better breathing.

3. any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ no **OR** reduced saltatory conduction **OR** action potential cannot ‘jump' from node of Ranvier to node of Ranvier **OR** transmission of action potential slowed, due to damage **OR** no myelin sheath [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ damage to myelin sheath of motor neurons that innervate intercostal muscles **OR** diaphragm could lead to reduced coordination of contraction [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ lesions in the brainstem **OR** medulla oblongata **OR** pons, impair the respiratory centres that regulate rhythm **OR** depth of involuntary breathing [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ lesions in primary motor cortex **OR** cerebral hemispheres, disrupt voluntary control of breathing muscles [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ damage to myelin of sensory neurons could interfere with the detection of blood gas levels **OR** blood pH **OR** blood carbon dioxide concentration which would disrupt feedback to respiratory centres [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The **damage** to the **myelin sheath** around the neurons would **disrupt saltatory conduction**, where **action potentials** normally **"jump"** between **nodes of Ranvier** ($\checkmark$). This would **slow neural signalling**, impairing **motor neuron** function required for **controlling** the **intercostal muscles** and **diaphragm** ($\checkmark$). The same applies to the **sensory neurons** involved in the **detection of blood pH** or **carbon dioxide levels**, so there would be **limited feedback** to the **respiratory centres** in the medulla oblongata ($\checkmark$). Lesions in the **medulla oblongata** would also disrupt the **respiratory centres** that regulate the **rhythm** and **rate** of **involuntary breathing**, which could lead to irregular breathing patterns or **apnea** ($\checkmark$).

4. any **four** of the following *(ignore references to the flow of impulses beyond the sensory neuron, in a reflex arc*

 $\ce{ -> }$ nociceptors **OR** pain receptors sense pain from **OR** harmful temperature [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ high temperatures cause specific ion channels **OR** channel proteins **OR** transient receptor protein channels **OR** TRPs to open [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ channels found in skin epidermal layer [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ positively charged ions **OR** sodium ions **OR** $\ce{Na^+}$ **OR** calcium ions **OR** $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$, enter the neuron through the open channels [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ entry of positively charged ions reduces the negative charge inside the neuron leading to depolarisation of the membrane potential [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ if the depolarisation reaches threshold potential **OR** -55 mV **OR** other suitable value, voltage-gated sodium channels open [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ rapid influx of sodium ions **OR** $\ce{Na^+}$, leads to spike in membrane potential **OR** membrane potential of +30 mV **OR** other suitable value [1 mark]
 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Nociceptors** are a type of sensory receptor that senses pain ($\checkmark$). High temperatures cause specific **ion channels**, known as **transient receptor protein channels**, to **open** in the free nerve endings of sensory neurons ($\checkmark$). **Sodium ions** **enter** the **neuron** ($\checkmark$), causing **depolarisation** of the **membrane potential** ($\checkmark$). If the depolarisation **reaches** the **threshold potential**, **voltage-gated sodium channels open** ($\checkmark$), and an action potential is generated.
