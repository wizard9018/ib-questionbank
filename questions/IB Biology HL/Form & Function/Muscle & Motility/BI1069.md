# BI1069

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Distinguish between a myofibril, the sarcoplasmic reticulum and the sarcolemma. [3 marks]

2. Outline the different proteins and their position in the sarcomere when the muscle is relaxed.    [4 marks]

3. Describe the process of muscle contraction.        [9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ myofibrils are the basic unit inside the muscle fibres **OR** they are composed of  end-to-end sarcomeres **OR** they are long contractile fibres **OR** they are bundles of protein filaments, mainly actin and myosin        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcolemma is the plasma membrane of the muscle fibre **OR** cell, **OR** it envelops the muscle fibre        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcoplasmic reticulum is a specialised endoplasmic reticulum that stores calcium, **OR** releases calcium ions when stimulated by a nerve impulse **OR** action potential        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Myofibrils, sarcolemma and sarcoplasmic reticulum are all found in muscle fibres. **Myofibrils** are the basic units of muscle fibres ($\checkmark$); they are made of **repeating sarcomeres** laid end-to-end and are responsible for muscle contraction. The **sarcolemma** is the membrane that surrounds each **muscle fibre**, similar to the **plasma membrane** ($\checkmark$) in any other cell. The **sarcoplasmic** reticulum is a **specialised endoplasmic reticulum**. When a nerve impulse reaches the muscle fibre, depolarisation spreads to the sarcoplasmic reticulum. This activates voltage-sensitive calcium channels in the sarcoplasmic reticulum causing them to **release calcium ions** ($\checkmark$) from the sarcoplasmic reticulum storage. This initiates the process of muscle contraction.

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ five types of protein involved in muscle contraction: titin, myosin, actin, troponin and tropomyosin    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tropomyosin binds to **OR** wraps around, actin, covering binding sites        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ actin, troponin and tropomyosin make up thin filaments            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin makes up majority of  thick filaments        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ titin holds myosin fibres in place in sarcomere     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ titin is an immense protein        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    There are **five types of protein** involved in muscle contraction: **titin, troponin, tropomyosin, myosin and actin** ($\checkmark$). They all together make up a sarcomere unit that is part of a muscle fibre. **Actin**, troponin and tropomyosin make up the **thin filaments** ($\checkmark$) and **myosin** makes up the **thick filaments** ($\checkmark$) in the sarcomere. **Titin** is an immense protein that **holds** the **myosin fibres** in place ($\checkmark$) in the sarcomere.

    **Tropomyosin is bound to the actin** and troponin ($\checkmark$), and when the muscle is relaxed, troponin covers myosin binding sites on actin.

3. any **nine** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcomeres are the repeating functional units of myofibrils        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcomeres can shorten    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a nerve impulse reaches the myofibril bringing about an action potential in the sarcolemma **OR** action potential passes down T-tubules of sarcolemma    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions bind to troponin     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium attachment to troponin causes a movement of tropomyosin exposing the binding sites on actin    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads attach to the binding sites on actin **OR** cross-bridges form between actin and myosin        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads release ADP and rotate **OR** carry out power stroke, pulling the actin filament inwards    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP attaches to the myosin head and breaks the cross-bridge        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis of ATP moves the myosin head into the cocked **OR** ready position    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ repeat of the process shortens the sarcomere        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the Z lines are pulled closer together        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shortening of the sarcomeres in a myofibril causes muscle contraction    [1 mark]
    [9 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The structure of sarcomeres allows them to **shorten, which causes muscle contraction** ($\checkmark$). When a nerve impulse reaches the sarcoplasmic reticulum of myofibril, **calcium ions** are released ($\checkmark$). **Calcium ions bind** to a protein called **troponin** in the sarcomere ($\checkmark$). **Troponin moves** another protein called **tropomyosin** away from the binding sites on actin ($\checkmark$). **Myosin heads** are in the **cocked position** and contain stored energy from **ATP hydrolysis** ($\checkmark$). They bind to the **binding sites on actin** to form a **cross bridge** ($\checkmark$). The stored energy and **ADP are released** ($\checkmark$). When they are released, the **myosin heads move inwards** (“power stroke”) and pull on the actin ($\checkmark$). **ATP attaches** to myosin heads and breaks the cross-bridge ($\checkmark$). **Hydrolysis of ATP moves the myosin heads into the cocked position** ($\checkmark$). This process repeats until the calcium ions are no longer available. **Repeating** this process **shortens the sarcomeres** ($\checkmark$) in a myofibril and causes **muscle contraction** ($\checkmark$).
