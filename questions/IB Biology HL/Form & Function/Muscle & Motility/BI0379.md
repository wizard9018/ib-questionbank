# BI0379

Which feature allows insects, such as crickets (*Gryllus bimaculatus*), to jump very high?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Strong tendons

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Strong exoskeleton

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Synovial joints

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Antagonistic muscles

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Antagonistic muscles work in pairs, with one muscle contracting while the other
relaxes. In insects, such as crickets, these muscles, particularly in their hind legs, are specialised for powerful and rapid contractions. This coordinated action of antagonistic muscles provides the force necessary for the rapid extension of the legs during a jump, propelling the insect into the air. **Choice D** is the correct answer to the question.

Tendons are fibrous tissues that connect muscles to bones, providing the mechanism for movement. While tendons do play a role in transmitting force from muscles to the exoskeleton, their strength alone isn't the primary reason for high jumps in insects. **Choice A** is not the correct answer to this question.

The exoskeleton of insects provides structural support and protection. While a strong exoskeleton is necessary for withstanding the stresses of jumping, it alone doesn't explain the high jumping ability. The exoskeleton serves as a rigid framework against which muscles can act, but it's not the main contributor to the jumping force. **Choice B** is not the correct answer to this question.

Synovial joints are characterised by the presence of a fluid-filled joint cavity, allowing for smooth movement between bones. Insects do not have synovial joints, instead insects have joints called ‘arthrodial joints' or ‘articulations'. **Choice C** is not the correct answer to this question.
