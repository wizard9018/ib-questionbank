# BI2916

During deep dives, dolphins often encounter lower oxygen levels. Which of the following adaptations best helps them maintain swimming efficiency under low-oxygen conditions?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Myoglobin's ability to store oxygen in muscles.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased blood volume to carry more oxygen.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The ability to slow metabolic rate and conserve oxygen.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Modification of lung tissue to increase oxygen absorption.

*Note: Starting May 2028, this question may be assessed differently due to changes to the Biology guide, including the use of a data booklet.*

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct.  Dolphins have high concentrations of myoglobin in their muscle tissue. Myoglobin is a protein that binds oxygen and stores it directly within the muscles, allowing dolphins to maintain muscle function during long dives without access to atmospheric oxygen. Unlike haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in the blood, myoglobin has a higher affinity for oxygen, meaning it holds onto oxygen more effectively and can supply it to muscles during periods of breath-holding.
This adaptation directly enables dolphins to maintain swimming efficiency during extended periods of submersion in low-oxygen conditions.

**Choice B** is incorrect because, while blood volume is important for oxygen transport, dolphins do not adapt by increasing blood volume to dive efficiently; they rely on the capacity to store oxygen in myoglobin.

**Choice C** is incorrect because, while dolphins can slow their metabolic rate during a dive to conserve oxygen, this is not the primary adaptation that enables them to maintain swimming efficiency in low-oxygen conditions. Slowing metabolism helps reduce overall oxygen consumption, but doesn't allow dolphins to continue swimming efficiently. The key adaptation for maintaining swimming efficiency during long dives is myoglobin, which stores oxygen directly in muscles.

**Choice D** is incorrect because there is no significant modification to lung tissue that allows for increased oxygen absorption to an extent that would support long dives. While lungs are essential for initial oxygen intake, they are not a key adaptation for swimming efficiency during extended periods underwater, especially under low-oxygen conditions. The primary adaptation for oxygen storage and efficient swimming during dives is myoglobin in the muscles, not modifications to lung tissue.
