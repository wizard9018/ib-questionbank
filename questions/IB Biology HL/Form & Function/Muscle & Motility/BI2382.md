# BI2382

The extinct early animal genus *Dickinsonia* included large, flat organisms that fed on microbial mats on the sea bed.

Some *Dickinsonia* fossils , like the one shown above, exhibit single imprints, suggesting these organisms were sessile or had limited mobility. However, other fossils show multiple overlapping trace imprints, indicating that they were motile. No discernible sense organs have been found in the *Dickinsonia* genus.
\
Which of the following was an evolutionary advantage that motile *Dickinsonia* would have had over sessile ones?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ The ability to specialise in different food types.
- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The ability to detect and respond to predators.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The ability to increase the efficiency of nutrient absorption.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The ability to access more food by moving across the substrate.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because movement across the substrate would allow *Dickinsonia* to access new areas of microbial mats once local food sources were depleted. This provides a clear evolutionary advantage over sessile organisms, which remain fixed in one location and cannot reach additional resources.

**Choice A** is incorrect because there is no evidence that motility leads to specialisation in different food types. Both motile and sessile *Dickinsonia* are thought to have fed on the same type of resource, namely microbial mats, so movement would not change diet.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the question stem states that no discernible sense organs have been found in *Dickinsonia*. Detecting and responding to predators requires sensory structures, so this advantage is not supported by the available evidence.

**Choice C** is incorrect because nutrient absorption efficiency depends on physiological factors such as surface area and transport mechanisms, rather than on movement. Being motile would not directly increase the efficiency of nutrient absorption.
