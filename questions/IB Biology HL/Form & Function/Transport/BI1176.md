# BI1176

A rose bush and radioactively labelled carbon dioxide (:sup[14]CO:sub[2]) were used in an experiment to investigate the time taken for sap to reach different groups of aphids located in different areas of the plant. The results are shown in the table below.

| Aphid Colony | Time taken /min |
| :-: | :-: |
| X | 9.2 |
| Y | 8.0 |
| Z | 11.1 |

What could be concluded from these results?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Colonies X, Y and Z are equally spaced along the stem of the rose bush.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Colony Y was located closest to the leaves.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Colonies X, Y and Z all received an equal volume of sap.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Colony Y fed on the sap more quickly than the other colonies.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Carbon dioxide enters the leaves, and is fixed during the light-independent stage of photosynthesis to form organic compounds. These organic compounds can then be loaded into phloem in the leaves (source) to be transported to areas of the plant which can't produce their own (sinks).

The time taken for radioactive carbon to be found in samples taken from the stylets of aphids was lowest in colony Y. This is because Colony Y was the closest colony to the leaves, which is where the radioactively labelled organic compounds were synthesised. **Choice B** is correct.

**Choices A, C** and **D** are not valid conclusions that could be drawn from these results.
