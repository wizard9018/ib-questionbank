# BI2818

Which structural feature of arteries allows them to withstand the pressure generated by ventricular systole?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Thin tunica media

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Elastic fibres in the tunica intima

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Smooth muscle in the tunica externa

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Thick tunica media with elastic fibres

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The tunica media is the middle layer of the artery wall and contains both smooth muscle and elastic fibres. During ventricular systole (when the heart contracts), blood is ejected into the arteries under high pressure.  A thick tunica media allows the artery to stretch (due to elastic fibres) and resist rupture (due to smooth muscle), helping to maintain the integrity of the vessel and buffer pressure surges. Additionally, elastic fibres enable recoil after stretching, ensuring consistent blood flow.  **Choice D** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because this is the opposite of what allows arteries to withstand high pressure. A thin tunica media would lack the structural strength and elasticity needed to resist the force of blood ejected from the heart.

**Choice B** is incorrect because although the tunica intima contains some elastic fibres, they are minimal compared to the tunica media, which plays the dominant role in stretch and recoil. The tunica intima primarily consists of an endothelial layer that reduces friction for smooth blood flow rather than providing strength or pressure resistance.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the tunica externa (also called the adventitia) is primarily composed of collagen fibres, which provide structural support but do not actively regulate pressure. The smooth muscle responsible for vasoconstriction and withstanding pressure is located in the tunica media.
