# BI1588

How do companion cells support phloem sieve tubes in plants?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ They have no nucleus and reduced cytoplasm to aid in sucrose transport.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ They supply ATP for active transport.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ They maintain a higher sucrose concentration than the sieve tubes, allowing for more efficient loading at the source.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ They support hydrostatic flow by exchanging water with xylem vessels by osmosis.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because sieve tube elements lack a nucleus and most organelles. As a result, they require assistance from the companion cells in the form of ATP and proteins delivered via plasmodesmata. ATP is needed for active transport to load sucrose into the sieve tubes from the source or to unload at the sink. The proteins include structural proteins for the maintenance of the sieve tube and enzymes needed for biochemical processes within the sieve tubes.

**Choice A** is incorrect because it is the sieve tube elements rather than the companion cells that have no nucleus. **Choice C** is incorrect because the companion cells have a lower sucrose concentration than the phloem sieve tubes, requiring active transport to move sap from source to sink. **Choice D** is incorrect because osmosis does not occur between the xylem and companion cells. Instead, water is exchanged between the xylem and sieve tubes.
