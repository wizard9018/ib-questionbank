# BI0346

Scientists can use aphids and radioactive carbon dioxide (:sup[14]CO$_2$) to measure the rate of sucrose transport in the phloem of plants. The aphids tap into the phloem and collect sap, allowing measurement of the time taken for radioactive sucrose to reach different parts of the plant.

Which factor could lead to a decrease in the time taken for radioactive carbon to be detected in samples of sap taken from the stylets of aphids, assuming all other factors remain constant?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Increasing the light intensity in the growing environment

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Decreasing the light intensity in the growing environment

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Decreasing the number of leaves on the plant

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Decreasing the amount of water in the growing medium of the plant

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Sources are the parts of the plant, such as leaves, which are capable of either producing or mobilising their own organic compounds through processes such as photosynthesis. These organic compounds, such as sucrose, can then be loaded into phloem to be transported to areas of the plant which can't produce their own (sinks).     Therefore, any factor which is likely to increase the rate of photosynthesis will increase the rate at which organic compounds such as sucrose are synthesised and transported around the plant. If we relate this to the experiment, an increase in the light intensity will lead to an increase in the rate of production of sucrose molecules containing radioactively labelled carbon, and a decrease in the amount of time taken before they are detected in the sap samples taken from the stylets of aphids.

**Choices B, C** and **D** would lead to a decrease in the rate of photosynthesis and therefore an increase in the time taken for radioactive carbon to be detected in samples.
