# BI1200

How is tissue fluid formed?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Plasma leaking out of capillaries due to hydrostatic pressure

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Plasma entering capillaries due to high pressure in the venules

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Plasma leaving the arteries under high pressure and entering cells

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Plasma leaking out of veins due to their high pressure

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because tissue fluid is formed by hydrostatic pressure, forcing plasma out of the capillaries so that it surrounds cells in the tissues of the body.

​​It is osmotic pressure that causes the tissue fluid to re-enter the capillaries at the venous end, not hydrostatic pressure.  Also, this is the way tissue fluid is taken back up, not formed; **Choice B** is, therefore, incorrect. Tissue fluid cannot pass out of arteries or veins as their walls are too thick and do not contain pores, therefore **Choices C** and **D** are incorrect.
