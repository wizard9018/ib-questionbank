# BI2817

Which of the following methods would most reduce uncertainty when measuring a resting heart rate using the radial pulse?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Use of thumb to feel for the pulse over 10 seconds

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Use of fingertips to measure for 30 seconds and multiply by 2

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Use of fingertips to measure for 60 seconds

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Use of stopwatch and estimation over 15 seconds

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Measuring the pulse with fingertips (not the thumb) for a full 60 seconds is the most accurate method for determining resting heart rate. This approach avoids the need to multiply or estimate, reducing uncertainty, and ensures that natural fluctuations in heart rhythm are accounted for. **Choice C** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the thumb has its own pulse, which can interfere with detecting the subject’s pulse accurately. Additionally, measuring for just 10 seconds increases uncertainty since small timing errors lead to a larger percentage error when extrapolated over a full minute.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because although using fingertips is good, multiplying a 30-second count by 2 increases uncertainty, especially if the pulse is irregular. Conditions such as sinus arrhythmia can cause beat-to-beat variations, making short-period extrapolations less reliable. Measuring for the full 60 seconds eliminates this issue.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because measuring over only 15 seconds significantly magnifies errors. A missed or extra beat during this brief window results in a fourfold discrepancy when multiplied, making it a much less reliable approach.
