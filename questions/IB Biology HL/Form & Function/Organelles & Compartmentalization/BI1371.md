## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **two** of the following:

    *Structure X*:\
    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicle **OR** secretory vesicle [1 mark]

    *Structure Y*:\
    $\ce{ -> }$ nucleus                [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Structure X is a **vesicle** ($\checkmark$). Structure Y is the **nucleus** ($\checkmark$).

2.
    1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ secretory proteins are synthesised by ribosomes [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ these ribosomes are attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ proteins enter the RER lumen, where they are folded and may be modified [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ transport vesicles containing the proteins bud off from the RER [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ these vesicles carry the proteins to the Golgi apparatus [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ in the Golgi apparatus, proteins undergo further processing **OR** are packaged into secretory vesicles, such as X [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ secretory vesicles transport the proteins towards the plasma membrane [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Secretory proteins are produced by the **ribosomes** ($\checkmark$) on the **rough endoplasmic reticulum** ($\checkmark$). **Vesicles** are **formed** from the membranes of the endoplasmic reticulum and are used to **transport these proteins** ($\checkmark$)  to the Golgi apparatus ($\checkmark$). At the **Golgi apparatus**, the proteins are **modified and repackaged into secretory vesicles** ($\checkmark$) before finally reaching the **plasma membrane** ($\checkmark$).

2.
    2. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ active transport **OR** exocytosis                [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Proteins are released from vesicles using a form of **active transport** ($\checkmark$) known as **exocytosis** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows organelles to have specialised functions            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ such as the enzymatic digestion of substances in lysosomes **OR** the ingestion of foreign particles into phagocytic vacuoles **OR** any other named example of a compartmentalised organelle and its specialised function            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows metabolic processes to be isolated from each other    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ improves efficiency **OR** prevents incompatible reactions from occurring in the same part of the cell **OR** allows different cellular processes to occur simultaneously without interfering with each other **OR** ensures that energy production is focused and efficient, preventing wasteful reactions in other parts of the cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows the maintenance of concentration gradients **OR** internal conditions that differ from that of the cytoplasm **OR** create distinct microenvironments with specific pH levels **OR** ion concentrations **OR** chemical conditions crucial for particular biochemical reactions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows the cell to store **OR** control the distribution of substances such as enzymes and metabolites **OR** the products of metabolism **OR** nutrients **OR** ions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ protects cellular components from potentially damaging substances **OR** isolates toxic substances    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows sorting **OR** modification of molecules            [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Cell compartmentalisation allows **organelles** to have **specialised functions** ($\checkmark$), such as the **digestion of wastes in lysosomes** ($\checkmark$). The use of membranes also allows **metabolic processes** to be **isolated** from each other ($\checkmark$) in order to **improve efficiency** and **prevent incompatible reactions** ($\checkmark$) from occurring simultaneously.
