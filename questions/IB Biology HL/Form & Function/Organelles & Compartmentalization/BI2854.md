# BI2854

Which statement best explains a functional advantage eukaryotic cells gain from separating transcription and translation?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ It prevents mRNA from being translated before it is transcribed.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ It increases the speed of protein synthesis by separating the processes spatially.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ It ensures ribosomes are only assembled in the nucleus before being exported.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ It allows time for extensive post-transcriptional modification of mRNA before translation.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct. In eukaryotic cells, transcription occurs in the nucleus and translation in the cytoplasm. This spatial separation allows time for the mRNA transcript to undergo post-transcriptional modifications (such as 5' capping, splicing, and polyadenylation) before it interacts with ribosomes in the cytoplasm.

**Choice A** is incorrect because transcription must occur before translation, regardless of compartmentalisation; the issue is not preventing premature translation of incomplete mRNA but enabling modification.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the separation actually slows the onset of translation compared to prokaryotes, but enables higher fidelity and regulation.

**Choice C** is incorrect because ribosomes are assembled in the nucleolus but function in the cytoplasm. This does not directly relate to mRNA processing.
