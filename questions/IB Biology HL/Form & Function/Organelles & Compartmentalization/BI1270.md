# BI1270

**Maximum mark: 16**

Organelles are adapted for their functions.

1. Outline the structure and function of vesicles.    [4 marks]

2. Compare and contrast the structure and function of the rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus.    [5 marks]

3. Explain how the membranes of the mitochondria, chloroplasts and the nucleus are adapted for their functions.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles are membrane-bound organelles    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles transport substances within cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles store substances    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles are used in exocytosis **OR** release of neurotransmitters **OR** any other suitable example of exocytosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles are used in endocytosis **OR** phagocytosis **OR** pinocytosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lysosomes are vesicles that contain digestive enzymes **OR** lysosomes digest material taken in by endocytosis **OR** phagocytosis **OR** lysosomes break down damaged cell organelles    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles are formed by the endoplasmic reticulum **OR** the Golgi apparatus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the vesicle membrane fuses with the plasma membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles are moved along microtubules by motor proteins    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ secretory vesicles transport substances to be released from the cell **OR** secretory vesicles transport substances such as hormones to the cell membrane to be released    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ clathrin is a protein that is important for vesicle formation **OR** clathrin coats the membrane and helps it bud inwards to form vesicles in endocytosis    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Vesicles are **membrane bound organelles** ($\checkmark$). A protein called **clathrin has a role in the formation of vesicles** ($\checkmark$). Vesicles **transport and store substances** ($\checkmark$). Secretory vesicles **transport substances for release from the cell** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to gain full marks*):

    ***Similarities:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ the rough endoplasmic reticulum **AND** the Golgi apparatus are made of flattened membrane sacs **OR** **BOTH** the rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus are membrane-bound organelles    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** the rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus are involved in modification **OR** processing of proteins **OR** post-translational modification    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are involved in the transport of proteins after translation    [1 mark]

    ***Differences:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ the Golgi apparatus membrane sacs are not interconnected **BUT** the rough endoplasmic reticulum is interconnected    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosomes are attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum **BUT** ribosomes do not attach to the Golgi apparatus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rough endoplasmic reticulum processes polypeptides formed by the ribosomes **WHEREAS** the Golgi apparatus processes proteins received from the rough endoplasmic reticulum    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the Golgi apparatus modifies (e.g. glycosylates), sorts and packages proteins into vesicles for secretion    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the rough endoplasmic reticulum synthesises proteins for secretion **OR** for membranes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the membrane of the rough endoplasmic reticulum is continuous with the nuclear envelope **BUT** the Golgi apparatus is not connected to the nuclear envelope    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Both** the rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus are made of **flattened membrane sacs** ($\checkmark$). The rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus **both** have a **role in processing proteins** ($\checkmark$) in cells, but the **rough endoplasmic reticulum processes polypeptides produced by the ribosomes**, whereas the **Golgi apparatus processes proteins** received **from** the **rough endoplasmic reticulum** ($\checkmark$). The **rough endoplasmic reticulum** has **ribosomes** attached and its membrane is **continuous** with the **nuclear envelope**, whereas the **Golgi apparatus** is **not associated with ribosomes** ($\checkmark$) and is **not connected to** the **nuclear envelope** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** mark point for each of the organelles to gain full marks*):

    ***Mitochondria:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ membrane infoldings of mitochondria provide a large surface area    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electron transport chain proteins are located on the inner membrane (cristae) of mitochondria    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inner membrane contains ATP synthases for ATP production    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ small intermembrane space between the inner and outer membranes allows a proton gradient to be established quickly    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inner membrane of mitochondria is impermeable to most ions allowing for a proton gradient to be established    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ outer membrane of mitochondria separates the contents of the mitochondria from the rest of the cell    [1 mark]

    ***Chloroplasts:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ thylakoid membranes give a large surface area in chloroplasts **OR** give a large surface area for the absorption of light    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ membranes of thylakoids in chloroplasts contain photosystems for converting light energy to chemical energy    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electron transport chain proteins in the thylakoid membranes pump protons into the thylakoid space, establishing a proton gradient    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP synthase in the thylakoid membranes produces ATP by chemiosmosis **OR** NADP is reduced to NADPH at the end of the electron transport chain, for use in the light-independent reactions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the small volume of fluid inside the thylakoids (thylakoid space) allows a proton gradient to be established quickly    [1 mark]

    ***Nuclear membrane:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ nuclear pores in the nuclear membrane allow substances to pass into and out of the nucleus **OR** nuclear pores allow mRNA to exit the nucleus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the double membrane separates transcription from translation, allowing mRNA to be modified before translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the nuclear membrane breaks down into vesicles during mitosis **OR** meiosis **AND** the vesicles fuse to reform the nuclear envelope afterwards    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The membranes of **cristae** in mitochondria give a **large surface area** for **electron transport chains** ($\checkmark$), the **small intermembrane** space allows **concentration gradients** to be **established** ($\checkmark$), and the membranes of the cristae **contain ATP synthases** for the **production** of **ATP** ($\checkmark$). The **nuclear membrane** is a double membrane that has **pores** for the **movement** of **substances into and out** of the **nucleus** ($\checkmark$) and the membrane **breaks down into vesicles** during **mitosis** and **meiosis**, which **later fuse** to **reform** the nuclear **envelope** ($\checkmark$). The membranes of the **thylakoids** in chloroplasts give a **large surface area** for **absorption of light** ($\checkmark$) and the **small volume** of the **thylakoid space** allows a **proton gradient** to be **established** ($\checkmark$).
