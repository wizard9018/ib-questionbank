# BI0337

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast the structure and function of free and bound ribosomes. [3 marks]

2. Describe the steps in the initiation of translation. [5 marks]

3. Explain how the bonds between amino acids contribute to the structure of proteins. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following (answer can be in a table format):

    $\ce{->}$ **BOTH** free and bound ribosomes synthesise proteins [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ **BOTH** free and bound ribosomes consist of small and big subunits, **OR** both have A P E and mRNA binding sites [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ free ribosomes are found in the cytoplasm **WHILE** bound ribosomes are attached to the endoplasmic reticulum **OR** ER [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ free ribosomes synthesise proteins for use within the cell unattached to any structures, **BUT** bound ribosomes synthesise proteins for secretion **OR** use in the lysosomes **OR** membrane-bound proteins [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    Both free and bound ribosomes **synthesise proteins** ($\checkmark$). However, free ribosomes are found floating in the **cytoplasm** , and bound ribosomes are attached to the **endoplasmic reticulum** ($\checkmark$). Free ribosomes synthesise proteins for use **within the cell** , but bound ribosomes synthesise proteins for **secretion** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$ mRNA molecule binds to the small ribosomal subunit [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ initiator tRNA (carrying methionine) binds to the start codon [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ mRNA/tRNA **OR** codon/anticodon binding is facilitated by complementary base pairing [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ large ribosomal subunit binds to the initiator tRNA at the P site [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ amino acid carrying tRNA attaches to the next codon at the A site [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ peptide bond is formed between amino acids in the P and A site [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ ribosome moves a codon space on the mRNA, so that tRNA at start codon is now at E site [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    Translation occurs in the cytoplasm. During the initiation stage, the mRNA molecule binds to the **small ribosomal subunit** ($\checkmark$). Next, the **initiator tRNA** binds to the start codon ($\checkmark$) on the mRNA, and finally, the **large ribosomal subunit** binds to the tRNA through the **P site** ($\checkmark$) in the ribosome and then the next codon signals another tRNA to bind in the **A site** ($\checkmark$), using complementary base pairing.  A **peptide bond** ($\checkmark$) is formed between the amino acids carried by tRNAs in the P and A sites.

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$ primary structure of a protein is the sequence of amino acids [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ peptide bonds link amino acids in the polypeptide chain [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ secondary structure is the formation of alpha helices **OR** beta pleated sheets within the same polypeptide [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (secondary structure is) the result of hydrogen bonding **OR** hydrogen bonds form between the carboxyl and amino group **OR** C=O and N-H [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ tertiary structure is caused by interactions between R groups of amino acids [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (in tertiary structure) positively charged R groups interact with negative charged R groups [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (in tertiary structure) polar R groups will form hydrogen bonds with other polar R groups [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (in tertiary structure) disulphide, **OR** S-S, bridges can form between cysteines in R groups [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (tertiary structure) gives the polypeptide its 3D shape [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ quaternary structure is formed in proteins with more than one polypeptide chain [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (quaternary structure is) formed by the interactions between R groups on different polypeptide chains [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$ (quaternary structure is) supported by S-S bridges, **OR** any other examples of R group bounds in the previous marking statements  [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    The primary structure of a protein is the **sequence** of amino acids ($\checkmark$), which are linked together with **peptide bonds** ($\checkmark$). The secondary structure of a protein is the formation of **alpha-helices** ($\checkmark$), which are stabilised by **hydrogen bonding** between the carboxyl and amino groups ($\checkmark$) from peptide bonds between different amino acids in the same chain. The tertiary structure is caused by interactions between **R groups** ($\checkmark$), which gives the polypeptide its **3D shape** ($\checkmark$). Some of the bonds formed between R groups include **polar R groups**  forming hydrogen bonds with other polar R groups ($\checkmark$) and cysteines in R groups, forming **disulphide bridges** ($\checkmark$). The quaternary structure of proteins describes proteins with **more than one** polypeptide chain ($\checkmark$) in their structure. The quaternary structure is supported by the same type of R-R group interactions as the tertiary level, such as **disulphide bridges** ($\checkmark$).
