5. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ in prokaryotes, nucleoid and cytoplasm are not separated, **WHEREAS** in eukaryotes, nucleus and cytoplasm are separated [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in prokaryotes, no membrane-bound organelles are present in the cytoplasm, **WHEREAS** in eukaryotes, there are membrane-bound organelles [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The difference between prokaryotes and eukaryotes in terms of compartmentalisation is that in **prokaryotes**, the **nucleoid and cytoplasm** are **not separated**, whereas in **eukaryotes**, the **nucleus and cytoplasm** are **separated** ($\checkmark$).

6. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ compartmentalisation is the separation of cellular components into compartments **OR** regions by a membrane **OR** phagocytosis is a type of endocytosis process where materials **OR** pathogens are ingested **OR** engulfed **OR** taken into another living cell within a membrane [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ endocytosis of pathogen **OR** material requires formation of vacuole using plasma membrane [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ phagosome, **OR** vesicle **OR** phagocytic vacuole, isolates, **OR** prevents spread of, the ingested, **OR** engulfed, pathogen, **OR** materials, from the rest of the cell's interior by a membrane [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ membrane of lysosome isolates digestive enzymes, **OR** prevents digestion of cell **OR** self-digestion [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ membrane of lysosome allows for ideal conditions **OR** pH for enzymes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ phagolysosome, **OR** fusing of phagosome and lysosome, allows targeting of enzymes **OR** provides controlled, **OR** isolated, environment for digestion of pathogen, **OR** material [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows processing and presentation of antigens **OR** allows cell to present antigens on cell surface membrane [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows for management, **OR** elimination, of waste of phagocytosis [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Compartmentalisation is the **separation of cellular components into compartments** by either a single or double **membrane** layer ($\checkmark$). Compartmentalisation is important during phagocytosis because **pathogens are engulfed** by the formation of a **vacuole using the plasma membrane** ($\checkmark$). The **membrane** around the **phagolysosome**, formed from the fusion of the phagosome and lysosome, provides an **isolated environment** for the digestion of the pathogen and **prevents spread of enzymes** into the rest of the cell's interior ($\checkmark$).
