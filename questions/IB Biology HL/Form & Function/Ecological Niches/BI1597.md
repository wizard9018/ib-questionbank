# BI1597

**Maximum mark: 5**

 A university student decided to mimic the experiments conducted by the ecologist Joseph Connell to investigate the competition between two species of barnacles (*Sp. A* and *Sp. B*) living in intertidal zones. They specifically wanted to see if one species could outcompete the other for space. The diagram below shows where *Sp. A* and *Sp. B* live, *Sp. A* on the upper intertidal zone and *Sp. B* on the lower intertidal zone.

![BI1597](images/BI1597.png)

The student transferred 3 rocks containing *Sp. A* from the upper intertidal zone to the lower intertidal zone and fastened them down, as shown in the diagram on the left below. They then allowed *Sp. B* to colonise these rocks.

![Joseph Connell colonisation by barnacles BI1597i](<images/Joseph Connell colonisation by barnacles BI1597i.png>)

After the colonisation period was over, the student removed *Sp. B* with a needle from one half of each of the 3 rocks that had been fastened down and left the other half undisturbed, as shown in the right-hand diagram above.

The student then monitored the percentage mortality of *Sp. A* on each half of these rocks for a year; the results are shown in the table below.

![BI1597T](images/BI1597T.png)
[Data adapted from original data obtained by the scientist]

1. Deduce what effect *Sp. B* had on *Sp. A*. [1 mark]

2. Outline the realised niches for the two species of barnacles. [2 marks]

In a second experiment, during unusually warm weather, the survival of both species was monitored at the margin where they coexisted. When there was no water for several days, mature *Sp. B* experienced a 50% mortality rate, while mature *Sp. A* suffered only about 2%.

3. Using all the data, explain why both species can sometimes coexist in the same tidal zone. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **one** of the following :

 $\ce{ -> }$ *Sp. B* causes an increased mortality of *Sp. A* **OR** *Sp. B* has a negative impact on the survival of *Sp. A* [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ *Sp. B* outcompetes *Sp. A* in the lower intertidal zone [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 ***Sp. B*** caused an **increase** in the **death** of ***Sp. A***, as shown in the data ($\checkmark$).

2. **Two** of the following :

 $\ce{ -> }$ the realised niche for *Sp. A* is the upper intertidal zone [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the realised niche for *Sp. B* is the lower intertidal zone [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The **realised niche** for ***Sp. A*** is the **upper intertidal** zone ($\checkmark$), and for *Sp. B*, it is the **lower intertidal** zone ($\checkmark$).

3. any **two** of the following :

 $\ce{ -> }$ at the margins of the lower and upper intertidal zone, when there was a drought *Sp. A* showed lower mortality than *Sp. B* [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ *Sp. B* is not as resistant to desiccation as, **OR** requires more constant moisture than, *Sp. A* therefore can not survive on the upper rocks of the intertidal zone [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the intertidal zone environment varies allowing both species to exploit different microhabitats reducing direct competition and allowing coexistence [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ fluctuating environmental conditions **OR** changes in temperature **OR** water exposure can give one species a temporary advantage preventing one species from completely excluding the other [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Based on the second experiment, *Sp. A*'s **higher resilience** to **lack of water**, as evidenced by its significantly **lower mortality rate** during **dry conditions** ($\checkmark$), allows it to survive in the upper intertidal zone where exposure to air and the risk of desiccation are greater. In contrast, ***Sp. B's*** **higher mortality rate** in the **absence of water** indicates it **requires more constant moisture**, confining it to the **lower intertidal zone** ($\checkmark$), where it is more frequently submerged.
