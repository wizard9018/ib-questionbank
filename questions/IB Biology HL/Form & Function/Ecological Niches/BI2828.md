# BI2828

The data below shows the growth (in micrometres) of two protist species over 24 hours. Glucose was added to the medium *only* in the dark conditions.

![BI2828](images/BI2828.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X is a facultative mixotroph

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Species Y is an obligate mixotroph

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Species X is an obligate autotroph

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Species Y is a facultative autotroph

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct. Species X grows in both the light and in the dark with glucose. This suggests it can switch between autotrophic nutrition via photosynthesis in light and heterotrophic nutrition in the dark with glucose (using external organic carbon). Thus, Species X is a facultative mixotroph (it can switch between both forms of nutrition depending on the environment).

**Choice B** is incorrect. Species Y grows in the dark with glucose but shows no growth in the light when no glucose is present. This means it must be heterotrophic, relying entirely on external organic carbon, here in the form of glucose. Species Y is not a mixotroph since it doesn’t photosynthesise, so it does not use autotrophic nutrition.

**Choice C** is incorrect. Species X is not an obligate autotroph, because it also grows in the dark with glucose. An obligate autotroph would only grow in the light conditions.

**Choice D** is incorrect. Species Y shows growth in the dark with glucose, but it cannot grow in light, meaning it is heterotrophic and does not perform photosynthesis, so it cannot be called a facultative autotroph.
