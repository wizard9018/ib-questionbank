# BI1530

Which of the following is likely to result from competition between two species with the same ecological niche?

- I. $\hspace{1.6em}$ Both species may compromise their fundamental niche in order to arrive at different realised niches

- II. $\hspace{1.3em}$ The population size of the less competitive species may decrease

- III. $\hspace{1em}$ The less competitive species may rapidly evolve to outcompete the more competitive species

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

Competition between two species with the same ecological niche can result in competitive exclusion.

The principle of competitive exclusion states that no two species can occupy the same niche. If they share the same niche or resources at the same place and at the same time, then the species that is unable to compete successfully will die out (**Statement II**), or migrate, which may result in the two populations becoming geographically isolated or both species may compromise their fundamental niche, in order to arrive at different realised niches (**Statement I**). These outcomes would result in the two species not being in direct competition. **Choice C** is correct.

Evolution is often a gradual process, therefore the less competitive species will most likely **not** evolve in time to outcompete the more competitive species, **Statement III**  is therefore not likely to happen. **Choice A**  is incorrect as it only includes **Statement I**. **Choices B** and **D**  are incorrect as they both include **Statement III**.
