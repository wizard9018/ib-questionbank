    1. Using the diagram and your knowledge, describe one adaptation of the wolf that makes it an effective predator and one adaptation of the deer that helps it avoid predation.    [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. any **two** of the following (*maximum **one** from each section*):

        *Wolf adaptations:*\
        $\ce{ -> }$ Wolves have forward-facing eyes, which provide binocular vision for accurately judging distance when hunting prey [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ strong jaw and sharp canines allow efficient killing and consumption of prey     [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ long limbs and muscular build enable endurance and speed for chasing prey [1 mark]

        *Deer adaptations:*\
        $\ce{ -> }$ deer have eyes on the sides of their head, giving a wide field of view to detect predators early   [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ long limbs and powerful muscles enable fast sprinting to escape predators   [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ keen senses such as hearing and smell help detect wolves at a distance  [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
        Wolves have **forward-facing eyes**, which provide **binocular vision** for accurately **judging distance** when hunting prey ($\checkmark$). Deer have eyes on the **sides** of their head, giving a **wide field of view to detect predators early** ($\checkmark$).

    2. any **two** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ wolves reduce deer populations through predation  [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ predation by wolves keeps deer numbers from increasing above carrying capacity   [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ fewer deer leads to less grazing pressure on the forest vegetation  [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ this allows plant species in the deciduous forest to regenerate **OR** less damage to plants     [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ **THEREFORE** increased population size of other primary consumers  [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ **THEREFORE** increased population size of some secondary and tertiary consumers    [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ biodiversity increases   [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ removal of wolves can destabilise the ecosystem and reduce forest health  [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
        Predation by wolves keeps deer numbers from **increasing above carrying capacity** ($\checkmark$). This means **less damage to plants** through overgrazing ($\checkmark$). **Biodiversity increases** at all trophic levels ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ lianas climb up other trees to reach sunlight without needing thick supportive trunks   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ epiphytes grow on high branches to access more light   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shade-tolerant plants on the forest floor have large, flat leaves to maximise light absorption [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ strangler figs begin life in the canopy and grow downward to eventually dominate light access   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fast-growing plants reach the canopy quickly to access more light   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ any other valid adaptation that improves access to light, such as leaf orientation **OR** high chlorophyll density   [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Shade-tolerant** plants on the forest floor have **large, flat leaves** to maximise light absorption ($\checkmark$). **Epiphytes** grow on tree branches **high up** near the tops of trees ($\checkmark$) **Lianas climb up other trees** to reach sunlight, without needing **thick trunks** ($\checkmark$).

3.  any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ wolves' fundamental niche includes all potential roles and environments they could occupy in the absence of limiting factors like humans or competitors   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ their realised niche is what they actually occupy when biotic and abiotic pressures are considered [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reintroduction into natural habitats shows a return to part of their realised niche, as human presence, prey availability, and other species still affect their role    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ it is not their full fundamental niche, as environmental and ecological **OR** biotic and abiotic constraints remain present   [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **fundamental niche of** wolves includes **all potential roles and environments they could occupy** in the absence of **limiting factors**. Their **realised niche** is what they **actually occupy** when **biotic** and **abiotic pressures** are present ($\checkmark$). Reintroduction into forests is a return to **some parts of their realised niche** ($\checkmark$) but it is **not** their **full fundamental niche**, as **biotic** and **abiotic** constraints remain present ($\checkmark$).
