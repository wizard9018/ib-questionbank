# BI1634

**Maximum mark: 6**

The Sakhalin fir (*Abies sachalinensis*) and Ezo spruce (*Picea jezoensis*) are two of the most dominant conifers found throughout the northern Japanese island of Hokkaido. A quadrat survey was conducted to determine if there was an association between these two species. The results of the survey can be found in the table.

    1. Using the chi-square formula $χ^2 = \sum \dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}$, calculate the $χ^2$ value for the data in the table, where O is the observed number of quadrats and E is the expected number of quadrats containing each combination of species.    [2 marks]

    2. Using the table below, determine whether the observed and expected values differ significantly and explain whether or not an association exists between *A. sachalinensis* and *P. jezoensis*.                         [3 marks]

2. Both *A. sachalinensis* and *P. jezoensis* germinate on mossy fallen logs in the shady understorey of coniferous forests. Suggest **one** explanation for how these two species may be able to coexist in the same ecosystem.        [1 mark]

    2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ critical value for one degree of freedom at 5% level **OR** 0.05 significance is 3.84    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ since calculated chi-square value **OR**  0.41 is less than 3.84, the difference between observed and expected values is not statistically significant                [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

    ![1634aii](images/1634aii.png)

    The **calculated chi-square value** of **0.41** is **less than** the **critical value** of **3.84** at the **5% significance level** ($\checkmark$). This indicates that there is **no significant difference** between the **observed** and **expected** values ($\checkmark$), leading us to **fail to reject** the **null hypothesis** ($\checkmark$). Therefore, it is **likely** that there is **no association** between ***A. sachalinensis*** and ***P. jezoensis*** ($\checkmark$).
