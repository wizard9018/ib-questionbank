# BI1559

Which of the following processes does **not** require active transport by membrane proteins?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Reabsorption of amino acids from the filtrate of the kidneys.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Movement of glucose into muscle cells.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Transport of calcium ions out of the presynaptic neuron following an action potential.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Transport of sodium ions out of the cytoplasm following an action potential.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
In some areas of the body, such as the kidneys and intestines, glucose is actively transported against its concentration gradient. However, muscle cells typically use a lot of glucose in respiration, resulting in a low internal glucose concentration. Consequently, glucose moves from a high concentration outside the muscle cell to a low concentration inside the cell via facilitated diffusion; **Choice B** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the reabsorption of amino acids from the filtrate in the proximal convoluted tubule of the kidney nephron is an example of indirect active transport. This process relies on the active transport of sodium ions out of the tubule wall cells, creating a concentration gradient that facilitates the movement of amino acids via cotransporter proteins into these cells from the filtrate. **Choice C** is incorrect because calcium ions are actively transported out of the neuron against their concentration gradient using calcium pumps.
**Choice D** is incorrect because the action of the sodium-potassium pump following depolarisation relies on active transport due to the charges of sodium and potassium ions and the fact that these molecules are pumped against their concentration gradients.
