# BI2853

The table shows the results of an experiment that measured the diffusion rate of different particles across a phospholipid bilayer.

| Particles | Relative Rate of Diffusion (arb) |
| :-: | :-: |
| Oxygen ($\ce{O_2}$) | 15.08 |
| Oestradiol (steroid hormone) | 20.45 |
| Glucose | 1.02 |
| Sodium ions ($\ce{Na^+}$) | 0.0001(negligible) |

Which conclusion is best supported by the data?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Larger molecules diffuse faster than smaller ones across phospholipid bilayers.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Charged particles diffuse rapidly across phospholipid bilayers.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The phospholipid bilayer is selectively permeable to non-polar molecules.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Smaller molecules diffuse faster than larger ones across phospholipid bilayers.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C** is correct. The data show that non-polar molecules like oxygen and oestradiol diffuse rapidly, while charged/polar particles like glucose and $\ce{Na^+}$ diffuse slowly or not at all. This supports the idea that the lipid bilayer is selectively permeable, and only allows non-polar molecules to move freely across.

**Choice A** is incorrect because while oestradiol diffuses faster than oxygen, glucose is larger than oxygen but diffuses more slowly, contradicting the idea that larger molecules diffuse faster.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because sodium ions (charged) show a negligible rate of diffusion, but uncharged oxygen has a high rate of diffusion. This is because the hydrophobic tails of the phospholipids make it harder for charged or polar molecules to cross.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because sodium ions are smaller than oestradiol, but diffuse much more slowly.
