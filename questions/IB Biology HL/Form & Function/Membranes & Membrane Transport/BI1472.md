# BI1472

**Maximum mark: 1**

Which of the following does not use amphipathic properties to help form the structure of animal cell membranes?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Cholesterol

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Glycoproteins

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Phospholipids

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Peripheral proteins

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Amphipathic molecules are partly hydrophobic and partly hydrophilic. Although proteins are often amphipathic, peripheral proteins are not embedded **within** the phospholipid bilayer. They are loosely attached to the surface of the membrane, often by interacting with the hydrophilic head groups of phospholipids or other proteins. They don't rely on amphipathic properties for this attachment. **Choice D**  is correct.

Cholesterol is amphipathic as it consists of a polar hydroxyl (OH) group whereas the rest of the molecule is nonpolar. The hydroxyl group can form hydrogen bonds with the phospholipid heads, while the hydrophobic steroid rings sit between the fatty acid tails. **Choice A** is therefore an incorrect answer to this question. Glycoproteins, with attached carbohydrate chains, are embedded in the phospholipid bilayer, often with their hydrophilic parts facing the outside or inside of the cell, and their hydrophobic parts interacting with the fatty acid tails. So, they too use amphipathic properties therefore **Choice B** is incorrect. Phospholipids are amphipathic as they consist of a polar, hydrophilic head and non-polar hydrophobic tail. This allows them to arrange themselves in a double layer with the hydrophilic heads facing the watery environment inside and outside the cell, and the hydrophobic tails facing each other in the interior of the membrane; **Choice C**  is incorrect.
