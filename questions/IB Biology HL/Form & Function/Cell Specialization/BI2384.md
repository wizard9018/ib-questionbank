# BI2384

The diagram shows a simplified view of a human ovum (egg cell).

![BI2384](images/BI2384.png)

Which structures play a role in preventing polyspermy?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ II and IV only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ III and IV only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II, III and IV

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Polyspermy occurs when more than one sperm enters the egg cell, which stops fertilisation from taking place. A viable zygote would not be formed in such circumstances.

**Structure I** is false. It is the egg’s nucleus. This is haploid as the egg is a gamete and only carries half of the mother’s genome. It plays no direct role in preventing polyspermy.

**Structure II** is true. It is the zona pellucida, which has multiple roles, one of which is to prevent a second and subsequent sperm from entering the ovum after the first sperm has gained access. \
**Structure III** is false. It is a mitochondrion, an important organelle in many cell types where energy is released from aerobic respiration. Mitochondria do not play a direct role in preventing polyspermy.

**Structure IV** is true. It is a cortical granule. These are vesicles that secrete their contents once a sperm has contacted the egg, and the contents modify the environment outside the cell so that further sperm cannot reach the egg.

**Choice A** is correct because it contains the two true structures that prevent polyspermy (**II** and **IV**).

**Choices B, C** and **D** are incorrect because they contain a combination of true and false structures.
