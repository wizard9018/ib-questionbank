    1. Identify the adaptation that increases the surface area-to-volume ratio of the cells in the micrograph.        [1 mark]

    2. State one other general adaptation of exchange surfaces, such as the small intestine, that increases the rate of simple diffusion.    [1 mark]

    3. Outline how the surface area-to-volume ratio of a cell affects its ability to carry out its functions.        [2 marks]

1.
    3. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ large cell volume requires higher rate of exchange of substances **OR** need for exchange is increased with increased cell volume        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ higher surface area-to-volume ratio increases rate of exchange **OR** lower surface area-to-volume ratio decreases rate of exchange             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ciliated cells have a high surface area-to-volume ratio to give a large surface area for movement of mucous **OR** root hair cells have a high surface area-to-volume ratio for absorption of water **OR** type I pneumocytes in the alveoli are flattened to have a large surface area-to-volume ratio for gas exchange **OR** red blood cells have a biconcave shape which gives a large surface area-to-volume ratio for absorption and release of oxygen **OR** cells of the villi in the small intestine have a large surface area-to-volume ratio for absorption **OR** any other relevant example    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The surface area-to-volume ratio affects the need for the exchange of substances and the exchange rate. An increased cell volume **increases the need for exchange** ($\checkmark$), but a lower surface area-to-volume ratio **decreases the rate of exchange** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following (must include **two** adaptations and explanations for **each** type of cell for full marks):

    *Erythrocytes:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ erythrocytes do not have a nucleus **OR** lack organelles to provide maximum space for haemoglobin                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haemoglobin to bind with **OR** to carry oxygen        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ have a biconcave shape **OR** disc-shaped to increase surface area-to-volume ratio for gaseous exchange **OR** to increase diffusion rate        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ flexible to squeeze through capillaries            [1 mark]

    *Sperm cells:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ flagellum **OR** tail to propel them forward **OR** allow them to swim to egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ midpiece has lots of mitochondria to provide energy for swimming to egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ acrosome contains enzymes to penetrate egg     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ streamlined shape to reduce drag **OR** friction **OR** to make it easier to swim to egg    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ small cells so can be produced in large quantities to increase chance of fertilisation **OR** to reach egg                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ little cytoplasm **OR** few organelles **OR** no Golgi apparatus **OR** no ribosomes to reduce size **OR** weight to make it easier  for them to swim **OR** move    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid nucleus **OR** half the normal number of chromosomes OR half the DNA for fertilisation of the egg        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Erythrocytes are **biconcave** to increase their **surface area-to-volume ratio** for **gaseous exchange** ($\checkmark$). They also **lack a nucleus** to provide **maximum space** for **haemoglobin** ($\checkmark$) to be packed in; **haemoglobin** binds to **oxygen** to carry it from the lungs to the rest of the **body** ($\checkmark$). Sperm have **flagella** to propel them forwards so they can **swim to the egg** to fertilise it ($\checkmark$). They also have lots of **mitochondria** in their **midpiece** to provide **energy** for **swimming** ($\checkmark$).

    Increased surface area-to-volume ratio is important for the **absorption of nutrients** ($\checkmark$) by villi and for **gas exchange** between alveoli and blood and body cells and blood ($\checkmark$).
