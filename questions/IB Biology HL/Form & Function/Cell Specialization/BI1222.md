# BI1222

The cubes represent models of unicellular organisms. Which cube has the lowest surface area to volume ratio?

![BI1222](images/BI1222.png)

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The surface area to volume ratio generally decreases as an organism or cuboid gets bigger unless the shapes of the cuboids are very different. Therefore, without calculation, the answer can be deduced. By calculation Surface area of C= 10mm $\times$ 10mm $\times$ 6mm = 600 mm:sup[2] and the volume of C = 10mm $\times$ 10mm $\times$ 10mm = 1000 mm:sup[3] . The surface area to volume ratio of C = 600 ÷ 1000, which is less than 1 (0.6). **Choice C** is correct.

**Choices A, B** and **D** are all incorrect as the SA:Vol of all three are greater than 1, and so greater than Choice C.

Surface area of A = (2mm $\times$ 2mm $\times$ 2mm) + (4mm $\times$ 2mm $\times$ 4mm) = 40 mm:sup[2]. Volume of A = 2mm $\times$ 4mm $\times$ mm2 = 16 mm:sup[3]. Surface area to volume ratio of A = 40 ÷ 16 = 2.5

Surface area of B = 2mm $\times$ 2mm $\times$ 6mm= 24 mm:sup[2]. Volume of B = 2mm $\times$ 2mm $\times$ 2mm = 8 mm:sup[3]. Surface area to volume ratio of B = 24 ÷ 8 = 3.

Surface area of D = (4mm $\times$ 4mm $\times$ 2mm) + (1mm $\times$ 4mm $\times$ 4mm) = 48 mm:sup[2]. Volume of D = 1mm $\times$ 4mm $\times$ 4mm = 16 mm:sup[3]. Surface area to volume ratio of A = 48 ÷ 16 = 3.
