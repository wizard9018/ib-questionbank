# BI1057

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how the use of stem cells may be used to treat type I diabetes in the future.     [5 marks]

2. Explain the factors that limit cell size in humans.     [4 marks]

3. Explain how red blood cells and striated muscle fibres are adapted to their function.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ embryonic stem cells **OR** induced pluripotent stem cells **OR** iPSC used     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ stem cells contain desired, **OR** insulin, allele     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ stem cells differentiate to become pancreatic **OR** β-cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cells are implanted **OR** inserted into the pancreas    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ insulin produced in response to increase in blood glucose    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ immunity suppressing drugs used        [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Embryonic stem cells** ($\checkmark$) with the **insulin gene** ($\checkmark$) are stimulated to divide and **differentiate into β-cells** ($\checkmark$) then **injected into the pancreas** ($\checkmark$).  Implanted cells **produce insulin** ($\checkmark$) in response to increasing blood glucose. **Immunosuppression drugs** ($\checkmark$) may need to be taken if using embryonic stem cells.

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ surface area affects the rate at which materials enter and leave the cell     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ volume affects the rate at which materials are used and produced by the cell  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the surface area to volume ratio affects the rate of metabolism in a cell     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the bigger the cell, the smaller the SA to volume ratio is    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cells having a low SA to volume ratio cannot exchange materials fast enough
[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some cells, **OR** motor neurons, **OR** muscle cells, **OR** other named example in humans, are long and thin, which maintains a high SA to volume ratio        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **surface area** of a cell affects the **rate** at which **materials enter** and **leave** cells ($\checkmark$). On the other hand, the cell’s **volume** affects the rate at which **materials** are **used and produced** ($\checkmark$). A **small surface area to volume ratio** is not ideal for human cells because it **limits** the cell’s ability to **export waste from the cytoplasm** rapidly enough ($\checkmark$) to meet the cytoplasm’s **metabolic needs** ($\checkmark$), such as the availability of oxygen. A **large cell** has a **small surface area to volume ratio** ($\checkmark$). Therefore, the transport of the substances is more efficient in small cells.

3. any **seven** of the following:

    ***red blood cells:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ small and flexible to fit through narrow capillaries [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ contain haemoglobin to bind to oxygen **OR** to carry oxygen [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ biconcave to increase surface area        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ no nucleus and lack other organelles to provide more space for haemoglobin    [1 mark]

    ***Striated muscle fibres:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ cell membrane capable of electrical impulse **OR** action potential propagation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ long cells so produce movement when work together        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lots of mitochondria to supply ATP for contraction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ contain contractile proteins to bring about movement    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ multinucleate supports large size of cell **OR** aids in controlling protein synthesis **OR** repair sarcoplasmic reticulum to store and release calcium ions    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Red blood cells** are adapted for **oxygen transport** ($\checkmark$). They are **small** and **flexible** to squeeze through capillaries ($\checkmark$) and have a **biconcave** shape to **increase** their **surface area** ($\checkmark$). They **lack** a **nucleus** and most **other organelles** ($\checkmark$) to make room for the **haemoglobin** which **binds**, and carries, **oxygen** ($\checkmark$) around the body.

    **Striated muscle fibres** are adapted for **voluntary movement** ($\checkmark$). They contain **contractile proteins** ($\checkmark$) and are **long** so when the fibres **contract together** they bring about significant **movement** ($\checkmark$). They contain **many mitochondria** to provide **ATP** for contraction ($\checkmark$) and also have **sarcoplasmic reticulum** which stores and releases **calcium ions** ($\checkmark$) to control muscle contraction. The **many nuclei** are a result of the fusing of cells to produce the long fibres, and these **support** the large cell and **control protein production** ($\checkmark$).
