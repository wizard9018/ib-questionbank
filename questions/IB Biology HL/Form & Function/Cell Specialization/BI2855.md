# BI2855

Which of the following best explains why striated muscle fibres are significantly larger than many other human cells?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ They are specialised for contraction and require large volumes of cytoplasm to accommodate myofibrils.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ They contain multiple nuclei, allowing rapid oxygen exchange with surrounding capillaries.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ They must produce large volumes of calcium ions, requiring more cytoplasm.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ They undergo continuous mitosis, increasing their size beyond normal somatic cell limits.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct. Striated muscle fibres are extremely large and multinucleated because they are specialised for contraction. They need a large cytoplasmic volume to accommodate contractile proteins (myofibrils) and support their high metabolic demands.

**Choice B** is incorrect because multiple nuclei support localised protein synthesis, not oxygen exchange. Oxygen delivery is primarily the role of red blood cells. \
**Choice C** is incorrect because muscle fibres store and release calcium ions for contraction, but they do not produce them. Calcium ions are stored primarily in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, and their presence does not explain the cell’s large size.\
**Choice D** is incorrect because striated muscle fibres do not undergo mitosis. In fact, mature muscle fibres are post-mitotic and arise from the fusion of many precursor cells (myoblasts), which contributes to their large size and multinucleation
