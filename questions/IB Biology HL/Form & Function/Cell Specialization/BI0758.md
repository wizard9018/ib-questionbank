# BI0758

Which of the following could be a reason that adult haematopoietic stem cells rather than embryonic stem cells are used in the treatment of leukaemia?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Embryonic stem cells have limited differentiation potential.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ It is difficult to control which cell type embryonic stem cells differentiate into.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Adult haematopoietic stem cells divide faster than embryonic stem cells.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Embryonic stem cells have a higher chance of accumulated mutations.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent, meaning they can become almost any cell type. While this is useful in theory, it also makes it hard to control their differentiation precisely. In contrast, adult haematopoietic stem cells (from bone marrow or cord blood) are multipotent and are already partially specialised — they naturally form blood cells, which is ideal for treating leukaemia. Therefore, adult stem cells are safer and more targeted for this use. **Choice B** is the correct answer.

**Choice A** is incorrect because embryonic stem cells actually have greater differentiation potential as they are pluripotent, and so not limited. Adult stem cells have more limited potential.

**Choice C** is incorrect because it is embryonic stem cells that divide more rapidly than adult stem cells, meaning they have greater proliferative capacity than adult stem cells. Adult haematopoietic stem cells divide at a controlled, slower rate, which limits their use in some therapies but helps reduce risks like tumour formation.

**Choice D** is incorrect because adult stem cells (especially from older donors) are more likely to have accumulated mutations due to age and environmental exposure. Embryonic stem cells are derived early in development and so typically have few mutations.
