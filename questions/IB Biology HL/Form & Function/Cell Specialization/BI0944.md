# BI0944

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Explain how the shape of erythrocytes and cells lining the proximal convoluted tubule make these cells more adapted to the functions they perform.  [4 marks]

2. Compare and contrast the structure of striated muscle cells and cardiac muscle fibres.              [5 marks]

3. Explain how the structure of egg and sperm cells are adapted to their functions.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  erythrocytes are biconcave, which increases their surface area    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  increased surface area increases rate of gas exchange between erythrocyte and its surroundings **OR** blood plasma [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  PCT cells are columnar - allowing close packing of cells / efficient use of space [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  cells of the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) have microvilli which increase their surface area [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  increased surface area of PCT cells provides more places for aquaporins **OR** amino acid transport proteins **OR** glucose transport proteins **OR** ion transport proteins [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  greater number of aquaporins **OR** other correctly named transport protein increases the rate substances **OR** water **OR** amino acids **OR** glucose **OR** ions are reabsorbed from the filtrate [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  invaginations on the outer cell membrane, **OR** membrane on opposite side to lumen, of PCT cells provide a large SA for transport of substances out of the cell [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Erythrocytes are **biconcave**, which **increases** their **surface area** ($\checkmark$).  Having a larger surface area **increases the rate** at which **gas exchange** occurs between the cell and its surroundings ($\checkmark$).  Cells lining the proximal convoluted tubule have **microvilli**, increasing their **surface area** ($\checkmark$).  This increased surface area means the cell can have **more aquaporins** and other **transport proteins** in its cell membrane ($\checkmark$), which **increases the rate** at which substances such as water, glucose and amino acids are **reabsorbed** from the filtrate ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (must contain at least 1 similarity and one difference for full marks):

    **Differences:**\
    $\ce{ -> }$ striated muscle cells have multiple nuclei **WHEREAS** cardiac muscle cells do not        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cardiac muscle cells are branched **WHEREAS** striated muscle cells are not        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ striated muscle cells are arranged side by side **WHEREAS** cardiac muscles are arranged side to side **AND** end to end [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ striated muscle fibres can be much larger than cardiac muscle cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cardiac muscle cells have intercalated discs **WHEREAS** striated muscle fibres do not [1 mark]

    **Similarities:**\
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** contain contractile myofibrils [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** contain sarcoplasm **OR** sarcoplasmic reticulum **OR** T-tubules [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Both** cardiac muscle cells and striated muscle fibres contain **contractile myofibrils** ($\checkmark$), **T-tubules** and a **sarcolemma** ($\checkmark$). However, **cardiac** muscle cells are **branched** and connected by **intercalated discs**, whereas **striated muscle** fibres are **not branched** ($\checkmark$) and are formed from **fused cells** ($\checkmark$).  Finally, **striated muscle** fibres have **multiple nuclei**, whereas **cardiac muscle** cells have only **one nucleus** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ egg cells are large, **OR** have a large volume of cytoplasm, which supplies the egg with nutrients **OR** food stores **OR** lipid stores **OR** makes it possible for multiple, rapid rounds of cell division after fertilisation  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ egg cells contain many mitochondria for energy production  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ eggs **OR** sperm cells are haploid, which allows them to fuse with another gamete to become diploid [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the zona pellucida prevents multiple sperm from fusing with the egg **OR** polyspermy **OR** prevents sperm from another species from fusing with the egg [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ corona radiata of egg protects the egg [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ egg cells contain cortical granules which undergo exocytosis after fertilisation which prevents polyspermy **OR** modifies zona pellucida **OR** results in the breakdown of sperm binding proteins [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ egg cells secrete chemicals which attract sperm [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm cells are streamlined, **OR** have few organelles, for swimming [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm cells have receptors for chemicals released by egg cells which guide sperm to the egg [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm cells have an acrosome which breaks down the zona pellucida [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm cells have receptor proteins which bind to the egg's cell membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm cells have a tail **OR** flagellum for locomotion [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sperm cells have many mitochondria which supply the energy for locomotion [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Egg** cells are large, so they have a **large volume of cytoplasm** containing **mitochondria** and **fat droplets** ($\checkmark$) to supply the egg with energy.  Because it is large, egg cells are also prepared for relatively **rapid** rounds of **division** after they are fertilised ($\checkmark$).  It is important that the zygote, formed after fertilisation, is diploid, so the **sperm and egg** are both **haploid** ($\checkmark$).  The egg's **zona pellucida** also **prevents polyspermy** ($\checkmark$) by hardening after fertilisation in response to the exocytosis of **enzymes** contained in **cortical granules** ($\checkmark$).  Sperm are also adapted to their function, which is to fertilise egg cells.  They have **receptors** that bind to **chemicals released by egg** cells which help them move towards egg cells ($\checkmark$).   Sperm cells also have an **acrosome** at the tip of their head, which **breaks down the zona pellucida** ($\checkmark$), allowing receptors on the head of the sperm to **bind** to receptors on the cell **membrane of the egg** ($\checkmark$).
