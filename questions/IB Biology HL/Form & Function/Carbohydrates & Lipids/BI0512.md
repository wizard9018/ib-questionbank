# BI0512

In the digestion of starch, amylase breaks down both amylose and amylopectin into simpler sugars. However, complete digestion to glucose requires additional enzymes due to certain structural characteristics.

Which of the following is the likely reason amylase alone cannot fully break down amylopectin into maltose, unlike its action on amylose?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ It contains β-glucose which amylase cannot interact with.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ It contains α-glucose which amylase cannot interact with.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ It has 1,4 glycosidic linkages that amylase cannot break.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ It has 1,6 glycosidic linkages that amylase cannot break.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D**  is correct because amylopectin contains both α-1,4 glycosidic linkages and α-1,6 glycosidic linkages. Amylase can hydrolyse α-1,4 linkages but cannot break α-1,6 linkages at the branch points. Additional enzymes are therefore required to completely digest amylopectin into glucose.

**Choice A** is incorrect because amylopectin is composed of α-glucose monomers, not β-glucose. β-glucose is found in cellulose.

**Choice B** is incorrect because amylase specifically acts on polysaccharides made from α-glucose, including starch components such as amylose and amylopectin.

**Choice C** is incorrect because amylase can break α-1,4 glycosidic linkages. In fact, this is the main reaction catalysed by amylase during starch digestion.
