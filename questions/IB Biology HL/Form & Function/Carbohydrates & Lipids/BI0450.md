2. Any **four** of the following (should include **AT LEAST ONE** for compare and **AT LEAST ONE** for contrast):

 *Compare:*\
 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are polysaccharides [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are made up of $\alpha$- glucose molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** have 1,4 and 1,6 linkages [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are energy storage molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** amylopectin in starch and glycogen are branched molecules [1 mark]

 *Contrast:*\
 $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen is more branched **IN CONTRAST TO** starch, **OR** amylose of starch is unbranched, amylopectin has few branched chains **WHEREAS** glycogen is more branched [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ Starch is composed of two different polymers, amylose and amylopectin **WHEREAS** glycogen is composed of only one polymer [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ starch is found in plant cells **WHEREAS** glycogen is found in animal cells [1 mark]

 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Both starch and glycogen are **polysaccharides** ($\checkmark$) made of **$\alpha$- glucose molecules** ($\checkmark$), which have **1,4 and 1,6 linkages** ($\checkmark$). Starch is composed of **two different** polymers; **amylose** (unbranched and forms a helix) and **amylopectin** (branched), whereas **glycogen** is composed of only **one type of polymer** ($\checkmark$). Both amylopectin, in starch, and glycogen are **branched** molecules ($\checkmark$), but **glycogen is more branched than starch** ($\checkmark$). Both are **energy storage** ($\checkmark$)molecules but **starch** is found in **plant cells** whereas **glycogen** is found in **animal cells** ($\checkmark$).
