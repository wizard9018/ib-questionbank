# BI0444

Which statement(s) about starch and glycogen is/are correct?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}

  | | |

  | :-- | :-- |

  | I. | Starch and glycogen are branched molecules. |

  | II. | Starch is made up $\beta-$ glucose molecules but glycogen is made up of $\alpha-$ glucose molecules. |

  | III. | Starch and glycogen are energy storing polysaccharides. |

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ II only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Both starch and glycogen are branched molecules (**Statement I**), though the degree and pattern of branching differ between them. Starch consists of two types of molecules: amylose (unbranched) and amylopectin (highly branched). Glycogen is more highly branched than amylopectin, which allows for rapid release of glucose when needed, especially important in animals for energy supply. 

**Statement II** is incorrect as both starch and glycogen are made up of α- glucose molecules.  

Both starch and glycogen serve as primary energy storage molecules (**Statement III**). Starch is the main form of stored carbohydrate in plants, while glycogen serves a similar function in animals and fungi.

**Choice C** is therefore correct as it includes **Statements I** and **III** only.

**Choices B** and **D** are incorrect as they include **Statement II**. **Choice A** is incorrect as it only includes one correct statement (**Statement I**).
