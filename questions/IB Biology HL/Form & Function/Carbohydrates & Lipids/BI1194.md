2.
    1. any **two** of the following (one for structure and one for how structure adds to the role):

    ***structure*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ alternating arrangement of beta-glucose monomers gives a straight chain that can form groups [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ linear arrangement of cellulose molecules provides a fibrous structure that is rigid    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ groups of  cellulose molecules are arranged in parallel with hydrogen bonds forming cross-links [1 mark]

    ***how structure supports the role*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ microfibrils of cellulose have tensile strength **OR** give strength to the plant cell walls [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Cellulose is an unbranched polymer of glucose, and **groups** of **cellulose molecules** are arranged in **parallel**, cross-linked by **hydrogen bonds** ($\checkmark$)  to provide **strength** and **rigidity** ($\checkmark$) to the cell walls of the plants.

2.
    2. any **two** of the following (one from each section):

    ***structure*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen has  α-1-6 glycosidic bonds **OR** α-1,6 linkages  between glucose molecules **OR** has  α-1-6 glycosidic bonds **OR** 1-6 linkages between α-glucose molecules **OR**  α-1-6 glycosidic bonds **OR** 1-6 linkages between α-D-glucose molecules **AND** making it branched  [1 mark]

    ***how structure supports the role*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ branching leads to compact **OR** efficient storage of glucose **OR** maximises the number of  glucose molecules that can be stored **OR** glycogen takes up less room so it can be stored in large amounts  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen is insoluble, so does not affect osmotic potential of the cell   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ branching means it can be broken down rapidly to release glucose for respiration **OR** rapid mobilisation of glucose when energy required **OR** glucose can be released more efficiently [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Glycogen contains **1,6 glycosidic bonds** between the α-glucose molecules making it **branched** ($\checkmark$). This is beneficial as it means that enzymes can simultaneously act on multiple ends of the branches, making **glucose release rapid** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ glucose is converted to sucrose by the addition of fructose     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sucrose is carried from source **OR** leaf to sink **OR** root by translocation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sucrose is loaded **OR** actively transported into phloem sieve tube elements    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ by companion cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lowers water potential **OR** makes water potential more negative **OR** increases solute potential in phloem sieve tubes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves into phloem sieve tubes **OR** water moves in by osmosis **WHICH** increases hydrostatic pressure            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at the sink **OR** root sucrose moves out of phloem sieve tube elements by diffusion **OR** active transport            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves out of phloem sieve tubes **OR** follows sucrose by osmosis **WHICH** lowers hydrostatic pressure                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sap **OR** sucrose flows **OR** moves from high to low hydrostatic pressure **OR** down pressure gradient                    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The carbohydrate transported in the phloem is the disaccharide **sucrose**, which is made up of **glucose** plus **fructose** ($\checkmark$). The sucrose is **actively transported** into the phloem **sieve tube elements** ($\checkmark$) by the **companion cells** ($\checkmark$). The sucrose **reduces** the **water potential** inside the **sieve tube elements** ($\checkmark$), so **water enters by osmosis, increasing** the **hydrostatic pressure** ($\checkmark$). At the **sinks**, sucrose concentrations are low, so the **sucrose moves out** of the sieve tube elements by **diffusion** or **active transport** ($\checkmark$). This **increases** the **water potential** in the sieve tube elements, so **water moves out** by osmosis, **reducing** the **hydrostatic pressure** ($\checkmark$). The sap in the sieve tube elements flows from the source to the sink **down** the **hydrostatic pressure gradient** ($\checkmark$).
