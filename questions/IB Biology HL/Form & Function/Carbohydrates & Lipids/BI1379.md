# BI1379

What type of reaction is involved in the digestion of the following molecule?

![image](images/image.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Reduction

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Condensation

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Neutralisation

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Hydrolysis

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because digestion involves catabolic reactions, such as hydrolysis, that break down larger molecules into smaller molecules. The molecule shown in the diagram is maltose, a disaccharide, which can be broken down into monomer subunits with the addition of water. This is a hydrolysis reaction.

**Choice A** is incorrect because sugars are not reduced during digestion. A reduction reaction involves an atom or ion gaining one or more electrons. **Choice B** is incorrect since condensation reactions are a type of anabolic reaction, where two smaller molecules are joined together to produce a larger molecule whilst releasing water. This is the opposite of what we would expect for digestion, which involves catabolic reactions that break substances down. **Choice C** is incorrect as the breakdown of maltose involves neither acid nor alkali and thus cannot be a neutralisation reaction. A neutralisation reaction involves the reaction between an acid and base to produce a solution closer to pH7.
