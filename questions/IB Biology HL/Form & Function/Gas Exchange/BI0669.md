# BI0669

The diagram below shows a simple apparatus which uses a porous pot to model water transport in plants.

![BI0669](images/BI0669.png)

Why is a porous pot used to model water transport in the xylem?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ It allows water molecules to flow down the capillary tube to represent water moving from leaves to roots.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ It allows water to leave the pot through the pores, which represents water evaporating out of the stomata.
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ It represents the thick, lignified walls of xylem vessels.
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ It generates adhesion and cohesion for transporting water inside plants.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A porous pot allows water to evaporate and diffuse out, which models water loss through the stomata in plant leaves. This evaporation creates tension, pulling water up the capillary tube, simulating how water is drawn up the xylem from roots to leaves during transpiration; **Choice B** is the correct answer.

**Choice A** is incorrect because water in the xylem moves upward, from roots to leaves, not downward, and not from leaves to roots.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the porous pot represents the leaves, where water is lost, not the xylem vessels. In this model, the capillary tube represents the xylem.

**Choice D** is incorrect because the porous pot does not generate adhesion and cohesion. These are intrinsic properties of water due to its polarity, which help maintain a continuous column of water in the xylem but are not caused by the porous pot.
