# BI2683

The table below shows % oxygen saturation at different partial pressures for adult and foetal haemoglobin.

| Partial pressure (kPa) | % Saturation (Foetal Hb) | % Saturation (Adult Hb) |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 2.0 | 60% | 30% |
| 4.0 | 85% | 65% |
| 8.0 | 98% | 90% |

What conclusion can be drawn from the data?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Adult haemoglobin is better at binding oxygen at low oxygen pressures.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Foetal haemoglobin binds oxygen more effectively than adult haemoglobin at low oxygen pressures.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ At high oxygen pressures, foetal haemoglobin releases oxygen more easily than adult haemoglobin.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Adult haemoglobin always binds oxygen more strongly than foetal haemoglobin.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because the table shows that at every partial pressure of oxygen, foetal haemoglobin has a higher percentage saturation than adult haemoglobin. This means that foetal haemoglobin has a higher affinity for oxygen, which allows it to extract oxygen from maternal blood efficiently.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the table shows the opposite of this statement: adult haemoglobin has lower saturation than foetal haemoglobin at all partial pressures. This means foetal haemoglobin is better, not worse, at binding oxygen at low oxygen pressures.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the table shows that at high oxygen pressures (e.g., 8.0 kPa), foetal haemoglobin remains more saturated (98%) than adult haemoglobin (90%). This means foetal haemoglobin holds onto oxygen more tightly, not that it releases oxygen more easily.

**Choice D** is incorrect because the table clearly shows that adult haemoglobin has lower oxygen saturation than foetal haemoglobin at all pressures, meaning foetal haemoglobin binds oxygen more strongly, not adult haemoglobin.
