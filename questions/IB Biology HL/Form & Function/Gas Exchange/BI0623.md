# BI0623

**Maximum mark: 7**

1. Describe the structure of the bronchioles.  [2 marks]

2. Distinguish between the external intercostal muscles and the internal intercostal muscles.    [2 marks]

3. Outline how the concentration gradients of oxygen and carbon dioxide are maintained in the lungs. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ wall contains smooth muscle fibres [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  tree-like structure of narrow airways [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  connects alveoli to bronchi **OR** connects to alveoli at the end **OR** branch from bronchi      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  epithial cells OR goblet cells line the airways [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
The walls of the bronchioles contain **smooth muscle fibres** ($\checkmark$). They are smaller **branches** of bronchi ($\checkmark$) and connect the bronchi with the **alveoli** ($\checkmark$).

2.    Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  during inhalation, external intercostal muscles contract, **OR** shorten, **WHEREAS** internal intercostal muscles relax, **OR** lengthen [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  during exhalation, external intercostal muscles relax, **OR** lengthen, **WHEREAS** internal intercostal muscles contract, **OR** shorten     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  during inhalation, external intercostal muscles pull ribcage upwards and outwards, **WHEREAS** internal intercostal muscles pull ribcage inwards and downwards during exhalation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  external intercostal muscles are located on the exterior surface of the ribs **WHILE** internal intercostal muscles are located on the interior surface of the ribs [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
The difference between external intercostal muscles and internal intercostal muscles is that during **inhalation**, external intercostal muscles **contract**, whereas internal intercostal muscles **relax** ($\checkmark$). When external intercostal muscles contract, the **ribcage** is pulled **upwards** and outwards, but when internal intercostal muscles contract, the ribcage is pulled inwards and **downwards** ($\checkmark$).

3.    Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  contraction of external intercostal muscles **OR** diaphragm brings about inhalation, **OR** contraction of internal intercostal muscle **OR** relaxation of external intercostal muscles **OR** relaxation of diaphragm brings about exhalation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  after inhalation **OR** inspiration, alveoli have higher O$_2$ **OR** lower CO$_2$ concentration, **OR** after exhalation, alveoli have lower CO$_2$ concentration, than the blood [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  oxygen diffuses into blood **OR** capillaries from alveoli **OR** carbon dioxide diffuses from blood **OR** capillaries into alveoli   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  concentration gradients of gases **OR** oxygen **OR** carbon dioxide maintained by blood flow in capillaries    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
The **contraction** of the **external intercostal** muscles and **diaphragm** brings about **inhalation**  ($\checkmark$). After **inhalation**, the **alveoli** have a **higher O$_2$** concentration and a **lower CO$_2$** concentration **than the blood** ($\checkmark$). **Oxygen** diffuses from the **alveoli into** into the **blood** ($\checkmark$). The constant **flow of blood** in the **capillaries** means oxygen is carried off to the body, **maintaining a steep diffusion gradient** ($\checkmark$).
