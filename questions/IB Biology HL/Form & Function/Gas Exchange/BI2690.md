# BI2690

The graph below shows two oxygen dissociation curves.

![BI2690](images/BI2690.png)

Which of the following best explains why the right-shifted curve occurs?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ A decrease in temperature

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ A decrease in carbon dioxide concentration

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ An increase in blood pH

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ An increase in $\ce{CO_2}$ levels

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice D** is correct because high $\ce{CO_2}$ levels lower pH, shifting the curve right, meaning haemoglobin releases more oxygen at a given oxygen partial pressure.

**Choice A** is incorrect because lower temperature shifts the curve left, increasing oxygen affinity, not right. **Choice B** is incorrect because lower $\ce{CO_2}$ shifts the curve left, meaning haemoglobin holds onto oxygen more tightly. **Choice C** is incorrect because higher pH shifts the curve left, increasing haemoglobin's oxygen affinity, opposite to the Bohr shift.
