# BI1131

The image shows an oxygen dissociation curve for human haemoglobin. Which sequence of events correctly describes how the Bohr shift occurs in heavily respiring tissues?

![BI1131](images/BI1131.png)
[Source: Adapted from Militia, M. (2006, December 25). *File: HB Saturation curve.png - wikimedia commons*. Wikimedia commons. Retrieved August 30, 2023 from URL  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hb_saturation_curve.png.  Copyright under CC BY 3.0]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased carbon dioxide concentration in blood → higher pH → curve shifts to the left → haemoglobin has a decreased affinity for oxygen

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased oxygen concentration in blood → lower pH → curve shifts to the left → haemoglobin has an increased affinity for oxygen

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased carbon dioxide concentration in blood → lower pH → curve shifts to the right → haemoglobin has a decreased affinity for oxygen

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Increased oxygen concentration in blood → higher pH → curve shifts to the right → haemoglobin has an increased affinity for oxygen

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice C**  is correct, as heavily respiring tissues produce more carbon dioxide, which lowers the blood pH (or makes it more acidic), this causes the oxygen dissociation curve to shift to the right. This shift results in a decreased affinity of haemoglobin for oxygen, meaning that oxygen is more readily released to the heavily respiring tissues.

**Choice A**  is incorrect, as a higher concentration of carbon dioxide in the blood does not result in a higher pH. **Choice B**  is incorrect, as an increase in the respiration rate of cells leads to a higher concentration of carbon dioxide in the blood, not oxygen. **Choice D**  is incorrect, as the Bohr shift results in haemoglobin having a decreased affinity for oxygen.
