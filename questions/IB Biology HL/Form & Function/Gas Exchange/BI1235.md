# BI1235

The diagram below shows an experiment to study the effect of light intensity on transpiration rates. What is the most likely effect of **high light intensity** on transpiration rates?

![BI1235](images/BI1235.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Faster transpiration because more light increases the water potential in the xylem

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Slower transpiration because more heat will increase the humidity around the shoot

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Faster transpiration because more stomata are likely to be open

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Slower transpiration because heat prevents cohesion of water molecules

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from a plant via the stomata. At high light intensity, more stomata are likely to be open to allow more carbon dioxide to diffuse into the leaf for photosynthesis, while at the same time resulting in more loss of water from the plant via transpiration. The lamp will also transfer heat energy to the plant, which will result in more evaporation from the leaf, increasing the rate of transpiration. **Choice C** is correct.

Light has no direct effect on water potential in a plant; **Choice A** is incorrect. Humidity is a measure of the moisture content of the air. As temperature increases, the air will become drier, decreasing the humidity, and increasing the rate of evaporation and transpiration; **Choice B** is incorrect. Heat increases the rate of transpiration as it results in more evaporation from the surface of the leaf, resulting in a faster transpiration stream; **Choice D** is incorrect.
