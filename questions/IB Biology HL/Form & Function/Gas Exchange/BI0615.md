How would this lung condition affect gaseous exchange?

-    A. $\hspace{1em}$ Reduced surface area

-    B. $\hspace{1em}$ Reduced ventilation rate

-    C. $\hspace{1em}$ Reduced affinity of haemoglobin to oxygen

-    D. $\hspace{1em}$ Increased density of blood vessels at the alveoli

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
This condition, typically associated with emphysema, involves the destruction of alveoli, which greatly reduces the surface area available for gaseous exchange. A smaller surface area limits the diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the alveoli and blood, impairing the efficiency of gas exchange. **Choice A** is, therefore, the correct answer.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the breakdown of alveoli primarily affects gas exchange, not the rate of ventilation. Ventilation rate depends on factors like muscle function and neural control, which are not directly impacted by alveolar destruction.

**Choice C** is incorrect because the affinity of haemoglobin for oxygen is not affected by the physical condition of the alveoli. Instead, it is influenced by factors like pH (Bohr effect), temperature, and levels of carbon dioxide. While less oxygen is available due to the reduced surface area, haemoglobin's intrinsic affinity remains unchanged.

**Choice D** is incorrect because the destruction of alveoli does not increase the density of blood vessels. In fact, the opposite may occur, where reduced blood flow and damaged capillaries further compromise gas exchange.
