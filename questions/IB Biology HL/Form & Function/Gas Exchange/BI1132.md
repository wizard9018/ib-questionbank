# BI1132

Which statement explains why haemoglobin is described as an ‘allosteric protein’?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Conformational changes to oxygen influence the ability of it to bind to haemoglobin

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Haemoglobin has multiple binding sites for oxygen

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The binding of oxygen to haemoglobin causes the oxygen dissociation curve to shift to the right

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Changes in concentration of oxygen cause conformational changes in haemoglobin that influence the ability of oxygen to bind

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Allosteric proteins typically have multiple binding sites, where the binding of a ligand at one site induces a conformational change that influences binding at other sites. With haemoglobin, when one oxygen molecule binds to a subunit, it increases the affinity of the remaining subunits for oxygen, known as cooperative binding. Similarly, when oxygen is released, the affinity of the remaining subunits decreases. This property defines haemoglobin as an allosteric protein, therefore **Choice D**  is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect, as oxygen does not undergo conformational changes to bind to haemoglobin. **Choice B**  is incorrect as, although haemoglobin has multiple binding sites for oxygen, this doesn’t directly explain why haemoglobin is described as an allosteric protein. The multiple binding sites for oxygen are related to its quaternary structure but not specifically to its allosteric nature. **Choice C**  is incorrect, as the binding of oxygen to haemoglobin does not cause the oxygen dissociation curve to shift.
