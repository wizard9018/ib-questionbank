# BI1133

Which is **not** a reason for oxygen dissociation curves to be S-shaped?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Cooperative binding of oxygen

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ The binding of the first oxygen molecule to haemoglobin causes the remaining oxygen molecules to bind more easily

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ It is easier for the first oxygen molecule to bind to haemoglobin than the others
- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The shape of haemoglobin is changed as each oxygen molecule binds

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
It is more difficult for the first oxygen molecule to bind to haemoglobin, but its binding induces a conformational change that enhances the affinity of the other subunits for oxygen. This causes the curve to be S-shaped. Seeming as this is NOT a reason for oxygen dissociation curves to be S-shaped, **Choice C** is the correct answer to the question.

**Choices A, B** and **D**  are all incorrect, as they are all reasons for the shape of the dissociation curve. When the first oxygen molecule binds to the haemoglobin, it increases the affinity of the remaining subunits for oxygen, making it easier for the subsequent oxygen molecules to bind, causing the S-shaped curve.
