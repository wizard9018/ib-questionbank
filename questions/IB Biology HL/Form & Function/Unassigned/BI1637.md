# BI1637

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the structure and function of pneumocytes in the human lung.     [4 marks]

2. Describe the roles of the internal and external intercostal muscles during exhalation.    [4 marks]

3. Explain how the depolarisation of skeletal muscle fibres brings about the shortening of sarcomeres in muscle contraction.         [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **four** of the following:

    ***Type I pneumocytes:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ flattened shape **OR**  thin **OR** minimal cytoplasm **OR** minimal organelles to reduce diffusion distance for gaseous exchange            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large surface area **OR** make up approximately 95% of the surface of alveoli for maximum rate of diffusion for gaseous exchange    [1 mark]

    ***Type II pneumocytes:*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ microvilli to increase surface area for secretion of surfactant    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ many Golgi apparatus **OR** extensive endoplasmic reticulum **OR** contain lamellar bodies to produce surfactants            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ surfactant reduces surface tension **OR** to prevent alveolar collapse        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can regenerate **OR** can differentiate into type I pneumocytes    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Type I** pneumocytes have a **flattened shape** with **minimal organelles** to **reduce** the **distance** that **gases** have to **diffuse** between the inside of the alveoli and the blood ($\checkmark$), and have a **large surface area** to **maximise the rate** of **gaseous exchange** ($\checkmark$). **Type II** pneumocytes are **cuboidal** with extensive **rough endoplasmic reticulum** and many **Golgi apparatus**. This is because **type II** pneumocytes are adapted to **produce surfactants** ($\checkmark$) to **reduce** the **surface tension** inside the alveoli ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ intercostal muscles work in antagonistic muscle pairs        [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ internal intercostal muscles contract **WHILE** external intercostal muscles relax          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribcage is pulled downwards and inwards            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ decreasing the volume of the thorax                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ air particles get pushed close together **OR** pressure inside the thorax increases     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ air moves out of the lungs         [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During expiration, the **internal** intercostal muscles **contract** while the **external** intercostals **relax** ($\checkmark$). This action pulls the ribcage **inwards** and **downwards** ($\checkmark$), **decreasing the volume** ($\checkmark$) of the thoracic cavity. As the volume decreases, the **pressure** inside the thoracic cavity increases ($\checkmark$), forcing **air out** of the lungs ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ action potential passes down T-tubules of sarcolemma **OR** nerve fibre membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions bind to troponin             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium attachment to troponin causes a movement of tropomyosin, exposing the binding sites on actin        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads attach to the binding sites on actin **OR** cross-bridges form between actin and myosin        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads release ADP, and rotate **OR** carry out power stroke, pulling the actin filament inwards            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP attaches to the myosin head and breaks the cross-bridge    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis of ATP moves the myosin head into the cocked **OR** ready position    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ repeat of the process shortens the sarcomere        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the Z lines are pulled closer together                [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When an action potential passes down the **T-tubules** of the **muscle fibre membrane** ($\checkmark$), **calcium ions** are released from the **sarcoplasmic reticulum** ($\checkmark$). **Calcium ions bind** to a protein called **troponin** ($\checkmark$) in the sarcomere. This causes troponin to **move** another protein, **tropomyosin** ($\checkmark$), away from the myosin binding sites on actin. **Myosin heads** bind to these **sites** forming a **cross-bridge** ($\checkmark$). **ADP** on the myosin heads is **released** ($\checkmark$) and the **heads rotate** through about 45$\degree$ in a **power stroke** ($\checkmark$), pulling on the actin ($\checkmark$). **ATP then attaches** to myosin heads, breaking the cross-bridge ($\checkmark$). **Hydrolysis of ATP** moves the myosin heads into the **cocked position** ($\checkmark$). This process repeats until the calcium ions are no longer available. **Repeating** this process **shortens the sarcomeres** ($\checkmark$) in a myofibril and causes **muscle contraction**.
