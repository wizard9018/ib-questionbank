1. Suggest how polyploidy may occur in plants.    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **one**  of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ hybridisation events between closely related plant species **OR** gametes of two closely related plant species fuse        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ extra set of chromosomes formed during DNA replication stage in interphase **OR** cell cycle [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a diploid gamete fuses with a haploid gamete **OR** two diploid gametes fuse together [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ failed spindle formation **OR** non-disjunction **OR** failed separation of chromosomes **IN** meiosis leading to gametes with extra set of chromosomes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chromosomal duplications in cells involved in vegetative **OR** asexual reproduction [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Polyploidy can occur in plants if there are **hybridisation** events between **closely related plant** species ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ organisms with a different number of chromosomes **OR** new tetraploid and original diploid cannot breed together to produce fertile offspring    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ results in reproductive isolation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polyploid plants may be larger **OR** more fertile **OR** better adapted than original populations allowing them to exploit different ecological niches leading to geographical isolation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ once isolated polyploid population diverges genetically from original populations    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The new polyploid organism has a different number of chromosomes than the original plant and so they **cannot breed** together to produce **fertile offspring** ($\checkmark$). This can result in **reproductive isolation** ($\checkmark$), which could lead to the formation of a new species.

3. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ plant stems **OR** *Allium oleraceum* stems respond to light by growing towards it **OR** plant stems are positively phototropic            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin is produced by actively dividing cells in the tip of the stem **OR** in the meristem     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ light causes auxin to accumulate on the shaded side of the stem     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin efflux pumps bring about increased concentration on shaded side    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this causes cells on the shaded side to elongate                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin causes protons to be pumped from cytoplasm of cells into cell wall lowering pH    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lowering of pH loosens linkages between cellulose fibres so when water enters cell wall stretches and cell elongates    [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    *Allium oleraceum* has a **positive phototropic** response to light ($\checkmark$).  **Auxin** is released from the **actively dividing cells** of the **meristem** ($\checkmark$). Light stimulates auxin to **accumulate** on the **shaded** side of the **stem** ($\checkmark$), so that **cells** on the **shaded side elongate** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **two**  of the following:\
    *Answer must include statements supporting bulbs being a sink and supporting bulbs being a source to gain full marks*

    **Bulbs are a source because…**\
    $\ce{ -> }$ when the plant is not actively photosynthesising, it uses sucrose **OR** starch **OR** carbohydrate **OR** nutrients **OR** amino acids stored in bulb        [1 mark]

    **Bulbs are a sink because…**\
    $\ce{ -> }$ when the plant is actively photosynthesising it will provide carbohydrates **OR** glucose **OR** sucrose **OR** starch, for storage in **OR** growth of, bulb    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    At the **beginning** of the **growing** season, the bulb is a **source** because the plant is **not** actively **photosynthesising**, so it needs to use the **sucrose and amino acids stored in the bulbs** for **growth** ($\checkmark$).   However, during the **middle** of the growing season, when the plant is **actively photosynthesising**, **sucrose** is **transported** from the **leaves to the bulb** to **store** the **excess carbohydrate** and so the bulb becomes a **sink** ($\checkmark$).
