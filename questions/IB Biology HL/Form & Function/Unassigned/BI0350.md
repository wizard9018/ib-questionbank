# BI0350

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast the phloem found in the stem and the root of a plant.       [3 marks]

2. Outline how root pressure is generated through the transport of minerals.       [5 marks]

3. Explain the changes in hydrostatic pressure within the phloem that facilitate the transport of phloem sap from sources to sinks.  [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following (award 2 marks maximum if no contrast given):

    $\ce{ -> }$ phloem transports organic molecules **OR** amino acids **OR** sap **OR** sucrose in **BOTH** the root and stem  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phloem is part of **OR** found in the vascular bundle in **BOTH** the stem and root  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phloem is positioned outside the xylem in **BOTH** stem and roots  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phloem (as part of vascular bundles) is distributed **OR** scattered **OR** spread out **OR** located around the edge in the stem **BUT** located at the centre of roots   [1 mark]
[3 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    In the stem and the root, the phloem is responsible for the transport of **organic molecules** ($\checkmark$) such as sucrose and is found as part of the **vascular bundle** ($\checkmark$). In the root, the phloem is located near the centre, however, it is more **spread out** in the stem ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ endodermal cells contain suberin **OR** have a Casparian strip [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ waterproof layer blocks movement through cell walls (apoplast pathway) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water **AND** ions are forced through cytoplasm **OR** across cell membranes (**OR** symplast pathway) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ensures regulated entry of water **AND** ions **OR** prevents uncontrolled loss from the stele [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ endodermal cells pump **OR** actively transport mineral ions into xylem [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ion accumulation lowers water potential **OR** increases solute concentration inside xylem [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water enters xylem down water potential gradient **OR** by osmosis. [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ influx of water generates positive pressure potential **OR** hydrostatic pressure [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Endodermal cells contain a **Casparian strip** ($\checkmark$) that is waterproof and **blocks** the **apoplast pathway** ($\checkmark$), **forcing water** and **ions** to move through the **symplast pathway** ($\checkmark$). Endodermal cells then **actively transport ions into** the **xylem** ($\checkmark$), **increasing** its **solute potential** ($\checkmark$) causing **water** to **enter by osmosis** and generate a **positive pressure** known as root pressure ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ sources synthesize organic compounds   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organic compounds **OR** named example, are loaded into phloem **OR** sieve tubes, at              sources       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ solute concentration increases (in the sieve tubes at sources)      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water from the xylem moves into the sieve tube **OR** phloem by osmosis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in hydrostatic pressure in the phloem  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sinks act as organic compound storage **OR** non-synthesising regions        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organic compounds, **OR** named example, are unloaded from phloem, **OR** sieve tubes  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ decreased solute concentration (in sieve tubes at sinks)       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves out of the sieve tube into the xylem by osmosis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrostatic pressure decreases     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrostatic pressure is the pressure exerted by a fluid as a result of gravity [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrostatic pressure gradient (as a result of the incompressibility of water) allows the sap to flow from source to sink **OR** differences in hydrostatic pressure between phloem at sources and sinks allows transport of sap        [1 mark]
[8 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Sources are areas of the plant which can synthesise **organic compounds** ($\checkmark$) such as sucrose. At sources, sucrose is **loaded** into the phloem ($\checkmark$), which creates a **high solute concentration** ($\checkmark$). This causes water to move, by **osmosis, into the sieve tubes** from xylem vessels ($\checkmark$). The flow of water into the sieve tubes increases the **hydrostatic pressure** ($\checkmark$) which causes water to **flow to areas of lower pressure** ($\checkmark$), such as sinks. Sinks are areas of the plant which **don't synthesise** organic molecules ($\checkmark$), such as the roots. At sinks, sucrose is **unloaded** from the sieve tubes ($\checkmark$), which **decreases the solute concentration** ($\checkmark$). This leads to water moving back into the xylem vessels by osmosis and as a result, the **hydrostatic pressure** in the sieve tubes **decreases** ($\checkmark$).
