# BI2816

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Explain how xylem vessels are adapted for their role in plants.  [4 marks]

2. Draw an annotated plan of the vascular tissues, cortex and epidermis in a transverse section of a dicotyledonous stem.  [4 marks]

3. Describe the movement of water in plants, from soil to atmosphere.    [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ no cytoplasm **OR** organelles for unobstructed water flow  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell walls thickened with lignin for structural support **OR** prevents collapse under tension  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pits in walls to allow lateral movement of water between adjacent xylem vessels **OR** into surrounding tissues  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cells joined end-to-end with no end walls to form a continuous tube for water transport [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lignin deposited in spiral **OR** ring patterns to maintais flexibility of the vessel **OR** allows plant growth   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ narrow diameter enhances capillary action **OR** water cohesion  [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Xylem vessels are made of dead cells **joined end to end**, forming long **tubes** with **no cytoplasm**, allowing for **unimpeded water flow** ($\checkmark$). Their **walls** are **thickened** with **lignin**, which **prevents collapse** and **provides support** ($\checkmark$). The presence of **pits** in the walls enables **water** to **move sideways between vessels** ($\checkmark$), and **spiral** or **ringed lignin** allows **flexibility** so the stem can bend without breaking ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ water enters root hair cells from the soil by osmosis due to a water potential gradient    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large surface area of root hairs increases rate of water uptake      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves across the root cortex via the apoplast **OR** symplast pathways (*no longer needed from May 2028*)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ casparian strip blocks the apoplast pathway, forcing water into the symplast **OR** allowing selective uptake into the xylem (*no longer needed from May 2028*)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves up the xylem due to cohesion between water molecules  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adhesion between water molecules and xylem vessel walls helps maintain the column of water  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transpiration at the leaves creates negative pressure that pulls water up the xylem [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water evaporates from the cell walls of mesophyll cells into the air spaces of the leaf     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water vapour diffuses out of the leaf through the stomata down a water potential gradient   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ stomata regulate the rate of water loss depending on environmental conditions [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Water **enters root hair cells** from the soil by **osmosis** due to a **water potential gradient** between the **soil** and the **cytoplasm** of the root hair cells ($\checkmark$). The **large surface area** of **root hairs** **increases** the rate of water **uptake** ($\checkmark$). Water moves across the root cortex  towards the xylem ($\checkmark$). Within the xylem, water is **pulled upward** due to **cohesion** between **water molecules** ($\checkmark$) and **adhesion** to the **walls** of the **xylem vessels** ($\checkmark$).

    **Transpiration** at the leaves creates **negative pressure**, **drawing water upward** ($\checkmark$). Water **evaporates** from the **surface of mesophyll cell walls** into **air spaces** ($\checkmark$), and water **vapour** then **diffuses out** of the **stomata** down a **water potential gradient** into the atmosphere ($\checkmark$).
