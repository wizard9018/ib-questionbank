# BI2413

**Maximum mark: 8**

Haem is the non-protein, iron-binding component found in haemoglobin.  Haem and chlorophyll are similar in structure but chlorophyll contains a magnesium ion instead of iron. The biochemical pathways that make haem and chlorophyll involve enzymes that are highly conserved across many distantly related species.  The diagram shows an amino acid sequence alignment for part of one such enzyme, delta-aminolevulinate dehydratase. Each amino acid is represented by a single-letter code.

1. The diagram suggests that certain metabolic pathways are conserved across diverse organisms. Discuss how these conserved pathways provide evidence for LUCA.      [2 marks]

2. Suggest two environmental conditions found in hydrothermal vents that may have facilitated the evolution of early metabolic pathways.        [2 marks]

3. Explain how genomic analysis can be used to estimate the time period in which the last universal common ancestor (LUCA) existed.        [2 marks]

4. Scientists discovered a bacterial enzyme with a significantly different amino acid sequence from its human counterpart but nearly identical function.  Suggest possible reasons why this function is conserved despite sequence divergence.     [2 marks]

1. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ conserved metabolic pathways **OR** similar enzymes in conserved pathways suggest a shared evolutionary origin **OR** fundamental processes like glycolysis **OR** ATP synthesis **OR** protein translation are found in all life forms    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ highly conserved pathways demonstrate biochemical efficiency favoured in natural selection **OR** strong selective pressure     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the lack of any other widespread metabolic mechanisms suggests competition and extinction of other forms of life    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Many **fundamental processes** like **glycolysis** are **conserved** across **all known life forms** ($\checkmark$).  This indicates a shared evolutionary origin and supports the idea of LUCA (the last universal common ancestor).  The **lack of other metabolic mechanisms suggests** that they may have existed in early species but were outcompeted and **became extinct** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ heat energy to increase the rate of chemical reactions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high pressure at thermal vents stabilised complex chemical reactions that could not occur in less extreme environments    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ minerals to serve as catalysts promoting biochemical reactions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mineral-rich vents may have mimicked cell membranes, concentrating molecules and enabling the development of metabolic pathways    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ proton gradients **OR** pH gradient for ATP synthesis via chemiosmosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy sources such as hydrogen **OR** methane **OR** sulfide gases as electron donors for metabolic reactions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ highly reduced compounds from vents coming into contact with the ocean’s oxidised compounds provided redox gradients driving early metabolism    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lack of oxygen prevented oxidative damage to early organic molecules **OR** supported anaerobic metabolism development    [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The heat and high-pressure environment of the hydrothermal vents may have enabled the evolution of metabolic pathways as **heat energy increases** the **rate** of **chemical reactions** ($\checkmark$) and **high pressure stabilises complex chemical reactions** ($\checkmark$) that may not have been able to occur in less extreme environments.

3. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ phylogenetic trees constructed from genomic data illustrate evolutionary relationships    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ comparing conserved genetic sequences helps infer evolutionary timelines, as more conserved sequences indicate older ancestry    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ genomic evidence combined with other evidence **OR** fossils helps establish LUCA’s timefram    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ molecular clocks estimate divergence time by assuming mutations accumulate at a constant rate
    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ highly conserved ribosomal RNA **OR** rRNA sequences are useful for estimating divergence events
 [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Phylogenetic trees**, constructed from genomic data, illustrate **evolutionary relationships**, helping estimate when LUCA existed by identifying **divergence points** ($\checkmark$)  **Molecular clocks** further refine this by comparing **genetic differences** and applying a **known mutation rate** to infer the **timing** of common ancestry ($\checkmark$).

4. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ proteins with different primary sequences can fold into similar three-dimensional conformations **OR** structures    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ an enzyme's function depends on its 3-D structure    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ variations in sites other than the active site may not affect function    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ convergence in evolution can lead to functionally similar enzymes despite a difference in sequence    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some amino acid substitutions are neutral and do not alter protein folding **OR** stability **OR** active site configuration    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ other structural adaptations **OR** flexible binding sites **OR** other suitable named adaptation allow variations in sequence without affecting overall enzyme function    [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Proteins with **different primary sequences** fold into **similar three-dimensional structures** ($\checkmark$). Since an **enzyme’s function** depends on its **3D structure, especially** the shape of the **active site** ($\checkmark$), as long as it has a **similar structure**, it should be able to perform a **similar function** ($\checkmark$).
