 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
When testosterone reaches its target cell it **diffuses** through the **cell membrane** ($\checkmark$) into the cytoplasm of the cell. Once there it attaches to a **specific intracellular receptor** ($\checkmark$), forming a **hormone-receptor complex** ($\checkmark$). The conformational change of this complex enables it to **enter the nucleus** ($\checkmark$) where it **binds** to the **promoter region** of the target genes ($\checkmark$), bringing about **transcription**, and enabling the production of specific **proteins** to bring about the **response** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following (should include **AT LEAST ONE** for cellulose and **AT LEAST ONE** for glycogen):

 *Cellulose:*\
 $\ce{ -> }$ cellulose is made up of the monomer $\beta$-glucose [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ cellulose forms straight chains because the $\beta$-glucose are oriented alternately upwards and downwards with $\beta$-1,4 linkages [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the straight chains in cellulose provide structural support [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ $\beta$-glucose molecules in cellulose form hydrogen bonds between polymers, which form microfibrils [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ cellulose microfibrils have a high tensile strength which makes the cell wall strong [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ high tensile strength of cellulose prevents the cell from bursting under osmotic pressure [1 mark]

 *Glycogen:*\
 $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen is made up of $\alpha$-glucose molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ $\alpha$-glucose molecules are oriented the same way, resulting in a curved/twisted structure [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the compact structure of glycogen means a lot can be stored in a small space [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen is branched so that glucose can quickly be released for respiration [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ glycogen does not have a fixed size so extra molecules of glucose can be added or taken away as needed [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** cellulose and glycogen are insoluble in water due to their sizes so do not affect the osmotic pressure of the cell [1 mark]

 [8 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Cellulose is made up of **$\beta$-glucose** ($\checkmark$) molecules oriented alternately upwards and downwards which results in a **straight chain** ($\checkmark$) and the **formation of hydrogen bonds**, contributing to the formation of **microfibrils** ($\checkmark$). Microfibrils have a **high tensile strength** which contributes to a **strong cell wall** ($\checkmark$). The **high tensile strength** ($\checkmark$) of microfibrils prevents the cell from bursting **under osmotic pressure** ($\checkmark$).

 Glycogen is made up of **$\alpha$-glucose molecules** ($\checkmark$), all oriented the same way, giving it a **twisted structure** ($\checkmark$), which means it can be **stored in a small space** ($\checkmark$). **Glycogen is branched** so that it can quickly be **digested by enzymes** and does not have a **fixed size** ($\checkmark$), so glucose molecules can easily be **added and taken away** ($\checkmark$). Both cellulose and glycogen are **insoluble in water** so do not affect the **osmotic pressure** of the cell ($\checkmark$).
