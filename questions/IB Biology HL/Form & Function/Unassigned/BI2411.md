# BI2411

**Maximum mark: 10**

A student wanted to compare the stomatal density on the leaves of two different species of tree. They took impressions of the lower surface of the leaves using clear nail varnish. Below are the student's diagrams of two of the impressions, examined at a magnification of x400.

![stomatal density drawing species A and B](<images/stomatal density drawing species A and B.png>)

The length of each side of the fields of view shown above is 250μm.
1. For species B calculate the number of stomata per mm$^{2}$ and show your working. [2 marks]

The student then removed four more leaves from each tree and spread a waterproofing agent onto the top surface of each leaf. The leaves were then hung from a piece of string tied between two clamp stands as shown below.

![drying leaves on washing line chemix](<images/drying leaves on washing line chemix.png>)

The leaves were then weighed at regular intervals, and their masses were recorded. The percentage change in mass of each leaf was calculated. The mean results for each species are shown below.

![graph to show percentage decrease in mass of leaves](<images/graph to show percentage decrease in mass of leaves.png>)

2. Explain why the percentage change in mass was recorded rather than just the change in mass. [2 marks]

3. Identify **one** factor, not already mentioned that would need to be controlled in this experiment. [1 mark]

4. Calculate the difference in percentage change in mass between species A and species B after 30 hours. [1 mark]

5. Explain why the greater stomatal density of species B leads to the results shown in the graph. [1 mark]

6. Suggest one reason why the rate of mass lost decreases towards the end of the experiment. [1 mark]

7. When transpiration is not enough to bring about the movement of water through the plant, root pressure is required. Describe how root pressure is generated. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. **both** of the following (*units required to get full marks*):

 $\ce{ -> }$ size of field of view = 250μm x 250μm = 0.25mm x 0.25mm = 0.0625mm$^{2}$ **OR** 1mm$^{2}$/0.0625mm$^{2}$ = 16 **OR** number stomata in 1mm$^{2}$ = 12 x 16 [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ 192 stomata mm$^{-2}$ [1 mark]

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Number of stomata in field of view = 12

 Size of field of view = 250μm x 250μm = 0.25mm x 0.25mm = 0.0625mm$^{2}$

 1mm$^{2}$/0.0625mm$^{2}$ = 16

 If there are 12 stomata in 0.0625mm$^{2}$

 Number of stomata in 1mm$^{2}$ = **12 x 16** ($\checkmark$)= **192 mm$^{-2}$** ($\checkmark$)

2. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ leaves may be of different lengths, **OR** widths **OR** sizes **OR** masses [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ it provides a standardised measure by expressing the change in proportion of the starting mass [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows for comparison between the changes in mass [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ percentage change reduces the impact of minor measurement inaccuracies as it is expressed as a ratio [1 mark]

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The change in mass was calculated as a percentage because the leaves may have had **different starting masses** ($\checkmark$), and percentage change allows for **direct comparisons** ($\checkmark$) between the two species despite these differences.

5. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ greater rate of evaporation of water **OR** transpiration from the leaves of species B compared to species A [1 mark]

 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The greater stomatal density in the lower epidermis of species B leads to a **higher rate of water loss** through **evaporation** ($\checkmark$), resulting in a greater percentage change in mass.

6. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ decreased water availability inside the leaves **OR** decreased difference in water concentration gradient between inside the leaf and surrounding air [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ fewer stomata open **OR** stomatal opening becomes less **OR** stomata not open as wide [1 mark]

 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The decrease in mass loss towards the end of the experiment may be due to **fewer stomata being open** ($\checkmark$) as a response to water stress.

7. any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ Casparian **OR** suberin strip in endodermal cells blocks apoplast pathway, forcing water to move through symplast pathway (*not needed from May 2028*) [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ active transport of mineral ions **OR** salts **OR** potassium ions **OR** nitrate ions **OR** any other suitable named mineral ion into the xylem by root **OR** endodermal cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mineral ions **OR** named mineral ion **OR** salts lower water potential in the xylem so water enters by osmosis from surrounding root cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ positive pressure potential in xylem develops, pushing water upwards [1 mark]

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Endodermal cells actively transport mineral ions into the xylem ($\checkmark$), so that water follows, generating positive pressure potential that pushes the water upwards ($\checkmark$).
