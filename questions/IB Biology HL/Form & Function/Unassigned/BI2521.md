# BI2521

**Maximum mark: 11**

A student is interested in comparing the protein concentration in cow’s milk and oat milk. They conduct a Biuret test (a chemical test that detects the presence of proteins in a sample) and use a spectrophotometer to measure their results.

If proteins are present in the milk sampled, copper ions in the Biuret reagent will bind to the nitrogen atoms of peptide bonds in the milk and cause a colour change from blue to purple. The intensity of the purple colour is directly proportional to the protein concentration in the sample.

The Biuret reaction forms a complex between copper ions and peptide bonds in the protein.

![BI2521a](images/BI2521a.png)

540 nm is the optimal wavelength for measuring the purple colour formed in the Biuret test, as it corresponds to the absorption peak of the copper-protein complex.

The student uses bovine serum albumin (BSA) to generate a standard curve that can be used to approximate the concentration of protein in the milk samples.
