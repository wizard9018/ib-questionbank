Scientists can infer the dietary habits of animals by examining their skull structure and dentition.  The images show six different mammal species.

1. Suggest what the differences in incisor shape between the Siberian Musk Deer and the Coyote indicate about their diets.     [2 marks]

2. Other than incisors, describe **one** observable feature in the dentition of the Domestic Cat that suggests a carnivorous diet.       [2 marks]

3. Explain a reason for the differing eye position in the skull of the Domestic Cat and the Siberian Musk Deer.         [1 mark]

4. The Siberian Musk Deer, Green Monkey, and Warthog all possess prominent canine teeth or tusks largely unrelated to diet.  Suggest two possible functions of these canines/tusks which are unrelated to diet.       [2 marks]

5. Besides dentition, describe **one** other source of evidence that palaeontologists can use to reconstruct the diets of extinct animals.         [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. **both** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the deer has broad, sloping incisors **WHILE** the coyote has smaller conical **OR** sharper incisors        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the deer incisors are more suitable for a herbivorous diet **OR** shearing vegetation WHILE the coyote incisors are adapted for grasping **OR** tearing small items, suggesting a carnivorous diet       [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **deer** has incisors that are **broad** and **slope outwards**, while the **coyote's** incisors are **small** and **sharp** ($\checkmark$).  The **coyote's** incisors make it more adapted to **grabbing, tearing, and eating meat**, while the deer's incisors help the **deer shear vegetation** to eat it ($\checkmark$).

2. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ large, pointed canines for incapacitating prey **OR** piercing **OR** killing prey        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sharp premolars **OR** molars for tearing meat      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced **OR** absent flat grinding surfaces **OR** molars; not needed for carnivorous diet     [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The cat's **sharp molars** are adapted for **tearing** and **chewing** raw **meat** ($\checkmark$) while that would not be possible with **flat molars** that are more suitable for **grinding vegetation** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ Domestic Cats are predators, so their eyes are positioned forward, providing depth perception and accuracy of distance, which is important for acting on prey **OR** forward-facing eyes (cat) increase overlap of the visual fields, improving focus on a single target     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  The Siberian Musk Deer is prey, and has side-facing eyes giving them a wide field of view, which is important in detecting predators **OR** side-facing eyes increases peripheral vision, helping them escape threats      [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The Siberian Musk Deer are **prey animals**, so their eyes are on the **side** of the skull, allowing them to **detect predators** ($\checkmark$).
The Domestic Cat is a **predator**, so their eyes are positioned on the **front**, giving them **binocular vision**, which is important for **stalking prey**
 ($\checkmark$).

4. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ fighting **OR** dominance **OR** aggression **OR** defence **OR** intraspecific competition [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sexual selection **OR** mating displays    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ digging **OR** foraging for food      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ territorial displays     [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Canines can be used in **fighting** against other members of the species for **dominance** ($\checkmark$) or for a **mate** ($\checkmark$).  The warthog can also use its tusks and canines for **digging**.

5. any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ fossilised stomach contents **OR** faeces    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ teeth wear patterns **OR** tooth microstructure      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ skeletal structure      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ analysis of nearby fossils      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ isotopic **OR** chemical analysis of teeth enamel  [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    To determine what a fossilised species may have eaten, scientists can look at **fossils** of the **stomach contents** or faeces that are found nearby ($\checkmark$).
