# BI0299

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the risk factors and consequences associated with plaque buildup in the coronary arteries. [4 marks]

2. Outline how the pressure changes in the left atrium and left ventricle affect the surrounding valves and blood flow.  [4 marks]
3. Explain how the structural features of the arteries, veins and capillaries are adapted to support their functions. [8 marks]

1. any **four** of the following (at least **one** from each of the causes and consequences):

    *Causes*

    $\ce{ -> }$ high blood pressure **OR** high blood cholesterol [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  smoking **OR** diabetes **OR** genetic factors [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a diet high in LDL cholesterol **OR** saturated fats **OR** trans fats[1 mark]

    *Consequences*

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood flow restriction in the arteries **OR** narrowing of the arteries [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  lack of oxygen     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ heart inflammation **OR** angina [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ decreases the ability of the cardiac muscle to contract    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ stimulates the formation of blood clots in the artery [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ heart attack  [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Occlusion is caused by the **buildup of fatty plaques** in the inner lining of the coronary arteries ($\checkmark$) which could be the effect of **smoking** ($\checkmark$) or **high blood pressure** ($\checkmark$). As a consequence, it can **restrict blood flow to the arteries** ($\checkmark$) and lead to **chest pain** ($\checkmark$).

2.  any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ when pressure increases in the left atrium **OR** pressure in the atrium is higher than the ventricle the atrioventricular **OR** bicuspid valve opens [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood flows from the left atrium to the left ventricle [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when the pressure increases in the left ventricle **OR** the pressure in the left ventricle is higher than the left atrium, the atrioventricular **OR** bicuspid valves close  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when the pressure increases in the left ventricle **OR** the pressure inside the left ventricle is higher than the aorta, the semilunar **OR** aortic valves open [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ blood flows from the left ventricle to the aorta [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    **Pressure** in the **left atrium increases** when the left atrium contracts, which causes the **atrioventricular** valve to **open** ($\checkmark$), allowing **blood** to flow **into the left ventricle** ($\checkmark$). The **pressure** in the **left ventricle increases** when the left ventricle contracts causing the **semilunar** valve to **open** ($\checkmark$) and the **atrioventricular valve to close** ($\checkmark$). This prevents the backflow of blood into the atrium from the ventricle. With the increase in pressure in the left ventricle, the **blood** is pumped **from the ventricle to the aorta** ($\checkmark$).

3.    any **eight** of the following (answer should contain at least **one** statement from each category, both the feature and explanation need to be present for each marking point)

    *Arteries*

    $\ce{ -> }$ thick walls to withstand high pressure **OR** prevent rupturing [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ walls have elastic fibers to allow them to, stretch **OR** expand, and recoil in response to, pulsatile blood flow **OR** heart beat  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ muscular walls for vasoconstriction **OR** vasodilation **OR** to regulate blood pressure **OR** control distribution of blood [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ narrow lumen helps maintain blood pressure [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ smooth inner lining, **OR** endothelium, to reduce friction [1 mark]

    *Capillaries*

    $\ce{ -> }$ thin walls **OR** one cells thick walls help to increase diffusion rate **OR** reduce diffusion distance between the blood and cells **OR** tissues [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ small diameter to allow passage of one blood cell at a time **OR** to optimize exchange [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ may contain pores, **OR** fenestrations,  to aid transport of materials [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ dense network, **OR** large surface area, to supply cells **OR** tissues with materials **OR** nutrients [1 mark]

    *Veins*

    $\ce{ -> }$ thin walls, **OR** close proximity to skeletal muscles, so muscle contraction can help support blood flow     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced elasticity (compared to arteries) due to lower pressure blood [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ valves to prevent the backflow of blood     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ wider lumen (compared to arteries) helps to reduce friction **OR** maximize blood flow [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    Arteries are adapted to suit their function because they have **thick walls** which help to **withstand the high pressure** blood they transport ($\checkmark$), which is also maintained by their **narrow lumen** ($\checkmark$). Their walls also have **elastic fibers** to allow them to **stretch and recoil** in response to **pulsatile blood flow**.  ($\checkmark$).
Arteries branch into arterioles, which branch into capillaries, which are the site of exchange between the blood and surrounding tissue. They are adapted for their function because they have very **thin walls**, which **decreases the diffusion distance** ($\checkmark$) for nutrients to travel and they have a very **dense network** to support the **delivery of nutrients** to all of the tissues ($\checkmark$).
Veins return blood from the tissues to the heart and they are adapted to their role because they have **valves to prevent the backflow** of blood ($\checkmark$) which is traveling at lower pressure and often against gravity. They also have a **wider lumen** which helps to maximize blood flow by **reducing friction** ($\checkmark$) and their walls are **less elastic compared to arteries**, because blood is flowing at a **lower pressure** ($\checkmark$).
