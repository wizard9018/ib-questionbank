# BI0331

What distinguishes the tertiary structure of a protein from the secondary structure?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ The secondary structure includes hydrogen bonds between different amino acids.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ The secondary structure does not contain bonds between the R- groups of amino acids.

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ The secondary structure has peptide bonds between amino acids in the polypeptide chain.

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The secondary structure contains disulfide bridges.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

The secondary structure (α-helices and β-pleated sheets) is stabilised by hydrogen bonds between the backbone atoms of the polypeptide chain. It does not involve interactions between the side chains (R-groups).

The tertiary structure is the overall 3D folding of a protein and is stabilised by interactions between R-groups, including hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions, and disulfide bridges.

Therefore, the key distinction is that tertiary structure depends on R-group interactions, whereas secondary structure does not. **Choice B**  is correct.

**Choice A**  is incorrect because hydrogen bonds occur in both secondary and tertiary structures. \

**Choice C**  is incorrect because peptide bonds exist in all protein structures, so this is not unique to secondary structure. \

**Choice D**  is incorrect because disulfide bridges are part of the tertiary structure, not the secondary.
