# BI1088

**Maximum mark: 9**

The properties of proteins influence the role they play within the body. The diagram below shows an integral protein in the plasma membrane of a cell.

![BI1088](images/BI1088.png)

1.
 1. Describe the influence of polarity on the position of amino acids within integral proteins. [3 marks]

 2. Other than polarity, state another chemical property of amino acids that can vary due to charge. [1 mark]

3. Using named examples, distinguish between conjugated and non-conjugated proteins. [2 marks]

4. Outline the role of interactions and bonding in the determination of protein 3D structure. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
 1. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ polar amino acids are hydrophilic **OR** interact favourably with water molecules **OR** are attracted to water molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ non-polar amino acids are hydrophobic **OR** are repelled by water molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in integral proteins, hydrophobic amino acids help to embed them in the membrane **OR** hydrophobic amino acids are found in regions embedded within the membrane **OR** located within hydrophobic interior of lipid bilayer [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in integral proteins, hydrophilic amino acids are often found in the regions exposed to the cytoplasm **OR** extracellular fluid **OR** aqueous environment inside and outside cell [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Polar amino acids are **hydrophilic** ($\checkmark$), whereas non-polar amino acids are **hydrophobic** ($\checkmark$). In regards to integral proteins, the regions that are **exposed to the cytoplasm** and **extracellular fluid** contain amino acids that are **hydrophilic** ($\checkmark$), but the regions that are **embedded** within the membrane have amino acids that are **hydrophobic** ($\checkmark$).

 2. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ hydrophilic amino acids can be acidic or basic [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ amino acids can be hydrophobic or hydrophilic [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ can be aromatic or non-aromatic [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ functional groups [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ redox properties [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Hydrophilic amino acids, depending on their R-groups, can be **acidic or basic** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ example of conjugated protein e.g. haemoglobin [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ example of non-conjugated protein e.g. collagen [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ conjugated proteins are proteins that are associated with other chemical groups **OR** are composed of proteins and non-proteins **WHEREAS** non-conjugated proteins are not associated with other chemical groups [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Conjugated proteins**, such as **haemoglobin** ($\checkmark$) are proteins that are associated with other chemical groups for example iron in haemoglobin. **Non-conjugated proteins**, such as **collagen** ($\checkmark$), are **not associated with other chemical groups** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ peptide bonds form between amino acids to give primary structure that determines overall 3D structure [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen bonding in the formation of protein secondary structure **OR** formation of alpha helices **OR** beta-pleated sheets **OR** hydrogen bonding stabilising repeating patterns of secondary structures [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ tertiary structure is formed by interactions between amino acid R-groups [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen bonds between polar amino acids in the formation of tertiary structure [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ionic bonding in the formation of protein tertiary structure **OR** ionic bonding between oppositely charged amino acids [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ disulfide covalent bonding in the formation of protein tertiary structure OR disulfide bonds between pairs of cysteines [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ hydrophobic interactions result in hydrophobic amino acids being orientated toward the inside of the protein molecule **OR** non-polar side chains cluster in protein’s core to minimise contact with water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ quaternary strucure stabilised by hydrogen **OR** ionic **OR** disulfide bonds **OR** similar interactions to tertiary structure but between different polypeptide subunits [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Hydrogen bonding** is involved in stabilising the protein’s **secondary structure**, such as the formation of alpha-helices and beta-pleated sheets ($\checkmark$). In the tertiary structure of proteins, **hydrogen bonds** can form between **polar amino acids** ($\checkmark$), **ionic bonds** can also form between oppositely **charged amino acids** ($\checkmark$) and **disulfide bonds** can form between sulfur atoms in **cysteine** R groups.
