# BI2380

Which amino acid in a membrane protein is likely to be oriented close to the centre of the membrane bilayer?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because alanine’s R group is an hydrocarbon-only side chain (a simple methyl group, $\ce{-CH_3}$). This gives alanine’s R group a hydrophobic character which allows it to form hydrophobic bonds with the largely hydrophobic phospholipid tails at the centre of a typical membrane. Membrane-bound proteins will often have a high proportion of hydrophobic amino acids situated close to the centre of the phospholipid bilayer.

**Choice B** is incorrect because aspartic acid has an acidic R group ($\ce{-CH_2-COOH}$), which ionises and becomes negatively charged. This makes it hydrophilic and more likely to be found in regions of the protein that interact with aqueous environments, such as the cytoplasm or extracellular fluid.

**Choice C** is incorrect because serine is classified as a polar amino acid because its side chain, –CH₂OH, contains a hydroxyl group that creates a dipole, enabling it to form hydrogen bonds with water molecules. This hydrogen bonding makes serine hydrophilic, in contrast to hydrophobic amino acids

**Choice D** is incorrect because lysine has a basic R group ($\ce{-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-CH_2-NH_2}$), which ionises to become positively charged. As a hydrophilic amino acid, lysine is more likely to be found in protein regions interacting with aqueous surroundings, not in the hydrophobic core of the membrane.
