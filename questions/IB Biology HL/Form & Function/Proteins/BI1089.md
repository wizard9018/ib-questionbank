# BI1089

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the chemical properties of amino acid R-groups based on their charge.[4 marks]

2. With reference to bonding and association, compare and contrast conjugated and non-conjugated proteins.     [5 marks]

3. Describe the four levels of protein structure.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ non-polar R-groups are hydrophobic    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polar R-groups are hydrophilic         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ positively charged  R-groups are basic     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ negatively charged R-groups are acidic    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ amphoteric R-groups can be positively, negatively or neutrally charged **OR** change charge depending on pH            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neutral polar R-groups are partially positively and partially negatively charged **OR** have polar functional groups        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Non-polar R-groups are **hydrophobic** ($\checkmark$), and polar R-groups are **hydrophilic** ($\checkmark$). Hydrophilic R-groups can be **acidic (-'vely charge) ($\checkmark$) or basic (+'vely charged)** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to gain full marks*):

    ***Similarities*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are composed of amino acids joined together by peptide bonds    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can contain ionic bonds         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can contain alpha-helices **OR** beta-pleated sheets     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can contain disulfide covalent bonds [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can contain hydrogen bonds         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can contain hydrophobic interactions [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can be fibrous **OR** globular             [1 mark]

    ***Differences*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ conjugated proteins are associated with other chemical **OR** non-protein **OR** prosthetic groups **WHEREAS** non-conjugated proteins are not associated with other chemical **OR** non-protein **OR** prosthetic groups            [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    There are many similarities in the bonding of conjugated and non-conjugated proteins because they can both contain **ionic bonds** ($\checkmark$), **disulfide covalent bonds** ($\checkmark$), **hydrogen bonds** ($\checkmark$), and **hydrophobic interactions** ($\checkmark$). However, there are some differences in their associations because conjugated proteins are associated with other **chemical groups**, such as carbohydrates, whereas non-conjugated proteins are not ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ primary structure is the sequence of amino acids **OR** precise position of each amino acid       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ amino acids held together by covalent **OR** peptide bonds [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sequence of amino acids determines overall 3D shape of protein due to different R-groups   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ secondary structure has pleating and coiling **OR** local folding of polypeptide chain        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ alpha helices **OR** beta-pleated sheets formed by hydrogen bonding [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ all hydrogen bonding of secondary structure between non-adjacent amine and carboxyl groups in peptide bonds                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tertiary structure as a result of interactions between R-groups **OR** adjacent parts of the polypeptide chain            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ionic bonding as a result of charged R-groups [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pairs of cysteines can form disulfide bonds     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrophobic interactions responsible for protein folding **OR** hydrophobic regions are oriented towards the inside of the protein molecule **OR** hydrophobic amino acids can be found clustered together in the centre of globular proteins            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in integral proteins, hydrophilic amino acids can be found exposed to the cytoplasm or extracellular matrix, **OR** hydrophilic amino acids can be found exposed to aqueous solution     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in integral proteins, hydrophobic amino acids can be found embedded within the membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ quaternary structure is the association of two or more polypeptide chains     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ quaternary structure stabilised by ionic **OR** hydrogen **OR** disulfide **OR** similar interactions to tertiary structure, but between different polypeptide chains            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ conjugated proteins are associated with other chemical **OR** non-protein **OR** prosthetic groups **OR** non-conjugated proteins are not associated with other chemical **OR** non-protein **OR** prosthetic groups [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The primary structure of a protein refers to the **sequence of amino acids** ($\checkmark$) that make up the polypeptide chain and their precise position. The secondary structure of a protein is a result of hydrogen bonding between **non-adjacent carboxyl** and **amine groups** ($\checkmark$), resulting in the formation of **alpha-helices and beta-pleated sheets** ($\checkmark$). The tertiary structure of a protein is the result of interactions between the **R-groups** of amino acids ($\checkmark$). Charged R-groups can form **ionic bonds** ($\checkmark$). **Hydrophobic interactions** due to the presence of non-polar R-groups can also cause the protein to fold into different shapes ($\checkmark$). In integral proteins, hydrophobic amino acids help to **embed** them in the plasma membrane ($\checkmark$). The quaternary structure of a  protein is when it is composed of **more than one** polypeptide chain ($\checkmark$). These proteins are referred to as **conjugated** if they are associated with other chemical groups, such as carbohydrates or **non-conjugated** if they are not associated with other chemical groups ($\checkmark$).
