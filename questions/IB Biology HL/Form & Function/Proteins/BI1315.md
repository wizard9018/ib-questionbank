# BI1315

Which level of protein structure is represented by the area circled in the diagram below?

![1315a](images/1315a.png)

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Tertiary structure held together by a covalent bond

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Quaternary structure held together by a covalent bond

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Secondary structure held together by a hydrogen bond

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Tertiary structure held together by an ionic bond

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because there are two polypeptide chains held together by disulfide bonds. Disulfide bonds are covalent bonds that form between two sulfur atoms in the R groups of cysteine amino acids. These bonds help to hold the polypeptide chains together in the quaternary structure of a protein.

Although tertiary structures are held together by covalent bonds, a tertiary structure (**Choice A**) is the overall 3D shape of a single polypeptide chain. Although the α-helices and β-pleated sheets, which are secondary structures, are held together by hydrogen bonds (**Choice C**), the secondary structure of a protein again refers to a single polypeptide chain, and disulfide, not hydrogen bonds are shown in this diagram. Ionic bonds do help maintain the tertiary structure of a protein (**Choice D**), however, a tertiary structure refers to a single polypeptide chain, and the bonds shown in this diagram are disulfide, not ionic bonds.
