# BI0597

The secondary structure of a protein is a result of which of the following:

-    I. $\hspace{1em}$ Hydrogen bonding
-    II. $\hspace{.7em}$ Covalent bonding
-    III. $\hspace{.4em}$ Ionic bonding

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and II

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A** is correct because the secondary structure of a protein includes α-helices and β-pleated sheets stabilised by hydrogen bonds between the oxygen atoms of carbonyl (C=O) groups and the hydrogen atoms of N–H groups in the polypeptide backbone. These hydrogen bonds occur in regular patterns that stabilise the coiling and pleating of the chain.

**Choice B** is incorrect because covalent peptide bonds join amino acids into a polypeptide chain, but they are not the hydrogen bonds that stabilise its characteristic secondary folding. Covalent disulfide bonds between cysteine R-groups are associated with stabilisation of tertiary structure.

**Choice C** is incorrect because ionic interactions between charged R-groups contribute to tertiary structure rather than forming the regular backbone hydrogen-bonding pattern of α-helices and β-pleated sheets.

**Choice D** is incorrect because it includes both covalent and ionic bonding; only statement I identifies the characteristic stabilising interactions described in the question.
