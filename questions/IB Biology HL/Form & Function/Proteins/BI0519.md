# BI0519

Which characteristics distinguish between an alpha helix and a beta-pleated sheet?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | The positions of hydrogen bonds between different amino acids |
    | II. | The location of hydrophobic interactions between nonpolar R groups |
    | III. | The positions of disulfide bridges between cysteine residues |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Alpha helices and beta-pleated sheets are different types of secondary structures in proteins.

Both alpha helices and beta sheets hold their shapes due to the hydrogen bonds that form between the O atom of the carbonyl (C=O) group of one amino acid and the H atom in the amino (N-H) group of the next. However, the arrangement of these hydrogen bonds differs between alpha-helices and beta-pleated sheets.
- In an alpha helix, hydrogen bonds form between one amino acid and another that is four amino acids away (down the chain). This pattern pulls the chain into a helix.
- A beta-pleated sheet consists of pairs of polypeptide chains lying side by side. Hydrogen bonds form between the carbonyl group on one chain and the amino group on the adjacent chain.

Hydrophobic interactions between nonpolar R groups (**Statement II**) and disulfide bridges between cysteine residues (**Statement III**) are features of tertiary and quaternary structures, not secondary structures.
