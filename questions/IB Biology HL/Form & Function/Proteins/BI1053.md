3. any **seven** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ temperatures which are too high, **OR** above the optimum, can lead to denaturation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ pH levels which are too high **OR** too low, **OR** too different from the optimum pH, can lead to denaturation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ denaturation is a change in the 3-dimensional structure **OR** shape of a protein [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ temperature increases motion **OR** kinetic energy of molecules in protein [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ increased motion **OR** kinetic energy puts stress on bonds holding structure together [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ break hydrogen bonds so structure breaks down [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ change in pH can affect the charges holding protein structure together [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ protein cannot maintain **OR** take on normal shape [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ proteins shape **OR** structure is specific to its function [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ enzymes can’t bind to substrates when denatured **OR** other suitable example of a protein and its function being affected [1 mark]
 [7 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 When **temperature** increases **too far** above the optimal temperature for a protein, it **increases** the **kinetic energy of molecules** ($\checkmark$) in the protein, putting **stress** on the **bonds** holding the protein together ($\checkmark$), so **hydrogen bonds** begin to **break** ($\checkmark$) and the **3-dimensional structure breaks** down ($\checkmark$). A **pH** which is **too high** above, or **too low** below the optimum pH **affects the charges** ($\checkmark$) holding the protein structure together. The protein can **no longer** form its **normal shape** ($\checkmark$). As the structure of a protein is **specific to its function** ($\checkmark$), a change in the structure will not permit it to carry out its function. When the normal protein shape is disrupted, leading to loss of activity, the protein is said to be **denatured** ($\checkmark$).
