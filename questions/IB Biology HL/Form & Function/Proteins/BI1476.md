# BI1476

Which is an **incorrect** explanation for why a very low pH could cause a protein to denature?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Interactions between the R$-$groups could be changed

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ The primary structure of the protein could be affected

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Hydrogen bonds within the protein could be broken

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The folding patterns of polypeptides within the protein could change

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

Low pH can result in the denaturation of the protein, as the presence of hydrogen ions affects the bonds that hold the three dimensional structure of proteins together. The primary structure of a protein refers to the amino acid sequence.  pH does not affect the sequence of amino acids that form the polypeptide chain; **Choice B**  is correct.

The presence of hydrogen ions  can affect the interactions between R groups in proteins, which would change the three dimensional structure; **Choice A**  is incorrect. The presence of hydrogen ions would disrupt hydrogen bonds in the secondary structure of the protein (**Choice C**), which would affect the folding patterns of polypeptides within the protein (**Choice D**). **Choices C** and **D**  are incorrect.
