# BI0948

Which row correctly describes the formation of vesicles when phospholipids are mixed with water?

| | Structure of vesicle | Feature of fatty acid tail | Formation in water |
| --- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | monolayer | hydrophilic | spontaneous |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | bilayer | hydrophobic | spontaneous |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | monolayer | hydrophilic | induced |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | bilayer | hydrophobic | induced |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
When phospholipids are mixed with water, they can spontaneously assemble into bilayers because they are amphipathic. Their hydrophilic phosphate heads face the surrounding water and the vesicle's aqueous contents, while their hydrophobic fatty acid tails are positioned together within the membrane's centre, away from water. The bilayer can close to form a spherical vesicle without requiring external induction. Therefore, **Choice B** is correct.

**Choices A** and **C** are incorrect because vesicle membranes are phospholipid bilayers rather than monolayers, and fatty acid tails are hydrophobic rather than hydrophilic.
**Choice C** is incorrect because, although the membrane is a bilayer and its fatty acid tails are hydrophobic, its assembly is spontaneous rather than induced.
