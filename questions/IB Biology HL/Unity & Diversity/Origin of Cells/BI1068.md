4. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ phospholipids have a hydrophilic OR polar head OR polar phosphate group which is attracted to water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ phospholipids have a hydrophobic **OR** non-polar tail that is repelled by water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ phospholipids are amphipathic [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ form double layer in aqueous solution **OR** water, with hydrophobic tails in centre and hydrophilic heads facing outwards into aqueous environment [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ spontaneous process driven by the tendency to minimise interactions between hydrophobic regions and water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as lipid bilayer forms it curves creating a closed spherical structure **OR** vesicle [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The **amphipathic** ($\checkmark$) nature of phospholipids, with **hydrophilic phosphate heads** and **hydrophobic fatty acid tails**, causes them to self-organise into **bilayers in aqueous solutions** ($\checkmark$). The **phosphate heads** stick outwards **into** the **aqueous environment** as they are attracted to water ($\checkmark$), and the **hydrophobic tails** arrange themselves in the **centre of the bilayer** as they are repelled by water ($\checkmark$). As the **bilayer** forms it **curves** ($\checkmark$) to form **closed vesicles**.

5. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ probably led to formation of protocells **OR** formation of early vesicles may have led to protocells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ formation of vesicles **OR** lipid bilayer structure led to development of cell membranes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ plasma membranes separate internal cellular environment from external surroundings, allowing for controlled interactions with the environment [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allow compartmentalisation of different cellular functions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ as cells became more complex, different vesicles or membrane-bound organelles evolved to carry out specific tasks within the cell [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ this specialisation led to increased efficiency and complexity in cellular processes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ facilitated concentration of molecules and enzymes in specific areas, which enhanced the efficiency of biochemical reactions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ acted as a barrier, preventing reactive molecules from interacting with the surroundings and protecting the delicate molecular structures within [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ this isolation provided stability in diverse environments [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ protection of genetic material from the external environment [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 It is believed that, early in the evolution of life, before the existence of fully developed cells, simple lipid structures similar to vesicles could have formed **protocells**($\checkmark$), likely precursors to modern cells. The formation of vesicles and the lipid bilayer structure provided a basis for the **development of cell membranes** ($\checkmark$). These membranes **separated** the **internal** cellular environment from the **external** surroundings, allowing for **controlled interactions** with the environment ($\checkmark$). As cells became more complex, **different vesicles** or membrane-bound **organelles** could have **evolved** to carry out **specific tasks** within the cell ($\checkmark$). As cellular life evolved, vesicles provided a means to **protect genetic material** ($\checkmark$) from the external environment, which was crucial for the stability and transmission of genetic information across generations.
