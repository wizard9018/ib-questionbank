# BI1720

**Maximum mark: 10**

Louis Pasteur disproved the theory of spontaneous generation by showing that life did not spontaneously arise from non-living matter but came from existing microorganisms. In his experiment, he set up the following flasks:

I. Open straight-neck flask containing boiled broth.

II. Swan-neck flask containing boiled broth (neck allowed air to flow but trapped dust and microbes).

III.
Control flask containing boiled broth and sealed from the air.

Researchers carried out a similar experiment to Louis Pasteur’s famous experiment, using the set-up below, and incubating the flasks at 30°C.

![spontaneous origin of cells flasks](<images/spontaneous origin of cells flasks.png>)

1. State the purpose of boiling the broth before placing it in some of the flasks. [1 mark]

2.
 1. State two variables that must be controlled in this experiment. [2 marks]

 2. Explain why it is important to have controlled variables in this study. [1 mark]

3.
 1. State the letters of three flasks which would show signs of decomposition of broth. [1 mark]

 2. Explain your answer to part (i). [3 marks]

4. Suggest, with reasons, how the results of the study would differ if the flasks were incubated at 4°C. [2 marks]
