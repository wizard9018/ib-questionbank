# BI1013

The image shows a water strider (*Aquarius paludum*) standing on the surface of a pond. Which property of water allows the water strider to stand on the surface without sinking?

![BI1013](images/BI1013.png)
[Source adapted from: Zinogre. (2021, July 11). *File: Aquarius paludum 142592609.jpg*. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved July, 04 2023, from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Aquarius_paludum_142592609.jpg. Copyright free]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Adhesion

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Cohesion

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ High specific heat capacity

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Buoyancy

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Water molecules are polar and form hydrogen bonds with each other. This cohesion creates surface tension, which acts like a thin, elastic film that supports the water strider's low body weight. **Choice B** is, therefore, correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because adhesion is when water forms hydrogen bonds with other polar substances. The water strider's legs are covered in water-repellent hairs, so water does not stick to them.

**Choice C** is incorrect because high specific heat capacity helps keep water temperature stable, but it has no part in holding up the water strider.

**Choice D** is incorrect because buoyancy is the upward force a fluid exerts on an object placed in it. The water strider stands on the surface rather than floating in the water, so it is held up by surface tension, not buoyancy.
