# BI1008

The "Goldilocks zone" is a critical concept in the search for extraterrestrial life. Which statement best explains why Earth is classified as being within this zone, while Saturn is excluded?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Earth is large enough to keep an atmosphere, whereas Saturn is a gas giant with gravity that is too strong.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Earth reflects light that shows a clear water signature, whereas Saturn does not.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Earth is at a distance where solar radiation allows water to remain liquid, whereas Saturn is too far away, so water is frozen.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Earth has water in all three states (solid, liquid, gas), whereas Saturn only has ice in its rings.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The Goldilocks zone is defined as the region around a star where temperatures allow liquid water to exist on a planet's surface. Earth receives enough solar radiation to maintain water in liquid form, which is essential for life as we know it. Saturn orbits far outside this temperature range; it receives too little solar energy, so water cannot exist as a liquid and instead remains frozen. **Choice C** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because planet size and gravity influence atmospheric retention, but they do not define the Goldilocks zone. Saturn actually has **much stronger** gravity and easily retains a massive atmosphere. Its exclusion from the Goldilocks zone is due to insufficient solar radiation, not its atmospheric or gravitational properties.

 **Choice B**  is incorrect because detecting a **water signature** uses transit spectroscopy, but this does not determine whether a planet is in the Goldilocks zone. The zone is defined purely by distance from the star and temperature suitability for liquid water, not the detection of water vapour or ice.

 **Choice D**  is incorrect because the presence of multiple states of water on Earth is a **consequence** of being in the Goldilocks zone, not the reason Earth is classified within it. Saturn's icy rings do not influence its habitability. The key criterion is whether liquid water can exist stably at the surface, which depends on temperature and solar energy, not the number of water states present.
