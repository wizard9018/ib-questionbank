# BI1006

Which of the following is a reason why water is retained on Earth and does not escape into space?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Variations in global precipitation and hydrological cycling

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Tidal forces exerted by the Moon on Earth’s oceans

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Atmospheric temperatures too low for significant escape of water vapour into space

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Earth’s gravitational strength preventing the escape of water molecules into space

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Earth's gravitational field exerts a force strong enough to retain gases and liquids, including water vapour, in the atmosphere and hydrosphere. Without gravity, water molecules would have sufficient energy to escape into space. Gravity is the key reason why Earth can retain an atmosphere and surface water.  **Choice D** is, therefore, correct.

Precipitation patterns determine where water falls, not whether water remains on the planet.
The hydrological cycle redistributes water but does not influence Earth’s ability to retain it.
Rainfall patterns do not prevent atmospheric gases or water vapour from escaping into space.
**Choice A**  is, therefore, incorrect.

The Moon’s gravity causes tides, not water retention. Tidal forces move water horizontally (rise and fall of sea levels) but do not affect whether water can escape Earth’s gravitational field. **Choice B** is, therefore, incorrect.

While temperature influences the kinetic energy of gas molecules, Earth’s atmospheric temperatures are not the main reason water is retained. Even in warmer regions, most water vapour still lacks enough velocity to escape; gravity is the controlling factor, not temperature.  **Choice C**  is incorrect.
