3.
    2. any **two** of the following (AT LEAST **ONE** from transcription and **ONE** from translation):

    ***transcription***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase adds nucleotides to the 3' end of the growing mRNA strand        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase creates the RNA strand in the 5' to the 3' direction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase reads the DNA template strand in the 3' to 5' direction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the new nucleotides are added to the 3' end through sugar-phosphate bonds between the 3' carbon of the last nucleotide and the phosphate group of the incoming nucleotide        [1 mark]

    ***translation***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosome moves along the mRNA from the 5' end to the 3' end         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ codons of the mRNA are read from the 5' end to the 3' end to add amino acids that form the polypeptide             [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\
    In transcription, RNA polymerase **reads the DNA template strand in the 3' to 5' direction** ($\checkmark$) and creates the **RNA strand in the opposite direction** ($\checkmark$). In translation, the **ribosome moves along the mRNA in the 5' to 3' direction** ($\checkmark$).

4. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ purine-to-pyrimidine bonding **OR** complementary base pairing    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adenine-thymine **OR** A-T and cytosine-guanine **OR** C-G have equal lengths    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen bonding between complementary bases  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrophobic interaction between adjacent bases     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sugar-phosphate bonds link the 3' carbon of one deoxyribose sugar to the phosphate group on the 5' carbon of the next, forming a strong sugar-phosphate backbone    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ by winding around central histone proteins in eukaryotes to form nucleosomes        [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\
    The DNA double helix maintains its stability through **complementary base pairing of purine to pyrimidine** ($\checkmark$). This base pairing is held together by 2 **hydrogen bonds** between adenine to thymine and 3 **hydrogen bonds** ($\checkmark$) between cytosine and guanine.

5.
    1. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ showed the ratio of adenine to thymine and guanine to cytosine are equal [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ showed that the four nucleotide bases are not in repeating sequences **OR** equally distributed                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ suggested that DNA consists of two complementary strands    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ showed that the composition of DNA varies across diverse life forms    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ helped Watson and Crick in building the double helix shape of their DNA model    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ base pairing data suggested A pairs with T and G pairs with C            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ base pairing data suggested  mechanisms whereby DNA could replicate **OR** be transcribed     [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\
    Chargaff's data showed that the molar **ratio of adenine to thymine** and the **ratio of guanine to cytosine** are close to 1:1 ($\checkmark$). This data then helped Watson and Crick to deduce that **DNA consists of two complementary strands** ($\checkmark$) and is **helical in shape** ($\checkmark$).
