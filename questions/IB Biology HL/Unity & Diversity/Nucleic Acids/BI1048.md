1. Suggest **one** possible problem that could arise if DNA consisted of two parallel strands instead of two antiparallel strands.      [1 mark]

2. State one place where DNA is found in cells. [1 mark]

3. Compare and contrast the structure of DNA and RNA.        [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ no hydrogen bonding between base pairs        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ two strands are not held together **OR** no double-stranded DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ two strands would be geometrically the same, thus unable to combine     [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    If DNA were made of two parallel strands, both strands would be oriented in the same 3' to 5' direction. Since the DNA strand is tilted and not straight, that would mean that two DNA strands **would not be able to combine** ($\checkmark$) to form a double helix.

2.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ nucleus **OR** nucleoid **OR** mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts **OR** plasmids    [1 mark]
[1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ One place where DNA is found in the cell is in the **nucleus** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **five** of the following (there must be at least **one** similarity and **one** difference given to gain full marks):

    **Similarities:** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** DNA and RNA are composed of nucleotides **OR** sugar, phosphate **AND** base [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** DNA and RNA contain four types of nitrogenous bases [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** DNA and RNA have covalent bonds holding nucleotides together [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** DNA and RNA exhibit base pairing **OR** complementary bases are connected through hydrogen bonds **OR** have hydrogen bonds between the complementary nitrogenous bases [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** DNA and RNA have a backbone made up of alternating sugar and phosphate groups [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** DNA and RNA are polymers, composed of repeating monomer units **OR** nucleotides, linked together in a chain [1 mark]

    **Differences:** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA contains deoxyribose sugar **WHEREAS** RNA contains ribose  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA contains the nitrogenous bases adenine, thymine, cytosine and  guanine **OR** ATCG **WHEREAS** RNA contains adenine, uracil, cytosine and guanine **OR** AUCG, **OR** in DNA adenine pairs with thymine and cytosine pairs with guanine **WHEREAS** in RNA adenine pairs with uracil and cytosine pairs with guanine  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA typically consists of two (antiparallel) strands **WHEREAS** RNA is usually single-stranded [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in DNA adenine pairs with thymine and cytosine pairs with guanine **WHEREAS** in RNA adenine pairs with uracil and cytosine pairs with guanine  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA typically forms a double-stranded helical structure with base pairing between the two strands  **WHEREAS** RNA can fold into complex secondary structures due to intra-strand base pairing, creating loops, stems, and hairpin structures [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Both DNA and RNA are made up of **nucleotides** ($\checkmark$), however, the sugar in DNA is **deoxyribose**, whilst that in RNA is **ribose** ($\checkmark$). Both DNA and RNA contain **four** different **nitrogenous bases** ($\checkmark$), however, DNA contains adenine, guanine, cytosine and thymine, whilst RNA contains **uracil instead of thymine** ($\checkmark$). Both DNA and RNA are **polymers** of nucleotides ($\checkmark$) held together by **covalent bonds** ($\checkmark$) between the sugar of one nucleotide and a phosphate group of the next, however, DNA is typically **double-stranded**, and RNA is typically **single-stranded** ($\checkmark$).
