# BI2319

Which statement explains why DNA is evidence of common ancestry?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ All living organisms have an identical DNA base sequence.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ All living organisms contain DNA.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Closely related species contain the same alleles.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ More recently evolved species have more similar DNA.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is correct because the presence of DNA in all organisms reflects a universal lineage, providing evidence of common ancestry. All organisms use the same four nucleotides (adenine, thymine, cytosine, and guanine) and the same genetic code to translate DNA into proteins, which supports the idea of a shared evolutionary origin.

**Choice A** is incorrect because while all organisms share common sequences in their DNA, their base sequences are not identical. Differences in DNA sequences accumulate over time, and species with more recent common ancestors have more similar DNA.

**Choice C** is incorrect because closely related species do not necessarily contain the same alleles. They share homologous genes and may have similar alleles, but their alleles often differ due to mutation and divergence after their lineages split. Also, this does not directly explain why DNA is evidence of common ancestry—rather, it describes a consequence of common ancestry.

**Choice D** is incorrect because evolution does not progress in a linear ladder from “older” to “more recently evolved” species. All extant species are equally evolved. DNA similarity reflects how recently two species share a common ancestor, not how “recently evolved” they are. The wording is conceptually flawed, so it cannot explain why DNA provides evidence of common ancestry.
