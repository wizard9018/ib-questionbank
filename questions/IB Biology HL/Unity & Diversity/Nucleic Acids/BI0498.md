# BI0498

Hershey and Chase conducted experiments using the radioactive isotopes :sup[35]S and :sup[32]P which demonstrated that DNA is the genetic material. What would they have observed about the distribution of isotopes in the centrifuged sample if protein, rather than DNA, were the genetic material?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ High proportion of :sup[35]S in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ High proportion of :sup[32]P in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ High proportion of both :sup[35]S and :sup[32]P in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Low proportion of both :sup[35]S and :sup[32]P in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Hershey and Chase grew bacteriophages in the presence of the radioactive isotope :sup[35]S or :sup[32]P, then allowed them to infect bacteria. If proteins, rather than DNA, had been the genetic material transferred to bacteria, the bacteria would have contained :sup[35]S (the radiolabel in proteins) and not  :sup[32]P (the radiolabel in DNA). Therefore, Hershey and Chase would have observed a high proportion of  :sup[35]S in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant.

A high proportion of :sup[32]P in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant (**Choice B**) indicates that DNA, not protein, was the genetic material. Hershey and Chase did not grow bacteria in the presence of both isotopes at the same time and so could not have observed either a higher (**Choice C**) or lower (**Choice D**) proportion of both :sup[35]S and :sup[32]P in the bacterial pellet relative to the supernatant.
