# BI1850

**Maximum mark: 10**

![1850i](images/1850i.png)

1. Describe the role of DNA in storing genetic information. [2 marks]

2. Outline the importance of the antiparallel arrangement of DNA strands in the structure of DNA. [3 marks]

3. Outline the importance of the diversity of DNA base sequences in the process of evolution. [3 marks]

4. Discuss the importance of the conservation of the genetic code in understanding universal common ancestry. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ genetic code is stored in the sequence of nucleotide bases **OR** adenine, thymine, cytosine, guanine within the DNA molecule [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ groups of three nucleotides **OR** bases form codons, which correspond to specific amino acids [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sequence of codons determines the primary structure of proteins, which controls the organism’s traits and functions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ double-helix structure ensures stability of genetic information and allows for accurate replication during cell division [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ genetic code is degenerate **OR** multiple codons can code for same amino acid providing some protection against mutations [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ genes can be turned on or off **OR** are used when needed **OR** can be expressed and repressed [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 DNA stores genetic information in the form of **codons**, which are **sequences** of **three nucleotide bases** ($\checkmark$) that **code** for specific **amino acids** ($\checkmark$). These codons **determine** the **structure of proteins**, which are essential for the **organism’s traits** and **functions** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ two DNA strands run in opposite directions **OR** one strand runs 5' to 3', while the other runs 3' to 5' [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows the complementary base pairs **OR** adenine-thymine AND guanine-cytosine to align properly and form hydrogen bonds [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen bonding stabilises the double helix [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ antiparallel strands allow for the twisting of the DNA molecule [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ensures that the DNA molecule is stable and can store genetic information effectively [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The antiparallel arrangement means that **one DNA strand** runs **5' to 3'**, while the **other runs 3' to 5'** ($\checkmark$). This allows the **complementary base pairs** to **align** correctly and form **hydrogen bonds** between adenine-thymine and guanine-cytosine ($\checkmark$). These **hydrogen bonds** help **stabilise** the **double helix** and allow the **DNA to twist** into its characteristic structure ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ leads to genetic variation between individuals within a population [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ provides the basis for natural selection, as certain traits may offer a selective advantage in specific environments [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ Individuals with advantageous traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing their favourable alleles **OR** genes to the next generation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ over time, advantageous traits become more common, driving evolution and the development of new species [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows populations to adapt to changing environments, contributing to species' survival [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The diversity of DNA base sequences leads to **genetic variation** between **individuals** in a **population** ($\checkmark$). This variation provides the basis for **natural selection**, where **certain traits** offer a selective **advantage** in **specific environments** ($\checkmark$). Over time, **individuals** with **advantageous traits** are **more likely** to **survive, reproduce**, and **pass on** their **favourable genes** ($\checkmark$), driving the process of evolution and the development of new species.

4. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ genetic code is nearly universal across all living organisms **OR** there are only a few minor variations in the genetic code observed in certain species [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in nearly all organisms the same triplet codons specify the same amino acids [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ universality of the genetic code implies a shared ancestral origin for all organisms [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ used in phylogenetic analysis to reconstruct evolutionary relationships, providing strong evidence for universal common ancestry [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ conservation of the genetic code helps maintain the integrity of proteins **OR** reduces risk of mutations that could disrupt essential cellular functions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ despite evolutionary changes in species, the genetic code remains largely unchanged over time [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The conservation of the genetic code across all organisms suggests that all life shares a common ancestor, as the **same triplet codons** specify the **same amino acids** in **all species** ($\checkmark$). This universality indicates that the **genetic code** has been **passed down** through **generations** with **little change** ($\checkmark$), providing evidence for universal common ancestry.
