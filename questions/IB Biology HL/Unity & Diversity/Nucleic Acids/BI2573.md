# BI2573

The graph shows the results of an experiment by Hershey and Chase. Bacteria (*E. coli*) were infected with virus particles labelled with either (radioactive phosphorus) $\ce{^{32}P}$ or (radioactive sulfur) $\ce{^{35}S}$. A blender was used to remove attached viruses from the bacterial cells and samples collected from the suspension were centrifuged to record the percentage of isotope remaining in the cells. \
$\text{\textquotedblleft}$Infected bacteria” refers to the percentage of live bacteria that survived the blending process.

![2573a](images/2573a.png)

Which of the following statements explain(s) why the extracellular $\ce{^{32}P}$ increased as the running time in the blender increased?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
 | | |
 | :-- | :-- |
 | I. | When bacteriophages infect bacteria, not all viral particles successfully inject their DNA into the host cells. Some phages remain attached to the cell surface or fail to transfer their DNA. |
 | II. | During blending, the infected bacteria take up more $\ce{^{32}P}$. |
 | III. | During blending, some of the infected bacterial cells are lysed (the reason the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$infected bacteria” percentage decreases). The lysed cells release their $\ce{^{32}P}$. |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ I and III

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Statement I** is true because not all bacteriophages successfully inject their DNA into the bacterial host cells during infection. Some phages may remain attached to the bacterial surface or fail to inject their DNA. During blending, these surface-bound phages are detached, and their $\ce{^{32}P}$-labelled DNA is released into the extracellular medium, contributing to the increase in extracellular $\ce{^{32}P}$ in the graph.

**Statement II** is false. Bacteria do not "take up more” $\ce{^{32}P}$ during blending. The blending process is designed to remove surface-bound phages and shear unattached components, not to facilitate further DNA uptake by bacterial cells.

**Statement III** is true. Some of the infected bacterial cells can be lysed during the blending process. When this happens, the $\ce{^{32}P}$-labelled viral DNA inside these lysed cells is released into the extracellular environment, further increasing extracellular $\ce{^{32}P}$.

**Choice A** is correct because it is the only choice that includes both the true **Statements I** and **III**.

**Choice C** is incorrect because it only includes one true statement.

**Choice B** and **D** are incorrect because they include the false **Statement II**.
