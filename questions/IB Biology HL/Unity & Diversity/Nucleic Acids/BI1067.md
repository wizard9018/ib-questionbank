# BI1067

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Distinguish between RNA and DNA.         [3 marks]

2. Explain the importance of orientation and directionality of DNA and RNA.          [5 marks]

3. Describe how the Hershey and Chase experiment demonstrated that DNA carries the genetic information .       [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  RNA is single-stranded  **WHEREAS** DNA is double-stranded    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA has a variety of shapes **WHEREAS** DNA is a double helix     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA contains ribose sugar  **WHEREAS** DNA contains deoxyribose        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA contains the nitrogenous base uracil **OR** U **WHEREAS** DNA contains thymine **OR** T [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA only contains permanent genetic code in RNA viruses **WHEREAS** DNA contains permanent genetic code in all cells (except red blood cells)        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    RNA is a **single stranded** molecule containing ribose sugar and uracil as one of its four nitrogenous bases. DNA on the other hand is **double-stranded** ($\checkmark$), contains **deoxyribose** instead of **ribose** sugar ($\checkmark$), and has **thymine** instead of **uracil** ($\checkmark$) as one of its four nitrogenous bases. DNA forms a regular **double helix**, whereas RNA can form **different shapes**  ($\checkmark$) depending on its role, such as the clover-shaped tRNA and long-stranded mRNA.

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ 5’ **OR** 5 prime end  refers to the end with a phosphate group bound to the 5th carbon of the deoxyribose **OR** pentose sugar, but not bound to another nucleotide, the 3’ **OR** 3 prime end refers to the end where the 3rd carbon of the deoxyribose **OR** pentose sugar is not bound to another nucleotide            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA has antiparallel strands **OR** one strand is in the 5’ to 3’ direction, and another strand is in the 3’ to 5’ strand         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ single DNA strand is tilted; therefore, two DNA strands can only bind together if they are in antiparallel orientation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in DNA replication, one lagging strand and one leading strand are produced     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in DNA replication DNA polymerase only adds free nucleotides to the 3’ end of the growing DNA strand **OR** adds free nucleotides in the 5’ to 3’ direction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in transcription RNA polymerase can only add nucleotides in the 5’ to 3’ direction to form mRNA        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ translation must start nearest to the 5’ end of the mRNA    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Both RNA and DNA have directionality they have a **5’** and **3’** end. The **5’ end** refers to the end with a **phosphate group** attached to carbon 5 of the deoxyribose, but **not attached** to another **nucleotide**, the **3’** end refers to the end where **carbon 3** of the deoxyribose is **not attached** to another **nucleotide** ($\checkmark$). DNA forms a double helix with **antiparallel** strands ($\checkmark$). During **replication DNA polymerase** can only **add free nucleotides** in the **5’ to 3’** direction ($\checkmark$). During **transcription RNA polymerase** can only **add nucleotides** in the **5’ to 3’** direction ($\checkmark$) to form a strand of mRNA. **Translation** of the mRNA must **start at the 5’** end ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ bacteriophages **OR** phage virus was used         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bacteriophage contains an inner core that has DNA and a protein coat    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ experiments tested the hypothesis that DNA and not protein carries the genetic information    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bacteriophage **OR** virus can attach to the surface of bacterial cell **OR** host and inject substance into the cell        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ substance contains information for bacteria cell **OR** host to duplicate **OR** produce more bacteriophages **OR** viruses        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bacteriophages **OR** virus were labeled with radioactive phosphorus (:sup[32]P) and radioactive sulfur (:sup[35]S) **OR** :sup[32]P was incorporated into DNA core and :sup[35]S was incorporated into protein coat        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phosphorus is found only in DNA and not protein        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sulfur is found only in protein and not DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ *Escherichia coli* **OR** bacterial cells were infected with bacteriophage **OR** virus  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ used centrifuge to separate bacteriophage **OR** virus from bacteria        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ virus in liquid **OR** supernatant did not contain radioactive :sup[32]P but bacterial sediment **OR** pellet contained radioactive :sup[32]P            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ :sup[32]P DNA entered the bacteria **OR** host cell for replication          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ virus in liquid **OR** supernatant contained radioactive :sup[35]S but bacterial sediment **OR** pellet did not contain radioactive :sup[35]S            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ :sup[35]S protein structure did not enter the bacteria **OR** host cell for replication     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic material needs to enter the bacterial cell in order to produce new viruses     [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Hershey and Chase's experiment was conducted to investigate whether DNA or protein carries the genetic material. In their experiment, they used **bacteriophages** ($\checkmark$) to infect bacteria cells. **Bacteriophage** contains an **outer coat made of protein** and an **inner core containing DNA** ($\checkmark$) which makes it suitable for comparing DNA and protein. The bacteriophage can then **attach itself to the surface of the bacteria cell** and **inject the substance** ($\checkmark$) which carries important genetic information into the bacteria cell.

    The bacteriophages were then cultured under two different conditions — **one was incorporated with a radioactive phosphorus isotope, :sup[32]P** and another with a radioactive **sulfur isotope, :sup[35]S** ($\checkmark$). Radioactive elements 32P and 35S were used because **phosphorus is found in the DNA structure** but **absent in protein** ($\checkmark$), whereas **sulfur**, is **found in** the **protein** structure but **absent** in the **DNA** ($\checkmark$) structure.

    The bacteria cells were then cultured together with the bacteriophages and were **infected with the bacteriophages** ($\checkmark$). After centrifugation, it was found that the pellets containing **bacteria cultured** with the **radioactive :sup[32]P bacteriophages contained radioactivity**, whereas the **bacteriophage protein coats** in the supernatant **didn’t** ($\checkmark$).  The pellets containing the **bacteria cultured** with the **radioactive :sup[35]S bacteriophages did not contain any radioactivity**, whereas the **bacteriophage protein coats** in the supernatant **did** ($\checkmark$). These provided evidence that DNA labelled with the **:sup[32]P entered the bacteria cells**, whereas the **protein** which was labelled with the **:sup[35]S did not** ($\checkmark$) enter the bacteria cells.
