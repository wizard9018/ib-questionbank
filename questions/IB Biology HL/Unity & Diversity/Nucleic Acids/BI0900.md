# BI0900

**Maximum mark: 10**

The Hershey-Chase experiment in 1952 involved cultured viruses that were radioactively labelled with either sulfur (:sup[35]S) or phosphorus (:sup[32]P) in two separate flasks. Each batch of viruses was then added to separate cultures of bacteria. After allowing time for the viruses to infect the bacteria, the culture solutions were centrifuged to separate the viral remains from the bacterial cells, forming two layers, the pellet and the supernatant.

![BI0900](images/BI0900.png)

1. Explain why radioactive sulfur (:sup[35]S) and radioactive phosphorus (:sup[32]P) were used in the Hershey–Chase experiment to distinguish between DNA and protein. [2 marks]

2. Suggest one technological development, other than radioisotopes, that helped open up possibilities in understanding the overall structure of DNA. [1 mark]

3.
 1. Outline the results of the experiment that provided evidence to support the conclusion that DNA is the genetic material. [2 marks]

 2. Describe how the 4 nitrogenous bases of DNA enables polypeptides to be coded for. [2 marks]

4. Explain how DNA can be packaged into compact units to form chromatin. [3 marks]

1. Any **two** of the following :

 $\ce{ -> }$ :sup[35]S is used to label protein because sulfur is found in protein but not in DNA [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ :sup[32]P is used to label DNA because phosphorus is found in DNA but not in protein [1 mark]

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 :sup[35]S is used because **sulphur is found in protein and not in DNA** ($\checkmark$), and :sup[32]P is used because **phosphorus is found in DNA and not protein** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ X-ray crystallography **OR** diffraction (by Rosalind Franklin) [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ paper chromatography (by Erwin Chargaff) [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Another technological development used was **X-ray diffraction** ($\checkmark$) by Rosalind Franklin which provided evidence that DNA has a helical shape.

 1.
 $\ce{ -> }$ :sup[35]S **OR** radioactively labelled protein was found in the supernatant **OR** not found in the pellet **OR** no radioactivity detected in bacteria infected with viruses labelled with :sup[35]S [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ :sup[32]P **OR** radioactively labelled DNA was found in the pellet **OR** not found in the supernatant **OR** radioactivity was detected in bacteria infected with viruses labelled with :sup[32]P [1 mark]
[2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The results from the Hershey-Chase experiment showed that **:sup[35]S**, used to **label protein, was found in the supernatant** ($\checkmark$), and the **:sup[32]P**, used to label DNA, was found in the pellet ($\checkmark$).

3.
 2. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ the four nitrogenous bases are A, T, G, C **OR** adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ polypeptides are coded for by genes OR a sequence of nucleotides [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ three nitrogenous bases **OR** codon **OR** triplet of bases, code for a specific amino acid **OR** start **OR** stop [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ degenerative code **OR** several codons can code for one amino acid [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA as a template **OR** antisense strand to form mRNA strand [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ complementary base pairing between DNA nucleotides and RNA nucleotides **OR** between codons on mRNA and amino acid [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA is used as template to form polypeptide chains [1 mark]
 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Polypeptides are coded for by **genes** ($\checkmark$) made up of a specific sequence of nucleotides. A nucleotide consists of a ribose sugar, a phosphate group and one of four nitrogenous bases - **adenine, guanine, cytosine and thymine** ($\checkmark$). DNA is used as a **template to form the mRNA strand** ($\checkmark$). A triplet of bases, **a codon**, along the mRNA strand codes for a **specific amino acid** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA tightly wraps around a core of eight histone proteins [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ held together by an additional histone protein **OR** H1 linker protein [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ H1 histone proteins binds the nucleosomes together to form a 30 nm chromatin fibre [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ long DNA molecules are packed into a small space [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the negatively charged DNA is attracted to the positively charged histones [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ form structures called nucleosomes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ nucleosomes are attached to other nucleosomes by linker DNA [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 DNA coils tightly around a core of **eight histone proteins** ($\checkmark$) which are held together by **H1 histone proteins** ($\checkmark$) to form structures known as **nucleosomes** ($\checkmark$). The **nucleosomes** are bundled **together** to form the **30nm fibre chromatin** ($\checkmark$).
