# BI3214

A newly discovered entity  has RNA rather than DNA as its genetic material and can replicate only inside host cells.

Which statement best evaluates this discovery in relation to the principle that DNA is the genetic material of all living organisms?

A. $\hspace{1em}$ It disproves the principle because any entity capable of replication is classified as a living organism.

B. $\hspace{1em}$ It disproves the principle because living organisms can use either DNA or RNA as their genetic material.

C. $\hspace{1em}$ It does not disprove the principle because the entity could be an RNA virus, which is not considered a living organism.

D. $\hspace{1em}$ It does not disprove the principle because RNA cannot function as genetic material.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The entity has characteristics consistent with an RNA virus: it contains RNA and depends on host cells for replication. Viruses are not considered living organisms, so the discovery does not contradict the principle that DNA is the genetic material of all living organisms. **Choice C** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the ability to replicate inside host cells is not sufficient for an entity to be considered living.

**Choice B** is incorrect because the presence of an RNA virus does not prove that living organisms can use RNA as their genetic material.

**Choice D** is incorrect because some viruses use RNA as their genetic material. These viruses do not contradict the principle because they are not considered living organisms—not because RNA cannot carry genetic information.
