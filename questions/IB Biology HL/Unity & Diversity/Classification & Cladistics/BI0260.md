# BI0260

**Maximum mark: 10**
1. Natural classification is used to group organisms with common ancestry together, based on similarities and shared characteristics. All organisms can be classified into one of the three domains.

 ![BI0260](images/BI0260.png)

 1. List three characteristics of Eubacteria. [3 marks]

 2. Distinguish between archaeans and eukaryotes. [1 mark]

2. Outline how a case study like the reclassification of the figwort family (*Scrophulariaceae*) demonstrates the limitations of morphology-based taxonomy.[4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Eubacteria are organisms that are **unicellular** ($\checkmark$) and have no true nucleus. They have **a single chromosome** ($\checkmark$) that is a **loop** ($\checkmark$) and **histone proteins may or may not be present** ($\checkmark$). The **ribosomes of Eubacteria are 70S** ($\checkmark$) and their **cell walls are made of peptidoglycan** ($\checkmark$).

 2. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ Archaea do not have membrane-bound organelles **WHEREAS** Eukaryota do have membrane-bound organelles [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ Archaea do not have a nucleus **WHEREAS** Eukaryota do have a nucleus [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ Archaea have circular chromosomes **WHEREAS** Eukaryota have linear chromosomes [1 mark]

 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Archaea **have circular chromosomes** but Eukaryota **have linear chromosomes** ($\checkmark$). Archaea **do not have a nucleus** but Eukaryota do ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ morphology-based taxonomy relies on physical similarities in structuress [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ similarities used in morphology-based classification may result from convergent evolution, not common ancestry [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ convergent evolution produces analogous structures that look similar but evolved independently [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the figwort family **OR** *Scrophulariaceae* was originally grouped based on morphological traits [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ molecular and cladistic analyses showed that some species classified in the figwort family were more closely related to species in other families [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ led to the reclassification of many species into different families based on genetic evidence [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ case study shows that relying only on morphology can mislead taxonomy because of homoplasy OR similar traits not due to common ancestry[1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ cladistics, using DNA **OR** molecular data, provides a more objective and accurate method for determining evolutionary relationships [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ reclassification highlights how scientific classifications are hypotheses that can be falsified or revised with new evidence [1 mark]

 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
The figwort family was **originally classified** based on **morphological traits**, such as **flower shape** ($\checkmark$). However, **DNA sequencing** and **cladistic analysis** showed that **some species** were more closely related to plants in other families, **not sharing** a **recent common ancestor** with the **figworts** ($\checkmark$). This finding demonstrated that the **similarities in structure** resulted from **convergent evolution**, where **unrelated species** develop **similar traits** independently ($\checkmark$). Therefore, **relying solely** on **morphology** can be **misleading in classification**, highlighting the importance of **molecular data** for **more accurate** evolutionary relationships ($\checkmark$).
