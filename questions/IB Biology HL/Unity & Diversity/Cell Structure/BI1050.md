# BI1050

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Distinguish between a colony of algal cells forming a multicellular sphere and a multicellular plant such as a water lily. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ colony of algal cells are generally undifferentiated **WHEREAS** multicellular plants like water lilies are composed of different types **OR** differentiated of cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ no **OR** minimal organisation **OR** minimal division of labour of cells in algal colony, **WHEREAS** in multicellular plant cells arranged into tissues, organs and organ systems, **OR** well-defined structures carry out specific roles in plants [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ each individual algal cell is capable of performing all necessary functions for life **OR** survival independently **WHEREAS** most individual cells of multicellular plants cannot survive independently **OR** away from the main body of the plant [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ cells in an algal colony show limited coordination **OR** communication, **WHEREAS** plant cells are connected by plasmodesmata and coordinated by plant hormones **OR** signalling [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ development of colonies is often simple **WHEREAS** plants undergo complex developmental processes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ reproduction in an algal colony typically involves asexual methods **WHEREAS** in multicellular plants such as the water lily typically involves sexual reproduction **OR** multicellular plants use both sexual and asexual reproduction [1 mark]
 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Unlike true multicellular organisms like water lilies, algal colonies **lack cellular differentiation** and **specialised tissues**. **Individual cells** within the colony are largely independent, being able to perform all vital functions, and show little coordination with one another. In contrast, cells in the multicellular plant communicate through plasmodesmata and hormones ($\checkmark$) and usually **do not survive** when **separated** from the plant ($\checkmark$). These cells become **specialised** ($\checkmark$) and form **tissues, organs**, and **organ systems** within the plant. This intricate **division of labour** ($\checkmark$) allows plants to achieve greater size and complexity.

3. any **seven** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ larger prokaryotes **OR** cells engulfed smaller prokaryotes **OR** cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ingested cell was kept alive [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ symbiotic relationship between both cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ingested cell acquired a second membrane [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ingested cell is ancestor of chloroplasts **OR** mitochondria [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ chloroplast **OR** mitochondrial structures provide evidence for the endosymbiotic theory [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ chloroplasts **OR** mitochondria contain 70S ribosomes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ chloroplasts **OR** mitochondria contain their own DNA **OR** looped DNA [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ chloroplasts **OR** mitochondria can conduct transcription **OR** translation [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ chloroplasts **OR** mitochondria divide in a similar manner to prokaryotic cells **OR** by binary fission [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ chloroplasts **OR** mitochondria are similar in size to prokaryotes [1 mark]
 [7 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The endosymbiotic theory explains the origin of some structures in eukaryotic cells, including chloroplasts and mitochondria. The **ancestors** of chloroplasts and mitochondria were prokaryotes that **were engulfed by larger prokaryotes** ($\checkmark$). The ingested cells were **kept alive** ($\checkmark$) and both cells eventually developed a **symbiotic** relationship ($\checkmark$) with the cell that engulfed them. When the larger cells engulfed the smaller cells, the smaller cells gained a **second membrane** ($\checkmark$) as they were engulfed via endocytosis. Further evidence for the theory is that chloroplasts and mitochondria contain their own **70s ribosomes** ($\checkmark$) so can **make their own proteins**, and also contain **their own DNA** ($\checkmark$) so can carry out **transcription** ($\checkmark$). Chloroplasts and mitochondria are also able to **divide in a similar manner to prokaryotic cells** ($\checkmark$).
