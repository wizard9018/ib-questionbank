# BI1322

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Distinguish between the structure and location of chromosomes found in eukaryotes and prokaryotes.            [5 marks]

2. Describe the stages of meiosis and how genetically unique, haploid gametes are created during the process.    [8 marks]

3. Explain the information that can be deduced from a human karyogram.        [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ eukaryotic chromosomes are found in the nucleus **WHEREAS** prokaryotic chromosomes are found in the nucleoid region **OR** chromosomes of eukaryotes are contained within a membrane **WHEREAS** prokaryotic chromosomes are not membrane bound     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotes have a circular chromosome **WHEREAS** eukaryotes have linear chromosomes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotes typically have one chromosome **WHEREAS** eukaryotes typically have multiple chromosomes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotic chromosomes, DNA is associated with histone proteins **WHEREAS** prokaryotic chromosomes do not contain histones        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic chromosomes do not contain centromeres **WHEREAS** eukaryotic chromosomes do [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic chromosomes do not contain telomeres **WHEREAS** eukaryotic chromosomes do     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ eukaryotic chromosomes carry more genetic information **OR** DNA **OR** genes **THAN** prokaryotic chromosomes    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Eukaryotic chromosomes are found in the **nucleus** whereas prokaryotic chromosomes are found in the **nucleoid** region ($\checkmark$). Prokaryotes typically contain **one, circular** chromosome ($\checkmark$)  whereas eukaryotes  have **multiple, linear** chromosomes ($\checkmark$). Prokaryotic chromosomes **do not** contain **centromeres** and **telomeres** ($\checkmark$), whereas eukaryotic chromosomes do ($\checkmark$).

2. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ all the DNA in a cell is replicated during interphase        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ so cells are diploid **OR** contain homologous chromosomes when they enter meiosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes pair **AND** become visible **OR** condense  in prophase I    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over occurs between the non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during prophase I    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes are moved to the equator of the cell by spindle microtubules **AND** randomly orientated OR independently assorted in metaphase I [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite sides of the cell by spindle microtubules in anaphase I            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ one diploid cell is split into two haploid cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chromatids condense **OR** become visible during prophase II        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pairs of sister chromatids are moved to the equator of the cell by spindle microtubules during metaphase II **AND** randomly orientated **OR** independently assorted    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pairs of sister chromatids are separated by spindle fibres in anaphase II        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nuclear membrane reforms in telophase II **AND** four haploid cells are formed from the two haploid cells of     meiosis         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over during prophase I **AND** independent assortment **OR** random alignment of homologous chromosomes in metaphase I **OR** metaphase II result in unique gametes    [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    All of the DNA in a cell is **replicated** during **interphase** ($\checkmark$), so cells are **diploid** when they enter meiosis ($\checkmark$). **Homologous** chromosomes **pair up**, become **visible** ($\checkmark$) and **crossing over** of non-sister chromatids occur during **prophase I** ($\checkmark$). Then, homologous chromosomes are moved to the **equator** of the cell by **spindle microtubules** and **randomly orientated** during **metaphase I** ($\checkmark$). Homologous chromosomes are pulled to the **opposite poles** of the cell by spindle fibres in **anaphase I** ($\checkmark$) and one **diploid cell** is split into **two haploid** cells during telophase I ($\checkmark$). The cells then enter meiosis II. The **pairs** of **sister chromatids** are **independently assorted** to form a single line along the equator of the cell, in **metaphase II** ($\checkmark$). During **anaphase II** the centromeres split and the **sister chromatids** are pulled to **opposite poles** of the cell ($\checkmark$). In **telophase II** the **nuclear membrane reforms** around the chromosomes, and after cytokinesis, **4 new haploid, non-identical**,  daughter cells are formed from the **2 haploid cells** that resulted from **meiosis I** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ a karyogram is an image showing the positions of centromeres and banding patterns of stained **OR** dyed homologous chromosomes ordered by decreasing length        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ biological sex determined by XX and XY **OR** sex chromosomes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ whether non-disjunction has occurred is determined by the presence or absence of chromosome (s)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ presence of chromosomal abnormalities (such as breakages or translocation) determined by the banding pattern **OR** size of chromosome        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ indication of chromosome disorders such as Down syndrome, which is caused by trisomy 21[1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    A karyogram is an image showing the positions of **centromeres** and **banding patterns** of stained **homologous chromosomes** ordered by decreasing length ($\checkmark$). A karyogram can be used to deduce **biological sex** ($\checkmark$) or the presence of **chromosomal abnormalities** ($\checkmark$).
