# BI0911

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline why scientists must have several approaches to conserve biodiversity.   [4 marks]

2. There are approximately 80 Javan Rhinoceros (*Rhinoceros sondaicus*) in the world. Discuss the environmental and economic implications the Indonesian government must consider before volunteering the Javan Rhinoceros to the EDGE programme.        [5 marks]

3. Explain why rapid human population growth is linked to the current biodiversity crisis.   [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ no single approach is sufficient **OR** establishing conservation areas is often effective, but without reintroduction of lost species it will have limited success **OR** other suitable example showing that when one approach is used, another is required for effective conservation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different species **OR** ecosystems require different measures        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ not all methods are affordable for different countries **OR** organisations, **OR** governments    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ biodiversity encompasses a vast range of organisms, habitats, and ecosystems with different interdependencies [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ socio-economic factors need to be considered to ensure that conservation efforts take into account local communities' needs and interests   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ongoing changes in biodiversity may mean that strategies need to be adapted accordingly      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ multiple approaches can create a more resilient and effective conservation strategy   [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Scientists must have multiple approaches to conserve biodiversity as **no single approach** is sufficient ($\checkmark$), different species require **different methods** ($\checkmark$) and not all methods are **affordable for different governments** ($\checkmark$).  In addition, biodiversity encompasses a vast range of organisms and habitats with **different interdependencies** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*implications must be stated*):

    ***Environmental negative considerations***

    $\ce{ -> }$ disruption of current food chains **OR** webs   [1 mark]

    ***Environmental positive considerations***

    $\ce{ -> }$ restoration of food webs **OR** chains  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ habitats **OR** forests will have to be restored     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the Javan Rhinoceros is a keystone species, so conserving this species would indirectly protect the ecosystem      [1 mark]

    ***Economic negative considerations***

    $\ce{ -> }$ can be expensive to put rhinos in breeding programmes **OR** zoos, **OR** EDGE program would require financial investments in research **OR** monitoring, **OR** conservation initiatives            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ land that has been used for urbanisation **OR** businesses **OR** agriculture  will have to be reforested            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ local communities might rely on the same resources as the rhinoceros, **OR** local communities' livelihoods might be impacted by conservation measures       [1 mark]

    ***Economic positive considerations***

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase tourism in the area      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ may improve international cooperation **OR** trade agreements, **OR** foreign investments      [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Before volunteering the Javan rhinoceros to the EDGE programme, economic considerations must be taken into account.  For example, protecting the Javan rhinoceros would likely **increase tourism** in the area ($\checkmark$) as people will travel to the country to see the rhinos. However, it can be **expensive** to put rhinos into **breeding programmes** ($\checkmark$) or **reforest** land that has been **urbanised** ($\checkmark$), and this may be **unaffordable** for the Indonesian government. A positive environmental consideration is that **reforestation will increase biodiversity** ($\checkmark$) in the area. However, it may also result in **disruption of current, stable food chains** ($\checkmark$)in the area.

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ more hunting **OR** poaching **OR** for food **OR** resources, which endangers animals [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ loss of habitats due to urbanisation **OR** agriculture [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased agriculture can lead to a monoculture **OR** a loss in diversity  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ deforestation for building infrastructure **OR** resources such as paper **OR** agriculture   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased pollution due to increased vehicles **OR** global transport **OR** industry **OR** factories can cause damage to organisms **OR** contribute to global warming   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased use of fertilisers leading to eutrophication      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased spread of diseases to plants **OR** animals due to increased global transport [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased risk of spread of invasive species due to increased global transport     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased spread of pests due to increased air travel damaging agriculture **OR** forest ecosystems     [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Rapid human population growth is linked to the current biodiversity crisis because as the global population grows, there is an increased need for resources which can result in **loss of habitat** due to **increased agriculture** ($\checkmark$), an increase in the use of **fertilisers** which can result in **eutrophication** ($\checkmark$) and more **hunting** of animals ($\checkmark$) which lowers animal populations.  An increase in agriculture which utilises **monocultures** lowers species diversity in the area ($\checkmark$).  An increase in the human population is also correlated with **increased global transport**, which can result in increased **pollution**, which contributes to **global warming** ($\checkmark$), which can have a detrimental effect on ecosystems and also can result in an increased **spread of invasive species** ($\checkmark$) and **pests** ($\checkmark$), which lowers biodiversity within ecosystems.
