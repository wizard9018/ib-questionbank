3. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the elephant has close genetic relatives **OR** more close relatives (than the crocodile)   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the lizard is more evolutionarily unique (than the elephant)   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ conservation data on the elephant is insufficient **OR** not up-to-date (to qualify for EDGE)      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the elephant may not be globally endangered **OR** it may be endangered in one country only **OR** have a healthy population in other countries   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is better ex-situ conservation **OR** better global zoo **OR** captive populations of the elephant than the  lizard    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lizard may occupy a more restricted **OR** fragmented habitat, increasing extinction risk   [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The Chinese crocodile lizard may be **very distinct genetically from its nearest relative**, which gives it priority for selection into the EDGE programme, whereas the elephant has more close relatives ($\checkmark$). The elephant is a popular species for zoos and conservation projects, so there will be **higher populations in captivity** ($\checkmark$), making its extinction risk lower. Also, the elephant may be endangered in one country but may **exist in large populations elsewhere**, lowering its risk of global extinction danger ($\checkmark$).
