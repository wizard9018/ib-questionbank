# BI1684

**Maximum mark: 10**

The Eurasian beaver (*Castor fiber*) has been described as a species that promotes biodiversity. Beavers are herbivores that feed on freshwater plants and tree bark. They use their sharp front teeth to fell trees and use the logs and branches to build dams that create pools in which they can build their lodge. A beaver lodge is a construction in which they nest and raise their young, providing protection from predators.

1. Suggest ways in which the reintroduction of beavers can increase biodiversity. [4 marks]

The effect of introducing beavers on the species diversity of birds and bats was studied in four areas: on river banks, one without any beaver activity and one where beavers are present, and then ponds, again, one without beaver activity and one where beavers were present. 2. State two necessary measurements to calculate Simpson's reciprocal index of diversity. [1 mark]

3. Calculate the percentage increase in the species diversity of bats in the pond after the introduction of the beaver. [2 marks]

Beavers became extinct in the UK over 400 years ago. Reintroduction programmes are now being carried out in various regions of the UK.

4. Suggest **three** anthropogenic activities that could have led to the extinction of the beavers in the UK. [3 marks]

1. any **four** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ many different habitats **OR** niches are created by beaver activity [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ dams create pools that slow the run-off of rivers, benefiting plant rooting [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ dams generate wetlands that harbour insect **OR** invertebrate species [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ this promotes a food chain with consumers such as birds [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ pools are habitats for aquatic plants **OR** fish [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ felling trees allows sunlight onto the forest floor, benefiting ground-level plants and shrubs [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ fallen trees are a source of food for microorganisms **OR** detritivores [1 mark]

 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

 The activity of beavers **creates many habitats** for other organisms ($\checkmark$), with an overall increase in biodiversity. For example, the felling of trees opens up patches of ground to sunlight, **promoting ground-level vegetation** ($\checkmark$). The pools created by beaver dams **attract insects** ($\checkmark$) and the consumers (such as birds) that **feed on those insects** ($\checkmark$). Dams slow the run-off of rainwater which has a **beneficial effect on plant roots** which are not washed away by fast-flowing water ($\checkmark$). The pools also attract **fish species and aquatic plants** that can oxygenate the pond water ($\checkmark$); beavers do not eat fish, so there is no harm to the fish from them.

 $\ce{ -> }$ hunting for fur **OR** castoreum [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ deforestation **OR** logging **OR** increase in agricultural land [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

 The extinction of beavers in the UK may have been caused by **overhunting** for their valuable **fur** and **castoreum** ($\checkmark$), combined with **extensive habitat destruction** due to the **draining of wetlands** ($\checkmark$) and **deforestation** for **agricultural expansion** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the **introduction of non-native species** ($\checkmark$) and **diseases**, through international **travel**, ($\checkmark$) further contributed to their decline.
