# BI2945

The graph compares the base mutation rates of different DNA and RNA viruses during replication in one host cell.

![BI2945](images/BI2945.png)
[Source: Adapted from Cuevas, J. M., Geller, R., Garijo, R., López-Aldeguer, J., & Sanjuán, R. (2015, September 16). *Extremely high mutation rate of HIV-1 in vivo*. PLOS Biology. Retrieved 17 June 2025 from, https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.1002251. Copyright under CC BY 4.0]

What can be concluded from this graph?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ DNA viruses have a higher mutation rate than RNA viruses

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ All RNA viruses have a higher mutation rate than DNA viruses

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ The polio virus has a mutation rate approximately 10 $\times$ higher than the HIV-1 virus

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ Herpes simplex has a higher mutation rate than lambda phage

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The polio virus has a mutation rate of 0.0001, and the HIV-1 virus has a mutation rate of 0.00001, which is 10 $\times$ less. **Choice C** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because the graph shows that RNA viruses have a much higher mutation rate than DNA viruses. This is due to the fact that RNA-dependent RNA polymerase lacks proofreading capability.\
**Choice B** is incorrect because while all RNA viruses shown in the graph have higher rates, you cannot conclude 'All RNA viruses' globally from just four examples. **Choice D** is incorrect because the herpes simplex virus has a lower mutation rate than the lambda phage.
