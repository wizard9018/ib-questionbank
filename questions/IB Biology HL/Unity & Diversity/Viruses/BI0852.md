3. any **four** of the following, but answer must include at least one similarity and one difference to earn full marks:

 **similarities**\
 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** cycles are mechanisms for phage, **OR** bacteriophage, reproduction [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** cycles require phage proteins to bind to specific receptors on the surface of the host bacterium [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** require phage DNA, **OR** phage genetic information **OR** phage genome, to be inserted into the host bacterium [1 mark]

 **differences**\
 $\ce{ -> }$ in lytic cycle, host bacterial cells are killed **OR** lysed, **WHILE** host cells are not destroyed **OR** lysed in lysogenic cycle [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in lytic cycle, phage genome **OR** DNA remains separate to bacterial DNA within the host cell, **WHILE** phage DNA is inserted into host cell in lysogenic cycle [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in lytic cycle, phage genes, **OR** DNA, **OR** genetic information is expressed immediately, **WHILE** in the lysogenic cycle, genes are not transcribed, **OR** expressed, **OR** genetic information lies dormant [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ in lytic cycle host cells are lysed rapidly, leading to immediate harm to the host bacterium, **WHILE** in lysogenic cycle, there is no harm to host for extended period of time, **OR** cycle is not immediate [1 mark]
 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Both** lytic and lysogenic cycles are processes of **phage reproduction** ($\checkmark$). **Both** cycles start with **phage proteins binding** to bacterial host cell **receptors** ($\checkmark$), and then the **genetic material** is **injected** ($\checkmark$) into the host cell. In the **lytic cycle, phage DNA remains separate** to the **bacterial DN** within the host cell, while in the **lysogenic cycle, viral DNA** is **inserted into the host chromosome** ($\checkmark$).
