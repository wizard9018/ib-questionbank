# BI0715

**Maximum mark: 6**

The male firefly (*P. pyralis*) signals to potential female mates by flashing its lights in specific patterns. Females will only respond to light patterns produced by their own species.

![BI0715A](images/BI0715A.png)
[Source: Adapted from Gallagher, J. (2014, June 3). *File: Firefly - Photinus pyralis, Woodbridge, Virginia.jpg - Wikimedia ...* Wikimedia commons. Retrieved July 1, 2022, from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Firefly_-_Photinus_pyralis,_Woodbridge,_Virginia.jpg Copyright under CC BY 2.0]

1. Identify the form of reproductive isolation that fireflies exhibit. Justify your answer.     [2 marks]

2. Suggest **one** possible advantage of this mating ritual.  [1 mark]

3. All fireflies seen today have diverged from the same ancestral species. Explain how speciation is responsible for the large variety of firefly species.  [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$  behavioural type [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  the different light patterns **OR** the mating behaviour **OR** rituals  [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  prevents the fireflies from mating with different species **OR** interspecies breeding  [1 mark]
[2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The fireflies are exhibiting **behavioural** isolation ($\checkmark$), as the specific **mating rituals** that they perform ($\checkmark$), **prevent interspecies breeding** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$  allows females to find males of the same species **OR** allows fireflies to identify members of the opposite sex     [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  allows females to select the fittest males **OR** mates      [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  brighter **OR** faster flashes can increase the chance of mating **OR** be more attractive to females      [1 mark]
[1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    An advantage to the firefly of exhibiting this type of behaviour is that it allows females to find **males** of the **same species**. ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{->}$  speciation is the formation of a new species **OR** the process by which populations diverge **OR** evolve over time to become distinct species     [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  members of a population are isolated from one another **OR** barriers prevent interbreeding      [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  allopatric speciation involves a physical barrier separating groups **OR** sympatric speciation involves a non-physical barrier separating groups         [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  over time selective pressures affect each group differently **OR** different mutations occur within each group **OR** over time each group can develop different behaviours **OR** different characteristics develop due to natural selection         [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  allele frequencies change in each population **OR** genetic drift         [1 mark]

    $\ce{->}$  groups are unable to interbreed **OR** are reproductively isolated when the barrier is removed **OR** cannot reproduce to produce fertile offspring         [1 mark]
[3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Speciation is when a group of organisms change so much that they can no longer mate together to produce **fertile offspring** ($\checkmark$). This can come about by a group of fireflies being **separated or isolated** ($\checkmark$) from the original population, say by being blown to an island. Over time, the separated group will change. For example the climate may be colder so those individuals with a larger abdomen have **more chance of surviving** ($\checkmark$) as they lose less heat, and so they pass on their favourable alleles and the **allele frequency** in the population **changes** ($\checkmark$). When the separated group encounter fireflies from the original population, they can **no longer reproduce together** ($\checkmark$) as their abdomens are so different.
