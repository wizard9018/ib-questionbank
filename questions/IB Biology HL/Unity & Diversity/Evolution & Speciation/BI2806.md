# BI2806

**Maximum mark: 10**

Chimpanzees (*Pan troglodytes*) and bonobos (*Pan paniscus*) diverged from a common ancestor around 1.25 million years ago. This event is thought to have been influenced by the formation of the Congo River.

The diagram shows the ranges of chimpanzees and bonobos.

1. Chimpanzees and bonobos are two different species that share a recent common ancestor. State **three** similarities or differences between them that would result from this shared ancestry and subsequent divergence.    [3 marks]

2.
    1. Name and explain which type of speciation is likely to have occurred between chimpanzees and bonobos. [2 marks]

    2. Describe the role of differential selection in the speciation event between chimpanzees and bonobos.                [2 marks]

3.
    1. Explain how the process of speciation affects biodiversity.                 [1 mark]

    2. Suggest why gradual evolutionary change within a single species is not considered speciation in the absence of reproductive isolation.        [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following *(at least **one** from each section)*:

    ***Similarities:***
    $\ce{ -> }$ similar DNA base sequences **OR** similar mitochondrial DNA sequences             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ similar amino acid sequences **OR** cytochrome c **OR** named example         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ similar features **OR** morphology **OR** homologous structures **OR** named example     [1 mark]

    ***Differences:***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ differences in some gene sequences **OR** proteins due to gene mutations since divergence  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ differences in specific behaviours **OR** social structures      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ differences in morphology due to adaptations since divergence          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ unable to breed and produce fertile offspring         [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Chimpanzees and bonobos have **similar DNA base sequences** ($\checkmark$) and **amino acid sequences** in **proteins** ($\checkmark$) because they share a recent common ancestor. However, they are now **unable to breed and produce fertile offspring** ($\checkmark$), which is why they are not the same species.

2.
    1. any **two** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ allopatric speciation          [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ a geographical barrier **OR** a river separates the two populations, preventing gene flow between them         [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
        It is likely to be **allopatric** speciation ($\checkmark$) because the **Congo River separates** the two populations, **preventing gene flow** ($\checkmark$).

    2. any **two** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ different environmental conditions **OR** selection pressures in the separated populations favoured different phenotypes         [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ natural selection acted differently on each population, increasing the frequency of different alleles in each group             [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ over time, genetic differences accumulated        [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ this led to reproductive isolation as the populations became too genetically distinct to interbreed     [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
        DIfferential selection helped lead to speciation because there were **different environmental conditions** ($\checkmark$) in the different places, This meant **different alleles** were selected and **increased in frequency** ($\checkmark$).

3.
    1.

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ speciation increases biodiversity **BECAUSE** it raises the total number of species on Earth    [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    $\hspace{1em}$ Speciation leads to two species where there was previously one, so it will **increase** the total **number of species**, which **increases biodiversity** ($\checkmark$).

    2. any **two** of the following:

        $\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolation is needed to prevent interbreeding **OR** to separate gene pools    [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ without this, individuals can still mate and produce fertile offspring **OR** alleles are shared within the population                    [1 mark]

        $\ce{ -> }$ gradual changes within a species without reproductive isolation represent variation within a species **NOT** the formation of a new species        [1 mark]

        [2 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
        Reproductive isolation is needed to **prevent interbreeding** ($\checkmark$). If individuals can still interbreed, they can **share alleles across the gene pool** ($\checkmark$). This means there is no accumulation of differences, which leads to speciation.
