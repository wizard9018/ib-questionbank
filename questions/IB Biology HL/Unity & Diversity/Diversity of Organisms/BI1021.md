2. **BOTH** of the following (answer must include at least one similarity and one difference to achieve full marks):

    **Similarities** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** seek to define a species     [1 mark]

    **Differences** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept refers to species being groups of organisms with shared physical traits, **WHEREAS** the biological species concept refers to species being groups of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Linnaeus proposed the morphological species concept **WHEREAS** the biological species concept was proposed by Mayr     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept is applicable to sexual and asexual organisms **HOWEVER**, the biological species concept is only applicable to sexually reproducing organisms     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept was proposed before the biological species concept [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ The biological species concept reflects the evolutionary relationship, **WHEREAS** morphological species concept may not represent genetic relatedness or evolutionary relationships     [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Both** the morphological and biological species concepts **aim to define** what constitutes a **species** ($\checkmark$) in the field of biology. However, the **morphological** species concept defines species based on their **shared physical characteristics**, whereas the **biological** species concept defines species based on the ability of individuals to **interbreed and produce fertile offspring** under natural conditions ($\checkmark$).
