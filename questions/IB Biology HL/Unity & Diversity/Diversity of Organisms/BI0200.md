# BI0200

**Maximum mark: 4**

The table below shows the genome size and diploid chromosome numbers of five different species.

| Organism | Chromosome number | Genome size (millions of base pairs)
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| T4 phage (virus) | Single piece of DNA | 0.17 |
| *Escherichia coli* (bacteria) | 1 | 5 |
| *Drosophila melanogaster* (fruit fly) | 8 | 140|
| *Homo sapiens* (human)| 46 | 3,000 |
| *Paris japonica* (woodland plant)| 40 | 150,000 |

1. Evaluate the evidence from the table in relation to the statement that “organisms with a higher number of chromosomes do not always have more base pairs”. [2 marks]

2. Discuss the data as evidence for the notion that increased genome size means that the organism is more complex. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ (comparison of) *Paris japonica* **AND** *Homo sapiens* data support the statement    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ *P. japonica* has fewer chromosomes than *H. sapiens* but has more base pairs **OR** *P. japonica* has 40 chromosomes with 150,000 million base pairs while *H. sapiens* has 46 chromosomes with 3,000 million base pairs.[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (comparison of) *E.coli*, *D. melanogaster* and *P. japonica* **OR** *H. sapiens* data does not support the statement                            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (because) as the number of chromosomes increases, so does the genome size, **OR** the number of base pairs [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The data shows that for all organisms apart from *Paris japonica* that higher chromosome number has a larger genome (greater number of base pairs). *Paris japonica* has **fewer chromosomes** ($\checkmark$) than *Homo sapiens* but has a **larger genome** ($\checkmark$). ***P. japonica*** ($\checkmark$) has 40 chromosomes which are less than ***Homo sapiens*** ($\checkmark$) but has 150,000 million base pairs compared to 3,000 million base pairs in *Homo sapiens*.

2. Any **two** of the following:

    **Supporting the statement**:

    $\ce{ -> }$ *Escherichia coli* is the simplest one-cell organism and has the smallest genome size **OR** has 5 million base pairs compared to more complex multicellular organisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ T4 phage is not a living organism and has a smaller genome than any of the living organisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fruit flies are simpler organisms than humans and have a smaller genome [1 mark]

    **Not supporting the statement**:

    $\ce{ -> }$ *Homo sapiens* is considered the most complex organism in the table but does not have the largest genome, **OR** *H. sapiens* has 3,000 million base pairs compared to 150,000 in *P. japonica*. [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a larger genome does not necessarily mean that an organism has more genes  [1 mark]

    *If only one side of the argument is mentioned (but not both), the maximum mark is one.*

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
The data **does not fully support** the statement that more complex organisms have a larger genome. \
For example, ***Homo sapiens*** is the most complex organism in the table but does not have the largest genome ($\checkmark$). ***Paris japonica*** , a woodland plant, has 150,000 million base pairs compared to 3,000 in *Homo sapiens*, which does not support the statement that more complex organisms have larger genomes because plants are simpler organisms than humans and achieve bigger genomes mostly due to polyploidy. ($\checkmark$) \
On the other hand, ***Escherichia coli*** is the simplest living organism in the table and has the smallest genome size, 5 million base pairs. The other organisms are more complex and have larger genomes, e.g. *Homo sapiens* has 3,000 million base pairs.  ($\checkmark$)  T4 page is not considered to be a living organism. The above statements **support** the notion that the increased genome size means that the organism is more complex.
