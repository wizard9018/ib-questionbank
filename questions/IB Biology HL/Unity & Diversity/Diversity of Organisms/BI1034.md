2.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ the biological species concept defines a species as a group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring, **WHEREAS** the morphological concept refers to species as groups of organisms having shared characteristics    [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ as the three vary significantly in their physical characteristics, the morphological concept wouldn’t identify them as the same species, **OR** they look different but can still interbreed to produce fertile offspring    [1 mark]
[2 marks]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ The **biological species concept defines species as groups of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring**; however, the **morphological concept refers only to species with shared physical characteristics** ($\checkmark$). So, because the **three forms of *Ensatina eschscholtzii* look different**, the morphological species concept **would not define them as the same species** ($\checkmark$), but if they interbreed, they can produce fertile offspring, so they are the same species according to the biological species concept.
