# BI1028

Which of the following would provide the strongest evidence for two populations belonging to different species?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | I. | Differences in genome base sequences across all members of two populations |
    | II. | Differences in genome size across all members of two populations |
    | III. | Difference in chromosome number across all members of two populations

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ III only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice A**  is correct because only **Statement III** provides the strongest evidence that two populations belong to different species. A consistent difference in chromosome number across all members of two populations is a strong indicator of reproductive isolation and speciation, as different chromosome numbers typically prevent successful interbreeding and the production of fertile offspring.

 **Statements I** and **II** (differences in genome base sequences and genome size) can arise through mutation and vary within a single species, making them insufficient on their own as evidence of different species.
