# BI1527

**Maximum mark: 16**

1.  Describe how the primordial soup hypothesis (an aqueous mix of simple organic molecules) explains the origin of carbon compounds on Earth. [3 marks]

2.  Mitochondria are organelles that are thought to have evolved from early prokaryotes. Discuss whether mitochondria can be considered living or nonliving. [4 marks]

3.  Discuss the evidence and limitations for the existence of a last universal common ancestor. [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ early atmosphere **OR** oceans contained a mixture of gases **OR** carbon dioxide AND methane **OR** water vapour **OR** ammonia **OR** hydrogen gas [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high levels of UV **OR** ultraviolet light **OR** electrical discharges **OR** lightning catalysed reactions in these gases    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the gases broke down into smaller, highly reactive components    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the smaller components reacted to form larger carbon compounds **OR** more complex organic molecules    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ abiogenesis **OR** chemical evolution    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in water **OR** pools **OR** oceans    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    The primordial soup hypothesis states that billions of years ago, there were much **higher levels of UV light** on Earth which **catalysed** reactions in the **gases** of the early atmosphere ($\checkmark$). The UV radiation **broke down** inorganic molecules into **smaller, highly reactive** components ($\checkmark$). The smaller, highly reactive components reacted to **form larger carbon compounds** ($\checkmark$).    

2.  Any **four** of the following:\

    *Answers must include at least one argument for living and one argument for non-living to gain full marks*\

    **Living because…**

    $\ce{ -> }$ they can replicate independently of the host cell **OR** carry our binary fission    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they contain their own ribosomes to make their own proteins    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they contain their own DNA **OR** genetic material    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they can make their own ATP **OR** release energy from organic molecules **OR** carry out a series of metabolic reactions independent of the host cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they have their own membranes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they can grow larger    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they can excrete waste **OR** carbon dioxide    [1 mark]

    **Non-living because…**

    $\ce{ -> }$ they cannot survive independently outside of a host cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the majority of proteins needed for mitochondrial function are synthesised in the host cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they do not regulate homeostasis independently of the host cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they cannot respond to stimuli **OR** adapt to changing environments    [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    Mitochondria can be thought of as non-living because they **cannot survive independently** outside the host cell ($\checkmark$) and the majority of **proteins** needed for mitochondrial function are **made in the host cell** ($\checkmark$). However, it could be argued that they are living as they carry out several life processes, such as **reproduction, independently** of the host cell ($\checkmark$), and contain their own **ribosomes** which they use to **make** their own **proteins** ($\checkmark$).

3.  Any **eight** of the following:\

    *Answers must include at least one argument in support of a last universal common ancestor (LUCA) and at least one gap in the evidence to gain full marks*

    ***Evidence that exists for the existence of a last universal common ancestor***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ universal genetic code **OR** all organisms have the same nucleotide bases **OR** genetic code     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ conserved molecular structures **OR** all organisms have the same degenerate amino acid code **OR** the same codons code for the same amino acids in all organisms **OR** there are highly conserved DNA sequences that are common across many domains of life    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ genomic analysis of fossils shows that there are many similarities in the DNA sequences between modern and extinct organisms    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ specific proteins produced by different organisms can be used interchangeably between species without any major side effects    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ all organisms have a similar process for gene transcription and protein synthesis **OR** transcription and translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations in DNA **OR** amino acid sequences can be tracked over time and used as a ‘molecular clock' to estimate the age of the last universal common ancestor    [1 mark]

    **Gaps in the evidence for the existence of a last universal common ancestor**\

    $\ce{ -> }$ incomplete fossil record makes it difficult to link all species together anatomically    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ very early organisms were unicellular **OR** made of soft tissues that do not readily fossilise    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lack of direct fossil evidence for the last universal common ancestor    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the last universal common ancestor does not exist today    [1 mark]

    [8 marks]        

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    The last universal common ancestor (LUCA) is proposed to be the evolutionary link between the abiotic phase of Earth's history and the biotic phase. It is the last ancestor that links all biological life on Earth. Evidence for the LUCA's existence comes from the fact that organisms are all biochemically similar; they share the **same DNA bases** ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, the **same codons** code for the **same amino acids** in all organisms ($\checkmark$), and all organisms have a similar process for **gene transcription and protein synthesis**($\checkmark$). Also, there are some **highly conserved sequences** across the majority of organisms such as **ribosomal RNA** which is common across all domains of life ($\checkmark$). **Genomic analysis** of fossils also shows that there are many similar DNA sequences between **extinct and modern organisms** ($\checkmark$). However, there **isn't** a **complete fossil record** which makes it difficult to link all species anatomically ($\checkmark$), there is **no direct fossil evidence** for the existence of the LUCA ($\checkmark$) and the LUCA does **not exist** on Earth **today** ($\checkmark$).
