# BI1191

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the role of auxin in positive phototropism. [4 marks]

2. Explain the evidence that supports the theory that eukaryotic cells evolved from prokaryotic cells. [5 marks]

3. Explain how the products of the light-dependent stage of photosynthesis are used in the light-independent stage to convert carbon dioxide into organic substances. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ light is detected by group of proteins **OR** phototropins which trigger the movement of auxins by active transport [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ auxin efflux pumps move auxin so the concentration of auxins is more on the shadier side
and lesser on the side with more light [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ plant cells have auxin receptors [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ when auxin binds to these receptors ,transcription of genes for enzyme **OR** expansin [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ auxin causes movement of hydrogen ions from cytoplasm into the cell walls [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen ions and expansion **OR** enzyme loosens cellulose fibres **OR** fibres of cell wall [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ causes cells on the shaded side of shoot tip to elongate [1 mark]
 [4 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Light is detected** by **phototropins** and this triggers the movement of auxins by **active transport** ($\checkmark$). Auxin **efflux pumps** move auxins from **cytoplasm** into **cell walls**, so that the **concentration** of auxin is **more** on the **shadier side** ($\checkmark$) and less on the side with more light. When auxin binds to **receptors** on the cells, **genes** for the enzyme **expansin** are **expressed** ($\checkmark$). **Expansin**, and the **pumping of protons** from the cytoplasm into the cell wall cause the **loosening** of **cellulose fibres** ($\checkmark$) in the cell walls, leading to **elongation** of the **cells** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ theory of endosymbiosis proposes that mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts have evolved from free-living prokaryotes that were engulfed **OR** taken in by endocytosis, by larger cells [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts have naked DNA [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts divide by binary fission [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts divide independently of cell cycle **OR** cell [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts about same size as prokaryotes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts have 70S ribosomes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts can synthesise their own proteins [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts have double membranes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts have inner membrane like **OR** similar in structure to prokaryotes **AND** outer membrane like **OR** similar in structure to eukaryotes
 [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA of mitochondria **OR** chloroplasts is more closely related **OR** more similar to prokaryotic DNA than to nuclear DNA of eukaryotic cells, **OR** rRNA **OR** ribosomal RNA more closely related **OR** more similar to prokaryotes than eukaryotes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ observations of modern examples of endosymbiosis OR given examples of present day endosymbiosis, provide support for the theory [1 mark]

 [5 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The endosymbiotic **theory** supports the evidence that **mitochondria** and **chloroplasts** in eukaryotic cells **evolved from prokaryotic** cells, which were taken in by larger cells through **endocytosis** ($\checkmark$). The evidence supporting this theory includes the fact that these organelles have **naked DNA** ($\checkmark$), divide by **binary fission** ($\checkmark$), can **synthesise** their **own proteins** ($\checkmark$) and have **double membranes** around the outside ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **seven** of the following: (drawing showing the Calvin cycle with the steps described below):

 $\ce{ -> }$ products of the light-dependent reaction are essential for the light-independent reactions [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ light-dependent reactions produce ATP, NADPH and oxygen [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ATP and NADPH are used in the light-independent reactions **OR** Calvin cycle [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ light-independent reactions convert carbon dioxide into glucose and other organic molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ causes 1 x RuBP to bind to 1 x carbon dioxide [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ 6 C compound is unstable and forms two 3C compounds of glycerate-3-phosphate **OR** GP [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ATP and NADPH are used to convert glycerate-3-phosphate **OR** GP to triose phosphate **OR** TP [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ some of the triose phosphate **OR** TP leaves the cycle to form carbohydrates, most is used to reform RuBP [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ regeneration of RuBP requires energy in the form of ATP [1 mark]
 [7 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 **Light-dependent** reactions of photosynthesis produce **ATP, NADPH** ($\checkmark$) and oxygen. Oxygen is a by-product, ATP and NADPH are used in the light-independent reactions. In the stroma of the chloroplast, **carbon dioxide** combines with **RuBP** ($\checkmark$), a 5C compound catalysed by the enzyme **Rubisco** ($\checkmark$). The **6C compound** formed is **unstable** and splits into **two molecules of glycerate-3-phosphate** ($\checkmark$).

 **ATP and NADPH** ($\checkmark$) are used to convert **glycerate-3-phosphate into triose phosphate** ($\checkmark$). Some of the **molecules** of **triose phosphate leave** the **cycle** to form **glucose** ($\checkmark$) or other carbohydrates, **most** of it is **used** to **regenerate** the **5C RuBP** ($\checkmark$). **Regeneration** of **RuBP** again requires **ATP** ($\checkmark$) obtained from the light-dependent reactions.
