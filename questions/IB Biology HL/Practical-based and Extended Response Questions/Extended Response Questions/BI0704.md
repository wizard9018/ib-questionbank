# BI0704

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how polygenic traits can show a normal distribution. [4 marks]

2. Outline the differences and similarities between homologous chromosomes.     [4 marks]

3. Explain why expected and observed Mendelian ratios may differ. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic traits are caused by two or more genes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the greater the number of genes that are responsible for a trait, the greater the number of phenotypes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic traits often show continuous variation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ which is a continuous range of variation rather than distinct categories [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is a large range of phenotypes, where the majority of organisms are in the middle of the normal distribution curve [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic traits are often influenced by the environment [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Polygenic traits are caused by **two or more genes** ($\checkmark$). The **higher the number of genes** that are responsible for a trait, the **higher the number of possible phenotypes** ($\checkmark$) which means that the traits show **continuous variation** ($\checkmark$). Continuous variation is when a phenotype shows a **continuous range of variation**, rather than having distinct categories ($\checkmark$) and usually shows a normal distribution, where the majority of organisms are in the **middle of the normal distribution curve** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following (at least **ONE** marking statement should come from the similarities and **ONE** from the differences):

    *Similarities:*\
    $\ce{ -> }$ length [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ centromeres are  in the same position [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sequence of genes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gene loci **OR** position of genes [1 mark]

    *Differences:*

    $\ce{ -> }$ alleles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ one chromosome is inherited maternally and one chromosome is inherited paternally [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Homologous chromosomes are pairs of chromosomes with the same genes, but **different alleles** ($\checkmark$). In a pair, one chromosome is inherited **paternally** and one is inherited **maternally** ($\checkmark$). Homologous chromosomes are similar in that they are the **same length** ($\checkmark$), have their **centromeres in the same place** ($\checkmark$) and have the **same sequence** ($\checkmark$) and **loci** of genes ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ mendelian ratios are the proportions of genotypes that would be expected from a punnett square     [1 mark]

    *Observed and expected ratio may be different due to:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ genes are not inherited as expected [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ interactions between genes and the environment     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ environment affects some phenotypes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ autosomal linkage **OR** when genes are found on the same autosome [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sex linkage **OR** when genes are found on the sex chromosomes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ so do not segregate independently during meiosis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when linked genes are far apart on the same chromosome, crossing over can occur making them seem unlinked  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organisms inheriting diseases that are fatal to embryos disrupts phenotypic ratios    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic inheritance **OR** inheritance caused by two or more genes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when scientists incorrectly assume that a trait is determined by one gene     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if inheritance is monogenic, but two different genes result in the same phenotype     [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Mendelian ratios are the **proportions of genotypes or phenotypes that are expected from a given cross** ($\checkmark$).  However, observed ratios are not always the same as expected ratios.  This can be due to a number of factors including random **mutations** ($\checkmark$), and **interactions between genes and the environment** ($\checkmark$);  the **environment** sometimes alters a **phenotype** ($\checkmark$). Observed and expected values can also be affected by **autosomal gene linkage** ($\checkmark$) which is when genes are found on the same autosome.  Autosomally linked genes **do not segregate independently during meiosis** ($\checkmark$). **Linked genes** that are found **far apart** from each other ont he same chromosome are more likely to **become unlinked** due to **crossing over** ($\checkmark$). Observed and expected values can also differ if organisms inherit diseases that are **fatal to embryos, which changes phenotypic ratios** ($\checkmark$).
