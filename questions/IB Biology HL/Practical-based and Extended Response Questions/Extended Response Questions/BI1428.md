# BI1428

**Maximum mark: 16**

Organisms have adapted over time to derive nutrients from their environments and protect themselves from being preyed upon.

1. Compare and contrast between chemosynthesis and photosynthesis.             [5 marks]

2. Outline how differences in dentition and jaw structure between chimpanzees (*Pan troglodytes*) and gorillas (*Gorilla gorilla*) are directly related to their respective diets. [3 marks]

3. The ‘evolutionary arms race' between predators and prey is an ongoing process of adaptation and counter-adaptation. Explain how named predators and named prey have adapted physical traits that prevent or aid predation.      [8 marks]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemosynthesis uses the energy from the oxidation of inorganic compounds **WHEREAS** photosynthesis uses energy from light to excite electrons to release energy        [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Both** chemosynthesis and photosynthesis are carried out by **autotrophs** ($\checkmark$) and result in the **synthesis of glucose** ($\checkmark$). However, **chemosynthesis** is a process in which organisms use the energy from the **oxidation of inorganic compounds** to produce glucose, with the release of **sulfur or nitrogen compounds** as **by-products**, whereas **photosynthesis** uses **light energy** ($\checkmark$) to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose, with the **release of oxygen** ($\checkmark$). **Photosynthesis** is carried out by **plants, algae** and **cyanobacteria**, whereas **chemosynthesis** is carried out by certain **archaea** and **bacteria** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ gorillas are mainly herbivores **WHEREAS** chimpanzees are mainly omnivores **OR** fruit eaters with occasional meat consumption        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gorillas have large masseter muscles **OR** temporal muscles which allow them to grind up **OR** bite through plants **OR** fibrous material **WHEREAS** chimpanzees have less developed masseter muscles **OR** temporal muscles as they do not need to grind up **OR** bite through plants **OR** fibrous material         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gorillas have a sagittal crest **OR** ridge of bone running along the centre of their skull for their strong temporal muscles to attach to **WHEREAS** chimpanzees have a less developed sagittal crest as they do not need to strongly bite through fibrous plant material [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Gorillas** are predominantly **herbivores**, whereas **chimpanzees** are mainly **omnivores**($\checkmark$). Therefore **gorillas** have **larger** and **stronger masseter** and **temporal muscles**, compared to chimpanzees,  which allows them to **grind up** and **bite** through **fibrous plant tissue** ($\checkmark$). **Gorillas** also have **wider, flatter premolars** and **molars** compared to chimpanzees, which enables gorillas to **grind up fibrous plants**($\checkmark$). **Chimpanzees** have more **pronounced canines** than gorillas to support their **omnivorous diet**, including the **cutting** and **tearing** of **meat**($\checkmark$).

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Adaptations of **predators** include having **eyes** on the **front** of their **heads**, which improves their **depth perception** so they can accurately gauge how far away prey are, for example, **eagles** can spot prey from a **great height** and **dive accurately** to capture it ($\checkmark$). **Predators** may also have **strong muscles** and a **streamlined shape** which allow them to **move quickly** to catch their prey, for example, the shape of **penguins** reduces drag, allowing them to **swim fast** ($\checkmark$), and **cheetahs** have **powerful leg muscles** to provide **power** for **rapid acceleration** ($\checkmark$). **Prey** also have **strong muscles**, which allow them to **move fast** and escape predators, for example, **rabbits** have powerful muscles for **bursts** of **speed** and to **turn quickly** ($\checkmark$). **Prey** often have **eyes** on the **sides** of their **heads**, which allows them to **spot predators** coming from **all directions**, for example, **frogs** have eyes on the side of their heads so they can **remain alert** to predators while **remaining stationary** ($\checkmark$). **Prey** may also have the ability to **camouflage** with their surroundings to **prevent detection**, for example, the **peppered moth** evolved **darker colouration** during the Industrial Revolution to better hide on **soot-covered trees** ($\checkmark$). **Predators** can also use the ability to **camouflage** with their surroundings to their advantage by blending in with their surroundings so that they are **not spotted by prey**, for example, the **arctic fox changes colour** with the **seasons**, so is **white** in **winter** to blend in with the snow and **brown** in **summer** to match the rocks and plants ($\checkmark$). **Mimicry** is also used by **prey** to avoid being attacked by predators. An example of this is the **larvae** of some **butterfly** species resemble the **plants** they feed on which are **poisonous** to many predators, and as they feed, they **sequester toxins** from the plant, becoming **toxic** themselves ($\checkmark$).
