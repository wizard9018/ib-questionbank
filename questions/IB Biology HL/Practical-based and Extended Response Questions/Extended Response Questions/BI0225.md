# BI0225

**Maximum mark: 16**
1. Describe how parents with blood type A and blood type B can have a child with blood type O.     [4 marks]

2.    Outline, using a named example, how polygenic inheritance results in continuous variation [4 marks]

3.    Explain the role of inheritance in genetic diseases.    [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    any **four** of the following (accept any marking point clearly shown in a genetic diagram)

    $\ce{ -> }$ the allele for blood group A **OR** allele I$^A$, is dominant over the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the allele for blood group B **OR** allele I$^B$, is dominant over the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ parents with blood group A or blood group B could be heterozygous **OR** I$^A$i and I$^B$i [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a parent who is heterozygous for blood group A can pass on the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a parent who is heterozygous for blood group B can pass on the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ offspring that receive the allele for blood type O **OR** i allele from both parents will be blood group O    [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    The alleles for blood type A and blood type B are **dominant** to the allele for blood type O ($\checkmark$). Parents with blood type A or B could be **heterozygous** ($\checkmark$) and possess the recessive allele for blood type O ($\checkmark$). If the offspring **inherits the recessive allele** ($\checkmark$) from both parents, then they will have blood type O.

    Parents:     $\hspace{2.5em}$      **I$^A$ i**  $\hspace{3em}$ x   $\hspace{2.5em}$  **I$^B$ i**

    Gametes:        $\hspace{1.5em}$     **I$^A$ and i**  $\hspace{1.5em}$ x   $\hspace{1.5em}$  **I$^B$ and i**

    Punnett square:

    | | I$^B$ | i |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | **I$^A$**|  $\hspace{1.5em}$ I$^A$ I$^B$ $\hspace{1.5em}$  |  $\hspace{1.5em}$ I$^A$ i  $\hspace{1.5em}$  |
    | **i** |  $\hspace{1.5em}$ I$^B$ i $\hspace{1.5em}$  |  $\hspace{1.5em}$ i i $\hspace{1.5em}$  |

    Genotypes of offspring: $\hspace{1.5em}$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ **I$^A$ I$^B$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ :  $\hspace{1.5em}$ I$^A$ i  $\hspace{1.5em}$ : $\hspace{1.5em}$ I$^B$ i $\hspace{1.5em}$ : $\hspace{1.5em}$ i i $\hspace{1.5em}$**

2.  any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ human skin colour OR any other suitable example                [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ at least three OR several genes control phenotype                [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ each gene has two or more alleles contributing to the phenotype            [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ no single allele is solely responsible for the phenotype                [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ many possible allele combinations                         [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ additive OR cumulative effect of alleles results in continuous range of phenotypes    [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ phenotype shows a bell-shaped OR normal distribution in the population        [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $ \underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    **Human skin colour** ($\checkmark$) is controlled by **several genes**  ($\checkmark$).  **Each allele** has a small **dditive effect** on the amount of melanin produced.  The **many possible combinations** of **alleles** ($\checkmark$) result in a **continuous range**  ($\checkmark$) of skin colours.

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic diseases can be caused by inherited mutant alleles [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ alleles are passed to offspring via haploid gametes **OR** sperm and eggs [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ gametes carry one allele per gene [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ fertilisation restores diploidy **OR** combines parental alleles [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ allele combination **OR** genotype influences whether genetic disease is expressed [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ autosomal dominant disease require one disease allele to be expressed [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ huntington's disease **OR** any other valid autosomal dominant example, is an autosomal dominant condition [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ autosomal recessive disease requires two disease alleles to be expressed [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ heterozygotes can be carriers of autosomal recessive diseases [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ phenylketonuria (PKU) **OR**  any other valid autosomal recessive example, is an autosomal recessive condition [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ many sex-linked genes are carried on the X chromosome [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ X-linked recessive diseases are more common in biological males [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ haemophilia **OR**  any other valid X-linked recessive example, is an X-linked recessive condition [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ affected X-linked recessive biological mother will pass diesease to all biological sons [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ carrier X-linked recessive biological mother will pass disease to 50% of biological sons **OR** 50% biological daughters, if father unaffected [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    Genetic diseases can be caused by **inheriting mutant alleles** of genes ($\checkmark$). Alleles are passed from parents to **offspring in haploid gametes** ($\checkmark$), and **fertilisation** combines **one allele** from **each parent** to form the offspring's genotype ($\checkmark$). If a disease allele is **dominant**, only **one copy** is needed for the condition to be **expressed** ($\checkmark$), for example, **Huntington's disease** ($\checkmark$). If a disease allele is **recessive, two copies** are needed for the condition to be **expressed** ($\checkmark$), and people with **one recessive** allele are **carriers** who can pass it on ($\checkmark$). Some genetic diseases are **sex-linked** because the gene is on the **X chromosome**, so X-linked recessive diseases are **more common** in biological **males** ($\checkmark$).
