# BI1311

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how the cerebellum helps control movement.    [3 marks]

2. Describe how the hypothalamus coordinates nervous and hormonal responses to regulate body temperature in cold environmental conditions. [5 marks]

3. Explain how negative feedback helps control the rate and depth of ventilation during strenuous exercise.    [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ helps maintain posture by adjusting the activity of muscles that support the body [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ helps maintain balance by coordinating muscular adjustments when body position changes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ coordinates the timing **OR** force of skeletal muscle contractions, producing smooth, precise movements **OR** fine-tunes movements, such as precise hand movements [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ contributes to motor learning, improving the accuracy and coordination of movements through practice [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    The cerebellum helps **maintain posture** by adjusting the **activity of muscles that support the body** ($\checkmark$). It helps **maintain balance** by coordinating **muscular adjustments when body position changes** ($\checkmark$). It also **coordinates the timing and force of skeletal muscle contractions**, making movements **smooth and precise** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ receives sensory nerve impulses from peripheral thermoreceptors, which detect changes in skin temperature [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ thermoreceptors within hypothalamus detect changes in the blood temperature [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ coordinates shivering in skeletal muscles which increases heat production [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ stimulates brown adipose tissue, increasing heat production through uncoupled respiration [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ coordinates vasoconstriction of skin arterioles, reducing blood flow to skin surface, reducing heat loss [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ stimulates pituitary hormone release, which stimulates thyroid gland to release thyroxin [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ thyroxin increases metabolic rate of target cells, increasing heat production [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ responses operate through negative feedback, counteracting initial temperature change **OR** as body temperature returns towards normal range, the corrective responses are reduced [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    In cold conditions, **peripheral thermoreceptors** detect a **decrease in skin temperature** and send **sensory nerve impulses to the hypothalamus** ($\checkmark$). The hypothalamus coordinates **shivering in skeletal muscles**, increasing **heat production** ($\checkmark$).

    It also stimulates the **pituitary gland** to release a hormone that stimulates the **thyroid gland to release thyroxin** ($\checkmark$). Thyroxin **increases the metabolic rate** of target cells, increasing **heat production** ($\checkmark$).

    As body temperature **returns towards its normal range**, these heat-producing responses are reduced through **negative feedback** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the respiratory centre is in the medulla of the hindbrain [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ strenuous exercise results in increased rate of aerobic cell respiration in active muscle cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ carbon dioxide is produced more rapidly by active muscle cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ results in increased blood carbon dioxide concentration [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid with water, which dissociates; hydrogen ions released which lower blood pH [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high carbon dioxide levels **OR** low blood pH detected by chemoreceptors / hydrogen ion detectors in brain [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peripheral chemoreceptors **OR** chemoreceptors in carotid arteries / aortic bodies, detect a fall in blood pH [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemoreceptors **OR** hydrogen ion detectors send impulses to the inspiratory centre [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inspiratory centre alters the signals sent through motor neurons to the diaphragm **AND** external intercostal muscles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ these muscles contract more frequently **AND** more forcefully **OR** increase rate **AND** depth of ventilation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased ventilation removes carbon dioxide more rapidly, helping blood pH to return towards normal range [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as carbon dioxide concentration / pH return towards normal ranges, chemoreceptor stimulation decreases, reducing response through negative feedback [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    During strenuous exercise, active muscles **produce carbon dioxide more rapidly**, releasing it into the **bloodstream** ($\checkmark$). If carbon dioxide accumulates, it **reacts with water to form carbonic acid, which dissociates** ($\checkmark$) and **releases hydrogen ions and lowers pH** ($\checkmark$).

    **Chemoreceptors** in the brainstem detect the associated **decrease in the pH** of their surrounding fluid ($\checkmark$). The **respiratory centre** responds by altering **motor nerve signals to the diaphragm and external intercostal muscles** ($\checkmark$). These muscles **contract more frequently and forcefully**, increasing the **rate and depth of ventilation** ($\checkmark$).

    **Increased ventilation** removes carbon dioxide more rapidly, helping to **counteract the fall in pH** ($\checkmark$). As **carbon dioxide concentration and pH return towards their normal ranges**, chemoreceptor stimulation decreases, reducing the corrective response through **negative feedback** ($\checkmark$).
