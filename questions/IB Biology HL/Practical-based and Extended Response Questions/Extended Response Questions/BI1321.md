# BI1321

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Explain why the processes that occur in interphase are necessary for meiosis.    [4 marks]

2. Outline the process of DNA replication in prokaryotes.        [5 marks]

3. Explain the factors that can result in increased and decreased genetic variation within a population.                    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ replication of DNA **OR** chromosomes **OR** genetic material is necessary to produce sister chromatids for homologous pair separation in meiosis I **OR** to ensure gametes receive one chromosome of each homologous pair            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell growth so that daughter cells are the correct size **OR** can fit all of the organelles    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ duplication of organelles **OR** mitochondria **OR** any other named necessary organelle for gametes, to ensure that daughter cells **OR** gametes have the organelles required for division        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ duplication of centrosomes **OR** microtubule organising structure so that homologous pairs can be separated in meiosis I **OR** sister chromatids can be separated during meiosis II    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ protein synthesis in the cytoplasm to ensure that the required proteins are found in the daughter cells **OR** gametes                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ respiration to provide energy **OR** ATP for interphase **OR** for meiosis     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ checkpoint control repairs errors in DNA replication which prevents transmission of damaged DNA to gametes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cyclin production regulates the progression through different phases of the cell cycle    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During interphase, the **DNA** is **replicated** to produce **sister chromatids** for the **separation** of **homologous pairs**  in **meiosis I**  ($\checkmark$) and the **cell grows** so that **daughter cells** are **large enough** to receive all the **organelles** they require ($\checkmark$). The **organelles** are **duplicated** so that the **gametes** receive all the **required organelles** to be **functional**  ($\checkmark$). In animal cells, the **centrosomes** are **duplicated** to allow **spindle formation** for the **separation** of **homologous pairs** in **meiosis I** and of **sister chromatids** in **meiosis II** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is unwound by helicase **OR** DNA helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between base pairs **OR** strands of DNA                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ topoisomerase **OR** gyrase prevents DNA from becoming supercoiled [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each parental strand acts as a template for the synthesis of a new complementary strand    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (RNA) primase adds one primer to the leading strand **AND** many primers to the lagging strand **OR** (RNA) primase synthesises a short RNA primer complementary to the DNA template strand            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ replication is continuous on the leading strand **AND** discontinuous on the lagging strand    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase III binds to RNA primer(s)         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase III synthesises the new DNA strand         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ new DNA strand synthesised in the 5'-3' direction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ using complementary base pairing between nucleotides **OR** A with T and G with C on the template strand                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ on the lagging strand, DNA polymerase III creates Okazaki fragments        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase I replaces the RNA primers with DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerases carry out proofreading to detect and correct errors during replication, increasing replication accuracy                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ligase sticks the DNA fragments together            [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During DNA replication, the DNA strands are **separated** by DNA **helicase** ($\checkmark$), and the enzyme **topoisomerase prevents** the DNA from becoming **supercoiled** ($\checkmark$), which would damage the DNA. DNA **primase** adds **one** primer to the **leading** strand ($\checkmark$), which it replicates **continuously** ($\checkmark$) and many primers to the **lagging** strand, which it replicates **discontinuously**. DNA **polymerase III** binds to the RNA **primers** ($\checkmark$) and **synthesises** the new DNA strand in the **5'-3'** direction.

3. any **seven** of the following (*Answer must include at least **one** factor from each category to achieve full marks*):

    ***Factors that increase variation*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over of non-sister chromatids in prophase 1 of meiosis results in recombination of alleles **OR** exchange of genetic material                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ independent assortment  **OR** random assortment of homologous chromosomes in metaphase 1 of meiosis, results in different combinations of chromosomes in gametes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sexual reproduction results in the random fusion of gametes during fertilisation        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ migration of organisms **OR** gene flow can lead to speciation by reproductive isolation **OR** geographic isolation            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ behavioural isolation **OR** temporal isolation can lead to speciation        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations can lead to differences between individuals in a population **OR** introduce new alleles into the gene pool            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a larger population means that there is a higher likelihood of different mutations arising **OR** different selection pressures acting on the population **OR** more mates to choose from                            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ errors during cell division can cause polyploid organisms    [1 mark]

    ***Factors that decrease variation*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ small population size means that there is likely to be less mutations **OR** less mates to choose from **OR** a higher chance of inbreeding            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ barriers to migration **OR** genetic drift **OR** geographic isolation **OR** ecological isolation **OR** behavioural isolation can lead to reduced gene flow    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ asexual reproduction **OR** budding results in clones **OR** organisms with the same genetic makeup in a population                            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ artificial selection **OR** selective breeding results in organisms in a population having similar traits                            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ natural selection **OR** directional selection **OR** stabilising selection tend to decrease variation                                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ population bottlenecks result in only a small subset of individuals surviving, leading to a loss of genetic diversity        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ founder effect **OR** when a small group of individuals establishes a new population in a different location, the gene pool of the founder population may not accurately represent the genetic diversity of the larger original population    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Factors that can result in increased genetic variation are **crossing over** during prophase 1 of meiosis, which results in **new combinations** of **alleles** ($\checkmark$), **random assortment** of homologous chromosomes during metaphase 1, which results in **different combinations** of **chromosomes** in gametes ($\checkmark$), **sexual reproduction** which results in the **random fusion of gametes** during fertilisation ($\checkmark$) and **migration** of individuals of a population which could lead to **speciation** through **reproductive isolation** ($\checkmark$). Factors that can result in decreased genetic variation are a **small population** size which means that there are **fewer mates** to choose from, which can lead to **inbreeding** ($\checkmark$), **barriers to migration**, such as physical changes to the environment, which **prevent** individuals from **leaving** their population ($\checkmark$) and **asexual reproduction** which results in a **genetically identical** population ($\checkmark$).
