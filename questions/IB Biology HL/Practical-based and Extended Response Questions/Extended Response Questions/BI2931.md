## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to gain full marks*):

    ***Similarities***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ **ALL** are polysaccharides        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **ALL** are made from glucose monomers            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **ALL** are held together by **OR** contain glycosidic bonds        [1 mark]

    ***Differences***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ starch is made up of amylose and amylopectin **OR** two different polysaccharides **WHEREAS** cellulose and glycogen are made up of a single type of polymer chain         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cellulose is composed of $\beta$-glucose monomers **WHEREAS** starch and glycogen are composed of $\alpha$-glucose monomers        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ amylose and cellulose form linear chains **WHEREAS** amylopectin and glycogen are branched polymers             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ amylose has only $\alpha$-1-4-glycosidic bonds **WHEREAS** amylopectin and glycogen have both $\alpha$-1-4 and $\alpha$-1-6 glycosidic bonds         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polymers of cellulose form many hydrogen bonds with each other **WHEREAS** starch and glycogen do not         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ compared to starch **OR** amylopectin **OR** amylose and cellulose, glycogen is more highly branched     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ every other monomer in cellulose is rotated 180$\degree$ **WHEREAS** in starch and glycogen the monomers are all oriented the same way         [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Cellulose, starch, and glycogen are **all polymers** of **glucose** monomers ($\checkmark$) joined by **glycosidic bonds** ($\checkmark$), with **cellulose** consisting of $\beta$**-glucose** and **starch and glycogen** composed of $\alpha$**-glucose** monomers ($\checkmark$). **Cellulose** and **amylose** form **linear chains**, whereas **amylopectin** and **glycogen** are **branched** polymers ($\checkmark$). **Amylose** contains $\alpha$**(1-4) glycosidic** bonds, while both **amylopectin** and **glycogen** contain both $\alpha$**(1-4)** and $\alpha$**(1-6)** glycosidic bonds, leading to their branching ($\checkmark$). **Cellulose** forms many **hydrogen bonds** between **polymer chains**, giving it structural strength, whereas **starch and glycogen do not** form these extensive bonds ($\checkmark$).

    All three are **polymers**, made up of only **glucose** monomers ($\checkmark$), joined together with **glycosidic bonds** ($\checkmark$).

    | Feature | Cellulose | Starch | Glycogen |
    | :-: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
    | Monomer | $\beta$**-glucose** | $\alpha$**-glucose** | $\alpha$**-glucose**  ($\checkmark$) |
    | Polymer Structure | **Unbranched** (linear) chains | Amylose (**unbranched**) and amylopectin (**branched**) ($\checkmark$) | **Highly branched** ($\checkmark$) |
    | Glycosidic Bonds | $\beta$**-1,4** glycosidic bonds | Amylose: $\alpha$**-1,4** glycosidic bonds  $\\$Amylopectin: $\alpha$**-1,4** and $\alpha$**-1,6** glycosidic bonds | $\alpha$**-1,4** and $\alpha$**-1,6** glycosidic bonds ($\checkmark$) |
    | Orientation of Monomers | Every other monomer is **rotated 180**$\degree$ | All monomers **oriented** the **same way** | All monomers oriented the **same way** ($\checkmark$) |
    | Hydrogen Bonding | Forms **many hydrogen bonds** between parallel chains to form microfibrils | **Do not** form extensive hydrogen bonds between chains | **Do not** form extensive hydrogen bonds between chains ($\checkmark$) |

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ phototropism is the directional growth response of plant shoots towards or away from light [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin is produced in shoot tip **OR** coleoptile in response to light        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin efflux carriers actively transport auxin to shaded side of shoot     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin binds to auxin receptors on target cells         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin stimulates activity of proton pumps in cell membrane **OR** causes movement of hydrogen ions into cell wall         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ auxin activates genes for expansins     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ low pH activates expansins         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ expansin enzymes break hydrogen bond **OR** cross links between cellulose molecules         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ change in cell wall allows more water to enter cell     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell turgor **OR** pressure from water in vacuole causes stretching **OR** elongation of cell wall  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ differential cell elongation on shaded side causes plant to bend towards light        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ feedback mechanism adjusts auxin distribution as shoot bends    [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Phototropism is the **directional growth response** of plant shoots **to light**, with the shoot bending towards the light source ($\checkmark$). The response is regulated by the phytohormone **auxin**, which is produced in the **shoot tip** or **coleoptile** in **response to light** ($\checkmark$). Auxin **efflux carriers actively transport auxin** to the **shaded side** of the shoot, where the **concentration of auxin** becomes **higher** ($\checkmark$). **Auxin binds** to auxin **receptors** on the **target cells**, triggering a cascade of cellular events ($\checkmark$). This binding **stimulates** the activity of **proton pumps** in the **cell membrane**, which causes **hydrogen ions** ($\ce{H^+}$) to be moved **into** the **cell wall, lowering** the **pH** ($\checkmark$). The **lowered pH activates expansins**, enzymes that play a key role in cell wall loosening ($\checkmark$). **Expansin enzymes break hydrogen bonds** or **cross-links** between **cellulose** molecules, making the **cell wall more flexible** ($\checkmark$). The change in the cell wall allows **more water** to **enter** the **cell** ($\checkmark$), **increasing** cell **turgor** or pressure from the vacuole, which causes the **stretching** or **elongation** of the **cell wall** ($\checkmark$). This **differential cell elongation** on the **shaded side** causes the **shoot** to **bend towards** the **light** ($\checkmark$).
