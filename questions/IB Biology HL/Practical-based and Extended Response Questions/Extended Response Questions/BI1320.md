# BI1320

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how water is formed at the end of aerobic respiration.    [3 marks]

2. Explain how the properties of water allow it to be transported around a plant.    [5 marks]

3. Describe how plants transport carbohydrates from source to sink.    [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ electrons donated by reduced NAD and FAD **OR** NADH and FADH at the beginning of the electron transport chain    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electrons are passed along electron carriers    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ final electron acceptor is oxygen **OR** :sup[1]/:sub[2] O:sub[2]      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ excess hydrogen ions **OR** protons, from intermembrane space **OR** coenzymes **OR** reduced NAD **OR** reduced FAD found in matrix **OR** accumulate in the matrix            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen ions **OR** protons react with oxygen to form water    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ 4e:sup[-] + 4H:sup[+] + O:sub[2] → 2H:sub[2]O **OR** 4 electrons + 4 protons + 1 molecule of oxygen →  2 molecules of water    [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Electrons** donated by **reduced NAD** and **FAD** at the beginning of the electron transport chain ($\checkmark$) are passed along **electron carriers** ($\checkmark$), with the **final** electron acceptor being **oxygen** ($\checkmark$). **Hydrogen ions** in the **matrix** react with **oxygen** to form water ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ polar so can form hydrogen bonds             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ strong cohesive forces between water molecules help pull other water molecules up the xylem                                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ strong adhesive properties mean water can stick to lignin **OR** cellulose **OR** inside of the xylem                                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cohesion and adhesion work together to enable capillary action **OR** allow water to move against gravity in xylem vessels            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ evaporation from leaves creates a negative pressure **OR** tension in the xylem vessels, which helps to “pull” more water up the plant            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cohesion-tension theory **OR** cohesive forces between water molecules and the tension created in the xylem vessels create a continuous column of water    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high latent heat of vaporisation so water can evaporate from the stomata and stimulate the transpiration stream                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ low viscosity, so can flow easily through small pores in the plant        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ root pressure results from the active pumping of ions into the root cells, creating an osmotic gradient that causes water to enter the roots    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Water is able to move around a plant because it is **polar**, so **hydrogen bonds** can form ($\checkmark$) between different water molecules, which allow them to be **cohesive** so they can **stick together** to be pulled up the **xylem** ($\checkmark$) at low pressure. Water molecules are **adhesive** which allows them to stick to **cellulose** in the plant ($\checkmark$). The **cohesion** and **adhesion** work together to enable **capillary action** up the xylem vessels ($\checkmark$).  Water has a **high latent heat of vaporisation**, so water can **evaporate** from the **stomata** and maintain the transpiration stream ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ sources are where carbohydrates are synthesised     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sinks are where carbohydrates are utilised            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sucrose and H:sup[+] are co-transported into the companion cells (in apoplastic route) near the source                                            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sucrose concentration increases in the companion cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sucrose diffuses into sieve tube via plasmodesmata        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sucrose diffuses through cytoplasm of neighbouring cells, via plasmodesmata into phloem (in symplastic route)                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the increase in concentration of carbohydrates **OR** sucrose **OR** decrease in solute potential results in water flowing into the phloem via osmosis            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ raises the hydrostatic pressure in the sieve tube elements near the source cell        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organic compounds, **OR** carbohydrates **OR** named example, are unloaded from phloem, **OR** sieve tubes near the sink                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ decreases the solute concentration (in sieve tubes at sinks)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water moves out of the sieve tubes reducing hydrostatic pressure [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water **OR** sap containing the sucrose **OR** carbohydrates moves down the hydrostatic pressure gradient towards the sink                    [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Sources** are where carbohydrates are **synthesised** in plants, such as photosynthesising leaves ($\checkmark$) and **sinks** are where carbohydrates are **used**, such as growing shoots ($\checkmark$). In plants, **sucrose and H:sup[+]** are **co-transported** into companion cells near the source ($\checkmark$), this causes the **sucrose concentration** to **increase** in companion cells ($\checkmark$). Sucrose then **diffuses** into **sieve tubes** via **plasmodesmata** ($\checkmark$) and then **diffuses** through the **cytoplasm of neighbouring cells**, into the phloem ($\checkmark$). This causes water to move into the phloem via **osmosis** from the **xylem** ($\checkmark$), **raising** the **hydrostatic pressure** ($\checkmark$). At the **sink**, the **carbohydrates** are **unloaded** from the phloem ($\checkmark$), **decreasing** the **solute concentration** ($\checkmark$), **water follows**, thereby **reducing hydrostatic pressure** ($\checkmark$). The sap containing the carbohydrates **move down** the **hydrostatic pressure gradient** towards the sink ($\checkmark$).
