# BI0201

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the different uses of karyograms. [4 marks]

2. Distinguish between cells with haploid and diploid nuclei. [4 marks]

3. Compare and contrast different types of chromosomes. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ karyograms are images of the chromosomes in an organism [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes are arranged in pairs of decreasing length OR Sex chromosomes are the 23rd pair in human karyograms        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ karyograms can be used to identify the sex of an individual    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ karyograms can be used to identify conditions caused by trisomy, e.g. Down syndrome **OR** Patau syndrome **OR** Klinefelter syndrome             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ karyograms can be used to determine if a chromosome is missing **OR** if part of a chromosome is missing                        [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    **Karyograms** ($\checkmark$) are images of the chromosomes in an organism. In a karyogram, the pairs of homologous chromosomes are arranged in pairs of **decreasing length** ($\checkmark$). The sex chromosomes are shown as the 23rd pair in human karyograms.
Karyograms can be used to determine if an individual is a biological **male or female** ($\checkmark$). Two X chromosomes are present in a human female cell, and one X chromosome and one Y chromosome are present in a human male cell.
Karyograms can be used to **identify** conditions caused by **trisomy** (three copies of a chromosome) e.g. **Down syndrome**, Patau syndrome, Edward’s syndrome and Klinefelter syndrome ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid nuclei have one set of chromosomes, **OR** one chromosome per type **WHEREAS** diploid nuclei have two sets **OR** two chromosomes of each type [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid nuclei have one allele for each gene **WHEREAS** diploid nuclei have two alleles for each gene [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid nuclei in humans have 23 chromosomes, and diploid nuclei in humans have 46 chromosomes **OR** any other valid species example [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid cells are usually gametes **WHEREAS** diploid cells are usually somatic cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haploid cells are usually the result of meiosis, **WHILE** diploid cells are usually the result of mitosis.  [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    Haploid nuclei have **one set of chromosomes**($\checkmark$), whereas diploid nuclei have two sets of chromosomes.  For example, in **humans**($\checkmark$), gametes have 23 chromosomes, while somatic cells have 46 chromosomes. Haploid nuclei have **one allele for each gene**, ($\checkmark$)  whereas diploid nuclei have two alleles for each gene. In humans, only **gametes**($\checkmark$)  have haploid nuclei, whereas the rest of the somatic cells have diploid nuclei.

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ (within one species) different chromosomes are composed of DNA molecules with different lengths     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (within one species) different chromosomes have different numbers of genes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (within one species) different chromosomes have different types **OR** different numbers of genes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ both prokaryotic **AND** eukaryotic chromosomes contain DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bacterial chromosomes are not associated with proteins, **WHILE** eukaryotic and archaeal chromosomes are associated with proteins [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic chromosomes are looped molecules of DNA, **WHILE** eukaryotic chromosomes are linear molecules of DNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** autosomes and sex chromosomes contain genes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the human Y chromosome carries information mainly coding for male sex determination and development, **WHILE** the X chromosome carries information for other body characteristics [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is only one pair of sex chromosomes, **WHILE** there can be more than one pair of autosomes (in different species)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ autosomes are pairs of homologous chromosomes of equal length, **WHILE** two sex chromosomes can be different lengths [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chromosomes can be homologous and non-homologous [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous chromosomes are pairs of chromosomes of equal length carrying the same genes, but different alleles, **WHILE** non-homologous chromosomes, carry different genes [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
    All chromosomes are made of DNA and contain genes, but they **differ in length** ($\checkmark$) and in the **number of genes** ($\checkmark$) and **type of genes** ($\checkmark$). Similarities and differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic chromosomes: Both prokaryotic and eukaryotic chromosomes **contain DNA** ($\checkmark$). Bacterial chromosomes are not associated with proteins, but eukaryotic chromosomes are **associated with proteins** ($\checkmark$). Prokaryotic chromosomes are looped, while eukaryotic chromosomes are **linear** ($\checkmark$).

    Similarities and differences between autosomes and sex chromosomes: \
    Both autosomes and sex chromosomes are made of DNA and contain genes. Autosomes are chromosomes that do not determine sex and do not have genes for sex-linked characteristics; sex chromosomes determine sex and have genes for sex-linked traits. There is one pair of sex chromosomes in a somatic nucleus, but there can be **more than one pair of autosomes** ($\checkmark$) in a somatic nucleus. X and Y chromosomes are of different lengths, while autosomal homologous chromosome pairs are **equal in length** ($\checkmark$) \
    Comparing non-homologous chromosomes and homologous chromosomes:\
    Homologous chromosomes are pairs of chromosomes that have genes for the same characteristics and can have the same or different alleles. Non-homologous chromosomes have genes for **different characteristics** ($\checkmark$).
