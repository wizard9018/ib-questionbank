# BI0163

**Maximum mark: 16**

Oxidative phosphorylation, which generates ATP for the cell, is comprised of the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis.

1. Distinguish between the electron transport chain in aerobic cellular respiration and the electron transport chain in photosynthesis.   [3 marks]

2. Outline how ATP can be recycled after being used by the cell.      [5 marks]

3. Explain how 38 molecules of ATP can be produced from one glucose molecule in a cell.     [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ ETC happens in the inner mitochondrial membrane, **WHILE** it happens in the thylakoid membrane in the chloroplast   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electrons are donated by the reduced NAD (**OR** FAD) at the beginning of the ETC in aerobic cellular respiration, **WHILE** in photosynthesis electrons are donated by chlorophyll a in PSII **OR** water  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ NADH (**OR** FADH$_2$) is oxidised **OR** loses electrons in the ETC of aerobic cellular respiration, **WHILE** NADP:sup[+] is reduced in the ETC of chloroplasts  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the ETC of aerobic cellular respiration **BUT** NADP:sup[+] is the final electron acceptor in the ETC of photosynthesis   [1 mark]
[3 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    In aerobic cellular respiration, the **ETC takes place in the inner mitochondrial membrane**, while in **photosynthesis it take place in the thylakoid membrane of the chloroplast** ($\checkmark$). In **aerobic cellular respiration, electrons are donated by NADH (and FADH$_2$**), while in **photosynthesis they are donated by chlorophyll a in photosystem II** ($\checkmark$). **NADH (and FADH$_2$) undergo oxidation in aerobic cellular respiration** when they donate the electrons at the beginning of the ETC, but in **photosynthesis NADP:sup[+] is reduced** as it accepts electrons at the end of the ETC ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and Pi **OR** inorganic phosphate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when ATP breaks, energy is released  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inorganic phosphate combines with ADP to form ATP [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when ATP is synthesized, energy is added [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxidative phosphorylation, **OR** energy released from the oxidation of glucose, **OR** energy from the proton, **OR** H:sup[+] gradient, **OR** chemiosmosis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electron transport chain, **OR** electron movement along the carriers, provides energy [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy released from ETC generates proton **OR** H:sup[+] gradient **OR** moves H:sup[+] across the inner mitochondrial membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP synthase synthesizes ATP, **OR** ATP synthase allows protons, **OR** H:sup[+], to diffuse      through the inner mitochondrial, **OR** thylakoid, membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the energy to convert ADP and inorganic phosphate to ATP comes from organic compounds **OR** sunlight [1 mark]
[5 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    ATP can be recycled continuously to meet the energy demands of cells. This is done after the **hydrolysis of ATP**, which **releases the energy** ($\checkmark$) needed to fuel cell processes. Hydrolysis of ATP produces **ADP and inorganic phosphate** ($\checkmark$). This inorganic phosphate can be **recombined with ADP** ($\checkmark$) with the addition of **energy** ($\checkmark$) from either **organic compounds or sunlight** ($\checkmark$) and then stored within the bonds formed. ATP is formed by **ATP synthase** ($\checkmark$) in oxidative phosphorylation , which involves **electron movement** and proton transport ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ aerobic respiration requires oxygen and leads to a high yield of ATP   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ products (of aerobic cell respiration) are CO$_2$, water and ATP   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the four stages of cellular respiration are glycolysis, link reaction, Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glycolysis happens in cytoplasm **OR** outside of mitochondria [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ products of glycolysis are pyruvate, NADH, and net of 2 ATP  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pyruvate is converted into acetyl CoA in the link reaction   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxidative phosphorylation happens in mitochondria **OR** Krebs cycle in the matrix of mitochondrion **OR** ETC on the inner mitochondrial membrane (**OR** cristae)  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Krebs cycle results in 2 ATP, reduced NADH (**OR** FADH$_2$), **OR** reduced electron carriers [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ NADH (**OR*  FADH$_2$) donate electrons and hydrogen to ETC    (**OR** electron transport chain) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electron movement in ETC moves H:sup[+] across the inner mitochondrial membrane **OR** produces an H:sup[+] gradient     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP synthase uses H:sup[+] concentration gradient, **OR** proton gradient, to synthesize ATP [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inorganic phosphate is added to ADP to form ATP       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ 34 ATP formed in oxidative phosphorylation  [1 mark]
[8 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Cellular respiration consists of **4 stages: glycolysis (which occurs in the cytoplasm** ($\checkmark$) **and is anaerobic, not needing oxygen)**, and then the other 3 stages, which occur in the mitochondrion and make up aerobic respiration: **the link reaction, the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation** ($\checkmark$).   In glycolysis, glucose is broken down into **pyruvate, NADH and a net of 2 ATP is produced** ($\checkmark$). The pyruvate then enters the mitochondrion, and the **link reaction converts it into acetyl-CoA** ($\checkmark$). The acetyl-CoA is delivered to the Krebs cycle in the **matrix of mitochondria** ($\checkmark$). Here **6 NADH, (2 FADH$_2$) and 2 ATP** ($\checkmark$) are produced per molecule of glucose . **NADH (and FADH$_2$) carry electrons to the electron transport chain (ETC)** ($\checkmark$), the first step in oxidative phosphorylation. The 2nd step is chemiosmosis, where **ATP synthase produces 34 molecules of ATP** ($\checkmark$), using the **energy from the proton gradient generated by the ETC** ($\checkmark$) to combine **ADP and inorganic phosphate** ($\checkmark$).
