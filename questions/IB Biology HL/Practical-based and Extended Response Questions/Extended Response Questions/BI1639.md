# BI1639

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast between the alpha-helices and beta-pleated sheets in polypeptide structure. [5 marks]

2. Outline the processes during meiosis that lead to genetic variation.    [4 marks]

3. Explain the roles of the different enzymes involved in the replication of a DNA molecule.        [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **five** of the following (at least **one** similarity and **one** difference required to achieve full marks):

    ***Similarities*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are secondary protein structures        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are stabilised by hydrogen bonds        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** contribute to the overall 3D **OR** tertiary structure of proteins        [1 mark]

    ***Differences*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ alpha-helices are coiled **WHILE** beta-pleated sheets are ribbon-like            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen bonding occurs within a single strand in alpha-helices **WHILE** hydrogen bonding occurs between strands in beta-pleated sheets        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ alpha-helices are common in  fibrous proteins **WHILE** beta-pleated sheets are common in globular proteins                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ alpha-helices provide elasticity **OR** flexibility **WHILE** beta-pleated sheets provide strength **OR** rigidity                [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Alpha-helices and beta-pleated sheets are **both secondary structures** ($\checkmark$) in proteins, **stabilised** by **hydrogen bonds** ($\checkmark$). **Alpha-helices** are **coiled** and **provide flexibility**, while **beta-pleated sheets** are **sheet-like** ($\checkmark$) and provide **rigidity** ($\checkmark$). In **alpha-helices**, **hydrogen bonding** occurs **within** a **single strand**, whereas in **beta-pleated** sheets, it occurs **between strands** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over **OR** chiasma formation between non-identical sister chromatids **OR** homologous chromosomes in prophase I        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ exchanges segments of DNA **OR** results in new combination of alleles         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ random orientation of homologous chromosomes in metaphase I **OR** random orientation of sister chromatids in metaphase II            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ leads to different combinations of maternal and paternal chromosomes in gametes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ independent assortment             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ produces different combinations of alleles in the gametes **OR** $\ce{2^n}$ possible combinations    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations introduce genetic variations        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During meiosis, genetic variation is achieved through **crossing over** in **prophase I** ($\checkmark$), where **non-identical sister chromatids exchange genetic material** ($\checkmark$). **Random orientation** of **homologous chromosomes** during **metaphase I** and **sister chromatids** during **metaphase II** ($\checkmark$) leads to different combinations of maternal and paternal chromosomes in the gametes ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ helicase unwinds the DNA double helix **OR** forms replication fork **OR** breaks hydrogen bonds between strands            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows each strand to be used as a template        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gyrase relieves strain caused by unwinding of the DNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA primase synthesises a short RNA primer        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ which provides a starting point with a free 3' hydroxyl group for DNA polymerase III to begin adding DNA nucleotides        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase III adds nucleotides to the growing DNA strand    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in the 5' to 3' direction **OR** adds nucleotides to the 3' end            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ continuously on leading strand **AND** discontinuously on lagging strand    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase III has proofreading activity **OR** removes any mismatched base and replaces it with correct nucleotide            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase I removes RNA primers and replaces them with DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA ligase joins Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During DNA replication, **helicase unwinds** the **double helix**, creating a **replication fork** ($\checkmark$), **allowing** the DNA **strands** to be **separated** ($\checkmark$). **Gyrase relieves** the **torsional strain** ($\checkmark$) caused by the unwinding of the helix. **DNA primase** synthesises a **short RNA primer** ($\checkmark$), providing a starting point for **DNA polymerase III**, which **adds nucleotides** ($\checkmark$) in the **5' to 3' direction** ($\checkmark$). **DNA polymerase I replaces RNA primers** with **DNA nucleotides** ($\checkmark$), ensuring the newly synthesised strand is continuous. **DNA ligase joins** the **Okazaki fragments** together on the **lagging strand** ($\checkmark$), forming a complete DNA strand.
