# BI2917

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe the process of epigenetic inheritance.      [3 marks]

2. Outline    the epigenetic origins of phenotypic differences in lion-tiger hybrids.     [6 marks]

3. Explain how transcription in eukaryotic cells is regulated by proteins that bind to specific DNA sequences.        [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ epigenetic inheritance involves passing on changes in gene expression without altering the DNA sequence            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can result in phenotypic differences being passed to daughter cells **OR** offspring        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA methylation is a chemical tag added to DNA            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ histone modification alters chromatin structure [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can lead to phenotypic differences between genetically identical cells or organisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA methylation can prevent transcription factor binding, blocking RNA polymerase from initiating transcription       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ histone acetylation can loosen chromatin, increasing gene expression        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ histone methylation can tighten **OR** loosen chromatin, affecting transcription    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ epigenetic tags **OR** DNA methylation **OR** histone modification can remain in place during mitosis or meiosis     [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Epigenetic inheritance occurs when **changes** in **gene expression** are passed to **daughter cells** or **offspring without altering** the **DNA nucleotide sequence** ($\checkmark$). Chemical modifications such as **DNA methylation** can **prevent transcription factors** from **binding** to **promoters** ($\checkmark$), while **histone modification**, such as **methylation**, alters **chromatin structure**, affecting whether **genes** are **accessible** for **transcription** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **six** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ ligers are the offspring of a male lion and a female tiger **AND** tigons are the hybrid offspring of a female lion and a male tiger    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ IGF2 is a growth-promoting gene involved in cell division **OR** development     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ IGF2 is expressed differently in the gametes **OR** sex cells of male and female lions due to epigenetic imprinting        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the IGF2 allele in a lion’s sperm is active and promotes rapid foetal growth     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the IGF2 allele in a lioness’ egg is silenced via imprinting **OR** DNA methylation **OR** epigenetic tags        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in tigers, both maternal and paternal IGF2 alleles are active **OR** expressed **OR** there is no imprinting on **OR** silencing of the tiger maternal IGF2 gene         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ligers are larger than both parents because both IGF2 alleles are active, leading to excessive growth        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tigons are smaller **OR** normal size because the maternal IGF2 allele from the lioness is silenced **OR** only the paternal IGF2 allele from the tiger is expressed     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phenotypic differences arise from mismatched epigenetic imprinting systems between lions and tigers        [1 mark]

    [6 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When lions and tigers interbreed, the resulting hybrids, **ligers** from **male lions** and **female tigers** and **tigons** from **male tigers** and **female lions** ($\checkmark$), show distinct phenotypic differences. These differences arise from **epigenetic imprinting** of the **IGF2 gene**, which **promotes growth** ($\checkmark$). In **lions**, the **maternal IGF2 allele** is **silenced** by **DNA methylation**, so only the **paternal allele** is **expressed** ($\checkmark$). In tigers, **both parental IGF2 alleles** are **active** ($\checkmark$). As a result, **ligers** inherit **two active IGF2 alleles**, one from the lion and one from the tigress, leading to **excessive growth** ($\checkmark$). **Tigons** are the **same size**, or slightly **smaller**, than their parents because **only** the **paternal IGF2 allele** is **expressed** ($\checkmark$).

    |Feature | Lion | Tiger | Liger | Tigon  |
    | :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | Parental combination | — | — | **Male lion** $\times$ **Female tiger** | **Female lion** $\times$ **Male tiger**   ($\checkmark$) |
    | Maternal IGF2  | **Silenced via methylation** ($\checkmark$) | **Active** | Active (from tigress) | Silenced (from lioness) |
    | Paternal IGF2  | Active | **Active** ($\checkmark$) | Active (from lion) | Active (from tiger) |
    | Total IGF2 expression | One active allele | Two active alleles | **Two active alleles** |  **One active allele** |
    | Phenotypic outcome | Normal growth | Normal growth | **Excessive growth (larger)** |  **Normal or smaller size** |
    | Cause of hybrid difference | — | — | **No silencing of maternal IGF2** ($\checkmark$) | **Silencing of maternal IGF2** ($\checkmark$) |

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase II and general transcription factors are required to initiate transcription    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase II reads the DNA template to build a strand of mRNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ general transcription factors help RNA polymerase II bind correctly to the promoter [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a promoter is a specific DNA sequence located near the start of a gene    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ promoter indicates where RNA polymerase should bind **AND** where transcription begins [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enhancers are DNA sequences that increase transcription of a gene    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enhancers can be located far upstream  **OR**  downstream from the gene to be transcribed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ activator proteins bind to enhancers to increase transcription of the gene [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ external signals **OR** hormones **OR** growth factors **OR** stress activate activator transcription factors         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA looping brings enhancer-bound activators close to promoter-bound factors to initiate transcription            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ repressor factors bind to silencers to inhibit transcription        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ co-activators **OR** mediator proteins facilitate communication between transcription factors and RNA polymerase     [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Transcription in eukaryotic cells is tightly regulated by proteins that bind to specific DNA sequences. **RNA polymerase II** and **general transcription factors** are essential for **initiating transcription** ($\checkmark$). General **transcription factors** help **RNA polymerase bind** to the **promoter** ($\checkmark$), a **DNA sequence** near the **start** of a **gene** that marks **where transcription begins** ($\checkmark$). **Enhancers** are regulatory DNA elements that **increase transcription rates** ($\checkmark$), even when located **far upstream** or **downstream** from the **gene** ($\checkmark$). **Activator proteins bind** to **enhancers** and are **activated** by **external signals** such as **hormones** or **stress** ($\checkmark$). **DNA looping** brings **enhancer-bound activators** into proximity with **promoter-bound transcription factors**, facilitating the **assembly** of the **transcription initiation complex** ($\checkmark$).
