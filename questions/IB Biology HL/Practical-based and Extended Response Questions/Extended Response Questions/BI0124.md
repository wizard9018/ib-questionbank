# BI0124

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Draw and label a trophic pyramid of energy showing four levels. [4 marks]

2. Explain how the presence of a food web in an ecosystem makes it more stable than the presence of a single food chain. [5 marks]

3. Describe how energy flows in and out of terrestrial ecosystems. [7 marks]

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ in a food chain, each organism is dependent on only one other organism for its food  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ food chains **AND/OR** food webs show the relationship between organisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ multiple food chains are linked by common organisms to form a food web     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in a food web, herbivores tend to eat different plants so they survive if one plant disappears  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in a food web, consumers tend to eat different herbivores **OR** consumers so survive if one prey species disappears    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in a food web, a single consumer may eat herbivores **OR** consumers from different trophic levels [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Ecosystems with a food web are not destabilized by the disappearance of one species **WHILE** in food chains the removal of one species will lead to the collapse of the food chain [1 mark]
[5 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Each organism in a food chain is **dependent on only one organism from the previous trophic level** for food ($\checkmark$). A food web includes many different food chains that are **interlinked by common organisms** ($\checkmark$). Since a herbivore **eats different plants** in a food web, it will never be short of food if one plant species disappears ($\checkmark$). Similarly, **a single consumer may eat multiple different herbivores or other consumers** making it less dependent on only one prey species for food ($\checkmark$). If a **predator or a consumer disappears** due to disease or other factors, the ecosystem is less likely to be destabilized as other species occupy similar niches ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy from the Sun is captured by autotrophs **OR** producers through the process of photosynthesis     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy assimilated by organisms is stored as biomass [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy transfer from one trophic level to the next is not 100% efficient [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy moves from producers to primary consumers to secondary consumers to tertiary consumers in most ecosystems [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the number of trophic levels differs in different ecosystems [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ all organisms lose energy through the process of cell respiration **OR** in the form of heat [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some energy is lost because some body parts (accept any valid example) are indigestible **OR** because some compounds are excreted or egested  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ egested **AND/OR** excreted organic material is consumed by decomposers [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the efficiency of energy production and transfer varies among  ecosystems [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy has to be continuously produced to maintain an ecosystem [1 mark]
[7 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    In terrestrial ecosystems, energy is captured by producers from the Sun through **photosynthesis** ($\checkmark$). This energy is **stored as biomass** ($\checkmark$) in plants and is transferred to herbivores or **primary consumers**, then to secondary and tertiary consumers ($\checkmark$).  Energy transfer is **not 100% efficient** ($\checkmark$). The length of the food chain varies in food webs depending on the initial production of energy by producers. The efficiency of production and transfer **varies** between terrestrial ecosystems ($\checkmark$).

    All organisms use energy for growth and maintenance, and **energy is lost through respiration** in the form of heat ($\checkmark$). Energy from the dead parts of organisms is consumed by **decomposers** and lost as heat ($\checkmark$). Energy from the Sun has to be **continuously captured** by producers to maintain the ecosystem ($\checkmark$).
