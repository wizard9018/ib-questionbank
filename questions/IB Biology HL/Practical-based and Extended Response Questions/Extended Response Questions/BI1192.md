1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ ADH is involved in osmoregulation **OR** the control of solute concentration in the blood plasma     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if the water content of the blood is low **OR** solute concentration is high, the pituitary gland secretes ADH    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ADH causes the cells of the collecting duct to be more permeable to water        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water channels **OR** protein channels called aquaporins are put into cell membranes   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as filtrate moves through collecting ducts, water is reabsorbed due to the solute concentration in the medulla being high    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ADH causes the blood to become more dilute by reabsorption of water in the collecting ducts of the nephrons            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ADH causes a small volume of concentrated urine to be formed for excretion              [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ no **OR** low ADH causes dilute urine to be excreted    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    ADH is involved in **osmoregulation** of the blood plasma, the control of solute concentration of the blood ($\checkmark$). If the **water content** of the **blood** is **low**, the **pituitary gland** secretes **ADH** ($\checkmark$). ADH causes the cells of the **collecting duct** of the **nephrons** to become **more permeable** to **water** ($\checkmark$). Water channels called **aquaporins** are put into the **membranes** of the cells of the **collecting duct walls** ($\checkmark$). The **high solute** concentration in the **medulla** will allow the **reabsorption** of **water** by **osmosis** ($\checkmark$) from the collecting ducts. It causes a **small volume** of **concentrated** urine ($\checkmark$) to be produced.

3. any **seven** of the following (at least one from each section, presented in a logical sequence):

    ***Helper T-cells*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ helper T-cells have  receptor proteins in their plasma membranes, to which a specific  antigen can bind [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the antigen is presented to the helper T cell by a macrophage **OR** antigen-presenting cell [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when the antigen binds, the helper T-cell is activated [1 mark]

    ***B-cells*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ inactive B-cells have antibodies **OR** receptors for antigens **OR** B-cell receptors in their plasma membrane  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when complementary antigen binds **OR** when activated T-cell with the antigen binds B-cell is activated   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ T-cell can send chemical message **OR** cytokines to help activate B-cells [1 mark]

    ***Production of plasma cells*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ activated B-cells start to divide once activated **OR** go through clonal expansion [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ differentiation into plasma cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ these cells have large volumes of cytoplasm and are called plasma cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they synthesise antibodies  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ antibodies are released by exocytosis [1 mark]

    ***Production of memory cells*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ activated T-cells and B-cells can also differentiate into memory T-cells and B-cells  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ these cells persist once the disease is gone [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they can be activated very quickly in response to the same antigen  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ long term immunity to a disease is thus developed by the body [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    In the initial phase of the disease, the **antigen** is presented by a **macrophage** to a **helper T-cell** with the **specific receptor** ($\checkmark$). This **helper T-cell** can bind the **antigen** and is **activated** ($\checkmark$), and **can now** bind to a **specific B -cell** containing the **complementary receptor** ($\checkmark$). The T-cell sends a message to the B-cell, thus activating it.

    **Activated B-cells** start to **divide** ($\checkmark$) and can **differentiate** into **plasma B-cells**, which have large amounts of cytoplasm ($\checkmark$). They **synthesise antibodies** ($\checkmark$) which are **released** by **exocytosis** ($\checkmark$).

    **Memory cells** are **T-cells and B-cells** that are **formed** when the **respective helper cells** are **made** ($\checkmark$). These **cells persist** in the body and are **activated** in response to the **same antigen** ($\checkmark$). **Long-term immunity** to the disease ($\checkmark$) is established.
