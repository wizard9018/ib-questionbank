2. any **three** of the following *(at least **one** similarity and **one** difference must be given to gain full marks):*

    ***Similarities***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** have a carbohydrate chain attached        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are found on the outer surface of the plasma membrane     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** involved in cell recognition or signalling        [1 mark]

    ***Differences***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ glycoproteins are composed of carbohydrate chain attached to proteins **WHEREAS** glycolipids are composed of carbohydrate chains attached to lipids            [1]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glycoproteins are part of integral **OR** peripheral proteins **WHEREAS** glycolipids are part of the phospholipid bilayer        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glycoproteins more commonly act as receptors **WHEREAS** glycolipids play a greater role in maintaining membrane stability            [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Glycoproteins and glycolipids **both** have a **carbohydrate chain attached** ($\checkmark$) and are found on the **outer surface** of the plasma membrane ($\checkmark$). However, **glycoproteins** consist of a **carbohydrate chain** attached to a **protein**, whereas **glycolipids** consist of a carbohydrate chain attached to a **lipid** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrophobic core of the phospholipid bilayer **AND** embedded **OR** transmembrane proteins are key structural features that enable selective absorption    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ non-polar or small uncharged molecules can cross the membrane by simple diffusion but this process is not selective            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the hydrophobic core of the bilayer prevents passage of polar **OR** charged molecules [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ channel proteins allow specific ions **OR** molecules to pass through the membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some channel proteins are gated and can switch between open and closed states    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ voltage-gated channel proteins respond to changes in membrane potential     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ligand-gated channels open in response to binding of a specific molecule    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ carrier proteins transport molecules by changing shape to move them across the membrane         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ facilitated diffusion through proteins allows transport of large polar molecules without using energy **OR** passively            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ active transport proteins **OR** protein pumps use ATP to move molecules against the concentration gradient             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ co-transport proteins couple the movement of one substance down its concentration gradient with the movement of another against its gradient     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the selective use of specific transport proteins allows the membrane to control which substances enter or leave the cell        [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The plasma membrane is made of a phospholipid bilayer which has a **hydrophobic core** and that, along with the **embedded proteins** are key in the selective absorption of substances into the cell ($\checkmark$). **Non-polar** or small **uncharged molecules** can pass through by **simple diffusion**, but this process is **not selective** ($\checkmark$). The **hydrophobic core blocks ions** and **polar molecules**, so specific transport proteins are needed ($\checkmark$). **Channel proteins** allow certain **ions** or **molecules** to pass through the membrane ($\checkmark$). Some of these channels are **gated** and only **open** under **specific conditions**, controlling the movement of substances ($\checkmark$). **Carrier proteins** move **specific molecules** by **changing shape** as they transport them across the membrane ($\checkmark$). **Active transport proteins** use **ATP** to pump substances **against** their **concentration gradient**, allowing selective uptake or removal of materials ($\checkmark$).  **Co-transport proteins** couple the **movement of one** substance **down** its **concentration gradient** with the movement of **another against its gradient** ($\checkmark$).
