# BI1066

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Explain how parents with blood type A and blood type B can have a child with blood type O.    [4 marks]

2. Outline how polygenic traits can show a normal distribution.        [4 marks]

3. Explain the role of inheritance in genetic diseases.        [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following (accept any marking point clearly shown in a genetic diagram):

    $\ce{ -> }$ the allele for blood group A **OR** allele I:sup[A], is dominant over the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the allele for blood group B **OR** allele I:sup[B], is dominant over the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ parents with blood group A or blood group B could be heterozygous **OR** I:sup[A]i and I:sup[B]i      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a parent who is heterozygous for blood group A can pass on the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a parent who is heterozygous for blood group B can pass on the allele for blood group O **OR** allele i        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ offspring that receive the allele for blood type O **OR** i allele from both parents will be blood group O    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The alleles for blood type A and blood type B are **dominant** ($\checkmark$) to the allele for blood type O. Parents with blood type A or B could be **heterozygous** ($\checkmark$) and possess the recessive allele for blood type O. If the offspring **inherits the recessive allele** ($\checkmark$) from both parents, then they will have blood type O.

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic traits are caused by two or more genes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the greater the number of genes that are responsible for a trait, the greater the number of phenotypes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic traits often show continuous variation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ which is a continuous range of variation rather than distinct categories    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is a large range of phenotypes, where the majority of organisms are in the middle of the normal distribution curve            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polygenic traits are often influenced by the environment    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Polygenic traits are caused by **two or more genes** ($\checkmark$). The **higher the number of genes** ($\checkmark$) that are responsible for a trait, the **higher the number of possible phenotypes** ($\checkmark$) which means that the traits show **continuous variation** ($\checkmark$). Continuous variation is when a phenotype shows a **continuous range of variation** ($\checkmark$), rather than having distinct categories and usually shows a normal distribution, where the majority of organisms are in the **middle of the normal distribution curve** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic diseases are controlled by genes **OR** alleles        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ alleles are passed from parents to offspring **OR** gametes have one allele for each characteristic from each parent        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at fertilization the alleles from both parents come together **OR** the combination of alleles determines the characteristic in the offspring    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ diseases caused by dominant alleles only need the allele from one parent to cause the disease in the offspring        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Huntington's is an autosomal dominant condition **OR** any other valid autosomal dominant condition example            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ diseases caused by recessive alleles need the allele from both parents to cause the disease in the offspring        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phenylketonuria is an autosomal recessive condition **OR** any other valid autosomal recessive condition example        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phenylketonuria caused by mutation of gene that codes for enzyme needed to convert phenylalanine to tyrosine **OR** other suitable example        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (most) sex-linked characteristics are controlled by alleles found on the X chromosome            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ haemophilia is a sex-linked recessive condition **OR** any other valid sex-linked recessive condition example            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sex-linked genetic diseases are more common in males than females     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if a mother is affected by a sex-linked characteristic all of her sons will have the condition        [1 mark]
    [9 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Genetic diseases are **controlled by alleles** ($\checkmark$), inherited from biological parents. During meiosis, gametes are produced, which contain **one allele for each characteristic** ($\checkmark$), and these come together during **fertilization**. The **combination** of alleles passed to offspring can determine whether the offspring is affected by a genetic disease or not ($\checkmark$).

    Some genetic diseases, such as **Huntington's disease** ($\checkmark$), are caused by **dominant alleles** ($\checkmark$), so one copy of the allele needs to be inherited for the disease to be expressed and therefore affected by the disease. Other genetic diseases, such as **cystic fibrosis**, are caused by **recessive alleles** ($\checkmark$), which means two copies need to be inherited, one from each biological parent, in order to be expressed and affected with the disease.

    Sex-linked genetic disorders, such as **haemophilia** ($\checkmark$), are mostly controlled by genes found on the **X chromosome** ($\checkmark$). If a male inherits an allele that codes for a genetic disorder from his biological mother, he will be affected; however, if a female inherits the same allele, she won't always be affected, depending on the allele she inherited on the X chromosome from her biological father. It's because of this that males have a **higher chance** ($\checkmark$) of being affected by sex-linked genetic diseases.
