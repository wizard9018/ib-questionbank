# BI0389

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Distinguish between a myofibril, the sarcoplasmic reticulum and the sarcolemma.         [3 marks]

2. Outline the proteins involved in muscle contraction.     [5 marks]

3. Describe the process of muscle contraction. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following are correctly shown in a labelled diagram:

    $\ce{ -> }$ myofibrils are the basic unit inside the muscle fibres **OR** they are composed of  end-to-end sarcomeres **OR** they are long contractile fibres **OR** they are bundles of protein filaments, mainly actin and myosin   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the sarcolemma is the plasma membrane of the muscle fibres **OR** cell, **OR** it envelops the muscle fibre          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the sarcoplasmic reticulum is specialized endoplasmic reticulum that stores calcium **OR** releases calcium ions when stimulated by a nerve impulse **OR** action potential  [1 mark]
[3 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    Myofibrils, sarcolemma and sarcoplasmic reticulum are all found in muscle fibres. **Myofibrils** are the basic units of muscle fibres; they are made of **repeating sarcomeres** laid end-to-end and are responsible for muscle contraction ($\checkmark$). The **sarcolemma** is the membrane that surrounds each **muscle fibre**, similar to the **plasma membrane** in any other cell ($\checkmark$). The **sarcoplasmic** reticulum is a **specialized endoplasmic reticulum**. When a nerve impulse reaches the muscle fibre, depolarisation spreads to the sarcoplasmic reticulum. This activates voltage-sensitive calcium channels in the sarcoplasmic reticulum causing them to **release calcium ions** ($\checkmark$) from the sarcoplasmic reticulum storage. This initiates the process of muscle contraction.

2. any **five** of the following (answer can be in a table format):

    $\ce{ -> }$ five types of protein involved in muscle contration - myosin, actin, troponin, tropomyosin and titin    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tropomyosin binds to actin    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ troponin moves tropomyosin from the binding sites, in presence of **OR** when it binds to, calcium ions      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ actin makes up thin filaments [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin makes up thick filaments    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads attach to the binding sites on actin **OR** forms cross-bridges with actin      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads rotate to pull the actin in **OR** carries out power stroke, shortening the sarcomere **OR** bringing about muscle contraction    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin and actin filaments, **OR** thin and thick filaments, slide past each other to cause muscle contraction     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ titin stabilises the sarcomere structure by anchoring myosin to the Z-lines    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ titin provides elasticity **OR** recoil, helping the sarcomere return to its resting length after contraction    [1 mark]
[5 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    There are **four types of protein** involved in the muscle contraction: **troponin, tropomyosin, myosin and actin** ($\checkmark$). They all together make up a sarcomere unit that is part of a muscle fiber. **Actin**, troponin and tropomyosin make up the **thin filaments** ($\checkmark$) and **myosin** makes up the **thick filaments** in the sarcomere ($\checkmark$).

    **Tropomyosin is bound to the actin** ($\checkmark$) and troponin and when the muscle is relaxed, troponin covers myosin binding sites on actin. When calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, **troponin moves the tropomyosin** from the actin, exposing myosin binding sites ($\checkmark$). When the **binding sites** are exposed **myosin heads** attach to actin and **rotate to pull the actin in** ($\checkmark$). This **slides thick filaments along thin filaments** ($\checkmark$) and cases the **sarcomere to shorten** ($\checkmark$) and muscle to contract.

    **Titin stabilises** the sarcomere structure by **anchoring** the **myosin** filaments to the **Z-line**, **preventing overextension** during contraction ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcomeres are the repeating functional units of myofibrils   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sarcomeres can shorten       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a nerve impulse reaches the myofibril bringing about an action potential in the sarcolemma **OR** action potential passes down T-tubules of sarcolemma [1 mark]

     $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium ions bind to troponin  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ calcium attachment to troponin causes a movement of tropomyosin exposing the binding sites on actin     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads attach to the binding sites on actin **OR** cross bridges form between actin and myosin   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myosin heads release ADP, and rotate **OR** carry out power stroke, pulling the actin filament inwards     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ATP attaches to the myosin head and breaks the cross-bridge [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hydrolysis of ATP moves the myosin head into the cocked **OR** ready position [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ repeat of the process shortens the sarcomere [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the Z lines are pulled closer together [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shortening of the sarcomeres in a myofibril causes muscle contraction     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ titin stabilises the sarcomere structure, anchoring myosin to the Z-line [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ titin provides elasticity **OR** recoil **OR** helps sarcomere return to its resting length after contraction [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    :u[Sample answer]:

    The structure of sarcomeres allows them to **shorten, which causes muscle contraction** ($\checkmark$). When a nerve impulse reaches the sarcoplasmic reticulum of myofibril, **calcium ions** are released ($\checkmark$). **Calcium ions bind** to a protein called **troponin** ($\checkmark$) in the sarcomere. **Troponin moves** another protein called **tropomyosin** away from the binding sites on actin ($\checkmark$). **Myosin heads** are in the **cocked position** and contain stored energy from **ATP hydrolysis** ($\checkmark$). They bind to the **binding sites on actin** to form a **cross bridge** ($\checkmark$). The stored energy and **ADP are released** ($\checkmark$). When they are released, the **myosin heads move inwards** (“power stroke”) ($\checkmark$) and pull on the actin. **ATP attaches** to myosin heads and breaks the cross-bridge ($\checkmark$). Hydrolysis of ATP moves the myosin heads into the cocked position. This process repeats until the calcium ions are no longer available. **Repeating** this process **shortens the sarcomeres** in a myofibril and causes **muscle contraction** ($\checkmark$). **Titin**, a **structural protein**, **anchors** the **myosin filaments** to the **Z-lines**, **stabilising** the **sarcomere** during contraction ($\checkmark$).
