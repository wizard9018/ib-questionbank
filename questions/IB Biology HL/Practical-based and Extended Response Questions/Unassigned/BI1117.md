# BI1117

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Define the term phenology, and describe the impact of climate change on phenological events associated with a named species.        [3 marks]

2. Outline the anthropogenic causes and positive feedback cycles associated with climate change.                                   [5 marks]

3. Explain the impacts of climate change on organisms and ecosystems, other than those already mentioned.        [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Phenology is the study of the **timing** of biological events which are **affected by environmental changes** ($\checkmark$). Climate change can disrupt the synchronisation of events such as the arrival of **migrating reindeer** ($\checkmark$)  to their **breeding ground** which **arrive earlier** due to **higher temperatures** ($\checkmark$); the earlier availability of vegetation due to **higher temperatures** means **less food** is available ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*answer must include reference to anthropogenic causes and positive feedback cycles to gain full marks*):

    ***Anthropogenic Causes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ combustion of fossil fuels **OR** anthropogenic increase in carbon dioxide concentrations     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ land use change **OR** deforestation **OR** increased cattle farming [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ many industrial processes release greenhouse gases, contributing to climate changes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ landfills **OR** waste treatment facilities generate methane as organic waste decomposes in anaerobic conditions         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ urbanisation as urban areas generate heat **OR** urban heat island effect, contributes to localised temperature increases and can impact local weather patterns     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the destruction of natural carbon sinks **OR** forests **OR** wetlands **OR** peatlands, reduces the Earth's ability to absorb carbon dioxide [1 mark]

    ***Positive Feedback Cycles*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ ocean temperatures increase and they release stored carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. This release of CO:sub[2] amplifies the greenhouse effect     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is a loss of snow **OR** ice cover **OR** arctic ice **OR** glaciers **OR** arctic ice-albedo feedback leading to an increase in the absorption of solar radiation, which contributes to global warming **OR** as polar ice sheets melt due to warming temperatures, sea levels rise, which contributes to further melting of ice sheets          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased rate of peat **OR** undecomposed organic matter decomposition in permafrost, which releases CO:sub[2] into the atmosphere, further enhancing the greenhouse effect          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ methane is released into the atmosphere from melting permafrost, which further enhances the greenhouse effect       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased incidence of forest fires, which release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere, which further enhances the greenhouse effect, **OR** there is an increased incidence of droughts, which can increase the chance of forest fires, which release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere when they burn    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the air becomes warmer, which can hold more water vapour, a greenhouse gas, contributing to further warming [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The major anthropogenic causes of climate change are the **combustion of fossil fuels** ($\checkmark$) and the change in land use, such as **deforestation** ($\checkmark$), to clear land for cattle farming. Positive feedback cycles that enhance climate change include the release of **carbon dioxide** from the oceans due to increasing ocean temperatures ($\checkmark$), the increase in the rate of **decomposition of peat** leading to the release of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere ($\checkmark$), and the melting of permafrost which releases **methane** into the atmosphere ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ climate change is the long-term change in weather patterns    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased temperatures **OR** reduced snowfall increases the incidence of drought **OR** forest fires in boreal forests **OR** taiga    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **WHICH** reduces primary productivity (in boreal forests **OR** taiga) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased temperatures lead to the melting of land-fast ice and sea ice [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **WHICH** reduces the breeding grounds of emperor penguins (*Aptenodytes forsteri*) **OR** habitat for walruses (*Odobenus rosmarus*)     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased surface water temperatures can prevent nutrient upwelling     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **WHICH** reduces ocean primary productivity **OR** energy flow [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased global average temperatures can affect the distribution of habitats **OR** ecosystems **OR** communities             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **WHICH** could lead to an upslope shift for montane bird species **OR** range contraction **OR** northward spread of tree species found in North America     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in carbon dioxide concentrations causes ocean acidification     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **WHICH** can lead to the suppression of calcification in corals [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in ocean temperatures can cause coral bleaching     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **WHICH** can lead to the reef ecosystems collapsing [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shifts in the geographical distribution of species could lead to species disappearing from previously inhabited areas **OR** local extinctions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species might struggle to adapt to rapidly changing conditions, leading to decreased biodiversity [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rising sea levels threaten coastal habitats        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ expanding range of disease vectors enabling spread of diseases    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased temperatures **OR** droughts can lead to more frequent forest fires **OR** tree diseases  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced water availability can lead to reduced habitat for aquatic organisms    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ecosystem changes can affect human communities that rely on them for resources     [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Climate change is the long-term change in **weather patterns** ($\checkmark$), which can have different impacts on different ecosystems. In boreal forests, increasing temperatures leads to an increased incidence of **drought** and forest fires ($\checkmark$). This leads to a reduction in **primary productivity** ($\checkmark$) and a net carbon loss due to the combustion of carbon compounds. Increased temperatures also have an impact on habitats such as sea ice as they reduce the coverage of sea ice because it **melts** ($\checkmark$), which **reduces the available habitat** for organisms such as the walrus ($\checkmark$). Increased temperatures also warm the surface of the ocean waters, which can cause **coral bleaching** ($\checkmark$) and prevent **nutrient upwelling** ($\checkmark$). A reduction in available nutrients near the surface can reduce the **primary productivity** of ocean ecosystems ($\checkmark$).
