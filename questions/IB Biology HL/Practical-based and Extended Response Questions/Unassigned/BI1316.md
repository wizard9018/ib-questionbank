# BI1316

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline three sources which provide evidence for evolution.    [3 marks]

2. Describe how both bats and butterflies have wings.        [5 marks]

3. Explain the process of speciation with at least one named example.     [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA sequence **OR** RNA sequence    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ amino acid sequence of proteins     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ selective breeding **OR** artificial selection    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous structures **OR** comparative anatomy **OR** vestigial structures     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ embryonic development    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the fossil record        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ geographic distribution of species    [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Evidence for evolution can come from **DNA sequences** ($\checkmark$), **amino acid sequences** ($\checkmark$), or **homologous structures** ($\checkmark$) such as the pentadactyl limb.

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ bat wings and butterfly wings  do not share a common ancestor        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bats share common ancestry with other mammals, whereas butterflies share common ancestry with other insects **OR** bats are mammals **OR** Mammalia, whereas butterflies are insects **OR** Arthropoda     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ wings evolved through convergent evolution     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ convergent evolution is the process where unrelated organisms evolve similar characteristics **OR** traits to perform similar functions     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ wings in bats and butterflies are an example of analogous structures         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ analogous structures share a similar function but are not the result of a common ancestor                         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ wings evolved in response to similar environmental pressures **OR** ecological niches **OR** as a result of natural selection favouring flight     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ wings in bats are modified forelimbs **OR** are made of skin and bone **WHEREAS** butterfly wings are not **OR** are made of chitin         [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Bats and butterflies do not share a recent **common ancestor** ($\checkmark$), but both evolved wings due to **convergent evolution** ($\checkmark$). Convergent evolution is the process by which unrelated organisms can **evolve similar traits** due to similar necessities, such as the need to fly ($\checkmark$). This means that wings in bats and butterflies are an example of **analogous structures** ($\checkmark$) because they share a **similar function** but do not result from a common ancestor ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ speciation is the process by which a population is split and evolves differently over time to form distinct species [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ diversity exists within a population **OR** genetic variation exists within a population      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ members of a population become reproductively isolated                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sympatric speciation occurs when new species evolve within the same geographic area       [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allopatric speciation occurs when new species form in separate geographic areas due to geographical isolation            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolation can be due to geographical **OR** physical **OR** behavioural **OR** temporal barriers [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each population is exposed to different environmental conditions **OR** selective pressures [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each population evolves differently over time **OR** different adaptations evolve over time **OR** mutations affect each group differently **OR** differential selection **OR** genetic drift affects populations differently    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ these differences act as barriers to gene flow     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ over time, the groups evolve differently and can no longer interbreed, so new species have been formed     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bonobos and chimpanzees **OR** any other suitable example of speciation     [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Speciation is the process by which a population becomes split, and each group evolves differently over time, leading to the formation of **new species** ($\checkmark$). This is possible because **variation** exists within a population ($\checkmark$). When members of a population become **reproductively isolated** ($\checkmark$) by a geographical barrier like a river ($\checkmark$), both groups are exposed to different environmental conditions or **selective pressures** ($\checkmark$). Over time, the two groups evolve differently as different mutations affect both groups, this is referred to as **differential selection** ($\checkmark$). These differences between the populations act as **barriers to gene flow** ($\checkmark$). Over time, the groups evolve so differently that they can no longer **interbreed** and are two distinct species ($\checkmark$).
