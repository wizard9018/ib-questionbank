# BI1114

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the differences between mixotrophic and holozoic nutrition.  [4 marks]

2. Describe how herbivores are adapted to eating plants. [5 marks]

3. Explain the differences between obligate anaerobes, facultative anaerobes and obligate aerobes.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following

    $\ce{ -> }$ holozoic nutrition is a form of heterotrophic nutrition    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ holozoic nutrition involves ingesting an organism **OR** part of an organism, **OR** digesting an organism **OR** part of an organism,  internally **OR** energy source is ingestion of organism **OR** part of an organism        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mixotrophs are autotrophic and heterotrophic    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy **OR** nutrient source is sunlight **OR** photosynthesis **AND** ingestion of organisms **OR** parts of organisms  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mixotrophs can use whichever nutrient or energy source is most readily available **OR** require both forms of nutrition    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Holozoic nutrition** is a form of **heterotrophic** nutrition ($\checkmark$). Organisms that obtain their food in this way are consumers and obtain their nutrients by **ingesting other organisms** ($\checkmark$). **Mixotrophs** are both **autotrophs and heterotrophs** ($\checkmark$). They obtain their nutrients by **photosynthesis** or by **ingesting** other organisms ($\checkmark$). One type of mixotroph uses sunlight energy when it is available to make their own food, or if there is **little sunlight** energy available, they can **consume their food** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ some are ruminants **OR** have fermentation chambers **OR** rumens in digestive system    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ long digestive systems **OR** large caecum for breakdown of cellulose **OR** plant material    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ coprophagy **OR** eat own faeces to re-digest plant material **OR** cellulose    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ back teeth **OR** molars **OR** premolars of herbivores are broad **OR** flat for grinding    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ bacteria **OR** microorganisms in digestive system help digest cellulose **OR** plant material    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tough tongue **OR** lips **OR** mouth to survive prickles **OR** thorns **OR** stings    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ modified mouth parts **OR** stylet to pierce phloem **OR** stem to obtain sap    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mandibles  of insects to cut leaves **OR** plant material        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ long necks **OR** legs to reach branches    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ detoxification mechanisms **OR** enzymes to remove plant toxins        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ specialised digestive enzymes to digest cellulose **OR** lignin **OR** cell walls    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Herbivores have many adaptations that allow them to consume and extract nutrients from plant material. Some possess **specialised teeth**, particularly **molars** and **premolars**, which are **flat** for **grinding** and **crushing** plant tissue ($\checkmark$), as opposed to the sharp teeth used for tearing. Herbivores often have **complex digestive systems**, complete with **fermentation chambers** like the **rumen** in **ruminants** ($\checkmark$). Within these chambers, **symbiotic microorganisms** help **break down** plant matter ($\checkmark$), such as cellulose, in plant cell walls. Insects have evolved physiological adaptations such as the **stylets** of aphids that **pierce** the **phloem** to **extract sap** ($\checkmark$) and the **mandibles** of the grasshopper, which can **cut through leaves** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **seven** of the following (at least two mark points needed for each type):

    ***Obligate anaerobes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ microorganisms **OR** unicellular organisms **OR** single-celled organisms that cannot survive or grow in the presence of oxygen    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxygen is toxic to them        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ obtain their energy through anaerobic respiration or fermentation processes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ *Clostridium botulinum* **OR** *Clostridium tetani* **OR** other suitable example    [1 mark]

    ***Facultative anaerobes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ microorganisms OR unicellular organisms, **OR** single-celled organisms that can survive and grow in the presence or absence of oxygen    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can switch between aerobic respiration (using oxygen if available) and anaerobic respiration or fermentation (when oxygen is scarce)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ *Escherichia coli* **OR** *Staphylococcus aureus* **OR** yeast **OR** other suitable example    [1 mark]

    ***Obligate aerobes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ organisms that require oxygen for their growth and survival        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rely on aerobic respiration    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* **OR** *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* **OR** fish **OR** humans **OR** other suitable example    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Microorganisms can be classified based on their oxygen requirements and how they produce ATP. First, there are **obligate anaerobes**, like ***Clostridium botulinum*** ($\checkmark$), which **cannot survive** in the presence of **oxygen** ($\checkmark$). They rely on **anaerobic respiration** ($\checkmark$), or fermentation, for the production of ATP. Then, there are **facultative anaerobes**, such as ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae*** ($\checkmark$), which are flexible. They can use **both aerobic and anaerobic** ($\checkmark$) respiration depending on the available oxygen levels. Finally, **obligate aerobes** like ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** ($\checkmark$) **cannot survive** for long **without oxygen** ($\checkmark$) and primarily use **aerobic respiration** ($\checkmark$) to release energy from organic compounds.
