# BI0611

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Distinguish between eukaryotic and prokaryotic transcription. [3 marks]

2. Describe the roles of non-coding DNA sequences.  [5 marks]

3. Describe the role of DNA-binding proteins in gene expression. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ in prokaryotic cells transcription and translation of a gene can happen simultaneously, **WHEREAS** in eukaryotes they occur separately [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotic cells there are post-transcriptional modifications, **OR** addition of 5\' cap and poly-A tail, **OR** RNA splicing, **OR** removal of introns and joining of exons, **BUT** in prokaryotes, there are no post-transcriptional modifications [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotic cells transcription takes place in the nucleus, **BUT** in prokaryotes, transcription takes place in the cytoplasm [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotic cells, mature mRNA is produced from pre-mRNA **WHEREAS** in prokaryotes, mRNA is formed immediately through the transcription of the DNA strand [1 mark]

    [3 marks]
    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
A difference between transcription in eukaryotes and prokaryotes is that transcription takes place in the **nucleus** for eukaryotes, whereas, in prokaryotes, transcription takes place in the **cytoplasm** ($\checkmark$). **Prokaryotic** cells can also transcribe and translate a gene at the **same time**, whereas in **eukaryotes**, these two processes are **separated in time and place** ($\checkmark$).

    Another difference is that in eukaryotes, there are **post-transcriptional modifications** such as RNA splicing, where introns are removed and exons are joined, while in prokaryotes, there are **no post-transcriptional modifications** ($\checkmark$) due to the absence of introns.

2.    any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ do not code for polypeptides [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase binds to promoters to initiate transcription **OR** promoters are where transcription is initiated     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ proteins **OR** activators bind to enhancers to increase transcription [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ proteins, **OR** repressors bind to silencers to prevent transcription [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ promoter-proximal elements regulate transcription [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ introns allow for alternative mRNA splicing [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ telomeres protect the ends of chromosomes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some DNA sequences code for tRNA and rRNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some functions are still unknown **OR** accept any other valid example of non-coding DNA and its function [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Non-coding DNA sequences **do not code for polypeptides** ($\checkmark$). For example the promoter is a section of DNA **where RNA polymerase binds** to initiate transcription ($\checkmark$). Enhancers **increase transcription** ($\checkmark$) when proteins bind to them and silencers **prevent transcription** ($\checkmark$) when proteins bind to them. There are also genes that **code for tRNA and rRNA** ($\checkmark$) as the final product.

3.    any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ transcription factors are proteins that bind to DNA sequences to regulate transcription [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ activator proteins bind to enhancer sequences [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (this) increases the rate of transcription    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ repressor proteins bind to silencer sequences [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (this) decreases the rate of transcription [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ proteins bind to promoter-proximal elements [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (this is) to modulate **OR** regulate the transcription of genes     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase binds to the promoter [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (this) initiates  transcription    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ histones bind to DNA in order to pack the DNA more tightly [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (this is)  to prevent gene expression [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Many proteins bind to DNA sequences to regulate gene expression. For example, **activator proteins** bind to enhancers ($\checkmark$) which are non-coding sections of DNA close to the promoter.  This **increases** the rate of **transcription** ($\checkmark$). Regulatory proteins also bind to **promoter-proximal elements** ($\checkmark$). The binding of proteins to this region **regulates transcription** ($\checkmark$) of the gene.  **Repressor proteins** bind to the **silencer** ($\checkmark$) and when this happens, there is a **decrease in the rate of transcription** ($\checkmark$). Finally, **RNA polymerase** binds to the **promoter** ($\checkmark$) to **initiate transcription** ($\checkmark$) of the gene.
