# BI0870

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how geographic isolation can lead to the formation of a new species from a single interbreeding population.        [5 marks]

2. Outline what is meant by sympatric speciation.        [4 marks]

3. Describe how sexual and asexual reproduction can lead to the formation of new species with different numbers of copies of each chromosome.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:

   $\ce{ -> }$ gene flow is prevented **OR** reduced between the separated groups  [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ different selection pressures act on each isolated population due to different environments [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ natural selection favours different alleles **OR** phenotypes in each population [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ mutations arise independently in each population **OR** increase genetic variation in each population  [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ genetic drift changes allele frequencies randomly     [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ allele frequencies diverge over generations [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolating mechanisms evolve  [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ pre-zygotic isolation **OR** different mating behaviour **OR** breeding seasons **OR** mechanical **OR** gamete incompatibility may occur [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ post-zygotic isolation **OR** reduced hybrid viability **OR** hybrid sterility **OR** reduced hybrid fertility may occur [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ if secondary contact occurs, the populations do not interbreed successfully **OR**  do not produce fertile offspring [1 mark]

[5 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Geographic isolation prevents **gene flow** between the separated populations ($\checkmark$). Different selection pressures, or genetic drift, cause **allele frequencies to diverge** over time in each population ($\checkmark$). **Mutations also arise independently** in each population, increasing genetic variation between them ($\checkmark$). As genetic differences accumulate, **reproductive isolating mechanisms** evolve ($\checkmark$).  If the populations later come into **secondary contact**, they **cannot interbreed** successfully to produce fertile offspring, so a new species forms ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ occurs without geographic isolation **OR** populations remain in the same area [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolation develops within a single population [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ gene flow between subgroups is reduced **OR** prevented [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ polyploidy creates immediate reproductive isolation [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ ecological niche differentiation occurs so subgroups use different resources **OR** microhabitats [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ disruptive selection favours extreme phenotypes **OR** selects against intermediates [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ assortative mating occurs so individuals preferentially mate with similar individuals [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ sexual selection causes divergence in mate preferences **OR** mating signals [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ temporal isolation occurs due to different breeding **OR** flowering times in the same area [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ behavioural isolation occurs due to different courtship behaviours **OR** mating calls [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ a host shift occurs so individuals feed **OR** mate on different host species [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ chromosomal rearrangements reduce successful interbreeding between subgroups [1 mark]
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Sympatric speciation occurs when new species form in the **same geographic area** ($\checkmark$). Reproductive isolation evolves within a population, so **gene flow is reduced** between subgroups ($\checkmark$). This can occur through **polyploidy**, especially in plants, causing immediate reproductive isolation ($\checkmark$). It can also occur through **sexual selection** or **assortative mating**, where differences in mate choice reduce interbreeding and lead to divergence ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    ***Asexual reproduction:***

    $\ce{ -> }$ chromosome number of daughter species is an odd number [1 mark]

    ***Sexual reproduction:***

    $\ce{ -> }$ the hybridisation of closely related species [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ new hybrid species has one set of chromosomes from each parent species     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ self-fertilisation **OR** autogamy occurs, and chromosome number is doubled **OR** halved    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fusion of two diploid gametes **OR** another valid example of fertilisation leading to a change in chromosome copy number    [1 mark]

    ***Both asexual and sexual reproduction:***

    $\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolation **OR** new species are sometimes brought about by differences in chromosome copy number        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ abnormal meiotic division in parent species [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ daughter species is reproductively isolated from the parent species     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ daughter species reproduces asexually because meiosis is no longer possible     [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Both sexually and asexually reproducing species can be produced when **two closely related species hybridise** ($\checkmark$) to produce a daughter species that can carry out mitosis and is **reproductively isolated from the parent species** ($\checkmark$).  Sometimes reproductive isolation in both sexually and asexually reproducing species is brought about by a **change in chromosome copy number** ($\checkmark$).  This is often due to **abnormal meiotic divisions** ($\checkmark$).  When an **odd number of chromosomes** is present ($\checkmark$) in the daughter species, for example, 3n, **meiosis is impossible** ($\checkmark$), and the daughter species can only reproduce asexually through mitosis.  If the daughter species **reproduces through sexual reproduction, the chromosome copy number must be even** ($\checkmark$) so that **meiosis is possible** ($\checkmark$).  Sometimes a single, **self-fertilizing species may undergo abnormal meiotic divisions** ($\checkmark$), which result in the production of a **new species that has half** ($\checkmark$) or **double** ($\checkmark$) the number of chromosomes and thus becomes reproductively isolated.
