# BI1547

**Maximum mark: 6**

A student carried out an experiment to measure the energy content of different foods. Below is a diagram of the apparatus used.

![BI1547ai](images/BI1547ai.png)

At the start of the experiment, the initial temperature of the water and the mass of the food sample were recorded. The food was then set alight and immediately placed under the boiling tube of water. Once the food had finished burning, the final temperature of the water was recorded.

It takes 4.2J of energy to raise the temperature of 1g (1mL) of water by 1:sup[o]C.

The increase in the temperature of 10mL of water from 0.5g of potato crisp was 35:sup[o]C.

1. Using the information provided, calculate the energy of the potato snack in kJ g:sup[-1] [2 marks]

Part of the nutrition details from the bag of potato snacks is shown below:

![BI1547b](images/BI1547b.png)

2. Assuming the students' repeated trials yielded similar results to those you have calculated, suggest two inaccuracies in the experimental method that could explain the discrepancy between the measured energy content in the experiment and the value listed on the food label. [2 marks]

The table below shows the energy and fat content on the packs of the other foods the student tested.

| Food Type | Energy / kJ 100 $\bf{g^{-1}}$ | Fat Content/%|
| :-- | :-: | :-: |
| Butterscotch popcorn| 1837 | 11.5 |
| Pitta bread | 1005 | 2.8 |
| Digestive biscuit | 2039 | 24.0 |
| Smoked almonds | 2520 | 52.0 |
| Oat cracker | 1807 | 14.2 |
| Tortilla crisp | 2051 | 22.5 |

3. Identify, with a reason, which food investigated by the student is least likely to increase the risk of obesity. [2 marks]

 Energy transferred (J) per gram of food

 = {(mass of water (g) x temperature rise (:sup[o]C) x 4.2 (J g:sup[-1] :sup[o]C:sup[-1] ))÷
 Mass of food sample (g)}

 = **2940J g:sup[-1]** ($\checkmark$)

 Energy in kJ g:sup[-1] = 2940J g:sup[-1] ÷ 1000

 = **2.940 kJ g:sup[-1]** ($\checkmark$)
