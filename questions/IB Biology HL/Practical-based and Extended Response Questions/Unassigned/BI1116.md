# BI1116

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline how treading on a drawing pin can bring about the reflex of lifting the foot.    [5 marks]

2. Describe how the cerebellum exerts overall control over the voluntary movements of the body.    [4 marks]

3. Explain how melatonin plays a part in the control of circadian rhythms.    [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ specialised pain receptors **OR** nociceptors, are activated by drawing pin entering skin [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ activation of nociceptors **OR** sensory receptors generates action potentials [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ action potentials travel along sensory neurons (located in the foot) [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ axons enter spinal cord, transmitting action potentials to spinal cord [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ synapse with interneurons **OR** relay neurons within spinal cord [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ interneurons synapse with **OR** transmit information **OR** action potentials to motor neurons  [1 mark]

     $\ce{ -> }$ motor neurons synapse with **OR** transmit information **OR** action potentials to leg muscles **OR** effectors  [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ leg muscles **OR** effectors which bring about response **OR** lift the foot [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When you step on a drawing pin, specialised **pain receptors**, called **nociceptors, are activated** ($\checkmark$). These trigger **action potentials** in **sensory neurons** ($\checkmark$), and the **axons** of these neurons **enter** the **spinal cord** ($\checkmark$), carrying the action potential with them. The sensory neurons **synapse with interneurons** ($\checkmark$), which in turn **synapse with motor neurons** ($\checkmark$). The **motor neurons carry** the **information to** the **effectors** ($\checkmark$), the leg muscles in this case, which bring about the **response** ($\checkmark$) of lifting the leg.

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ cerebellum receives sensory input from sensory receptors when body moves [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ provides information about current position of body **OR** tension of muscles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cerebellum processes this information to form model of body's position in space [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ compares the intended movement, instructed by the cerebral cortex, with the actual sensory feedback from the body **OR** allows detection of errors in motor execution [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sends corrective signals to the motor centres in the brainstem **OR** cerebral cortex [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ controls timing and sequencing of movement **OR** controls precise sequence of muscle contractions required for complex motor tasks [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ important for maintaining balance and posture [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ involved in motor learning and memory **OR** muslce memory **OR** improved motor performance through practice and repetition  [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The cerebellum is important in **controlling** the **balance** ($\checkmark$) of the body and the **coordination of voluntary movements** ($\checkmark$). It **receives information from sensory receptors** around the body which allow it to work out the **current position** ($\checkmark$) of the body. It then **processes this information**, compares it with the instruction from the cerebral cortex, and **detects any errors** ($\checkmark$). It then sends out signals to the correct these errors. It is the cerebellum that allows us to **improve** our **motor performance** through **practice and repetition** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ melatonin is secreted **OR** produced by the pineal gland [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ production is controlled by an area in the hypothalamus [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ secretion is controlled by light and dark [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ controls sleep cycle [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cells in retina **OR** photoreceptors in retina are sensitive to light [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ triggering these cells **OR** presence of light causes signal from these cells to cause pineal gland to adjust melatonin production [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ for diurnal animals **OR** animals that are active in day melatonin levels are high at night **OR** for nocturnal animals melatonin levels are hiigh in day [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as light increases, melatonin levels drop and the animal becomes active **OR** alert  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ helps synchronise circadium rhythms as is cue for other physiological processes  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ E.g.  hormone release **OR** mating **OR** hibernation **OR** other suitable example  [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Melatonin is a hormone produced by the **pineal gland** ($\checkmark$) which is involved in regulating the **sleep-wake cycle** ($\checkmark$). Melatonin production is **controlled by the hypothalamus** ($\checkmark$). **Photoreceptors in the retina** detect **light** and send impulses to the brain that **controls** the **secretion** of the melatonin ($\checkmark$). In diurnal animals, such as humans, the **absence of light increases melatonin production** ($\checkmark$), and this promotes a state of sleepiness, contributing to the onset of sleep. Conversely, as **morning approaches** and melatonin **levels decline**, the body receives a signal to wake up and become **alert** ($\checkmark$). Beyond the sleep-wake cycle, melatonin helps **synchronise** various **circadian rhythms**  throughout the body, ensuring that different **physiological processes** ($\checkmark$) occur at the correct time, such as **mating** and **hibernation** ($\checkmark$).
