# BI1372

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe centrifugation and how developments in this technique allowed scientists to improve their understanding of the components of cells.  [4 marks]

2. Distinguish between transcription and translation in prokaryotic and eukaryotic organisms.    [5 marks]

3. Explain the advantages of cell compartmentalisation in eukaryotes.  [7 marks]

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ centrifugation allows the separation of molecular components based on differences in their relative densities **OR** sizes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a centrifuge **OR** ultracentrifuge is a device that spins at high speeds to separate substances in a suspension using centrifugal force    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows the separation of individual organelles            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell contents are homogenised **OR** broken up **OR** plasma membrane is broken to release organelles into the solution                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ substances that are less dense stay at the top **OR** form the supernatant, **WHILE** substances that are more dense settle at the bottom **OR** form the pellet    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ using cell fractionation, a multi-step process that involves successively higher speeds of centrifugation to collect progressively smaller cell components **OR** denser cell components can be separated at lower speeds    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows biologists to study **OR** determine the roles of individual organelles **OR** molecules **OR** specific chemical pathways in a controlled environment without the presence of confounding substances **OR** separate from the other reactions that occur in the cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ used in the famous experiment by Meselson and Stahl to discover the semi-conservative nature of DNA replication **OR** discovery of translation of mRNA **OR** any other example of an experiment or discovery involving centrifugation            [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Centrifugation** is a process used to **separate cell parts based on their relative densities** ($\checkmark$). An **ultracentrifuge** is a device that **rotates at high speeds** to **separate cell components** using **centrifugal force** ($\checkmark$). This allows scientists to **isolate** individual **organelles for experimentation** ($\checkmark$). **Dense organelles settle to the bottom** of the sample following centrifugation, while **less dense organelles stay at the top** ($\checkmark$). By **successively separating** the **supernatant** and **pellets** formed during centrifugation, many organelles can be isolated in a process known as **cell fractionation** ($\checkmark$). These techniques have allowed scientists to make many **discoveries** regarding the nature of various organelles and cell processes, such as the **semi-conservative nature of DNA** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotes, the genetic material **OR** DNA is separated from the cytoplasm by the nuclear membrane **OR** envelope **WHEREAS**, in prokaryotes, the genetic material is found in the cytoplasm [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotes, transcription takes place in the nucleus, and translation occurs in the cytoplasm **WHEREAS** in prokaryotes, both processes take place in the cytoplasm    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transcription and translation are discontinuous **OR** completed separately in eukaryotes **WHEREAS** transcription and translation are continuous **OR** completed simultaneously in prokaryotes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ post-transcriptional modification occurs in eukaryotes **WHEREAS** no post-transcriptional modification **OR** minimal RNA modification occurs in prokaryotes     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ eukaryotic genes contain non-coding segments called introns **WHILE** prokaryotic genes do not **OR** eukaryotes have introns and exons **WHILE** prokaryotes do not [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotes, introns are removed to produce mature mRNA **OR** RNA splicing occurs  **WHEREAS**, in prokaryotes, this process does not occur         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ movement of mRNA through nuclear pores into cytoplasm is controlled in eukaryotes, which helps regulate gene expression  **WHEREAS**, in prokaryotes, this form of gene expression regulation cannot happen    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    In **eukaryotic** organisms, the **DNA** is **separated from** the **cytoplasm** by the **nuclear membrane**, whereas the genetic material of **prokaryotes** is **within the cytoplasm** ($\checkmark$).  In eukaryotes, **transcription** occurs **within the nucleus**, while **translation** occurs **in the cytoplasm**. In prokaryotes, however, **both processes** occur in the **cytoplasm** as these organisms do not have a nucleus ($\checkmark$). Due to this lack of separation, both **transcription and translation happen simultaneously** in prokaryotes, while in eukaryotes, **transcription** must be **completed first** before **translation** can begin ($\checkmark$). This allows **post-transcriptional modification** to take place in eukaryotes ($\checkmark$), whereby the **immature mRNA is edited** through the process of **RNA splicing**, which **removes introns** ($\checkmark$), sections of a gene that **do not code for proteins**. This **post-transcriptional modification** does not occur in **prokaryotes** as they **do not have introns** ($\checkmark$).

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows organelles to have specialised functions                [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Cell **compartmentalisation** uses **membranes** to separate cell components into **specialised organelles** ($\checkmark$). This allows each organelle to take on unique roles, such as **absorbing foreign particles**, as seen in **phagocytic vacuoles** ($\checkmark$). Various **cell processes** can be **completed separately** ($\checkmark$), making them more **efficient** ($\checkmark$). Compartmentalisation also allows the cell to **control** the production and **distribution** of substances such as **enzymes** ($\checkmark$), directing them to areas where they are needed. Furthermore, compartmentalisation provides organelles such as the **mitochondria** with vastly **increased surface area** ($\checkmark$) so that **metabolic reactions** can occur, allowing it to produce much more ATP than would otherwise be possible ($\checkmark$).
