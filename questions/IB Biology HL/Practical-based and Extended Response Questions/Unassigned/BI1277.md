# BI1277

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the concept of homeostasis.    [3 marks]

2. Describe the physiological mechanisms of thermoregulation in humans and their effects on the body.    [5 marks]

3. Compare and contrast type I and type II diabetes.    [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ homeostasis is the maintenance of the internal conditions **OR** internal environment of an organism        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ conditions inside the body are kept within a range of acceptable limits **OR** preset limits **OR** optimal range **OR** safe range                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ changes in the external environment **OR** conditions outside the body **OR** external conditions are not reflected                        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ receptors detect changes **OR** receive stimulus and pass to control centre **OR** CNS **OR** brain [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ control centre **OR** CNS **OR** brain sends information **OR** nerve impulses to effector **OR** muscle or gland         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ use of negative feedback loops to return homeostatic variables to set point        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ named example, such as blood pH, body temperature, blood glucose concentration, or blood osmotic concentration        [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Homeostasis is the body's way of **maintaining** stable **internal conditions** ($\checkmark$). Homeostatic variables are **kept within an optimal range** ($\checkmark$) through the use of **negative feedback loops**, which return internal conditions to the **set point** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following (*must include the mechanism **and** effect on the body to receive each mark*):

    $\ce{ -> }$ vasodilation **OR** relaxation of smooth muscle in arterioles supplying the skin **WHICH** increases blood flow near the skin surface so more heat is lost by radiation **OR** lowers body temperature **OR** cools the body    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vasoconstriction **OR** contraction of smooth muscle in arterioles supplying the skin **WHICH** diverts blood away from the skin surface so less heat is lost by radiation **OR** prevents heat loss **OR** keeps the body warm    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ shivering **WHICH** increases cellular respiration in muscles, releasing heat **OR** raises the body temperature **OR** warms the body    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sweating **WHICH** removes heat as the water in sweat evaporates **OR** lowers body temperature **OR** cools the body    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ arrector pili muscles relax **OR** hairs lie flat **WHICH** reduces the insulating layer of trapped air, increasing heat loss **OR** lowers body temperature **OR** cools the body    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ arrector pili muscles contract **OR** hairs stand erect **WHICH** traps a layer of air that insulates the skin **OR** prevents heat loss **OR** keeps the body warm    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ uncoupled respiration in brown adipose tissue **WHICH** releases energy as heat instead of producing ATP **OR** raises body temperature **OR** warms the body    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Shivering** and **sweating** are both used by humans to adjust the core body temperature. When humans **shiver**, rapid involuntary muscle contractions increase cellular respiration, releasing heat, which **raises** the **body temperature** slightly ($\checkmark$). Conversely, **sweating** releases water onto the skin; as this water evaporates it removes heat from the body, lowering the **body temperature** ($\checkmark$). Hairs on the skin are also involved in thermoregulation. When thermoreceptors detect a rise in body temperature, the arrector pili muscles relax, causing the hairs to lie flat. This reduces the layer of trapped air, increasing heat loss and cooling the body ($\checkmark$). On the other hand, when body temperature falls, the arrector pili muscles contract, causing the hairs to stand erect. This traps a layer of insulating air next to the skin, reducing heat loss and keeping the body warm ($\checkmark$). Blood vessels also respond: when body temperature rises, arterioles supplying the skin dilate (vasodilation), increasing blood flow near the skin surface so that more heat is lost by radiation ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following (must include at least **one** similarity and at least **one** difference to receive full marks):

    *similarities:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** types are characterised by high blood sugar **OR** blood glucose concentrations **OR** hyperglycaemia    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** types can lead to excessive urination **OR** thirst (although more severe for type I)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ glucose can be excreted in urine in **BOTH** types of diabetes when blood glucose levels exceed renal threshold    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ patients with type I **AND** type II must monitor and adjust their diets carefully    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** conditions, if untreated, can lead to serious health effects, such as renal failure **OR** heart attacks **OR** strokes **OR** blindness **OR** death    [1 mark]

    *differences:* \
    $\ce{ -> }$ type I diabetes is rare **WHEREAS** type II diabetes is common (~90% of cases)    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ type I diabetes is also known as ‘early onset diabetes' **OR** more common in young people **OR** people under the age of 20 **WHILE** type II diabetes is also known as ‘late onset diabetes' **OR** historically associated with older adults but is now increasingly found in younger people    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ type I is an autoimmune disorder **WHILE** type II is not    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ type I patients produce very little **OR** no insulin **WHEREAS** type II patients may produce insulin but in insufficient amounts **OR** in a form the body cannot use effectively **OR** body does not respond to it    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ type I is caused by the destruction of **OR** failure of beta (β) cells in the pancreas to produce insulin **WHEREAS** type II is caused by insulin resistance **OR** a failure of receptors to respond to insulin **OR** impaired insulin secretion    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ type I must be treated by insulin injection **OR** insulin pumps **WHILE** type II can often be treated with diet **OR** weight management **OR** exercise **OR** may require medication **OR** insulin as the disease progresses    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ type I can develop rapidly **OR** over days or weeks **WHEREAS** type II develops gradually **OR** over years    [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Both type I and type II diabetes are characterised by **high blood glucose levels** ($\checkmark$) due to problems with insulin regulation, with **glucose** being **excreted** in the **urine** when blood **glucose levels exceed** the **renal threshold** ($\checkmark$). Both sets of patients must modify their **diets** ($\checkmark$) carefully to avoid long-term **health effects** such as **renal failure** and **stroke** ($\checkmark$). Type I diabetes is considered an **autoimmune disorder**, while type II diabetes is not ($\checkmark$). This is because type I diabetes is caused by the **destruction of pancreatic beta cells**, which produce **insulin**, whereas type II diabetes is caused by **insulin resistance**, where **cells do not respond properly** to insulin, even though it may still be produced ($\checkmark$). Type I diabetes typically requires **lifelong insulin therapy**, whereas type II diabetes can often be **managed with lifestyle changes** such as **diet** and **exercise**, though **medication** may be necessary as the **condition progresses** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, type I diabetes **develops rapidly**, often **over weeks**, while type II **progresses slowly** over **months or years** ($\checkmark$).
