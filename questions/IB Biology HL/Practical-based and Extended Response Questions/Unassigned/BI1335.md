# BI1335

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how the structure of water molecules gives water its solvent properties. [4 marks]

2. Outline the importance of isotonic solutions in medical applications. [5 marks]

3. A turgid plant cell is placed in a solution with a higher solute concentration than its cell contents.
 Explain the changes in the solute potential, pressure potential and water potential of the plant cell.

*(Assume constant temperature, no movement of solutes across the cell membrane, and atmospheric pressure in the surrounding solution.)* [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

$\ce{ -> }$ electrons are shared unequally in the covalent bonds between oxygen and hydrogen [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ water molecules are polar **OR** oxygen has a partial negative charge **AND** hydrogen has a partial positive charge [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ positively charged ions are attracted to the oxygen atoms of water molecules [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ negatively charged ions are attracted to the hydrogen atoms of water molecules [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ hydrogen bonds form between water molecules and polar solute molecules [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ water molecules surround solute particles, allowing them to disperse through the water [1 mark]

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

Water molecules are **polar**, with a **partial negative charge** on **oxygen** and **partial positive charges** on **hydrogen** ($\checkmark$). **Positively charged ions** are **attracted** to the **oxygen atoms** of water molecules ($\checkmark$), whereas **negatively charged ions** are **attracted** to the **hydrogen atoms** ($\checkmark$). Water molecules surround **solute molecules**, allowing them to **disperse** through aqueous solutions ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

$\ce{ -> }$ isotonic solutions have the same effective solute concentration as the cell contents [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ no net movement of water into or out of cells [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ used in intravenous fluids [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ used to bathe organs before transplantation [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ animal cells lack a cell wall to prevent bursting [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ prevent cells from swelling and bursting/lysis [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ prevent cells from shrinking/crenation [1 mark]

[5 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

**Isotonic** solutions have the **same solute concentration** as the **cells** ($\checkmark$) and so are used in **intravenous fluids** ($\checkmark$) and to **bathe organs** before **transplantation** ($\checkmark$). They **prevent cells** from **swelling** and **bursting** ($\checkmark$) or **shrinking** and becoming **crenated** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** statement about the change in each of: solute potential, pressure potential **AND** water potential to gain full marks*):

***Water movement***\

$\ce{ -> }$ the surrounding solution initially has a lower water potential than the cell contents [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ water moves out of the cell by osmosis [1 mark]

***Solute potential***\

$\ce{ -> }$ the solute concentration within the cell increases [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ solute potential decreases **OR** becomes more negative [1 mark]

***Pressure potential***\

$\ce{ -> }$ the cell contents exert less pressure against the cell wall **OR** the cell wall exerts less pressure on the cell contents [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ pressure potential decreases [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ the vacuole/cytoplasm shrinks **OR** the volume of the protoplast decreases [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ pressure potential may fall to zero **OR** the cell becomes flaccid/plasmolysed (if the solution is sufficiently concentrated) [1 mark]

***Water potential and equilibrium***\

$\ce{ -> }$ water potential is equal to solute potential plus pressure potential **OR** $\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$ [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ the water potential of the cell decreases [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ net movement continues until the water potential of the cell equals that of the surrounding solution [1 mark]
$\ce{ -> }$ at equilibrium, water continues to move in both directions at equal rates [1 mark]

[7 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

Water moves **out** of the cell by **osmosis** ($\checkmark$). This **increases** the **solute concentration within** the **cell** ($\checkmark$), making its **solute potential more negative** ($\checkmark$).

As **water** is **lost from** the **vacuole**, **pressure potential decreases** ($\checkmark$). As $\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$, the decreases in solute potential and pressure potential **lower the water potential** of the cell ($\checkmark$).

**Net water movement continues until** the **water potential** of the **cell equals** that of the surrounding **solution** ($\checkmark$). Once **equilibrium** has been reached, **net movement** of water in **each direction** is **equal** ($\checkmark$).
