# BI1002

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Adaptive radiation results in a single ancestral species evolving into multiple, closely related new species which may live in close proximity to each other.  Outline 3 mechanisms by which closely related species living in close proximity may be reproductively isolated.    [4 marks]

2. Outline the process by which two species may be formed from an ancestral population through the process of allopatric speciation.                        [5 marks]

3. Explain what polyploidy is and how it can lead to speciation.        [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ behavioural isolation **OR** failure to recognise members of different species as possible mates        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ temporal isolation **OR** reproduction occurs at different times [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ postzygotic barriers        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ habitat isolation, whereby a subsection of one population occupies a different ecological niche [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hybrid offspring are sterile         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mechanical isolation **OR** different morphological structures evolve, resulting in physical incompatibility            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ two closely related species may have different numbers of chromosomes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ incompatibility of gametes **OR** inability of sperm to fertilise egg [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Species living close to one another may be **reproductively isolated** due to **behavioural isolation** ($\checkmark$), which is a **failure** to **recognise** potential **mates**.  **Two species** may also be **reproductively isolated** by **postzygotic barriers** ($\checkmark$). For example, **differences in chromosome number** between closely related species ($\checkmark$) may result in **hybrid offspring** that are **sterile** because their chromosomes cannot pair correctly during meiosis ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ geographic isolation **OR** ancestral population is separated into two areas **OR** any named example, e.g.  mountain range, isthmus [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different random mutations accrue in the two different populations **OR** different alleles are produced in the two different populations    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different phenotypes are produced in the two different populations    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different selection pressures in two different areas    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different traits selected for in two different areas  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic drift            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolation **OR** behavioural isolation **OR** temporal isolation **OR** post-zygotic barriers        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gene flow cannot occur        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ changes occur over many generations    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The ancestral population could have been divided into **two populations** by the formation of a **mountain range** ($\checkmark$), which means **gene flow** between the two populations **does not occur** ($\checkmark$).  **Random mutations** then build up ($\checkmark$) between the two populations leading to **new phenotypes** ($\checkmark$).  **Selection** pressures may **differ** ($\checkmark$) between the two populations which means **different phenotypes** would be selected for in the **two different environments**.  Eventually, the two different populations may become **reproductively isolated** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic condition in which an organism has more than two complete sets of chromosomes in its cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ more common in plants            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ formation of new species is through two main mechanisms: autopolyploidy and allopolyploidy  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ autopolyploidy occurs due to failure of chromosomes to separate during meiosis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ autopolyploidy occurs within one species    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reproductive isolation of polyploid individuals from diploid population    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced **OR** cessation of gene flow in population due to polyploids being incompatible with diploids                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polyploid population begins to evolve independently from the diploid population **OR** genetic divergence occurs    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allopolyploidy occurs when two different species interbreed and produce hybrid offspring [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can result in an entire extra set of chromosomes from each parent species    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allopolyploid individuals often reproduce asexually due to the mismatched chromosomes from different species        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ asexual reproduction acts as a reproductive barrier between the allopolyploid and its parent species [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced gene flow allows for independent evolution **OR** genetic divergence    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ over time, the allopolyploid population may accumulate genetic differences and adapt to specific ecological niches, leading to the formation of a new species        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polyploidy can result in odd ploidy number **OR** 3n, 5n, etc. in daughter species        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ individuals with odd ploidy number cannot undergo meiosis which acts as a reproductive barrier     [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Polyploidy is the condition of having **more** than **two sets** of **chromosomes** ($\checkmark$). **Autopolyploidy** is due to the failure of chromosomes to **separate** during **meiosis** ($\checkmark$).  Often, autopolyploid individuals are **reproductively isolated** ($\checkmark$) from the parent species due to their **gametes** being **incompatible** with the diploid parent’s gametes.

    **Allopolyploid** species are produced when two different **species hybridise** ($\checkmark$) resulting in a daughter species with an **extra set of chromosomes** from each parent species ($\checkmark$).

    In both **allopolyploids and autopolyploids** ($\checkmark$), occasionally individuals are produced with **odd ploidy numbers** (ex: 3n, 5n) ($\checkmark$).  These individuals will generally be **unable** to undergo **meiosis** ($\checkmark$), will **reproduce asexually** and thus be **reproductively isolated** from their parent species ($\checkmark$).

    Once two populations are reproductively isolated there is **no gene flow** ($\checkmark$) and a new species is produced.
