# BI1716

**Maximum mark: 16**

1.  Explain how mitosis is regulated in eukaryotic cells.    [5 marks]

2.  Describe the stages of interphase and their importance to the process of mitosis.    [4 marks]

3.  Discuss the importance of mitosis to living organisms using named examples.     [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  any **five**  of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ cyclins are proteins involved in the regulation of mitosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ levels of cyclins rise and fall at specific times in cell cycle        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ kinases  **OR** cyclin-dependent kinases are enzymes activated by cyclins    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ signalling occurs at specific checkpoints **OR** stop points of cell cycle    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ G$_2$ checkpoint controls whether cell goes into mitosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as cyclin level rises, it attaches to specific kinase or cyclin-dependent kinase **OR** CDK        [1 mark]

    

    $\ce{ -> }$ acts as mitosis-promoting factor    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ As mitosis-promoting factor **OR** MPF **OR** cyclin-kinase complex builds up switches on mitosis **OR** triggers nuclear membrane breakdown [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    **Cyclins** are **proteins** that **regulate mitosis** ($\checkmark$), with their **levels rising** and **falling** at **specific times** ($\checkmark$) in the cell cycle. They **activate cyclin-dependent kinases** ($\checkmark$) (CDKs), forming a **complex** that acts as a **mitosis-promoting factor** ($\checkmark$) (MPF). This complex **triggers** processes such as **nuclear membrane breakdown**, enabling **mitosis** to **proceed** ($\checkmark$). The **G$_2$ checkpoint** ensures **favourable conditions** before the **cell enters mitosis** ($\checkmark$).

2.  Any **four**  of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ in G$_1$ **OR** G$_2$  the cell grows larger    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in G$_1$ the cell performs its normal metabolic functions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in S phase, the cell replicates its DNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in S phase, the cell replicates its centrosomes **OR** centrioles    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in G$_2$ phase, the cell duplicates its organelles **OR** synthesises proteins required for cell division    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the replication of DNA ensures that each daughter cell receives an identical set of chromosomes during mitosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in animal cells the replication of centrosomes **OR** centrioles is critical for forming the mitotic spindle during mitosis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the growth and preparation during G$_1$ and G$_2$ phases provide the necessary cellular components and energy for successful cell division during mitosis    [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    Interphase is split into three stages. In **G$_1$**, the **cell grows larger** ($\checkmark$)  and performs its **normal metabolic functions** ($\checkmark$), while in the **S phase**, it **replicates** its **DNA** ($\checkmark$)  and **centrosomes** ($\checkmark$). During **G$_2$**, the cell grows, **duplicates** its **organelles**, and **synthesises proteins** ($\checkmark$)  necessary for cell division. These stages ensure the cell has **enough components** and an **identical set of chromosomes** to successfully **divide into two daughter cells** ($\checkmark$)  during mitosis.

3.  Any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ necessary to produce genetically identical cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ happens only in eukaryotes   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ provides new cells for growth    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ suitable examples of growth include embryonic development **OR** organ growth    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tissue repair **OR** wound healing    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tissue repair can protect harmful microorganisms from entering a wound    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ essential for regeneration of limbs in certain organisms  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ asexual reproduction    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ suitable examples of asexual reproduction include budding in hydra **OR** budding in yeast **OR** runners in plants **OR** tubers in plants    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ replacement of old **OR** dying cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ suitable example of replacement of cells include renewing of skin cells **OR** renewing intestinal lining [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ uncontrolled mitosis can result in the growth of tumours    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ maintains chromosome number in somatic cells.    [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \

    Mitosis is essential for producing **genetically identical cells** ($\checkmark$), a process **exclusive to eukaryotes** ($\checkmark$). It enables **growth** ($\checkmark$), for example, **embryonic development** and **organ growth** ($\checkmark$) and **tissue repair**, such as in **wound healing** ($\checkmark$), which **protects against** harmful **microorganisms** ($\checkmark$).  **Asexual reproduction** ($\checkmark$) relies on mitosis, for example, budding in **Hydra** and **yeast**. Mitosis **replaces old** or **dying cells** ($\checkmark$) and **maintains chromosome number** in **somatic cells** ($\checkmark$). However, **uncontrolled mitosis** can lead to **tumour** formation ($\checkmark$).
