# BI1243

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Suggest reasons why some DNA sequences are highly conserved.        [3 marks]

2. Describe how CRISPR-Cas9 can be used to remove a gene from a section of DNA.        [5 marks]

3. Discuss the economic, social and ethical issues surrounding the use of CRISPR-Cas9 technology to edit genes in humans.        [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ they have slower rates of mutation than other sequences        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they regulate genes that are vital for life **OR** they are DNA sequences that control gene expression **OR** promoters OR enhancers **OR** they are binding sites **OR** transcription factor binding sites [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they code for proteins that are vital for life **OR** they code for proteins with crucial functions **OR** required for basic metabolism **OR** reproduction **OR** embryonic development  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they code for structural and functional RNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they provide structural stability      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ required for immune system recognition        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondrial DNA **OR** encodes essential components for respiration in mitochondria     [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The hypotheses that account for highly conserved sequences in genes, are that they have **slower** rates of **mutation** than other sequences ($\checkmark$), they **regulate genes** or code for transcription factors that are **vital for life** ($\checkmark$), or they **code for genes** that are functionally **important** ($\checkmark$), such as those involved in cell structure or metabolism.

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA guide **OR** gRNA sequence made that is complementary to a specific DNA sequence **OR** DNA strand with target gene is used as a template to make guide RNA **OR** gRNA **OR** guide RNA **OR** gRNA produced to identify gene of interest **OR** that matches gene of interest        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA guide **OR** gRNA and Cas9 enzyme bind together **OR** Cas9 enzyme is attached to guide RNA **OR** gRNA        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ guide RNA **OR** gRNA and Cas9 injected **OR** delivered into the cell        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA guide **OR** gRNA sequence binds to **OR** identifies target DNA sequence **OR** gene of interest to be cut    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ PAM sequence **OR** protospacer adjacent motif assists Cas9 to attach to DNA        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Cas9 cuts both strands of DNA **OR** cuts out gene of interest **OR** mutated DNA **OR** DNA sequence    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell’s own DNA repair machinery silences gene of interest **OR** targeted gene        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ removed **OR** mutated gene can be replaced by correct gene        [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    CRISPR-Cas9 can be used to remove genes from cells by researchers making a **RNA guide**  that is **complementary** to the specific target DNA sequence ($\checkmark$). The **RNA guide binds** to the **Cas9 enzyme** ($\checkmark$), as it will **direct** the enzyme to the correct DNA sequence to be cleaved. The  **RNA guide binds** to the **target DNA** sequence ($\checkmark$) and **Cas9 cuts both strands** of the DNA ($\checkmark$). Finally, the cell’s own **DNA repair machinery** will **silence the targeted gene** ($\checkmark$) and repair the strand.

3. any **eight** of the following (at least **one** from each area):

    **Ethical** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ any changes to genes in embryos **OR** gametes will be inherited by offspring    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ has only been used in a few clinical trials    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ humans are ‘playing God’ [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could lead to unexpected mutations **OR** off-target effects that radically change humans **OR** potential consequences may not be seen until later generations        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could lead to ‘designer babies’ **OR** designing aesthetically pleasing humans    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ensuring informed consent from individuals or parents is challenging        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there must be international governance **OR** coordination to address ethical standards and safe usage    [1 mark]

    **Economic** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ cures for diseases may be too expensive for poorer countries **OR** may strain healthcare budgets        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could save governments money in the long term as diseases could be eliminated        [1 mark]

    **Social** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ has the potential to eliminate diseases from the human population        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can be used to target molecular cancer pathways which is less stressful to the body than current cancer treatments        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could introduce genes that improve the human race e.g. genes that boost immunity [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is some public mistrust of genetically modified organisms        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ availability and affordability could be limited to certain socio-economic groups **OR** exacerbate existing social disparities        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ could create unrealistic expectations about genetic enhancement **OR** discrimination against people with genetic diseases        [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    CRISPR-Cas9 technology can be used to edit genes, however, there are many issues surrounding its use in humans. Some of the ethical issues include that any changes to genes in **embryos** will be **inherited by offspring** ($\checkmark$) who have no say over these changes. As humans are genetically complex and CRISPR has only been used in a **few clinical trials** ($\checkmark$),  there may be **off target effects** ($\checkmark$) introduced to people which may pose a risk to the population. Economic issues include that the technology may be too **expensive for poorer countries** ($\checkmark$) but could also **save** governments or charities a **large amount of money** in the long term ($\checkmark$), as **diseases could be eliminated** from the population ($\checkmark$). Socially, there is already some **public mistrust** of GMOs ($\checkmark$) so people may either be reluctant to receive treatments or people with treatments may be treated differently. CRISPR could be used to introduce genes into the human population that **‘improve’ the human race** ($\checkmark$) for example, genes that protect people from catching flu. A positive outcome is if it could be used to **target pathways involved in cancer progression** ($\checkmark$), which may be a less stressful treatment to the body than current cancer treatments such as chemotherapy.
