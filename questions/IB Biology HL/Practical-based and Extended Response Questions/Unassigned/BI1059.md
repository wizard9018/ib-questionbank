# BI1059

**Maximum mark: 16**

Gas exchange is very important for both plants and animals.

1. Outline the properties of gas-exchange surfaces.    [4 marks]

2. Describe how muscles bring about pressure changes during inspiration in humans.        [4 marks]

3. Transpiration is a result of gas exchange in plants. Explain how different environmental factors affect the rate of transpiration.     [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ thin **OR** one cell thick for short diffusion distance     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large surface area for maximum diffusion rate                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ moist for gasses to dissolve, facilitating movement            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ often supplied with good transport system to maintain steep diffusion gradient    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ partially permeable membranes **OR** permeable to gases        [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Gas-exchange surfaces have a **large surface area** to maximise **diffusion** rate ($\checkmark$). They are **thin**, often one cell thick, to make the **diffusion distance** as **short** as possible ($\checkmark$). They are usually **moist** so the **gases** can **dissolve** in them ($\checkmark$), to facilitate movement. The membranes are **permeable** to gases to **diffuse** through them ($\checkmark$), and they often have a **good transport system** ($\checkmark$) to maintain a **steep diffusion gradient** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ diaphragm contracts and moves downwards     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ external intercostal muscles contract **OR** internal intercostal muscles relax    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribcage is pulled upwards and outwards        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increasing the volume of the thorax            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ air particles become more spread out **OR** lower pressure inside the thorax      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ air flows into the lungs     [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    During inspiration, the **diaphragm contracts** and moves downwards ($\checkmark$).  **External intercostal muscles** contract ($\checkmark$) and pull the ribcage **upwards** and outwards ($\checkmark$) which **increases the volume** ($\checkmark$) of the thoracic cavity. When the volume increases, the **pressure** inside the thoracic cavity **decreases** ($\checkmark$) moving **air into** the lungs ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ transpiration involves the loss of water through stomata    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ environmental factors that affect water loss through transpiration are temperature, humidity **and** wind **OR** air movement        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at higher temperatures, the rate of transpiration increases        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ because heat increases rate of evaporation in leaves **OR** rate water diffuses out of stomata **OR** the kinetic energy of the water molecules is greater    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at extremely high temperatures, the rate of transpiration may decrease    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ because stomata will close to prevent further water loss            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at higher humidity, the rate of transpiration decreases        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ because when surrounding humidity is high, the rate water evaporates in leaves decreases **OR** the rate water vapour diffuses out of stomata decreases **OR** the concentration gradient between the inside of the leaf and the surrounding air is lower [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at higher light intensity, the rate of transpiration increases        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ because light intensity stimulates stomata to open allowing water to diffuse out    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increased wind speed increases the rate of transpiration            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ because wind carries the water vapour away from the stomata increasing the concentration gradient around the pores            [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Transpiration involves the loss of water from leaves through **stomata** ($\checkmark$). The rate of transpiration is affected by environmental factors such as **temperature, humidity, and air movement** ($\checkmark$). **Higher temperatures** cause the rate of transpiration to **increase** ($\checkmark$) because **heat energy** absorbed by water in the inner air space of leaves causes water to **evaporate more rapidly** ($\checkmark$). **Higher humidity** causes the rate of transpiration to **decrease** ($\checkmark$). This is because air that is more humid carries more water particles in it which reduces the **concentration gradient** ($\checkmark$) between the inside of the stomata and outside, thus reducing the rate water diffuses out of the stomata.  Lastly, **increases in wind speed** can **increase** the rate of transpiration ($\checkmark$).  Wind carries away water vapour from the surface of leaves, which **increases the concentration gradient** ($\checkmark$) of water between the inside and outside of the stomata.
