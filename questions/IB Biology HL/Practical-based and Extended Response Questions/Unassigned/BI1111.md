# BI1111

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Outline the primary sources of variation in a species.     [4 marks]

2. Describe the possible outcomes of a population producing more offspring than available resources can support.         [5 marks]

3. John Endler carried out a series of experiments in the 1970s on the selection pressures that influenced the colour of guppy fish. State the independent variable in Endler's experiments and explain the selective pressures exerted on male guppies. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations result in changes to the base sequence of DNA        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations can be neutral, advantageous or detrimental          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ recombination of genes during sexual reproduction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ random orientation of chromosomes in metaphase 1 of meiosis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ crossing over during meiosis [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Genetic variation within a species arises primarily from several sources. First, **mutations** are spontaneous **changes in the DNA sequence**,($\checkmark$) which can result in new alleles or gene variants. **Recombination of chromosomes** during **sexual reproduction** ($\checkmark$) results in unique combinations of alleles in offspring. Then, in meiosis, **crossing over** ($\checkmark$) can occur during prophase 1, and **random orientation of chromosomes** ($\checkmark$) does occur in metaphase 1 of meiosis.

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in population increases genetic diversity **OR** variation **OR** increases gene pool        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ individuals with favourable characteristics OR advantageous traits **OR** better adapted, survive and reproduce     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species may become better adapted over time    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in population causes an increase in competition for resources     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ depletion in essential resources lowers overall fitness of population **OR** reduces reproductive success **OR** struggle for survival **OR** increases mortality rates **OR** increased suffering of individuals less adapted **OR** with unfavourable characteristics  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ higher population densities increase disease transmission **OR** environmental degradation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ emigration reduces gene pool **OR** changes population dynamics    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Overproducing offspring in an environment with limited resources can have the advantageous effect of **increasing** the **genetic diversity** ($\checkmark$) of the population. This increased diversity can lead to more individuals with **favourable characteristics** surviving to reproductive age ($\checkmark$). This could eventually lead to a **better adapted population** ($\checkmark$) over the generations.  However, this population rise also has disadvantages, as there is **greater competition for resources** ($\checkmark$), and if **essential resources** are **depleted** too much, the **death rate may increase** ($\checkmark$). There is also an **increased risk** of the spread of **disease** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ independent variable is presence or absence of predators    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ predation pressure from visual predators **OR** birds **OR** larger fish     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ brighter colours make guppies more vulnerable to predation **OR** more conspicuous [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sexual selection pressure [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ female guppies prefer males with vibrant colours **OR** that are more colourful     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ female guppies prefer males with more elaborate courtship displays    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in low-predation environments **OR** areas with fewer predators, guppies have brighter colours **OR** in high-predation areas, males have duller **OR** less bright colours    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ environmental conditions can influence health **OR** size of male guppies, indirectly affecting attractiveness to females    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ male-male competition can affect size **OR** aggressiveness of male guppies    [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **independent variable** in Endler's experiments is the presence or absence of **predators** ($\checkmark$). **Predation pressure** from visual predators ($\checkmark$) poses a shared risk to males with bright colours, leading to **duller colours** in **high predation** environments ($\checkmark$) to reduce detection. **Sexual selection** ($\checkmark$), driven by female mate choice, favours **more colourful traits** ($\checkmark$) and more **elaborate courtship displays** ($\checkmark$) if they are to reproduce successfully. **Environmental conditions**, such as **water temperature**, also affect the **health** and **size** of males ($\checkmark$) and indirectly **influence their attractiveness** to females. Additionally, selection pressures stemming from **male-male competition** for access to females can result in variations in traits like **size or weaponry** ($\checkmark$), particularly in areas with high population density or resource scarcity.
