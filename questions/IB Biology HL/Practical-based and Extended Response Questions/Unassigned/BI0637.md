# BI0637

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe the structure of a typical human motor neuron. [4 marks]

2. Outline why myelination is essential for the rapid transmission of impulses. [4 marks]

3. Explain the importance of positive ions in the generation and propagation of an action potential. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    any **four** of the following (***accept a correctly annotated diagram as an answer***)  :

    $\ce{ -> }$ long axon extends from cell body [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myelin sheath **OR** Schwann cells wrapped around axon    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  nodes of Ranvier are gaps between Schwann cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  cell body contains nucleus and other organelles [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ dendrons extend from **OR** lead towards cell body [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ dendrites are short fibres branching from dendrons [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  axon terminal **OR** synaptic bulb **OR** synaptic knob at end of axon     [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    A typical motor neuron consists of a **cell body** containing the **nucleus** and other **organelles** ($\checkmark$). **Dendrons extend** from the **cell body** ($\checkmark$) and **branch** into **dendrites** ($\checkmark$), which receive incoming signals. The **axon**, which also **extends** from the **cell body**,($\checkmark$)  is **myelinated** by **Schwann cells** ($\checkmark$) with **nodes of Ranvier** in between ($\checkmark$).

2.    any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  myelin sheath is composed of proteins and lipids **OR** myelin is made up of Schwann cells, **OR** oligodendrocytes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  myelin acts as an insulator     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  there are gaps **OR** nodes of Ranvier between Schwann cells on the axon [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (myelin) allows for nerve impulses to jump from node to node **OR** saltatory conduction [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  sodium ions diffuse from node to node without leaking out of the axon **OR** local currents stimulate downstream nodes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  sodium and potassium channels are concentrated at the nodes (increasing the speed of neurotransmission)    [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Schwann cells deposit layers of **proteins and lipids** ($\checkmark$) called myelin around axons. Gaps between myelin-containing Schwann cells on the axon are called **nodes of Ranvier** ($\checkmark$).  Myelin acts as an **insulator** ($\checkmark$),  so nerve impulses can **jump from node to node** ($\checkmark$), making neurotransmission faster than in an unmyelinated neuron. **Sodium and potassium channels** are concentrated at the nodes ($\checkmark$), which increases the speed of neurotransmission.

3.    any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  an action potential is the depolarisation and repolarisation of a neuron due to the movement of positive ions    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  inside the neuron, there is a high concentration of  K$^+$ ions and a low concentration of Na$^+$ ions     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  depolarisation is due to  Na$^+$ ions diffusing into the neuron [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  Na$^+$ ions diffuse in through ligand-gated channels on dendrites, **OR** cell body, **OR** synaptic transmission, causes initial Na$^+$ ion influx    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  if threshold potential is reached, **OR** at -55mV, voltage-gated Na$^+$ channels open [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  the voltage-gated influx of Na$^+$ ions makes the inside more positive compared to the outside, **OR** raising the membrane potential up to +30mV    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (at +30mV) voltage-gated sodium channels close    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (at +30mV) voltage-gated potassium channels open    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  repolarization happens due to K$^+$ ions diffusing out of the neuron     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  cell interior becomes more negative compared to the outside of the cell **OR** reaches -75mV **OR** hyperpolarisation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  (after -75mV) voltage-gated K$^+$ channels close    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  Na$^+\text{\textendash}$K$^+$ pump restores the relative concentrations of Na$^+$ and K$^+$ in the cell [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
An **action potential** is the **depolarisation and repolarisation** of a neuron due to the movement of positive ions ($\checkmark$). The initial depolarisation occurs on the cell body or on dendrites at synapses. Here **neurotransmitters** cause sodium channels to open and **Na$^+$ ions diffuse** into the neuron ($\checkmark$). There is an ion imbalance in the neuron where the concentration of **Na$^+$ is higher outside the neuron** and the concentration of **K$^+$ is higher inside** the neuron ($\checkmark$). The resting potential of a neuron is about -70 mV.

    If the initial depolarisation reaches the **threshold potential**, an action potential is generated and **voltage-gated Na$^+$ channels open** ($\checkmark$).  **Na$^+$ ions diffuse into** the neuron, leading to the membrane potential rising to **+30mV** ($\checkmark$).  At +30mV, voltage-gated **sodium channels close** ($\checkmark$), and voltage-gated **potassium channels open** ($\checkmark$). This is followed by **repolarisation**, where K$^+$ ions diffuse out of the neuron ($\checkmark$), making the inside of the cell **more negative** compared to the outside of the cell ($\checkmark$).  During the final stage of an action potential, the **Na$^+\text{\textendash}$K$^+$ pump** re-establishes the initial relative concentrations ($\checkmark$) of sodium and potassium in and out of the neuron.
