2. any **five** of the following (*must include at least **one** from each section to gain full marks*):

    ***Type I pneumocytes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ for gas exchange    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large surface area to maximise gas exchange        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ thin to reduce diffusion distance **OR** to reduce distance between capillaries and inside alveoli [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ lack many organelles to reduce barriers for gas exchange     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tight junctions between adjacent cells to prevent fluid **OR** gas **OR** air leakage    [1 mark]

    ***Type II pneumocytes*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ secrete **OR** produce surfactant **OR** a fluid to reduce surface tension in alveoli    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cuboidal to provide space for organelles **OR** numerous organelles **OR** secretory granules for production **OR** storage **OR** secretion of surfactant    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ can differentiate into type 1 pneumocytes **OR** can regenerate type 1 cells [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
    **Type 1 pneumocytes** are **thin** so there is a **short diffusion distance** between the capillaries and the inside of the alveoli ($\checkmark$). Their shape **increases** their **surface area**, and they have **fewer organelles** so there are **fewer barriers** to diffusion ($\checkmark$). All these adaptations enable **rapid exchange of gases** ($\checkmark$). The type 1 cells have **fewer organelles**, so there are fewer barriers, enabling rapid **exchange** of **gases** ($\checkmark$).

    **Type II** pneumocytes secrete **surfactant** to reduce **surface tension** inside the alveoli ($\checkmark$). **Numerous organelles** are found inside the type II cells to produce and store **surfactant** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    ***Passive transport*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ small molecules **OR** gases can move through the membrane **OR** pass across the bilayer by (simple) diffusion [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nonpolar **OR** lipid-soluble substances move through the membrane **OR** phospholipid bilayer by (simple) diffusion    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ water molecules can pass across the membrane **OR** bilayer by osmosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ channel proteins allow facilitated diffusion of ions **OR** polar molecules **OR** water molecules **OR** water-soluble molecules **OR** named example of type of channel protein and molecules that pass through it        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ gated proteins **OR** voltage-gated channels **OR** ligand-gated channels **OR** other suitable example control movement of substances due to changes in environment **OR** by specific signals    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ diffusion is the net movement of molecules down their concentration gradient **OR** from a higher concentration to a lower concentration, without use of energy **OR** is a passive process    [1 mark]

    ***Active transport*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ protein pumps **OR** carrier proteins are involved in active transport of ions (but not channel proteins )        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ active transport of substances against a concentration gradient, which requires energy **OR** ATP                  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ indirect active transport **OR** secondary active transport when the movement of one substance against its concentration gradient is coupled to the movement of another substance down its concentration gradient         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ endocytosis is active transport of substances into cell by formation of vesicle from cell surface membrane                [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ phagocytosis is endocytosis of solid particles **AND** pinocytosis is endocytosis of dissolved substances **OR** liquid      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ exocytosis is the release of substances from the cell via vesicles fusing with the cell surface membrane                     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ different types of cells have different proteins in their cell membrane for transport of different substances                [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Nonpolar** ($\checkmark$) and **small molecules**, such as **gases**, can pass across the phospholipid bilayer by **simple diffusion** ($\checkmark$). **On** the other hand, polar molecules and ions tend to pass through **channel proteins** by **facilitated diffusion** ($\checkmark$). These **channel proteins** may be **gated** to control when these substances pass through them, depending on the **environment** ($\checkmark$). Whilst **diffusion** is a **passive process**, with the molecules passing **down** their **concentration gradient** ($\checkmark$), some molecules need to be moved **against** their **concentration gradient** by **active transport**, which requires **energy** ($\checkmark$). **Protein pumps** or **carrier proteins** embedded in the membrane are required for **active transport** ($\checkmark$). Molecules may also **enter** the cell via **vesicles** pinched off from the cell membrane, a process known as **endocytosis** ($\checkmark$). **Exocytosis** allows the **secretion** of substances **from cells**, when **vesicles** containing the substance **fuse with the cell membrane** and release their contents to the outside ($\checkmark$).
