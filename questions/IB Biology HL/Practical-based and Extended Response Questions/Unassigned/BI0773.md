1. any **one** of the following

 $\ce{ -> }$ charge **AND** size [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 DNA fragments are separated by their **charge** and **size** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA sample is obtained from child, alleged biological father and mother [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is extracted from the sample **OR** separated from other cellular components [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sequences that vary between individuals **OR** VNTRs **OR** variable number of tandem repeats **OR** STRs **OR** short tandem repeats are selected [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is amplified **OR** copied using PCR [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ DNA is fragmented using restriction endonucleases **OR** enzymes [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ fragments are separated by charge and size **OR** using gel electrophoresis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ stain the DNA fragments with a dye to make visible [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the gel is observed under UV light to see the DNA bands [1 mark]
 [3 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 To create a DNA profile, a **DNA sample is obtained** from the child, alleged biological father and mother ($\checkmark$), and analysed. **Sequences that vary** a lot between individuals such as **VNTRs** ($\checkmark$) are selected and amplified using **PCR** ($\checkmark$). The DNA is then split into **fragments** using **restriction endonucleases** ($\checkmark$) and the fragments are separated by size and charge, using **gel electrophoresis** ($\checkmark$).

6. any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ half of the DNA fragments in offspring will be shared with the mother **AND** the rest must come from the father [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ helps to identify any potential errors **OR** contamination of samples by comparing child’s and mother’s DNA markers [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ some genetic markers are common in the general population, which can lead to false positives if child’s DNA is not compared to both mother and alleged father [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ father may have similar fragment pattern to another male relative [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 It is important to analyse maternal DNA as well as paternal DNA, in paternity testing as **half** of the DNA fragments in an offspring’s samples will be the **same as the mother** , which will show which **DNA fragments come from the biological father**($\checkmark$).
