2. any **five** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ light is absorbed by chlorophyll **OR** pigments arranged in photosystems in the thylakoid membrane [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ absorbed energy excites electrons, which are emitted from the reaction centre of photosystem II [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ excited electrons pass along an electron transport chain **OR** chain of electron carriers [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ energy from the electrons is used to pump protons (H:sup[+]) into the thylakoid space [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ protons (H:sup[+]) diffuse down their concentration gradient through ATP synthase, driving ATP synthesis by chemiosmosis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ photolysis of water produces electrons that replace those lost from photosystem II, together with oxygen **AND** H:sup[+] [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ electrons are re-excited at photosystem I and used to reduce NADP **OR** produce reduced NADP [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ ATP and reduced NADP are used in the Calvin cycle **OR** light-independent reactions to convert glycerate 3-phosphate into triose phosphate (accept to produce triose phosphate **OR** carbohydrate) [1 mark]
[5 marks]

 :u[Sample answer]:

 **Light** is **absorbed** by **pigments** in **photosystem II** in the **thylakoid membrane**, **exciting electrons** ($\checkmark$). The excited electrons pass along an **electron transport chain** ($\checkmark$), **releasing energy** that **pumps protons** into the **thylakoid space** ($\checkmark$). The **protons diffuse** back through **ATP synthase**, producing **ATP** by **chemiosmosis** ($\checkmark$). **Electrons lost** from photosystem II are **replaced** by the **photolysis** of **water**, which releases **oxygen** ($\checkmark$). At **photosystem I**, electrons are **re-excited** and used to **reduce NADP** ($\checkmark$). **ATP** and **reduced NADP** are then used in the **Calvin cycle** to convert **glycerate 3-phosphate** into **triose phosphate**, which is used to synthesise **carbohydrates** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ select a range of five different concentrations of sodium hydrogen carbonate/sodium bicarbonate solution/other suitable source of carbon dioxide, including control with no added sodium hydrogen carbonate/no added source of carbon dioxide **OR** prepare 0.000 M, 0.005 M, 0.010 M, 0.015 M and 0.020 M sodium hydrogen carbonate/any acceptable range with consistent intervals provided [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ use aquatic plants/*Elodea*/pondweed **OR** land plants [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ for aquatic plants, submerge the plant in water with the cut end of the stem facing upwards [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ for land-based plants, measure oxygen gas production using a gas sensor **OR** for aquatic plants, count the number of bubbles/water displacement [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ use boiled and cooled water to reduce dissolved carbon dioxide **OR** use a tray with sodium hydroxide to absorb CO:sub[2] from the air in terrestrial plants set-up [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ for land plants, enclose the plant in a sealed chamber and vary CO:sub[2] by adding CO:sub[2] gas **OR** absorbing CO:sub[2] with soda lime/sodium hydroxide, checking concentrations with a CO:sub[2] sensor [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ multiple trials to obtain reliable results **OR** five trials for each concentration [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ use a thermometer to check for temperature changes **OR** place the beaker/boiling tube containing aquatic plants in water bath [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ place light source at a constant distance from the plant [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ description of appropriate controlled variable, e.g. plant size in different trials and experimental groups is the same [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ rate of photosynthesis can be calculated as oxygen gas percentage over time **OR** number of bubbles over time **OR** water displacement over time [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ carbon dioxide concentration is the independent variable **AND** rate of oxygen production is the dependent variable [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allow the plant to acclimatise for a few minutes at each new concentration before measuring [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ collect the gas in a gas syringe **OR** photosynthometer to measure volume, as bubble size varies [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ check pH **OR** use a buffer, as sodium hydrogen carbonate can change the pH of the water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ plot a graph of rate of photosynthesis against carbon dioxide concentration [1 mark]
[7 marks]

 :u[Sample answer]:

 Select **five** different **concentrations** of carbon dioxide (0.000 M to 0.020 M sodium hydrogen carbonate) ($\checkmark$). For the control with no added sodium hydrogen carbonate, use **boiled** and then **cooled water** to **reduce dissolved carbon dioxide** ($\checkmark$). Use **pondweed** ($\checkmark$) and **submerge** it in the **water**, with the **cut end** of the **stem pointing upwards** ($\checkmark$). The **oxygen** produced can be **collected** in a small **gas syringe** ($\checkmark$). The **rate of photosynthesis** can then be calculated by **dividing** the **volume** collected **by** the **time** taken ($\checkmark$). **Repeat** the experiment **five times** at **each concentration** and **calculate** a **mean rate** ($\checkmark$).

 The **other limiting factors**, temperature and light intensity, must be **controlled**. **Temperature** can be controlled by placing the beaker containing the plant in a **thermostatically controlled water bath** ($\checkmark$), and **light intensity** by placing the **light source** at a **fixed distance** ($\checkmark$).
