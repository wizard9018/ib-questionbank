# BI1096

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe how a mutation may be a silent mutation. [4 marks]

2. Outline five causes of gene mutations.    [5 marks]

3. Compare and contrast somatic and germ cell mutations including their biological implications [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ silent mutation doesn't alter the amino acid sequence of the polypeptide chain encoded by the gene [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ brought about by a point mutation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ occurs because of degenerate OR redundant genetic code  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ multiple codons can code for the same amino acid [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ change in nucleotide may not affect amino acid it codes for [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ protein's structure OR function may remain unchanged [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    A **silent mutation** is a type of point mutation that **doesn't alter** the **amino acid sequence** of the protein encoded by the gene ($\checkmark$). This occurs because of the **degenerate genetic code** ($\checkmark$), where **multiple codons** can code for the **same amino acid** ($\checkmark$). Therefore, a **change** in the third position of a codon can result in a different **nucleotide without changing** the **amino acid** it codes for ($\checkmark$). As a result, the **protein's structure** and function remain **unchanged** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ during DNA replication, mistakes can occur [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ DNA polymerase may insert the wrong nucleotide, **OR** DNA polymerase may slip during replication, resulting in the addition or deletion of nucleotides [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ errors in the cellular machinery responsible for correcting DNA mismatches during replication can result in mutations [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ spontaneous loss of amino groups **OR** deamination from bases can lead to the conversion of one base to another [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reactive oxygen species generated during normal cellular processes can damage DNA bases [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemical mutagens **OR** suitable example can directly alter DNA bases [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ radiation **OR** ionising radiation **OR** X-rays **OR** gamma rays **OR** ultraviolet **OR** UV radiation can damage DNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ biological agents **OR** some viruses can integrate their genetic material into the host DNA, potentially disrupting or altering host genes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ exposures to environmental toxins **OR** pollutants **OR** industrial chemicals **OR** carcinogens [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transposons **OR**  "jumping genes," **OR** DNA sequences that can move around within the genome can disrupt genes  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mechanical **OR** physical damage to DNA **OR** breaks **OR** lesions caused by mechanical forces can lead to mutations if not repaired correctly [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ errors in the cellular machinery responsible for correcting DNA mismatches during replication can result in mutations [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Causes of mutations can be split into spontaneous and induced mutations. An example of a spontaneous mutation is an **error during DNA replication**, such as slipping of DNA polymerase leading to the **insertion or deletion** of nucleotides ($\checkmark$). Then, there can be **errors** in the cellular machinery responsible for **correcting DNA mismatches** during replication, which can also result in mutations ($\checkmark$).  **Deamination of bases** can occur, which leads to the change of one base into another ($\checkmark$). On the other hand, induced mutations result from external factors, including exposure to **chemical mutagens** like ethidium bromide ($\checkmark$) and **radiation** sources such as **ionising** or **UV** radiation ($\checkmark$), which can damage DNA directly. Additionally, **biological agents** like certain viruses can integrate their genetic material into host DNA, potentially disrupting genes ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    **similarities** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** arise from changes in the DNA sequence **OR** involve substitutions **OR** insertions **OR** deletions **OR** rearrangements of nucleotides [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can lead to changes in the encoded proteins or RNA molecules [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can have biological effects [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** can be influenced by environmental factors [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** have DNA repair mechanisms that attempt to correct mutations [1 mark]

    **differences** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ somatic cell mutation occurs in body cells **WHEREAS** germ cell mutation occurs in reproductive organs [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mutations in somatic cells are not passed on to offspring **OR** do not affect the genetic makeup of future generations **OR** mutations in somatic cells can lead to health issues **OR** diseases in the affected individual **WHEREAS** mutations in germ cells can be inherited by offspring **OR** can be passed onto next generation **OR** mutations in germ cells directly affect offspring **OR** next generation [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ somatic mutations are relatively common **OR** can occur throughout an individual's life **OR** readily exposed to mutagens **WHEREAS** germ cell mutations are relatively rare in comparison **OR** are isolated in the reproductive organs **OR** are not as exposed to the same mutagenic factors as somatic cells [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ somatic cells have more DNA repair mechanisms **OR** can correct more types of mutations **OR** trigger cell death to prevent the spread of mutated cells **WHEREAS** germ cells have fewer DNA repair mechanisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ somatic mutations primarily impact the health **OR** functioning of the individual in which they occur **OR** are not directly related to evolutionary changes **WHEREAS** germ cell mutations can contribute to genetic diversity within a population **OR** can drive evolutionary changes  [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Somatic** cell mutations can occur in most of the **cells of the body**, whereas **germ** cell mutation can only occur in **reproductive organs** ($\checkmark$). **Both** somatic and germ cell mutations come about by **changes in the base sequences** of the DNA ($\checkmark$), which can lead to **changes** in the **resulting proteins** ($\checkmark$) and therefore can have **biological effects** in the organisms ($\checkmark$). However, **mutations in somatic** cells are **not passed on to offspring**, they primarily **impact** the **health** of the **individual** in which they occur, whereas mutations in **germ** cells can be **inherited by offspring** ($\checkmark$) and so can **drive evolutionary changes** ($\checkmark$). Mutations in **somatic** cells are **more common** than those in germ cells, as they are more readily exposed to **environmental pollutions**, whereas **germ** cells are relatively **isolated** in the reproductive organs ($\checkmark$); and when mutations do occur **somatic cells** have **more DNA repair mechanisms** than germ cells ($\checkmark$).
