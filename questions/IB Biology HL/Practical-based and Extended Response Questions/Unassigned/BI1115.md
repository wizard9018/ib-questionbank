# BI1115

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Describe the characteristics of the genetic code. [4 marks]

2. Outline, with examples, the importance of complementary base pairing. [5 marks]

3. Explain the roles of  RNA and ribosomes in the formation of a polypeptide chain once mRNA has exited the nucleus. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.    any **four** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic code consists of sequence of three nucleotides **OR** three bases **OR** triplet code **OR** codons on mRNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ 64 **OR** 4$^3$ possible combinations [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ each of the 20 amino acids is coded for by at least one codon [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  more codons exist than there are amino acids to be coded for **OR** most amino acids coded for by more than one codon     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ degenerate code [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ code is conservative **OR** the first two bases of a codon are often more critical than the third in determining the amino acid specified **OR** wobble effect allows some flexibility in third base [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ universal **OR** nearly universal across all organisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ non-overlapping **OR** codons are read sequentially without overlapping [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ specific codons start **OR** stop protein synthesis [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ directional **OR** codons read in a specific direction [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The genetic code consists of **sequences of three nucleotides** ($\checkmark$), known as **codons** which make up mRNA. As there are **64** different **codons** ($\checkmark$) most of the **20 amino acids** are coded for by **more than one** ($\checkmark$); this is known as a **degenerate code** ($\checkmark$). The code includes **specific start** and **stop** codons ($\checkmark$) that initiate and terminate translation respectively. Additionally, the genetic code is **nearly universal** applying across **almost all living organisms** ($\checkmark$).

2.    any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$  allows for semi-conservative replication [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in DNA replication, each parent DNA strand acts as a template for a new one so new DNA molecules produced are identical to the parent DNA strands [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ adenine pairs with thymine **OR** uracil and cytosine pairs with guanine **OR** A pairs with T **OR** U  and C with G [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  to provide structural stability to the DNA molecule    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ complementary bases pair up through hydrogen bonds **OR** A forms two hydrogen bonds with T, and C forms three hydrogen bonds with G  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ to synthesize complementary RNA from a DNA template [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  in transcription **OR** formation of tRNA OR formation of rRNA **OR** formation of mRNA, complementary base pairing between free RNA nucleotides and the DNA strand forms RNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ U OR uracil is found in RNA instead of  T **OR** thymine [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ enables synthesis of polypeptides OR proteins from mRNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ensures accuracy **OR** less or no mistakes **OR** errors [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$  in translation, mRNA codons are complementary to anticodons of tRNA, which carry amino acids **OR** enabling correct amino acids to be brought into place    [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
    Complementary base pairing between nitrogenous bases of nucleotides occurs between **cytosine and guanine**, and between **adenine and thymine** (in DNA) or **uracil** (in RNA) ($\checkmark$). This allows for the **semi-conservative replication** of  DNA ($\checkmark$). Here **parental strands** of DNA act as **templates** for the new strands to be formed, so that new DNA molecules are **identical to the parental** ones ($\checkmark$). It is also important for the **synthesis of RNA** and **proteins** ($\checkmark$). This can be seen in **transcription**, where bases of the **DNA** molecule serve as a template for the complementary pairing of bases in **RNA** ($\checkmark$) and in **translation** where **mRNA codons pair** up with the **anticodons** found on tRNA ($\checkmark$) molecules which carry the amino acids.

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA **OR** messenger RNA serves as a template for the synthesis of polypeptides **OR** for translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA **OR** messenger RNA carries the code from the DNA in the nucleus to the ribosomes in the cytoplasm [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rRNA **OR** ribosomal RNA, along with ribosomal proteins, forms ribosomes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tRNA **OR** transfer RNA carries the correct amino acid to the correct codon on the mRNA
    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA binds with  small subunit of ribosome [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosome binds at 5’ end of mRNA [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tRNA carrying correct amino acid, binds to large subunit [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ anticodon to codon on mRNA **OR** through complementary base pairing **OR** through G to C and U to A [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ second, tRNA brings in next amino acids and attaches to large subunit [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ peptide bond forms between adjacent amino acids **OR** condensation reactions form bond between the amino acids **OR** enzyme catalyses formation of covalent bond between amino acids [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ first tRNA then released to collect its next amino acids    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosome moves along mRNA to help bring in next tRNA     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ process repeats until stop codon **OR** until polypeptide complete     [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
   The **mRNA** carries the **genetic code** for the formation of the polypeptide from the DNA **into the cytoplasm** ($\checkmark$) to act as a **template for transcription** ($\checkmark$). The **mRNA binds** with the **small unit** of the **ribosome** ($\checkmark$). The **first tRNA**, carrying the corresponding amino acid, then **binds** to the **large subunit** of the ribosome ($\checkmark$), bringing the **correct amino acid** into the correct place along the messenger RNA, due to **complementary base pairing** between the **anticodon** on the tRNA and **codon** ($\checkmark$)on the mRNA. A **second tRNA**, also **binds** to the **large subunit** of the ribosome and brings in the **next amino acid** ($\checkmark$). A strong **covalent peptide bond** forms between the **two amino acids** ($\checkmark$), and the first tRNA is released to collect its next amino acid. This **process continues until** a **stop codon** on the mRNA is reached ($\checkmark$), and then the polypeptide is released.
