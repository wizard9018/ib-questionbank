# BI1079

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Explain why a resting potential is negative.    [3 marks]

2. Explain how the structure of a neuron is adapted to its function. [5 marks]

3. Describe the events that occur to propagate an action potential within a neuron and between two neurons.        [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ sodium-potassium pumps move 3 Na$^+$ ions out of the cell and 2 K$^+$ ions into the cell     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ membrane is more permeable to  K$^+$ ions, so  K$^+$ ions leak back out of the cell    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there are negative ions **OR** anions, **OR** substances carrying negative charge,  inside the cell than outside    [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    A resting potential is negative because **sodium-potassium pumps** in the membrane pump **3 Na$^+$** ions **out** of the cell for every **2 K$^+$** ions that are pumped **in** ($\checkmark$), making the outside of the cell more positive and the inside less positive. Additionally, the membrane is **more permeable to  K$^+$ ions**, so  K$^+$ ions **leak back out** of the cell ($\checkmark$), adding further to the charge imbalance. Finally, there is higher concentration of **negative charge**, such as Cl$^-$ ions, **inside the cell** ($\checkmark$), which make the inside of the cell more negative.

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ long axon fibre to carry electrical impulses over relatively long distances in the body    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ many branched dendrites to receive **OR** send electrical impulses from many neurons    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ myelination **OR** Schwann cells provide insulation so that electrical impulses are conducted faster    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ saltatory conduction **OR** nodes of Ranvier allow impulses to jump from node to node so that the impulse is transported faster        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ contain many mitochondria which provide energy to construct neurotransmitters    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cell body processes and integrates incoming signals from dendrites before determining whether to generate an action potential     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at the end of the axon, terminal branches form synapses with other neurons **OR** target cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the presence of synaptic vesicles within axon terminals enables the storage and release of neurotransmitters, facilitating efficient communication        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neurons possess a variety of ion channels, including voltage-gated channels, which regulate the movement of ions across the cell membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ postsynaptic membrane contains receptors that specifically bind to neurotransmitters released by other neurons        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neurons can undergo structural and functional changes **OR** neuronal plasticity in response to experience or learning        [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The structure of a neuron is adapted to its function by having **long** axon fibres to carry electrical impulses over relatively **long distances in the body** ($\checkmark$). Neurons have many-branched **dendrites** to receive multiple signals from **other neurons** ($\checkmark$). In the axons **myelination** provides **insulation** so that electrical impulses are conducted **faster** ($\checkmark$).  Inbetween the myelin sheaths there are myelin-free gaps called **nodes of Ranvier** which allow action potential to move **faster** ($\checkmark$) by. **jumping from node to node**.

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ stimulus, such as change in membrane voltage or neurotransmitter binding to a receptor, triggers the opening of sodium channels in the axon’s initial segment    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ leads to depolarising of membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if change in potential reaches threshold potential **OR** -55 to -50mV, it triggers the opening of voltage-gated sodium channels    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rapid influx of sodium ions, causing action potential        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ positive feedback loop **OR** greater change in potential results in more sodium voltage-gated channels opening        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ after reaching peak positive potential **OR** +40 mV, voltage-gated sodium channels start to close, and voltage-gated potassium channels open    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ potassium ions move out of the cell, repolarising the membrane and bringing it back towards the resting potential    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hyperpolarisation **OR** refractory period prevents further action potential in that region until sodium-potassium pump restores the resting membrane potential and ionic balance    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ action potential in one region means sodium ions diffuse along inside of axon to neighbouring regions, which changes membrane potential in this region    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ causing this region to surpass the threshold potential        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ action potential cannot go backwards, despite diffusion of sodium ions as sodium voltage-gated channels can’t open during absolute refractory period        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ action potential reaching the presynaptic knob, leading to the opening of voltage-gated calcium channels        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ influx of  Ca:sup[2+] ions causes vesicles containing neurotransmitter to fuse with the presynaptic membrane        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neurotransmitter is released into the synapse by exocytosis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ neurotransmitter diffuses across the synapse **AND** binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ triggers the opening of sodium channels in the postsynaptic membrane    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if postsynaptic potential reaches threshold potential (-55 mV to -50 mV), an action potential is generated in the next neuron [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Local currents control the propagation of an action potential within a neuron. This occurs when **sodium channels open**, letting Na:sup[+] ions into the cell ($\checkmark$), which raises the membrane potential so that it meets the **threshold potential** ($\checkmark$), this triggers an **action potential** ($\checkmark$). However, due to the presence of a higher concentration of Na:sup[+] ions within the axon, **Na:sup[+] ions diffuse inside** the axon to the next region of the axon, which is at resting potential ($\checkmark$), this causes the next region to depolarise and **reach the threshold potential** ($\checkmark$), so that the voltage-gated **sodium channels open** ($\checkmark$), and the process repeats. Once the wave of **depolarisation** reaches the presynaptic membrane, it **causes the voltage-gated calcium channels** in the presynaptic knob to **open** ($\checkmark$). **Calcium ions** therefore **diffuse into the cell**, which triggers **vesicles containing neurotransmitters** to **fuse with** the **presynaptic membrane** ($\checkmark$).  The neurotransmitters **diffuse** across the **synapse and bind to receptors** on the postsynaptic membrane ($\checkmark$), which triggers sodium channels to open, and the process begins again.
