# BI1418

**Maximum mark: 16**

Genetic variation is the root of phenotypic diversity between and within species.

1. Phenotypic expression relies on the genome and proteome. Outline the differences between the genome and the proteome    [4 marks]

2. While genetic variations can be beneficial, others can lead to detrimental health conditions, such as diabetes. Compare and contrast Type I and Type II diabetes.    [4 marks]

3. Describe the approaches used to classify different species.    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **genome** is the **complete set of genetic material** present in an organism, including **all** the **genes, regulatory elements** and **non-coding regions**, whereas the **proteome** is the **entire set** of **proteins** produced by a **cell, tissue,** or **organism** at a specific time under defined conditions ($\checkmark$). The **genome** is made up of **nucleotides**, whereas the **proteome** is composed of **amino acids** ($\checkmark$). Functionally, the **genome** acts as a source of genetic **instructions**, providing the information necessary for development and regulation, while the **proteome** actively **participates** in **cellular processes** ($\checkmark$) based on these instructions. The **genome** is relatively **stable** with occasional genetic variations, whereas the **proteome** is highly **dynamic**, responding to environmental factors ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **four** of the following (**AT LEAST ONE** similarity and **AT LEAST ONE** difference must be given):

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** Types of diabetes affect blood glucose concentration **OR** affect the body's ability to control blood glucose concentration        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** types of diabetes cause health complications if blood glucose concentration is not controlled                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I is an autoimmune disease **WHEREAS** Type II is linked to genetics **OR** lifestyle **OR** ageing [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I diabetes is caused when the beta cells are destroyed by the immune system and do not secrete enough insulin **WHEREAS** Type II diabetes is caused when body cells become insensitive **OR** resistant to insulin    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I diabetes usually begins in childhood **WHEREAS** Type II diabetes usually begins in adulthood [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I diabetes cannot be controlled by lifestyle changes **WHEREAS** Type II diabetes can often be controlled by lifestyle changes            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I diabetes requires frequent measurement of blood glucose concentration **WHEREAS** Type II diabetes does not require frequent measurement of blood glucose concentration                    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I diabetes requires insulin injections from start **WHEREAS** Type II diabetes is only treated with insulin if the disease progresses        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Type I tends to be diagnosed at early age **WHEREAS** Type II tends to be diagnosed in older people                                [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Both** Type I and Type II diabetes **disrupt** the **regulation** of **normal blood glucose concentration** ($\checkmark$). **Type I diabetes** is an **autoimmune disease** where the **immune system destroys beta cells in the pancreas**, resulting in a **lack of insulin** production. The causes of **Type II diabetes** are **multifactorial**, including **genetics, lifestyle** and **ageing** ($\checkmark$), and it manifests when body **cells** become **resistant to insulin** ($\checkmark$). **Type I diabetes** requires **frequent measurement of blood glucose** concentration, whereas **Type II diabetes** does **not usually require** frequent measurements of blood glucose concentration ($\checkmark$). **Type I** diabetes requires lifelong **insulin injections**, whereas **Type II** diabetes only requires **insulin** injections if the **condition progresses** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ classification is used to organise organisms into groups             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ binomial nomenclature **OR** binomial system used to name organisms **OR** name is genus and species      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows communication and understanding between scientists all around the world        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there are three domains of life                     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ a species is the smallest unit of classification    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ hierarchy is used to group species **OR** the taxa are arranged in order of decreasing group size: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ morphological **OR** artificial classification is based on physical characteristics        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ morphology can lead to errors **OR** analogous structures can mislead classification  [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ biochemical classification analyses molecular components **OR** proteins **OR** DNA **OR** RNA  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ base sequences in nucleic acids **OR** amino acid sequences of proteins are analysed     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cladistics is based on shared characteristics traced to most recent common ancestor **OR** cladogram is a visual representation of how species are evolutionarily related, with a node representing a common ancestor        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ modern approach combines evidence **OR** uses all types of classification **OR** uses morphology, biochemistry and genetics      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ natural classification groups organisms based on evolutionary history    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ classification includes fossils        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organisms with a recent common ancestor are classified more closely together    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species in the same genus share many characteristics          [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organisms can be reclassified when new evidence is found      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ natural classification allows scientists to predict characteristics of species within a group  [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Classification is the process of **organising organisms** into appropriate categories or **groups** based on their **shared characteristics** ($\checkmark$). Scientists use **binomial nomenclature** to assign **each organism** a unique two-part name made up of the **genus** and **species** ($\checkmark$), facilitating **global scientific communication** ($\checkmark$). Organisms are traditionally classified **hierarchically**, starting with the broadest category, the **domain**, then working downwards -  **kingdom, phylum, class, order, family genus, species** ($\checkmark$) (which can then be split into sub-species). **Traditional, artificial**, classification has been based on **physical traits. However**, this **morphological** approach ($\checkmark$) can be **misleading** due to **analogous structures** ($\checkmark$). **Modern** classification integrates **morphological, biochemica**l, and **genetic** information, ($\checkmark$). **Biochemical** classification examines the **molecular components** of organisms, including the **sequences** of **bases** of  **DNA** and **amino acids** of **proteins** ($\checkmark$). **Cladistics** is based on **shared characteristics** traced to the **most recent common ancestor**, and these relationships can be represented visually on **cladograms** ($\checkmark$). As **new data emerges, reclassification** may occur ($\checkmark$).
