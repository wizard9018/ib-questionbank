# BI1319

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast transcription and translation between eukaryotes and prokaryotes.[5 marks]

2. Describe how vesicles link translation and exocytosis.    [3 marks]

3. Explain how errors in transcription may result in altered enzyme function.    [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to achieve full marks*):

    ***Similarities*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ ribosomes are the site of translation in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ translation occurs in the cytoplasm  in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mRNA is synthesised in the 5’ to 3’ direction during transcription in  **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the mRNA code is read in the 5’ to 3’ direction in  **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** prokaryotes and eukaryotes have promoter regions **OR** sequences which enzymes **OR** transcription factors can bind to initiate **OR** enhance **OR** repress transcription    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ RNA polymerase is involved in transcription in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transcription involves the use of a DNA template to synthesise RNA in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ translation involves the use of an RNA template **OR** mRNA to synthesise protein in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the base pairing rules for DNA and RNA are the same **OR** Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) in DNA **OR** Uracil (U) in RNA, while Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G)  in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there are specific stop signals that mark the end of transcription and translation in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** prokaryotes and eukaryotes use transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules in translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the same codons typically code for the same amino acids in **BOTH** eukaryotes and prokaryotes            [1 mark]

    ***Differences*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ transcription and translation are coupled **OR** occur simultaneously in prokaryotes **WHEREAS** in eukaryotes transcription occurs before translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in eukaryotes transcription occurs in the nucleus **AND** translation occurs in the cytoplasm **WHEREAS** in prokaryotes **BOTH** processes occur in the cytoplasm    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nucleosomes are present in eukaryotic DNA and help to regulate transcription **BUT** are absent from prokaryotic DNA    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$eukaryotic mRNA undergoes post-transcriptional modification **OR** splicing **OR** addition of a poly-A tail **OR** addition of a 3’ cap **WHEREAS** prokaryotic DNA does not **OR** eukaryotes have introns **WHEREAS** prokaryotes do not        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ 70S ribosomes carry out translation in prokaryotes **WHEREAS** 80S ribosomes carry out translation in eukaryotes                    [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Ribosomes** are the site of **translation** in both eukaryotes and prokaryotes ($\checkmark$), however, **70S ribosomes** carry out translation in prokaryotes, and **80S ribosomes** carry out translation in eukaryotes ($\checkmark$). During transcription, **mRNA is synthesised** in the **5’ to 3’** direction in both eukaryotes and prokaryotes ($\checkmark$), and both involve **RNA polymerase** ($\checkmark$). However, in eukaryotes, the mRNA undergoes **post-transcriptional modification**, whereas this does not occur in prokaryotes ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ polypeptides are translated by ribosomes on the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) **OR** after translation, polypeptide chains are folded into proteins **OR** modified inside RER    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles containing polypeptides bud off the RER **AND** are transported to the Golgi apparatus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ polypeptides are processed into their final form in the Golgi apparatus    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles bud off the Golgi apparatus        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane of the cell     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ releasing proteins in the process of exocytosis    [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Exocytosis is the process by which **vesicles** (fluid-filled membrane sacs) within cells move towards the **plasma membrane** of the cell and **fuse** with it ($\checkmark$), which allows them to **release their contents** ($\checkmark$). This process can be linked to translation, as polypeptides are **translated** by ribosomes on the **RER** ($\checkmark$), and then transferred in **vesicles** to the **Golgi** apparatus where they are **processed**  ($\checkmark$) and then are transferred via vesicles to where they are needed outside the cell, via **exocytosis** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ a substitution mutation **OR** the incorrect RNA nucleotide is incorporated into the growing mRNA chain            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ an extra RNA nucleotide **OR** base is incorporated **OR** inserted **OR** removed **OR** deleted from the growing mRNA chain    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ may result in incorrect splicing of pre-mRNA     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ may result in translation **OR** production of a longer **OR** shorter polypeptide chain    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sequence of amino acids in the polypeptide chain can only be altered if the mutation occurs in the coding region of the gene **OR** exon    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ genetic code dictates the correspondence between codons and specific amino acids [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ may result in the wrong amino acid being incorporated into the polypeptide chain **OR** wrong primary structure during translation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ leads to incorrect folding of the tertiary structure of the polypeptide chain        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as the incorrect amino acid may have an R-group that interacts differently with the rest of the chain            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the incorrect polypeptide chain is incorporated into the tertiary **OR** quaternary structure of the globular enzyme structure        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ results in an altered active site shape **OR** altered allosteric sites **OR** loss of active site [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ therefore substrate is unable to bind **OR** loss of catalytic activity        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ results in potential loss of stability in enzyme structure **OR** enzyme is more easily affected by changes in the environment **OR** enzyme denatures more easily        [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Transcription is the process by which an RNA copy of DNA is made. Errors in transcription can result in the **incorrect RNA** ($\checkmark$) nucleotide, or an **extra RNA** nucleotide being incorporated into the growing mRNA chain ($\checkmark$). This may result in **incorrect splicing** of the **pre-mRNA** molecule ($\checkmark$) before it leaves the nucleus, which can result in the translation of a **truncated polypeptide** chain ($\checkmark$) or the **wrong amino acid** being incorporated into the polypeptide chain ($\checkmark$). This can result in the incorrect polypeptide chain being incorporated into the **tertiary structure** of the **globular** enzyme structure ($\checkmark$) , which may result in an **altered active site** ($\checkmark$), thereby **preventing** a **substrate** from successfully binding ($\checkmark$).
