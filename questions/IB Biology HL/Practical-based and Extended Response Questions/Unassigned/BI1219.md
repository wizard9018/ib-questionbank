# BI1219

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast anaerobic and aerobic respiration in humans.        [5 marks]

2. Outline the roles of NAD:sup[+] **or** FAD in aerobic respiration.    $^*$ [3 marks]

3. Describe the key biochemical reactions that occur in the mitochondrial matrix during aerobic respiration, excluding the roles of NAD and FAD.            [8 marks]

$^*$ *Note: Starting May 2028, this question may be assessed differently due to changes to the Biology guide.*

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **five** of the following: \
*Answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to achieve full marks*

    **Similarities** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** produce ATP    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** use glucose as a substrate **OR** reactant    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** involve glycolysis **OR** the oxidation of glucose **OR** involve the breakdown of one glucose molecule into 2 pyruvates        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** are multi-step processes [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** use enzymes     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** use NAD as a coenzyme **OR** electron carrier            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** initially take place in the cytoplasm    [1 mark]

    **Differences** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ aerobic respiration requires oxygen **WHEREAS** anaerobic respiration does not    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ aerobic respiration produces 36-38 ATPs **OR** a high number of ATPs **WHEREAS** anaerobic respiration produces 2 ATPs **OR** a low number of ATPs    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the products of aerobic respiration are carbon dioxide and water, **WHEREAS** the product of anaerobic respiration is lactate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mitochondria are required for aerobic respiration **WHEREAS** they are not for anaerobic respiration        [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Anaerobic and aerobic respiration are similar in that they **both** produce **ATP** ($\checkmark$), they both use **glucose** as a **substrate** ($\checkmark$), and both use **enzymes** ($\checkmark$) to catalyse many reactions. However, aerobic respiration **requires oxygen**, whereas anaerobic respiration **does not** ($\checkmark$), and aerobic respiration produces **36 ATPs**, whereas anaerobic respiration only produces **2 ATPs** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **three** of the following:

    ***NAD***

    $\ce{ -> }$ NAD:sup[+] acts as electron carrier between metabolic pathways [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ involved  in glycolysis **OR** link reaction **OR** Krebs cycle [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced to NADH by accepting electrons and hydrogen ions so oxidation reactions can take place [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced NADH donates electrons to electron transport chain **OR** ETC where it is oxidised back to NAD:sup[+] [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ NADH contributes to the formation of ATP **OR** oxidative phosphorylation by generating the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxidised NAD:sup[+] is used repeatedly in earlier stages **OR** glycolysis **OR**  link reaction **OR** Krebs cycle to accept electrons and continue cellular respiration [1 mark]

    ***FAD***

    $\ce{ -> }$ FAD is involved in the Krebs cycle    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reduced to FADH:sub[2] by accepting electrons and hydrogen ions, so oxidation reactions can take place[1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ FADH:sub[2] donates electrons to electron transport chain **OR** ETC, where it is oxidised back to FAD [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ FADH:sub[2] contributes to the formation of ATP **OR** oxidative phosphorylation by generating the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ oxidised FAD is regenerated and used in the Krebs cycle to continue metabolic reactions    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
   **NAD:sup[+]** is involved in **glycolysis**, the **link reaction**, and the **Krebs cycle** ($\checkmark$), where it plays a crucial role in **oxidation reactions** by **accepting electrons** and **hydrogen ions**, **reducing** to **NADH** ($\checkmark$). The reduced NADH then **donates electrons** to the **electron transport chain (ETC)**, where it is **oxidised** back to **NAD:sup[+]** ($\checkmark$), providing **electrons** that help **generate** the **proton gradient** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ link reaction **AND** Krebs cycle    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as pyruvate enters matrix it is oxidised **AND** decarboxylated        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ an acetyl group **OR** acetate is formed        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the acetyl group **OR** acetate is combined with coenzyme A **OR** coA, forming acetyl-coenzyme A    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ acetyl-coenzyme A combines with a four-carbon compound **OR** oxaloacetate in the Krebs cycle    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ forming a six-carbon compound **OR** citrate         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ releasing coenzyme A **OR** coA to be reused in the link reaction        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the six-carbon compound **OR** citrate is decarboxylated **AND** oxidised into a five-carbon compound **OR** α-ketoglutarate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the five-carbon compound **OR** α-ketoglutarate is decarboxylated **AND** oxidised into a four-carbon compound **OR**  succinate    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ through additional steps, oxaloacetate **OR** original four-carbon compound is regenerated        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ one molecule of ATP is generated per turn **OR** two per glucose molecule via substrate-level phosphorylation             [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The two reactions that occur in the mitochondrial matrix as a part of aerobic respiration are the **link reaction** and the **Krebs cycle**($\checkmark$). The **pyruvate** from glycolysis **enters** the **link reaction** and is **oxidised** and **decarboxylated** ($\checkmark$) to  form an **acetyl group** ($\checkmark$). The acetyl group then **combines with coenzyme A** to form **acetyl coenzyme A** ($\checkmark$). **Acetate** from acetyl coenzyme A acts as a **substrate** for the **Krebs cycle**, where it **combines** with the **four-carbon molecule, oxaloacetate** ($\checkmark$), forming **citrate, a six-carbon molecule** ($\checkmark$), This is then **decarboxylated and oxidised** into  **α-ketoglutarate**, a **five-carbon molecule** ($\checkmark$), which is further **oxidised and decarboxylated**, to form **succinate, a four-carbon molecule**  ($\checkmark$), before **oxaloacetate** is **reformed** ($\checkmark$).
