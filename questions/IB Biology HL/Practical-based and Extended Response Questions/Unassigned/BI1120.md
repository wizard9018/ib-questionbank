# BI1120

**Maximum mark: 10**

Flu vaccinations are administered to millions of people every year and are often targeted at vulnerable members of the population, such as the elderly. To ascertain the effectiveness of vaccinations at reducing the spread of viruses, a study was conducted in which researchers investigated the effect of vaccination on the viral load of aerosols from pigs.

In the experiment, pigs were categorised and treated according to the following table.

1.
    1. Suggest one environmental variable which could influence viral transmission between pigs that should be kept constant.     [1 mark]

    2. State two ways in which the vaccination could be administered.     [1 mark]

    3. Describe how the pig's non-specific immune system would recognise the inert viruses present in the vaccine as foreign molecules.     [2 marks]

2.
    1. Suggest two safety precautions that investigators could take to limit their health risks during sample collection.          [1 mark]

    2. Describe an advantage to using pigs instead of humans in this experiment. [1 mark]

3. State the advantage of increasing the number of oral aerosol samples taken to 9.      [1 mark]

4. From the data, discuss whether vaccinating pigs with different strains of virus effectively reduces the transmission of viral particles in aerosols. Justify your answer.          [3 marks]

In conclusion, the treatment is **not effective** because there are more positive samples in the **VAC-HETMU group** compared to the **VAC-HOMO group** ($\checkmark$).
