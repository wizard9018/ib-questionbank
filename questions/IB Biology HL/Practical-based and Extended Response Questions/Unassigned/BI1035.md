# BI1035

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast the biological species concept and the morphological concept.[4 marks]

2. Explain the difficulties with applying the biological species and morphological concept to all organisms.    [5 marks]

3. Describe the similarities and differences within and between species. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** similarity and **one** difference to achieve full marks*):

    ***Similarities***

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** concepts seek to define a species    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** concepts acknowledge that there can be variation within species    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** concepts are connected to the broader concept of evolution    [1 mark]

    ***Differences***

    $\ce{ -> }$ the biological species concept defines a species as a group of organisms which can interbreed to produce fertile offspring, **WHEREAS** the morphological concept defines a species as a group of organisms with shared traits        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ Mayr proposed the biological species concept **WHEREAS** the morphological concept was proposed by Linnaeus        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept was proposed before the biological species concept    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept is applicable to sexual and asexual organisms, **WHEREAS** the biological species concept is applicable only to sexually reproducing organisms     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the biological species concept reflects the evolutionary relationship OR speciation OR gene flow, **WHEREAS** the morphological species concept may not represent genetic relatedness or evolutionary relationships [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Both concepts are used to **define species** ($\checkmark$), but Mayr proposed the biological species concept, whereas the morphological concept was proposed by **Linnaeus** ($\checkmark$). The morphological concept also came **first** ($\checkmark$). The biological species concept defines a species as a group of organisms that can interbreed to produce **fertile offspring**; however, the morphological concept defines a species as a group of organisms with **shared traits** ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept cannot be applied to species that show variation due to reproductive isolation **OR** the morphological concept cannot be applied to species with different forms **OR** polymorphism **OR** named example of a species with different forms     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the morphological species concept does not take into account DNA **OR** genetic information   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the biological species concept is defined in terms of sexual reproduction **HOWEVER**, not all organisms reproduce sexually     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ example of species that reproduce asexually, e.g. budding in yeast **OR** horizontal gene transfer in bacteria                     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the biological species concept relies on the ability to observe and study interbreeding in real-time, which is not possible with extinct species **OR** fossilised organisms              [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some different species can interbreed and produce viable offspring, but these hybrids might have reduced fitness or be sterile, which challenges the clear-cut boundaries proposed by the biological species concept     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ convergent evolution can make it difficult to accurately group organisms based solely on morphological characteristics     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ rapid evolution and morphological changes in response to the environment might make individuals appear distinct, even if they share a recent common ancestor, which challenges the reliability of morphological traits as indicators of species relationships         [1 mark]
    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The biological species concept defines a species as a group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring, however, not all species reproduce **sexually** ($\checkmark$), for example, yeast reproduce asexually by **budding** ($\checkmark$). It also relies on the ability to see species interbreeding, which cannot be done with **extinct** species or **fossils** ($\checkmark$). The morphological concept defines a species as a group of organisms with shared traits, however, due to variation caused by **reproductive isolation** and mutation, not all members of a species **look the same** ($\checkmark$). In addition, **rapid evolution** might make individuals appear distinctly related when in fact, they shared a recent common ancestor. This could make the morphological species concept **less reliable** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **seven** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ species can be defined as a group of organisms that are capable of interbreeding to produce fertile offspring    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species usually share the same number of chromosomes      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species usually share identical karyotypes             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ example of chromosome complement of a species, e.g. humans have 46 chromosomes           [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the chromosome complement of a species can vary due to genetic conditions such as Down syndrome     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the size genome of a species can vary within a species   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the base sequence of the genome can vary within a species     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ due to mutations or single-nucleotide polymorphisms [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ homologous genes **OR** similarities in gene pathways **OR** similarities in the functions of genes can be found between species that share evolutionary origins     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species-specific genes are not found in other closely related species     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ horizontal gene transfer between different species, often through mechanisms like viral infections or symbiotic relationships, leads to the acquisition of genes in the recipient that may not be shared by other members of the species         [1 mark]
    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    According to the biological species concept, a species can be defined as a group of organisms that are capable of interbreeding to produce **fertile offspring** ($\checkmark$). They can produce fertile offspring as a species usually have the **same chromosome number** ($\checkmark$), for example, in humans, this is **46** ($\checkmark$) unless genetic conditions such as **Down syndrome** affect their karyotype ($\checkmark$). The **size** of a species' genome can vary slightly within a species ($\checkmark$), as can the **base sequence** of the genome ($\checkmark$) and these are both due to **mutations** ($\checkmark$).
