# BI1162

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Compare and contrast anaerobic and aerobic respiration in animal cells, in terms of substrates, products and yield of ATP.     [4 marks]

2. Outline the process of negative feedback, referring to either temperature regulation or control of blood glucose concentration, in humans as an example.    [4 marks]

3. Define an ecosystem, and explain how the flow of energy and recycling of nutrients make a terrestrial ecosystem sustainable. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **four** of the following:( at least one from each section)

    ***Similarities*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** use glucose as a substrate for energy release     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ in **BOTH**, pyruvate is the end product of glycolysis    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ **BOTH** processes release energy **OR** produce ATP    [1 mark]

    ***Differences*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ in aerobic respiration, oxygen is required **WHEREAS**  anaerobic respiration occurs in the absence of oxygen        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ aerobic respiration can use substrates other than glucose **OR** can use fatty acids **OR** amino acids **WHEREAS** in anaerobic respiration only substrate is glucose

    $\ce{ -> }$ end products of aerobic respiration are carbon dioxide and water, **WHEREAS** the end product of anaerobic respiration is lactic acid         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ aerobic respiration is more efficient at generating ATP **OR** generates more molecules of ATP per glucose molecule **THAN** anaerobic respiration    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Aerobic** and anaerobic respiration use **glucose** as the **substrate** ($\checkmark$) to **release energy** in the form of **ATP** ($\checkmark$).  In **aerobic** respiration, **oxygen** is required, whereas **anaerobic respiration** occurs in the **absence of oxygen** ($\checkmark$). The **end products of aerobic respiration** in animal cells are **carbon dioxide and water**, whereas the end product of **anaerobic** respiration is **lactic acid** ($\checkmark$). **Aerobic respiration** produces many **more** molecules of **ATP per glucose molecule** than anaerobic respiration ($\checkmark$) (approximately 15 times more).

2. any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ negative feedback is involved in maintaining an equilibrium **OR** involved in homeostasis, since it brings the value back to the original    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ when there is a change from a set value **OR** optimal level, brings about change to return to set value **OR** reduce changes    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ involves receptors detecting change and sending information to control centre **OR** brain    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ control centre **OR** brain sends information to effector **OR** muscle **OR** gland to carry out response [1 mark]

    Any **one** of the following examples (max two marks):

    ***Blood glucose*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ increase in blood glucose detected by the pancreatic **OR** β-cells cells which release insulin, **OR** fall in blood glucose detected by the pancreatic **OR** α-cells which release of glucagon      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ insulin causes glucose to be converted to **OR** stored as glycogen in liver **OR** muscle, so decreasing blood glucose, **OR** glucagon causes glycogen to be broken down to glucose **OR** amino acids to be converted to glucose in liver, so increasing blood glucose [1 mark]

    ***Body temperature*** \
    $\ce{ -> }$ increase **OR** decrease in body temperature signals to hypothalamus in the brain      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (increase in temperature) leads to vasodilation **OR** arterioles supplying blood to the skin dilate **OR** sweat glands increase sweat production (do not allow hairs lie flat as not significant in humans) and so cooling **OR** decrease in body temperature, **OR** (decrease in body temperature) leads to vasoconstriction **OR** arterioles supplying blood to the skin constrict **OR** sweat glands decrease  sweat production (do not allow hairs stand up as not significant in humans) **OR** shivering, to help raise body temperature    [1 mark]
    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    When there is a change from the normal, **negative feedback** mechanisms are a form of **control** that brings the value back to **within a normal range** ($\checkmark$). The **change** is **detected by receptors**, which send **signals** to the **brain** ($\checkmark$) and then signals from the brain to the **effector** to bring about the **response** ($\checkmark$).  In the control of blood glucose concentration, an **increase** in **blood glucose** will be detected by the **β-cells** and lead to the **release of insulin** ($\checkmark$), causing **glucose** to be **converted** to and stored as **glycogen** in **liver** and skeletal **muscle** cells. This then restores the **blood glucose** concentration to **within normal levels** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ an ecosystem is a community interacting with its physical environment     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy from the Sun is used for photosynthesis **OR** light energy is converted to chemical energy by autotrophs **OR** plants        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this initial capture of energy then moves through the trophic levels **OR** from producers to consumers in a food chain            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ only 5 to 10% of the energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy transfer from one level to the next trophic level is not 100% efficient    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy is lost as heat  [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy flow is depicted in a pyramid shape and shows the loss of energy   [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ organisms absorb nutrients from food that they consume        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ decomposers break down organic matter after death, releasing nutrients    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ (saprophytic) bacteria and fungi are decomposers and help release nutrients back to the soil             [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ nutrients in soil are in simpler compounds and are absorbed by plants    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ recycling of nutrients in ecosystems takes place    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ energy needs to be supplied continuously for initial capture by plants    [1 mark]
    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    An **ecosystem** is a **community** of living organisms interacting with its **physical environment** ($\checkmark$). **Energy** from the Sun is used by plants for **photosynthesis** ($\checkmark$) to make organic compounds, and this **energy moves** from **one trophic level** to the **next** ($\checkmark$) when organisms consume plants or other organisms. This **transfer** of energy is not efficient, only about **5 to 10%** is transferred ($\checkmark$) to the next level, much is **lost as heat** ($\checkmark$).

    **Organisms** absorb the **nutrients** from the food they consume ($\checkmark$). After their death, **decomposers break down** the **organic matter** ($\checkmark$), releasing the nutrients. The **nutrients** are in **simpler compounds** and can be **absorbed by plants** ($\checkmark$) from the soil to be used to make further organic compounds in plants.
