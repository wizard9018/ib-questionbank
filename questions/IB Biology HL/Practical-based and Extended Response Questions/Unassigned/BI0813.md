# BI0813

**Maximum mark: 10**

A study has been conducted on the effect of hormonal contraception pollution on the mating calls of the adult male African clawed frog (*Xenopus laevis*). The spectrogram shows the frequency and pitch of clicks (mating call) of control *X.laevis* and those exposed to oestrogen.

![BI0813ai](images/BI0813ai.png)
![BI0813aii](images/BI0813aii.png)
[Source: Adapted from Hoffmann, F., & Kloas, W. (2012, February 15). *Estrogens can disrupt amphibian mating behavior*. PLOS ONE. Retrieved May 13, 2023, from https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0032097.  Copyright under CC BY 4.0]

1. Distinguish between the clicks in the spectrogram between the control *X. laevis* and the *X. laevis* treated with oestrogen in 1 second.            [2 marks]

The graph shows the average duration of the *X. laevis* mating calls when exposed to the hormone oestrogen.

![BI0813b](images/BI0813b.png)
[Source: Adapted from Hoffmann, F., & Kloas, W. (2012, February 15). *Estrogens can disrupt amphibian mating behavior*. PLOS ONE. Retrieved May 13, 2023, from https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0032097.  Copyright under CC BY 4.0]

2. Describe the effect of oestrogen on the duration of the mating call shown in this study.        [2 marks]

3. Explain why it is important to have the *X.laevis* control group.        [1 mark]

4. Identify the independent variable in this study.    [1 mark]

5.
    1. Predict the relative reproductive success of *X.laevis* exposed to 29.6 ngL:sup[-1] of oestrogen.            [1 mark]

    2. Outline the possible consequences of oestrogen exposure on *X. laevis*.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ control *X.laevis* have a more regular click pattern **WHEREAS** *X.laevis* treated with oestrogen have an irregular click pattern        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ control *X.laevis* have a lower pitch of clicks **OR** a pitch of approximately 2.5 kHz **WHEREAS** *X.laevis* have a higher pitch of clicks **OR** a pitch of approximately 3 kHz    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the *X.laevis*  exposed to oestrogen have longer clicks **COMPARED** to the control *X.laevis* **OR** the control *X.laevis* has more frequent clicks **OR** faster clicks than the *X.laevis* exposed to oestrogen        [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

   ![BI0813ci](images/BI0813ci.png)
   ![BI0813cii](images/BI0813cii.png)
    [Source: Adapted from Hoffmann, F., & Kloas, W. (2012, February 15). *Estrogens can disrupt amphibian mating behavior*. PLOS ONE. Retrieved May 13, 2023, from https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0032097.  Copyright under CC BY 4.0]

    The control *X.laevis* have a more **regular click pattern compared** to the *X.laevis* treated with oestrogen, which have an **irregular click pattern** ($\checkmark$). The control *X.laevis* have a **lower pitch** of clicks at approximately **2.5 kHz**, whereas the *X.laevis* that were exposed to oestrogen have a **higher pitch** of approximately **3 kHz** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ 29.6 ngL:sup[-1] of oestrogen **OR** the highest concentration of oestrogen has the shortest mean mating call duration        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the lowest level of oestrogen OR 0.296 ngL:sup[-1] of oestrogen has the longest mean mating call duration    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ at the middle concentration of oestrogen OR at ngL:sup[-1], the mean mating call duration was similar to the control OR only 1s longer than the control        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ as oestrogen levels increase the mean duration of the mating call decreases            [1 mark]
    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The **longest** mean mating **call** of 33s was seen at the **lowest concentration of oestrogen** (0.296 ngL:sup[-1]) ($\checkmark$).  *X.laevis* exposed to an **oestrogen concentration of 2.96 ngL:sup[-1]** had a mean mating call **duration similar** to the **control** ($\checkmark$), just 1s longer.  However, at the **highest concentration of oestrogen** (29.6 ngL:sup[-1]), *X.laevis* had a much **lower** mean mating call **duration**, 4 seconds less than the control ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ to ensure that any effect being seen on the mating call duration **OR** frequency of clicks **OR** pitch of clicks is only due to the effect of hormones **OR** oestrogen    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ to allow for a baseline comparison            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ allows for establishing causality            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ helps to minimise the impact of confounding variables    [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    It is important to have a control group because it helps establish that **any observed changes in mating call duration are due to oestrogen** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ the concentration of oestrogen that *X.laevis* is exposed to [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The independent variable in this study is the **concentration of oestrogen** ($\checkmark$) that the *X.laevis* is exposed to.

5.
    1. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ lower reproductive success than the control **OR** reduced successful matings    [1 mark]
    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The *X.laevis* exposed to oestrogen may have **lower** reproductive success than the control ($\checkmark$).

5.
    2. Any **three** of the following

    $\ce{ -> }$ *X.laevis* exposed to oestrogen have a higher pitch of clicks **OR** longer clicks **OR** a shorter mating call duration, so may not be recognised by female *X.laevis*    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ resulting in reduced mating opportunities and so reduced reproductive success    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ *X.laevis*  exposed to oestrogen may have shorter life spans and die without passing their genes onto the next generation reducing population size and gene pool        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ this can result in the population being more vulnerable to disease **OR** environmental changes **OR** limit the population's adaptive potential [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ if the altered mating call resembles calls of a different species of frog **OR** attracts females from another species, this can result in hybridisation of the species    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ altered calls may disrupt the social dynamics **OR** may reduce ability of male to establish dominance over competitors **OR** defend territory [1 mark]
    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Exposure to oestrogen in  *X.laevis* frogs could result in **reduced reproductive success due to reduced mating opportunities** ($\checkmark$) as a result of females **not recognising the altered mating call** ($\checkmark$). The population size and gene pool may also decrease if oestrogen pollution **reduces the lifespan of the frogs** ($\checkmark$). Altered calls may also reduce the ability of the males to defend their territories against competitors and so may **disrupt the social dynamics** of the population ($\checkmark$).
