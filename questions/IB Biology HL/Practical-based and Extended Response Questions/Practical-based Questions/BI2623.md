# BI2623

**Maximum mark: 10**

Hospital doctors compared the mitotic index of some tumour cells with that of some non-tumour cells retrieved from a patient. They prepared a light microscope slide for each specimen. The doctors diagnosed the patient’s tumour as melanoma, a skin cancer characterised by abnormally shaped, sometimes coloured moles on the skin surface.

![2623 Edit](<images/2623 Edit.png>)

1. Calculate the mitotic index for each sample.        [2 marks]

2. Describe **three** other requirements of the slide and microscope setup that enable the mitotic index to be calculated by light microscopy.         [3 marks]

3. Melanomas have a high probability of giving rise to secondary tumours. State the name of the process by which secondary tumours form.    [1 mark]

4. Suggest how the doctors could have improved the accuracy and reliability of their determination of the mitotic index.   [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ tumour sample, mitotic index 9.41 **OR** 9.4%         [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ non-tumour sample, 2.17 **OR** 2.2%        [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ *ignore omission of %*\
$\hspace{1em}$ *do not credit decimal values e.g. 0.91, 0.02*

[2 marks]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ Mitotic index formula = $(\dfrac{\ce{number of cells in the active stages of mitosis}}{\ce{total number of cells observed}}) \times 100$%

$\hspace{1em}$ For the tumour sample, mitotic index = $\dfrac{8}{85} \times 100$% = **9.4%** ($\checkmark$)

$\hspace{1em}$ For the non-tumour sample, mitotic index = $\dfrac{2}{92} \times 100$% = **2.2%** ($\checkmark$)

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ sample must be one cell thick to allow enough light to pass through the sample            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sample must be stained    to colour the cell nuclei **OR** to provide visual contrast            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cells must be fixed **OR** dead **OR** no longer actively dividing so that no further cell division takes place **OR** to prevent the mitotic index from fluctuating            [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ high magnification (200$\times$ to 1000$\times$) to allow enough detail to be observed within cell nuclei **OR** to be able to see condensed chromosomes of cells undergoing mitosis        [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The sample must be **dead** so that no cells are still actively dividing; this **removes the risk** of the mitotic index **fluctuating** during the study ($\checkmark$). Tissue samples must be **one cell thick** so that **enough light** can pass through the sample to give an adequate visual picture ($\checkmark$). The sample should be **stained** appropriately to provide **visual contrast** so that chromosomes can be observed ($\checkmark$).

3.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\ce{ -> }$ metastasis [1 mark]

[1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$ $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
$\hspace{1em}$ This is called **metastasis** ($\checkmark$).

4. any **four** of the following:

   $\ce{ -> }$ examined additional fields of view **OR** calculated the mitotic index from different areas of the same tissue samples [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ taken additional biopsies **OR** tissue samples from different regions **AND** analysed them microscopically [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ counted a larger number of cells **OR** increased the sample size [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ identified anomalous results **AND** investigated them [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ calculated mean mitotic indices from replicate measurements [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ used higher-resolution imaging **OR** microscopy to identify cells undergoing mitosis more accurately [1 mark]

   $\ce{ -> }$ carried out an appropriate statistical test to determine whether the difference between tumour and non-tumour samples was statistically significant [1 mark]

   [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    The doctors could have examined **additional fields of view** from each tissue sample ($\checkmark$). They could have taken **additional biopsies from different regions** of the tumour and comparable non-tumour tissue ($\checkmark$). They could have **counted a larger number of cells** ($\checkmark$) and **calculated mean mitotic indices** from **replicate measurements** ($\checkmark$). They could also have used **higher-resolution microscopy** to identify cells undergoing mitosis more accurately ($\checkmark$).
