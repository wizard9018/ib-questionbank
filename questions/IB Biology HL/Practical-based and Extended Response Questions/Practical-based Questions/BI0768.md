# BI0768

**Maximum mark: 10**

The graph shows the results of an experiment on osmosis in potato tissue (*Solanum tuberosum*), cactus tissue (*Carnegiea gigantea*) and sunflower seeds (*Helianthus annuus*). Similar sized pieces of tissue were immersed in different solutions of sucrose for 30 hours.

![BI0768A](images/BI0768A.png)

1. Estimate the concentration of sucrose that is isotonic to the interior of the potato tissue. [1 mark]

2. Using the graph deduce, with a reason, the tissue that has the highest solute concentration. [1 mark]

3. Suggest one reason for the difference in internal solute concentration between the sunflower seed and cacti tissue. [1 mark]

4. The epidermis of the potato and cactus and the testa of the sunflowers seeds were removed. Suggest a reason for this. [1 mark]

5. Predict what the appearance of the cacti tissue would be like at 0.5 mol dm-3 sucrose solution. [1 mark]

6.
 1. State how percentage change in mass is calculated. [1 mark]

 2. Explain why percentage change in mass is calculated rather than change in mass. [2 marks]

7. Explain why temperature has to be controlled in this study. [1 mark]

8. State one way in which this experiment could be made more reliable. [1 mark]

1. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ 0.4 mol dm:sup[-3] [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

![BI0768ANSA](images/BI0768ANSA.png)

$\hspace{1em}$ The concentration of sucrose in the potato tissue is **0.4 mol dm:sup[-3]** ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **one** of the following:

 ***Answer must include a reason to gain a mark***

 $\ce{ -> }$ sunflower seed **BECAUSE** it gained the most mass **OR** it still gains mass at the most concentrated solution whereas the other tissues are losing mass at higher sucrose concentrations **OR** the sunflower seed is the only tissue to gain mass at all sucrose concentrations [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The tissue with the highest solute concentration is the **sunflower seed**, because it **gained the most mass** via osmosis ($\checkmark$), as it has a higher solute concentration compared to the solution that it was immersed in.

3. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ sunflower seed is a storage organ for carbohydrates and other molecules **WHEREAS** the cacti tissue is a storage organ for water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sunflower seed acts as a sink for sucrose **WHEREAS** the cacti tissue contains a higher concentration of water [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ sunflower plants may prioritise synthesis and accumulation of various solutes **OR** carbohydrates **OR** proteins, **WHEREAS** cacti prioritise water conservation [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The sunflower seed has a higher concentration of solutes, because it is a **storage organ for carbohydrates, proteins** and other molecules, which can be used for plant growth, whereas the cacti tissue is a **storage organ for water** ($\checkmark$), which cacti need to grow in dry areas.

4. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ the rate of osmosis **OR** movement of water may be different through the testa or epidermis [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ to eliminate potential barriers to water molecules [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ removing testa and epidermis from all tissue samples minimises confounding variables **OR** makes the results more valid [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 The rate of osmosis through the epidermis or testa may **vary from the rate of osmosis** ($\checkmark$) in underlying tissue. Removing the epidermis or testa from all samples makes the results **more valid** ($\checkmark$).

5. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ shrivelled **OR** smaller in size **OR** wilted **OR** dehydrated [1 mark]
 [1 mark]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 At 0.5 mol dm:sup[-3], the cacti tissue would appear **shrivelled** ($\checkmark$).

6.

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
 Percentage change in mass = **((final mass - initial mass) ÷ initial mass) x 100%** ($\checkmark$).

6.
 2. Any **two** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ it provides a standardised measure by expressing the change in proportion to the starting mass [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ the pieces of tissue may not have the same mass at the start of the experiment [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ allows for comparison between the changes in mass of samples of different initial masses [1 mark]

 $\ce{ -> }$ percentage change in mass is less sensitive to small variations in the mass measurement (as it calculated as a ratio of two values) [1 mark]

 Percentage change in mass is used rather than mass in this experiment, as the pieces of tissue may **not** be the **same mass at the beginning** ($\checkmark$) of the experiment, so it allows for accurate **comparison** ($\checkmark$) in the changes in mass.

7. Any **one** of the following:

 $\ce{ -> }$ temperature affects the structure of the plasma membrane, which can alter the permeability of the membrane, thus altering the rate of osmosis [1 mark]

 Temperature has to be controlled in this study because the **higher** the **temperature**, the **faster** the rate of **osmosis** ($\checkmark$).
