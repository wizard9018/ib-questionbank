# AI1146

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider two sinusoidal functions with the same period, $S_1$ and $S_2$, given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

S_1= 8 \cos (bx - 30^{\circ}) \hspace{3em} \text{and} \hspace{3em} S_2 = 6 \cos (bx + 30^{\circ})

\end{aligned}

$$

The two sinusoidal functions are combined to make the equation $S_1+S_2 = A \cos \left(bx + \alpha\right)$. 

Find the value of $A$ and the value of $\alpha$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

First, converting to radians, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

S_1= 8 \cos \left(bx - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) \hspace{3em} \text{and} \hspace{3em} S_2 = 6 \cos \left(bx + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)

\end{aligned}

$$

Next, we note that $S_1$ is the real part of the complex number $z_1 = 8\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cis}\left(bx - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$, and $S_2$ is the real part of the complex number $z_2 = 6\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cis}\left(bx + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$.

Therefore $S_1 + S_2$ is the real part of $z_1 + z_2$. We have

$$

\begin{aligned}

z_1 + z_2 &= 8\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cis}\left(bx - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) + 6\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cis}\left(bx + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) \\[6pt]

&= 8\hspace{0.1em}e^{\left(bx - \frac{\pi}{6}\right)\mathrm{i}} + 6\hspace{0.1em}e^{\left(bx + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)\mathrm{i}} \hspace{3em} \text{[converting to exponential form]} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

&= 8\hspace{0.1em}e^{{bx\mathrm{i}}-\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}} + 6\hspace{0.1em}e^{{bx\mathrm{i}}+\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}}\\[6pt]

&= 8\hspace{0.1em}e^{bx\mathrm{i}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}} + 6\hspace{0.1em}e^{bx\mathrm{i}}\cdot e^{\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}} \hspace{3em} [a^{m+n} = a^m \cdot a^n] \\[6pt]

&= e^{bx\mathrm{i}}\left(8\hspace{0.1em}e^{-\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}} + 6\hspace{0.1em}e^{\frac{\pi}{6}\mathrm{i}}\right) \\[6pt]

&= e^{bx\mathrm{i}}\left(12.17\hspace{0.1em}e^{-0.08229\mathrm{i}}\right) \hspace{3em} \text{[using G.D.C.]} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

&= 12.17\hspace{0.1em}e^{bx\mathrm{i} - 0.08229\mathrm{i}} \hspace{3em} [a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}] \\[6pt]

z_1 + z_2 &= 12.17\hspace{0.1em}e^{(bx-0.08229)\mathrm{i}} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Returning to polar form we have $z_1+z_2 = 12.17\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cis}\hspace{0.1em}(bx-0.08229)$.

The real part of $z_1 + z_2$ is $12.17\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cos}\hspace{0.1em}(bx-0.08229)$. Converting back to degrees from radians, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

$$

\begin{aligned}

S_1 + S_2 &= 12.17\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{cos}\hspace{0.1em}(bx-4.715^{\circ}) 

\end{aligned}

$$

$A \approx 12.2 \text{ and } \alpha \approx -4.72^{\circ}$

$$                    

\begin{aligned}            

\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}

$$
