# AI1289

**Maximum mark: 9**

Consider the matrix
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{B}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
6 & 2\\
3 & 7
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$

1.    Use the characteristic polynomial of __B__ to find its eigenvalues. [2 marks]

2.    Find the corresponding eigenvectors of $\mathbf{B}$. [3 marks]

3.    Hence write down matrices $\mathbf{Q}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ such that $\mathbf{B}=\bm{\mathbf{QDQ}}^{-1}$. [2 marks]

4.    Hence show that $\mathbf{B}^n=\dfrac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix}2(9^n)+3(4^n) & 2(9^n)-2(4^n)\\3(9^n)-3(4^n) & 3(9^n)+2(4^n)\end{pmatrix}$ [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The characteristic polynomial of __B__ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \det(\mathbf{B}-\lambda\mathbf{I})
    &=
    \begin{vmatrix}
    6-\lambda & 2\\
    3 & 7-\lambda
    \end{vmatrix}\\[6pt]
    &=(6-\lambda)(7-\lambda)-3(2)\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
    &=\lambda^2-13\lambda+36
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving the characteristic equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \det(\mathbf{B}-\lambda\mathbf{I})=0\\[6pt]
    \lambda^2-13\lambda+36&=0\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    on a G.D.C. the two eigenvalues, $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\lambda_1=9,\quad \lambda_2=4\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. Let $\mathbf{X}_1$ and $\mathbf{X}_2$ be the corresponding eigenvectors.
    For $\lambda_1=9$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (\mathbf{B}-\lambda_1\mathbf{I})\mathbf{X}_1
    &=
    \bm{0}\\[12pt]
    \begin{bmatrix}
    6-9 & 2\\
    3 & 7-9
    \end{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{X}_1
    &=
    \bm{0}\\[12pt]
    \begin{bmatrix}
    -3 & 2\\
    3 & -2
    \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix}
    a\\
    b
    \end{bmatrix}
    &=
    \bm{0}
    \hspace{2em}
    \left[\text{by letting }
    \mathbf{X}_1=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    a\\
    b
    \end{bmatrix}
    \right]\\[12pt]
    \begin{bmatrix}
    -3a+2b\\
    3a-2b
    \end{bmatrix}
    &=
    \bm{0}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[12pt]
    -3a+2b&=0\\[6pt]
    a&=\dfrac{2}{3}b
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3cm}
    \mathbf{X}_1
    &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    2b/3\\
    b
    \end{bmatrix}\\[10pt]
    &
    \begin{bmatrix}2\\
    3
    \end{bmatrix}
    \hspace{3em}
    \left[\text{by choosing } b=3\right]
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    For $\lambda_2=4$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (\mathbf{B}-\lambda_2\mathbf{I})\mathbf{X}_2
    &=
    \bm{0}\\[12pt]
    \begin{bmatrix}
    6-4 & 2\\
    3 & 7-4
    \end{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{X}_2
    &=
    \bm{0}\\[12pt]
    \begin{bmatrix}
    2 & 2\\
    3 & 3
    \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix}
    a\\
    b
    \end{bmatrix}
    &=
    \bm{0}
    \hspace{2em}
    \left[\text{by letting }
    \mathbf{X}_2=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    a\\
    b
    \end{bmatrix}
    \right]\\[12pt]
    \begin{bmatrix}
    2a+2b\\
    3a+3b
    \end{bmatrix}
    &=
    \bm{0}\\[12pt]
    2a+2b&=0\\[6pt]
    a&=-b
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3cm}
    \mathbf{X}_2
    &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    -b\\
    b
    \end{bmatrix}\\[12pt]
    &
    \begin{bmatrix}-1\\
    1
    \end{bmatrix}
    \hspace{3em}
    \left[\text{by choosing } b=1\right]
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. We can recognise that the matrix equation is in the form of the __power formula for a matrix (1.15)__.

    Hence, using the eigenvectors as columns, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{Q}
    &=

    \begin{pmatrix}2 & -1\\
    3 & 1
    \end{pmatrix}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{B}$ are $9$ and $4$, the diagonal matrix is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{D}
    &=

    \begin{pmatrix}9 & 0\\
    0 & 4
    \end{pmatrix}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4. Using the __power formula for a matrix (1.15)__ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{B}^n
    =
    \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}^n\mathbf{Q}^{-1}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using our previous answer and a G.D.C. we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{Q}
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    2 & -1\\
    3 & 1
    \end{pmatrix},
    &
    \mathbf{D}^n
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    9^n & 0\\
    0 & 4^n
    \end{pmatrix},
    &
    \mathbf{Q}^{-1}
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    \frac15 & \frac15\\
    -\frac35 & \frac25
    \end{pmatrix},
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    substituting into formula above we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{B}^n
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    2 & -1\\
    3 & 1
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
    9^n & 0\\
    0 & 4^n
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
    \frac15 & \frac15\\
    -\frac35 & \frac25
    \end{pmatrix}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[12pt]
    &=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    2(9^n) & -4^n\\
    3(9^n) & 4^n
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
    \frac15 & \frac15\\
    -\frac35 & \frac25
    \end{pmatrix}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[12pt]
    &=

    \dfrac15
    \begin{pmatrix}2(9^n)+3(4^n) & 2(9^n)-2(4^n)\\
    3(9^n)-3(4^n) & 3(9^n)+2(4^n)
    \end{pmatrix}
    \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
