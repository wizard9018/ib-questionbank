# AI1184

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the complex number $z$, such that $z=2\hspace{0.05em}e^{\frac{\pi}{a}i}$, where $|a|>1, a \in \mathbb{R}$.

1. Show that $z$ can never be a positive real number.     [2 marks]

Now consider the complex number $w$, such that $w=-\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{2}i$. There is one value of $a$ such that $\frac{z}{w}$ is a positive real number.

2. Find this value of $a$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. For $z$ to be a positive real number, the argument of $z$ must be a multiple of $2\pi$. The argument of $z$ is $\frac{\pi}{a}$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\pi}{a} &= 2\pi k \,,\enskip k \in \mathbb{Z} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]

    a &= \dfrac{\pi}{2\pi k} \\[6pt]

    a &= \dfrac{1}{2k} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Given that $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, $|a|<1$. But we have been given that $|a|>1$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\hspace{-3em}\boxed{\text{Therefore, $z$ can never be a positive real number.}} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Let's write $w$ in exponential form. The following Argand diagram shows $w$ geometrically:

    ![1184a](images/1184a.JPG)

    The modulus of $w$ is $2$ and the argument of $w$ is $\frac{3\pi}{4}$.

    Hence, $w=2e^{\tfrac{3\pi}{4}i}$.  

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{z}{w} &= \dfrac{2e^{\frac{\pi}{a}i}}{2e^{\tfrac{3\pi}{4}i}} \\[8pt]

    &= \left(e^{\frac{\pi}{a}i}\right)\left(e^{-\frac{3\pi}{4}i}\right) \hspace{3em} \left[\frac{1}{a^{m}}=a^{-m} \right] \\[8pt]

    &= e^{\left(\frac{\pi}{a}-\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)i} \hspace{3em} \left[a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\right]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    For $\frac{z}{w}$ to be a positive real number, the argument of $w$ must be a multiple of $2\pi$. The argument of $\frac{z}{w}$ is $\frac{\pi}{a}-\frac{3\pi}{4}$. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\pi}{a}-\frac{3\pi}{4} &= 2\pi k \,,\enskip k \in \mathbb{Z} \\[6pt]

     \dfrac{1}{a} - \dfrac{3}{4} &= 2k \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    \dfrac{4-3a}{4a} &= 2k \\[6pt]

    4 - 3a &= 8ak \\[6pt]

    4 &= 8ak + 3a \\[6pt]

    4 &= a(8k+3) \\[6pt]

    a &= \dfrac{4}{8k+3} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Recall that $|a|>1$. If we consider that $k$ must be an integer, we can say the following:

    - If $k \geq 1$, then $0<a<1$.

    - If $k \leq -1$, then $-1 < a < 0$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(R1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore the only possible integer value for $k$ is $k=0$ and hence $a=\frac{4}{3}$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
