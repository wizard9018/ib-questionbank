# AI753

**Maximum mark: 16**

A hotel is built so that the highest rooms have an outstanding view of a local tourist attraction. The hotel has 26 floors, and the number of rooms per floor decreases in a regular pattern by a fixed amount of $d$ rooms. There are $96$ rooms on the third floor and $84$ on the sixth floor.

1. Write down an equation, in terms of $u_1$ and $d$, for the number of rooms on

    1. the third floor.

    2. the sixth floor.[2 marks]

2. Hence find the value of $u_1$ and $d$.[3 marks]

3. Calculate the total number of rooms in the hotel. Give your answer to the nearest whole number.[2 marks]

The cost of booking a room increases the higher the floor on which the room is located. The price of booking a room on the first floor is $$\$ 98$$ dollars and the price increases by $5 \%$ per floor. Thus the price for booking a room on the second floor is $$\$102.90$$ and $$\$108.05$$ on the third floor, and so on.

4. Find the price of booking a room on the eighth floor. Give your answer to two decimal places.[2 marks]

5. Determine the floor at which the price of booking a room first increases above $$\$200$$.[3 marks]

6. Calculate the total revenue for the hotel if three rooms on each of the 26 floors are booked. Give your answer to the nearest dollar.[4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The $n$th term of an arithmetic sequence can be found by $u_n=u_1+(n-1)d$. Hence, we get

    1. $u_1+2d=96$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2. $u_1+5d=84$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2. Using the equations we found in part (a), we get
    $$
    \left\{\begin{array}{lcl}
    u_1+2d=96 \\[8pt]
    u_1+5d=84
    \end{array}\right. \\[4pt]
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using G.D.C. to solve, we get $u_1=104$ and $d=-4$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A2}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. Using the formula for the sum of terms of an arithmetic sequence, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S_{n}
    &= \dfrac{n}{2}(2u_1+(n-1)d)\\[6pt]
    S_{26} &= \dfrac{26}{2}(2(104)+(25)(-4))\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 1\,404} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4. Using the formula for the $n$th term in a geometric sequence, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    u_n &= u_1r^{n-1} \\[8pt]
    u_8 &= 98(1.05)^7 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &= 137.895... \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{=\text{\$}137.90} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5. We can form an inequality using the formula for the $n$th term in a geometric sequence.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    98(1.05)^{n-1} &> 200
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the numerical solver, we can determine the value of $n$ for which both sides are equal, and then interpret our answer, given that $n$ must be a positive integer.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    98(1.05)^{n-1} &= 200 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    n &= 15.62... \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    When $n=15$, $98(1.05)^{14}=194.03$.

    When $n=16$, $98(1.05)^{15}=203.73$.

    Therefore, booking first increases above \$200 on the $16\text{th floor}.$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6. The sum of terms in a geometric sequence can be found by $s_n=\dfrac{u_1(1-r^n)}{1-r}$. Hence, the revenue for the hotel of having 1 room booked on each floor is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    s_{26} &=\dfrac{98(1-1.05^{26})}{1-1.05} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[7pt]
    &= 5\,009.118...\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Thus, the income for 3 rooms booked on each floor can therefore be calculated by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3 \times s_{26} &= 3 \times 5\,009.118... \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[9pt]
    &= 15\,027.35...\\[9pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= \text{\$}15\,027} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
