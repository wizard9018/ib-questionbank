# AI115

**Maximum mark: 6**

The equation of a line $L_1$ is $y - 3x + 5 = 0$.

1.  For the line $L_1$, find:
    1.  the $x$-intercept;

    2.  the gradient. [2 marks]

A second line, $L_2$, intersects the $y$-axis at P$(0,2)$ and is
parallel to $L_1$.

2.  Find the equation of $L_2$. Give your answer in the form
    $y = mx+c$. [1 mark]

A third line, $L_3$, passes through the point Q$(3,1)$ and is
perpendicular to $L_1$.

3.  Find the gradient of the line $L_3$. [1 mark]

4.  Find the equation of $L_3$, giving your answer in the form
    $ax + by + d = 0$,\
    where $a, b, d \in \mathbb{Z}$.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Substituting $y = 0$ in the equation of $L_1$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        0 - 3x + 5 &= 0 \hspace{2.3em} \\[9pt]
        5 &= 3x \\[4pt]
        x &= \dfrac{5}{3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the point
         $\text{A}(5/3,0)$
        is the $x$-intercept of $L_1$.

    2.  If we rewrite the equation of $L_1$ in the form $y = mx + c$, we
        have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y - 3x + 5 &= 0 \\[6pt]
        y &= 3x - 5
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the gradient of $L_1$ is
         $m_{L_1} = 3$

2.  The gradient of $L_2$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6.9em} m_{L_2} &= m_{L_1} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $L_2 \parallel L_1$}] \\[6pt]
    &= 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the point-gradient formula with
    P$(0,2)$ and $m = 3$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \hspace{0.6em} \\[6pt]
    y - 2 &= 3\hspace{0.05em}(x-0) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    y = 3x + 2 \hspace{0.1em}
    $$

3.  The gradient of $L_3$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.2em} m_{L_3} &= -\dfrac{1}{m_{L_1}} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $L_3 \perp L_1$}] \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using the point-gradient formula with Q$(3,1)$ and $m = -1/3$, we
    obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \hspace{0.7em} \\[6pt]
    y - 1 &= -\dfrac{1}{3}\hspace{0.05em}(x-3) \\[8pt]
    3y - 3 &= -x + 3 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    x + 3y - 6 = 0 \hspace{6.5em}
    $$
