# AI121

**Maximum mark: 6**

The equation of a line $L_1$ is $2x + 3y = -5$.

1.  Find the gradient of $L_1$. [1 mark]

A second line, $L_2$, is perpendicular to $L_1$.

2.  Find the gradient of $L_2$. [1 mark]

The point P$(4,0)$ lies on $L_2$.

3.  Find the equation of $L_2$, giving your answer in the form
    $ax + by + d = 0$, where $a, b, d \in \mathbb{Z}$. [2 marks]

The point Q is the intersection of $L_1$ and $L_2$.

4.  Find the coordinates of Q. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we rewrite the equation of $L_1$ in the form $y = mx + c$, we
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x + 3y &= -5 \\[12pt]
    3y &= -2x - 5 \\[6pt]
    y &= -\dfrac{2}{3}x-\dfrac{5}{3} \hspace{0.4em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the gradient of $L_1$ is
     $m_{L_1} = -2/3$

2.  The gradient of $L_2$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9em} m_{L_2} &= -\dfrac{1}{m_{L_1}} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $L_2 \perp L_1$}] \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{(-2/3)} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{3}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the point-gradient formula with P$(4,0)$ and $m = 3/2$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.2em} y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[5pt]
    y - 0 &= \dfrac{3}{2}\hspace{0.05em}(x - 4) \\[6pt]
    2y &= 3x - 12 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{3.45em} 0 = 3x - 2y - 12
    $$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    L_1&:\hspace{1.0em} 2x + 3y = -5  \\[6pt]
    L_2&:\hspace{1.0em} 3x - 2y = 12
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving
    the system of linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$ for $x$ and $y$, we
    obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}

    \hspace{10.8em} \begin{array}{rcl}
    x &=& 2 \\[6pt]
    y &=& -3
    \end{array} \hspace{4em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the coordinates of Q are
     $(2,-3)$
