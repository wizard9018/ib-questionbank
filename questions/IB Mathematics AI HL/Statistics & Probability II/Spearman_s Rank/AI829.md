# AI829

**Maximum mark: 8**

The amount of dry matter in tomatoes, measured in grams, is the amount
of solid content of the fruit, subtract the water content. The table
below shows weights of dry matter in 10 tomatoes grown in soil with
different amounts of calcium, Ca, measured in mg/cm$^3$.

![08349198dd7b840be4d80322b2397b4e5a115266.svg](images/08349198dd7b840be4d80322b2397b4e5a115266.svg)

1.
    1.  Find the Pearson's product moment correlation coefficient, $r$,
        for this data.

    2.  Comment on the strength of the $r$ value found. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Find the Spearman's rank correlation coefficient, $r_s$, for
        this data.

    2.  Comment on the strength of the $r_s$ value found. [4 marks]

3.  In the context of this question and the data provided, comment on if
    both correlation coefficients should be considered when determining
    the relationships between soil calcium content and dry matter
    weight, or if one should be used in favour of the other. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.
        $r \approx 0.97$
        \[$\text{by using G.D.C.}$\]

    2.

        The Pearson's correlation coefficient is positive and close to 1. We can conclude that there is a strong, positive linear relationship between the calcium content in the soil and weight of the dry matter in the tomatoes.

2.  Ranking the values of soil calcium and dry matter weight in tomatoes
    in descending order, we obtain the following table.

    ![e22ebc2964e4deac0f1639bfc36cb0d1c0ead492.svg](images/e22ebc2964e4deac0f1639bfc36cb0d1c0ead492.svg)

    1.
        $r_s \approx 0.967$
        \[$\text{by using G.D.C.}$\]

    2.

        Since the Spearman's rank correlation coefficient is positive and close to 1, we can conclude that the ranks of the calcium content in the soil and weight of dry matter in tomatoes are strongly associated.

3.

    Since there are no outliers and the data has clear positive strong correlation, either or both of the correlation coefficients are useful to use.
