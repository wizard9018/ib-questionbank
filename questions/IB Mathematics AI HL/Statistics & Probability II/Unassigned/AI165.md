**Maximum mark: 14**

A particular species of microbes is studied in the laboratory. Microbes
are grown under controlled conditions in pure culture. The number of
microbes, $N$, present in the culture $t$ hours after the start of the
experiment are recorded. The results are shown in the table below.

![05f74794f9493b7052849a256cb95a218dff9b51.svg](images/05f74794f9493b7052849a256cb95a218dff9b51.svg)

1.  Complete the third row of the table above. [2 marks]

2.  Draw a scatter diagram of $\log N$ versus $t$, scaling the axes if
    needed. [3 marks]

3.  State the type of model that best fits the data displayed on your
    scatter\
    diagram from **part (b)**. [1 mark]

A scientist in the laboratory claims that the number of microbes, $N$,
grown in the laboratory during the experiment can be modelled by an
exponential function of the form $N(t) = a\times b^t$, where $a$ and $b$
are positive constants.

4.  Explain why the scientist is correct. [4 marks]

5.  Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$. [4 marks]

4.  Using the properties of exponents, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.2em} \log N(t) &= mt + c \hspace{3em} [\text{by parts (b) and (c)}] \\[8pt]
    10^{mt+c} &= N(t) \hspace{3em} [\text{by definition of log}] \\[8pt]
    (10^{mt})(10^c) &= N(t) \\[8pt]
    N(t) &= (10^c)(10^{m})^t
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we obtain
    $N(t) = a\times b^t$ for $a = 10^c$ and $b = 10^m$.

5.  The equation of the regression line of $\log N$ on $t$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{11em} \log N(t) &= 0.14521t + 1.69035 \hspace{2em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using part (d), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.2em} a &= 10^{1.69035} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 49.0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.3em} b &= 10^{0.14521} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 1.40
    \end{aligned}
    $$
