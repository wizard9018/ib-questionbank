# AI597

**Maximum mark: 9**

A fragrance oil manufacturer has launched a new perfume which it claims lasts $6$ hours on average after being sprayed on the body. An inspector believes that the perfume doesn't last that long. She performs a test by selecting a random sample of $14$ people, letting them spray on the perfume and recording how long it lasts, $x$ hours. She finds the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum x = 78.68; &\, \sum x^2 = 467.96
\end{aligned}
$$
Assume that the time the perfume lasts is normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$.

1.  State suitable null and alternate hypotheses to test the claim.
    [1 mark]

2. Find an unbiased estimate for $\mu$. [1 mark]

To find an unbiased estimate for $\sigma^2$, the inspector uses the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^2_{n-1} &= \dfrac{1}{n-1} \left[ \sum x^2 - \dfrac 1 n \left(\sum x \right)^2 \right].
\end{aligned}
$$

3.  Find an unbiased estimate for $\sigma^2$. [2 marks]

4.  Determine the $p$-value for the test. [3 marks]

5.  State the conclusion using a $10\%$ significance level and justify
    your answer. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The null and alternative hypotheses are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\text{H}_0:  \mu= 6 \\[8pt]
    &\text{H}_1: \mu < 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The unbiased estimate of $\mu$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu &= \dfrac{1}{n} \sum x \phantom{\hspace{4em}}\\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{14}(78.68) \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 5.62
    \end{aligned}
    $$
3.  The unbiased estimate of $\sigma^2$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \phantom{\hspace{4em}} s^2_{n-1} &= \dfrac{1}{n-1} \left[ \sum x^2 - \dfrac 1 n \left(\sum x \right)^2 \right] \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{13}\left[467.96 - \dfrac{1}{14}(78.68)^2\right] \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 1.98
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The appropriate test is a one-tailed $t$-test. Using GDC, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &p\text{-value} \approx 0.165
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.

    Since $p$-value $= 0.165 > 0.10$, we do not reject the null hypothesis. There is insufficient evidence at the 10% significance level.
