# AI596

**Maximum mark: 14**

It is believed that music can help reduce stress levels. A sample of ten healthy volunteers was taken for a psychological stress test using music therapy. Their salivary cortisol response, measured in micrograms per decilitre (mcg/dL), was recorded before and $60$ minutes after listening to relaxing music. The results are shown below.

![bd0182fe3709e6bebd9e7a1821fdce0eaffba76b.svg](images/bd0182fe3709e6bebd9e7a1821fdce0eaffba76b.svg)

1.  An appropriate hypothesis test is conducted at the $5\%$ significance level to see if listening to music has reduced salivary cortisol response and therefore had a beneficial effect on stress levels.

    1.  Write down the null and alternative hypotheses for this test.
    2.  Find the $p$-value of the test.

    3.  State the conclusion to the test. [6 marks]

The volunteers were asked to sit on massage chairs while listening to music. A large control group shows that sitting on a massage chair without music therapy also helps reduce stress levels by $0.02$ mcg/dL of cortisol on average.

2.  By considering this new information, another hypothesis test is carried out on the data at the same significance level, assuming a constant effect of $0.02$ mcg/dL for each volunteer.

    1.  State the null and alternative hypotheses for this new test.
    2.  Determine the $p$-value of the test.

    3.  Write down the conclusion to the test. [6 marks]

3.  State one criticism of the second hypothesis test, and one recommendation to improve it. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $D$ be the difference in cortisol responses before and after
    listening to music.

    1.  We conduct a paired $t$-test:
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &\text{H}_0:  \mu_D= 0 \\[6pt]
        &\text{H}_1: \mu_D < 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Based on the given data, we find the difference $D$ in cortisol
        responses before and after listening to music:

        ![11f1e8bab4508a00157e6854280606fecca40d3b.svg](images/11f1e8bab4508a00157e6854280606fecca40d3b.svg)

        Given that the population standard deviation is unknown, using the GDC, we conduct a $t$-test and obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &p\text{-value} = 0.00197
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  The $p$-value is less than the significance level.

        Since $0.00197 < 0.05$, we reject the null hypothesis. There is sufficient evidence at the 5% significance level to conclude that listening to music has a beneficial effect on stress levels.

2.
    1.  If the music therapy works, the cortisol response must drop significantly more than what the effect from sitting on massage chairs yields. Hence we carry out a paired $t$-test where
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &\text{H}_0:  \mu_D = -0.02 \\[6pt]
        &\text{H}_1: \mu_D < -0.02
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the GDC, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &p\text{-value} = 0.0739
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  The $p$-value is greater then the significance level.

        Since $0.0739 > 0.05$, we do not reject the null hypothesis. There is insufficient evidence at the 5\% significance level to conclude that listening to music has a positive effect on stress release, given the assumed effect of the massage chair.

3.  Criticism:

    It is not fair to assume that the massage chair will reduce cortisol levels by $0.02$ mcg/dL for each volunteer.

    Improvement:

    New data needs to be collected with volunteers not sitting in massage chairs.
