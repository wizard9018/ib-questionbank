# AI374

**Maximum mark: 16**

Applicants to a special division in the Australian Military Forces need
to undertake a fitness test with five components: Sprint, Endurance Run,
Push-Ups, Pull-Ups and Squats. Their fitness level on each component is
classified as either \"At Standard\" or \"Below Standard\". To pass the
test, applicants must perform at an At Standard fitness level in each of
the five components. In increase intake this year, it was decided that
applicants were allowed to omit any one of the five components to
increase their chances to pass the test.

The table below shows the number of At Standard and Below Standard
performances in each component.

![e8276df5361b2facca17898462ae8e1784497991.svg](images/e8276df5361b2facca17898462ae8e1784497991.svg)

1.  A military officer assesses the results and chooses a performance at
    random. Find the probability that this randomly chosen performance
    is

    1.  a performance in Sprint;
    2.  a Below Standard performance in Sprint;

    3.  a Below Standard performance, given that it is a performance in
        Sprint. [6 marks]

2.  The military officer groups the performances by component and
    chooses [3 marks] three performances in Squats.
    Find the probability that all three are At Standard fitness level.

A $\chi^2$ test is carried out at the $5\hspace{0.05em}\%$ significance
level for the data in the table.

3.  State the null hypothesis for this test. [1 mark]

4.  Show that the expected frequency of Below Standard
    performances in Sprint [1 mark] is $8$.

5.  Write down the number of degrees of freedom for this test.
    [1 mark]

6.  Use your graphic display calculator to find the $\chi^2$
    statistic for this data. [2 marks]

The critical value for this test is $9.488$.

7.  State the conclusion of this $\chi^2$ test. Give a reason
    for your answer. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  The contingency table shows that there are $32$ performances in
        Sprint out of a total of $200$ performances. Hence we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(Sprint)} &= \dfrac{32}{200} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.16
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The contingency table shows that there are $10$ Below Standard
        performances in Sprint out of a total of $200$ performances.
        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(Below Standard in Sprint)} &= \dfrac{10}{200} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.05
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Using the Conditional Probability rule
        $\text{Pr}(A\hspace{0.1em}|\hspace{0.1em}B) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap B)}{\text{Pr}(B)}$,
        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(Below Standard | Sprint)} &= \dfrac{\text{Pr(Below Standard)} \cap \text{(Sprint)}}{\text{Pr(Sprint)}} \\[2pt]
        &= \dfrac{10}{32} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.313
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  Let $S_i =$ {the $i$ th performance is At
    Standard}, $i = 1,\,2,\,3$. Then $S_i'=$ {the
    $i$ th performance is Below Standard}.

    When choosing three performances in Squats, all of the outcomes that
    might occur are illustrated in the following diagram. Note that the
    paths highlighted in red represent the event that all three are At
    Standard fitness level.

    ![6f4583ca3372184430170419a83964ee61de646a.svg](images/6f4583ca3372184430170419a83964ee61de646a.svg)

    So we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(S_1 \cap S_2 \cap S_3) &= \text{Pr}(S_1) \cdot \text{Pr}(S_2|S_1) \cdot \text{Pr}(S_3|S_1 \cap S_2) \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{41}{56} \cdot \dfrac{40}{55} \cdot \dfrac{39}{54} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.385
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.

    $H_0$: The fitness level and components of fitness test are independent of each other

4.  The marginal frequencies of the performances in Sprint and those
    Below Standard are highlighted in the table below.

    ![b6f649c187751218b26503561cc1bf5f80adde03.svg](images/b6f649c187751218b26503561cc1bf5f80adde03.svg)

    So the expected frequency of Below Standard performances in Sprint
    is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    E_{21} &= \dfrac{32 \times 50}{200} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 8 \hspace{3em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The degrees of freedom in a test of independence is given by
    $df = (r-1)(c-1)$, where $r$ represents the number of rows and $c$
    represents the number of columns. In this case, there are $2$ rows
    and $5$ columns. Therefore,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    df &= (2-1)(5-1) \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}= 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.
    $\chi^2 \approx 3.75$
    \[$\text{by using G.D.C.}$\]

7.  Recall that we would reject the null hypothesis $H_0$ if
    $\chi^2 > \chi^2_{\text{crit}}$. In this case, we are given
    $\chi^2_{\text{crit}} = 9.488$.

    Since $\chi^2 = 3.75 < 9.488$, we do not reject $H_0$. Hence there is insufficient evidence to conclude that the fitness level is dependent on the component of fitness test.
