# AI962

**Maximum mark: 8**

At a university, newly admitted first-year students can start their
degrees at any of its seven campuses. The admissions director for the
university is interested in finding out whether first-year students show
any specific preference in their choice of campus. She forms a model in
which she expects the same number of first-year students will select
each campus.

The director records the number of first-year students registering at
each campus this year to test the model's suitability. The results are
shown in the table below.

![25b836ef99b037839cd39cecf454359ac6a188cd.svg](images/25b836ef99b037839cd39cecf454359ac6a188cd.svg)

A goodness of fit test at a $5\%$ significance level is carried out to
test the accuracy of her model.

1.  State the null and alternative hypotheses.[2 marks]

2.  Determine the expected number of registrations at each
    campus.[1 mark]

3.  Write down the number of degrees of freedom for this test.
    [1 mark]

4.  State the conclusion to the test, justifying your answer.
    [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The null and alternative hypotheses are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \\
    & \mathrm{H}_0: \text{The data follows the model} \\[12pt]
    &\mathrm{H}_1: \text{The data does not follow the model}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The total number of first-year students in the sample is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \\
    n &= 322 + 287 + 248 + 293 + 274 + 306 + 265 \\[12pt]
    &= 1995 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    According to the model, the first-year students
    are evenly distributed among the campuses. Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Expected registrations per campus} &= 1995 \times \dfrac{1}{7} \\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-15em}285 \text{ registrations expected per campus}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Degrees of freedom} &= k - 1 \\[12pt]
    &= 7 - 1 \\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-8.3em}\text{Degrees of freedom} = 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using the G.D.C., we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    p\text{-value} &\approx 0.0397 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Recall that we reject $\text{H}_0$ if the $p$-value is
    less than the significance level. In this case, the significance
    level is set at $0.05$.

    Since the $p$-value $= 0.0397 < 0.05$, we reject the null hypothesis. Therefore, there is sufficient evidence to conclude that the data does not follow the model.
