# AI919

**Maximum mark: 16**

The time taken for a type of $1$-minute hourglass sand timer to complete
one cycle (the sand falling from the upper to the lower bulb) is
designed to follow a normal distribution with a mean of $60$ seconds and
a standard deviation of $0.4$ seconds.

1.  Find the probability that an hourglass chosen at random completes
    one cycle

    1.  in less than $59.4$ seconds
    2.  in more than $60.8$ seconds [4 marks]

A random sample of $500$ hourglasses are chosen and their cycle times
tested.

2.  Find the expected number of hourglasses that complete their
    cycles

    1.  between $59.4$ seconds and $60$ seconds
    2.  between $60$ seconds and $60.8$ seconds [4 marks]

The recorded times of the $500$ hourglasses are summarised in the table
below.

![b2b139f3d242a25520cc68099693d41b7064a3a1.svg](images/b2b139f3d242a25520cc68099693d41b7064a3a1.svg)

A $\chi^2$ goodness of fit test at a $5\%$ level of significance is
conducted to validate if the time taken by the hourglasses to complete a
cycle can be modelled by a normal distribution with a mean of $60$
seconds and a standard deviation $0.4$ seconds.

3.  State, in words, the null and alternative hypotheses of the
    test. [2 marks]

4.  Find the $p$-value of the test. [4 marks]

5.  State the conclusion to the test. Give a reason for your
    answer. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $T$ be the time, in seconds, for an hourglass to complete a
    cycle.\
    We have $T \sim N(60,0.4^2)$.
    1.  The probability that the hourglass takes less than $59.4$
        seconds to complete a cycle is represented by the shaded region
        below.

        ![fadd811eddec8cd05df7d9a4ec5ae3a5661daab4.svg](images/fadd811eddec8cd05df7d9a4ec5ae3a5661daab4.svg)

        Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(T < 59.4) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(0,\, 59.4,\, 60,\, 0.4) \\[8pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.0668
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The shaded area below represents Pr$(T > 60.8)$.

        ![7e69dc4dcaa6ee6826b4a02352f1c4b8d035d4cc.svg](images/7e69dc4dcaa6ee6826b4a02352f1c4b8d035d4cc.svg)

        Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3.75em} \text{Pr}(T > 60.8) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(60.8,\, 180,\, 60,\, 0.4) \\[5pt]
        &\approx 0.0228
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.75em} \text{Pr}( 59.4 < T < 60) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(59.4,\, 60,\, 60,\, 0.4)  \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.4332
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Let the random variable $X$ represent the number of hourglasses
        taking between $59.4$ seconds and $60$ seconds to complete a
        cycle.
        $X \sim \mathrm{B(500,\hspace{0.2em}0.4332)}$,
        and hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(X) &= np \\[3pt]
        &= 500 \times 0.4332 \\[5pt]
        &\approx 217
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(60 < T < 60.8) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(60,\, 60.8,\, 60,\, 0.4) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.4772
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Let the random variable $Y$ represent the number of hourglasses
        taking between $60$ seconds and $60.8$ seconds to complete a
        cycle.
        $Y \sim \mathrm{B(500,\hspace{0.2em}0.4772)}$,
        and hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(Y) &= np \\[3pt]
        &= 500 \times 0.4772 \\[6pt]
        &\approx 239
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.

    H$_0$: The time taken by an hourglass to complete one cycle is normally distributed with a mean of $60$ seconds and a standard deviation of $0.4$ seconds

    H$_1$: The time taken by an hourglass to complete one cycle is not normally distributed with a mean of $60$ seconds and a standard deviation of $0.4$ seconds

4.  If we calculate the expected number of hourglasses taking less than
    $59.4$ seconds to complete a cycle and the expected number of
    hourglasses taking more than $60.8$ seconds to complete a cycle in
    the same way as part (b), we obtain

    ![bff8212020d99ddcb9a71ecd83b27a81cf287c2e.svg](images/bff8212020d99ddcb9a71ecd83b27a81cf287c2e.svg)

    Using the formula for the degrees of freedom $df = k - 1$ with
    $k = 4$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.5em} df &= 4 - 1  \\[3pt]
    &= 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using G.D.C to find
    the $p$-value, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{1em} p\text{-value} \approx 0.0217
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Recall that we reject the null hypothesis if the $p$-value is less
    than significance level. In this case, the significance level is set
    at $0.05$.

    Since $p$-value $= 0.0222 < 0.05$, we reject H$_0$. Therefore, there is sufficient evidence to conclude that the time taken by a $1$-minute hourglass to complete a cycle should not be modelled by a normal distribution with a mean of $60$ seconds and a standard deviation of $0.4$ seconds.
