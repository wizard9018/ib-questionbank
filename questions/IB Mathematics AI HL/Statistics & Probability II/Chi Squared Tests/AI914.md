# AI914

**Maximum mark: 6**

A dry cleaning store is open $6$ days per week and closed on Sunday. The
manager of the store forms a model to forecast the number of customers
visiting the store, so that he can effectively roster the staff.
According to his model, customers visiting the store are supposed to be
evenly distributed across the $6$ operating days of the week.

To ensure his model is accurate, the manager records the number of
customers visiting the store each day in a particular week. The results
of this sample are shown in the table below.

![c34689f4069aff674e47b476c55468f06d561bd2.svg](images/c34689f4069aff674e47b476c55468f06d561bd2.svg)

The manager conducts a $\chi^2$ goodness of fit test at a $5\%$
significance level to test the accuracy of his model. His hypotheses are
as follows.
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{H}_0: \text{The data follows the model} \\[-2.2pt]
&\mathrm{H}_1: \text{The data does not follow the model}
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Determine the expected number of customers visiting the store each
    operating day, according to the model. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Write down the number of degrees of freedom for this test.

    2.  Write down the conclusion to the test, justifying your answer.
        [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The total number of customers visiting the store in the sample week
    is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10em} n &= 134 + 146 + 127 + 133 + 111 + 153 \\[6pt]
    &= 804
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    According to the model, the customers are evenly
    distributed across the $6$ operating days of the week. Hence, the
    expected number each day is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Expected} &= 804 \times \dfrac{1}{6}  \hspace{1.75em}\\[6pt]
    &= 134
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Degrees of freedom} &= k - 1 \hspace{12em}\\[8pt]
        &= 6 - 1 \\[6pt]
        &= 5
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using GDC, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        p\text{-value} &\approx 0.151 \hspace{7em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Recall that we reject $H_0$ if the $p$-value
        is less than the significance level. In this case, the
        significance level is set at $0.05$.

        Since the $p$-value $= 0.151 > 0.05$, we do not reject the null hypothesis. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that the data does not follow the model.
