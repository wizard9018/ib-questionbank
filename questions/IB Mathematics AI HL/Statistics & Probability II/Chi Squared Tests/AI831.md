# AI831

**Maximum mark: 15**

The sales manager of a hotel in Morocco estimates that the number of
guests from USA, Europe (EU) and the Middle East (ME) are four, two and
three times greater than the number of guests from the rest of the world
(RoW) combined, respectively.

1.  Create a probability distribution table reflecting the probabilities
    of the nationality of a randomly chosen guest visiting the hotel.
    [3 marks]

2.  Find the probability that at least 2 guests from 8 randomly selected
    guests are from the Middle East. [2 marks]

The sales manager wants to validate her estimates and collects data on
guest nationalities for one month. The results of the sample are as
follows.

![e3c2dc95814002fafef4768d98c67ccef10fbbae.svg](images/e3c2dc95814002fafef4768d98c67ccef10fbbae.svg)

3.  Determine the expected number of guests from each
    nationality for this month, given the probability estimates of the
    sales manager. [2 marks]

A $\chi^2$ goodness of fit test is carried out at a $5\%$ significance
level to determine if guest nationalities are in proportion to the
estimates made by the sales manager.

4.
    1.  State the null and alternative hypotheses.

    2.  Write down the number of degrees of freedom.
    3.  Using your calculator, find the p-value.

    4.  Determine the conclusion for the test, justifying your answer.
        [8 marks]

1.  Let $X$ be the discrete random variable representing the nationality
    of randomly selected guests visiting the hotel. The following table
    shows the probability distribution for $X$.

    Since the probabilities must add to $1$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    4p+2p+3p+p&=1 \\[3pt]
    10p &= 1 \\[3pt]
    p&=0.1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, we get the following probability
    distribution table for $X$.

    ![0410461f9b566dfbbdb4698dad8ec8b978695913.svg](images/0410461f9b566dfbbdb4698dad8ec8b978695913.svg)

2.  Let $Y$ be the discrete random variable representing the number of
    hotel guests from the Middle East. We have $Y\sim B(8, 0.3)$. Hence,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(Y \geq 2) &= \text{BinomCdf}\left(8, \,0.3,\,2,\,8\right) \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.745
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The total number of guests in the month is $150$. We can find the
    expected number by multiplying the total number by the respective
    probabilities. Hence, we get

    ![4b0b5a6a690427883bd1e873c74cc60c7da4d7fa.svg](images/4b0b5a6a690427883bd1e873c74cc60c7da4d7fa.svg)

4.
    1.

        $H_0:$ Guest nationalities are in proportion to the est. probabilities

        $H_1:$ Guest nationalities are not in proportion to the est. probabilities

    2.  Using $df=k-1$, where $k$ is the number of categories, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        df &= 4-1 \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}=3
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.
        $\text{p-value} \approx0.42$

    4.  Recall that we reject the null hypothesis, $H_0$, if the p-value
        \< the significance level. In this test, the significance level
        is set at $0.05$.

        Since p-value = $0.42$ > SL = $0.05$, we do not reject $H_0$. Hence, we do not reject the probability estimates made by the sales manager.
