# AI918

**Maximum mark: 16**

All tennis balls manufactured by Wilson Sports Company have to meet ITF
regulations in order to be approved for tournament play. During the test
for bouncing, balls are dropped from a height of
$254$ cm onto a granite surface. The heights of
the first bounce are assumed to follow a normal distribution with mean
of $140.6$ cm and a standard deviation of
$2.8$ cm.

1.  Find the probability that a randomly chosen ball bounces

    1.  less than $135$ cm

    2.  more than $145$ cm [4 marks]

An inspector selects $800$ tennis balls at random for the bounce test.
The bounce height of each ball is measured and recorded.

2.  Find, correct to one decimal place, the expected number of
    balls that bounce between

    1.  $135$ cm to
        $140$ cm

    2.  $140$ cm to
        $145$ cm [4 marks]

The actual recorded bounce heights of the $800$ tennis balls are shown
in the following table.

![71b5a9e2e5353cf4c5382950056a19fd4157e903.svg](images/71b5a9e2e5353cf4c5382950056a19fd4157e903.svg)

The inspector decides to perform a $\chi^2$ goodness of fit test at a
$5\%$ level of significance to validate if a normal distribution with
mean of $140.6$ cm and a standard deviation of
$2.8$ cm should be used to model the bounce
heights of the balls in the bounce test.

3.  State, in words, the null and alternative hypotheses of the
    test. [2 marks]

4.  Find the $p$-value of the test. [4 marks]

5.  State the conclusion to the test. Give a reason for your
    answer. [2 marks]

1.  Let $H$ be the bounce height, in cm, of a ball. We have
    $H \sim N(140.6,2.8^2)$

    1.  The probability that the ball bounces less than
        $135$ cm is represented by the shaded
        region below.

        Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(H < 135) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(0,\, 135,\, 140.6,\, 2.8) \\[8pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.0228
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The probability that the ball bounces more than
        $145$ cm is represented by the shaded
        region below.

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3em} \text{Pr}(H > 145) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(145,\, 300,\, 140.6,\, 2.8) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.058

2.
    1.  Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.25em} \text{Pr}(135 < H < 140) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(135,\, 140,\, 140.6,\, 2.8) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.3924
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Let the random variable $X$ represent the number of balls out of
        $800$ that bounce between $135$ cm and
        $140$ cm.
        $X \sim \mathrm{B(800,\hspace{0.2em}0.3924)}$ and hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(X) &= np \\[3pt]
        &= 800 \times 0.3924 \hspace{1.75em} \\[6pt]
        &\approx 313.9
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using G.D.C, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(140 < H < 145) &= \text{NormCdf}\hspace{0.1em}(140,\, 145,\, 140.6,\, 2.8) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.5268
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Let the random variable $Y$ represent the number of balls out of
        $800$ that bounce between $140$ cm and
        $145$ cm.
        $Y \sim \mathrm{B(800,\hspace{0.2em}0.5268)}$ and hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(Y) &= np \\[3pt]
        &= 800 \times 0.5268 \\[6pt]
        &\approx 421.4
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.

    H$_0$: Bounce heights are normally distributed with a mean of $140.6\,$cm and a standard deviation of $2.8\,$cm

    H$_1$: Bounce heights are not normally distributed with a mean of $140.6\,$cm and a standard deviation of $2.8\,$cm

4.  If we calculate the expected number of balls out of $800$ that
    bounce less than $135$cm and the expected number of balls that
    bounce more than $145$cm in the same way as part (b), we obtain

    ![235866dafc25e3867930cfa8972e36f68766f6cf.svg](images/235866dafc25e3867930cfa8972e36f68766f6cf.svg)

    We also have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Degrees of freedom} &= k - 1 \hspace{5.25em} \\[4pt]
    &= 4 - 1 \\[4pt]
    &= 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using G.D.C to find the $p$-value, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &p\text{-value} = 0.118
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Recall that we reject the null hypothesis if the $p$-value is less
    than significance level. In this case, the significance level is set
    at $0.05$.

    Since $p$-value $= 0.118 > 0.05$, we do not reject H$_0$. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that the bounce heights of the tennis balls are not normally distributed with a mean of $140.6\,$cm and a standard deviation of $2.8\,$cm.
