# AI589

**Maximum mark: 6**

The body mass index (kg/m${^2}$) and the systolic blood pressure (mmHg)
of $10$ study volunteers are recorded, with the results shown in the
table below.

![1a71474d6ed001b17c3ad4d062574a4e51d50e6f.svg](images/1a71474d6ed001b17c3ad4d062574a4e51d50e6f.svg)

A biomedical researcher claims that there is a positive correlation
between body mass index and systolic blood pressure. She conducts a test
and decides to choose a $1\%$ level of significance to verify her claim.

1.  State
    1.  the null hypothesis H$_0$,

    2.  the alternative hypothesis H$_1$. [2 marks]

2.  Find the correlation coefficient, $r$, and the $p$-value of the
    test. [2 marks]

3.  Interpret the values found in part (b) and hence state the
    conclusion for the test. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $\rho$ be the correlation coefficient between body mass index
    and systolic blood pressure. We are testing

    1.
        $\text{H}_0: \rho = 0$

    2.
        $\text{H}_1: \rho > 0$

2.  Using GDC, we obtain
     $\text{r = 0.892}$
    and
     $p \text{-value} = 2.59 \times 10^{-4}$

3.

    Since $p$-value $= 2.70 \times 10^{-4} < 0.01$, we can reject the null hypothesis. Therefore, there is sufficient evidence to conclude that there is a positive correlation between body mass index and systolic blood pressure. In addition, a correlation coefficient (r) value of $0.891$ indicates a strong, positive correlation between the two variables.
