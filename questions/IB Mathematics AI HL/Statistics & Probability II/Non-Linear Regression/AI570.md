# AI570

**Maximum mark: 8**

The tables below show the grip strength of volunteer, measured in
kilogram-force (kgf) with a dynamometer, at various times of a certain
day starting at midnight.

![1a63ac4db5739609fa4d7a5e0d04c0ffff2fa8e6.svg](images/1a63ac4db5739609fa4d7a5e0d04c0ffff2fa8e6.svg)

![2c3635118848db3a7745cea0922098b860d91ecf.svg](images/2c3635118848db3a7745cea0922098b860d91ecf.svg)

1.  Draw the scatter plot on the axes provided below and describe it.
    [2 marks]

    ![9edb5fe1fd352718e2572d96389b7359a5caadbd.svg](images/9edb5fe1fd352718e2572d96389b7359a5caadbd.svg)

2.  Find an appropriate model for the volunteers grip strength $S$ in
    terms of hours since midnight $h$. [2 marks]

3.  Using your model, find the average grip strength of the volunteer.
    [2 marks]

4.  Use your model to predict the volunteers grip strength at $15$:$00$.
    [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  There appears to be strong sinusoidal association between the
    volunteers grip strength and time throughout the day.

    ![43534ebcb51d152f4a28d8dd0a3ff98f402a2809.svg](images/43534ebcb51d152f4a28d8dd0a3ff98f402a2809.svg)

2.  Using GDC, we get
    $$
    S = 2.37 \sin(0.255h -3.03) + 18.0
    $$

3.  Let's graph the sinusoidal curve and its middle line.

    ![ccd5f6351fd0c5a9974233df22e60e157b4d7268.svg](images/ccd5f6351fd0c5a9974233df22e60e157b4d7268.svg)

    The middle line of the graph suggests that the average grip strength of the volunteer is $S = 18$ kgf.

4.  Substituting $h = 15$ into the model, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S &= 2.37 \sin(0.255(15) -3.03) + 18.0 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 19.7\hspace{0.25em}\text{kgf}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
