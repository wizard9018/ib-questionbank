# AI552

**Maximum mark: 8**

The UK National Institute for Clinical Excellence recommends that
healthy blood glucose levels for adults should be within the range of
$4.0$ to $5.9$ mmol/L.

Kelly, a nutritionist and advocate of the Paleo diet, believes that
following a Paleo diet maintains low blood glucose levels and those who
follow it will have average blood glucose levels of about $4.0$ mmol/L
when in a fasted state.

Kelly conducts a test to validate her claim and samples $8$ colleagues
that follow a Paleo diet. Their blood glucose levels upon waking are as
follows:

$$
4.2,\, 3.7, \, 3.8,\, 3.7,\,  4.4,\,
4.1,\, 3.8,\, 4.0
$$

Assume that these sample participants have been randomly selected from a normal distribution N$(\mu, \sigma^2)$.

1.  Determine unbiased estimates for $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$. [2 marks]

2.  Calculate a $90\%$ confidence interval for $\mu$. [3 marks]

3.  To validate her claim, Kelly sets the null hypothesis
    H$_0: \mu = 4.0$.

    1.  Determine the value of an appropriate test statistic for the
        given data and find the corresponding $p$-value using a
        two-tailed test.

    2.  State and justify your conclusion at a $5\%$ significance level.
        [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The unbiased estimates for $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{2.8cm} \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu \approx 3.96 \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{2.65cm} s_{n-1}^2 \approx 0.0655   \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since the population variance is unknown, we use the
    $t$-distribution to determine the critical value. In addition, we
    are given $1-\alpha = 0.9$ and $n = 8$. Hence we have $\alpha = 0.1$
    and degrees of freedom $\nu = 8 - 1 = 7$, and so the margin of error
    can be calculated as
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ \hspace{2.25cm} \text{ME} &= t_{\nu, \textstyle \frac{\alpha}{2}} \cdot \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}} \\[3pt]
    &=  t_{7, 0.05} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{0.0655}}{\sqrt{8}} \\[3pt]
    &= \text{invT}(0.95,7) \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{0.0655}}{\sqrt{8}} \\[3pt]
    &= 1.89 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{0.0655}}{\sqrt{8}} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[3pt]
    &\approx 0.171
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Thus a $90\%$ confidence interval for $\mu$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu - \text{ME} &< \mu < \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu + \text{ME} \\[3pt]
    3.96 - 0.171 &< \mu < 3.96 + 0.171 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-2.3em}3.79 < \mu < 4.13
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  We are testing
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{H}_0: \mu = 4.0 \\[3pt]
        \rm{H}_1: \mu \neq 4.0
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can find the $t$-statistic using our GDC.
        We get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        t \approx -0.414
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Since it is a two-tailed test, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \ \hspace{2.25cm} p\text{-value} &= 2 \cdot \rm{tCdf}(-100, -0.414, 7) \\[3pt]
        &\approx 2 \cdot 0.346 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} \approx 0.691 \hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.

        Since the $p$-value = 0.691 > 0.05, we do not reject H$_0$. This means there is insufficient evidence to reject the claim that average blood glucose levels on a Paleo diet will be $4.0$ mmol/L on average.
