# AI1150

**Maximum mark: 6**

Sam really likes to snack on _Niggly Nuts_.

He recently started weighing, $w$, the packets that he buys with each packet being measured in grams.

So far he has weighed $7$ packets and has found the following summary statistics for his sample.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \sum w &=591.3\, g &\sum w^2=49952.53\,g^2
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  1.  Using the formula for the variance of a sample, $s^2_n=\frac{\sum w^2}{n}-\bar{w}^2$, find an unbiased estimate of the population standard deviation of the weight of nuts in each packet. Give your answer to $4$ significant figures.

    2.  Hence, calculate a $80\%$ confidence interval for the population mean weight of the packets. Give your answer to $4$ significant figures. [4 marks]

2.  State one assumption you have made in order for the interval to be valid. [1 mark]

The manufacturers of _Niggly Nuts_ claim there is "at least $85$ grams of nuts in each packet".

3.  Briefly comment on the manufacturers claims. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  First we can find the mean of the sample
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bar{w}&=\frac{\sum w}{n}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{591.3}{7}\\[6pt]
        &=84.4714... \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next we can find the variance of the sample
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        s^2_n&=\frac{\sum w^2}{n}-\bar{w}^2\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{49952.53}{7}-84.4714...^2\\[6pt]
        &=0.653469... \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now we can use the formula to find an unbiased estimate of the population standard deviation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        s^2_{n-1}&=\frac{n}{n-1}s^2_n\\[6pt]
        s^2_{n-1}&=\frac{7}{6}\times 0.653469...\\[6pt]
        s_{n-1}&=\sqrt{0.76238...}\\
        &=0.8731442...\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}0.8731  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    2.  Note that we cannot calculate a $z$-interval because we do not know the population standard deviation. Using the G.D.C. we can find a confidence $t-$interval using $\bar{w}=84.4714...$, $S_w=0.8731...$, $n=7$ and C-Level=$0.8$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &\left[83.996..., 84.946...\right]\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\left[84.00, 84.95\right] \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  As the sample size is small ($<30$) we must assume the population weights follow a normal distribution.
    $$
    \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
    $$

    $\text{However, if Sam used a slightly larger confidence interval, say the more common examples of 95\% or 99\%, then he would likely catch the population mean within the upper limit of his interval. So the claims could be interpreted as being valid.}$
