# AI543

**Maximum mark: 6**

A local electronics distributor would like to import digital
thermometers from a manufacturer overseas. Prior to the purchase, they
test a random sample of $6$ of the thermometers by recording the
forehead temperature of a volunteer. It is believed that if the true
temperature of the volunteer is $\mu$, the recorded temperatures will be
normally distributed with mean of $\mu$ and standard deviation of
$0.32^\circ$C.

The recorded temperatures, in degrees Celsius, of the volunteer on the
$6$ thermometers are as follows:

$$
36.2,\, 35.7,\, 35.8,\, 36.5,\, 36.2,\, 35.4.
$$

1.  Find a $99\%$ confidence interval to estimate $\mu$, correct to $2$
    decimal places. [3 marks]

A second sample of 6 thermometers is tested. The mean temperature recorded for this sample is $36.6^\circ\,\text{C}$. The distributors claim that this set of thermometers could not have came from the same batch as the first 6.

2.  By calculating a second $99\%$ confidence interval, determine whether the distributors are correct. Justify your answer. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Since the population variance is known, we calculate a $z$-interval, not a $t$-interval. Because we do have a set of data, we can use this.

    Inputting our list of data, the value $\sigma = 0.32$ and $\text{C Level}=0.99$ into the G.D.C.,

    We find that $\text{C Lower}=35.630...$ and $\text{C Upper}=36.303...$

    Thus the $99\%$ confidence interval of the
    population mean correct to $2$ decimal places is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    35.63 < \mu < 36.30
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Again we calculate a $z$-interval. Because we do not have the values in the second set of data, we must do this using statistics.

    Inputting the values $\overline{x}=36.6$, $\sigma = 0.32$, $n=6$ and $\text{C Level}=0.99$ into the G.D.C.,

    We find that $\text{C Lower}=36.26...$ and $\text{C Upper}=36.93...$

    Because the two confidence intervals cross over, there is insufficient evidence that the mean temperature is different, hence the claim is incorrect.
