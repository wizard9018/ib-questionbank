# AI546

**Maximum mark: 14**

A waist reduction study was conducted on a large population of volunteer
participants. The participants went on a low sugar diet for a duration
of two months. Each participant measured their waist circumference, in
cm, before and after the two month study. The results of a sample of
$10$ participants are shown in the table below.

![f6663ba11d641160a7d0c649f26cf5b8f48ad66b.svg](images/f6663ba11d641160a7d0c649f26cf5b8f48ad66b.svg)

1.  Determine unbiased estimates for the mean and variance of the waist
    size reduction of all participants in the waist reduction study.
    [4 marks]

2.  The study aims to determine if a low sugar diet significantly
    reduces waist size circumference.

    1.  State the null and alternative hypotheses.
    2.  Determine the value of the statistic used for testing the
        hypotheses.

    3.  Find the critical value at a significance level of $2\%$ and
        state your conclusion. [6 marks]

One of the study participants tries to estimate the mean waist size
reduction using the data table above and obtains a confidence interval
of $(-2.93, 7.95)$.

3.  Find the confidence level of this interval. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Determining the difference between the waist sizes before and after
    taking the two month period, we obtain

    ![5593c73c4d208495a2220067f895d57e2f2992b5.svg](images/5593c73c4d208495a2220067f895d57e2f2992b5.svg)

    Using GDC, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ \hspace{1em} &\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu \approx 3.95 \\[8pt]
    &s_{n-1}^2 \approx 37.7
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  $\text{H}_0: \mu_x = 0$
        $\text{H}_1: \mu_x > 0$

    2.  Since the population variance of $x$ is unknown, we find the
        $t$-statistic, which can be found using our GDC

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        t \approx 2.03
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  This is a one-tailed test with $\alpha = 0.02$, and with
        $n = 10$, the degrees of freedom $\nu = 10-1 = 9$. Therefore we
        get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        t_{\text{crit}} &= t_{\nu,\alpha} \\[3pt]
        &=  t_{9, 0.02} \\[3pt]
        &= \text{invT}(0.98,9) \\[6pt]
        &= 2.40 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Since $t = 2.03 < t_{\text{crit}} = 2.40$, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that this diet reduces waist circumference over a two month period.

3.  The margin of error is half the interval length. Hence, we form the
    equation:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    t_{9,\textstyle \frac{\alpha}{2}} \cdot \dfrac{s_{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}  &= \dfrac 1 2 (7.95-(-2.93)) \\[3pt]
    t_{9,\textstyle \frac{\alpha}{2}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{37.7}}{\sqrt{10}}  &= 5.44 \\[3pt]
    t_{9,\textstyle \frac{\alpha}{2}} &\approx 2.80  \\[6pt]
    1 - \dfrac{\alpha}{2} &= \text{tCDF}(-100,2.80,9) \\[3pt]
    1 - \dfrac{\alpha}{2} &\approx 0.9896 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[3pt]
    \alpha &\approx 0.0207 \\[6pt]
    1 - \alpha &\approx 0.979
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the confidence level of the mean waist size reduction being within the interval (-2.93,7.95) based off the sample data provided is $\approx$ 97.9\%.
