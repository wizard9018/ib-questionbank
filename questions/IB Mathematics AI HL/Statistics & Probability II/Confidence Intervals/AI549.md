# AI549

**Maximum mark: 8**

James, an experienced software engineer, develops a new natural language
processing algorithm. He wants to assess how long it takes for the
algorithm to analyse a $500$ megabyte data set. He records the running
time on $10$ random data sets of this size with the following results,
in seconds.

$$
10.2,\, 8.7, \, 9.3,\, 14.5,\,  8.5,\,
15.7,\, 10.4,\, 9.8,\, 13.6,\, 16.3
$$
Assume that these times are a random sample from a normal distribution N$(\mu, \sigma^2)$.

1.  Determine unbiased estimates for $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$. [2 marks]

2.  Calculate a $95\%$ confidence interval for $\mu$. [3 marks]

3.  Before James calculated the confidence interval, he guessed that the
    algorithm run time would be 10 seconds on average. To check this, he
    sets up the null hypothesis H$_0: \mu = 10$.

    1.  Determine the value of an appropriate test statistic for the
        given data and find the corresponding $p$-value using a
        two-tailed test.

    2.  State and justify your conclusion at a $5\%$ significance level.
        [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The unbiased estimates for $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{2.8cm} \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu \approx 11.7 \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{2.65cm} s_{n-1}^2 \approx 9.02   \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since the population variance is unknown, we use the
    $t$-distribution to determine the critical value. In addition, we
    are given $1-\alpha = 0.95$ and $n = 10$. Hence we have
    $\alpha = 0.05$ and degree of freedom $\nu = 10 - 1 = 9$, and so the
    margin of error is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ \hspace{2.25cm} \text{ME} &= t_{\nu, \textstyle \frac{\alpha}{2}} \cdot \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}} \\[3pt]
    &=  t_{9, 0.025} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{9.02}}{\sqrt{10}} \\[3pt]
    &= \text{invT}(0.975,9) \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{9.02}}{\sqrt{10}} \\[3pt]
    &= 2.262 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{9.02}}{\sqrt{10}} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[3pt]
    &\approx 2.15
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Thus a $95\%$ confidence interval for $\mu$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu - \text{ME} &< \mu < \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu + \text{ME} \\[3pt]
    11.7 - 2.15 &< \mu < 11.7 + 2.15 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-2.3em}9.55 < \mu < 13.9
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  We are testing
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{H}_0: \mu = 10 \\[3pt]
        \rm{H}_1: \mu \neq 10
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can find the $t$-statistic using our GDC.
        We get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        t \approx 1.79
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Since it is a two-tailed test, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \ \hspace{2.25cm} p\text{-value} &= 2 \cdot \rm{tCdf}(1.79, 100, 9) \\[3pt]
        &\approx 2 \cdot 0.5354 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} \approx 0.107 \hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.

        Since the $p$-value = 0.107 > 0.05, we do not reject H$_0$. This means there is insufficient evidence to reject the claim that the average algorithm run time is $10$ seconds.
