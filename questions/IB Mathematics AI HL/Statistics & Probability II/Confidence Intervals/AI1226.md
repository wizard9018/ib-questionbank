# AI1226

**Maximum mark: 7**

The grade 12 students at Fourier high school sit a mathematics test that has a maximum mark of 40.

A random sample of 12 students is selected from all those who sat the test. It is found that the mean for this sample is 25 marks out of 40, and the standard deviation for the sample is 4 marks.

The marks of the students are multiplied by 2, and 20 is added, to give a final mark out of 100.

1. For the random sample of 12 students, determine

    1. The mean score out of 100;

    2. The standard deviation of the scores out of 100. [4 marks]

2. Hence calculate a $95\%$ confidence interval for the mean score out of 100 for the cohort. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The mark out of 100 is a linear transformation of the mark out of 40. Specifically, $Y=2X+20$, where $X$ represents the sample mark out of 40 and $Y$ represents the sample mark out of 100.

    We can therefore determine the mean and standard deviation of the sample scores out of 100 by using the formulae for linear transformations of a single random variable. 

    1. For the mean, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \mathrm{E}(Y) &= \mathrm{E}(2X+20) \\[8pt]

        &= 2\,\mathrm{E}(X)+20 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

        &= 2 \times 25 + 20 \\[8pt]

        \mathrm{E}(Y) &= 70

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        $\boxed{\text{The mean score out of 100 for the 12 students in the sample is 70.}}$ 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. For the standard deviation, we must first find the variance. The sample variance is 16. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \mathrm{Var}(Y) &= \mathrm{Var}(2X+20) \\[8pt]

        &= 2^2 \,\mathrm{Var}(X) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

        &= 4 \times 16 \\[8pt]

        \mathrm{Var}(Y) &= 64

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The standard deviation is the square root of the variance, therefore

        $\boxed{\text{The standard deviation for the 12 students in the sample is 8.}}$ 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. We can now calculate our confidence interval, which will be a $t$-interval, because the population standard deviation is unknown. We can use the sample mean of $70$ as an unbiased estimate of the population mean.

    We cannot use the sample standard deviation as an unbiased estimate for population standard deviation ($s_{n-1})$, but we can use the formula for an unbiased estimate of population variance to find this. We have $n=12$, and $s_n=8$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    s_{n-1}^2 &= \frac{n}{n-1}\,s_n^2 \\[8pt]

    s_{n-1} &= \sqrt{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,s_n \\[8pt]

    &= \sqrt{\frac{12}{11}}\times 8 \\[8pt]

    s_{n-1} &= 8.355... \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    As noted, this will be a $t$-interval, and it will be calculated using statistics, because we do not have a list of data.

    The following input values are required.

    - $\bar{x}=70$

    - $s_{n-1}=8.355...$ or $\sqrt{\frac{12}{11}}\times8$

    - $n=12$

    - C Level = 95\%

    Using the G.D.C., we find a lower bound of $64.69...$ and an upper bound of $75.30...$ 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\text{A 95\% confidence interval for the mean score out of 100 for the cohort is $(64.7,75.3)\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$}$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
