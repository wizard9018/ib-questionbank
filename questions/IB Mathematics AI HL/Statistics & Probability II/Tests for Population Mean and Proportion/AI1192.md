# AI1192

**Maximum mark: 7**

Steph is a keen basketballer. He has been practicing his free-throw shots, and believes that each time he attempts a free-throw shot, there is an $80\%$ of scoring.

1. Assuming that Steph is correct, calculate the probability that if Steph attempts 40 free-throw shots, he will score at least 35 of them.      [2 marks]

His coach, Steve, believes that Steph's free-throw percentage is actually higher than $80\%$. He asks Steph to take 40 free-throws, and tests his belief at the $5\%$ significance level. The null and alternate hypotheses for Steve's test are as follows:

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \text{H}_0:\,\,\, & p = 0.8 \\[6pt]

    \text{H}_1:\,\,\, & p > 0.8

\end{aligned}

$$

2. Determine the critical region for Steve's test.         [2 marks]

3. State the probability that Steve makes a type I error.      [1 mark]

4. Given that Steph's free-throw accuracy is in fact $90\%$, calculate the probability that Steve makes a type II error.          [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let the discrete random variable $X$ represent the number of shots Steph scores out of $40$. We are looking for $\mathrm{P}(X\geq35)$.

    We could note the following:

    $\hspace{2em}\bullet$ There are a fixed number of trials (40 shots)

    $\hspace{2em}\bullet$ Each trial has exactly two outcomes (score or not)

    $\hspace{2em}\bullet$ Each trial is independent

    $\hspace{2em}\bullet$ There is a constant probability $(p=0.8)$

    All the criteria for a binomial distribution are met, therefore $X\sim\mathrm{B}(40,0.8)$.

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(X\geq35) &= \text{binomCdf}(40,0.8,35,40) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= 0.1613...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\text{The probability that Steph scores at least 35 shots is approximately $0.161$.}$$

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. We are looking for the smallest value $x$ such that $\mathrm{P}(X \geq x) < 0.05$.

    We know this value will be greater than $35$, because from part (a), $\mathrm{P}(X\geq35) > 0.05$. We therefore start from $x=36$. By trial and error, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(X\geq36) &= 0.07591... \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    \mathrm{P}(X\geq37) &= 0.02846...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $x=37$ is therefore the smallest value $x$ such that $\mathrm{P}(X \geq x) < 0.05$ and hence the critical region is $X \geq 37$

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. A type I error occurs when a null hypothesis is rejected, when it should not have been. 

    In the context of this question, it is the probability that $X \geq 37$ (The critical region - where $H_0$ is rejected), given that Steph was correct ($p=0.8$). 

    It is in fact equal to the probability of falling within the critical region, as shown here:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(\text{type I error}) &= \mathrm{P}(X \geq 37 \,\, | \,\, p = 0.8) \\[6pt]

    &= \text{binomCdf}(40,0.8,37,40)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have already worked out this probability in part (b) and therefore $$\text{The probability of a type I error is approximately $0.0285$.}$$ 

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. A type II error occurs when a null hypothesis is $\textbf{not}$ rejected, when it should have been.

    In the context of this question, it is the probability that $X \leq 36$ (The acceptance region - where $H_0$ is not rejected), given that Steph was incorrect ($p=0.9$). We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(\text{type II error}) &= \mathrm{P}(X \leq 36 \,\, | \,\, p = 0.9) \\[6pt]

    &= \text{binomCdf}(40,0.9,0,36) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= 0.5768...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

   $$\text{The probability of a type II error is approximately $0.577$.}$$

   $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$
