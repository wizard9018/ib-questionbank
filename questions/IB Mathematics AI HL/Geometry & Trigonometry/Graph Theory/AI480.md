# AI480

**Maximum mark: 6**

A team of network technicians at a cloud storage company randomly move
between data centers A, B, C and D, with probabilities shown on the
graph below.

![b7fb5835d9d0a49ef264e8302ce40e8ade81b31c.svg](images/b7fb5835d9d0a49ef264e8302ce40e8ade81b31c.svg)

1.  Find the value of $p$ and the value of $q$. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Complete the weighted adjacency table below.

        ![93cd1369f1ec774e0c656ad487e6f67f0e2d4d26.svg](images/93cd1369f1ec774e0c656ad487e6f67f0e2d4d26.svg)

    2.  Hence write down a transition matrix $\bm{T}$ for the graph.
        [2 marks]

3.  Given that the team is currently in data center C, find the
    probability that they will return to C
    after visiting exactly two other data centers. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Looking at the edges going out of $C$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    p + 0.8 &= 1 \hspace{2em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{1.4em} p = 0.2
    $$
    Similarly, looking at the
    edges going out of $A$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0.3 + q + 0.5 &= 1 \hspace{4.4em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{1.4em} q = 0.2
    $$

2.
    1.  If we complete the weighted adjacency table below, we obtain

        ![4aa0d0ddb49e4eceb66b8105752f2e36d5b3a745.svg](images/4aa0d0ddb49e4eceb66b8105752f2e36d5b3a745.svg)

    2.  Note that in an $\textbf{adjacency matrix}$, the rows would represent the data center that the team of technicians is coming from, and the columns would represent the data center that the team of technicians is going to, as shown in the table in part (i).

        However, the opposite is true for a $\textbf{transition matrix}$, which is what we are looking for here. The columns represent the data center that the team of technicians is coming from, and the rows represent the data center that the team of technicians is going to. We would $\bold{transpose}$ the rows and columns (swap them over). Hence the transition matrix $\boldsymbol{T}$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \boxed{\bm{T} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0.6 & 0.2 & 0.7 \\[3pt] 0.3 & 0 & 0.8 & 0 \\[3pt] 0.2 & 0.4 & 0 & 0.3 \\[3pt] 0.5 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $\textbf{Note: by checking that each column adds up to exactly 1, we can}$
        $\textbf{ensure we have the columns and rows the correct way around.}$

3.  We can use the formula $\bm{T}^n\bm{s}_0=\bm{s}_n$, where $n$ is the number of transitions we need to make, $\bm{s}_0$ is the initial state vector and $\bm{s}_n$ is the $n$th state vector.

    Because the team is currently at data center C, the initial state vector is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm{s}_0 &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\[3pt] 0 \\[3pt] 1 \\[3pt] 0 \end{bmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    From data center C, the team visits two other data centers then returns to C. There are three transitions in total so $n=3$.

    The calculation we require is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm{s}_3 &= \bm{T}^3\bm{s}_0 \\[8pt]
    &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0.6 & 0.2 & 0.7 \\[3pt] 0.3 & 0 & 0.8 & 0 \\[3pt] 0.2 & 0.4 & 0 & 0.3 \\[3pt] 0.5 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}^3 \times \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\[3pt] 0 \\[3pt] 1 \\[3pt] 0 \end{bmatrix} \\[8pt]
    &= \begin{bmatrix} 0.178 \\[3pt] 0.432 \\[3pt] \color{red}{0.15} \\[3pt] 0.24 \end{bmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the team currently in C will return to C after visiting
    exactly two other

    data centers with probability
     $0.15$
