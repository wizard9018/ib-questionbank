# AI1069

**Maximum mark: 14**

Seven major cities on the mainland of Australia are Adelaide, Brisbane, Cairns, Darwin, Melbourne, Perth and Sydney. The following graph shows these cities as A, B, C, D, M, P and S respectively, and the time it takes, in hours, to drive between them using the interstate road system.

![ai1069a](images/ai1069a.PNG)

1. 1. Write down a Hamiltonian cycle for the graph.

    2. State whether the graph contains an Eulerian trail. Justify your answer. [3 marks]

Cathy is currently in Adelaide and has hired an RV for a month, which must be returned in Adelaide. She wants to visit each of the seven cities, but would also like to travel every one of the major roads between each city.

2. Calculate the minimum number of hours driving it will take Cathy to drive along each of the major roads, starting and finishing in Adelaide.[4 marks]

Cathy decides this will take too long, and so decides to visit each city, without driving along every major road.

3. Starting at vertex A, use the nearest neighbour algorithm to find an upper bound for Cathy's trip.[3 marks]

4. By deleting vertex A, find a lower bound for Cathy's trip.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. A Hamiltonian cycle passes through every vertex in a graph exactly once, starting and ending at the same vertex. There are many Hamiltonian cycles in the graph. 

        For example: $$\boxed{\text{A-M-S-B-C-D-P-A}}$$

    2. An Eulerian trail traverses all the edges in a graph exactly once. For an Eulerian trail to exist, a graph must have exactly two odd vertices, and the trail must start at one odd vertex and finish at the other odd vertex. 

        Our graph has exactly two odd vertices: A (degree 5) and S (degree 3). Hence an Eulerian trail exists starting and finishing at Adelaide and Sydney in either order.

2. Cathy's intention to travel to each city and along every major road requires an Eulerian circuit. For this, each vertex must be even. We can create an Eulerian circuit by adding an additional journey between Adelaide and Sydney:

    ![ai1069b](images/ai1069b.PNG)

    This makes vertex A even (degree 6) and also vertex S (degree 4). Hence we have an Eulerian circuit for the addition of one edge of weight 14.

    The total time of the trip will be the sum of all the edges in this new graph. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    41+28+31+29+19+37+22+14+10+8+9+14 = 262

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    It will take Cathy a minimum of 262 hours to drive along each road, starting and finishing in Adelaide.

3. Starting from vertex A, we add the edge of least weight, continuing to do so without creating a cycle, until we arrive back at vertex A. The first edge is from A to M (weight = 8):

    ![ai1069c](images/ai1069c.PNG)

    The second edge is from M to S (weight = 9):

    ![ai1069d](images/ai1069d.PNG)

    The third edge is from S to B (weight = 10):

    ![ai1069e](images/ai1069e.PNG)

    The fourth edge is from B to C (weight = 19):

    ![ai1069f](images/ai1069f.PNG)

    The fifth edge is from C to D (weight = 29):

    ![ai1069g](images/ai1069g.PNG)

    The sixth edge is from D to P (weight = 41), noting that although edges AD and BD have less weight, they form a cycle.

    ![ai1069h](images/ai1069h.PNG)

    Finally, we join P to A (weight = 28) to complete the cycle.

    ![ai1069i](images/ai1069i.PNG)

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    8+9+10+19+29+41+28=144

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \boxed{\text{An upper bound for Cathy's trip is $144$ driving hours.}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. By deleting vertex A, we have:

    ![ai1069j](images/ai1069j.PNG)

    We now find the minimum spanning tree for the remaining subgraph using either Prim's or Kruskal's algorithm. We will use Kruskal's, and will therefore select the edge of least weight, which is MS (weight = 9).

    ![ai1069k](images/ai1069k.PNG)

    We now now add the edge of least weight not already selected, which is SB (weight = 10).

    ![ai1069l](images/ai1069l.PNG)

    Continuing the process, we add BC, CD and DP to obtain our minimum spanning tree:

    ![ai1069m](images/ai1069m.PNG)

    We now add the two edges of least weight that connect vertex A to the MST, which are AM and AS:

    ![ai1069n](images/ai1069n.PNG)

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    8+9+14+10+19+29+41=130

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \boxed{\text{A lower bound for Cathy's trip is $130$ driving hours.}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
