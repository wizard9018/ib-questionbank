# AI485

**Maximum mark: 11**

The distances by road, in kilometres, between six cities in the
Netherlands are shown in the following table.

![7e2f76314838c61fadac46c5036031518236f773.svg](images/7e2f76314838c61fadac46c5036031518236f773.svg)

A telecommunications company has gained approval to connect the six
cities with fiber optic cabling along the road system.

1.  Using Kruskal's algorithm, calculate the minimum length of fiber
    optic cable needed for all six cities to be connected to the fibre
    optic network. [5 marks]

Visitors to the Netherlands can travel between cities on a train network
represented by the following graph.

![568eea03580bbdc9735ad571beef7c131c4bae8d.svg](images/568eea03580bbdc9735ad571beef7c131c4bae8d.svg)

2.  State, justifying your answer, whether the graph has any
    Hamiltonian:

    1.  paths;
    2.  cycles. [2 marks]

3.  State, justifying your answer, whether the graph has any
    Eulerian:

    1.  trails;
    2.  circuits. [2 marks]

Clare plans to visit the Netherlands. She wants to arrive in Eindhoven,
travel all the available train routes exactly
once, and then depart from Amsterdam.

4.  Find the route between two cities Clare will need to travel
    by an alternate method of transport in order
    to make this possible. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we use Kruskal's algorithm, we have

    ![594806a9457f6e891b963d9cb4359d06c6539649.svg](images/594806a9457f6e891b963d9cb4359d06c6539649.svg)

    ![33a6d12ca4b5f239aa5edd5ae651f02946396368.svg](images/33a6d12ca4b5f239aa5edd5ae651f02946396368.svg)

    If we calculate the weight of the above minimum spanning tree, we
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8em} \text{weight of tree} &= 23.2 + 35.3 + 44.9 + 57.8 + 79.5 \\[6pt]
    &= 240.7
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the minimum length of cable needed to
    connect the six cities is
     $241 \text{ km}$

2.
    1.  The graph has Hamiltonian paths.

        For example, Eindhoven -  Hertogenbosch - Rotterdam - The Hague - Amsterdam - Utrecht - Arnhem - Zwolle - Leeuwarden - Groningen.

    2.  We need to pass Hertogenbosch twice in order to reach Eindhoven.

        Hence the graph has no Hamiltonian cycles.

3.
    1.  There are exactly two vertices of odd degree (Arnhem \& Eindhoven).

        Hence the graph has Eulerian trails.

    2.  Not all vertices are of even degree.

        Hence the graph has no Eulerian circuits.

4.  If we add an edge between Amsterdam and Arnhem, the only vertices of
    odd degree in the resulting graph are Amsterdam (deg $5$) and
    Eindhoven (deg $1$).

    Hence the resulting graph has an Eulerian trail staring at Eindhoven
    and ending at Amsterdam.

    Therefore Clare will need to travel the route
     $\text{Amsterdam - Arnhem}$
    by an alternative method of transport.

    **Backwards thinking**

    Clare's plan will work if, after adding an edge b$/$n two vertices
    of the graph, we can find an Eulerian trail starting at Eindhoven
    and ending at Amsterdam.

    We can find such a trail if the only vertices of odd degree in
    the new graph are Amsterdam and Eindhoven.

    Hence we need to add an edge b$/$n Amsterdam and Arnhem to make
    Clare's plan possible.

    Therefore the route is
     $\text{Amsterdam - Arnhem}$
