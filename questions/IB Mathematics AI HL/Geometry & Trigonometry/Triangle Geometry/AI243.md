# AI243

**Maximum mark: 15**

The quadrilateral ABCD shown below represents a sports complex, where AB
$= 210$ m, BC $= 90$ m
and CD $= 130$ m. Angle
$\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C}$ is $\ang{70}$ and angle
$\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{D}}\mathrm{A}$ is $\ang{105}$.

![5d600c6e3ca9a545b0f2ff4d914edf95815cf70e.svg](images/5d600c6e3ca9a545b0f2ff4d914edf95815cf70e.svg)

A straight path through the sports complex joins points A and C.

1.  Find the length of the path AC. [3 marks]

2.  Show that the angle $\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C}$ is
    $39.4^\circ$, correct to three significant figures. [3 marks]

3.  Find the area of the sports complex.[4 marks]

A new path, DE, is to be built such that E is the point on AC closest to
D.

4.  Find the length of the path DE. [2 marks]

The section of the sports complex represented by triangle CDE will be an
outdoor running track. A track will be marked along the sides of this
section.

5.  Calculate the total length of the track. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the cosine rule on triangle ABC, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{AC}^2     & = \textrm{AB}^2 + \textrm{BC}^2 - 2(\textrm{AB})(\textrm{BC})\cos(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C}) \\[6pt]
    & = 210^2 + 90^2 - 2(210)(\textrm90)\cos(70) \\[6pt]
    & = 39271.6 \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{-5mm}\text{AC} \approx 198\hspace{0.25em}\text{m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the sine rule on triangle ACD, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{130}{\sin\big(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C}\big)}
    & = \dfrac{198}{\sin \ang{105}} \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{-8mm}\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C} \approx \ang{39.4} \quad \quad \quad [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{D} & = \ang{180}-\ang{105}-\ang{39.4} \\[6pt]
    & \approx \ang{35.6}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Summing the
    areas for triangles ABC and ACD using the area of a triangle
    formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Area} &= \dfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{AB})(\mathrm{BC})\sin(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C})+\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{AC})(\mathrm{CD})\sin(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{D}) \\[5pt]
    & = \dfrac1 2 (210)(90)\sin \ang{70} + \dfrac{1}{2}(198)(130)\sin (\ang{35.6}) \\[11pt]
    &\approx 16\hspace{0.1em}400 \hspace{0.25em}\mbox m^2  \quad \quad \quad [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The closest point to D on AC will lie perpendicular to AC, as
    illustrated below

    ![39a07e5c7205dfa8025b234e4ea02eeefe55c14c.svg](images/39a07e5c7205dfa8025b234e4ea02eeefe55c14c.svg)

    Using the sine ratio, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{D}) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{DE}}{\mathrm{CD}} \\[5pt]
    \sin (\ang{35.6}) & = \dfrac{\text{DE}}{130} \\[11pt]
    &\hspace{-5mm}\text{DE}    \approx 75.7 \hspace{0.25em}\text{m}  \quad \quad \quad [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using Pythagorean theorem to find CE we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{CD}^2 &= \mathrm{CE}^2 + \mathrm{DE}^2 \\[6pt]
    130^2 & = \text{CE}^2 + 75.7^2 \\[9pt]
    \text{CE} &\approx 106 \hspace{0.25em}\text{m} \quad \quad \quad [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The length of the track can be found by summing the side lengths CD,
    DE and CE:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    130 + 75.7 + 106 &\
    \approx 312 \text{ m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
