# AI1054

**Maximum mark: 7**

The side of a hill can be modelled by a triangular prism, as shown in the following diagram. Rectangle ABCD is vertical, and rectangle CDEF is horizontal.

![ai1054a](images/ai1054a.PNG)

The height of the hill is $35\,$ metres and the angle of elevation from the base of the hill at point F to the top of the hill at point B is $\ang{35}$.

1. Calculate the distance from point B to point F. Give your answer correct to the nearest metre.[2 marks]

So that the walk to the top of the hill is not too steep, a path has been constructed from point E to point A, passing through point G. The path is shown in red on the diagram. Point G is the midpoint of $\overline{\text{BF}}$.

2. Calculate the total length of the path.[3 marks]

3. Determine the angle at which walkers will rise when taking the path from point E to point G.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using right-angled triangle trigonometry, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \sin \ang{35} &= \dfrac{35}{\text{BF}} \\[6pt]

    \text{BF} &= \dfrac{35}{\sin \ang{35}} \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-1.4em}\boxed{\text{BF} = 61 \text{ metres}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Given that point G is the midpoint of BF, $\text{FG}=30.5\,$metres. We also note that triangle EFG and triangle ABG are congruent, hence $\text{AG}=\text{EG}$. Thus, the length of the path can be expressed as $d= 2 \times \text{EG}$.

    Consider the following right-angled triangle:

    ![ai1054b](images/ai1054b.PNG)

    Using the Pythagorean theorem, $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$, with $a = 150$, $b = 30.5$ and $c = h$,  we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h^2 &= 150^2 + 30.5^2 \\[6pt]

    h         & = \sqrt{150^2 + 30.5^2}\\[6pt]

    2h        & = 2 \times \sqrt{150^2 + 30.5^2}\\[6pt]

    &\approx 306 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\boxed{\text{The total length of the path is approximately $306\,$ metres.}}$$

3. Let H be the midpoint of CF. Then GH will be half the length of BC ($\text{GH}=17.5\,$m) and from part (b) we have $\text{EG}=\sqrt{150^2 + 30.5^2}$. Hence we can form the right-angled triangle EGH:

    ![ai1054c](images/ai1054c.PNG)

    The angle at which walkers will rise is the angle $\theta$ in the diagram. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \sin \theta &= \dfrac{17.5}{\sqrt{150^2 + 30.5^2}} \\[8pt]

    \theta &= \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{17.5}{\sqrt{150^2 + 30.5^2}}\right) \\[8pt]

    &\approx 6.56

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    When following the path from point E to point G, walkers will rise at an angle of approximately $6.56^{\circ}$
