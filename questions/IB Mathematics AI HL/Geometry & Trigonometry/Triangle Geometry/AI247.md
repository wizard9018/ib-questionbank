# AI247

**Maximum mark: 14**

A large plot of land is shaped like a trapezoid, ABCD, where AB is
parallel to CD, AB $= 12$ km and CD
$= 8$ km. The diagonal BD of the trapezoid is
equal to $10$ km. The internal angle
$\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{D}$ is equal to $\ang{58}$.

![e54fc931c880a7419e18b16aa436f121e42be716.svg](images/e54fc931c880a7419e18b16aa436f121e42be716.svg)

Let a new point, H, be a point on AB closest to D.

1.  Calculate the distance from H to D. [2 marks]

2.  Calculate the length of AD. [2 marks]

3.  Calculate the size of the angle
    $\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B}$. [2 marks]

A landowner estimates that the length of AD is equal to
$11.5$ km.

4.  Calculate the percentage error in the landowner's estimate.
    [2 marks]

The landowner decides to install cone-shaped concrete bollards along the
perimeter of the plot of land. The bollards are to be installed at a
distance of $100$ m from each other. The radius
of the base of each bollard is $20$ cm and the
height of each bollard is $40$ cm.

5.  Calculate the volume of one of the bollards. [2 marks]

6.  Calculate the total volume of concrete needed to install
    all the bollards. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The length from H to D (let's call it DH), is shown in the diagram
    below.

    ![95520ffb1b238eed8608c2994bff581d3e1e51b3.svg](images/95520ffb1b238eed8608c2994bff581d3e1e51b3.svg)

    Let's consider the right-angled triangle BDH:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{H}) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{DH}}{\mathrm{BD}}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    \sin(\ang{58})  & = \dfrac {\text{DH}}{10}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    \text{DH} & = 10 \sin(\ang{58})\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    &\approx  8.48\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{km}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the cosine rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{AD}^2 &= \mathrm{AB}^2+\mathrm{BD}^2-2(\mathrm{AB})(\mathrm{BD})\cos(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{D})\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    \text{AD}^2 & = 12^2 + 10^2 - 2(12)(10)\cos(\ang{58})\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    & \hspace{-5.7mm} \text{AD} \approx  10.8\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{km}
    \qquad\quad \mbox{[by using G.D.C.]}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the cosine rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{BD}^2 &= \mathrm{AD}^2 + \mathrm{AB}^2 - 2(\mathrm{AD})(\mathrm{AB})\cos(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B}) \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    \cos (\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B})     & = \dfrac {\mathrm{AD}^2 + \mathrm{AB}^2 - \mathrm{BD}^2} {2(\mathrm{AD})(\mathrm{AB})}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \cos (\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B})     & = \dfrac {10.8^2 + 12^2 - 10^2} {2(10.8)(12)}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B} & \approx \cos^{-1} (0.6197) \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    & \approx  \ang{51.7}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using the percentage error formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \varepsilon &= \left| \dfrac {V_A - V_E} {V_E} \right| \times 100 \% \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9.5mm}\\
    \varepsilon & = \left|\dfrac {11.5 - 10.8} {10.8}\right|\times 100\%\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9.5mm}\\
    & \approx  6.48\%\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using the volume of a cone formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \dfrac 1 3 \pi r^2h \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{4mm}\\
    & = \dfrac 1 3 \pi (20^2)(40)\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    & \approx 16\hspace{0.25em}800\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{cm}^3\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  We need to first find the perimeter of the plot of the land. This
    can be found by $P = \text{AD} + \text{DC} + \text{CB} + \text{BA}$.
    DC and BA is provided in the question. We know AD from part (b). We
    therefore need to find the length of CB.

    Consider the triangle BCD. Since AB and CD are parallel,
    $\mathrm{B}\hat{\mathrm{D}}\mathrm{C}$ is equal to
    $\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{D}$. Using the cosine rule, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{CB}^2 & = (\mathrm{BD})^2 + (\mathrm{CD})^2 - 2(\mathrm{BD})(\mathrm{CD})\cos(\mathrm{B}\hat{\mathrm{D}}\mathrm{C})\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    \text{CB}^2 & = 10^2 + 8^2 - 2(10)(8)\cos(\ang{58})\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    \text{CB}   & = \sqrt{ 10^2 + 8^2 - 2(10)(8)\cos(\ang{58})}\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    & \approx  8.9\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{km}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We can now find the perimeter

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    P   & = \text{AD} + \text{DC} + \text{CB} + \text{BA} \\
    & = 10.8 + 8 + 8.9 + 12\\
    & = 39.7\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{km}\\
    & = 39\hspace{0.25em}700\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the number of bollards required can be calculated by:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    N   & = \dfrac {39\hspace{0.25em}700}{100}\hspace{2cm}\\[5pt]
    & = 397
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Finally, the total volume required to
    install the 397 bollards can be found by multiplying 397 by the
    volume per bollard (found in part (e)):
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V_{\textrm {total}}     & = 16\hspace{0.25em}800 \,(397)\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    & = 6\hspace{0.25em}669\hspace{0.25em}600\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{cm}^3\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    &\approx 6\hspace{0.25em}670\hspace{0.25em}000\hspace{0.25em}\mbox{cm}^3\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
