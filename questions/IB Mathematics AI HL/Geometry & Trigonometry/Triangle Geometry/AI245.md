# AI245

**Maximum mark: 15**

A triangular farm is shown in the diagram below. A river runs along the
edge from A to B and the farm house is located at C, a safe distance
from the river. The distance from the farm house to point A is
$270$ m and to point B is
$360$ m. The angle
$\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{B}$ is $\ang{130}$.

![5a59e971dcdfb2a8bf1d85500989f46c10ae8b95.svg](images/5a59e971dcdfb2a8bf1d85500989f46c10ae8b95.svg)

1.  Calculate the distance along the river from A to B. [2 marks]

The cost of fencing is $130$ Australian dollars (AUD) per metre.

2.  Calculate the **total** cost of fencing the whole perimeter
    of the farm, providing your answer to the nearest dollar.
    [2 marks]

3.  Find the sizes of angels
    $\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B}$ and
    $\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C}$. [2 marks]

4.  Calculate the area of the farm. Give your answer correct to
    the nearest whole square metre. [2 marks]

5.  Calculate the shortest distance from the farm house to the
    river. [3 marks]

A tower is located at point B. The angle of elevation to the top of the
tower, H, from point C is measured to be $\ang{1}$.

6.  Calculate the vertical height, BH, of the tower, correct to
    the nearest metre. [2 marks]

7.  Calculate the distance between the top of the tower, H, to
    point A.[2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the cosine rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textrm{AB}^2 &= \mathrm{AC}^2 + \mathrm{BC}^2 - 2(\mathrm{AC})(\mathrm{BC}) \cos (\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{B}) \\[5pt]
    \textrm{AB}^2   & = 270^2 + 360^2 - 2(270)(360) \cos(\ang{130})\\[6pt]
    & \approx  327\,457.91\\[6pt]
    \textrm{AB}     & \approx  \sqrt{327\hspace{0.25em}457.91 }\\[6pt]
    & \approx  572\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  First, let's find the perimeter of the farm
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    p   & = \textrm{AB} + \textrm{BC} + \textrm{AC}\hspace{1.5cm}\\[6pt]
    & = 572 + 360 + 270\\[6pt]
    & = 1\hspace{0.25em}202\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The
    total cost of fencing can be found by multiplying the number of
    metres by the cost per metre:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textrm{Cost}   & = (1202)(130)\\[6pt]
    & = \$156\hspace{0.25em}260
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the sine rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac {\textrm{AB}} {\sin (\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{B})}
    &= \dfrac {\textrm{CB}} {\sin (\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B})}\\[4pt]
    \dfrac {572}{\sin(\ang{130})}   & = \dfrac {360}{\sin(\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B})}\hspace{.8cm}\\[8pt]
    572\sin(\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B})   & = 360 \sin (\ang{130})\\[9pt]
    \sin(\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B})      & = \dfrac {360 \sin (\ang{130})} {572}\\[12pt]
    \mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{B}        & \approx \sin^{-1} (0.482)\\[17pt]
    & \approx \ang{28.8}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the angle $\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C}$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textrm{A\^BC}  & = \ang{180} - \ang{130} - \ang{28.8}\\[6pt]
    & \approx \ang{21.2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using the area of a triangle formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \dfrac{1}{2} (\mathrm{AC})(\mathrm{BC})\sin(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{B})\\[4pt]
    A   & = \dfrac 1 2 (270)(360) \sin(\ang{130})\\[10pt]
    & \approx 37\hspace{0.25em}230\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using the area formula for triangle ABC in terms of its base
    $\mathrm{AB}$ and its height $h$ (ie, the shortest distance from AC
    to C), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \dfrac{1}{2} (\mathrm{AB}) (h) \\[5pt]
    37\,230 & = \dfrac 1 2 (572) (h) \qquad [\,\text{from part (d)}\,] \\[5pt]
    h & = \dfrac {37\hspace{0.25em}230} {\frac 1 2 (572)}\\[6pt]
    & \approx  130\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  The diagram below shows the tower located at B with a height of H

    ![5f105ef14cc6f9bf39193222df07c5789f8747e1.svg](images/5f105ef14cc6f9bf39193222df07c5789f8747e1.svg)

    Using the definition of the tangent to find BH, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan (\mathrm{H}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{B}) & = \dfrac {\textrm{BH}} {\mathrm{BC}}\\[5pt]
    \tan (\ang{1})  & = \dfrac {\textrm{BH}} {360}\\[9pt]
    \textrm{BH}     & = 360 \tan (\ang{1})\\[15pt]
    &\approx  6.28\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  The diagram below shows the tower located at B, 572m away from point
    A

    ![d6af0e1ceae432a4cf070c018828e0ff108eed01.svg](images/d6af0e1ceae432a4cf070c018828e0ff108eed01.svg)

    Using Pythagoras theorem, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textrm{AH}^2   & = \textrm{AB}^2 + \textrm{BH}^2\\[6pt]
    \textrm{AH}^2 & = 572^2 + 6.28^2\\[6pt]
    \textrm{AH} & = \sqrt {572^2 + 6.28^2}\\[9pt]
    & \approx   572\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
    \end{aligned}
    $$
