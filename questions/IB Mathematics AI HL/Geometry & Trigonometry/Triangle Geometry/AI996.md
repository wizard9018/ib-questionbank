# AI996

**Maximum mark: 18**

An airplane leaves Doha airport bound for Paris Charles de Gaulle airport. There are lights located at the end of the runway at points $A(0, 4)$ and $B(6, 8)$, relative to a terminal at the origin. The takeoff path of the airplane is the perpendicular bisector of line $AB$.

1.  Find the equation of the takeoff path in the form $ax + by + d = 0$, where $a, b, d \in \mathbb{Z}$.
    [4 marks]

The airplane travels at an average speed of $570\,\text{km} \hspace{0.15em} \text{hr}^{-1}$ in a straight line. Once the airplane has reached cruising altitutde, an air traffic controller at the top of a $200\hspace{0.15em}\text{m}$ high air traffic control tower at $C(7,0)$ observes that the angle of elevation to the airplane is $\ang{40}$. Five minutes later, the controller observes that the angle of elevation is $\ang{10}$.

2.  Find the cruising altitude of the airplane in metres. [5 marks]

As the airplane is about to land at the Paris Charles de Gaulle airport, the pilot is asked to delay the landing due to a traffic issue. The pilot is instructed to turn the airplane on a bearing of $\ang{045}$ for $10\,$km until reaching point P, then travel on a bearing of $\ang{165}$ for $30\,$km to point Q before flying back to the original point O for landing.

3.  Find the angle OP̂Q. [2 marks]

4.  Find the shortest distance from Q back to O for landing.
    [3 marks]

5.  Find the bearing the airplane must travel on to get back to
    O from Q. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the gradient formula, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    m_{AB} &= \dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} \\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{8-4}{6-0} \\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac 2 3

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence the gradient of the takeoff path is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &= -\dfrac{3}{2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    If we find the midpoint of the line segment connecting the points $(0,4)$ and $(6,8)$, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{M}\left( \dfrac{x_1+ x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right) &= \left( \dfrac{0+6}{2}, \dfrac{4+8}{2}\right)  \\[10pt]

    &= (3,6)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the formula for the equation of a line in the form $y - y_1 = m(x-x_1)$ where $m = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (3,6)$, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y - 6 &= -\dfrac{3}{2}(x-3) \\[6pt]

    2y - 12 &= -3x + 9 \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-5.5em}\boxed{3x + 2y - 21 = 0} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The point $C(7,0)$ is on the line of the takeoff path, therefore the airplane is travelling directly away from the air traffic controller.

    The distance that the airplane travels in five minutes is 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d &= 570 \times \dfrac{5}{60} \\[6pt]

    &= 47.5 \text{ km} \\[6pt]

    &= 47\hspace{0.15em}500 \text{ m}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Let $h$ be the altitude of the airplane from the top of the tower, as shown in the following diagram. Note that it is not drawn to scale.

    ![AI996a](images/AI996a.PNG)

    In the right triangle ACN, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tan \ang{10} &= \dfrac{h}{\text{AC}} \\[6pt]

    \text{AC} &= \dfrac{h}{\tan \ang{10}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Likewise, in the right triangle ABM we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tan \ang{40} &= \dfrac{h}{\text{AB}} \\[6pt]

    \text{AB} &= \dfrac{h}{\tan \ang{40}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{AC} - \text{AB} &= 47\hspace{0.15em}500 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    And therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{h}{\tan \ang{10}} - \dfrac{h}{\tan \ang{40}} &= 47\hspace{0.15em}500 \\[6pt]

    h \left( \dfrac{1}{\tan \ang{10}} - \dfrac{1}{\tan \ang{40}}  \right) &= 47\hspace{0.15em}500 \\[6pt]

    h &\approx 10\hspace{0.15em}604 \text{ m}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore, the altitude of the airplane from the ground is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{[altitude]} &\approx 10\hspace{0.15em}604 + 200

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The cruising altitude of the airplane is approximately $ 10\hspace{0.15em}800 \text{ m}$

3. Consider the angle $\gamma$ on the diagram below

    ![AI996b](images/AI996b.PNG)

    Looking at the angles marked at P, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \gamma &= \ang{180}-\ang{165} \\[6pt]

    &=\ang{15}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now looking at the angle $\beta$ and the angle $\ang{45}$, we have $\beta = \ang{45}$ as they are alternate angles on parallel lines.

    Hence we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{O\^PQ} &= \beta + \gamma \\[6pt]

    &= \ang{45}+\ang{15}\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-2.25em}\boxed{\text{O\^PQ} = \ang{60}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. Using the cosine rule, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{OQ}^2 & = \text{OP}^2+\text{PQ}^2-2(\text{OP})(\text{PQ})\cos \text{O\^PQ} \\[6pt]

    \text{OQ} &= \sqrt{10^2+30^2-2(10)(30)\cos \ang{60}}\\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-1.5em}\boxed{\text{OQ} \approx 26.5 \text{ km}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. Using the sine rule, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\sin \text{O\^{Q}P}}{\text{OP}} &= \dfrac{\sin \text{O\^{P}Q}}{\text{OQ}} \\[6pt]

    \dfrac{\sin \text{O\^{Q}P}}{10} &= \dfrac{\sin \ang{60}}{26.5} \\[6pt]

    \sin \text{O\^{Q}P} &= \dfrac{10\sin \ang{60}}{26.5} \\[6pt]

    \text{O\^{Q}P} &\approx\ang{19.07}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now consider the angle $\alpha$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \alpha &= \text{O\^{Q}P} + \ang{15} \hspace{3em} [\text{co-interior angle with $\ang{165}$}]\\[6pt]

    &\approx \ang{19.07} + \ang{15} \\[6pt]

    &\approx \ang{34.07}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore the bearing is 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \ang{360} - \alpha &= \ang{360} - \ang{34.07}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The bearing is approximately $\ang{326}$
