# AI473

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $\tan \theta = k$, for $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \theta < 2\pi$, where $k$
is a negative constant.

Express in terms of $k$:

1.  $\cos \theta$; [4 marks]

2.  $\sin \theta$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we draw a unit circle and the angle $\theta$, we have

    ![14b096fe2c9b63b29af37ce169aed5bb704d615d.svg](images/14b096fe2c9b63b29af37ce169aed5bb704d615d.svg)

    Hence the value of $\sin \theta$ is negative and the value of
    $\cos \theta$ is positive.

    Using the tangent identity, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan \theta &= \dfrac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} \\[6pt]
    k &= \dfrac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} \\[10pt]
    \sin \theta &= k\cos \theta \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the Pythagorean identity and expression $(1)$, we
    deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta &= 1 \\[9pt]
    \cos^2 \theta + (k\cos \theta)^2 &= 1 \\[9pt]
    \cos^2 \theta + k^2\cos^2 \theta &= 1 \\[9pt]
    (1+k^2)\cos^2 \theta &= 1 \\[6pt]
    \cos^2 \theta &= \dfrac{1}{1+k^2} \\[9pt]
    \cos \theta &= \pm \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+k^2}} \hspace{4.6em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{10.1em} \cos \theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+k^2}} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\cos \theta > 0$}]
    $$

2.  Substituting $\cos \theta = 1/\sqrt{1+k^2}$ into expression $(1)$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.7em} \sin \theta &= k\bigg(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+k^2}}\bigg) \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^2}} \hspace{3em} [\text{where $k < 0$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
