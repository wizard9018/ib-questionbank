# AI248

**Maximum mark: 15**

The quadrilateral ABCD represents a recreational park, where AB
$= 250$ m, BC $= 110$ m
and CD $= 130$ m. Angle
$\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C}$ is $\ang{80}$ and angle
$\mathrm{C}\hat{\mathrm{D}}\mathrm{A}$ is $\ang{115}$. This information
is shown in the following diagram.

![59493623de0a566e28bd8ba07c1a7c185811b0ab.svg](images/59493623de0a566e28bd8ba07c1a7c185811b0ab.svg)

A straight path through the park joins the points A and C.

1.  Find the length of the path AC. [3 marks]

2.  Show that the angle $\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C}$ is
    $\ang{27.5}$, correct to three significant figures. [3 marks]

3.  Find the total area of the recreational park. [4 marks]

A new path, DE, is to be built such that E is the point on AC closest to
D.

4.  Find the length of the path DE. [2 marks]

  The section of the park
represented by triangle CDE will be used for a school cross country
carnival. A track will be marked along the sides of this section.

5.  Calculate the total length of the track CDE. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the cosine rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{AC}^2 &= \text{BC}^2+\text{AB}^2-2(\text{BC})(\text{AB})\cos(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C})\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}\\
    \text{AC}^2     & = 110^2 + 250^2 - 2(110)(250)\cos(\ang{80})\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    \text{AC}^2     & \approx 65\hspace{0.25em}049.35\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    \text{AC}       & \approx \sqrt{65\hspace{0.25em}049.35}\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &  \approx  255\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the sine rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac {\text{DC}}{\sin (\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C})} &= \dfrac {\text{AC}} {\sin (\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{D}}\mathrm{C})}\rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \dfrac {130} {\sin(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C})}
    & = \dfrac {255} {\sin (\ang{115})}\hspace{3.3cm}\rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \sin (\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C})         & = \dfrac {130 \sin(\ang{115})} {255}\rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \sin (\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C}) & = 0.462\rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{A}}\mathrm{C}        & = \sin^{-1}(0.462)\rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    & \approx  \ang{27.5}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The park consists of two triangles; ADC and ABC. The total area of
    the park can be found by the sum of the areas of these two
    triangles.

    For ABC: Using the area of a triangle formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_{\triangle\text{ABC}}     &= \dfrac{1}{2} (\mathrm{AB})(\mathrm{BC})\sin(\mathrm{A}\hat{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{C})\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    & = \dfrac 1 2 (250)(110)\sin(\ang{80})\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    & \approx  13\hspace{0.25em}541\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m^2\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    For ADC: we need to first determine the angle
    $\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{A}$

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{A}    & = \ang{180} - \ang{115} - \ang{27.5}\\
     & = \ang{37.5}
     \end{aligned}
     $$
     Then

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_{\triangle\text{ADC}}  &=\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{AC})(\mathrm{CD})\sin(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{A})\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    & = \dfrac 1 2 (255)(130) \sin(\ang{37.5})\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    & = 10\hspace{0.25em}090\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m^2\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the total area of the park:

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     A   & = A_{\triangle\text{ABC}} + A_{\triangle\text{ADC}}\\
     & = 13\,541 + 10\,090\\
     & = 23\,631\\
     &\approx 23\hspace{0.25em}600\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m^2
     \end{aligned}
     $$

4.  If E lies on AC and is the shortest length from AC to D, it will be
    perpendicular to D. This creates the right-angled triangle CDE:

    ![feb3e2d765d19c813fd30c8d01a36638e5a1cf39.svg](images/feb3e2d765d19c813fd30c8d01a36638e5a1cf39.svg)

    DE can then be found by:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{E})&= \dfrac{\mathrm{DE}}{\mathrm{CD}}\\[2pt]
    \sin(\ang{37.5}) & = \dfrac {\text{DE}} {130}\\[2pt]
    \text{DE}   & = 130\sin(\ang{37.5}) \\[4pt]
    & \approx 79.1\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The track length can be found by the sum of CD, DE and EC. We first
    need to find length EC. Using the diagram in part (d) we get:

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \cos(\mathrm{D}\hat{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{E})&= \dfrac{\mathrm{EC}}{\mathrm{CD}}\\[2pt]
     \cos(\ang{37.5}) & = \dfrac {\text{EC}} {130}\\[2pt]
     \text{EC}   & = 130 \cos (\ang{37.5})\\[4pt]
     & \approx  103\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m\\
     \end{aligned}
     $$

    Therefore, the total length of the track is:

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     p   & = \text{CD} + \text{DE} + \text{EC}\\
     & = 130 + 79.1 + 103\\
     & \approx  312\hspace{0.25em}\mbox m
     \end{aligned}
     $$
