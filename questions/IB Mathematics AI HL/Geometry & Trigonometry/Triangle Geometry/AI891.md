# AI891

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the triangle below, which has side lengths AB = $25 \text{ cm}$
and BC = $35\text{ cm}$. The angle adjacent to these side lengths, AB̂C,
is $\ang{70}$.

![054c55de133e18663c40108bf1758726ceb896bf.svg](images/054c55de133e18663c40108bf1758726ceb896bf.svg)

1.  Find the area of the triangle, rounding your answer to the nearest
    cm$^2$. [2 marks]

When drawing the triangle, George measured each side length correct the
nearest centimetre, and angle AB̂C correct to the nearest degree.

2.  Calculate the maximum possible area of the triangle,
    rounding your answer to the nearest cm$^2$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the formula for the area of a triangle, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \dfrac{1}{2}(\text{AB})(\text{BC})\sin \text{A\^{B}C}\\[6pt]
    &=\dfrac{1}{2}(25)(35)\sin\ang{70}\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.5em}\approx411 \text{ cm}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Let $A_{max}$ be the maximum possible area of the triangle.
    Since AB̂C is measured correct to the nearest degree, then

    $$
    69.5 < \text{A\^{B}C}<70.49
    $$

    ![1aa1a3482d39c4b0d7afa08e6cdf9b0704870029.svg](images/1aa1a3482d39c4b0d7afa08e6cdf9b0704870029.svg)

    From the graph, the value of $\sin \text{A\^{B}C}$ is maximum for
    AB̂C$=\ang{70.49}$.
    Since AB and BC are measured to the nearest centimetre, then

    $$
    \text{AB}=25.49 \text{ cm and } \text{BC} = 35.49 \text{ cm}
    $$

    Substituting these values into the area of a triangle formula, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_{max} &= \dfrac{1}{2}(\text{AB})(\text{BC})\sin \text{A\^{B}C}\\[6pt]
    &=\dfrac{1}{2}(25.49)(35.49)\sin\ang{70.49}\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.5em}\approx426 \text{ cm}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
