# AI1059

**Maximum mark: 8**

A geometric transformation $T_1: \hspace{0.15em}\textbf{x} \hspace{0.15em}\mapsto\hspace{0.15em} \textbf{x}'\hspace{0.15em}$ is defined by

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textbf{x}'\hspace{0.15em} &= \bm{A}\textbf{x}

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $\bm{A}$ is the $2\times2\,$ matrix $\,\Bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \,\,\,\,\,1 & 2 \\[6pt] -3 & 4 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\Bigg)\hspace{-0.15em}$

The image of a parallelogram transformed by $T_1$ has an area of $140\,$ cm$^2$.

1. Find the area of the parallelogram before it was transformed.[3 marks]

Now consider a second geometric transformation $T_2: \hspace{0.15em}\textbf{x} \hspace{0.15em}\mapsto\hspace{0.15em} \textbf{x}'\hspace{0.15em}$ which is defined by

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textbf{x}'\hspace{0.15em} &= \bm{A}^{-1}\textbf{x} + \textbf{b}

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $$\textbf{b} = \hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$1$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$2$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)$$

Under transformation $T_2$, the point P maps to itself.

2. Find the coordinates of P.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the formula for the determinant of $2\times2$ matrix, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \det\hspace{0.05em}(\bm{A})\hspace{-0.05em} &= \Bigg|\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \,\,\,\,\,1 & 2 \\[6pt] -3 & 4 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\Bigg| \\[16pt]

    &= (1)(4) - (2)(-3) \\[6pt]

    &= 10 \\[-12pt]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence, using the formula relating the area of the parallelogram with its image,

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{Area}_{\text{(image)}} &= |\hspace{-0.1em}\det\hspace{0.05em}(\bm{A})|\hspace{-0.05em}\times\text{Area}_{\text{(pre-image)}}\\[12pt]

    140 &= 10 \times \text{Area}_{\text{(pre-image)}} \\[12pt]

    \text{Area}_{\text{(pre-image)}} &= \dfrac{140}{10}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\boxed{\text{Before it was transformed, the parallelogram had an area of $14\,$ cm$^2$.}}$$

2. Let the coordinates of point P be $(x,y)$. Substituting the values we have for $\bm{A}$ and $\textbf{b}$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) &= \Bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \,\,\,\,\,1 & 2 \\[6pt] -3 & 4 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\Bigg)^{-1}\,\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) + \hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$1$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$2$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the G.D.C. we find that $\bm{A}^{-1} = \Bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 0.4 & -0.2 \\[6pt] 0.3 & \,\,\,\,\,0.1 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\Bigg)$. Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) &= \Bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 0.4 & -0.2 \\[6pt] 0.3 & \,\,\,\,\,0.1 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\Bigg)\,\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) + \hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$1$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$2$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can form a system of linear equations in $x$ and $y:$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x &= 0.4x - 0.2y + 1 \\[8pt]

    y &= 0.3x + 0.1y + 2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Rearranging, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    0.6x + 0.2y &= 1 \\[8pt]

    -0.3x + 0.9y &= 2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the G.D.C. we find that $x=\dfrac{5}{6}$ and $y=\dfrac{5}{2}$.

    $$\boxed{\text{The coordinates of P are $\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\,,\,\dfrac{5}{2}\right)$}}$$
