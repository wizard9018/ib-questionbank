# AI1151

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider two transformations.

The first is a reflection in the line $y=\sqrt{3}x$ and is described by matrix $\bm{A}$.

The second is a rotation of $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ radians in an anti-clockwise direction about the origin and is described by matrix $\bm{B}$.

1.  Determine the matrices $\bm{A}$ and $\bm{B}$. [2 marks]

The point $X(4, 2)$ is transformed by $\bm{A}$ and then the image is transformed by $\bm{B}$, the resulting point is labelled $X'$.

2.  Determine the coordinates of $X'$ to $4$ significant figures. [2 marks]

The line $l_1$, with equation $y=ax$, makes an angle $\alpha$ with the $x$-axis, where $\tfrac{\pi}{2}< \alpha< \pi$.

A single reflection in $l_1$ maps $X'$ back to $X$.

3.  Determine the value of $a$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the formula for a reflection in the line $y=(\tan{\theta})x$ we can see that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tan{\theta}&=\sqrt{3}\\
        \theta&=\tfrac{\pi}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now use the formula to write down $\bm{A}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bm{A}&=\begin{pmatrix}
                \cos{\tfrac{2\pi}{3}} & \sin{\tfrac{2\pi}{3}} \\[3pt]
                \sin{\tfrac{2\pi}{3}} & -\cos{\tfrac{2\pi}{3}}
                \end{pmatrix}\\[6pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}
                -\tfrac{1}{2} & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[3pt]
                \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \tfrac{1}{2}
                \end{pmatrix}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    For $\bm{B}$ the angle of rotation is $\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, therefore using the relevant formula we can write down
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bm{B}&=\begin{pmatrix}
                \cos{\tfrac{\pi}{2}} & -\sin{\tfrac{\pi}{2}} \\[3pt]
                \sin{\tfrac{\pi}{2}} & \cos{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}
                \end{pmatrix}\\[6pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}
                0 & -1 \\[3pt]
                1 & 0
                \end{pmatrix}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  Let the position vector of the point $X'$ be
    $$
    \begin{pmatrix}
            x' \\
            y'
    \end{pmatrix}
    $$

    We can setup the following matrix equation with $\bm{A}$ being applied first and then $\bm{B}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bm{B}\bm{A}\begin{pmatrix}
    4 \\
    2
    \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
    x' \\
    y'
    \end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can resolve $\bm{B}\bm{A}$ using our previous answer and the G.D.C.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \begin{pmatrix}
    -\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\tfrac{1}{2} \\[3pt]
    -\tfrac{1}{2}  & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
    \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
    4 \\
    2
    \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}
    x' \\
    y'
    \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Finally
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \begin{pmatrix}
    x' \\
    y'
    \end{pmatrix}=  \begin{pmatrix}
    -4.4641... \\
    -0.26794...
    \end{pmatrix} \hspace{2em}\text{[by using G.D.C.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore the coordinates of $X'$ are $(-4.464, -0.2679)$
    $$
         \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

3.  Consider the following matrix equation where $\bm{T}$ represents a reflection in the line $y=ax$ which makes an angle $\alpha$ with the $x-$axis.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix}
    4 \\
    2
    \end{pmatrix}&=\bm{T} \begin{pmatrix}
    -4.4641... \\
    -0.26794...
    \end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the formula we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bm{T}&=\begin{pmatrix}
    \cos{2\alpha} & \sin{2\alpha} \\[3pt]
    \sin{2\alpha} & -\cos{2\alpha}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix}
    4 \\
    2
    \end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}
    \cos{2\alpha} & \sin{2\alpha} \\[3pt]
    \sin{2\alpha} & -\cos{2\alpha}
    \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
    -4.4641... \\
    -0.26794...
    \end{pmatrix}  \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now form two equations
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        4&=-4.4641...\cos{2\alpha}-0.26794...\sin{2\alpha}\\[3pt]
        2&=-4.4641...\sin{2\alpha}+0.26794...\cos{2\alpha} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can solve both equations simultaneously using a sketch and recalling that $\tfrac{\pi}{2}< \alpha< \pi$.

    The graph of $y=-4.4641\cos{2\alpha}-0.26794\sin{2\alpha}$ and $y=4$ yields one solution in the desired range, $\alpha=1.83259...$

    ![a1151v1](images/a1151v1.png)

    The graph of $y=-4.4641\sin{2\alpha}+0.26794\cos{2\alpha}$ and $y=2$ yields two solutions in the desired range, $\alpha=1.83259..., 2.9397...$

    ![a1151v2](images/a1151v2.png)

    Therefore for both equation to be solved simultaneously $\alpha=1.83259...$ radians.

    From the formula
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        a&=\tan{1.83259...}\\
        a&=-3.732...\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-3.73} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
