# AI1187

**Maximum mark: 8**

A geometric transformation $T:(x, y)\rightarrow(x' ,y') $ consists of three transformations in order:

$\hspace{3em}\bullet$ A rotation of $\theta$ radians about the origin, followed by      

$\hspace{3em}\bullet$ An enlargement with scale factor 2, centred at the origin, followed by

$\hspace{3em}\bullet$ A translation of 1 unit right and 3 units down.

Given that the transformation $T$ maps the point $(1.4,-0.2)$ to itself, find the angle and direction of the rotation.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We can write a matrix equation, showing the rotation matrix, $\bm{R}$, the enlargement matrix, $\bm{E}$, and the translation vector $\textbf{b}$ in the correct order:

$$

\begin{aligned}

\begin{pmatrix*}

x' \\ y'

\end{pmatrix*} = \bm{E} \cdot \bm{R} \cdot \begin{pmatrix*}

x \\ y

\end{pmatrix*} + \textbf{b} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

The order of the matrices is $\textbf{very}$ important!

Using the formula sheet, we can see that the enlargement matrix $\bm{E}$ will be

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \bm{E} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}2 & 0 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}0 & 2 \end{pmatrix*}

\end{aligned}

$$

We have not been told the angle nor the direction of the rotation, so we will use the anticlockwise rotation matrix from the formula sheet, which we can interpret when we get to our final solution.

We have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \bm{R} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] \hspace{-0.1em}\cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \hspace{-0.1em}\sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix*}

\end{aligned}

$$

The translation vector is $\mathbf{b} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-3  \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}$.

Hence our transformation equation is

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \begin{pmatrix*}

        x' \\ y'

    \end{pmatrix*} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}2 & 0 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}0 & 2 \end{pmatrix*} \, \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] \hspace{-0.1em}\cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \hspace{-0.1em}\sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix*} \, \begin{pmatrix*}

        x \\ y

    \end{pmatrix*} + \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-3  \end{pmatrix*} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

We can now use the fact that $T:(1.4,-0.2)\rightarrow(1.4,-0.2)$:

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        1.4 \\ -0.2

    \end{pmatrix*} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}2 & 0 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}0 & 2 \end{pmatrix*} \, \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] \hspace{-0.1em}\cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \hspace{-0.1em}\sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix*} \, \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        1.4 \\ -0.2

    \end{pmatrix*} + \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-3  \end{pmatrix*} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[10pt]

    \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        1.4 \\ -0.2

    \end{pmatrix*} - \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-3  \end{pmatrix*} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r] \hspace{-0.1em}2\cos \theta & -2\sin \theta \\ \hspace{-0.1em}2\sin \theta & 2\cos \theta \end{pmatrix*} \, \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        1.4 \\ -0.2

    \end{pmatrix*} \\[10pt]

    \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        0.4 \\ 2.8

    \end{pmatrix*} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        2.8 \cos \theta + 0.4 \sin \theta \\ 2.8 \sin \theta - 0.4 \cos \theta

    \end{pmatrix*} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Hence we can form two equations:

$$

\begin{aligned}

    2.8 \cos \theta + 0.4 \sin \theta &= 0.4 \\[6pt]

    2.8 \sin \theta - 0.4 \cos \theta &= 2.8

\end{aligned}

$$

To solve these equations simultaneously, we could let $x = \cos \theta$ and $y = \sin \theta$, forming two new equations:

$$

\begin{aligned}

    2.8 x + 0.4 y &= 0.4 \\[6pt]

    2.8 y - 0.4 x &= 2.8

\end{aligned}

$$

Using the simultaneous equation solver on the G.D.C.,

$$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

we obtain $x=0$ and $y=1$. 

$$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

And so we have 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \cos \theta &= 0 \\[6pt]

    \sin \theta &= 1

\end{aligned}

$$

For $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$, the only solution is $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$. 

Therefore, $$\boxed{\text{the rotation is $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians in the anticlockwise direction.}}$$

$$

\begin{aligned}

\tag*{\textbf{A2}}

\end{aligned}

$$

$$\textbf{Note: if we had used a clockwise rotation at the start of the question, we would have obtained an answer of a rotation of either $\bm{\frac{3\pi}{2}}$}$$

$$\textbf{radians or $\bm{-\frac{\pi}{2}}$ radians in the clockwise direction, which would also be correct.}$$
