# AI1237

**Maximum mark: 12**

An automatic card dealing machine can be programmed to deal cards to any number of players from 1 to 8, by using geometric transformations. 

The machine is placed at the origin and always deals the first card to the point $(0.4,0)$.

The machine then rotates $\textbf{clockwise}$, dealing a card at a particular angle, depending on the number of players. The distance the card travels is always 0.4 metres.

For example, if there are three players, the machine deals the first card to the point $p_1=(0.4,0)$, rotates $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ radians clockwise about the origin to deal to the second player ($p_2$), then another $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ radians clockwise about the origin to deal to the third player ($p_3$).

This is shown in the following diagram.

![ai1237a](images/ai1237a.png)

This can also be expressed with transformation matrices, as follows.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    d_3^2 &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} & \sin \frac{2\pi}{3} \\ -\sin \frac{2\pi}{3} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $d_3^2$ is the position vector of the point that the card is dealt to for player 2 when three players are playing, and

$$

\begin{aligned}

    d_3^3 &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} & \sin \frac{2\pi}{3} \\ -\sin \frac{2\pi}{3} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} \end{pmatrix}^2 \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $d_3^3$ is the position vector of the point that the card is dealt to for player 3 when three players are playing.

1. Calculate the position vectors given by $d_3^2$ and $d_3^3$. [2 marks]

2. Write down an expression for $d_n^x$, the position vector of the point that the card is dealt to for player $x$ when there are $n$ players in total. [2 marks]

3. Hence or otherwise, determine the coordinates of the point that the card is dealt to for the 4th player when there are 7 players in total. [3 marks]

Phil is hosting a card game night and there are 14 players in total. He decides to have two 7 player games running simultaneously. He sets up a second automatic card dealing machine at the point $(2,1)$, relative to the origin at which the first machine is placed.

The second machine is identical to the first machine, except it rotates $\textbf{anticlockwise}$ as it deals.

4. Calculate the distance between the point the card is dealt to for the 4th player by the first machine, and the point the card is dealt to for the 4th player by the second machine. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Calculating these directly in the G.D.C., ensuring the calculator is in radians mode, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d_3^2 &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} & \sin \frac{2\pi}{3} \\ -\sin \frac{2\pi}{3} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\\[15pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix} -0.2 \\ -0.3464... \end{pmatrix} \\[15pt]

    d_3^2 &= \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\begin{pmatrix} -0.2 \\ -0.346 \end{pmatrix}}\hspace{0.525em} \, \text{(3 s.f.)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[20pt]

    d_3^3 &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} & \sin \frac{2\pi}{3} \\ -\sin \frac{2\pi}{3} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{3} \end{pmatrix}^2 \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \\[15pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix} -0.2 \\ 0.3464... \end{pmatrix}\\[15pt]

    d_3^3 &= \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\begin{pmatrix} -0.2 \\ 0.346 \end{pmatrix}}\hspace{0.525em} \, \text{(3 s.f.)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. If we examine the expression we have just used to generate $d_3^2$ and $d_3^3$, we could note that the denominator in each fraction inside the transformation matrix is 3. This divides a full circle ($2\pi$ radians) into 3 equal parts because there are three players. When there are $n$ players, the denominators will all be $n$.

    The exponent on the transformation matrix is 1 for player 2, and 2 for player 3, so the exponent is always one less than the player number who is being dealt to. Therefore the exponent for player $x$ will be $x-1$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \boxed{d_n^x = \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{n} & \sin \frac{2\pi}{n} \\ -\sin \frac{2\pi}{n} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{n} \end{pmatrix}^{x-1} \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}} \tag*{\textbf{A2}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Substituting in $n=7$ and $x=4$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d_7^4 &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{7} & \sin \frac{2\pi}{7} \\ -\sin \frac{2\pi}{7} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{7} \end{pmatrix}^{4-1} \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[15pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix} -0.3603... \\ -0.1735... \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The coordinates of the point that the card is dealt to for the 4th player when there are 7 players in total are $(-0.360,-0.174)\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. We can produce an expression that describes the position vector of the point which the second card machine deals to, noting the two key differences to the first machine:

    - The machine deals anticlockwise, which changes the transformation matrix;

    - The machine deals from the point (2,1), not the origin, so each position vector will be translated by the vector $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ after the rotation is applied.

    Using the transformation matrix for an anticlockwise rotation about the origin, which can be found in the formula booklet, we have 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    p_7^x &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{7} & -\sin \frac{2\pi}{7} \\ \sin \frac{2\pi}{7} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{7} \end{pmatrix}^{x-1} \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Where $p_7^x$ is the position vector of the card dealt to player $x$ by the second machine when there are 7 players.

    Hence, the position vector of the card dealt to player 4 is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    p_7^4 &= \begin{pmatrix} \cos \frac{2\pi}{7} & -\sin \frac{2\pi}{7} \\ \sin \frac{2\pi}{7} & \cos \frac{2\pi}{7} \end{pmatrix}^{3} \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[15pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix} 1.639... \\ 1.173... \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We need to find the distance between the points $(-0.3603...,-0.1735...)$ (from part(c)) and $(1.639...,1.173...)$. This is shown in the following diagram as the red dashed line.

    ![ai1237b](images/ai1237b.png)

    Using the formula for the distance between two points, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d &= \sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2} \\[8pt]

    &= \sqrt{(-0.3603...-1.639...)^2+(-0.1735...-1.173...)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &= 2.411...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The distance between the point the card is dealt to for the 4th player by the first machine, and the point the card is dealt to for the 4th player by the second machine is $2.41\,\text{m}\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
