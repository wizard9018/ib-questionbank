# AI523

**Maximum mark: 6**

A linear transformation maps the point A$(2,3)$ to
A $^\prime(2,-5)$ and the point B$(-2,1)$ to
B $^\prime(6,9)$. Determine the linear
transformation, giving your answer in the form
$\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$ where $\mathbf{A}$ is a $2\times2$
matrix.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let
$\mathbf{A} = \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}$
be the transformation matrix where $a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{R}$.

Using the position vectors of A and
A $^\prime$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2a + 3b \\ 2c + 3d \end{pmatrix*}
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, using the position vectors of B and
B $^\prime$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 6 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 6 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-2a+b \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-2c+d \end{pmatrix*}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, using the vector equations $(1)$-$(2)$, we deduce

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 &= 2a + 3b  \\[3pt]
6 &= \phantom{2}b - 2a  \\[6pt]
&\hspace{0.25em}\& \\[6pt]
-5 &= 2c + 3d  \\[3pt]
9 &= \phantom{2}d - 2c
\end{aligned}
$$
Solving the system of
linear equations $(3)$-$(4)$ for $a$ and $b$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{7.9em} a = -2 \hspace{0.75em}\&\hspace{0.75em} b = 2 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, solving the system of linear equations $(5)$-$(6)$ for $c$
and $d$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{7.9em} c = -4 \hspace{0.75em}\&\hspace{0.75em} d = 1 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore we find
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}' = \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-2 & 2 \\ \hspace{-0.1em}-4 & 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}\mathbf{x} \hspace{3em}
\end{aligned}
$$
