# AI518

**Maximum mark: 6**

Parallelogram ABCD is translated by the vector
$\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}$, then enlarged by a scale factor of  $\dfrac{3}{2}$ from the origin and then reflected in the $y$-axis.

1.  Determine a single transformation that maps the parallelogram ABCD
    to its image. Give your answer in the form\
    $\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$. [4 marks]

The area of the image is equal to $k$ times the area of parallelogram
ABCD.

2.  Find the value of $k$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The effect of translating the parallelogram by the vector $\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}$ can be shown as
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{x} + \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The matrices
    $\mathbf{E} = \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*} \frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}$
    and
    $\mathbf{R} = \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] \hspace{-0.1em}-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}$
    represent the enlargement

    with a scale factor of $3/2$ and the reflection in the $y$-axis
    respectively.

    The enlargement followed by the reflection is represented by

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{R}\mathbf{E} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] \hspace{-0.1em}-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*} \frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{1em} \\[10pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*} \hspace{-0.1em}-\frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ \hspace{0.5em}0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{x}' &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-\frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ \hspace{0.5em}0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}\left(\mathbf{x} + \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\right) \\[10pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-\frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ \hspace{0.5em}0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}\mathbf{x} + \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-\frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ \hspace{0.5em}0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    And therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \boxed{\mathbf{x}' = \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] \hspace{-0.1em}-\frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ \hspace{0.5em}0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.1em}\mathbf{x} + \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -\frac{3}{2} \\ 6 \end{pmatrix*}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  For a transformation $\mathbf{T}$, we can use the formula
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Area}_{image} = |\text{det}(\mathbf{T})| \times \text{Area}_{pre-image}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We know that in this case, $\text{Area}_{image} = k \times \text{Area}_{pre-image}$ and our transformation matrix is $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{E}$.

    Hence $k = |\text{det}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{E})|$.

    Using the formula for the determinant of a $2\times2$ matrix, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{det}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{E}) &= \left|\begin{matrix*} -\frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ \hspace{0.5em}0 & \hspace{-0.25em}\frac{3}{2} \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[6pt]
    &= (-\frac{3}{2})(\frac{3}{2}) - (0)(0) \\[10pt]
    &= -\dfrac{9}{4}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     k &= |\text{det}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{E})| \hspace{0.8em} \\[10pt]
     &= \dfrac{9}{4}
     \end{aligned}
     $$
