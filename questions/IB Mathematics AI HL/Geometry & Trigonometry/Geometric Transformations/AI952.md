# AI952

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the geometric transformation

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$'} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 & a \\[2pt] 3 & b-3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} + \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} b \\[2pt] a-9 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}.
\end{aligned}
$$

that maps points from $(x,y)$ to $(x',y')$.

It is given that the transformation maps the point $(4,-4)$ to the point
$(-4,-11)$.

1.  Show that $a = 6$ and $b = 8$. [2 marks]

2.  Given that the transformation maps the point $(p,q)$ to itself, find
    the value of $p$ and the value of $q$. [3 marks]

A rectangle $R$ with vertices lying on the $xy$-plane undergoes this
transformation.

3.  Show that the area of the image is three times the size of
    $R$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting $a = 6$, $b = 8$, $x = 4$ and $y = -4$ into the
    definition of the transformation, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 & 6 \\[2pt] 3 & 5 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} \hspace{0.2em}4 \\[2pt] -4 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} + \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 8 \\[2pt] -3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \hspace{2em} \\[8pt]
    %
    &= \bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} -12 \\[2pt] -8 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} + \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 8 \\[2pt] -3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \\[8pt]
    %
    &=\bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} -4 \\[2pt] -11 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The coordinates of the image are $(-4,-11)$, verifying that
     $a=6\text{ and }b=8$

2.  Substituting $x'=x = p$ and $y'=y = q$ into the transformation, we
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$p$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$q$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 & 6 \\[2pt] 3 & 5 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$p$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$q$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} + \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 8 \\[2pt] -3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \\[8pt]
    %
    \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$p$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$q$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$3p + 6q$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$3p + 5q$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} + \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 8 \\[2pt] -3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \\[8pt]
    %
    \bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} -8 \\[2pt] 3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.05em} &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$2p + 6q$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$3p + 4q$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using vector equation $(1)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2p + 6q &= -8  \\[11pt]
    3p + 4q &= 3 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, solving the system of
    linear equations $(2)$--$(3)$ for $p$ and $q$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    p = 5 \hspace{0.7em}&\hspace{0.3em}\&\hspace{1.0em}q = -3 \hspace{3em}[\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the formula for the determinant of $2\times2$ matrix, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \det\hspace{0.05em}(T)\hspace{-0.05em} &= \bigg|\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 & 6 \\[2pt] 3 & 5 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg| \\[8pt]
    %&= (3)(5) - (3)(6) \\[6pt]
    &= -3 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the formula relating the area
    of the rectangle with its image,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{area}\hspace{0.05em}(T(R)\hspace{-0.05em})\hspace{-0.05em} &= |\hspace{-0.1em}\det\hspace{0.05em}(T)|\hspace{-0.05em}\times\text{area}\hspace{0.05em}(R) \\[12pt]
    %\text{area}\hspace{0.05em}(T(R)\hspace{-0.05em})\hspace{-0.05em} &= |\hspace{-0.15em}-3|\hspace{-0.05em}\times\text{area}\hspace{0.05em}(R) \\
    &\hspace{-4.9em}\text{area}\hspace{0.05em}(T(R))\hspace{-0.05em} = 3\times\text{area}\hspace{0.05em}(R\hspace{-0.05em})
    \end{aligned}
    $$
