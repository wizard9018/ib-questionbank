# AI1024

**Maximum mark: 18**

The Voronoi diagram below shows four hotels in a small town represented by points with coordinates $\mathrm{A}(-4,4)$, $\mathrm{B}(3,5)$, $\mathrm{C}(3,-3)$, and $\mathrm{D}(-1,3)$. The vertices $\mathrm{V}_1$, $\mathrm{V}_2$ and $\mathrm{V}_3$ are also shown. Distances in the direction of the $x$ and $y$ axes are measured in increments of $100$ metres.

![AI1024a](images/AI1024a.PNG)

1. Find the midpoint of AD. [2 marks]

2. Hence, find the equation of the line that passes through $\mathrm{V}_1$ and $\mathrm{V}_2$. [4 marks]

The equation of line that passes through $\mathrm{V}_1$ and $\mathrm{V}_3$ is $y=-2x+6$.

3. Find the coordinates of $\mathrm{V}_1$. [3 marks]

The coordinates of $\mathrm{V}_2$ are $(-5,-4)$ and the coordinates of $\mathrm{V}_3$ are $(2.5,1)$.

4. Find the distance from $\mathrm{V}_1$ to $\mathrm{V}_2$. Give your answer to the nearest metre. [2 marks]

5. Given that the distance from $\mathrm{V}_1$ to $\mathrm{V}_3$ is $783$ metres, find the angle $\mathrm{V_2}\widehat{\mathrm{V}}_1\mathrm{V}_3$. Give your answer to the nearest degree. [4 marks]

6. Hence, find the area of the Voronoi cell containing hotel $\mathrm{D}$, giving your answer in $\text{m}^2$, to three significant figures. [2 marks]

The manager of hotel $\mathrm{D}$ believes that the larger the area of triangle $ \mathrm{V}_1\mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{V}_3$, the more people will stay at hotel $\mathrm{D}$.

7. State one criticism of the manager's belief. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let M be the midpoint of $\mathrm{AD}$. Using the formula for the coordinates of a midpoint with the points $\mathrm{A}(-4,4)$ and $\mathrm{D}(-1,3)$, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \textrm M &= \left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2} 2,\dfrac{y_1 + y_2} 2\right) \\[8pt]

    &= \left(\dfrac {-4 + (-1)} 2,\dfrac{4 + 3}2\right) \\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-1em}\boxed{\textrm M = \left(-2.5,3.5\right)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The line passing through $\mathrm{V}_1$ and $\mathrm{V}_2$ is the perpendicular bisector of $\mathrm{AD}$. Therefore it passes through the midpoint of $\mathrm{AD}$, which we have from part (a), $\mathrm{M} = (-2.5,3.5)$. 

    The gradient will be the negative reciprocal of the gradient of $\mathrm{AD}$. Therefore we must find the gradient of $\mathrm{AD}$. Using the gradient formula, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    m &= \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \\[8pt]

    m &= \dfrac {3 - 4}{-1-(-4)} \\[8pt]

    & = -\dfrac{1}{3}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence the gradient of the line passing through $\mathrm{V}_1$ and $\mathrm{V}_2$ is $3$. 

    Using the formula for the equation of a straight line, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y - y_1     & = m(x - x_1)\\[8pt]

    y - 3.5     & = 3(x - (-2.5))\\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-1.85mm}\boxed{y = 3x + 11}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. $\mathrm{V}_1$ is the point of intersection of the lines $y = 3x + 11$ and $y = -2x + 6$. Hence, at this point, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    3x + 11 & = -2x + 6\\[11pt]

    5x & = -5  \\[11pt]

    x &= -1

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting the value $x = -1$ into the equation $y = 3x + 11$, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y & = 3(-1) + 11\\[11pt]

    &= 8

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore the coordinates of $\mathrm{V}_1$ are $ (-1,8)$

4. Using the formula for the distance between two points with $\mathrm{V}_1(-1,8)$ and $\mathrm{V}_2(-5,-4)$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d &= \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}\\[11pt]

    &= \sqrt{(-1 - (-5))^2 + (8 - (-4))^2}\\[11pt]

    &= 12.649...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Given that that each unit is equivalent to 100 metres, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d &= 12.649... \times 100\\[11pt]

    &\approx 1265

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    To the nearest metre, the distance from $\mathrm{V}_1$ to $\mathrm{V}_2$ is $1265 \text{ metres}$

5. $\mathrm{V_2}\widehat{\mathrm{V}}_1\mathrm{V}_3$ is an angle in the triangle formed by points $\mathrm{V}_1$, $\mathrm{V}_2$ and $\mathrm{V}_3$, as shown:

    ![AI1024b](images/AI1024b.PNG)

    We have $\overline{\mathrm{V}_1\mathrm{V}_2} = 1265 \text{ m}$ and $\overline{\mathrm{V}_1\mathrm{V}_3} = 783 \text{ m}$. 

By using the formula for the distance between two points and then multiplying by $100$, we can find $\overline{\mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{V}_3}$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \overline{\mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{V}_3} &= \sqrt{(-5 - 2.5)^2 + (-4 - 1)^2} \times 100\\[11pt]

    &\approx 901.4 \text{ m}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence, we can use the cosine rule to find $\mathrm{V_2}\widehat{\mathrm{V}}_1\mathrm{V}_3$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{(1265)^2 + (783)^2 - (901.4)^2}{2(1265)(783)}\\[11pt]

    &\approx 0.7071\\[11pt]

    \theta &\approx \cos^{-1}(0.7071)\\[11pt]

    &\hspace{-3.5em}\boxed{\mathrm{V_2}\widehat{\mathrm{V}}_1\mathrm{V}_3 \approx 45^\circ}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. Using the area rule for triangles, with $a = 1265$, $b = 783$ and $\mathrm{C} = 45^\circ$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A &= \dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin{\mathrm{C}} \\[11pt]

    &= \dfrac{1}{2}(1265)(783)\sin{45^\circ}\\[11pt]

    &\hspace{-1em}\boxed{A \approx  350\hspace{0.15em}000\hspace{0.25em}\text{m}^2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

7. There are several correct answers, for example:

    People who stay in hotels are likely to come from out of town, and if their trip is planned, they might choose the best value, or highest rated hotel, which may not be D.

    People coming from out of town without a hotel reservation are likely to stay at the first hotel they come across. This could make hotel D the least likely choice.
