# AI1133

**Maximum mark: 6**

The following Voronoi diagram shows the sites of four cell phone towers situated in a city centre. There are two edges missing from the Voronoi diagram.

In the diagram, 1 unit represents 1 kilometre.

![ai1133a](images/ai1133a.JPG)

1. Complete the Voronoi diagram. [2 marks]

Alex is standing at the point with coordinates $(6,3)$. He walks in a straight line to meet his friend at the point with coordinates $(3,0)$.

2. Find the coordinates of the point on his route at which he is equidistant from two cell phone towers. [4 marks]

2.  We can sketch Alex's route on the diagram

    ![a1133v2](images/a1133v2.png)

    The point marked $X$ is equidistant from cell towers $C$ and $D$. To find the coordinates of $X$ we need to find the intersection point of the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $CD$ and  the line Alex is walking.
    $$
     \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    $$

    Let's begin with the perpendicular bisector of $CD$.

    The line CD is shown below in blue

    ![Screenshot 2026-06-05 095703](<images/Screenshot 2026-06-05 095703.JPEG>)

    The gradient of the blue line is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        m_{CD}&=\frac{1-2}{6-2}\\[9pt]
        &=-\frac{1}{4}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore the perpendicular bisector will have a gradient of $4$.

    Now we must find the mid-point of $CD$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        M_{CD}&=\left(\frac{6+2}{2}, \frac{1+2}{2}\right)\\[9pt]
        &=(4, 1.5)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the formula and the information we have just found we can write an equation for the perpendicular bisector of $CD$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y-1.5&=4(x-4)\\
        y&=4x-14.5 \hspace{5em}(1) \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now we can find the equation of the line describing Alex's route shown in red

    ![Screenshot 2026-06-05 095804](<images/Screenshot 2026-06-05 095804.JPEG>)

    Using formulae from the formula booklet we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y-0&=\left(\tfrac{3-0}{6-3}\right)(x-3)\\[9pt]
        y&=x-3\hspace{7em}(2) \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The coordinates of $X$ are the solutions to system of linear equations $(1)$ and $(2)$. Hence using a G.D.C. the coordinates of the point equidistant from $C$ and $D$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \hspace{0.575em}\boxed{(3.83, 0.833)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note, the exact value of point $X$ is $(\tfrac{23}{6}, \tfrac{5}{6})$.
