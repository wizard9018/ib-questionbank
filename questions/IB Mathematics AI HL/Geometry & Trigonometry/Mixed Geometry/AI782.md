# AI782

**Maximum mark: 15**

Melissa is playing with a geometric wood puzzle. She can join two triangles, shown in the following figure as $\triangle\textrm{PQS}$ and $\triangle\textrm{QRS}$, along a common side, $\textrm{QS}$. It is given that $\textrm{PS}=14\,$cm, $\textrm{QR}=12\,$cm, $ \textrm{Q}\hat{\textrm{P}}\textrm{S}=66.8^\circ$, and $ \textrm{P}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{S}=52.9^\circ$.

Note that $\triangle\textrm{PQS}$ and $\triangle\textrm{QRS}$ are $\textbf{not}$ congruent.

![55e362d68d63d46c8cf9f6d1059ef564a25c999d.svg](images/55e362d68d63d46c8cf9f6d1059ef564a25c999d.svg)

1. Calculate the length of QS. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [3 marks]

It is given that T is the midpoint of QS and RT$=11\,$cm.

2. Hence calculate $\textrm{T}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{R}$. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [4 marks]

3. Calculate the area of triangle QRT.[3 marks]

Melissa cuts a cardboard circle of a radius of $11\,$cm and puts the two triangles so the point T lies on the centre and point R is on the circumference of the circle.

4. Show that the point P lies outside the circle. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. We can select the sine rule, because the length we are trying to find, QS, is opposite a known angle, and we have another known side, PS, opposite a known angle.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the sine rule, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\textrm{QS}}{\sin\left( \textrm{Q}\hat{\textrm{P}}\textrm{S}\right)} &= \dfrac{\textrm{PS}}{\sin\left( \textrm{P}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{S}\right)} \\[7pt]
    \textrm{QS} &= \dfrac{14 \sin(66.8^\circ)}{\sin(52.9^\circ)} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &= 16.133... \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 16.13\,\text{(2 d.p.)}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. We can select the cosine rule, because we know the lengths of all three sides in triangle QRT.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    QR = 12, RT = 11, and from part (a) we can determine that $\mathrm{QT}=\frac{16.13}{2}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the cosine rule, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos\left(\textrm{T}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{R}\right) &= \dfrac{\textrm{QT}^2+\textrm{QR}^2-\textrm{RT}^2}{2(\textrm{QT})(\textrm{QR})} \\[9pt]
    %\cos\left(\textrm{T}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{R}\right) &= \dfrac{(16.134/2)^2+12^2-11^2}{2(16.134/2)(12)} \\[8pt]
    \textrm{T}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{R} &= \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\left(\frac{16.13}{2}\right)^2+12^2-11^2}{2\left(\frac{16.13}{2}\right)(12)}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[11pt]
    &= 62.943...\\[11pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 62.94^\circ\,\text{(2 d.p.)}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. We can use the area rule for triangles, because we have one known angle, and we know the length of the two adjacent sides.

    We have been given QR = 12. From part (b), $\textrm{T}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{R}=62.94^\circ$.

    From part (a) we can determine that $\mathrm{QT}=\frac{16.13}{2}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the area formula for a triangle given two sides and the angle between them, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_{\triangle \textrm{QRT}} &= \dfrac{1}{2}(\textrm{QT})(\textrm{QR})\sin(\textrm{T}\hat{\textrm{Q}}\textrm{R}) \\[7pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\frac{16.13}{2}\right)(12)\sin(62.94^\circ) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &= 43.09...\\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 43.1\hspace{0.15em}\textrm{cm}^2\,\text{(3 s.f.)}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4. To show that point P is outside the circle, we must show that length $\text{PT}>11$.

    If we consider triangle PST, we have 2 known side lengths. PS = 14 and $\mathrm{ST}=\frac{16.13}{2}$. We have no known angles, however we can use the fact that the sum of the angles in a triangle is $180^\circ$ to determine the value of $\textrm{P}\hat{\textrm{S}}\textrm{Q}$. We have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textrm{P}\hat{\textrm{S}}\textrm{Q} &= 180 - 66.8 - 52.9 \\[8pt]
    &= 60.3^\circ \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We now have enough information to use the cosine rule to calculate length PT. We have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{PT}^2 &= \mathrm{PS}^2 + \mathrm{ST}^2 - 2(\mathrm{PS})(\mathrm{ST})\cos\left(\textrm{P}\hat{\textrm{S}}\textrm{Q}\right) \\[8pt]
    &= 14^2 + \left(\frac{16.13}{2}\right)^2 - 2(14)\left(\frac{16.13}{2}\right)\cos \left(60.3^\circ\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &= 149.15... \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[8pt]
    \mathrm{PT} &= \sqrt{149.15...} \\[8pt]
    &= 12.21...\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the point does not lie inside the circle because the length PT is greater than $11\,$cm.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
