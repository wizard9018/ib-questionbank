# AI1206

**Maximum mark: 8**

Samira is designing a running track for the $200$ metre race of her school sports day.

The $200$ metre track is formed by two straight lines of $75\,$m each and a semi-circular part of $50\,$m.

    

Here is her initial design.

![AD113_q1](images/AD113_q1.JPEG)

$d$ is the diameter of the semi-circular part of the track.

1.  Find the value of $d$. [2 marks]

Samira wants to add a second lane to the track. 

The width of each lane is ${90}\,$ centimetres.

    

The runner in lane $1$ starts at Start$_1$ and follows the solid line around the track, while the runner in lane $2$ starts at Start$_2$ and follows the dashed line around the track.

    

This is shown below.

    

![AD113_qb](images/AD113_qb.JPEG)

In order to make sure both lanes are exactly $200\,$ metres in length, Samira must move Start$_2$ forward $k\,$ metres.

2.  Find the value of $k$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We can use the formula for the circumference of a circle.

    We know the perimeter of the semi-circle is $50$ metres, therefore the circle has a circumference of $100$ metres. The formula for the circumference of a circle is $C = 2\pi r$, where $r$ is the radius. We have to find $d$, the diameter, which is twice the radius. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    C &= 2 \pi r \\[6pt]

        &=\pi \times d\\[6pt]

        100&=\pi \times d\\[6pt]

        d&=\frac{100}{\pi} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

        &=31.83...\\[6pt]

        d&=31.8\,\text{metres} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2.  The path followed by the runner in lane 2 is shown below, split into 3 different parts. 

    ![ad113_a1b](images/ad113_a1b.JPEG)

    The $\text{\textcolor{green}{green  dotted line}}$ is the finishing straight, and this is $75\,\text{m}$.

    The $\text{\textcolor{blue}{blue  dotted line}}$ is the starting straight, and this is $(75-k)\,\text{m}$. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The $\text{\textcolor{red}{red  dotted line}}$ is half the circumference of a circle. 

    The sum of these three parts must be exactly $200\,\text{m}$, so that both runners run the same distance.

   

    We know from part (a) that $d=31.83...\,\text{m}$, and we have been told that each lane is $90\,\text{cm}$ wide, which is $0.9\,\text{m}$. 

    So we are looking for half the circumference of a circle that has a diameter of $d = 0.9+0.9+31.83...\,\text{m}$. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the formula for the circumference of a circle, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        C &= 2 \pi r \\[6pt]

        &=\pi \times d\\[6pt]

        &=\pi \times (0.9+0.9+31.83...)\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

        \frac{C}{2}&=\frac{\pi \times (0.9+0.9+31.83...)}{2}\\[6pt]

        &=52.82... \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now form an equation using the three separate parts from the diagram.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{\textcolor{green}{green  dotted line}} + \text{\textcolor{blue}{blue  dotted line}} + \text{\textcolor{red}{red  dotted line}} &= 200

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting in our values from above, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    75 + (75-k) + 52.82... &= 200 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    k &= 2.827...\quad \quad \quad [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-0.65em}\boxed{k = 2.83\,\text{m}\,\text{(3 s.f.)}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}    

    $$
