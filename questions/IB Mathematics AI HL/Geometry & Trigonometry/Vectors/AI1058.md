# AI1058

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider two lines in three-dimensional space.

Line $L_1$ has vector equation $\bm{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[r] b\color{white}{1} \\ 0\color{white}{1} \\ -2\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} + \lambda \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}$

Line $L_2$ has vector equation $\bm{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] {4} \\ -\sqrt{6} \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*} + \mu \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ \sqrt{6} \\ a \end{pmatrix*}$

Where $a,b \in \mathbb{R}^+$.

The acute angle between the two lines is $60^{\circ}$.

1. Find the value of $a$.[4 marks]

2. Determine the coordinates of the point at which the two lines intersect.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let $\bm{v} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}$, the direction vector of $L_1$.

    Let $\bm{w} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ \sqrt{6} \\ a \end{pmatrix*}$, the direction vector of $L_2$.

    Using the formula for the angle between two vectors, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{v_1w_1 + v_2w_2 + v_3w_3}{|\bm{v}||\bm{w}|} \\[8pt]

    \cos 60^{\circ} &= \dfrac{(1)(1) + (0)(\sqrt{6}) + (1)(a)}{\sqrt{1^2+0^2+1^2}\sqrt{1^2 + \left(\sqrt{6}\right)^2+a^2}} \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{1}{2} &= \dfrac{a+1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{a^2+7}} \\[8pt]

    \sqrt{2(a^2+7)} &= 2(a+1) \\[8pt]

    2(a^2+7) &= 4(a+1)^2 \\[8pt]

    2a^2 + 14 &= 4a^2 + 8a +4 \\[8pt]

    2a^2 + 8a -10 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    a &= -5 \text{ or } 1 \hspace{3em} \text{[using G.D.C.]}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Given that $a \in \mathbb{R}^+$, $\boxed{a=1}$

2. Given that $a=1$, we form three equations be equating the corresponding components of each line equation:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    b + \lambda &= 4 + \mu \tag*{[1]}\\[8pt]

    0 &= -\sqrt{6} +\sqrt{6}\mu \tag*{[2]}\\[8pt]

    -2 + \lambda &= -1 + \mu \tag*{[3]}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    From equation [2] we have that $\mu = 1$. Substituting this value into equations [1] and [3] we have 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    b + \lambda &= 5 \tag*{[4]} \\[8pt]

    -2 + \lambda &= 0 \tag*{[5]}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    From equation [5] we have that $\lambda = 2$ and hence from equation [4], $b=3$.

    $$\boxed{\text{The point at which the two lines intersect has coordinates $(5,0,0)$.}}$$
