# AI509

**Maximum mark: 6**

A civil engineer is measuring the area of sloped land to be used for a
residential property development project. She uses a total station
theodolite device to determine the coordinates of the corners of the
land and finds them to be $(15,25,9)$, $(20,5,5)$, $(-10,-10,2)$ and
$(-15,10,6)$ where the coordinates are in metres from a fixed origin
where she is standing.

Using vectors, determine the area of the land.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

If we draw the land, we have

![76b0bf85a84b0504150bfcf2ce0a256d5a284407.svg](images/76b0bf85a84b0504150bfcf2ce0a256d5a284407.svg)

Using the position vectors of A and B, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.5em} \vec{\text{AB}} &= \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
&= (20\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}) - (15\mathbf{i} + 25\mathbf{j} + 9\mathbf{k}) \\[6pt]
&= 5\mathbf{i} - 20\mathbf{j} - 4\mathbf{k}
\end{aligned}
$$
Similarly,
using the position vectors of A and D, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2.75em} \vec{\text{AD}} &= \vec{\text{OD}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
&= (-15\mathbf{i} + 10\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}) - (15\mathbf{i} + 25\mathbf{j} + 9\mathbf{k}) \\[6pt]
&= -30\mathbf{i} - 15\mathbf{j} - 3\mathbf{k}
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the
vector product formula, we find
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{7.25em} \vec{\text{AB}} \times \vec{\text{AD}} &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[c] \hspace{0.25em}\textbf{i} & \textbf{j} & \hspace{0.25em}\textbf{k} \\ \hspace{0.25em}5 & \hspace{-0.25em}-20 & \hspace{-0.25em}-4 \\ -30 & \hspace{-0.25em}-15 & \hspace{-0.25em}-3  \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[6pt]
&= [60-60]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [-15-120]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [-75-600]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[10pt]
&= 135\textbf{j} - 675\textbf{k} \hspace{2.8em}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the area of a parallelogram formula, we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\square \text{ABCD}} &= \big|\vec{\text{AB}} \times \vec{\text{AD}}\big| \\[8pt]
&= |135\textbf{j} - 675\textbf{k}| \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{0^2 + 135^2 + (-675)^2} \hspace{0.25em} \\[8pt]
&\approx 688 \text{ m}^2
\end{aligned}
$$
