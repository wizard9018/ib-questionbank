# AI955

**Maximum mark: 19**

Mia is riding a bicycle in a town square. Her position vector
from a fixed origin O at time $t$ seconds is modelled by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em} = \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$re^{kt}\sin t$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$re^{kt}\cos t$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $r$ and $k$ are non-zero constants to be determined. All distances
are in metres.

1.  Find the velocity vector at time $t$. [3 marks]

2.  Given that $r > 0$, show that the magnitude of the velocity vector
    at time $t$ is given by $re^{kt}\sqrt{k^2+1}$.

    [4 marks]

At time $t = 0$, the velocity vector is
 $\bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} \hspace{0.2em}6 \\[2pt] -2.4 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)$ .

3.  Find the value of $r$ and the value of $k$. [3 marks]

4.  Find Mia's speed after 4 seconds. [2 marks]

At point P, Mia is riding parallel to the $x$-axis for the first time.

5.  Calculate the distance from Mia's starting position to
    point P. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we differentiate $x$ with respect to $t$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x' &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[re^{kt}\sin t\big] \\[12pt]
    &= rke^{kt}\sin t + re^{kt}\cos t \hspace{3em} {[\text{by product rule}]} \\[12pt]
    &= re^{kt}[k\sin t + \cos t] \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we differentiate
    $y$ with respect to $t$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y' &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[re^{kt}\cos t\big] \\[12pt]
    &= rke^{kt}\cos t - re^{kt}\sin t \hspace{3em} {[\text{by product rule}]} \\[12pt]
    &= re^{kt}[k\cos t - \sin t]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{-8.4mm}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em} = \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{-0.015em}{$re^{kt}[k\sin t + \cos t]$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$re^{kt}[k\cos t - \sin t]$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \hspace{3.1em} \tag*{$(1)$}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the magnitude of a vector formula, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\bigg|\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg| = \sqrt{[\hspace{0.05em}x']^2 + [\hspace{0.05em}y']^2} \\[12pt]
    %
    &= \sqrt{[re^{kt}]^2[k\sin t + \cos t]^2 + [re^{kt}]^2[k\cos t - \sin t]^2} \hspace{3em} {[\text{by $(1)$}]} \\[12pt]
    %
    &= |r|e^{kt}\sqrt{[k^2\sin^2 t + \cancel{2k\sin t \cos t} + \cos^2 t] + [k^2\cos^2 t - \cancel{2k\cos t \sin t} + \sin^2 t]} \\[12pt]
    %
    &= re^{kt}\sqrt{k^2[\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t] + [\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t]}  \hspace{3em} {[\text{since $r > 0$}]}\\[12pt]
    %
    &\bigg|\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg| = re^{kt}\sqrt{k^2+1} \hspace{3em} {[\text{by Pythagorean identity}]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Substituting $t = 0$ into velocity vector $(1)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg|_{t=0} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{-0.015em}{$r[0 + 1]$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$r[k \hspace{0.03em}-\hspace{0.03em} 0]$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \\[12pt]
    %
    &= \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.175em}{$r$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$rk$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} \hspace{0.2em}6 \\[2pt] -2.4 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em} = \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.175em}{$r$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$rk$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \hspace{0.25em} &\Rightarrow \hspace{1em} r = 6\hspace{0.525em}\text{ and }\hspace{0.525em}k = -0.4
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using part (b), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg|\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg| &= 6e^{-0.4t}\sqrt{(-0.4)^2+1} \hspace{2.0em} \tag*{$(2)$}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, if we evaluate expression $(2)$ for $t = 4$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg|\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg| &= 6e^{-0.4(4)}\sqrt{(-0.4)^2+1} \hspace{1.3em} \\[12pt]
    %
    &\hspace{-3em}\bigg|\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$x'$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y'$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg| \approx 1.30 \text{ m\hspace{0.05em}s}^{-1}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Solving the equation $y' = 0$ for the smallest $t > 0$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    6e^{-0.4t}[-0.4\cos t - \sin t] &= 0 \\[12pt]
    t &\approx 2.761 \text{ seconds}
    \hspace{3em} \text{[by using G.D.C.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating
    $x$ for $t = 2.761$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x &= 6e^{-0.4(2.761)}\sin (2.761) \\[12pt]
    &\approx 0.7387 \text{ m}
    \hspace{3em} \text{[by using G.D.C.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating
    $y$ for $t = 2.761$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= 6e^{-0.4(2.761)}\cos (2.761) \\[12pt]
    &\approx -1.846 \text{ m}
    \hspace{3em} \text{[by using G.D.C.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, point P has coordinates $(0.7387,-1.846)$.\
    Mia's starting position is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em} &= \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{$6e^{-0.4(0)}\sin (0)$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{$6e^{-0.4(0)}\cos (0)$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) \\[12pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0em}{0} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0em}{6} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, Mia's starting position has coordinates $(0,6)$\
    Using the formula for the distance between two points, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    d &= \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2} \\[12pt]
    &= \sqrt{(0.7387-0)^2 + (-1.846-6)^2} \\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-0.5em}d \approx 7.88 \text{ m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
