# AI1189

**Maximum mark: 9**

The position vector of a particle, $P$, relative to a fixed origin $O$ at time t is given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

\overrightarrow{OP}=\begin{pmatrix}

t\,\sin t \\

t\,\cos t\\

\end{pmatrix}

\end{aligned}

$$

1. Show that $|OP| = t$.        [2 marks]

2. Show that the velocity vector of $P$ can be expressed as        [2 marks]

$$

\begin{aligned}

\textbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}

\sin t + t\cos t \\

\cos t - t\sin t \\

\end{pmatrix}

\end{aligned}

$$

3. Find an expression for the scalar product $\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \textbf{v}$             [2 marks]

4. Hence show that the angle between the position vector and the velocity vector is always $\theta= \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\right)$         [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the formula for the magnitude of a vector, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    |OP| &= \sqrt{(t\,\sin t)^2 + (t\,\cos t)^2} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]

    &= \sqrt{t^2\,\sin^2 t + t^2\,\cos^2 t} \\[6pt]

    &= \sqrt{t^2\left(\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t\right)} \\[6pt]

    &= \sqrt{t^2} \hspace{3em} [\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1] \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-2.3em}\boxed{|OP| = t} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The velocity vector is found by differentiating each component of the position vector. 

    Using the product rule, we can differentiate each component.

    $$                        

    \begin{aligned}                

    \tag*{\textbf{M1}}            

    \end{aligned}                

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(t\,\sin t\right) &= \sin t + t\cos t \\[6pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(t\,\cos t\right) &= \cos t - t\sin t \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-0.85em}\boxed{

    \textbf{v}=\begin{pmatrix}

    \sin t + t\cos t \\

    \cos t - t\sin t \\

    \end{pmatrix}

    } \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Using the formula for the scalar product, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \bm{v} \cdot \bm{w} &= v_1w_1 + v_2w_2 \\[6pt]

    \overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \textbf{v} &= (t\,\sin t)(\sin t + t\cos t) + (t\,\cos t)(\cos t - t\sin t) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

    &= t\,\sin^2 t + t^2\,\sin t \cos t + t\,\cos^2 t - t^2\,\sin t \cos t \\[6pt]

    &= t\,\sin^2 t + t\,\cos^2 t + \cancel{t^2\,\sin t \cos t} - \cancel{t^2\,\sin t \cos t} \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-3em}\boxed{\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \textbf{v} = t} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

4. Using the formula for the angle between two vectors, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{v_1w_1 + v_2w_2}{|\bm{v}||\bm{w}|} \\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \textbf{v}}{|OP||\textbf{v}|}

\end{aligned}

$$

We have expressions for $|OP|$ and $\overrightarrow{OP} \cdot \textbf{v}$ from parts (a) and (c) respectively. We have $\textbf{v}$ from part (b). Therefore

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{t}{t\sqrt{(\sin t + t\cos t)^2+(\cos t - t\sin t)^2}} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sin^2 t + 2t\,\sin t \cos t + t^2\, \cos^2 t + \cos^2 t - 2t\,\sin t \cos t + t^2\, \sin^2 t}} \\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + t^2 \left(\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t\right)}} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]

    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + t^2}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

\end{aligned}

$$
