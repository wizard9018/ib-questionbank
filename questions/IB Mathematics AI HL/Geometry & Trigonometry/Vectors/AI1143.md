# AI1143

**Maximum mark: 9**

A rectangular solar array is positioned on a fence to provide power to a house. It is fixed to the $2\hspace{0.1em}$m high fence at points A and B, and fixed to the ground by vertical poles from points C and D.

Two edges of the solar array can be represented by the vectors

$$

\begin{aligned}

\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}} = \begin{pmatrix} -2.4 \\ 4.16 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \hspace{1.5em}\text{and}\hspace{1.5em}\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}} = \begin{pmatrix} -1.04 \\ -0.6 \\ 1.6 \end{pmatrix}

\end{aligned}

$$

All distances are measured in metres.

![ai1143a](images/ai1143a.PNG)

1. Show that $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}}$ and $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}$ are perpendicular.[2 marks]

2. Calculate the vector product $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}} \times \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}$. [2 marks]

3. Hence find the area of the solar array.[2 marks]

The solar array should be mounted at an angle of between $50^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$ to the horizontal plane.

4. Verify that the array is mounted at a correct angle. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. If $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}}$ and $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}$ are perpendicular, the scalar product will equal zero.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{R1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}} \cdot \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}} &= (-2.4)(-1.04) + (4.16)(-0.6) + (0)(1.6) \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[8pt]

    &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\text{The scalar product is zero, therefore $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}}$ and $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}$ are perpendicular.}$$ 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Using the formula for the vector product, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}} \times \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}} &= \begin{pmatrix} (4.16)(1.6) - (-0.6)(0) \\ (0)(-1.04) - (-2.4)(1.6) \\ (-2.4)(-0.6) - (4.16)(-1.04) \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-4.55em}\boxed{\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}} \times \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 6.66 \\ 3.84 \\ 5.77 \end{pmatrix}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Note that a rectangle is a type of parallelogram, hence we can use the formula for the area of a parallelogram. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{Area} &= |\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AB}} \times \overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}| \\[8pt]

    &= \sqrt{6.66^2 + 3.84^2 + 5.77^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-2.4em}\boxed{\mathrm{Area} \approx 9.61\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}^2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. We can construct a triangle between points A and D, and a point E on the vertical pole in the same horizontal plane as point A:

    ![ai1143b](images/ai1143b.PNG)

    It is sufficient to find the size of angle $\mathrm{D\hat{A}E}$.

    The magnitude of vector $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}$, $\sqrt{(-1.04)^2+(-0.6)^2+1.6^2}=2$ tells us that $\mathrm{AD}=2.00\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The $z$ component of vector $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AD}}$ tells us that point D is $1.6\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}$ higher than point A. Hence $\mathrm{DE}=1.6\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}$.

    Updating our diagram, we have

    ![ai1143c](images/ai1143c.PNG)

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \theta &= \mathrm{sin}^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1.6}{2}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &\approx 53.1^{\circ} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The angle between the array and the horizontal plane is approximately $53.1^{\circ}$, which lies between $50^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$. The array is mounted at a correct angle.
