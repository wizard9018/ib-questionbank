# AI498

**Maximum mark: 14**

Jack is a student pilot flying a Cessna $172$ Skyhawk and his flight is
being monitored by the air traffic control centre. The aircraft's
position is given by the coordinates $(x, y, z)$, where $x$ and $y$ are
the aircraft's displacement east and north of the airport,
and $z$ is the height of the aircraft above the
ground. All displacements are given in kilometres.

The aircraft's velocity is given by the vector
$\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -160 \\ -64 \\ -12 \end{pmatrix*}$
km h$^{-1}$.

At 11 : 00 the air
traffic control centre detects Jack's aircraft at a position
$40$ km east and
$16$ km north of the airport, and at a height of
$4.5$ km. Let $t$ be the
length of time, in hours, from
11 : 00.

1.  Write down a vector equation for the aircraft's displacement,
    $\mathbf{r}$, in terms of $t$.[2 marks]

2.  Given that Jack's aircraft continues to fly at the same velocity,
    1.  verify that it will pass directly over the airport;

    2.  find the time at which this happens;
    3.  calculate its height at this point. [4 marks]

When Jack's aircraft is $3.3$ km above the
ground, it continues to fly on the same bearing but
adjusts the angle of descent so that it will land at the point
P$(0,0,0)$.

3.
    1.  Find the time at which the aircraft is
        $3.3$ km above the ground.
    2.  Find the direct distance of the aircraft from the airport at
        this point. [5 marks]

The aircraft's velocity, after the adjustment of the angle of descent,
is given by

the vector $\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -160 \\ -64 \\ p \end{pmatrix*}$
km h$^{-1}$.

4.  Find the value of $p$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    $\mathbf{r} = \hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 40 \\ 16 \\ 4.5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em} + \hspace{0.1em} t\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -160 \\ -64 \\ -12 \end{pmatrix*}$

2.
    1.  Using the equation from part (a), we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        x &= 40 - 160t  \hspace{2.0em} \\[6pt]
        y &= 16 - 64t  \\[6pt]
        z &= 4.5 - 12t
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Substituting $x = 0$
        into equation $(1)$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{6.4em} 0 &= 40 - 160t \\[6pt]
        t &= 0.25 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Evaluating equation $(2)$ for $t = 0.25$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y &= 16 - 64(0.25) \hspace{0.3em}\\[6pt]
        &= 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence $x = y = 0$ when $t = 0.25$.
        Therefore Jack's aircraft will pass directly over the airport.

    2.  Converting hours to minutes, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        t &= 0.25 \text{ hours} \\[6pt]
        &= 0.25(60) \\[6pt]
        &= 15 \text{ minutes} \hspace{1.1em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence Jack's
        aircraft will pass directly over the airport at
         $\text{11\hspace{-0.025em}:\hspace{-0.025em}15}$
    3.  Evaluating equation $(3)$ for $t = 0.25$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        z &= 4.5 - 12(0.25) \\[6pt]
        &= 1.5 \text{ km}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.
    1.  Substituting $z = 3.3$ in the equation $(3)$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{5.1em} 3.3 &= 4.5 - 12t \\[6pt]
        t &= 0.1 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Converting hours to minutes, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        t &= 0.1 \text{ hours} \\[6pt]
        &= 0.1(60) \\[6pt]
        &= 6 \text{ minutes} \hspace{1.6em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence Jack's
        aircraft is $3.3$ km above the ground at
         $\text{11\hspace{-0.025em}:\hspace{0.05em}06}$

    2.  Evaluating $\mathbf{r}$ for $t = 0.1$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.6em} \mathbf{r} &= \hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 40 \\ 16 \\ 4.5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em} + \hspace{0.1em} 0.1\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -160 \\ -64 \\ -12 \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
        &= \hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 24 \\ 9.6 \\ 3.3 \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, using the magnitude of a vector formula, we find

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        |\mathbf{r}| &= \sqrt{24^2 + 9.6^2 + 3.3^2} \\[6pt]
        &\approx 26.1 \text{ km}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  If we sketch Jack's flight from
    11 : 00 until he
    lands the aircraft at P, we have

    $$
