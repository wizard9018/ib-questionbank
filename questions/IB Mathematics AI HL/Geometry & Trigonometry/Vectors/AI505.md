# AI505

**Maximum mark: 14**

Emily starts flying her drone from the roof of a residential building.
The displacement of the drone at time $t$ seconds is given by the vector
equation

$$
\hspace{0.9em} \mathbf{r}_{\text{d}} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 0 \\ 0 \\ 42 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 6 \\ 0.4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.75em} t \geq 0.
$$

where distances are measured in metres.

1.  Find the position vector of Emily's drone one minute after takeoff.
    [1 mark]

Frank starts flying his quadcopter (a type of drone) from a tennis
court. The displacement of the quadcopter at time $s$ seconds is given
by the vector equation

$$
\hspace{2.1em} \mathbf{r}_{\text{q}} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -420 \\ -360 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}s\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 10 \\ 12 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.75em} s \geq 0.
$$

where distances are are also measured in metres.

2.  Find the distance between Emily and Frank. [2 marks]

3.  Determine if the two drone flight paths intersect, and if
    so, write down the point of intersection. [4 marks]

Emily's drone and Frank's quadcopter started flying at the same time.

4.  State whether the two drones actually collide. Justify your
    answer. [2 marks]

5.
    1.  Find the time when Emily's drone is closest to Frank's
        quadcopter.

    2.  Calculate the minimum distance between the two drones.
        [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Evaluating $\mathbf{r}_{\text{d}}$ at $t = 60$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.1em} \mathbf{r}_{\text{d}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 0 \\ 0 \\ 42 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}60\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 6 \\ 0.4 \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
    &=\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 240 \\ 360 \\ 66 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.25em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Emily is}\hspace{0.5em} &\text{at}\hspace{0.5em} \text{P}(0,0,42) \\[6pt]
    \text{Frank is}\hspace{0.5em} &\text{at}\hspace{0.5em} \text{Q}(-420,-360,0) \hspace{3em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the distance between two points formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.8em} \text{PQ} &= \sqrt{(-420-0)^2 + (-360-0)^2 + (0-42)^2} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 555 \text{ m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the vector equation
    $\mathbf{r}_{\text{d}} = \mathbf{r}_{\text{q}}$, we deduce
    $$
    \hspace{9.1em}\begin{array}{rcrclcl}
    && 4t &=& -420 &+& 10s \hspace{13.6em}(1) \\[6pt]
    && 6t &=& -360 &+& 12s \hspace{13.6em}(2) \\[6pt]
    42 &+& 0.4t &=& s && \phantom{12s} \hspace{13.6em}(3)
    \end{array}
    $$
    Solving the system of linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$ for
    $s$ and $t$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.3em} s = 90 \hspace{0.75em}\&\hspace{0.75em} t = 120 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we try to verify equation $(3)$ for values $(4)$, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    42 + 0.4(120) &= 90 \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
    90 &= 90
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\text{Hence the two flight paths do intersect.}$
    Evaluating $\mathbf{r}_{\text{q}}$ at $s = 90$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1em} \mathbf{r}_{\text{q}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -420 \\ -360 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}90\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 10 \\ 12 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
    &=\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 480 \\ 720 \\ 90 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.25em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  We have $s = t$ at any time. Hence Frank's quadcopter passes the
    point of intersection $30$
    seconds earlier (at $s = 90$) than Emily's drone (at
    $t = 120$).

    $\text{Hence, the two drones do not collide.}$

5.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{r}_{\text{d}} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{q}} &= \left[\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 0 \\ 0 \\ 42 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 6 \\ 0.4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}\right] - \left[\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -420 \\ -360 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 10 \\ 12 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}\right] \\[6pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 420-6t \\ 360-6t \\ 42-0.6t \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the magnitude of a vector formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1em} d &= |\mathbf{r}_{\text{d}} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{q}}| \hspace{3em} [\text{distance b$/$n aircrafts}] \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{(420-6t)^2 + (360-6t)^2 + (42-0.6t)^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
