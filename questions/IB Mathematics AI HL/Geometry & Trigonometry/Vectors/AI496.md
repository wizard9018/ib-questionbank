# AI496

**Maximum mark: 13**

**In this question, distance is in metres.**

Jack and John are flying airplanes in a straight line at a constant
speed.

Jack's airplane passes through a point P. Its position, $t$ seconds
after it passes

through P, is given by
$\mathbf{r}_1 = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 5 \\ 8 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.05em}t\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*}$ ,
$t \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.
    1.  Write down the coordinates of P.

    2.  Find the speed of Jack's airplane in ms$^{-1}$. [3 marks]

2.  After six seconds, Jack's airplane passes through a point Q.
    1.  Find the coordinates of Q.

    2.  Find the distance the airplane has travelled during the six
        seconds. [4 marks]

John's airplane passes through a point R. Its position, $s$ seconds
after it passes

through R, is given by
$\mathbf{r}_2 = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 4 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.05em}s\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*}$ ,
$s \in \mathbb{R}$.

3.  Find the coordinates where the two airplanes intersect.
    [4 marks]

4.  Determine who is flying faster, Jack or John. Justify your
    answer. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  We have
        $\vec{\text{OP}} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 5 \\ 8 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}$ .
        Hence the coordinates of P are
         $(5,8,2)$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Jack's speed} &= \textstyle \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 2^2 + 3^2} \hspace{2.25em}  \\[10pt]
        &\approx 3.74 \text{ ms}^{-1}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  Evaluating $\mathbf{r}_1$ for $t= 6$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.5em} \vec{\text{OQ}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 5 \\ 8 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.05em}6\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
        &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 20 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix*} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the coordinates of Q are
         $(-1,20,20)$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{PQ}} &= \vec{\text{OQ}} - \vec{\text{OP}} \\[6pt]
        &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 20 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} - \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 5 \\ 8 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{1.15em} \\[6pt]
        &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -6 \\ 12 \\ 18 \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} \big|\vec{\text{PQ}}\big| &= \textstyle \sqrt{(-6)^2 + 12^2 + 18^2} \\[8pt]
        &\approx 22.4 \text{ m}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Using the vector equation $\mathbf{r}_1 = \mathbf{r}_2$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    5 - t &= 4 - s  \\[6pt]
    8 + 2t &= 4 + 5s  \\[6pt]
    2 + 3t &= 5 + 3s
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, solving the system
    of linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.95em} t = 3 \hspace{0.75em} \& \hspace{0.65em} s = 2 \hspace{2.25em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we try to verify the equation $(3)$ for values $(4)$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2 + 3(3) &= 5 + 3(2) \hspace{0.1em} \\[6pt]
    11 &= 11
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating $\mathbf{r}_2$ for $s= 2$, we
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.3em} \mathbf{r}_2 &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 4 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.05em}2\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 14 \\ 11 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the coordinates where the two airplanes intersect are
     $(2,14,11)$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{John's speed} &= \textstyle \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 5^2 + 3^2} \hspace{0.2em} \\[10pt]
    &\approx 5.92 \text{ ms}^{-1} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

     $\text{Since \(5.92 \text{ ms}^{-1} > 3.74 \text{ ms}^{-1}\), John is flying faster.}$
