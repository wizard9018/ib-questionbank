# AI765

**Maximum mark: 13**

The sunset time in Paris can be modelled by the function
$$
\mathrm{S}(t) = 1.85\sin\hspace{0.05em}(0.52\hspace{0.05em}t - 1.57) + 19.5
$$

where $t$ is time in months, from the beginning of January.

The sunrise time can similarly be modelled by
$$
\mathrm{R}(t) = 1.35\sin\hspace{0.05em}(0.52\hspace{0.05em}t - 4.7) + 7.5
$$

Both $\mathrm{S}(t)$ and $\mathrm{R}(t)$ are measured in hours since midnight.

$\mathrm{S}(t)$ can be expressed as $\mathrm{im}(z_1)+19.5$ and $\mathrm{R}(t)$ can be expressed as $\mathrm{im}(z_2)+7.5$, where $z_1,z_2 \in \mathbb{C}$.

1. Write down expressions for $z_1$ and $z_2$. Give your answers in the form $z_{1,2}=r\,e^{\theta i}$.[2 marks]

The number of daylight hours in Paris, $\mathrm{D}(t)$, is given by $\mathrm{D}(t)=\mathrm{S}(t)-\mathrm{R}(t)$.

2. Hence show that the number of hours of daylight in Paris can be modelled by the function
    $$
    \mathrm{D}(t)=3.20\sin\hspace{0.05em}(0.52t-1.57) + 12
    $$
    [6 marks]

3. According to the model, find:

    1. the highest number of hours of daylight in a year;

    2. The approximate number of months per year in which there is at least 14 hours of daylight. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Equating our function $\mathrm{S}(t)$ to $\mathrm{im}(z_1)+19.5$, we must have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{im}(z_1) &= 1.85 \sin (0.52t - 1.57)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    This is the imaginary part of the complex number

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z_1 &= 1.85 \cos (0.52t - 1.57) + i\left(1.85 \sin (0.52t - 1.57)\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    In the required form, we have $z_1 = 1.85e^{(0.52t - 1.57)i}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using similar reasoning with $\mathrm{R}(t)$, we have $z_2 = 1.35e^{(0.52t - 4.7)i}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{D}(t) &= \mathrm{S}(t) - \mathrm{R}(t) \\[8pt]
    &= \mathrm{im}(z_1) + 19.5 - \left(\mathrm{im}(z_2) + 7.5\right) \\[8pt]
    &= \mathrm{im}(z_1) - \mathrm{im}(z_2) + 12 \\[8pt]
    &= \mathrm{im}(z_1 - z_2) + 12 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Let's find $z_1 - z_2$ so that we can identify the imaginary part.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z_1 - z_2 &= 1.85e^{(0.52\hspace{0.05em}t-1.57)i} - 1.35e^{(0.52\hspace{0.05em}t-4.7)i} \\[8pt]
    &= 1.85e^{0.52ti-1.57i} - 1.35e^{0.52ti-4.7i} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &= 1.85\left(e^{0.52ti}\right)\left(e^{-1.57i}\right) - 1.35\left(e^{0.52ti}\right)\left(e^{-4.7i}\right) \\[8pt]
    &= e^{0.52ti}\left(1.85e^{-1.57i}-1.35e^{-4.7i}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We have now factorised the variable $t$ out of the brackets, allowing us to compute a single complex number inside the brackets.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= e^{0.52ti}\left(3.199...e^{-1.565...i}\right) \hspace{3em}\text{[by G.D.C.]} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &= 3.199...e^{0.52ti-1.565...i} \\[8pt]
    &= 3.199...e^{(0.52t-1.565...)i} \\[8pt]
    z_1 - z_2 &= 3.20e^{(0.52t-1.57)i} \,\text{(3 s.f.)} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{D}(t) &= \mathrm{im}\big[3.20e^{(0.52t-1.57)i}\big] + 12 \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 3.20\sin\hspace{0.05em}(0.52\hspace{0.05em}t-1.57) + 12} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. 1. According to the model, the highest number of hours of daylight is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{D_{max}} &= 3.20 + 12 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 15.2 \text{ hours}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    2. If we sketch the graph of $y = \mathrm{D}(t)$, and also the graph of $y=14$ [by using G.D.C.], we can find the points of intersection. Our solution is the domain for which $\mathrm{D}(t) > 14$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        ![complex](images/complex.JPG)

        We have

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        7.762... - 4.317... &= 3.444... \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        There are at least 14 hours of daylight for approximately $\text{3.44 months}$ of the year.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $\textbf{Note: An answer of 3 months is acceptable.}$
