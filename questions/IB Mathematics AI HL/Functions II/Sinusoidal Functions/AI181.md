# AI181

**Maximum mark: 15**

The water depth, $D$, in metres, at the entrance of a boating harbour
can be modelled by the function
$$
\begin{aligned}
D(t) &= -4.5\sin\hspace{0.15em}(\ang{30}\hspace{-0.15em}\times t) + 15, \hspace{0.5em} 0 \leq t \leq 24,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t$ is the elapsed time, in hours, since midnight.

1.  Determine the depth of water at midnight. [2 marks]

2.  The cycle of water depths repeats every T hours. Find the value
    of T. [2 marks]

3.
    1.  Write down the minimum and maximum depths of water
        during the day.

    2.  Find the times at which the minimum and maximum depths of water
        occur during the day.[4 marks]

4.  Draw the graph of $D$ versus $t$ on the grid below.[3 marks]

    ![5ebec9fd250b988a5cdb00654083b4ecdda5ad72.svg](images/5ebec9fd250b988a5cdb00654083b4ecdda5ad72.svg)

A ship with $10$ m draught is allowed to
    enter the harbour if the depth of $\text{water}$ is greater
    than $12$ m.

5.  Calculate the total amount of time
    **after** midday during which the ship is
    **not** allowed to enter the harbour. Give your answer to the nearest minute. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Midnight is the equivalent of time $t=0$. Substituting this value into our function we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    D(0) &= -4.5\,\sin (0) + 15 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
    &= 15
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The depth of water at midnight is $15\text{ m}$

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  In a general sine function of the form $y=a \sin (bx) + d$, the period is given by dividing 360 by $b$. In this case, the period is T hours and we have $b=30$. Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{T} &= \dfrac{360}{30} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
    &=12 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  The minimum and maximum values of the sine function are $-1$ and $+1$.
        Hence the minimum and maximum depths of water are
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        D_{\text{min}} &=  4.5(-1)+15 \hspace{0.2em} \\[6pt]
        &=10.5\text{ m} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[15pt]
        D_{\text{max}} &=  4.5(+1)+15 \\[6pt]
        &=19.5\text{ m}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Solving the equation $D(t) = 10.5$ for $t$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.2em} -4.5\sin\hspace{0.15em}(\ang{30}\hspace{-0.15em}\times t) + 15 &= 10.5 \\[6pt]
        t &= 3,\hspace{0.25em} 15 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the minimum depth of water occurs at
         $3 \text{ am}$
        and
         $3 \text{ pm}$.

         $$\\[-2.5em]$$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.1em} -4.5\sin\hspace{0.15em}(\ang{30}\hspace{-0.15em}\times t) + 15 &= 19.5 \\[6pt]
        t &= 9,\hspace{0.25em} 21 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]

    Identifying the two intersection points with $t>12$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    t_1 &= 13.39\ldots \\[6pt]
    t_2 &= 16.60\ldots \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since the ship is not allowed to enter when $D\leq12$, which is between these two times, the length of the time interval is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    t_2-t_1 &= 16.60\ldots-13.39\ldots \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
    &= 3.212\ldots\text{ hours}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Converting the decimal part to minutes and rounding to the nearest minute, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0.212\ldots\times60 &= 12.75\ldots\text{ minutes} \\[6pt]

    \end{aligned}
