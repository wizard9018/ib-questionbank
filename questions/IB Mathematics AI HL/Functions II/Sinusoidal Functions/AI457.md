# AI457

**Maximum mark: 6**

The Chicago Wheel, the world's first Ferris wheel, was designed and
built in 1893 by an American civil engineer, George W. G. Ferris Jr.

The height, in metres, above the ground of a passenger on the Chicago
Wheel after

$t$ minutes can be modelled by the function
$h(t) = 38\cos\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-10)\Big) + 42$.

1.  Find the maximum height reached by a passenger on the Chicago Wheel.
    [1 mark]

2.  Find the height above the ground of a passenger $12$ minutes after
    the ride has started. [1 mark]

3.  Find the time, in minutes, it takes for the Chicago Wheel to
    complete one $\text{rotation}$. [2 marks]

4.  Given that passengers only complete one rotation on the Chicago
    Wheel, calculate how long they are more than $65$ metres above the
    ground. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    h_{\text{max}} &= 38(1) + 42 \hspace{2.6em} \\[6pt]
    &= 80 \text{ m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Evaluating $h(t)$ for $t = 12$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} h(12) &= 38\cos\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(12-10)\Big) + 42 \\[6pt]
    &\approx 72.7 \text{ m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.1em} h_{\text{min}} &= 38\cos(-1) + 42 \\[6pt]
    &= 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, solving the equation
    $h(t) = h_{\text{min}}$ for the smallest $t > 0$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    38\cos\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-10)\Big) + 42 &= 4 \hspace{8.5em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{15em} t = 20 \text{ min} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    $$
