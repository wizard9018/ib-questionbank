# AI1239

**Maximum mark: 12**

Consider the following diagram modelling the height of a wave as it approaches a beach.

![ai1238](images/ai1238.png)

The height of the wave, in metres, can be modelled by the function 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    h_1 = a \sin (bt) + d

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $a,b,d \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $t$ is time in seconds.

1. Find the value of

    1. $d$;

    2. $a$;

    3. $b$. [4 marks]

2. Hence determine the length of time that the height of the wave is more than 4 metres in the first two seconds. [3 marks]

A second wave can be modelled as $h_2=3\sin (11t-1.1)+3$. 

The cumulative effect of the two waves, $h_T$, can be modelled as $h_T = h_1+h_2$.

3. Given that $h_T=\text{im}(z_1+z_2)+6$, where $z_1$ and $z_2$ are complex functions of $t$, find an expression for $h_T$. Give your answer in the form 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h_T &= p\sin(11t - q) + 6

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Where $p,q \in \mathbb{R}^+$. [5 marks]

3. First let's write an expression $h_T=h_1+h_2$ using the function $h_1$ we found in part (a), and the function $h_2$ we were given. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h_T &= h_1 + h_2 \\[8pt]

    &= 2 \sin (11t) + 3 + 3\sin (11t-1.1)+3 \\[8pt]

    h_T &= 2 \sin (11t) + 3\sin (11t-1.1) + 6

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have been asked to use the fact that $h_T=\text{im}(z_1+z_2)+6$ to find our solution, so let's compare the two expressions.

    First, note that $\text{im}(z_1+z_2)=\text{im}(z_1)+\text{im}(z_2)$. This is true for all complex numbers and all complex functions.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h_T &= \color{red}{\text{im}(z_1)}\color{black}{\,+\,}\color{green}{\text{im}(z_2)}\color{black}{\,+\,6} \\[8pt]

    h_T &= \color{red}{2 \sin (11t)}\color{black}{\,+\,}\color{green}{3\sin (11t-1.1)}\color{black}{\,+\,6}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Comparing the two expressions, $\color{red}{2 \sin (11t)}$ must be the imaginary part of $\color{red}{z_1}$, and $\color{green}{3 \sin (11t-1.1)}$ must be the imaginary part of $\color{green}{z_2}$.

    In polar form, $2 \sin (11t)$ is the imaginary part of the complex number 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z &= 2 \left(\cos (11t) + \text{i}\sin (11t)\right)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    And $3 \sin (11t-1.1)$ is the imaginary part of the complex number

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z &= 3 \left(\cos (11t-1.1) + \text{i}\sin (11t-1.1)\right)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    These, therefore, are the complex numbers we are looking for. In exponential (Euler) form, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z_1 &= 2e^{(11t)\text{i}} \\[8pt]

    z_2 &= 3e^{(11t-1.1)\text{i}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z_1 + z_2 &= 2e^{(11t)\text{i}} + 3e^{(11t-1.1)\text{i}} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    A couple of notes before we proceed. 

    - We have written $z_1$ and $z_2$ in exponential form, because we need to manipulate them to form a single complex function. The G.D.C. cannot do this for us, and it is much easier to do this manipulation with complex numbers in exponential form than it is with complex numbers in polar form or rectangular form.

    - Once we have formed a single complex function in exponential form, we will convert it back to polar form. This will make it easy to identify the imaginary part, which is what we require for our final solution.

    Let's go through the steps to acquire a single complex function. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z_1 + z_2 &= 2e^{(11t)\text{i}} + 3e^{(11t-1.1)\text{i}} \\[8pt]

    &= 2e^{11t\text{i}} + 3e^{11t\text{i}-1.1\text{i}} \\[8pt]

    &= 2e^{11t\text{i}} + 3e^{11t\text{i}}\times e^{-1.1\text{i}} \\[8pt]

    &= e^{11t\text{i}}\left(2 + 3e^{-1.1\text{i}}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now use the G.D.C. to simplify the expression inside the brackets, because it is no longer a function of $t$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z_1 + z_2 &= e^{11t\text{i}}\left(4.294...e^{-0.6720...\text{i}}\right) \\[8pt]

    &= 4.294...e^{11t\text{i}-0.6720...\text{i}} \\[8pt]

    z_1 + z_2 &= 4.294...e^{(11t-0.6720...)\text{i}} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have formed a single complex function. Let's now write this in polar form.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    z_1+z_2 &= 4.294...\left(\cos (11t - 0.6720...) + \text{i} \sin (11t - 0.6720...)\right) \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can identify the imaginary part.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{im}(z_1+z_2) &= 4.294...\sin (11t - 0.6720...)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Finally, we can form an expression for $h_T$ in the form required. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h_T &= \text{im}(z_1+z_2) + 6 \\[8pt]

    &= 4.294...\sin (11t - 0.6720...) + 6 \\[8pt]

    h_T &= \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{4.29\sin (11t - 0.672) + 6}\hspace{1em}\text{(3 s.f.)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
