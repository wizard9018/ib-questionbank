# AI1207

**Maximum mark: 8**

The number of fish in a lake, $f$, is given by the function

$$

\begin{aligned}

    f(x) &= 450\,e^{-0.1x}+50

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $x$ is the approximate concentration of algae in the lake, measured in parts per billion (ppb) and $x \geq 0$.

1.  Determine the range of $f$. [2 marks]

2.  Find an expression for $f^{-1}(x)$. [3 marks]

The lake is considered "healthy" if there are at least $430$ fish at any given time.

3.  Calculate the range of algae concentrations for which the lake can be considered healthy. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  A sketch of $f(x)$ on the G.D.C. shows that the function has a $y$-intercept at $500$, and is a decreasing function.

    ![1207](images/1207.JPEG)

    We can also see that as $x \to \infty$, $f(x) \to 50$ (but it never reaches $50$). Therefore the range of $f$ is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\boxed{50 < f \leq 500} \tag*{\textbf{A2}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2.  To find the inverse function, we will first re-write our function using $y$ to represent $f(x)$, this will make manipulation of the function easier. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y &= 450\,e^{-0.1x}+50 \hspace{3em}\text{[Where $y=f(x)$]}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We now switch $x$ and $y$, thereby creating the inverse function.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x &= 450\,e^{-0.1y}+50 \hspace{3em}\text{[Where $y=f^{-1}(x)$]} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can rearrange to make $y$ the subject. This will be our inverse function.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x - 50 &= 450\,e^{-0.1y} \\[8pt]

    \frac{x-50}{450} &= e^{-0.1y} \\[8pt]

    \ln \left(\frac{x-50}{450}\right) &= -0.1y \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    -10\,\ln \left(\frac{x-50}{450}\right) &= y

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now substitute $y=f^{-1}(x)$ for our final answer.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\boxed{f^{-1}(x) = -10\,\ln \left(\frac{x-50}{450}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3.  We found in part (a) that the maximum number of fish in the lake is $500$, and this occurs when the concentration of algae is 0 ppb. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

     \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    For the lake to be considered healthy, the minimum number of fish is $430$, and this will coincide with the maximum allowable algae concentration. 

    That is, the maximum algae concentration is $a$, such that $f(a)=430$.

    Using the relationship between a function and its inverse, we could therefore say $f^{-1}(430)=a$.

    Substituting the value $430$ into our inverse function from part (b), we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f^{-1}(430) &= -10\,\ln \left(\frac{430-50}{450}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &= 1.6907... \\[8pt]

    &= 1.69\,\text{(3 s.f.)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence the range of algae concentrations for which the lake can be considered healthy, in parts per billion, is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\boxed{0 \leq x \leq 1.69} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
