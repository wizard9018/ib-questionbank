# AI341

**Maximum mark: 5**

During an exam that has a total of eight questions, Paula correctly
solved Questions $3$, $4$, $6$, $7$ and $8$. During the same exam,
Quentin correctly solved Questions $1$, $2$, $3$, $6$ and $8$.

1.  Represent this information on the Venn diagram below. $P$ represents
    the [2 marks]set of questions correctly solved by Paula. Likewise,
    $Q$ represents the set of questions correctly solved by Quentin.

    ![32a343f48ae321c58dafcee21debd0c33a85b02a.svg](images/32a343f48ae321c58dafcee21debd0c33a85b02a.svg)

2.  Find $n(P \cup Q)$. [1 mark]

3.  Find the probability that a question at random has been solved
    correctly [2 marks] by Paula or Quentin, but
    not both.

2.  Counting the number of questions presented in both sets $P$ and $Q$,
    we get
    $$
    n(P \cup Q) = 7
    $$

3.  The numbered questions that were solved correctly by Paula or
    Quentin, but not both, are highlighted on the following Venn diagram
    in red.

    ![fc6f55cece14e8f73025b9d47e06bd6024d93421.svg](images/fc6f55cece14e8f73025b9d47e06bd6024d93421.svg)

    Hence, the probability of a question being solved correctly by Paula
    or Quentin, but not both, is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(Paula or Quentin, not both)} &= \dfrac 4 8 \\[4pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}=0.5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
