# AI333

**Maximum mark: 6**

In a college lottery, there are two types of tickets: 'standard' tickets
and 'lucky' tickets. Every student is given one ticket at random. The
total number of the standard tickets is four times greater than the
number of lucky tickets.

The probability of winning the lottery with a standard ticket is
$\dfrac{1}{100}$ and $\dfrac{1}{40}$ for a lucky ticket. The tree
diagram below illustrates this.

![31cce4a2200474104de8ffe3e34c6deb706fdcd1.svg](images/31cce4a2200474104de8ffe3e34c6deb706fdcd1.svg)

1.  Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen student will win
    the lottery. [2 marks]

2.  Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen student will get a
    lucky ticket and still lose the lottery. [2 marks]

3.  Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen student, given they
    won the lottery, had a standard ticket. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $S$ = {the student gets the standard ticket},
    $Y$ = {the student gets the lucky ticket},

    $W$ = {the student wins the lottery},

    $L$ = {the student loses the lottery}.

    The event that the student will win the lottery can be illustrated
    by the paths highlighted in red on the tree diagram below.

    ![d70d1ea64755f7c74e8939cacf8ee625d7553970.svg](images/d70d1ea64755f7c74e8939cacf8ee625d7553970.svg)

    Hence the probability that the student will win the lottery is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(W) &= \text{Pr}(S) \cdot \text{Pr}(W|S) + \text{Pr}(Y) \cdot \text{Pr}(W|Y)\\[3pt]
    &= (\dfrac{4}{5} \cdot \dfrac{1}{100}) + (\dfrac{1}{5} \cdot \dfrac{1}{40})\\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}= 0.013
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The paths highlighted in red on the following tree diagram represent
    the event that the student gets the lucky ticket and will lose the
    lottery.

    ![cfe1e8ea8e6e5327d89eb492f2d0f172363a429d.svg](images/cfe1e8ea8e6e5327d89eb492f2d0f172363a429d.svg)

    Hence the probability that the student will get the lucky ticket and
    lose the lottery is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(Y \cap L) &= \text{Pr}(Y) \cdot \text{Pr}(L|Y) \\
    &= \dfrac{1}{5} \cdot \dfrac{39}{40}\\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}= 0.195
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the Conditional Probability formula
    $\text{Pr}(A|B) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap B)}{\text{Pr}(B)}$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(S|W) &= \dfrac{\text{Pr}(S \cap W)}{\text{Pr}(W)}\\[4pt]
    &=  \dfrac{\dfrac{4}{5} \cdot \dfrac{1}{100}}{0.013} \hspace{3em} [\text{Pr($W$) = $0.013$ found in part (a)}]\\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.615
    \end{aligned}
    $$
