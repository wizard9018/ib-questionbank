# AI347

**Maximum mark: 14**

A group of $80$ students are asked which books they read from a choice
of fiction books, non-fiction books and autobiographies. The results are
as follows.
$$
\begin{aligned}
5  & \text{ read \textbf{only} autobiographies} \\[-0.3em]
8  & \text{ read \textbf{only} fiction books} \\[-0.3em]
10 & \text{ read \textbf{only} non-fiction books} \\[-0.3em]
12 & \text{ read autobiographies and fiction books, \textbf{but not} non-fiction books} \\[-0.3em]
27 & \text{ read autobiographies and non-fiction books, \textbf{but not} fiction books} \\[-0.3em]
4  & \text{ read non-fiction books and fiction books, \textbf{but not} autobiographies}  \\[-0.3em]
x  & \text{ read all three kinds of books}
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Represent this information on a Venn diagram. [4 marks]

2.  Find the value of $x$, if one student does not read any of these
    book types. [2 marks]

3.  A student is randomly chosen from this group of $80$ students.
    Calculate the probability that this student reads

    1.  non-fiction books;
    2.  fiction books;

    3.  autobiographies, given that this student reads non-fiction
        books. [6 marks]

4.  Determine whether the events in **part (c)(i)** and **part (c)(ii)**
    are independent. Give a reason. [2 marks]

2.  Given one student does not read any of these book types, let's

    Since there are $80$ students surveyed in total, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    8 + 4 + 10 + 12 + x + 27 + 5 + 1 &= 80 \\
    67 + x &= 80
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{13.5em}x = 13
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  Using $x = 13$ found in part (b), we obtain a complete diagram
        as follows.

        ![fddc90ee4d2bcaacfe2545b8ef691417e270a80d.svg](images/fddc90ee4d2bcaacfe2545b8ef691417e270a80d.svg)

        Summing all of the numbers in the set $N$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        n(N) &= 4 + 10 + 13 + 27 \\
        &= 54.
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, the probability that the student
        reads non-fiction books is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(N) &= \dfrac{54}{80} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}= 0.675
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Summing all of the numbers in the set $F$ with $x = 13$ as found
        in part (b), we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        n(F) &= 8 + 4 + 12 + 13 \\
        &= 37.
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, the probability that the student
        reads fiction books is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(F) &= \dfrac{37}{80} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.463
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Using the Conditional Probability rule
        $\text{Pr}(A \hspace{0.1em} | \hspace{0.1em} B) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap B)}{\text{Pr}(B)}$,
        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(A \hspace{0.1em} | \hspace{0.1em} N) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap N)}{\text{Pr}(N)} &= \dfrac{13+27}{4 + 10 + 13 + 27} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.741
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  Two random events are considered as independent if the occurrence of
    one event does not affect the occurrence of the other. To test if
    two events are independent, we check whether
    $\text{Pr}(A \cap B) = \text{Pr}(A) \cdot \text{Pr}(B)$.

    In this case, we're testing if events $N$ and $F$ are independent.
    So we need to check if
    $\text{Pr}(N \cap F) = \text{Pr}(N) \cdot \text{Pr}(F)$ where the
    set $N \cap F$ is shaded in the following Venn diagram.

    ![936023bd791563089dd3fbe5d39430c53fb20285.svg](images/936023bd791563089dd3fbe5d39430c53fb20285.svg)

    So we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(N \cap F) &= \dfrac{4+13}{80}\\[3pt]
    &= 0.2125
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(N) \cdot \text{Pr}(F) &= 0.675 \cdot 0.4625 \\
    &\approx 0.3122
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\text{Since \(\text{Pr}(N \cap F) \neq \text{Pr}(N) \cdot \text{Pr}(F)\), the events are not independent.}$
