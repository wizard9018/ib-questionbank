# AI336

**Maximum mark: 15**

A jar of candy contains $14$ sweet pieces and $8$ sour pieces. Sarah
selects one piece at random and eats it. This piece Sarah selected was
sweet.

The tree diagram below represents the outcomes for Sarah, given this
first selection.

![76af120d0e750054927ec25ff1ed10deec1e1eb4.svg](images/76af120d0e750054927ec25ff1ed10deec1e1eb4.svg)

1.  Determine the values of:
    1.  $a$

    2.  $b$
    3.  $c$ [3 marks]

2.  Determine the probabilities of:
    1.  Sarah selecting two sweet pieces of candy in a row.

    2.  Sarah selecting two different types of candy. [4 marks]

A second jar of candy contains only sweet pieces, $15$ of which are
yellow and $11$ are blue. Charlotte selects two pieces of candy from
this new jar at random, **without replacement**. Determine the
probabilities of:

3.
    1.  Both pieces being blue.

    2.  Both pieces being the same colour.
    3.  The second piece being yellow, given the first piece was blue.
        [6 marks]

4.  If Charlotte didn't like the yellow flavour and kept
    selecting (and removing) pieces at random until she selected the one
    blue piece she wanted, calculate the probability of $5$ pieces being
    selected in total. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.
        $\dfrac 8 {22}$

    2.
        $\dfrac{13}{21}$

    3.
        $\dfrac{8}{21}$

2.
    1.  Let $W_1$ = {$1$st candy is sweet},   $O_1$ = {$1$st candy is
        sour},

        $W_2$ = {$2$nd candy is sweet},  $O_2$ = {$2$nd candy is sour}.

        ![fe84e10ecb09bbd8822d10a8efaef4963bdc9f57.svg](images/fe84e10ecb09bbd8822d10a8efaef4963bdc9f57.svg)

        Based off the paths highlighted on the tree diagram above, we
        get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(two sweet in a row)} &= \text{Pr($W_1$)} \cdot \text{Pr($W_2|W_1$)} \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{14}{22} \cdot \dfrac{13}{21} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.394
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Since the first piece that Sarah selected was sweet, the second
        piece must be sour to result in different types of candy. This
        can be represented in the tree diagram below.

        ![59a0491d6e4ede0af6917ea0d9da1aa208f6c815.svg](images/59a0491d6e4ede0af6917ea0d9da1aa208f6c815.svg)

        So we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(two of different types )} &= \text{Pr($W_1$)} \cdot \text{Pr($O_2|W_1$)} \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{14}{22} \cdot \dfrac{8}{21} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.242
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.
    1.  Let $B_1$ = {$1$st candy is blue},   $Y_1$ = {$1$st candy is
        yellow},

        $B_2$ = {$2$nd candy is blue},  $Y_2$ = {$2$nd candy is yellow}.

        The paths highlighted on the following tree diagram represent
        the event of both pieces being blue.

        ![74b2609903a0b88401732c3dc35f5fe87e5633f3.svg](images/74b2609903a0b88401732c3dc35f5fe87e5633f3.svg)

        Hence,
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(two blue)} &= \text{Pr($B_1$)} \cdot \text{Pr($B_2|B_1$)} \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{11}{26} \cdot \dfrac{10}{25} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.169
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The event that both pieces are the same colour is represented by
        the paths highlighted on the tree diagram below.

        ![92406e82bfa70d96b628c7eff32307075e9e7404.svg](images/92406e82bfa70d96b628c7eff32307075e9e7404.svg)

        So we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(two of same colour)} &= \text{Pr($B_1$)} \cdot \text{Pr($B_2|B_1$)} + \text{Pr($Y_1$)} \cdot \text{Pr($Y_2|Y_1$)}\\[3pt]
        &= (\dfrac{11}{26} \cdot \dfrac{10}{25}) + (\dfrac{15}{26} \cdot \dfrac{14}{25}) \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.492
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  The outcome that the second piece is yellow given the first
        piece was blue is highlighted on the tree diagram below.

        ![5dcf326f620bae272af510a398e0287df2013850.svg](images/5dcf326f620bae272af510a398e0287df2013850.svg)

    Hence,
     $\text{Pr}(Y_2|B_1) = \dfrac{15}{25} = 0.6.$

4.  The following tree diagram illustrates the outcome of $5$ pieces
    being selected in total.

    ![1d914f5465153e9e7fb9160f5e06ce5e09334546.svg](images/1d914f5465153e9e7fb9160f5e06ce5e09334546.svg)

    Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr($5$ pieces selected in total)}  &= \dfrac{15}{26} \cdot \dfrac{14}{25} \cdot \dfrac{13}{24} \cdot \dfrac{12}{23} \cdot \dfrac{11}{22} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.0457
    \end{aligned}
    $$
