# AI344

**Maximum mark: 14**

$100$ students are asked what pets they like from a choice of cats, dogs
and hamsters. The results are as follows.
$$
\begin{aligned}
60 & \text{ like cats} \\[-0.3em]
64 & \text{ like dogs} \\[-0.3em]
61 & \text{ like hamsters} \\[-0.3em]
34 & \text{ like cats and dogs} \\[-0.3em]
46 & \text{ like dogs and hamsters} \\[-0.3em]
43 & \text{ like cats and hamsters} \\[-0.3em]
32 & \text{ like cats, dogs and hamsters}
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Represent this information on a Venn diagram. [4 marks]

2.  Find the number of students who
    1.  like dogs only;

    2.  like both dogs and cats but not hamsters;
    3.  do not like pets. [6 marks]

A student is randomly chosen from this group of $100$ students.

3.  Calculate the probability that this student likes only two
    kinds of pets. [2 marks]

4.  Calculate the probability that this student likes only
    dogs, given that this student only likes one kind of pet.
    [2 marks]

3.  The region shaded below represents the event that this student likes

    ![ac89c185f502f4c00728115d7d7455ddda97cedb.svg](images/ac89c185f502f4c00728115d7d7455ddda97cedb.svg)

    Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(likes two pets only)} &= \dfrac{2 + 14 + 11}{100} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}=0.27
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using the Conditional Probability rule
    $\text{Pr}(A \hspace{0.1em} | \hspace{0.1em} B) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap B)}{\text{Pr}(B)}$,
    we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Pr($D$ | likes only one kind)} &= \dfrac{\text{Pr}(D \cap \text{(likes only one kind)})}{\text{Pr(likes only one kind)}} \\[3pt]
&= \dfrac{16}{15 + 16 + 4}\\[6pt]
&\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.457
\end{aligned}
$$
