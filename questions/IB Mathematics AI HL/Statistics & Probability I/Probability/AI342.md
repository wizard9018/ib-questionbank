# AI342

**Maximum mark: 16**

A group of international students at a college were surveyed. They were
asked which foreign languages they spoke at a conversational level.

The following results were received:
$$
\begin{aligned}
22 & \text{ spoke Spanish}\\[-0.3em]
35 & \text{ spoke French}\\[-0.3em]
5 & \text{ spoke Chinese}\\[-0.3em]
6 & \text{ spoke Spanish and French}\\[-0.3em]
2 & \text{ spoke French and Chinese}\\[-0.3em]
2 & \text{ spoke Spanish and Chinese}\\[-0.3em]
1 & \text{  spoke Spanish, French and Chinese}\\[-0.3em]
2 & \text{ students spoke Chinese \textbf{only}}\\[-0.3em]
7 & \text{ students did not speak any foreign languages}
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Represent this information on a Venn diagram. Denote with $S$, $F$,
    and [4 marks] $C$ the sets of students who
    spoke Spanish, French, and Chinese respectively.

2.  Find the number of students who were surveyed. [2 marks]

3.  Shade the set $S \cap F\cap C'$. [1 mark]

4.  Find $n(S \cap F\cap C')$. [1 mark]

5.  A student is chosen at random from those surveyed. Calculate the
    probability that this student spoke

    1.  Spanish;
    2.  Chinese, given that this student did not speak French;

    3.  at least two foreign languages. [6 marks]

6.  Two students who were surveyed are chosen at random. Calculate the
    probability that both students spoke Spanish. [2 marks]

2.  Summing all of the numbers on the Venn diagram, we get

    \text{[number of students]} &= 15 + 5 + 28 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 7 \\[4pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}=60
    \end{aligned}

3.

    ![79d5dab64c0ee8f598b2984432b605c3b949ce84.svg](images/79d5dab64c0ee8f598b2984432b605c3b949ce84.svg)

4.  The shaded Venn diagram in part (c) shows that
    $$
    n(S \cap F \cap C') = 5
    $$

5.
    1.  Summing all the numbers presented in the set $S$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        n(S) &= 15 + 5 + 1 + 1 \\
        &= 22.
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the probability that this student
        spoke Spanish is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(S) &= \dfrac{22}{60} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.367
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the Conditional Probability formula
        $\text{Pr}(A\hspace{0.1em}|\hspace{0.1em}B) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap B)}{\text{Pr}(B)}$,
        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(C\hspace{0.1em}|\hspace{0.1em}F') &= \dfrac{\text{Pr}(C \cap F')}{\text{Pr}(F')} \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{1 + 2}{60 - 35} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}=0.12
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  The event that this student spoke at least two foreign languages
        is illustrated in the shaded Venn diagram below.

        ![1ca39da381473a2a3f04cddf52b81e8191948ba5.svg](images/1ca39da381473a2a3f04cddf52b81e8191948ba5.svg)

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(at least two foreign languages)}&= \dfrac{1 + 1 + 1 + 5}{60} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.133
        \end{aligned}
        $$

6.  Let $S_1$ = {the first student spoke Spanish},
    $S_2$ = {the second student spoke Spanish}.

    The paths highlighted on the tree diagram below represent the event
    that both students spoke Spanish.

    ![4546bc7813125d98d4efaa989f994411721fe12f.svg](images/4546bc7813125d98d4efaa989f994411721fe12f.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Pr(both spoke Spanish)} &= \text{Pr}(S_1) \cdot \text{Pr}(S_2 \hspace{0.1em} | \hspace{0.1em} S_1) \\
&= \dfrac{22}{60} \cdot \dfrac{21}{59} \\[6pt]
& \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.131
\end{aligned}
$$
