# AI339

**Maximum mark: 8**

Jennifer is about to take three exams **in order**: Mathematics,
Chemistry and Biology. Based on what she has learned and practiced, she
estimates that the probabilities that she will pass Mathematics,
Chemistry and Biology exams are $0.8, \, 0.6$ and $0.7$, respectively.
Assuming that the probability of Jenifer being passing an exam is
independent of the others, find the probability that

1.  she will pass **all** three exams; [2 marks]

2.  she will pass **only** one of the exams; [2 marks]

3.  she will pass the last two exams **given that** she doesn't pass the
    first exam; [2 marks]

4.  she will pass **at least** one exam. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $M$ = {she passes Mathematics exam},
    $C$ = {she passes Chemistry exam},

    $B$ = {she passes Biology exam}.

    All of the outcomes for these three exams are illustrated in the
    following tree diagram.

    ![9ed6d94f4101beb18b2de8745547931426c067ab.svg](images/9ed6d94f4101beb18b2de8745547931426c067ab.svg)

    The highlighted path on the diagram represents the event that she
    passes all three exams.

    Since the probability of Jenifer passing an exam is independent of
    the others, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(M \cap C \cap B) &= \text{Pr}(M) \cdot \text{Pr}(C) \cdot \text{Pr}(B) \\[3pt]
    &= 0.8 \cdot 0.6 \cdot 0.7 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.336
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The paths highlighted on the diagram below represent the event that
    she will pass only one of these exams.

    ![c9814e650687b9d2d744e4da92ac34d4e5bc8da6.svg](images/c9814e650687b9d2d744e4da92ac34d4e5bc8da6.svg)

    Using the tree diagram above, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(pass exactly one exam)} &= \text{Pr}(M \cap C' \cap B') + \text{Pr}(M' \cap C \cap B') \\[2pt]
    & \hspace{1em} + \text{Pr}(M' \cap C' \cap B) \\[3pt]
    &= (0.8 \cdot 0.4 \cdot 0.3) + (0.2 \cdot 0.6 \cdot 0.3) + (0.2 \cdot 0.4 \cdot 0.7) \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.188
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The outcome of one exam is independent of the others. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}\left((C \cap B)\hspace{0.1em}|\hspace{0.1em}M'\right) &= \text{Pr}\left(C \cap B\right) \\[3pt]
    &= \text{Pr}(C) \cdot \text{Pr}(B) \\[3pt]
    &= 0.6 \cdot 0.7 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.42
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Passing at least one exam is the complement of passing none of the
    three exams. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(pass at least one exam)} & = 1 - \text{Pr(pass none of the exams)} \\[3pt]
    &= 1 - \text{Pr}(M' \cap C' \cap B') \\[3pt]
    &= 1 - (\text{Pr}(M') \cdot \text{Pr}(C') \cdot \text{Pr}(B')) \\[3pt]
    &= 1 - (0.2 \cdot 0.4 \cdot 0.3) \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.976
    \end{aligned}
    $$
