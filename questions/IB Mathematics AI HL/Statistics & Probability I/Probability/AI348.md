# AI348

**Maximum mark: 16**

A group of $150$ students are asked which beverage they prefer from a
choice of coffee, tea and juice. The results are shown below.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4  & \text{ like only tea} \\[-0.3em]
2  & \text{ like only juice} \\[-0.3em]
1  & \text{ like none of these beverages} \\[-0.3em]
35 & \text{ like coffee and juice} \\[-0.3em]
x & \text{ like tea and coffee} \\[-0.3em]
30 & \text{ like all three beverages}
\end{aligned}
$$
The number of
students that like coffee and juice only is half the number who like
coffee only.

![3d96cc5d1514e48c58f1ef36f17d0af3819511a8.svg](images/3d96cc5d1514e48c58f1ef36f17d0af3819511a8.svg)

1.  Include all information provided so far on the Venn Diagram above.
    [4 marks]

2.  The number of students that like tea and coffee only is equal to the
    number of students that like tea and juice only. Find the value of
    $x$. [3 marks]

3.  Find the number of students who like
    1.  tea and juice;

    2.  tea or juice. [2 marks]

4.  A student is randomly chosen from the $150$ students. Calculate the
    probability that this student likes

    1.  all three beverages;
    2.  only two beverages;

    3.  juice, given that she likes coffee. [4 marks]

5.  Two students are randomly chosen from the $150$ students. Calculate
    the probability that these two students like all three beverages.
    [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The given information can be illustrated in the following Venn
    diagram, where the blue region represents those who like coffee and
    juice only whilst the green region represents those who like coffee
    only.

    ![36310baa10cfa291ff85571e227f274c996398ea.svg](images/36310baa10cfa291ff85571e227f274c996398ea.svg)

    Since the number of students who like coffee and juice only is half
    the number who like coffee only, the number in the green region is
    double the number in the blue region. That gives

    ![71d936dec8f88ad1c739321a0b21d476221782b0.svg](images/71d936dec8f88ad1c739321a0b21d476221782b0.svg)

2.  Let's shade the regions that represent those who like tea and coffee
    only, and who like tea and juice only.

    ![244951c12ebc79505c256424a2c2a01d844ca45b.svg](images/244951c12ebc79505c256424a2c2a01d844ca45b.svg)

    Since these two regions have the same values as given, the complete
    Venn diagram is as follows.

    ![f4b44b06274ac8911ef065149ba50346796d4142.svg](images/f4b44b06274ac8911ef065149ba50346796d4142.svg)

    Summing all of these numbers, we get a total of $150$ students.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    150 &= 10 + (x - 30) + 4 + 5 + 30 + (x-30) + 2 + 1 \\
    150 &= 2x - 8 \\[4pt]
    &\hspace{-0.6em}x = 79 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  Substituting $x = 79$ into the Venn diagram, we obtain a
        complete diagram as follows. The region that represents those
        who like both tea and juice is also shaded.

        ![c780758477a631bcfab2ec736eacbb55d998c49c.svg](images/c780758477a631bcfab2ec736eacbb55d998c49c.svg)

        So we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        n(T \cap J) &= 30 + 49 \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}=79
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The region that represents those who like tea or juice is shaded
        in the Venn diagram below.

        ![b4fac18de0f25b714406ffd049f2edfb8db441c3.svg](images/b4fac18de0f25b714406ffd049f2edfb8db441c3.svg)

        Summing all of the numbers in the shaded region, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        n(T \cup J) &= 49 + 4 + 30 + 49 + 5 + 2 \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}= 139
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.
    1.  The probability that the student likes all three beverages is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(C \cap T \cap J) &= \dfrac{30}{150} \\[6pt]
        & \hspace{0.525em}= 0.2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Those who like only two beverages are represented by the shaded
        region in the following diagram.

        ![4da77d5c0130efcd613684b6394b84c6afac95fb.svg](images/4da77d5c0130efcd613684b6394b84c6afac95fb.svg)

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(only two beverages)} &= \dfrac{49 + 49 + 5}{150} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.687
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Using the Conditional Probability rule
        $\text{Pr}(A \hspace{0.1em} | \hspace{0.1em} B) = \dfrac{\text{Pr}(A \cap B)}{\text{Pr}(B)}$,
        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr}(J \hspace{0.1em} | \hspace{0.1em} C) &= \dfrac{\text{Pr}(J \cap C)}{\text{Pr}(C)} \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{30 + 5}{10 + 49 + 30 + 5} \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.372
        \end{aligned}
        $$

5.  The following tree diagram shows all the outcomes for whether each
    of these two students like all three beverages or not. The event
    that both of them like all tree beverages is highlighted in red.

    ![dcdb50976fd8df1050ca95eb343b9161e2211418.svg](images/dcdb50976fd8df1050ca95eb343b9161e2211418.svg)

    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(both like all three)} &= \dfrac{30}{150} \cdot \dfrac{29}{149} \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.0389
    \end{aligned}
    $$
