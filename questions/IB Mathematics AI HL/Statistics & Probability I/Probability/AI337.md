# AI337

**Maximum mark: 7**

The Future House factory produces LED light strips,
$5$ % of which are found to be defective.

1.  Write down the probability that a light strip produced by Future
    House is not defective. [1 mark]

Jack buys two light strips produced by Future House.

2.
    1.  Find the probability that both light strips are **not**
        defective.

    2.  Find the probability that **at least** one of Jack's light
        strips is defective. [4 marks]

The Kingstar Lighting factory also produces LED light strips. The
probability that a light strip produced by Kingstar Lighting is **not**
defective is $p$.

John buys three light strips produced by Kingstar Lighting.

3.  Write down an expression, in terms of $p$, for the
    probability that **at least** one of John's three light strips is
    defective. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\begin{aligned}
      \text{Pr (not defective)} &= 1 - \text{Pr(defective)} \\
                                        &= 1 - 0.05 \\[4pt]
                                        & \hspace{0.525em}=0.95
    \end{aligned}$

2.
    1.  Let $D_1$ = {$1$st light strip is defective},
        $D_2$ = {$2$nd light strip is defective}.

        The event that both light strips are not defective is
        highlighted on the tree diagram below.

        ![54b409aaa34889b22b74c3461c7c04d2b16a70dd.svg](images/54b409aaa34889b22b74c3461c7c04d2b16a70dd.svg)

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(neither defective)} &= \text{Pr}(D_1') \cdot \text{Pr}(D_2')\\
        &= 0.95 \cdot 0.95\\[4pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.903
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  At least one strip defective is the complement of neither strip
        being defective. Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Pr(at least one defective)} &= 1 - \text{Pr(none defective)} \\
        &= 1 - \text{Pr}(D_1') \cdot \text{Pr}(D_2') \\
        &= 1 - 0.9025 \\[4pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}= 0.0975
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Let $D_1$ = {$1$st light strip is defective},
    $D_2$ = {$2$nd light strip is defective}.

    $D_3$ = {$3$rd light strip is defective}.

    We're given that
    $\text{Pr}(D_1') = \text{Pr}(D_2') = \text{Pr}(D_3') = p$.

    The tree diagram below highlights the paths that represents the
    event of none of the LED light strips being defective.

    ![56cd53bd98d29c1b6f74c72de741705f062c78b8.svg](images/56cd53bd98d29c1b6f74c72de741705f062c78b8.svg)

    Using our knowledge of the complement
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(at least one defective)}   &= 1 - \text{Pr(none defective)} \\
    &= 1 - \text{Pr}(D_1') \cdot \text{Pr}(D_2') \cdot \text{Pr}(D_3') \\
    &= 1 - p \cdot p \cdot p \\[5pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}= 1 - p^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
