# AI299

**Maximum mark: 16**

The table below shows the distribution of the number of fish caught
during a fishing contest by $100$ contestants.

![bc95503f3e544f9605b60cad5f27267f811796f6.svg](images/bc95503f3e544f9605b60cad5f27267f811796f6.svg)

1.  Calculate
    1.  the mean number of fish caught by a contestant;

    2.  the standard deviation. [2 marks]

2.  Find the median number of fish caught in the contest. [1 mark]

3.  Find the interquartile range. [2 marks]

4.  Determine if a contestant who caught $10$ fish would be considered
    an\
    outlier.  [2 marks]

One of the contestants is chosen at random.

5.  Find the probability that this chosen contestant caught $6$
    fish or more. [2 marks]

A second contestant is randomly chosen.

6.  Given that the first contestant caught $6$ fish or more,
    find the probability that each of these two contestants caught
    exactly $7$ fish. [3 marks]

The cost of the fishing equipment used by the $100$ contestants is
normally distributed with a mean of $1200$ US Dollars (USD) and a
standard deviation of $300$ USD.

7.
    1.  Calculate the probability that a contestant chosen at random
        spent at least $1000$ USD for fishing equipment. [3 marks]

    2.  Calculate the expected number of contestants that spent at least
        $1000$ USD on fishing equipment. [1 mark]

2.  Given the frequency table, we can create the cumulative frequency
    table as follows.

    ![850e201ecf2d610523a9553bcbee85a5fe134304.svg](images/850e201ecf2d610523a9553bcbee85a5fe134304.svg)

    Using the formula
    $\text{Median} = \dfrac{1}{2}(n+1) \text{ th value}$ with $n=100$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[median]} &= \dfrac{1}{2}(100+1) \text{ th value} \\
    &= 50.5 \text{ th value}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The cumulative
    frequency table above shows that it belongs to the group including
    from the $31$th to the $53$th values, which corresponds to $4$ fish.

    Thus,
     $\text{median} = 4.$
      \[$\text{or by using G.D.C.}$\]

3.  The lower and upper quartiles are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_1 &= \dfrac{1}{4}(100+1) \text{ th value} \\
    &= 25.25 \text{ th value} \\
    Q_3 &= \dfrac{3}{4}(100+1) \text{ th value} \\
    &= 75.75 \text{ th value}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the
    cumulative frequency table in part (b), we see that they are $3$ and
    $6$, respectively. Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{IQR} &= Q_3 - Q_1 \\
    &= 6 - 3 \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}=3
    \hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  A data value is considered as an outlier if it falls outside the
    interval\
    $( Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR},\, Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR})$. We
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} &= 3 - 1.5(3) \\
    &= -1.5 \\
    Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} &= 6 + 1.5(3) \\
    &= 10.5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

     $\text{Since \(10\) is within the interval \((-1.5, 10.5)\), it is not an outlier.}$

5.  Let $C_1$ be the number of fish caught by the first contestant.
    Then we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(C_1 \geq 6) &= \dfrac{15+8+4}{100} \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}=0.27
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  Let $C_2$ be the number of fish caught by the second contestant.
    Using the conditional probability formula , we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr$(C_1 = 7, \, C_2 = 7 \, \big | \, C_1 \geq 6)$} &=  \dfrac{\text{Pr}[(C_1 = 7,\, C_2 = 7)\cap (C_1 \geq 6)]}{\text{Pr}(C_1 \geq 6)} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\text{Pr}(C_1 = 7,\, C_2 = 7)}{\text{Pr}(C_1 \geq 6)} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\frac{8}{100} \times \frac{7}{99}}{0.27} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.021
    \end{aligned}
    $$
