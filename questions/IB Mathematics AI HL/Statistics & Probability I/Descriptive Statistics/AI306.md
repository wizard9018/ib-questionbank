# AI306

**Maximum mark: 6**

Peter owns a diary farm in New Plymouth, New Zealand, where hundreds of
cows are bred for milk. In an effort of evaluating the cows
productivity, he recorded the amount of milk that the cows produce over
several days. The following box-and-whisker diagram represents the
summary of the data.

![506c3f7ed6111179b6c9b0605405f47128abfa91.svg](images/506c3f7ed6111179b6c9b0605405f47128abfa91.svg)

1.  Write down the median amount of milk that a diary cow produces per
    day at his farm. [1 mark]

2.  Write down the lower and upper quartiles. [2 marks]

3.  Find the interquartile range. [1 mark]

The amount of milk that these cows produce each day is known as being
normally distributed.

4.  Find the lowest amount of milk that a cow can produce and
    still not be considered an outlier. [2 marks]

1.  Let's label the five summary numbers on the given box-and-whisker
    diagram.

    Hence,
     $ \text{median = \(11.4\) litres per day.}$

2.
    $Q_1 = 10.4 \text{ litres per day}$

    $Q_3 = 12.8 \text{ litres per day}$

3.  The interquartile range is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{IQR} &= Q_3 - Q_1 \\
    &= 12.8 - 10.4 \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}= 2.4
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  A data value is identified as an outlier if it falls outside the
    interval
    $(Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}, \, Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR})$.
    Testing this interval, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} &= 10.4 - 1.5(2.4) \\
    &= 6.8 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, a diary cow must produce no less than $6.8$ litres of milk per day to not be considered as an outlier.
