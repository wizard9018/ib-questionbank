# AI304

**Maximum mark: 14**

Uber, the ride sharing technology service, conducts a study of $80$ of
their drivers in San Fransisco to determine how many kilometers they
travel each month. The distance each driver travelled in April is
recorded. The cumulative frequency curve for this data is shown below.

![0e12c358a276d9688c64208c58008609077dc45d.svg](images/0e12c358a276d9688c64208c58008609077dc45d.svg)

1.  Find the number of drivers that travelled a distance between $3200$
    and $5000$ kilometres. [2 marks]

2.  Use the cumulative frequency curve to find the
    1.  median distance;

    2.  lower quartile;
    3.  upper quartile. [3 marks]

3.  Hence, find the interquartile range. [1 mark]

4.  Write down the percentage of drivers that travelled a distance
    greater than the upper quartile. [1 mark]

5.  Find the number of drivers that travelled a distance less than or
    equal to $2750$ km. [1 mark]

It is known that $11$ drivers travelled more than $m$ kilometres.

6.  Find the value of $m$. [2 marks]

The smallest distance travelled by one of the drivers was
$500$ km. The longest distance travelled by one
of the drivers was $6000$ km.

7.  **On graph paper**, draw a box-and-whisker diagram for
    these data. Use a scale of $2$ cm to
    represent $1000$ km. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let's identify the points on the curve where the $x$-coordinates are
    $3200$ and $5000$.

    ![86d0a5d486dffe34150678bdef8a7523cc74d499.svg](images/86d0a5d486dffe34150678bdef8a7523cc74d499.svg)

    The number of drivers that travelled a distance between $3200$ and
    $5000$ kilometres is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[number of drivers]} &= 78 - 49 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}=29
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since the total number of data values is $80$, the lower quartile,
    median and upper quartile are the points where the cumulative
    frequencies are $20$, $40$ and $60$, respectively. Let's identify
    these points on the curve.

    ![cf90641829506c5d0f60d472c3a6c7c8c7ffc525.svg](images/cf90641829506c5d0f60d472c3a6c7c8c7ffc525.svg)

    1.
        $\text{Median} = 3000 \text{ km}$

    2.
        $Q_1 = 2500 \text{ km}$

    3.
        $Q_3 = 3500 \text{ km}$

3.  The interquartile is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{IQR} &= Q_3 - Q_1 \\
    &= 3500 - 2500 \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}=1000
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The upper quartile splits the lowest $75\%$ from the highest $25\%$
    of the data. Hence, the percentage of drivers that travelled a
    distance greater than the upper quartile is

    $$
    \text{[percentage]} = 25\%.
    $$

5.  Let's locate the point on the curve where the $x$-coordinate is
    $2750$.

    ![904aaa6ff4c06d56e0a96bd632d0384546674fdb.svg](images/904aaa6ff4c06d56e0a96bd632d0384546674fdb.svg)

    $\text{Hence there were \(29\) drivers that travelled no more than \(2750\) km.}$

6.  $11$ drivers travelled more than $m$ kilometres. Hence, the number
    of drivers who travelled no more than $m$ kilometres is
    $80 - 11 = 69$. Let's locate the point on the curve where the
    $y$-coordinate is $69$ and identify its $x$-coordinate.

    ![7ff6dac07a12ed496dd1639d29fbade902dacc66.svg](images/7ff6dac07a12ed496dd1639d29fbade902dacc66.svg)

    Hence,  $m = 3900.$

7.  We have the five-number summary as follows and hence draw the
    box-and-whisker diagram accordingly.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Min} = 500, \quad \,\, Q_1 = 2500, \quad \,\, \text{Median} = 3000, \quad \,\, Q_3 = 3500, \quad \,\, \text{Max} = 6000
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![3f1e85306896592b3e219c60654449def8a6d6be.svg](images/3f1e85306896592b3e219c60654449def8a6d6be.svg)
