# AI294

**Maximum mark: 16**

The table below shows the time, in minutes, taken to complete a
Statistics test by a group of $100$ students.

![cdb3f5acc0afb0ac1a0aa861b7f448003dc99f7e.svg](images/cdb3f5acc0afb0ac1a0aa861b7f448003dc99f7e.svg)

1.  Write down the value of $x$. [2 marks]

2.  Use the time interval midpoints to estimate the average time taken
    to complete the test. [3 marks]

The time taken to complete the test is displayed on the cumulative
frequency graph below.

![6db57cd23bb672e551827686198e6cfc20050ee2.svg](images/6db57cd23bb672e551827686198e6cfc20050ee2.svg)

3.  Estimate the median time of test completion. [1 mark]

4.  Estimate the number of students that spend at least 30
    minutes on the test.  [2 marks]

5.  Calculate the interquartile range. [3 marks]

6.  Draw a box-and-whisker diagram for this data. [3 marks]

7.  Determine if a student who takes 5 minutes to complete the
    test would be considered an outlier. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The cumulative frequency of an interval is the sum of its frequency
    and all the frequencies of its predecessors. We find the cumulative
    frequency of the interval $[30,40)$ by adding its frequency to the
    cumulative frequency of its preceding interval, $[20,30)$. Hence,

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x &= 59 + 31 \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}=90
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    $\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=27.8 \text{ minutes}$
      \[$\text{by using G.D.C.}$\]

3.  The median is the point at which the cumulative frequency is half of
    the total number of data values. There are $100$ students in total.
    Therefore, we find the point at which the cumulative frequency is
    $50$. The graph shows that this is $28.5$ minutes.
    Thus,
     $\text{median}=28.5 \text{ minutes}$.

4.  We can see that the last two rows in the table represents those who
    spend at least $30$ minutes on the test. The number of such students
    is the sum of those two frequencies. Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[number of students]} &= 31 + 10 \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}=41
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Alternatively, we can also use the graph to find it. The graph shows
    that $59$ students spent less than $30$ minutes on the test. Hence,
    the number of those spending at least $30$ minutes on the test is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[number of students]} &= 100 - 59 \\[6pt]
    &=41.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  To find the interquartile range, we need to find the lower ($Q_1$)
    and upper ($Q_3$) quartiles, at which the cumulative relative
    frequencies are $25\%$ and $75\%$, respectively. Since the total
    number of data values is $100$, these two quartiles correspond to
    the cumulative frequencies of $25$ and $75$. Using the graph, we see
    that the lower quartile is $Q_1 = 22$ and the upper quartile is
    $Q_3 = 33$. Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{IQR} &= Q_3 - Q_1 \\
    &= 33 - 22 \\[6pt]
    &=11
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  Given the graph, we see that the minimum value is $11.5$ and the
    maximum value is $45$. So we have the five-number summary as
    follows.

    -   Min $= 11.5$
    -   $Q_1 = 22$

    -   Median $= 28.5$
    -   $Q_3 = 33$

    -   Max $= 45$

    Based off the summary above, we can draw the box-and-whisker diagram
    as following.

    ![ecfd30516d79c51c1b6c9f1593ad5d6ab56490dd.svg](images/ecfd30516d79c51c1b6c9f1593ad5d6ab56490dd.svg)

7.  A point is considered as an outlier if it falls outside the
    interval\
    $(Q_1-1.5 \times \text{IQR}, \, Q_3+1.5 \times \text{IQR})$. Testing
    this interval, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} &= 22 - 1.5(11) \\
    &= 5.5 \\
    Q_3 + 1.5 \times\text{IQR} &= 33 + 1.5(11) \\
    &= 49.5 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\text{Since \(5\) minutes falls outside the interval \((5.5, 49.5)\), this student is an outlier.}$
