# AI563

**Maximum mark: 14**

Tesco PLC, a British multinational grocery retailer, offers online food
shopping and delivery services 7 days a week. The number of deliveries
to an incorrect home address in a day from Tesco Superstore A, denoted
by $X$, follows a Poisson distribution with mean of $7$.

1.  Find the probability that Superstore A delivers to

    1.  exactly $7$ incorrect addresses in a particular day;

    2.  less than $7$ incorrect addresses in a particular day;

    3.  more than $45$ incorrect addresses in a particular week.
        [6 marks]

Another Tesco Superstore, B, also makes incorrect home deliveries in a
day. Let $Y$ represent the number of incorrect home deliveries in a day
of Superstore B and assume that $Y$ follows a Poisson distribution of
mean of $5$ and that these two Superstores operate independently.

2.  Find the probability that the combined number of incorrect
    deliveries from the two Superstores in a day is $10$ or less.
    [3 marks]

A new random variable is defined by $Z = 2X + 4Y$.

3.  For the random variable $Z$, find
    1.  the mean;

    2.  the variance. [3 marks]

4.  Determine whether $Z$ could satisfy a Poisson distribution.
    Justify your answer. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Since $X \sim \rm{Po}(7)$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{Pr}(X = 7) &= \text{poissonPdf}(7,7) \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} \approx 0.149  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Since $X \sim \rm{Po}(7)$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{Pr}(X < 7) &= \rm{Pr}(X \leq 6) \\[3pt]
        &= \text{poissonCdf}(7,0,6) \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} \approx 0.45  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Let $W$ be the number of incorrect deliveries in a week. Since
        we expect there to be $7$ incorrect deliveries in a day, we
        expect there to be $\lambda = 7 \times 7 = 49$ incorrect
        deliveries in a week.

        Hence $W \sim \rm{Po}(49)$, and then
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{Pr}(W > 45) &= \rm{Pr}(W \geq 46) \\[3pt]
        &= \text{poissonCdf}(49,46,1000) \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} \approx 0.685  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  Let $T$ represent the combined number of wrong-address deliveries
    from the two Superstores in a day; $T = X + Y$. The sum of two
    independent Poisson distributed random variables also has Poisson
    distribution, as follows
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    T &\sim \rm{Po}(7 + 5) \\[3pt]
    T &\sim \rm{Po}(12)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \rm{Pr}(T \leq 10) &= \text{poissonCdf}(12,0,10) \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.275em} = 0.347  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{E}(Z) &= \rm{E}(2X + 4Y) \\[3pt]
        &= 2\rm{E}(X) + 4\rm{E}(Y) \\[3pt]
        &= 2(7) +4(5) \\[3pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} = 34
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Since the random variables are independent, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \rm{Var}(Z) &= \rm{Var}(2X + 4Y) \\[3pt]
        &= 2^2(7) + 4^2(5) \\[3pt]
        &\hspace{0.275em} = 108
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.
    $ \text{Since } \rm{E}(Z) \neq \rm{Var}(Z), Z \text{ couldn't satisfy a Poisson distribution.}$
