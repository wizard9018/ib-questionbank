# AI973

**Maximum mark: 14**

Dana is a patient at a local chiropractor. The number of times he visits
the chiropractor per month can be modelled by a Poisson distribution
with a mean of $3.7$.

1.  Find the probability that Dana visits the chiropractor exactly four
    times in one month.[1 mark]

2.  Over the course of $2$ months, find the probability that Dana visits
    the chiropractor:

    1.  no more than $8$ times in total.
    2.  at least once during the first month, but not at all in the
        second month.[5 marks]

3.  Find the probability that over a $12$ month period, there will be at
    least\
    $3$ months in which Dana does not visit the chiropractor at all.
    [4 marks]

Besides Dana, the chiropractor has many other patients. It is assumed
that these patients visit randomly and independently, and that the
number of visits made by each of them per month is modelled by a Poisson
distribution with mean $1.8$. This month the chiropractor targets at
least $60$ visits in total.

4.  Determine the least number of patients required, in
    addition to Dana, for which the probability that the chiropractor
    fulfills his target is greater than $0.8$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $X$ be the number of times Dana visits the chiropractor during
    the month.\
    We have $X \sim \text{Po}(3.7)$. Hence, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P}(X = 4) &= \text{PoissonPdf}(3.7,4) \\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-4.2em}\text{P}(X = 4) \approx 0.193
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Let $Y$ be the number of times Dana visits the chiropractor over
        the course of $2$ months. We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{E}(Y) &= 2(3.7) \\[12pt]
        &= 7.4
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence $Y \sim \text{Po}(7.4)$, and
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{P}(Y \leq 8) &= \text{PoissonCdf}(7.4,8) \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-4.1em}\text{P}(Y \leq 8) \approx 0.676
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Let $p$ be the probability that Dana does not visit the
        chiropractor during a particular month.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        p &= \text{P}(X = 0) \\[12pt]
        &= \text{PoissonPdf}(3.7,0) \\[12pt]
        &\approx 0.02472
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{P(visit only during first month)} &= (1-p) \times p \\[12pt]
        &= (1-0.02472)(0.02472) \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-13.9em}\text{P(visit only during first month)} \approx 0.0241
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  If we let $N$ be the number of months Dana makes no visits to the
    chiropractor during the period of $12$ months, we will have
    $N \sim \text{B}(12, 0.02472)$.\
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P}(N \geq 3) &= 1 - \text{P}(N \leq 2) \\[8pt]
    &= 1 - \text{BinomialCdf}(12, 0.02472, 2) \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-4.2em}\text{P}(N \geq 3) \approx 0.00281
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $n$ be the number of patients in addition to Dana visiting the
    chiropractor during a month, and $T$ be the total number of their
    visits during the month.

    We have $T \sim \text{Po}(m)$, where $m = 1.8n + 3.7$.
    The following table shows the value of $m$ for some values of $n$,
    and the corresponding values of
    $\text{P}(T \geq 60) = 1 - \text{PoissonCdf(m, 59)}$ by using G.D.C.

    ![0905cd9b06b7b91805153b0808c5fe8c72def8cb.svg](images/0905cd9b06b7b91805153b0808c5fe8c72def8cb.svg)

    Since we are finding smallest value of $n$ for which

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P}(T \geq 60) &> 0.8,\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    we obtain $n = 35$.

    $\text{There must be at least \(35\) patients in addition to Dana.}$
