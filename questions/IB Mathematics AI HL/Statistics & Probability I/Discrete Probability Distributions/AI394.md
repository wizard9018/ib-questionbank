# AI394

**Maximum mark: 15**

Tom enters a tennis competition where he plays three matches every
day. The $\text{results}$ of each match are independent of each
other. Let $A$ be the number of matches Tom wins on any given day of the
competition. The probability distribution for $A$ can be modelled by the
following table.

![a39b1ee0a2213a835fcfd6e1e39912306fddc69c.svg](images/a39b1ee0a2213a835fcfd6e1e39912306fddc69c.svg)

1.  Find the value of $p$. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  A day is chosen at random. Write down the probability that Tom
        wins every match he plays.

    2.  The competition goes for four days. Find the probability that
        Tom wins every match on exactly three of these days. [4 marks]

Clare enters the same tennis competition. Let $B$ be the number of
matches Clare wins on any given day of the competition. The probability
distribution for $B$ can be modelled by the following table.

![99c1ca807931b0f304aa3e0e964d8595e0262d65.svg](images/99c1ca807931b0f304aa3e0e964d8595e0262d65.svg)

3.  Find the expected number of games Clare wins on any given
    day, $\mathrm{E}[B]$. [3 marks]

On the final day of the competition, both Tom and Clare play their three
respective matches and their results are independent. The number of wins
Tom and Clare obtain are then added together to form a total of six.

4.
    1.  Find the probability that they win more than four matches
        combined.

    2.  Given that they win more than four matches combined, find the
        probability that Clare won all of her matches. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sum \mathrm{Pr}(A = a) &= 1 \\[6pt]
    0.15 + 0.2 + p + 0.25 &= 1 \\[9pt]
    p &= 1 - 0.6 \hspace{4.2em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{2.6em} p = 0.4
    $$

2.
    1.
        $\mathrm{Pr}(A = 3) = 0.25$
    2.  Let $X$ be the number of days of the competition Tom wins every
        match.

        We have $X \sim \mathrm{B}(4,0.25)$.
        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(X = 3) &= \text{binomPdf}(4,0.25,3) \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.0469
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Using the formula for the expected value of $B$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathrm{E}[B] &= \sum b\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{Pr}(B = b) \\[6pt]
    &= (0)(0.05) + (1)(0.1) + (2)(0.35) + (3)(0.5) \\[8pt]
    &= 2.3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.
    1.  Using the independence of Tom's and Clare's results, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(A + B > 4) &= \mathrm{Pr}(A = 2, B = 3) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 3, B = 2) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 3, B = 3) \\[6pt]
        &= (0.4)(0.5) + (0.25)(0.35) + (0.25)(0.5) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.413
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the conditional probability formula, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.75em} \mathrm{Pr}(B = 3\hspace{0.15em}|\hspace{0.15em}A+B > 4) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{Pr}(A = 2, B = 3) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 3, B = 3)}{\mathrm{Pr}(A + B > 4)} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{(0.4)(0.5) + (0.25)(0.5)}{0.4125} \\[13pt]
        &\approx 0.788
        \end{aligned}
        $$
