# AI389

**Maximum mark: 6**

The UK Farleigh Hospice offers lottery prizes every week. During a given
week, $8000$ tickets are sold at a price of $\textsterling$&ZeroWidthSpace;$1$
per ticket. The lottery prizes include one $\textsterling$&ZeroWidthSpace;$2000$
prize, two $\textsterling$&ZeroWidthSpace;$1000$ prizes, five
$\textsterling$&ZeroWidthSpace;$100$ prizes and $150$
$\textsterling$&ZeroWidthSpace;$10$ prizes.

1.  Find the probability of winning each prize in the lottery.
    [2 marks]

2.  Calculate the expected net gain from buying one ticket. [2 marks]

3.  Determine the price the ticket should be to make the lottery a fair
    game. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the information given in the problem, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P(winning a \textsterling$2000$ prize)} &= \dfrac{1}{8000} \\[6pt]
    &= 0.000125 \\[12pt]
    \text{P(winning a \textsterling$1000$ prize)} &= \dfrac{2}{8000} \\[6pt]
    &= 0.00025 \\[12pt]
    \text{P(winning a \textsterling$100$ prize)} &= \dfrac{5}{8000} \\[6pt]
    &= 0.000625\\[12pt]
    \text{P(winning a \textsterling$ 10$ prize)} &= \dfrac{150}{8000} \\[6pt]
    &= 0.01875
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  From part (a), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P(wining nothing)} &= 1 - (0.000125 + 0.00025 + 0.000625 + 0.01875) \\[4pt]
    &= 0.98025
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let $G$ be the expected net gain from buying one
    ticket. Since the tickets cost $\textsterling$&ZeroWidthSpace;$1$ each, we
    deduct it from the prize if winning, or even if winning nothing. The
    possible outcomes and the probability distribution for $G$ is shown
    in the table below.

    ![9a479f7cc1ac6c0246d2d30123eecd9755b98710.svg](images/9a479f7cc1ac6c0246d2d30123eecd9755b98710.svg)

    Using the formula of expected value
    $\mathrm{E}(X) = \displaystyle{\sum_{i = 1}^n} x_i \cdot \text{P}(X = x_i)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{E}(G) &=  ( 1999\times 0.000125) + (999 \times 0.00025) + (99 \times 0.000625) \\[3pt]
    &\quad + (9 \times 0.01875) + ((-1) \times 0.98025)  \\[6pt]
    &=  -\text{\textsterling}0.25
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  To make the lottery a fair game, the expected net gain must be zero.
    In other words, the ticket price must take this expected payoff into
    account. Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[ticket price]} &= 1 - 0.25 \\[6pt]
    &= \text{\textsterling}0.75
    \end{aligned}
    $$
