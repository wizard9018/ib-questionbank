# AI909

**Maximum mark: 9**

A mathematics teacher designed a new type of probability game to play
with her students. In the game, a student draws two marbles at random
from a bag in succession, with replacement. The bag contains 8 marbles:
$1$ red, $3$ white, $2$ blue and $2$ green. The points scored for each
coloured marble is shown in the table below.

![71fd452c71e88ff04f30437d488909faad630624.svg](images/71fd452c71e88ff04f30437d488909faad630624.svg)

The score the student receives in the game is the sum of the points from
the two draws. This is illustrated by the sample space diagram shown
below.

![38433f2a7ab3347733c9e55b6b7ba056228a9113.svg](images/38433f2a7ab3347733c9e55b6b7ba056228a9113.svg)

1.  Determine the value of $c$ in the sample space diagram above.
    [1 mark]

Lin plays the game once. Let the random variable $L$ represent Lin's
score.

2.  Using your answer in part (a), complete the missing column
    in the following probability distribution table for $L$. [3 marks]

    ![7967aa41dc27a0e48cb09a1ee293411ccafd795b.svg](images/7967aa41dc27a0e48cb09a1ee293411ccafd795b.svg)

3.  Find the probability that
    1.  Lin scores at least $4$.

    2.  Lin scores exactly $6$, given that she scores at least $4$.
        [3 marks]

4.  Find Lin's expected score in the game. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  A student will score $c$ points if they, as an example, draw a green
    ball ($4$) and then a red ball ($-2$). Hence, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    c &= 4 + (-2) \\[6pt]
    &= 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  There are four possible outcomes where a students scores $2$ ($c$).
    The probability of scoring $2$ can be calculated by

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \text{Pr}(2) &= \text{Pr(green, red)} + \text{Pr(red, green)} \\
     &\hspace{0.9em} + \text{Pr(blue, white)} + \text{Pr(white, blue)}\\[6pt]
     &= \left(\dfrac{2}{8} \cdot \dfrac{1}{8} \right) + \left(\dfrac{1}{8} \cdot \dfrac{2}{8}\right) +  \left(\dfrac{2}{8} \cdot \dfrac{3}{8}\right) + \left(\dfrac{3}{8} \cdot \dfrac{2}{8}\right) \\[6pt]
     &\hspace{0.275em}= \dfrac{1}{4}
     \end{aligned}
     $$
     Hence, we get

    ![8596a08b4077d6117fba6904230c13c66a245388.svg](images/8596a08b4077d6117fba6904230c13c66a245388.svg)

3.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(L \geq 4) &= \mathrm{Pr}(L = 4) + \mathrm{Pr}(L = 6) +   \mathrm{Pr}(L = 8)\\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{16}\\[10pt]
        &= \dfrac{7}{16}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(L = 6\hspace{0.15em}|\hspace{0.15em}L \geq 4) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{Pr}(L = 6 \cap L\geq 4)}{\mathrm{Pr}(L \geq 4)} \hspace{8.5em} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{8}}{\dfrac{7}{16}} \\[10pt]
        &= \dfrac{2}{7}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  Using the formula for the expected value of $L$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{E}[L] &= \sum l_i\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{Pr}(L=l_i) \\[10pt]
    &= (-4)\times\dfrac{1}{64} + (-2)\times\dfrac{3}{32} + 0\times\dfrac{13}{64} + 2\times\dfrac{1}{4} + 4\times\dfrac{1}{4} + 6\times\dfrac{1}{8} + 8\times\dfrac{1}{16}\\[10pt]
    &= \dfrac{5}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
