# AI704

**Maximum mark: 16**

Harry participates in a target archery competition where participants
shoot at a traditional yellow, red, blue, black, and white target that
scores 10 for the inner ring and one for the outer ring, as shown in the
following figure.

![8eff874469b3eff7e9f030dead3d20a7af85106c.svg](images/8eff874469b3eff7e9f030dead3d20a7af85106c.svg)

Let $A$ be the score Harry obtains on a single shot. The probabilities
Harry has for each score are independent and are shown in the following
table.

![2342dff9255ca1d44e56b775a5c7339710a92d20.svg](images/2342dff9255ca1d44e56b775a5c7339710a92d20.svg)

1.  Find the value of $p$. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Find the probability that Harry hits the blue region (5 or 6) in
        a shot.

    2.  Find the probability that Harry scores 10 points one time from 4
        shots.[4 marks]

Sophie is participating in the same competition. Let $B$ be the score
Sophie obtains in a shot. Her probabilities for scoring $b$ points are
given as follows.

![74c832622cd20b64a089551c5c2d16f5abff1fae.svg](images/74c832622cd20b64a089551c5c2d16f5abff1fae.svg)

3.  Find the expected score Sophie gets on any shot,
    $\mathrm{E}[B]$. [3 marks]

Harry and Sophie both take a single shot and then add their scores
together.

4.
    1.  Find the probability that one of them scored 10 on their shot
        and that the sum of both their shots totaled to 18.
    2.  Find the probability that the sum of their scores was $18$ or
        higher.

    3.  Given that the sum of their scores is greater than $17$, find
        the probability that Sophie scored $9$ from her shot.[7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1 &= \sum \mathrm{Pr}(A = a) \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
    1 &= 0.11 + 0.12 + p + 0.11+0.11\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
    &\hspace{1em} +0.08+0.10+0.09+0.08+0.07 \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
    p &= 1 - 0.87  \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
    &\hspace{-0.5em}p = 0.13 \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  The shot hits the blue region only if $a=6$ or $a=5$. Thus,
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(a=6 \text{ or } a=5) &= \mathrm{Pr}(a=6)+\mathrm{Pr}(a=5)\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
        &=0.11+0.08\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
        &\hspace{0.3em} = 0.19\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{7mm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Let $X$ be the number of times Harry scores 10 in four shots. We
        have $X \sim \mathrm{B}(4,0.11)$. Hence, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(X = 1) &= \text{binomPdf}(4,0.11,1) \hspace{2em} \\[7pt]
        &\approx 0.31
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Using the formula for the expected value of $B$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathrm{E}[B] &= \sum b\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{Pr}(B = b) \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{6mm} \\[6pt]
    &= (10)(0.12) + (9)(0.10) + (8)(0.12) + (7)(0.11) \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{6mm} \\
    &\hspace{0.5em} + (6)(0.10)+(5)(0.09)+(4)(0.11)\rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{6mm} \\
    &\hspace{0.5em}+(3)(0.09)+(2)(0.07)+(1)(0.09)\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5mm} \\[8pt]
    &= 5.82 \rule[0mm]{0pt}{3mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.
    1.  Using the independence of Harry's and Sophie's scores, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace*{-2.35cm}\mathrm{Pr}(\text{Getting one 10})
        &= \mathrm{Pr}(A = 10, B = 8) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 8, B = 10) \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
        &= (0.11)(0.12) + (0.13)(0.12) \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
        &\approx 0.0288\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Assessing the ways Harry and Sophie's scores can exceed 17, we
        get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace*{-2.35cm}\mathrm{Pr}(\text{A + B > 17})
        &= \mathrm{Pr}(A = 10, B = 10) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 10, B = 9) \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{6mm} \\
        &+ \mathrm{Pr}(A = 10, B = 8) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 9, B = 10) \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{6mm} \\
        &+ \mathrm{Pr}(A = 9, B = 9) + \mathrm{Pr}(A = 8, B = 10) \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
        &= (0.11)(0.12) + (0.11)(0.10) + (0.11)(0.12) \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
        &+ (0.12)(0.12) + (0.12)(0.10) + (0.13)(0.12) \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{7mm} \\
        &\approx 0.0794\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Using the conditional probability formula, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        %\\
        \hspace*{-1.1cm}\mathrm{Pr}(B = 9\hspace{0.15em}|\hspace{0.15em}A+B > 17) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{Pr}(A = 9, B = 9)+ \mathrm{Pr}(A = 10, B = 9)}{\mathrm{Pr}(A + B > 17)} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{(0.12)(0.10)+(0.11)(0.10)}{0.0794}\\[13pt]
        &\approx 0.29
        \end{aligned}
        $$
