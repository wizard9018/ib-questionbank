# AI396

**Maximum mark: 6**

Students in a Physics class are about to take an oral exam. The teacher
prepares a batch of questions for the exam which consists of $7$
theoretical questions and $11$ practical questions. Every question is
written on a folded piece of paper. Each student selects one of these
pieces of paper at random and answers the question on it. The piece of
paper will be placed back to the batch after being answered.

1.  Find the probability of a student selecting a theoretical question.
    [1 mark]

There are $18$ students in the class. Find the probability that

2.  $6$ students select a theoretical question; [2 marks]

3.  no more than $12$ students select a practical question.
    [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Since there are $7$ theoretical questions out of $18$ questions in
    total, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(choose a theoretical question)} &= \dfrac{7}{18} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.389
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Let $X$ be the number of students that select a theoretical
    question. From part (a), we know that the probability of a
    theoretical question being chosen is $p = \dfrac 7 {18}$. Hence, we
    get $X \sim \mathrm{B}\left(18, \dfrac 7 {18} \right)$. This gives

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(X = 6) &= \text{BinomPdf}\left(18, \,\dfrac 7 {18}, \,6 \right) \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.174 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Since there are $11$ theoretical questions out of $18$ questions in
    total, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(a practical question is chosen)} &= \dfrac{11}{18}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let $Y$ represent the number of students
    choosing a practical question. Then we have
    $Y \sim \mathrm{B}\left( 18, \dfrac{11}{18}\right)$. This gives

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(Y \leq 12) &= \text{BinomCdf}\left(18, \,\dfrac{11}{18},\,0,\,12 \right) \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.763 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
