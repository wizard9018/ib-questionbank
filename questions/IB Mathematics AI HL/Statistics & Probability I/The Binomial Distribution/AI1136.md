# AI1136

**Maximum mark: 17**

A large international school tests their teaching staff for a virus that is prevalent in the community. They interpret a positive test result as meaning a staff member has the virus. A negative result means that they do not.

The probability that a staff member has the virus is estimated to be $0.03$.

If a staff member has the virus, the probability that they will test positive is $0.95$.

If a staff member does not have the virus, the probability that they will test negative is $0.99$.

1. Using the given information, copy and complete the following tree diagram. [2 marks]

    ![ai1136a](images/ai1136a.PNG)

2. 1. Determine the probability that a randomly selected staff member does not have the virus and tests negative.

    2. If two staff members are selected at random, calculate the probability that both staff members do not have the virus and both test negative. [4 marks]

3. 1. Calculate the probability that a randomly selected staff member will receive an **incorrect** test result.

    2. The entire staff of 220 are tested. Calculate the expected number of staff members that will receive an incorrect result. [5 marks]

The mathematics faculty has 14 members. It is known that none of the mathematics faculty members have the virus.

4. Calculate the probability that none of the mathematics faculty will test positive.[4 marks]

5. Determine the probability that more than 3 mathematics faculty members will test positive.[2 marks]

        \mathrm{P}(\text{not have virus} \cap \text{test negative}) &= 0.97 \times 0.99 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    2. Using the formula for independent events, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \mathrm{P}(\text{both not have virus and test negative}) &= 0.9603 \times 0.9603 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-17.8em}\boxed{\mathrm{P}(\text{both not have virus and test negative}) \approx 0.922}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

4. Given that it is known that none of the mathematics faculty have the virus, the probability that a randomly selected member tests positive is 0.01.

    

    We have

    

    - A fixed number of trials (14 faculty members)

    - Each trial is independent (valid assumption)

    - Each trial has 2 outcomes (either test positive or test negative)

    - Constant probability ($p=0.01)$

    Therefore all the criteria for a binomial distribution are met.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{(R1)}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Let $X$ represent the number of faculty members who test positive. We have that $X\sim\mathrm{B}(14,0.01)$.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(X=0) &= \mathrm{binomPdf}(14,0.01,0) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-4.55em}\boxed{\mathrm{P}(X=0) \approx 0.869} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(X>3) &= \mathrm{P}(4\leq X \leq 14) \\[8pt]

    &= \mathrm{binomCdf}(14,0.01,4,14) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-4.55em}\boxed{\mathrm{P}(X>3) \approx 0.00000924} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
