# AI401

**Maximum mark: 6**

In a particular high school in Victoria, Australia, the probability of a
student being left-handed is $0.475$. In a class of $15$ students, find
the probability that

1.  exactly $8$ students are left-handed; [2 marks]

2.  there are no left-handed students; [2 marks]

3.  there are more right-handed students than left-handed students.
    [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $L$ be the number of left-handed students in the class. So we
    have $L \sim {B}(15,\,0.475)$. We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(L = 8) &= \text{BinomialPdf}(15,\,0.475,\,8) \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.183  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The probability that there are no left-handed students is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(L = 0) &= \text{BinomialPdf}(15,\,0.475,\,0) \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.0000634  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Since $L$ represents the number of left-handed students out of a
    class of 15 students, the number of right-handed students can be
    expressed as $15-L$. If there are are more right-handed students
    than left-handed students, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    15-L &> L \\[3pt]
    15 &> 2L \\[3pt]
    L &< \dfrac{15}{2} \\[3pt]
    L &< 7.5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    This is equivalent to $L \leq 7$ as $L$ must
    be an integer. Hence, the probability that there are more
    right-handed students than left-handed students is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr}(L \leq 7) &= \text{BinomialCdf}(15,\,0.475,\,0,\,7) \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.578  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
