# AI1070

**Maximum mark: 15**

At the company Space$Y$ it is known that $68$\% of the employees drive to work and $61$\% use the company canteen for lunch.

It is also known that $23$\% do not drive to work and do not use the company canteen for lunch.

This information can be shown on a Venn diagram such that the percentage in each region is represented by the letters $k, s, u$ and $m$.

![venn_q](images/venn_q.png)

1.  Find the value of

    1. m

    2. s

    3. u

    4. k [4 marks]

2. Find the probability a randomly chosen employee

    1. does not drive to work.

    2. does not drive to work given they eat in the canteen.[3 marks]

Each month a single employee is randomly chosen from those employees who eat at the canteen.

The lucky employee is then awarded a free parking pass for that month!

Only employees who eat in the canteen have a chance to win the free parking pass.

3.  What is the probability a randomly chosen employee drives to work given they eat in the canteen? [2 marks]

The CEO of Space$Y$ is using a binomial distribution to see how many winners, over a complete year ($12$ months), of the parking pass actually drive to work.

It is assumed that driving to work and eating in the company canteen are independent and that the percentages found in part (a) will be constant over the entire year.

Use the binomial distribution model to work out the probability

4. 1. that exactly $8$ winners of the free parking pass drive to work.

   2. more than $8$ winners of the free parking pass drive to work.

   3. all of the winners drive to work given that at least $9$ winners drive to work.[6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1.  As $23$\% do not drive to work or use the company canteen $m=0.23$.
   2. Using the combined events formula
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{ \mathrm{P}}(A\cup B) &=  \mathrm{P}(A) +  \mathrm{P}(B) -  \mathrm{P}(A\cap B)\\[6pt]
        1 - 0.23 &= 0.68 + 0.61 - s\\[6pt]
        s&=0.52
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    3.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        u &= 0.61 - 0.52\\
        u&=0.09
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    4.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        k &= 0.68 - 0.52\\
        k&=0.16
    \end{aligned}
    $$

Here is the Venn diagram with the values in the correct regions.

![venn_a](images/venn_a.png)

Let the set of employees who drive to work be named $D$ and the set of employees who eat in the canteen be named $C$.

2.  1.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
         \mathrm{P}(\text{Does not drive to work}) &=  \mathrm{P}(D')\\[3pt]
         \mathrm{P}(D') &= 1 - 0.68\\[3pt]
         \mathrm{P}(D') &=0.32
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    2. We can use the conditional  probability formula for this question.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
         \mathrm{P}(\text{Does not drive given eat in canteen)} &=  \mathrm{P}(D'|C)\\[6pt]
         \mathrm{P}(D'|C) &= \dfrac{ \mathrm{P}(D'\cap C)}{ \mathrm{P}(C)}\\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{0.09}{0.61}\\[6pt]
        &=0.14754... &\left(=\frac{9}{61}\right)\\[6pt]
        &=\text{\hspace{0.5em}\boxed{0.148}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
3. Recognise that $ \mathrm{P}(D|C) +  \mathrm{P}(D'|C)=1$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
         \mathrm{P}(D|C) &= 1 -  \mathrm{P}(D'|C)\\[3pt]
        &= 1-0.148\\[3pt]
        &= 0.852
    \end{aligned}
    $$
4. First we can define the binomial distribution where $n=12$ and $ p=0.852$.

    Let the discrete random variable $W$ be the total number of winners over a year.

    We can say that $W \sim \mathrm{B}(12, 0.852)$

    1.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
         \mathrm{P}(W=8)&=\text{binomPdf}(12, 0.852, 8)\\[3pt]
        &=0.065943...\\[3pt]
        &\approx \text{\hspace{0.5em}\boxed{0.0659}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
   2. Recognise that more than $8$ means $W\geq 9$
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
         \mathrm{P}(W\geq 9)&=\text{binomCdf}(12, 0.852, 9, 12)\\[3pt]
        &=0.91139...\\[3pt]
        &\approx 0.911
    \end{aligned}
    $$
   3. We can use the formula for conditional  probability.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(W=12 |W \geq 9)&=\dfrac{ \mathrm{P}((W=12 \cap W \geq 9))}{ \mathrm{P}(W \geq 9)}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        As $W=12$ is a subset of $W\geq 9$ we can simplify the numerator...
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\dfrac{ \mathrm{P}(W=12 )}{ \mathrm{P}(W \geq 9)}\\[8pt]
        &=\dfrac{\text{binomPdf}(12, 0.852, 12)}{\text{binomCdf}(12, 0.852, 9, 12)}\\[8pt]
        &=0.16053...\\[3pt]
        &\approx 0.161
        \end{aligned}
        $$
