# AI1227

**Maximum mark: 6**

A local coffee shop tracks customer preferences between three types of drinks: coffee, hot chocolate and tea. It is noted that each week, customers may change their preference. This is shown in the following transition diagram.

![ai1227a](images/ai1227a.png)

1. State the meaning of the value $50\%$ in the diagram, in context. [1 mark]

2. Write down a transition matrix $\bm{T}$, showing the changes in preference from week to week. [1 mark]

At the start of the first week in which the coffee shop tracks preferences, they record 50 coffee drinkers, 10 hot chocolate drinkers and 20 tea drinkers.

3. Calculate the expected number of coffee drinkers at the start of week 5. [2 marks]

4. Determine the proportion of customers who the coffee shop expects to prefer to drink tea over a long period of time. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The value $50\%$ is associated with the arrow that goes from hot chocolate to itself.

    $50\%$ of hot chocolate drinkers continue to drink hot chocolate the following week.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The given diagram can be described using the table below. Note that the columns represent the state we are coming from and the rows represent the state we are going to after one transition. The percentages have been converted to decimals.

    |   | $\textbf{Coffee}$ | $\textbf{Hot Chocolate}$ | &nbsp;&nbsp;$\textbf{Tea}$&nbsp;&nbsp; |

    |---|:---:|:---:|:---:|

    | $\textbf{Coffee}$  | $0.60$ | $0.40$ | $0.20$ |

    | $\textbf{Hot Chocolate}$  | $0.25$ | $0.50$ | $0.20$ |

    | $\textbf{Tea}$  | $0.15$ | $0.10$ | $0.60$ |

    Hence the transition matrix is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \boxed{\bm{T}  = \begin{bmatrix} 0.60 & 0.40 & 0.20 \\ 0.25 & 0.50 & 0.20 \\ 0.15 & 0.10 & 0.60 \end{bmatrix}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Given the information provided, our initial state vector will be

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \bm{s}_0 &= \begin{bmatrix} 50 \\ 10 \\ 20 \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    From the start of week 1 until the start of week 5, there are four transitions. Hence we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \bm{s}_4 &= \bm{T}^4\bm{s}_0 \\[10pt]

    &= \begin{bmatrix} 0.60 & 0.40 & 0.20 \\ 0.25 & 0.50 & 0.20 \\ 0.15 & 0.10 & 0.60 \end{bmatrix}^4 \begin{bmatrix} 50 \\ 10 \\ 20 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[19pt]

    \bm{s}_4 &= \begin{bmatrix} 35.044 \\ 25.2775 \\ 19.6785 \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    There are expected to be 35 coffee drinkers at the start of week 5.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. We need to find the steady state matrix. To do this, we can use a sufficiently large power of $n$ and calculate $\bm{T}^n$. Using $n=50$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \bm{T}^{50} &= \begin{bmatrix} 0.60 & 0.40 & 0.20 \\ 0.25 & 0.50 & 0.20 \\ 0.15 & 0.10 & 0.60 \end{bmatrix}^{50} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[20pt]

    &= \begin{bmatrix} 0.4390... & 0.4390... & 0.4390... \\ 0.3170... & 0.3170... & 0.3170... \\ 0.2439... & 0.2439... & 0.2439... \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The entries along each row are identical, therefore a steady state has been reached. Tea is represented by the third row of the matrix.

    The proportion of customers expected to prefer to drink tea over a long period of time is $0.244\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$.
