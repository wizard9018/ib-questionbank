# AI1199

**Maximum mark: 16**

Kim keeps $240$ chickens in two chicken coops that are connected by a gate so that the chickens can move between the coops when the gate is open. She keeps the gate closed during the day, but keeps the gate open overnight.

Kim records the number of chickens that are in coop A and coop B respectively, every night, before she opens the gate. She then records the number of chickens that are in each coop the next morning when she closes the gate.

Kim finds that every night, 20\% of the chickens in coop A move to coop B, and $(100x)$\% of the chickens in coop B move to coop A.

1. Copy and complete the following transition matrix, $\bm{T}$, in terms of $x$, that describes the movement between the coops each night. [1 mark]

![1199a](images/1199a.JPG)

The matrix $\bm{T}$ has an eigenvalue $\lambda_1=0.5$.

2. Determine the value of $x$. [3 marks]

Initially, there are $30$ chickens in coop A and $210$ chickens in coop B.

3. Show that after 3 nights, there are more chickens in coop A than in coop B for the first time. [3 marks]

The other eigenvalue of matrix $\bm{T}$ is $\lambda_2=1$.

4. Find the eigenvectors of matrix $\bm{T}$. [5 marks]

5. Write the matrix $\bm{T}^n$ in the form $\bm{P}\,\bm{D}^n\,\bm{P}^{-1}$. [1 mark]

6. Hence or otherwise, determine, after a long period of time, how many chickens there will be in coop A, and how many chickens in coop B. You may assume that the total number of chickens will remain unchanged.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. A transition matrix is set up so that the columns represent the current state, and the rows represent the future state after one transition. The position of the entry $0.2$, representing the $20\%$ moving from A to B, tells us the order of the columns and rows, as shown:

    ![1199b](images/1199b.JPG)

    The entries in each column must add up to $1$, therefore we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\hspace{-6em}\bm{T} =  \begin{bmatrix*} 0.8 & x \\[4pt] \hspace{0.5em}0.2 & 1-x \end{bmatrix*} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. To find the eigenvalues of a $2 \times 2$ matrix, we use the formula $|\bm{T} - \lambda I|=0$. Using this formula with $\lambda = 0.5$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \Bigg|\begin{bmatrix} 0.8 - 0.5 & x \\[4pt] 0.2 & 1-x-0.5 \end{bmatrix} \Bigg| &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[15pt]

    0.3(0.5 - x) - (x)(0.2) &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[9pt]

    0.15 - 0.3x - 0.2x &= 0 \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-0.9em}\boxed{x = 0.3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. We know that $x=0.3$ and therefore $1-x=0.7$. Our matrix equation for the number of chickens in each coop after $3$ nights is as follows:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & 0.3 \\[4pt] 0.2 & 0.7 \end{bmatrix}^3 \,\times \, \begin{bmatrix} 30 \\[4pt] 210 \end{bmatrix}  &= \begin{bmatrix} 129.75 \\[4pt] 110.25 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This shows that after three nights, there are more chickens in coop A than in coop B $(129.75 > 110.25)$. To show that this is the $\textbf{first}$ time that this has occurred, when can compute a similar calculation for the first night and the second night: 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & 0.3 \\[4pt] 0.2 & 0.7 \end{bmatrix}^1 \,\times \, \begin{bmatrix} 30 \\[4pt] 210 \end{bmatrix}  &= \begin{bmatrix} 87 \\[4pt] 153 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & 0.3 \\[4pt] 0.2 & 0.7 \end{bmatrix}^2 \,\times \, \begin{bmatrix} 30 \\[4pt] 210 \end{bmatrix}  &= \begin{bmatrix} 115.5 \\[4pt] 124.5 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    In both cases we see that the number in coop B is still greater than that in coop A.

    Therefore, it as after $$\text{$3$ nights}$$ that there are first more in coop A than B.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{AG}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. To find the eigenvectors of the $2 \times 2$ matrix $\bm{T}$, we solve the equation

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{bmatrix} 0.8 - \lambda & 0.3 \\[4pt] 0.2 & 0.7 - \lambda \end{bmatrix} \,\times \, \begin{bmatrix} x \\[4pt] y \end{bmatrix}  &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\[4pt] 0 \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    using firstly $\lambda_1=0.5$ and secondly $\lambda_2=1$. The values we find for $x$ and $y$ will be the elements of eigenvectors $\bm{p}_1$ and $\bm{p}_2$.

    For $\lambda_1=0.5$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & 0.3 \\[4pt] 0.2 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \,\times \, \begin{bmatrix} x \\[4pt] y \end{bmatrix}  &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\[4pt] 0 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This leads to two linear equations in $x$ and $y$, which will yield the same values:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    0.3x + 0.3y &= 0 \tag*{(1)} \\[6pt]

    0.2x + 0.2y &= 0 \tag*{(2)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using equation $(1)$, we find that $x=1$ and $y=-1$ is a solution.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore our first eigenvector is $\bm{p}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$ associated with $\lambda_1=0.5$. 

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    For $\lambda_2=1$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{bmatrix} -0.2 & 0.3 \\[4pt] 0.2 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix} \,\times \, \begin{bmatrix} x \\[4pt] y \end{bmatrix}  &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\[4pt] 0 \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This leads to two linear equations in $x$ and $y$, which will yield the same values:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    -0.2x + 0.3y &= 0 \tag*{(3)} \\[6pt]

    0.2x - 0.3y &= 0 \tag*{(4)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using equation $(3)$, we find that $x=3$ and $y=2$ is a solution.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore our second eigenvector is $\bm{p}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ associated with $\lambda_2=1$. 

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. The matrix $\bm{P}$ will be a matrix of the eigenvectors (written in the order $\bm{p}_1,\bm{p}_2$), and the matrix $\bm{D}$ will be a diagonal matrix with the eigenvalues on the diagonal (written in the order $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$). 

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\boxed{\bm{T}^n = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\[4pt] -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \, \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0 \\[4pt] 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}^n \, \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\[4pt] -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}^{-1}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. The long term behaviour will be determined by the matrix $\bm{T}^n$ as $n \to \infty$.

    For a diagonal matrix, we can note that this is a special case where if the matrix is raised to a power, each element can individually be raised to that power. Thus,

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \bm{D}^n &= \begin{bmatrix} 0.5^n & 0^n \\[4pt] 0^n & 1^n \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Hence as $n \to \infty$, we have 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

     \displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \bm{D}^n &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\[4pt] 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Our calculation will be

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \bm{T}^n &= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\[4pt] -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \, \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\[4pt] 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \, \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\[4pt] -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \, \begin{bmatrix} 30 \\ 210 \end{bmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= \begin{bmatrix} 144 \\ 96 \end{bmatrix}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\text{Long term, there will be $144$ chickens in coop A, and $96$ in coop B.}$$ 

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$
