# AI1175

**Maximum mark: 6**

A factory produces lightbulbs. Their life expectancy is normally distributed, with a mean of 800 hours and a standard deviation of 50 hours.

A lightbulb that lasts less than 700 hours is called “defective”.

1. Calculate the probability that a randomly selected lightbulb is defective.     [2 marks]

Andrew buys a batch of 100 lightbulbs.

2. Find the probability that more than 5 of Andrew’s lightbulbs are defective.     [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let the random variable $X$ represent the life expectancy of a lightbulb. We have that $X\sim\mathrm{N}(800,50^2)$.

    The probability that a lightbulb is defective is $\mathrm{P}(X < 700)$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(X < 700) &= \text{normCdf}(-\infty,700,800,50) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= 0.02275...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\text{The probability that a randomly selected lightbulb is defective is approximately $0.0228$.}$$

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Let the discrete random variable $L$ represent the number of Andrew's lightbulbs that are defective. We can say that $L \sim \mathrm{B}(100,0.02275...)$. 

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can say this because

    - We have a fixed number of trials ($100$ lightbulbs)

    - Each trial is independent (valid assumption)

    - Each trial has two possible outcomes (defective or not defective)

    - We have a constant probability ($\mathrm{P}\text{(defective)}=0.02275...$)

    Therefore all the criteria for a binomial distribution are fulfilled. 

    Hence we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{P}(L > 5) &= \mathrm{P}(6 \leq L \leq 100) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= \text{binomCdf}(100,0.02275...,6,100) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= 0.02702...\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-4.25em}\boxed{\mathrm{P}(L > 5) = 0.0270\,(3\,\mathrm{s.f.})} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
