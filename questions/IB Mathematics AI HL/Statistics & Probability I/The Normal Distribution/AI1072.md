# AI1072

**Maximum mark: 18**

A grocery store sells zucchini and cabbages.

The weights of the zucchini are known to be normally distributed with a mean of $195$ g and a standard deviation of $39$ g.

The weights of the cabbages are also known to be normally distributed with a mean of $936$ g and a standard deviation of $80$ g.

The weights of the vegetables are independent of each other.

1. Find the probability that a randomly chosen zucchini and cabbage would have a combined weight of less than $1$ kg. [4 marks]

2. Find the probability that the combined weight of $4$ independently chosen zucchinis are heavier than $1$ cabbage.[5 marks]

The grocery store has two suppliers of cabbages. The manager thinks that the cabbages from supplier A are heavier than supplier B.

To test this claim the manager took a sample of weights from each supplier.

He took a sample of $7$ cabbages from supplier A and found the mean to be $965$ g and the unbiased estimate of the standard deviation to be $63.4$ g.\
He took a sample of $10$ cabbages from supplier B and found the mean to be $923$g and the standard deviation of the sample to be $73.2$ g.

3. Find an unbiased estimate for the standard deviation for supplier B. [3 marks]

4. Perform a suitable test at the $10$\% significance level to test the managers claim. You can assume the cabbages from both suppliers are normally distributed and with equal standard deviation. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. First define both distributions.

    Let $Z$ by the weight of a single zucchini and $C$ be the weight of a single cabbage.

    $Z \sim \mathrm{N}(195,\,39^2)$\
    $C \sim \mathrm{N}(936,\,80^2)$

    Let the CRV $A=Z+C$. $A$ will follow a normal distribution.

    We must sum the means of $Z$ and $C$ to find $\mathrm{E}(A)$ and we must sum the **variances** of $Z$ and $C$ to find $\mathrm{Var}(A)$, therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(A)&=195+936 \hspace{3em}\mathrm{Var}(A)=39^2+80^2 \\
        &=1131 \hspace{8.4em}=7921
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Make sure to sum the variance and not the standard deviations!

    The standard deviation of $A$ is $\sqrt{39^2+80^2} = 89$.\
    Therefore $A \sim \mathrm{N}(1131,\,89^2)$.

    We need to find the probability $A$ is less than $1$ kg ($1000$ g).
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A<1000) &= 0.070523...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The probability a single zucchini and cabbage will weigh less than 1kg is $y$-coordinate is $0.0705$.

2. Note that we are taking $4$ independent observations of $Z$.

    Therefore we need to find $\mathrm{P}(Z_1+Z_2+Z_3+Z_4>C)$.

    It is often easier to rearrange inequalities such as this to the following form

    $\mathrm{P}(Z_1+Z_2+Z_3+Z_4-C>0)$.

    We can then define a new C.R.V. $B=Z_1+Z_2+Z_3+Z_4-C$ where $B$ will follow a normal distribution.

    Now find $\mathrm{E}(B)$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(B) &= \mathrm{E}(Z_1+Z_2+Z_3+Z_4-C)\\
        &=\mathrm{E}(Z_1)+\mathrm{E}(Z_2)+\mathrm{E}(Z_3)+\mathrm{E}(Z_4)-\mathrm{E}(C)\\
        &= \mathrm{E}(Z) + \mathrm{E}(Z) + \mathrm{E}(Z)+ \mathrm{E}(Z)-\mathrm{E}(C)\\
        &= 4\mathrm{E}(Z)-\mathrm{E}(C)\\
        &= 4\times195 - 936\\
        &=-156
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    And then $\mathrm{Var}(B)$, noting, that according to the formula we must $\textcolor{red}{\text{add}}$ all variances.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{Var}(B) &= \mathrm{Var}(Z_1+Z_2+Z_3+Z_4-C)\\
    &=\mathrm{Var}(Z_1)+\mathrm{Var}(Z_2)+\mathrm{Var}(Z_3)+\mathrm{Var}(Z_4)\textcolor{red}{+}\mathrm{Var}(C)\\
    &= \mathrm{Var}(Z) + \mathrm{Var}(Z) + \mathrm{Var}(Z)+ \mathrm{Var}(Z)+ \mathrm{Var}(C)\\
    &= 4\mathrm{Var}(Z)+\mathrm{Var}(C)\\
    &= 4\times39^2 + 80^2\\
    &=12484
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The standard deviation of $B$ is $\sqrt{12484} = 111.7...$.

    From the distribution $B \sim \mathrm{N}(-156,\,111.7^2)$ we need to find the probability $B$ is greater than $0$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(B>0)&=0.081267...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The probability of 4 zucchinis weighing more than 1 cabbage is $0.0813$.

3. From the formula booklet
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        s^2_{n-1}&=\frac{n}{n-1}s^2_n\\[10pt]
        &=\frac{10}{10-1}73.2^2\\[10pt]
        &=5953.6\\[10pt]
        s_{n-1}&=\sqrt{5953.6}\\[10pt]
        &=77.15...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    An unbiased estimate for the standard deviation for supplier B is $77.2 \text{ g}$.

4. State the _null_ and _alternate_ hypotheses.

    $H_0: \mu_A=\mu_B$ and\
    $H_1: \mu_A>\mu_B$.

    Use a two-sample $t$-test.

    $p$-value $=0.12747...$ [by using G.D.C.].

    Note $0.12747...>0.1$.

    As the $p$-value is greater than the significance level $\text{we do \textbf{not} reject} H_0$.

    There is insufficient evidence to suggest that the cabbages from supplier A are heavier than supplier B.
