# AI366

**Maximum mark: 15**

The table below shows the distribution of trip numbers made by a group
of $100$ taxi drivers surveyed on a working day in Sydney.

![8d3d148e3223c65bfdcce6918067214a9ab741bb.svg](images/8d3d148e3223c65bfdcce6918067214a9ab741bb.svg)

1.  Find
    1.  the mean number of trips made by the taxi drivers;

    2.  the standard deviation of the number of trips made. [2 marks]

2.  Find the median number of trips made by taxi drivers. [1 mark]

3.  Find the interquartile range. [2 marks]

A taxi driver is chosen at random from the group of $100$ taxi drivers.

4.  Find the probability that this taxi driver made $13$ or
    more trips. [2 marks]

A second taxi driver is chosen at random from the group of $100$ taxi
drivers.

5.  Given that the first taxi driver chosen at random made $13$
    or more trips, find [3 marks] the probability that both taxi
    drivers made $14$ trips.

The amount of time that the $100$ taxi drivers waited for their next
client was normally distributed with a mean of $15$ minutes and a
standard deviation of $4$ minutes.

6.
    1.  Calculate the probability that a taxi driver chosen at random
        waited at least $12$ minutes for the next client.

    2.  Calculate the expected number of taxi drivers that waited at
        least $12$ [4 marks] minutes for their next client.

The $100$ taxi drivers were selected for the survey by ordering taxi
identification numbers in ascending order, then selecting every $10$th
number.

7.  Identify the sampling technique used in this sampling
    method. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $X$ be the trip number on a working day. Then we get
    1.
        $\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu \approx 12.5$
          \[$\mathrm{by using G.D.C.}$\]

    2.
        $s_x \approx 1.67$
        \[$\mathrm{by using G.D.C.}$\]

2.  Using the formula
    $\mathrm{Med} = \left ( \dfrac{n+1}{2} \right) \mathrm{th value}$ with
    $n = 100$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{Med} &= \left (\dfrac{100+1}{2}\right) \mathrm{th value} \\[4pt]
    &= 50.5 \hspace{0.2em}\mathrm{th value}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let's create a cumulative frequency table for
    this data. Note that the class that contains the median is
    highlighted in red.

    ![4e81fa1efb000a101c063d3313ada2747aa6a69e.svg](images/4e81fa1efb000a101c063d3313ada2747aa6a69e.svg)

    $\mathrm{Hence, median} = 13.$
    \[$\mathrm{or by using G.D.C.}$\]

3.  We need to find the first and third quartiles using the formulas
    below.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_1 &= \left ( \dfrac{n+1}{4} \right) \mathrm{th value} \\[6pt]
    &= \left ( \dfrac{100+1}{4} \right) \mathrm{th value} \\[6pt]
    &= 25.25 \hspace{0.2em}\mathrm{th value} \\[8pt]
    Q_3 &= \left [ \dfrac{3(n+1)}{4} \right] \mathrm{th value} \\[6pt]
    &= \left [ \dfrac{3(100+1)}{4} \right] \mathrm{th value} \\[6pt]
    &= 75.75 \hspace{0.2em}\mathrm{th value}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The cumulative frequency table shows that these
    two values are $12$ and $14$, respectively. Hence,
    $Q_1 = 12,\, Q_3 = 14$. That gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{IQR} &= Q_3 - Q_1 \\[3pt]
    &= 14 - 12 \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The number of taxi drivers who made $13$ or more trips is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m &= 22 + 18 + 9 + 2 \\[3pt]
    &= 51
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the probability that the taxi driver made
    $13$ or more trips is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{Pr}(X \geq 13) &= \dfrac{51}{100} \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}= 0.51
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The data table shows that there are $18$ out of $100$ taxi drivers
    that made $14$ trips. Hence the event that both taxi drivers made
    $14$ trips can be illustrated on the tree diagram as follows
    (highlighted in red).

    ![752425d001f0780796b586dae10cc72a17a605b5.svg](images/752425d001f0780796b586dae10cc72a17a605b5.svg)

    Using the Conditional Probability rule
    $\mathrm{P}(A\hspace{0.1em}|\hspace{0.1em}B) = \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)}{\mathrm{P}(B)}$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}((X_1 = X_2 = 14)\hspace{0.1em}|\hspace{0.1em}(X_1 \geq 13)) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}((X_1 = X_2 = 14) \cap (X_1 \geq 13))}{\mathrm{Pr}(X_1 \geq 13)} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(X_1 = X_2 = 14)}{\mathrm{P}(X_1 \geq 13)} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\dfrac{18}{100} \times \dfrac{17}{99}}{0.51} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.0606
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.
    1.  Let $T$ be the amount of time, in minutes, that a taxi driver
        waited for the next client. Then we have
        $T \sim \mathrm{N}(15, 4^2)$.

        The probability that a taxi driver waited for $12$ minutes or
        more for the next client is represented by the shaded region
        below.

        ![bbe50245870ded05aa6958da0d132f53e8d09ed1.svg](images/bbe50245870ded05aa6958da0d132f53e8d09ed1.svg)

        So we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Pr}(T \geq 12) &= \mathrm{NormCdf}(12,\,100,\,15,\,4) \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.773 \hspace{3em} [\mathrm{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Let $Y$ be the number of taxi drivers that waited at least $12$
        minutes for their next client. Using part (a), we get
        $Y \sim \mathrm{B}(100, 0.773)$. Hence,
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        E(Y) &= np \\[3pt]
        &= 100 \times 0.773 \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}= 77.3
        \end{aligned}
        $$
