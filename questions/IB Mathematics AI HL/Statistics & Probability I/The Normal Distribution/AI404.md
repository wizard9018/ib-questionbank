# AI404

**Maximum mark: 15**

The heights $X$, in metres, of trees in a forest are normally
distributed with a mean of $56.7$ m and a
standard deviation of $18.2$ m.

1.  A tree is selected at random. Find the probability that the tree is:

    1.  between $65$ m and
        $85$ m;

    2.  at least $70$ m. [3 marks]

The tallest $10\%$ of trees are considered giant trees and the smallest
$15\%$ of trees are considered small trees.

2.
    1.  Calculate the minimum height for a tree to be considered giant.

    2.  Calculate the maximum height for a tree to be considered small.
        [4 marks]

Two trees are selected at random.

3.  Find the probability that one of these trees is considered
    giant, and one considered small. [4 marks]

Consider the case where twenty trees are selected at random.

4.  Find the probability that:
$\\[6pt]$
    1.  no trees are giant;

    2.  at least three trees are giant. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  We have $X \sim \mathrm{N}(56.7,18.2^2)$.
        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} \mathrm{P}(65 \leq X \leq 85) &= \text{normCdf}(65,85,56.7,18.2) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.264
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.3em} \mathrm{P}(X \geq 70) &= \text{normCdf}(70,\infty,56.7,18.2) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.232
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  Let $h_\text{min}$ be the minimum height for a tree to be
        considered giant.

        Hence we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(X \geq h_{\text{min}}) &= 0.1 \\[6pt]
        h_{\text{min}} &= \text{invNorm}(0.9, 56.7,18.2) \hspace{0.9em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        h_{\text{min}} \approx 80.0 \text{ m} \hspace{4.55em}
        $$

    2.  Let $h_\text{max}$ be the maximum height for a tree to be
        considered small.

        Hence we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(X \leq h_{\text{max}}) &= 0.15 \\[6pt]
        h_{\text{max}} &= \text{invNorm}(0.15, 56.7,18.2) \hspace{0.55em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        h_{\text{max}} \approx 37.8 \text{ m} \hspace{4.7em}
        $$

3.  Let
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    G_1 &=  \text{\{the $1$st tree is a giant tree\}},\\[2pt]
    S_1 &=  \text{\{the $1$st tree is a small tree\}}, \\[2pt]
    G_2 &=  \text{\{the $2$nd tree is a giant tree\}}, \\[2pt]
    S_2 &=  \text{\{the $2$nd tree is a small tree\}}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The outcomes for selecting two trees randomly is illustrated in the
    following tree diagram. The outcomes of one tree being giant and the
    other one being small are highlighted in red.

    ![deb5adc2f5ed1d3701fd9b32b068130d9cea2526.svg](images/deb5adc2f5ed1d3701fd9b32b068130d9cea2526.svg)

    Using the diagram, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(one giant and one small)} &= \text{Pr}(G_1) \cdot \text{Pr}( S_2) + \text{Pr}(S_1) \cdot \text{Pr}(G_2) \\[3pt]
    &= (0.1 \cdot 0.15) + (0.15 \cdot 0.1) \\[6pt]
    &= 0.03
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $Y$ be the number of giant trees selected from the twenty trees.
    We have $Y \hspace{-0.1em}\sim \mathrm{B}(20,0.1)$.

    1.  Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(Y = 0) &= \text{binomPdf}(20,0.1,0) \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.122
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(Y \geq 3) &= \text{binomCdf}(20,0.1,3,20) \hspace{0.5em}  \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.323
        \end{aligned}
        $$
