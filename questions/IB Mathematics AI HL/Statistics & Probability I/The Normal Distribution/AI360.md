# AI360

**Maximum mark: 7**

In the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO), the participants are
ranked on their individual scores. During the $58$th Mathematical
Olympiad held in Rio de Janeiro, the individual scores followed a normal
distribution with a mean of $32$ and a standard deviation of $3$.

1.  Find the probability that the score of a randomly chosen participant
    is [2 marks] greater than $35$.

According to the IMO rules, approximately $1$ in $12$ participants will
be awarded the top award: a Gold Medal.

2.  Calculate the score a participant would have had to achieve
    to be awarded a [2 marks] Gold Medal.

3.  Estimate the interquartile range of the distribution.
    [2 marks]

4.  Using your answer from **part (c)**, approximate the
    minimum score [1 mark] required to be
    considered a high-achieving outlier.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The region shaded below represents the probability that the score of
    a participant is greater than $35$.

    ![977fca3f57477011c6fb940671990934cc5e869d.svg](images/977fca3f57477011c6fb940671990934cc5e869d.svg)

    Let $S$ be the score of a randomly chosen participant.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{Pr}(S > 35)  &=  \text{NormCdf}(35,100,32,3) \\[6pt]
    & \hspace{0.525em}\approx 0.159 \hspace{3em}[\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The probability that a participant will be awarded a Gold Medal is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Pr(awarded Gold Medal)} &= \dfrac{1}{12} \\[5pt]
    &= 0.0833
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let $x$ be the score a participant would have
    had to achieve to be awarded a Golden Medal. This probability is
    represented by the region shaded below.

    ![e8f2b50874029a741055f552e6d23b80a50973b1.svg](images/e8f2b50874029a741055f552e6d23b80a50973b1.svg)

    We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x &= \mathrm{InvNorm}(1-0.0833,\,32,\,3)\\[3pt]
    &= \mathrm{InvNorm}(0.9167,\,32,\,3)\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}\approx 36.1 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The quartiles $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ split the bottom $25\%$ and top $25\%$
    of a population, respectively. The following diagram shows the
    quartiles for this distribution.

    ![2d60eb52cc13aeebfdc7b45a088cf3f59d26ef6e.svg](images/2d60eb52cc13aeebfdc7b45a088cf3f59d26ef6e.svg)

    We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_1 &= \mathrm{InvNorm}(0.25, 32, 3) \\[3pt]
    &= 29.976... \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[6pt]
    Q_3 &= \mathrm{InvNorm}(1-0.25, 32, 3) \\[3pt]
    &=\mathrm{InvNorm}(0.75, 32, 3) \\[3pt]
    &= 34.023... \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence subbing the values into our **interquartile range formula (4.2)** we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{IQR} &= Q_3 - Q_1 \\[3pt]
    &= 34.023... - 29.976... \\[6pt]
    &=4.046...\\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 4.05 \text{\hspace{0.1em}(3 s.f.)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  A data value is considered as an outlier if it falls outside of the
    range
    $\left(Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}, \hspace{0.1em}Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}\right)$.
    Hence, the minimum score required to be considered as a
    high-achieving outlier is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} &= 34.023... + 1.5 \times 4.046... \\[3pt]
    &=40.093...\\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 40.1\text{\hspace{0.1em}(3 s.f.)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
