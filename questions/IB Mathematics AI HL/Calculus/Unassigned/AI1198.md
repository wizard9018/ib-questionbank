# AI1198

**Maximum mark: 17**

Grace is planning on making a 3D printed model of a well-known hill in her local area. She knows that the hill itself is approximately $400$ metres wide and $200$ metres high. A cross-section of the hill is shown in the following diagram.

![1198a](images/1198a.JPG)

Grace plans to model the cross-section of the hill using a piecewise function, $f(x)$, consisting of a first function, $f_1(x)$ for $0 \leq x \leq 200$ and a second function, $f_2(x)$ for $200 < x \leq 400$.

For the first section, Grace decides on a logistic function of the form

$$

\begin{aligned}

    f_1(x) &= \dfrac{202}{1+Ce^{-kx}} \text{ , where } C,k>0

\end{aligned}

$$

A point approximately halfway up the hill has coordinates $(100,120.684)$. Using these coordinates, Grace makes the equation

$$

\begin{aligned}

    C &= 0.6738\,e^{100k}

\end{aligned}

$$

The summit of the hill has approximate coordinates $(200,201.087)$.

1. Using these coordinates, find a second expression for $C$ in terms of $k$. [3 marks]

2. Hence find the value of $C$ and the value of $k$. [3 marks]

For the second section, Grace creates a function $f_2(x)$, which she finds by performing the following two transformations on $f_1(x)$ in order:

$\hspace{2em}\bullet$ A reflection in the $y$-axis;

$\hspace{2em}\bullet$ A translation of $400$ units in the direction of the positive $x$-axis.

3. Write a complete expression for $f_2(x)$. [3 marks]

4. Hence write $f(x)$ as a piecewise function. [1 mark]

Grace wants her 3D printed model to be of scale $1:2\,000$. This is equivalent to applying both a vertical stretch and horizontal stretch of scale factor $\frac{1}{2\,000}$ to her piecewise function.

5. Write a piecewise function for Grace's model, $g(x)$. [4 marks]

To determine the volume of plastic she requires for her 3D printed model, Grace finds the volume generated when $g(x)$ is rotated $\bm{180^{\circ}}$ about the $x$-axis.

6. Determine the volume of plastic Grace will require.[3 marks]

1. Substituting $x=200$ and $f_1(200)=201.087$ into our equation, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f_1(x) &= \dfrac{202}{1+Ce^{-kx}} \\[6pt]

    f_1(200) &= \dfrac{202}{1+Ce^{-200k}} \\[6pt]

    201.087 &= \dfrac{202}{1+Ce^{-200k}} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    1+Ce^{-200k} &= \dfrac{202}{201.087} \\[6pt]

    Ce^{-200k} &= \dfrac{202}{201.087} - 1 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    \dfrac{C}{e^{200k}} &= 0.004540... \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-1.11em}\boxed{C = 0.00454e^{200k}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    0.6738e^{100k} &= 0.00454e^{200k}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

3. Our $\textbf{first}$ function is $f_1(x)=\frac{202}{1+100e^{-0.05x}}$.

    Our first step is to reflect this function in the $y$-axis. This is equivalent to generating the function $f_1(-x)$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f_1(-x) &= \frac{202}{1+100e^{0.05x}} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Our second step is to translate the function $400$ units to the right. This is equivalent to generating the function $f_1(-(x-400))$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f_2(x) &= f_1(-(x-400)) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= \frac{202}{1+100e^{0.05(x-400)}} \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-2.55em}\boxed{f_2(x) = \frac{202}{1+100e^{0.05x-20}}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
