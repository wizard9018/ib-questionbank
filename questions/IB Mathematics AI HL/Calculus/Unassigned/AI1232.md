# AI1232

**Maximum mark: 9**

Molly is planning to make a tagine, a type of cooking pot, out of clay. She decides to mark several points on a Cartesian plane and fit a smooth curve through the points.

Molly marks the points $(1,3), (2,1), (3,0.6)$ and $(4,0.4)$, and fits the curve $f(x)=3-2\ln x$. This is shown in the following diagram.

![ai1232a](images/ai1232a.png)

For this model, Molly calculates the sum of square residuals ($SS_{res}$) to be $0.448\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$.

Molly then tries a second model, $g(x)=5(0.5)^x$.

1. Calculate $SS_{res}$ for the second model, $g(x)$. [3 marks]

Molly decides to use a comparison of the $SS_{res}$ values to determine which model, $f(x)$ or $g(x)$, is the better fit for the points she marked on the Cartesian plane.

2. State which model Molly should use for her tagine. Justify your answer. [1 mark]

Molly will take the model selected in part (b), for $1 \leq x \leq 4$, and rotate the function $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis to form her tagine.

3. Calculate the volume of the tagine generated by this model. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The following table shows

    - The values generated by the model, $g(x)$;

    - The residual for each value;

    - The square of each residual.

    | &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;$x$&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; | &nbsp;&nbsp;$y \,\text{(observed)}$&nbsp;&nbsp; | $g(x)$ | $\text{Residual}$ | $\text{(Residual)}^2$ |

    | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

    | 1   | 3     | 2.5   | -0.5    | 0.25  |

    | 2   | 1     | 1.25  | 0.25   | 0.0625|

    | 3   | 0.6   | 0.625 | 0.025  | 0.000625|

    | 4   | 0.4   | &nbsp;&nbsp;0.3125&nbsp;&nbsp;| &nbsp;&nbsp;-0.0875&nbsp;&nbsp; | &nbsp;&nbsp;0.00765625&nbsp;&nbsp;|

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $SS_{res}$ is the sum of the values in the $(\text{Residual})^2$ column. Hence we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    SS_{res} &= 0.25 + 0.0625 + 0.000625 + 0.00765625 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &= 0.32078125 \\[8pt]

    SS_{res} &= \boxed{0.321} \hspace{1em}\text{(3 s.f.)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The model with the lower value for $SS_{res}$ would be considered the better fit, because essentially, there is a smaller difference between the observed values and the values generated by that model.

    For $f(x)$, we have $SS_{res}=0.448$. For $g(x)$, we have $SS_{res}=0.321$.

    We can see that $0.321 < 0.448$.

    Molly should choose $g(x)$ as the model for her tagine, because its $SS_{res}$ value is lower than that of $f(x)$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Note: The following diagram shows $f(x)$ in blue, and $\color{red}{g(x)}$ in $\text{\color{red}{red}}$. Visually, we can see that in general, $g(x)$ is passing closer to the marked points than $f(x)$.

    ![ai1232b](images/ai1232b.png)

3. We need to calculate a volume of revolution about the $y$-axis using the function $g(x)=5(0.5)^x$. 

    The function $g(x)$ is shown on the following set of axes, for $1 \leq x \leq 4$, which is the domain that was specified.

    ![ai1232c](images/ai1232c.png)

    We have marked the coordinates of the endpoints, which we have from our table in part (a).

    We are rotating about the $y$-axis, so the $y$-coordinates of these two endpoints will be the lower and upper limits for our integration, as shown in the following diagram. The shaded area will be rotated $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis.

    ![ai1232d](images/ai1232d.png)

    When we rotate about the $x$-axis, we use the formula

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    V &= \int_{a}^{b} \pi y^2 \,\mathrm{d}x \hspace{3em}\text{ where } y = f(x)

    \end{aligned} 

    $$

    For this rotation about the $y$-axis, we will be using the formula

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    V &= \int_{a}^{b} \pi x^2 \,\mathrm{d}y \hspace{3em}\text{ where } x = f(y)

    \end{aligned} 

    $$

    Let's rearrange our function to make $x$ the subject. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y &= 5(0.5)^x \\[8pt]

    \frac{y}{5} &= 0.5^x \\[8pt]

    \ln \left(\frac{y}{5}\right) &= \ln 0.5^x \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    \ln \left(\frac{y}{5}\right) &= x\ln 0.5 \\[8pt]

    \frac{\ln \left(\frac{y}{5}\right)}{\ln 0.5} &= x \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This expression can be directly substituted into the volume formula in place of $x$.

    We need values for $a$ and $b$. These will be equal to $g(1)$ and $g(4)$, because we are rotating about the $y$-axis, not the $x$-axis. We have shown these in the previous diagram, $g(1)=2.5$ and $g(4)=0.3125$. Hence $a=0.3125$ and $b=2.5$ because $a$ must be the lower of the two values. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Finally, we can substitute these into the formula, and calculate our volume using the G.D.C. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    V &= \int_{a}^{b} \pi x^2 \,\mathrm{d}y \\[8pt]

    &= \int_{0.3125}^{2.5} \pi \left(\frac{\ln \left(\frac{y}{5}\right)}{\ln 0.5}\right)^2 \,\mathrm{d}y \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

    &= 32.08...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the model $g(x)$, the volume of the tagine will be $32.1\,\text{cm}^3\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
