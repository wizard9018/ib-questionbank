# AI440

**Maximum mark: 7**

A curve $y = f(x)$ passes through point P$(2,7)$ and has a gradient of
$f'(x) = 4x - 3$.

1.  Find the gradient of the curve at point P. [2 marks]

2.  Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at point P.
    [2 marks]

3.  Determine the equation of the curve. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting $x = 2$ into $f'(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= 4x - 3 \\[6pt]
    f'(2) &= 4(2) - 3 \\[6pt]
    &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  From part (a), the gradient of the curve at point P$(2,7)$ is
    $m = 5$. Using the point-slope form of a straight line, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[3pt]
    y - 7 &= 5(x - 2) \\[3pt]
    y - 7 &= 5x - 10 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.55em}y = 5x - 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Integrating $f'(x)$ to get $f(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= \int (4x - 3) \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= 4\left(\dfrac{x^2}{2} \right) - 3(x) + C \\[3pt]
    &= 2x^2 - 3x + C
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    where $C$ is a constant.

    Since the curve passes through point P($2,7$), the coordinates of
    point P must satisfy the equation $y = f(x)$. Hence,

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    7 &= f(2) \\[3pt]
    7 &= 2(2)^2 - 3(2) + C \\[3pt]
    7 &= 8 - 6 + C \\[3pt]
    7 &= 2 + C \\[6pt]
    C &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, the equation of the curve is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y = 2x^2 - 3x + 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
