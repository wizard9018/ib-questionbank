# AI647

**Maximum mark: 12**

Water is flowing out of a tank at a rate modelled by the function

$$
R'(t)=4\sin\left(\dfrac{t}{100}\right)
$$
Water is flowing into the
same tank at a rate modelled by the function

$$
S'(t)=\dfrac{12t^2}{1+t^3}
$$
Both $R'$ and $S'$ are measured in m$^3$,
and $t$ in hours for $0\leq t \leq 10$.

1.  Find the interval on which the amount of water in the tank is
    increasing.[3 marks]

2.  Find an expression, $T$, for the amount of water in the tank at time
    $t$ if initially there was $25$ m$^3$ of
    water in the tank.[6 marks]

3.  Hence, or otherwise, find the value of the maximum amount of water
    in the tank and the time it occurs.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The net rate of change of water in the tank is given by
    $S'(t)-R'(t)$. If we graph $S'(t)-R'(t)$, using G.D.C., we get

    ![eabd52d7e8c3454ceb6408a15b0e90d241fceb9f.svg](images/eabd52d7e8c3454ceb6408a15b0e90d241fceb9f.svg)

    Therefore, we conclude the amount of water in the tank is increasing
    on the interval $0< t\leq10$

2.  The amount of water in the tank is given by the integral of
    $S'(t)-R'(t)$. First, we find the indefinite integral

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int [S'(t)-R'(t)] \, \mathrm{d}t &= \int \bigg[\dfrac{12t^2}{1+t^3}-4\sin\left(\dfrac{t}{100}\right)\bigg] \, \mathrm{d}t \rule[-5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    &= 4\ln (t^3+1)+400\cos\left(\dfrac{t}{100}\right)+C\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now, using that $T(0)=25$ m$^3$, we solve
    for $C$ in equation (1):
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    4\ln ((0)^3+1)+400\cos\left(\dfrac{0}{100}\right)+C &=25 \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{4mm}\\
    400 + C &= 25 \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    C &= -375\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore the expression for the amount of water in the tank is

    $$
    T(t)=4\ln (t^3+1)+400\cos\left(\dfrac{t}{100}\right)-375
    $$

3.  From part (a), we found that the amount of water in the tank is
    always increasing, for $t>0$. Therefore the maximum amount of water
    in the tank will occur at $t=10$, and the amount of water is

    $$
    T(10)\approx50.6\hspace{0.15em}\text{m}^3
    $$
