# AI419

**Maximum mark: 6**

A toy racing car is driving over a hill on a path that can be modelled
by part of the function
$$
f(x) = -\dfrac 1 4 x^2 + 5.
$$

1.  Find the gradient of the hill at the point where $x = 2$.
    [3 marks]

2.  Find the $x$ value of the point where the gradient perpendicular to
    the hill is equal to $\dfrac 3 2$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The gradient of the hill at any given point is found by the
    derivative of $f(x)$, $f'(x)$. Differentiating $f(x)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left( -\dfrac{1}{4}x^2 + 5 \right) \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac 1 4 (2x) + 0 \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, the gradient of the hill at $x = 2$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(2) &= -\dfrac 1 2 (2) \\[6pt]
    &= -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Let P be the point where the gradient perpendicular to the hill is
    equal to $1$. The following diagram shows the tangent and normal to
    the hill at point P.

    ![939aab187d673011b19b5150a8f6d334d1745f22.svg](images/939aab187d673011b19b5150a8f6d334d1745f22.svg)

    Using the relationship between the gradients of perpendicular lines,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m_T &= \dfrac{-1}{m_N} \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{-1}{3/2} \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac{2}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence $f'(x_P) = -\dfrac{2}{3}$. Substituting
    $f'(x) = -\dfrac 1 2 x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac{1}{2}x_P &= -\dfrac{2}{3} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.6em}x_P = \dfrac{4}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
