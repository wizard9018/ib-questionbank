# AI622

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f(x) = g(x)h(x)$, where $g(3) = 6$, $h(3) = 2$, $g'(3) = 4$ and
$h'(3) = 1$.

Find the equation of the normal to the graph of $f$ at $x = 3$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let's first find the $y$-coordinate on $f$ when $x=3$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y   & =  f(x) \\[5pt]
& =  f(3) \\[5pt]
& =  g(3)h(3) \\[5pt]
& =  6\cdot 2 \\[5pt]
& =  12.
\end{aligned}
$$
Now let's find the slope of the tangent of
$f$ when $x=3$. We will need to use the product rule to do this

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.3cm}
f'(3)       & =  g'(3)h(3) + g(3)h'(3) \\[5pt]
& = 4\cdot2 + 6\cdot 1 \\[5pt]
& =  14.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence the slope of the normal to the
curve at $(3,12)$ is $m = -\dfrac{1}{14}$ and the equation of the normal
is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_1     & =  m(x - x_1) \hspace{4mm}\\[8pt]
y - 12  & =   -\dfrac{1}{14}(x - 3) \\
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\hspace{.7cm}y      =  -\dfrac{1}{14}x + \dfrac{171}{14}
$$
