# AI629

**Maximum mark: 7**

A conical tank is being filled with water. The dimensions of the tank
are shown in the following diagram.

![45028fa4e153a20692110ae83851d07547ede425.svg](images/45028fa4e153a20692110ae83851d07547ede425.svg)

1.  Show that an expression for the volume, $V$, in the tank in terms of
    the water level height, $h$, is[3 marks]

    $$
    V =\dfrac{\pi h^3}{27}
    $$

2.  The tank is being filled at a rate of
    $1$ m$^3$ min$^{-1}$.
    Find the rate of change of the water height $h$ at the instant when
    the water level is $2$ m high. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  First, let's express the radius of the water level in terms of $h$.
    Considering the cross-sectional view of the right side of the tank

    ![cb5df27a3dc3442c8d5cba70fff217566af49039.svg](images/cb5df27a3dc3442c8d5cba70fff217566af49039.svg)

    Using similarity between the triangle delimited by the tank and the
    triangle corresponding to the portion of water, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{r}{2} &= \dfrac{h}{6}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    r &= \dfrac{h}{3} \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now, substituting (1) in the volume equation for a cone we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \dfrac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{1}{3} \pi \left(\dfrac{h}{3}\right)^2 h \rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{12mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{\pi h^3}{27} \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the chain rule, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}V} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using $V(h)$, we can find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \dfrac{\pi h^2}{9} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \therefore \hspace{1.5em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}V} &= \dfrac{9}{\pi h^2}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, we now have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{9}{\pi h^2} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now, substituting $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}=1$ and $h=2$, we
    obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{9}{\pi (2)^2} \times (1) \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &\approx 0.716197 \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the water level height is rising at a rate of
    $\text{0.716}\text{ m/min}$
