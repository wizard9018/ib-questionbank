# AI617

**Maximum mark: 12**

Consider the function defined by

$$
f(x)=\dfrac{-4x^2+12}{x^3}, \qquad x\neq 0
$$

1.  Find an expression for $f'(x)$, the derivative of $f(x)$.[3 marks]

2.  Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point
    $x=1$.[4 marks]

3.  Find the $x$-coordinates of the points on the curve where the
    gradient is zero.[2 marks]

4.  Determine the intervals on which $f(x)$ is increasing.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Taking the derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$, using the quotient
    rule, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{(x^3)(-8x)-(-4x^2+12)(3x^2)}{(x^3)^2} \rule[-5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{[(-8x^4)-(-12x^4+36x^2)]}{x^6}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{13mm}\\
    &\hspace{0.29em}= \dfrac{4(x^2-9)}{x^4} \rule[0mm]{0pt}{8mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  First we find the slope of the tangent curve at $x=1$ by evaluating
    the derivative at $x=1$,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(1) &=  \dfrac{4((1)^2-9)}{(1)^4}=-32 \rule[-6mm]{0pt}{13mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Thus, using that $f(1)=8$ and $f'(1)=-32$ we obtain the equation of
    the tangent to the curve at $x=1$ as follows
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y-y_1 &= m (x-x_1) \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    y-8 &= -32(x-1) \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    y-8 &= -32x+32 \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    & \hspace{-1.8mm}y= -32x+40\rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Letting $f'(x)=0$ and solving for $x$ we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{4(x^2-9)}{x^4} &= 0\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    4(x^2-9) &=0\rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm} \hspace{3em} [\text{as $x^4>0$}] \\
    x^2-9 &= 0 \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    &\hspace{-2mm}x=\pm 3 \rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  **Method 1.** Using the ID Test, we
    have

    Therefore, $f$ is increasing on the intervals
    $\left(-\infty, -3\right)\text{ and }\left(3, \infty\right)$

    **Method 2.** To determine on which interval(s) $f$ is increasing we
    solve the inequality $f'(x)>0$, thus we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{4(x^2-9)}{x^4}&>0\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    4(x^2-9) &>0 \hspace{3em} [\text{As $x^4>0$}] \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    x^2-9 &>0\rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    x^2&>9\rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    x &>3 \hspace{0.8em}\text{and}\hspace{0.8em} x <-3\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, $f$ is increasing on the intervals
    $\left(-\infty, -3\right)\text{ and }\left(3, \infty\right)$
