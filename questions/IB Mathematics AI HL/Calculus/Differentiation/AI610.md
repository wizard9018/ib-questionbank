# AI610

**Maximum mark: 15**

The number of fish in a lake after $t$ weeks is modelled by

$$
F(t)=\dfrac{4\hspace{0.05em}500}{1+4e^{-0.5t}} \hspace{0.5em},\hspace{0.5em} t\geq0.
$$

1.  Find the initial fish population.[2 marks]

2.  Find the percentage increase of the population after 1
    week.[3 marks]

3.  Find the limit of the population size. [3 marks]

4.  Find $F'(t)$, and determine if the population is increasing or
    decreasing over time.[3 marks]

5.  Find the rate at which the population is increasing after 8
    weeks.[2 marks]

6.  Sketch the graph of $F(t)$.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The initial population is obtained by evaluating $F(t)$ when $t=0$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    F(0) &= \dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500}{1+4e^{-0.5(0)}} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500}{5} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{12mm}\\
    &\hspace{0.3em}= 900\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The population after 1 week is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    F(1) &= \dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500}{1+4e^{-0.5(1)}} \rule[-5.5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    &\approx 1\hspace{0.05em}313.44 \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{9.5mm}\hspace{4em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    therefore, the percentage increase of the population after 1 week is

    $$
    \dfrac{F(1)-F(0)}{F(0)}\times 100\% = \dfrac{1313.44-900}{900}\times 100\% \approx 45.9\%.
    $$

    Thus, the population increase is
    $\text{45.9\%}$

3.  Taking the limit of $F(t)$ as $t$ tends to infinity, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{t\to \infty} F(t) &= \lim_{t\to \infty} \dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500}{1+4e^{-0.5t}} \rule[-5.5mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500}{1+4\cancel{\left( \displaystyle\lim_{t\to \infty}e^{-0.5t}\right)}}\rule[-5.5mm]{0pt}{12mm}\\
    &\hspace{0.29em}= 4\hspace{0.1em}500 \rule[0mm]{0pt}{6.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    This limit means that the population size stabilizes at
    $4\hspace{0.1em}500$ fish, this size is known as the carrying
    capacity.

4.  Taking the derivative of $F$ with respect to $t$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    F'(t) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \left(\dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500}{1+4e^{-0.5t}}\right)\rule[-5.5mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}\\
    &= -\dfrac{4\hspace{0.1em}500\big((-0.5)4e^{-0.5t}\big)}{(1+4e^{-0.5t})^2}\rule[-5.5mm]{0pt}{12.5mm}\\
    &\hspace{0.3em}= \dfrac{9\hspace{0.1em}000e^{-0.5t}}{(1+4e^{-0.5t})^2}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{7.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The derivative found above will always positive as the numerator and
    the denominator will always be positive for any value of $t$. Thus,
    the population is always increasing over time.

5.  Substituting $t=8$ in $F'(t)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    F'(8) &= \dfrac{9\hspace{0.1em}000e^{-0.5(8)}}{(1+4e^{-0.5(8)})^2}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &\approx 143.104 \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{8mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore,
    the population is increasing at a rate of
    $143\text{ fish per week}$
