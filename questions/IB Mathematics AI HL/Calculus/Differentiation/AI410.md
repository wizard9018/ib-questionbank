**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the graph of the function $f(x)=-x^3+3x^2+1$.

![0bbb30502fe1ff981ff2cb2c927916600eb820a0.svg](images/0bbb30502fe1ff981ff2cb2c927916600eb820a0.svg)

1.  Label the local minimum as A on the graph. [1 mark]

2.  Label the local maximum as B on the graph. [1 mark]

3.  Write down the interval where $f'(x) > 0$. [1 mark]

4.  Draw the tangent to the curve at $x=-1$ on the graph. [1 mark]

5.  Write down the equation of the tangent at $x=-1$. [2 marks]

3.  The condition $f'(x) > 0$ implies that the function is increasing.
    This occurs between points A and B on the graph. Using our GDC, we
    find that the function is increasing on the interval $(0,2)$.

    Hence, $f'(x) > 0$ where
    $0< x < 2$

4.  Substituting $x = -1$ into $f(x)$ to find the corresponding $y$
    value, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(-1) \\[3pt]
    &= -(-1)^3 + 3(-1)^2 + 1 \\[3pt]
    &= 5 \hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The tangent passes through the coordinate
    $(-1, 5)$ and is parallel to the graph at this point. The tangent is
    shown in red on the graph below.

    ![40cecbd2efd0b595c761bcb6047e665878f824cd.svg](images/40cecbd2efd0b595c761bcb6047e665878f824cd.svg)

5.  The equation of a straight line in slope-intercept form is
    $y = mx + c$.\
    $m$ is the gradient of the tangent at $x=-1$. By using GDC, we find
    that $m = -9$.\
    So far we have $y = -9x + c$.\
    Substituting $(-1,5)$ into the equation to find $c$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    5   & = -9(-1) + c\\[6pt]
    5   & = 9 + c\\[6pt]
    c & = -4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, the equation of the tangent at $x=-1$
    is   $y = -9 x - 4$
