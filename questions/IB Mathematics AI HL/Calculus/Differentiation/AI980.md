# AI980

**Maximum mark: 17**

The cross-sectional view of a two-lane road tunnel system is shown on
the axes below. The left and right lane tunnels are separated by a 2
metre thick concrete wall. The right-hand tunnel passes through the
points A, B, C and D and its height, in metres, above the base of the
tunnel, is modelled by
$f(x) = -0.04x^3 + 0.41x^2,\hspace{0.25em} 4 \leq x \leq 10$, relative
to an origin O.

![bd2faf45e9322e2e5ea6944efbec6b7df161abe4.svg](images/bd2faf45e9322e2e5ea6944efbec6b7df161abe4.svg)

Point A has coordinates $(4,4)$ and point D has coordinates $(10,1)$.

1.  Find the height of the right-hand tunnel when:
    1.  $x=6$;

    2.  $x = 8$. [3 marks]

  The left-hand tunnel can be
modelled by a function $g(x)$, found by reflecting $f(x)$ in the line
$x = 3$.

2.  Find the equation of $g(x)$. [4 marks]

3.
    1.  Find $g'(x)$.

    2.  Hence find the maximum height of the left-hand tunnel.
        [6 marks]

4.
    1.  Write down an integral which can be used to find the
        cross-sectional area of the left-hand tunnel.

    2.  Hence find the combined cross-sectional area of both tunnels.
        [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Evaluating $f(x)$ for $x = 6$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(6) &= -0.04(6)^3 + 0.41(6)^2 \hspace{1.3em} \\[12pt]
        &= 6.12
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         $\text{When \(x = 6\), the height of the right-hand tunnel is \(6.12\)\,m.}$
    2.  Evaluating $f(x)$ for $x = 8$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(8) &= -0.04(8)^3 + 0.41(8)^2 \\[12pt]
        &= 5.76
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         $\text{When \(x=8\), the height of the right-hand tunnel is \(5.76\)\,m.}$

2.  Given that $g(x)$ is found by reflecting $f(x)$ in the line $x = 3$,
    we can find four points that lie on $g(x)$ (A$'$, B$'$, C$'$ and
    D$'$) by using points A, B, C and D that lie on $f(x)$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\text{A} = (4,4) \Rightarrow \text{A}' = (2,4) \hspace{7em}\text{B} = (6,6.12) \Rightarrow \text{B}' = (0,6.12)\\[8pt]
    &\text{C} = (8,5.76) \Rightarrow \text{C}' = (-2,5.76) \hspace{3.7em}\text{D} = (10,1) \Rightarrow \text{D}' = (-4,1)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

     By performing a cubic regression on points
    A$'$, B$'$, C$'$ and D$'$, we find that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{-0.4em}g(x)=0.04x^3-0.31x^2-0.6x+6.12
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        g'(x) &= 3 \times 0.04 x^2 - 2 \times 0.31 x - 0.6\\[8pt]
        &\hspace{-2.1em}g'(x) = 0.12x^2 - 0.62x - 0.6
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  If we solve the equation $g'(x) = 0$ for $x \in [-4,2]$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        0.12x^2 - 0.62x - 0.6 &= 0 \\[8pt]
        x &\approx -0.8333 \hspace{2.9em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \hspace{2.6em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, evaluating $g(x)$ for $x = -0.8333$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        g(x) &= 0.04(-0.8333)^3 - 0.31(-0.8333)^2 - 0.6(-0.8333) + 6.12 \\[8pt]
        &\approx 6.38
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         $\text{The maximum height of the left-hand tunnel is approximately \(6.38\)\,m.}$

4.
    1.  Using $A_L$ to denote the cross-sectional area of the left-hand
        tunnel, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &\hspace{-1.3em}A_L = \int_{-4}^{\,2} \big[\hspace{0.15em}0.04x^3-0.31x^2-0.6x+6.12\big]\,\mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using $A_C$ to denote the combined cross-sectional area of both
        tunnels and given that both tunnels have an identical
        cross-section, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        A_C &= 2 \times \int_{-4}^{\,2} \big[\hspace{0.15em}0.04x^3-0.31x^2-0.6x+6.12\big]\,\mathrm{d}x  \\[12pt]
        &= 60.96\hspace{0.25em}\text{m}^2 \hspace{2.9em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         $\text{The combined cross-sectional area of both tunnels is approximately \(61\,\text{m}^2\).}$
