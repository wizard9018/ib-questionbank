# AI413

**Maximum mark: 13**

The function $f$ is in the following form, where $p$ is a constant.

$$
f(x)= x^3 + px^2 - 9x - 2
$$
 A part of the
graph of the function $y = f(x)$ is shown in the diagram below.

![4bb47bb66976a8a8f018af0425a963a34cd16d96.svg](images/4bb47bb66976a8a8f018af0425a963a34cd16d96.svg)

1.  Write down the $y$-intercept of the graph. [1 mark]

2.  Find $f'(x)$. [2 marks]

The tangent line $L$ to the graph $y = f(x)$ at $x = 1$ is parallel to
the $x$-axis.

3.  Find:
    1.  the value of $p$;

    2.  the equation of line $L$. [5 marks]

4.  Determine the coordinates of another point on the graph
    $y = f(x)$, where the tangent line to the graph is parallel to the
    $x$-axis. [2 marks]

5.  Write down the interval where the gradient of the graph of
    $y = f(x)$ is negative. [1 mark]

6.  Determine all the possible values of $m$, for which the
    equation $f(x) = m$ has three solutions. Write down your answer
    using interval notation. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting $x = 0$ into $f(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(0) &= 0^3 + p(0)^2 - 9(0) - 2 \\[3pt]
    &= -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Thus the $y$-intercept is
     $(0, -2)$
     .

2.  Differentiating $f(x)$ gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= x^3 + px^2 - 9x - 2 \\[6pt]
    f'(x) &= 3x^{2} + p(2x^{1}) - 9(1x^{0}) - 0 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 3x^2 + 2px - 9
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  Since the tangent line $L$ is parallel to the $x$-axis, we have
        $f'(1) = 0$. This gives
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        3(1)^2 + 2p(1) - 9 &= 0 \\[3pt]
        3 + 2p - 9 &= 0 \\[3pt]
        &\hspace{-1.3em}p = 3 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  From part (c)(i), we obtain the function in complete form:
        $$
        f(x) = x^3 + 3x^2 - 9x - 2.
        $$
        To find the $y$-coordinate of
        the point where line $L$ is tangent to the graph $y = f(x)$, we
        substitute in $x = 1$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(1) &= (1)^3 + 3(1)^2 - 9(1) - 2 \\[3pt]
        &= -7
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Using the point-slope form of a straight
        line where $(x_1, y_1) = (1,-7)$ and $m = f'(1) = 0$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y - y_1 &= m(x-x_1) \\[3pt]
        y - (-7) &= 0(x - 1) \\[3pt]
        &\hspace{-0.5em}y = -7
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  A tangent to a graph parallel to the $x$-axis occurs at either a
    local maximum or minimum. Hence, another tangent parallel to the
    $x$-axis occurs at the local maximum, identified in red below.

    ![f034fae849df5db59534dc32f710eeed28606d80.svg](images/f034fae849df5db59534dc32f710eeed28606d80.svg)

    Using GCD, we find this other tangent occurs at
     $(-3, 25)$
     .

5.  The gradient of the graph is negative when the function is
    decreasing. Using parts (c) and (d), the function is decreasing
    between the local maximum located at $x=-3$ and the local minimum
    located at $x=1$.

    Hence the gradient of the graph is negative on the interval
     $(-3,1)$.

6.  The diagram below shows an example of a value for $m$, such that
    $f(x) = m$ has three solutions
    (intersections).

    ![f2112068ca7f1d88faf18f6ebf91ddd86a439a1d.svg](images/f2112068ca7f1d88faf18f6ebf91ddd86a439a1d.svg)

    The line $y = m$ can intersect the curve of $f(x)$ at three points
    only if the line lies between the local maximum and minimum of
    $f(x)$.

    Hence the interval of $m$ is  
    $(-7,25)$ .
