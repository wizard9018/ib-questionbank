# AI415

**Maximum mark: 6**

The equation of a curve is $y = 1 - \dfrac 9 x$.

1.  Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at
    $x = 3$. [3 marks]

2.  Find the coordinates of the points on the curve where the gradient
    is $\dfrac{1}{4}$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting in $x = 3$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= 1 - \dfrac{9}{x} \\[3pt]
    &= 1 - \dfrac{9}{3} \\[3pt]
    &= -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The tangent passes through $(3,-2)$. Now we find
    its gradient at this point. Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left( 1 - \dfrac 9 x\right) \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left( 1 - 9x^{-1}\right) \\[3pt]
    &= 0-9(-1x^{-2}) \\[3pt]
    &= 9x^{-2} \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{9}{x^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting in $x = 3$ to find the gradient at
    the point $(3,-2)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{9}{3^2} \\[3pt]
    &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the point-slope form of a straight line,
    where $(x_1,y_1) = (3,-2)$ and $m = 1$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[3pt]
    y - (-2) &= 1(x - 3) \\[3pt]
    y + 2 &= x - 3 \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{-0.7em}y = x - 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  From part (a), we have
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{9}{x^2}$. Let's
    substitute in $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac 1 4$ to
    find the $x$-coordinates where the gradient of the curve is
    $\dfrac 1 4$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac 1 4 &= \dfrac{9}{x^2} \\[3pt]
    x^2 &= 36 \\[3pt]
    x &= \pm 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We now use these $x$-coordinates to find their
    respective $y$-coordinates.

    \
    For $x = 6$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= 1 - \dfrac 9 6 \\[2pt]
    &= -\dfrac 1 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    For $x = -6$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= 1 - \dfrac 9 {-6} \\[2pt]
    &= \dfrac 5 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, the coordinates of the points are
     $\left(6, -\dfrac 1 2\right) \text{and} \left(-6, \dfrac 5 2\right)$
     .
