# AI818

**Maximum mark: 20**

Consider the function $f(x) = \dfrac {2}{x^2} - ax$, where $a$ is a
constant and $x \neq 0$.

1.  Find $f'(x)$. [2 marks]

The function $f(x)$ has a local minimum at $x = -2$.

2.  Show that $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$. [2 marks]

3.  Find the $y$-coordinate of the local minimum of the
    function. [2 marks]

4.  Sketch the graph of $f(x)$, for $-5 \leq x \leq 6$ and
    $-3 \leq y \leq 6$. [4 marks]

5.  State the values of $x$ for which $f(x)$ is increasing.
    [2 marks]

6.  Find the $x$-intercept of the graph of the function $f(x)$.
    [2 marks]

7.  Calculate $f'(2)$. [2 marks]

8.  Find the equation of the normal to the graph of $y = f(x)$
    at $x = 2$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We can rewrite $f(x)$ as follows.
    $$
    f(x) = 2x^{-2} - ax
    $$
    Differentiating $f(x)$ gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= 2 (-2x^{-3}) -a (1x^{0}) \\[3pt]
    &= -4x^{-3}-a \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= -a- \dfrac{4}{x^3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since the function has a local minimum at $x = -2$, $f'(-2) = 0$.
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(-2) &= 0 \\[3pt]
    -a- \dfrac{4}{(-2)^3} & = 0 \\[3pt]
    -a +\dfrac{1}{2}& = 0 \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{-1.3em}a = \dfrac{1}{2} \hspace{3em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We now have the completed function
    $$
    f(x) =   \dfrac {2}{x^2} - \dfrac{1}{2}x

    $$
    Since the function has
    a local minimum at $x = -2$, we substitute $x = -2$ into $f(x)$ to
    find its corresponding $y$-coordinate
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(-2) \\[3pt]
    & = \dfrac {2}{(-2)^2} - \dfrac{1}{2}(-2)\\[3pt]
    & = \dfrac {1}{2} + 1\\[3pt]
    &=1.5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  Substituting $x = 2$ into $f'(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= -\dfrac12 - \dfrac{4}{x^3}  \\[6pt]
    f'(2) &= -\dfrac 12 - \dfrac{4}{2^3} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}=-1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

8.  Let $m_T$ and $m_N$ be the gradients of the tangent and normal to
    the graph of $f(x)$ at $x = 2$. Using the relationships of the
    gradients of perpendicular lines, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m_N &= -\dfrac{1}{m_T} \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{f'(2)} \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{(-1)} \hspace{3em} [\text{from part (g)}]\\[3pt]
    &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We also need to find the coordinates of $f(x)$
    at $x = 2$. Substituting $x = 2$ into $f(x)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(2) \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac 2{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{2}(2) \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the point-slope form of a straight line,
    where $(x_1,y_1) = \left(2,-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ and $m =1$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[3pt]
    y - \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right) &= 1 (x - 2) \\[3pt]
    y + \dfrac{1}{2} &= x -2 \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-0.575em}y =x -\dfrac{5}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
