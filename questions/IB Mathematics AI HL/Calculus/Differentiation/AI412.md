# AI412

**Maximum mark: 14**

A function, $f$, is given by
$$
f(x)= 2 \cdot 3^{-x+1} + x - 3.
$$

1.  Calculate $f(1)$. [2 marks]

2.  Use your graphic display calculator to solve $f(x) = 0$. [2 marks]

3.  Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$ for $0 \leq x \leq 5$ and
    $-1 \leq y \leq 4$. Clearly show the coordinates of the $x$ and $y$
    intercepts. Use a scale of $1$ cm to represent $1$ unit on both the
    horizontal and vertical axes. [4 marks]

The function $f$ is the derivative of the function $g$. It is known that
$g(0) = 2$.

4.
    1.  Calculate $g'(0)$.

    2.  Find the equation of the normal of the graph of $y = g(x)$ at
        the point $(0,2)$. Give your answer in the
        form $ay + bx + c = 0$. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting $x = 1$ into $f(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(1) &= 2 \cdot 3^{-1+1} + 1 - 3 \\[3pt]
    &= 2 \cdot 1 + 1 - 3 \\[3pt]
    &\hspace{0.525em}= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  We want to solve for $x$ for when $f(x) = 0$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= 2 \cdot 3^{-x+1} + x - 3 \\[3pt]
    0    &= 2 \cdot 3^{-x+1} + x - 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using GDC, we get
     $x = 1 \text{ or }  x = 2.69$

4.
    1.  Since $g'(x) = f(x)$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        g'(0) &= f(0) \\[3pt]
        &= 2 \cdot 3^{-0 + 1} + 0 -3 \\[3pt]
        &= 2 \cdot 3^1 + 0 -3 \\[3pt]
        &\hspace{0.525em}= 3
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The gradient of the tangent ($m_T$) of $g(x)$ at $x=0$ is 3. We
        can find the gradient of the normal ($m_N$) using the
        relationship of the gradients of perpendicular lines

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        m_N &= \dfrac{-1}{m_T} \phantom{a\hspace{1.6cm}} \\[3pt]
        &= -\dfrac{1}{3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Using the point-slope form of a straight
        line where $(x_1, y_1) = (0,2)$ and $m = -\dfrac 1 3$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y - y_1 &= m(x-x_1) \\[3pt]
        y - 2 &= -\dfrac 1 3(x - 0) \\[3pt]
        y - 2 &= -\dfrac 1 3 x \\[3pt]
        3y - 6 &= -x \hspace{3em} [\text{x 3}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Rearranging into standard form, we get  
        $x + 3y - 6 = 0$  .
