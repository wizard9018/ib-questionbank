# AI619

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the curve $y = \dfrac{12x}{1+4x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$x \neq -\dfrac{1}{4}$.

1.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$. [2 marks]

2.  Determine the equation of the normal to the curve at the point
    P$\left(-1,4\right)$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[\dfrac{12x}{1+4x}\bigg] \rule[-5mm]{0pt}{13mm}\\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{(1+4x)(12) - (12x)(4)}{(1+4x)^2}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm} \hspace{3em} [\text{by quotient rule}] \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{12+48x - 48x}{(1+4x)^2}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{12}{(1+4x)^2}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{10mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Evaluating the gradient of the tangent to the curve at P, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.8em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg|_{x=-1} &= \dfrac{12}{(1+4(-1))^2} \\[5pt]
    &= \dfrac{12}{9} \\[11pt]
    &= \dfrac{4}{3} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the gradient of the normal
    to the curve at P is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m &= -\dfrac{3}{4}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the point-gradient formula
    with P$(-1,4)$ and $m = -\dfrac{3}{4}$, we find

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \hspace{2.8em} y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[10pt]
     y-4&= -\dfrac{3}{4}(x-(-1)) \\[10pt]
     y-4&= -\dfrac{3}{4}x -\dfrac{3}{4}\\[10pt]
     y&= -\dfrac{3}{4}x -\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{16}{4}\\
     \end{aligned}
     $$

    $$
    \hspace{2.6em} y =-\dfrac{3}{4}x+\dfrac{13}{4}
    $$
