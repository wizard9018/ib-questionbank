# AI1216

**Maximum mark: 7**

A cone has a vertical height of $h\,$cm and a base radius of $r\,$cm.
The cone has a fixed volume of $500\,$cm$^3$.
1.  Show that the surface area $A$ of the cone (including the base) can be written in terms of $r$ as

    $$A = \pi r \sqrt{r^2 + \left( \frac{1500}{\pi r^2} \right)^2} + \pi r^2$$ [3 marks]

2. Find the value of $r$ that minimizes the surface area. Give your answer correct to 2 decimal places. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the corresponding height $h$ of the cone when the surface area is minimized. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    The formula for the surface area is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \pi r \color{red}{l}\color{black}{ + \pi r^2} \\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    And we want to show it written as
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A = \pi r \color{red}{\sqrt{r^2 + \left( \frac{1500}{\pi r^2} \right)^2}}\color{black}{ + \pi r^2} \\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We have noted the difference between the two expressions in $\color{red}{\text{red}}$. What we need to do is express the slant height, $l$, in terms of $r$.

    The formula for $l$, in terms of $h$ and $r$, is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    l &= \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We need this only in terms of $r$. We can use information we have about the volume to do this.

    Starting with the formula for the volume of a cone
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h \\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We substitute in the given volume of 500
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    500 &= \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h  \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[8pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    And make $h$ the subject.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    h &= \frac{1500}{\pi r^2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting this expression for $h$ back into the slant height formula, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    l &= \sqrt{r^2 + \left( \frac{1500}{\pi r^2} \right)^2} \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[8pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Remembering the surface area
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \pi r l + \pi r^2 , \\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    we can substitute for $l$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{A = \pi r \sqrt{r^2 + \left( \frac{1500}{\pi r^2} \right)^2} + \pi r^2 }\hspace{1em}\text{...as required.}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. Using the G.D.C. to find radius for minimum surface area
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Sketch of }  A(r) = \pi r \sqrt{r^2 + \left( \dfrac{1500}{\pi r^2} \right)^2} + \pi r^2
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![ai1216graph](images/ai1216graph.png)

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{or Let } A'(r) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    r &= 5.526\ldots \\[6pt]
    r &=\boxed{5.53 \,\text{cm}}\hspace{1em}\text{(3 s.f.)}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. From part (a) we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    h &= \frac{1500}{\pi r^2} \\[8pt]
    h &= \frac{1500}{\pi (5.526...)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}  \\[8pt]
    &=  15.63... \, \text{cm} \\[8pt]
    h &=\boxed{15.6 \,\text{cm}}\hspace{1em}\text{(3 s.f.)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
