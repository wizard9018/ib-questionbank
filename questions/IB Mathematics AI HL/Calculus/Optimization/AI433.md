# AI433

**Maximum mark: 15**

A manufacturer makes chemical transport vessels in the form of a
cylinder with a hemispherical front. The vessel has a total length of
$4$ m. The base radius of both the cylinder and
the hemispherical front is $\text{\(1\)\hspace{0.25em}m}$.

![a61c482068ebdc28519400df94dea46bebca3eca.svg](images/a61c482068ebdc28519400df94dea46bebca3eca.svg)

1.  Write down the length of the cylinder. [1 mark]

2.  Find the total volume of the vessel. [4 marks]

A truck company is looking to optimize and improve the dimensions of the
vessel. The new vessel will also be in the form of a cylinder with a
hemispherical front. It will have a length of
$l$ m and a base radius of
$r$ m.

![be0693c387ec1dbefb1c422238b61c2ce0b1dec4.svg](images/be0693c387ec1dbefb1c422238b61c2ce0b1dec4.svg)

There is a design constraint such that
$l + 4r = 8$ m. The manufacturer wants to
maximize the volume of the vessel.

3.  Write down the length of the new vessel, $l$, in terms of
    $r$. [1 mark]

4.  Show that the volume, $V$ m$^3$, of
    the new vessel is given by [3 marks]
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= 8\pi r^2 - \dfrac{13\pi r^3}{3} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using your graphic display calculator, find the value of
    $r$ which maximizes the value of $V$. [2 marks]

The manufacturer claims that the new vessel has a capacity
$20$ % greater than the capacity of the original
vessel.

6.  State whether the manufacturer's claim is correct. Justify
    your answer.[4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The length of the cylinder, $l_c$, is the vessel's total length
    subtract the radius of the hemispherical front.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    l_c &= 4 - 1 \\[6pt]
    & = 3 \hspace{0.2em} \text{m}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The total volume of the vessel ($V_t$) is the sum of the volume of
    the cylinder ($V_c$) and the volume of the hemisphere ($V_h$)

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V_t &= V_c + V_h \\[3pt]
    &= \pi r^2 h + \dfrac{1}{2} \left(\dfrac 4 3 \pi r^3\right) \\[3pt]
    &=  \pi (1)^2 (3) + \dfrac 2 3 \pi (1)^3 \hspace{3em} [\text{$h$ = $l_c$}] \\[6pt]
    &\approx 11.5 \hspace{0.2em} {\text{m}^3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Rearranging for $l$ from the constraint $l + 4r = 8$, we get
    $$
    l = 8 - 4r
    $$

4.  The length of the cylinder, $l_c$, can be expressed as
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    l_c &= l - r \\[3pt]
    &= 8 - 4r - r \hspace{3em} [\text{from part (c)}]\\[3pt]
    &= 8 - 5r
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The total volume of the vessel is the sum of
    the volume of the cylinder and the volume of the hemispherical
    front. We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \pi r^2 h + \dfrac{1}{2} \left(\dfrac 4 3 \pi r^3 \right) \hspace{3em}\\[3pt]
    &= \pi r^2 (8 - 5r) + \dfrac 2 3 \pi r^3 \hspace{3em} [\text{$h$ = $l_c$}]\\[3pt]
    &= 8\pi r^2 - 5 \pi r^3 + \dfrac 2 3 \pi r^3 \\[6pt]
    &= 8\pi r^2 - \dfrac{13\pi r^3}{3} \hspace{3em} [\text{shown}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using G.D.C., we obtain the graph of $V$, in m$^3$, as shown. Note
    that we only the part of the graph when $V$ and $r$ are positive.

    ![40efc4f6f63ec07fbe37aa9d4e6542a5e49cf2ee.svg](images/40efc4f6f63ec07fbe37aa9d4e6542a5e49cf2ee.svg)

    Using G.D.C., we find the volume is maximised at
     $r \approx 1.23$

6.  Substituting $r = 1.23$ into $V$ to find the new maximum volume, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= 8 \pi (1.23)^2 - \dfrac{13\pi (1.23)^3}{3} \\[6pt]
    & \approx 12.7 \hspace{0.25em} \text{m}^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Comparing this to the volume of the original vessel (part (b)), its
    capacity has increased by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= \dfrac{12.7-11.5}{11.5} \times 100\%\\[6pt]
    &\approx 10.4\%
    \end{aligned}
    $$

     $\text{Thus, the manufacturer's claim is not correct.}$
