# AI422

**Maximum mark: 16**

The cost to manufacture badminton rackets at a company in Japan can be
modelled by the cost function
$$
C(x) = 8x^3 - 24x^2 + 28x

$$
where $x$ is
in hundreds of rackets and $C(x)$ is in hundreds of Japanese Yen (JPY).

1.  Find $C'(x)$. [2 marks]

The marginal cost of production is the cost of producing one additional
unit. This can be approximated by the gradient of the cost function.

2.  Find the marginal cost when $100$ rackets are produced and
    interpret its meaning in this context.[3 marks]

The revenue from selling the rackets is given by the function
$R(x) = 26x$ where $x$ is in hundreds of rackets and $R(x)$ is in
hundreds of JPY.

3.  Given that Profit $=$ Revenue $-$ Cost, determine a
    function for the profit, $P(x)$, in hundreds of
    JPY from selling $x$ hundreds of badminton rackets. [2 marks]

4.  Find $P'(x)$. [2 marks]

5.  Determine the intervals where $P(x)$ is increasing and
    decreasing. [4 marks]

The derivative $P'(x)$ gives the marginal profit. The production will
reach its optimal level when the marginal profit is zero and $P(x)$ is
positive.

6.  Find the optimal production level and the expected profit
    at this level. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Differentiating the cost function, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    C(x) &= 8x^3 - 24x^2 + 28x \\[6pt]
    C'(x) &= 8(3x^2) - 24(2x) + 28(1) \\[6pt]
    &= 24x^2 - 48x + 28
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since $x$ represents hundreds of rackets, we substitute $x = 1$ into
    $C'(x)$ and evaluate:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    C'(1) &= 24(1)^2 - 48(1) + 28 \\[3pt]
    &= 4 \text{ [hundreds of JPY]} \\[3pt]
    &= \text{JPY}400
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the marginal cost when $100$ rackets are
    produced is JPY$400$. For every additional racket produced, the cost
    increases by JPY$400$.

3.  Given that Profit $=$ Revenue $-$ Cost, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    P(x) &= R(x) - C(x) \\[3pt]
    &=  26x - (8x^3 - 24x^2 + 28x) \\[6pt]
    &= -8x^3 + 24x^2 - 2x
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Differentiating $P(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    P'(x) &= -8(3x^2) + 24(2x) - 2(1) \\[6pt]
    &= -24x^2 + 48x - 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The graph below shows the curve of $P(x)$ with the coordinates of
    the turning point shown. Note that $x$ (number of rackets) is
    restricted to be non-negative.

    ![0c8701ebbcec376afc3706ca972055f390d933cc.svg](images/0c8701ebbcec376afc3706ca972055f390d933cc.svg)

    $\text{Hence, \(P(x)\) is increasing on \((0,1.96)\) and decreasing on \((1.96,\infty)\).}$

6.  From part (e) we see that the marginal profit is zero at $x = 1.96$
    (i.e. production of $196$ rackets). We can therefore calculate the
    expected profit at this production level
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    P(1.96) &=  -8(1.96)^3 + 24(1.96)^2 - 2(1.96) \\[3pt]
    &\approx 28 \text{ hundreds of JPY} \\[3pt]
    &\approx \text{JPY}2800
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Thus, the optimal production level is $196$ rackets and the expected profit at this level is JPY$2800$.
