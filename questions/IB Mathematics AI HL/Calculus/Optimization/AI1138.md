# AI1138

**Maximum mark: 16**

Danyon has a backyard that consists of a triangular swimming pool, of area $A\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}^2$, and concrete tiling surrounding it. His triangular backyard has a total area of $900\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}^2$.

The width of the concrete tiling, from east to west, on both sides of the pool, is such that the horizontal distance from the corner of the backyard to the corner of the pool is $3\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}$. 

The height of the concrete tiling, from north to south, is $2\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}$ on the south side and $4\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}$ on the north side of the pool.

The width of Danyon's backyard, from east to west, is $x$ metres, $6 < x < 300$.

![ai1138ex](images/ai1138ex.PNG)

1. 1. Write down the width of the swimming pool in terms of $x$.

    2. Find an expression for the height of the swimming pool in terms of $x$.

    3. Hence show that $A = 918 - 3x - \dfrac{5400}{x}$. [5 marks]

2. Find possible heights and widths of Danyon's backyard if the area of the swimming pool is $648\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}^2$. [4 marks]

3. 1. Find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}x}$.

    2. Hence find the value of $x$ that maximises the area of the swimming pool.

    3. Find the maximum possible area of the swimming pool.[7 marks]

    2. Let the height of the backyard be $h$, then the height of the swimming pool is $h-6$. We now need an expression for $h$ in terms of $x$.

        Given that the total area is $900\hspace{0.1em}\mathrm{m}^2$, we can use the formula for the area of a triangle to make an equation relating $h$ and $x$. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        A &= \dfrac{1}{2}bh \\[8pt]

        900 &= \dfrac{1}{2}xh \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]

        1800 &= xh \\[8pt]

        h &= \dfrac{1800}{x} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$
