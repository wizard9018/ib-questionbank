# AI427

**Maximum mark: 17**

A water tank is made from glass in the shape of a cylinder with a
hemisphere on its bottom base. The internal height of the cylinder is
$h$ cm and the internal radius is $r$ cm. The tank has no top.

![40161c1b9b29cbbaf26931771eb06fbb2f3c07db.svg](images/40161c1b9b29cbbaf26931771eb06fbb2f3c07db.svg)

1.  Write down a formula, in terms of $r$ and $h$, for $A$, the total
    surface  [2 marks] area of the tank.

The volume of the tank is $120$ litres.

2.  Express this volume in cm$^3$. [1 mark]

3.  Write down an equation, in terms of $r$ and $h$, for the
    volume of this tank. [2 marks]

4.  Show that
    $A = \dfrac 2 3 \pi r^2 + \dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r}$.
    [3 marks]

5.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r}$.[2 marks]

6.  Find the value of $r$ which minimizes the surface area,
    $A$. [3 marks]

7.  Hence, find the minimum surface area, $A$, correct to the
    nearest cm$^2$. [2 marks]

The glass surface of these water tanks is to be covered by a special
dirt protection spray. Each bottle of the spray can be used to cover
$6400$ cm$^2$ of glass surface.

8.  Using your answer from part (g), calculate the number of
    tanks the company can coat using $40$ bottles this dirt protection
    spray.  [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Since the tank has no top, the total surface area consists of two
    areas; the cylinder curved surface and the hemisphere. Hence, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \text{Area of cylinder curved surface} + \text{Area of hemisphere} \\[3pt]
    &= 2\pi r h + \dfrac 1 2 (4 \pi r^2) \\[6pt]
    &= 2\pi r h + 2 \pi r^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the conversion rate $1$L = $1000$ cm$^3$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    120 \text{L} &= 120 \times 1000 \\[6pt]
    &= 120\hspace{0.15em}000 \text{ cm}^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The volume of the tank consists of two portions; the volume of a
    cylinder and the volume of a hemisphere. Hence, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V &= \text{Volume of cylinder} + \text{Volume of hemisphere} \\[3pt]
    &= \pi r^2 h + \dfrac 1 2 \left(\dfrac 4 3 \pi r^3\right) \\[6pt]
    &= \pi r^2 h + \dfrac 2 3 \pi r^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Starting with volume, using parts (b) and (c), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \pi r^2 h + \dfrac 2 3 \pi r^3 &= 120\hspace{0.15em}000 \\[3pt]
    3\pi r^2 h + 2\pi r^3 &= 360\hspace{0.15em}000 \\[3pt]
    3\pi r^2 h &= 360\hspace{0.15em}000 - 2\pi r^3 \\[3pt]
    h &= \dfrac{1}{3\pi r^2}(360\hspace{0.15em}000 - 2\pi r^3) \\[3pt]
    h &= \dfrac{120\hspace{0.15em}000}{\pi r^2} - \dfrac{2r} 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting this expression for $h$ into $A$
    (from part (a)), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= 2\pi r h + 2 \pi r^2 \\[3pt]
    &= 2\pi r \left( \dfrac{120\hspace{0.15em}000}{\pi r^2} - \dfrac{2r} 3 \right) + 2 \pi r^2 \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r} - \dfrac{4\pi r^2}{3} + 2\pi r^2 \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r} + \dfrac{2\pi r^2}{3} \hspace{3em} [\text{shown}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Differentiating $A$ with respect to $r$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r} \left(\dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r} + \dfrac{2\pi r^2}{3} \right) \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r} \left(240\hspace{0.15em}000 r^{-1} + \dfrac 2 3 \pi r^2 \right) \\[3pt]
    &= 240\hspace{0.15em}000(-1r^{-2}) + \dfrac 2 3 \pi (2r^{1}) \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r^2}  + \dfrac 4 3 \pi r
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  Let's visualize the graph of $A$ for a given radius $r$

    ![995bd33bbf3d8f2b7df39f0df6635a4d67662d20.svg](images/995bd33bbf3d8f2b7df39f0df6635a4d67662d20.svg)

    The surface area $A$ is minimised where
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r} = 0$. This gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r^2}  + \dfrac 4 3 \pi r &= 0 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.6em}r \approx 38.6 \text{ cm} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  Substituting in $r = 38.6$ into our area expression, $A$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{r} + \dfrac{2\pi r^2}{3} \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{240\hspace{0.15em}000}{38.6} + \dfrac{2\pi (38.6)^2}{3} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 9338 \text{ cm}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

8.  The number of tanks that the company can coat is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    n &= \dfrac{40 \times 6400}{9338.2} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 27 \text{ tanks}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
