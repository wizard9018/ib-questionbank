# AI772

**Maximum mark: 16**

The cost to manufacture an electronic micro-component at a company can
be modelled by the cost function
$$
C(x) = x^3 -3x^2 + 4x

$$
where $x$ is
in hundreds of micro-components, and $C(x)$ is in hundreds of dollars.

1.  Find $C'(x)$. [2 marks]

The marginal cost of production is the cost of producing one additional
unit. This can be approximated by the gradient of the cost function.

2.  Find the marginal cost when $200$ micro-components are
    produced and interpret its meaning in this context.[3 marks]

The revenue from selling the micro-components is given by the function

$$
\begin{aligned}
R(x) = 0.6x^3+x^2+10x-2
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x$ is in hundreds of micro-components
and $R(x)$ is in hundreds of dollars.

3.  Given that Profit $=$ Revenue $-$ Cost, determine a
    function for the profit, $P(x)$, in hundreds of
    dollars from selling $x$ hundreds of micro-components. [2 marks]

4.  Find $P'(x)$. [2 marks]

5.  Determine the intervals where $P(x)$ is increasing and
    decreasing. [4 marks]

The derivative $P'(x)$ gives the marginal profit. The production will
reach its optimal level when the marginal profit is zero and $P(x)$ is
positive.

6.  Find the optimal production level and the expected profit
    at this level. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Differentiating the cost function, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        C(x) &= x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x \\[6pt]
        C'(x) &= 3x^2 - 3(2)x + 4 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= 3x^2 - 6x + 4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since $x$ represents hundreds of micro-components, we substitute
    $x = 2$ into $C'(x)$ and evaluate:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        C'(2) &= 3(2)^2 - 6(2) + 4 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
              &= \$ 4\, [\text{hundreds of dollars}] \\[6pt]
              &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= \$ 400} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the marginal cost when $200$ micro-components are produced is
    $$\$400$$.

    For every additional $100$ micro-components produced, the
    cost increases by $$\$400$$.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Given that Profit $=$ Revenue $-$ Cost, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        P(x) &= R(x) - C(x) \\[6pt]
             &=  0.6x^3+x^2+10x-2 - (x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[7pt]
        &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= -0.4x^3 + 4x^2 + 6x - 2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Differentiating $P(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        P'(x) &= -0.4(3)x^2 + 4(2)x + 6(1) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[7pt]
        &\hspace{0em}\boxed{= -1.2x^2 + 8x + 6} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The graph below shows the curve of $P(x)$ with the coordinates of
    the turning point shown. Note that $x$ (number of micro-components)
    is restricted to be non-negative.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M2)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![ai772](images/ai772.JPG)

    We can see that $P(x)$ is increasing to the left of the turning point (the gradient is positive) and decreasing to the right of the turning point (the gradient is negative).

    Hence, $P(x)$ is increasing for $0 \leq x < 7.35$ and decreasing for $x > 7.35$.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A2}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  From part (e) we see that the marginal profit is zero at $x = 7.35$
    (i.e. production of $735$ micro-components). We can therefore
    calculate the expected profit at this production level

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        P(7.35) &=  -0.4(7.35)^3 + 4(7.35)^2 + 6(7.35) -2 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[3pt]
            &\approx 99.36 \text{ hundreds of dollars} \\[3pt]
            &\approx \$9\,936
    \end{aligned}
    $$

     $$\text{Thus, the optimal production level is \(735\) micro-components and the expected profit at this level is \(\$9\,936\).}$$
     $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A2}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
