# AI420

**Maximum mark: 16**

The diagram below shows the travel path of the Orange Star cargo ship
from an aerial view.

![c0ce3f8f430c4dc9f5b02f8d9cc1b92a0dd23ba4.svg](images/c0ce3f8f430c4dc9f5b02f8d9cc1b92a0dd23ba4.svg)

The travel path of the Orange Star can be modelled by the quadratic
equation

$$
y = -\dfrac{x^2}{12} + \dfrac{35x}{2}, \,\, x \geq 0, \, y \geq 0
$$

where $x$ and $y$ represent the distance, in kilometres, due east and
north from a port located at O$(0,0)$, respectively. The ship stops at a
port located at P$(180,450)$ for refuelling before continuing its
journey.

There is another port located at a point C$(120,900)$.

1.  Determine whether the Orange Star cargo ship passes through port C.
    Give a reason for your answer. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$.

    2.  Hence determine whether port C is the point furthest north on
        the travel path between the ports O and P. Give a reason for
        your answer.[5 marks]

3.
    1.  Find the midpoint of the line segment OP.

    2.  Find the gradient of the line segment OP. [4 marks]

Another cargo ship, The Black Pearl, departs from a new point B$(0,100)$
and travels along a straight path that is parallel to the line segment
OP.

4.  Find the equation of the Black Pearl's travel path. Express
    your answer in the form $ax + by + d = 0$, where $a, b$ and
    $d \in \mathbb{Z}$.  [3 marks]

5.  Find the coordinates of the point where the Black Pearl's
    travel path crosses the Orange Star's travel path for a second time.
    [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  This ship passes through C if substituting the coordinates of
    C($120$,$900$) satisfy the quadratic equation modelling the travel
    path
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    900 &= -\dfrac{(120)^2}{12} + \dfrac{35(120)}{2} \\[3pt]
    900 &= 900
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\text{Hence, the Orange Star passes through port C.}$

2.
    1.  Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(-\dfrac{x^2}{12} + \dfrac{35x}{2}\right) \\[3pt]
        &= -\dfrac{2x}{12} + \dfrac{35}{2} \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{x}{6} + \dfrac{35}{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Port C will be point furthest north on the travel path if it is
        the local maximum of the graph. At $x = 120$,
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= -\dfrac{120}{6} + \dfrac{35}{2} \\[3pt]
        &=-\dfrac 5 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$
         Since
        $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} \neq 0$ at $x = 120$, C is not
        a stationary point on the graph.\
        $\text{Hence port C is not the point furthest north on the travel path}$

3.
    1.  Using the midpoint formula, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        M &= \left( \dfrac{x_\text{O} + x_\text{P}}{2}, \dfrac{y_\text{O} + y_\text{P}}{2}\right) \\[3pt]
        &= \left( \dfrac{0 + 180}{2}, \, \dfrac{0 + 450}{2}\right) \\[3pt]
        &= \left( 90, 225 \right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The gradient of the line segment OP is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        m_\text{OP} &= \dfrac{y_\text{P} - y_\text{O}}{x_\text{P} - x_\text{O}} \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{450-0}{180-0} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac 5 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  Since the path of the Black Pearl is parallel to the line segment
    OP, its gradient is equal to $m_\text{OP}$. Using the point-slope
    form of a straight line where $(x_1,y_1) = (0,100)$ and
    $m = \dfrac 5 2$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[3pt]
    y - 100 &= \dfrac 5 2 (x - 0) \\[3pt]
    y - 100 &= \dfrac 5 2 x \\[3pt]
    2y - 200 &= 5x \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-3.3em}5x - 2y + 200 = 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The Orange Star and Black Pearl's path are shown on the diagram
    below

    The red point shows the location where the Black Pearl crosses the
    Orange Star's path for a second time.

     $\text{(173,533)}$
