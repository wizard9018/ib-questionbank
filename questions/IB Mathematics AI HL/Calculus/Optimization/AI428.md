# AI428

**Maximum mark: 15**

A steel pail is made in the shape of a cylinder with an internal height
$h$ cm and internal base
radius $\text{\(r\)\hspace{0.2em}cm}$.

![19995a04341d11f80a3d4bd4e752af23d53f71a4.svg](images/19995a04341d11f80a3d4bd4e752af23d53f71a4.svg)

The steel pail has an open top. The inner surfaces of the pail are to be
coated with a protective resin.

1.  Write down a formula for $A$, the internal surface area to be
    coated. [2 marks]

The volume of the steel pail is
$10\hspace{0.1em}000$ cm$^3$.

2.  Write down, in terms of $r$ and $h$, an equation for the
    volume of [1 mark] this steel pail.

3.  Show that $A = \pi r^2 + \dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{r}$.
    [3 marks]

The steel pail is designed so that the area to be coated is minimised.

4.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r}$. [2 marks]

5.  Using your answer to part (d), find the value of $r$ which
    minimizes $A$.  [3 marks]

6.  Hence, find the value of this minimum area, correct to the
    nearest cm$^2$. [2 marks]

One can of protective resin coats a surface area of
$350$ cm$^2$.

7.  Find the minimum number of cans of protective resin
    required to coat [2 marks]the area found in part (f).

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Since the steel pail has open head, its total internal surface area
    consists of two portions only;
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \text{Area of cylinder curved surface} + \text{Area of bottom face (circle)} \\[3pt]
    &= 2\pi r h + \pi r^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  This steel pail is made in the shape of a cylinder of which the
    volume is given by $V = \pi r^2 h$. Hence we get

    $$
    \pi r^2 h = 10\hspace{0.1em}000
    $$

3.  Rearranging our volume expression (part (b)) to be in terms of $h$,
    we get
    $$
    h = \dfrac{10\hspace{0.1em}000}{\pi r^2}
    $$
    Substituting
    this into $A$ from part (a), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= 2\pi r h + \pi r^2\\[3pt]
    &= 2\pi r \left(\dfrac{10\hspace{0.1em}000}{\pi r^2} \right) + \pi r^2\\[6pt]
    &=  \dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{r} + \pi r^2 \hspace{3em} [\text{shown}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Differentiating $A$ with respect to $r$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r} \left(\dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{r} + \pi r^2 \right) \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r} \left(20\hspace{0.1em}000 r^{-1} + \pi r^2 \right) \\[3pt]
    &= 20\hspace{0.1em}000(-1r^{-2}) + \pi (2r^{1}) \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{r^2}  + 2 \pi r
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Let's visualize the graph of $A$, in cm$^2$, for a given radius $r$

    ![4009d0ca3525773996ede4bb75bcbd8fb6788297.svg](images/4009d0ca3525773996ede4bb75bcbd8fb6788297.svg)

    The surface area $A$ is minimised when
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r} = 0$. We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{r^2}  + 2 \pi r &= 0 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.6em}r \approx 14.7 \text{ cm} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  Substituting $r = 14.7$ into our expression for $A$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{r} + \pi r^2 \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{20\hspace{0.1em}000}{14.7} + \pi (14.7)^2 \\[6pt]
    &\approx 2039 \text{ cm}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  The number of cans of protective resin needed to coat the area is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    n &= \dfrac{2039}{350} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 6 \text{ cans} \hspace{3em} [\text{rounding up}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
