# AI679

**Maximum mark: 19**

A proposed differential equation modelling the displacement of a person descending a water slide is
$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2 x}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = 10-0.8\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)
$$
where $x$ is the displacement of the person in metres at time $t$ seconds.

It can be assumed that initially, $x=\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=0$.

1. By using the substitution $v=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}$, find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}$ in terms of $v$.[2 marks]

2. Hence use Euler's method for coupled systems, with a step size of 0.5, to approximate a person's velocity after 5 seconds. [3 marks]

3. Solve the differential equation formed in part (a) to find an expression for $v$ in terms of $t$.[6 marks]

The $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to \infty} v(t)$ is known as the terminal velocity.

4. State the value of the terminal velocity.[1 mark]

5. Determine the time at which $v(t)$ reaches $96\%$ of the terminal velocity. Give your answer to the nearest second.[3 marks]

6. Calculate the percentage error in the approximation of the velocity found by Euler's method at this time.[2 marks]

7. Explain why Euler's method has overestimated the velocity at this time.[2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. If $v=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}$, then $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2 x}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting these expressions into our original differential equation, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\boxed{\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = 10-0.8v} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. In order to use euler's method for coupled systems, we need to determine formulae that will generate the next of values, $x_{n+1}, v_{n+1}$ and $t_{n+1}$, based on the values of $x_n, v_n$ and $t_n$.

    We also have been given $h=0.5$, $x_0=0$, $v_0=0$ and $t_0=0$.

    Our formulae will be

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x_{n+1} &= x_n + h \times f_1(x_n,v_n,t_n) \\[8pt]
    v_{n+1} &= v_n + h \times f_2(x_n,v_n,t_n) \\[8pt]
    t_{n+1} &= t_n + h
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Where $f_1(x_n,v_n,t_n)=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x_n}{\mathrm{d}t_n}$ and $f_2(x_n,v_n,t_n)=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v_n}{\mathrm{d}t_n}$

    Hence we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x_{n+1} &= x_n + 0.5 v_n \\[8pt]
    v_{n+1} &= v_n + 0.5 (10-0.8v_n) \\[8pt]
    t_{n+1} &= t_n + 0.5 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the G.D.C., we can generate 10 steps of Euler's method, which will take us to $t=5$, the time at which we need to approximate the velocity.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The following table shows these steps, the final step is highlighted in red.

    ![euler](images/euler.JPG)

    Therefore after $5$ seconds, the approximate velocity is $12.4\,\mathrm{ms}^{-1}\,\text{(3 s.f.)}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. We have here the differential equation formed in part (a).

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = 10-0.8v
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    To solve this, we must integrate. But first we must separate the variables, in order to have our expression for $v$ on the left hand side (so we can integrate with respect to $v$). We have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \displaystyle{\int}\dfrac{1}{10-0.8v}\,\mathrm{d}v &= \displaystyle{\int}\mathrm{d}t \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    -\frac{1}{0.8} \ln |10-0.8v| &= t + C \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    At this point we could note that $-\frac{1}{0.8} = -\frac{5}{4}$. Hence we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\frac{5}{4} \ln |10-0.8v| &= t + C
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using our initial condition, $v=0$ when $t=0$, we can solve for $C$.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\frac{5}{4} \ln |10-0.8(0)| &= 0 + C \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    C &= -\frac{5}{4} \ln 10
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\frac{5}{4} \ln |10-0.8v| &= t -\frac{5}{4} \ln 10 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We could now multiply through by $-\frac{4}{5}$ to make things more straightforward:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \left(-\frac{4}{5}\right)\left(-\frac{5}{4} \ln |10-0.8v|\right) &= \left(-\frac{4}{5}\right)\left(t -\frac{5}{4} \ln 10 \right) \\[8pt]
    \ln |10-0.8v| &= -\frac{4t}{5} + \ln 10 \\[8pt]
    e^{\ln |10-0.8v|} &= e^{-\frac{4t}{5} + \ln 10} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    |10-0.8v| &= e^{-\frac{4t}{5}}\cdot e^{\ln 10} \hspace{3em}[\text{because $a^{m+n}=a^m \cdot a^n$}] \\[8pt]
    10 - 0.8v &= 10\,e^{-\frac{4t}{5}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Note that in the last step we have removed the absolute value signs, because the right hand side is greater than 0 for all values of $t$.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -0.8v &= -10 + 10\,e^{-\frac{4t}{5}} \\[8pt]
    v &= \dfrac{-10 + 10\,e^{-\frac{4t}{5}}}{-0.8} \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-0.9em}\boxed{v = 12.5 - 12.5\,e^{-\frac{4t}{5}}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\textbf{Note: there are several correct ways to write the final answer.}$

4. As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-\frac{4t}{5}} \to 0$, so the function approaches a value of $12.5$ (from below).

    $$\text{The terminal velocity is $12.5\,\mathrm{ms}^{-1}$.}$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5. We need to solve the following equation for $t$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    12.5 - 12.5\,e^{-\frac{4t}{5}} &= 0.96(12.5) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the numerical solver on the G.D.C., we find that $t=4.023...$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    To the nearest second, $v(t)$ reaches $96\%$ of the terminal velocity after 4 seconds.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6. Using our table from part (b), we see that Euler's method predicted a velocity of $12.29...\mathrm{ms}^{-1}$ after 4 seconds.

    Using the percentage error formula with $v_A = 12.29...$ and $v_E = 0.96(12.5) = 12$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \varepsilon &= \bigg|\dfrac{12.29... - 12}{12}\bigg| \times 100\% \\[8pt]
    &= 2.417...\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-0.6em}\boxed{\varepsilon = 2.42\%\,\text{(3 s.f.)}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7. Our function, $v(t)$, is always concave down.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Euler's method will always overestimate if a function is concave down, and always underestimate if a function is concave up.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
