# AI668

**Maximum mark: 14**

Consider the differential equation

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\sqrt{x}\hspace{0.1em}y, \quad x>0,\, y>0 \text{ where }y(1)=4.
$$

1.  Use Euler's method with a step length of 0.25 to fill in the
    following table, rounding each value to two decimal
    places.[4 marks]

    ![40174fe42b7244671fe04cf8aa4aee19a197d890.svg](images/40174fe42b7244671fe04cf8aa4aee19a197d890.svg)

2.  Solve the differential equation.[6 marks]

3.  Hence, find the exact value of $y(2)$ to $2$ decimal
    places.[2 marks]

4.  Calculate the percentage error in the value of $y(2)$ found by using
    Euler's method. Give your answer to $2$ significant
    figures.[2 marks]

1.  Using Euler's method and rounding to two decimal places, we

2.  Solving the differential equation by separating variables, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{y} &= \sqrt{x} \mathrm{d}x \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{y} &= \int \sqrt{x} \mathrm{d}x \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \ln y &= \dfrac{2x^{(3/2)}}{3} + C \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    y &=Ae^{\mbox{$\frac{2x^{(3/2)}}{3}$}}\hspace{3em} [\text{by letting  $A=e^C$}] \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    4 &=Ae^{\mbox{$\frac{2(1)^{(3/2)}}{3}$}}\hspace{3em} [\text{using $y(1)=4$}] \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    A &=4e^{\mbox{$-\frac{2}{3}$}} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    y &=4e^{\mbox{$-\frac{2}{3}$}}\cdot e^{\mbox{$\frac{2x^{(3/2)}}{3}$}} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &\hspace{-0.5em}y = 4e^{\frac{2}{3}(x^{(3/2)}-1)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using our equation from part (b), we sub in $x$ = 2
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y(2) &= 4e^{\frac{2}{3}(2^{(3/2)}-1)}\\
    y(2) &= 13.534...\\
    y(2) &= 13.53\,\ \text{(2 d.p.)} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
4.  Using the **formula for the percentage error (1.6)**, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \epsilon &= \left\lvert \dfrac{V_A-V_E}{V_E} \right\rvert \times 100\% \\[6pt]
    \epsilon &= \left\lvert \dfrac{11.12-13.53...}{13.53... }\right\rvert \times 100\% \\[6pt]
    &= 18\%\,\ \text{(2 s.f.)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
