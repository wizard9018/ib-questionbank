# AI982

**Maximum mark: 17**

A marine biologist is studying the population growth of a penguin colony
in a small archipelago that is free from predators. At the start of her
study, there was estimated to be $200$ penguins in the colony. It is
assumed the population growth of penguins can be modelled by the
differential equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 1.1P\\
\end{aligned}
$$
where $P$ is
the penguin population at time $t$ years.

1.  Find the population of penguins after $3$ years.[5 marks]

When the population of penguins reaches $5\,000$, it is noticed that a
group of $100$ leopard seals have settled in the area. The subsequent
population growth of penguins and leopard seals, where $L$ is the
population of leopard seals at time $t$, can be modelled by the
coupled differential equations
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}t} = P(2.2 - 0.011L) \qquad&\qquad
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}L}{\mathrm{d}t} = L(0.0002P-0.7) \\
\end{aligned}
$$

2.  Using Euler's method with a step size of $0.25$,
    estimate

    1.  the population of penguins $1$ year
        after the leopard seals were noticed;

    2.  the population of leopard seals
         $1$ year
        after they were noticed. [6 marks]

The graph of the population sizes, according to this model, for the
first $4$ years after the
leopard seals were noticed is shown below.

![ff76eaef0ece081ef841a5d36c5e7510373804cb.svg](images/ff76eaef0ece081ef841a5d36c5e7510373804cb.svg)

3.  Describe the changes in the populations of penguins and
    leopard seals for these $4$ years,

    1.  at point A;
    2.  at point B. [3 marks]

4.  Find the non-zero equilibrium point for the populations of
    penguins and leopard seals.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Solving the differential equation by separating variables, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{P}\,\mathrm{d}P &= 1.1\,\mathrm{d}t \hspace{4.2em}[P>0] \\[8pt]
    \int \dfrac{1}{P}\,\mathrm{d}P &= \int 1.1\,\mathrm{d}t \\[8pt]
    \ln P &= 1.1t + C \hspace{3em}[\text{for some } C \in \mathbb{R}] \\[8pt]
    P &= e^{1.1t + C} \\[8pt]
    P &= Ae^{1.1t}  \hspace{4em} [\text{where } A = e^C]\hspace{6.4mm}(1)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $t = 0$ and $P = 200$ into equation $(1)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    200 &= Ae^0 \hspace{0.4em} \\[8pt]
    A &= 200
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, evaluating $P = 200e^{1.1t}$ for
    $t = 3$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    P &= 200e^{1.1(3)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

     $\text{After 3 years there are approximately \(5420\) penguins}$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f_1(P,L) &= P(2.2 - 0.011L) \\[12pt]
    f_2(P,L) &= L(0.0002P - 0.7) \\[12pt]
    h &= 0.25\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \\
    t_{n+1} &= t_n + 0.25 \\[12pt]
    P_{n+1} &= P_n + 0.25P_n(2.2 - 0.011L_n) \\[12pt]
    L_{n+1} &= L_n + 0.25L_n(0.0002P_n - 0.7) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using
    Euler's method with $t_0 = 0$, $P_0 = 5\hspace{0.15em}000$ and
    $L_0 = 100$, we get

    ![a135cf3af169b575f6e19bcd800efe8320c5b59b.svg](images/a135cf3af169b575f6e19bcd800efe8320c5b59b.svg)

    Hence, using the table above, we find

    1.
        $\text{After 1 year there are approximately 11000 penguins}$

    2.
        $\text{After 1 year there are approximately 197 leopard seals}$

3.
    1.
        $\text{At point A, both populations are increasing.\phantom{\hspace{-0.15em}}}$
    2.
        $\text{At point B, the penguin population is decreasing. The leopard seal population is steady, but starting to decrease.}$

4.  Since both derivatives are zero at an equilibrium point, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    P(2.2 - 0.011L) &= 0 \\[12pt]
    L(0.0002P-0.7) &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note that at a non-zero
    equilibrium point, $P \neq 0$ then $L \neq 0$.\
    This implies that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2.2 - 0.011L &= 0 \hspace{5em}{(2)} \\[12pt]
    0.0002P-0.7 &= 0 \hspace{5em}{(3)} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving equations
    $(2)$ and $(3)$ for $P$ and $L$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \\
    P = 3\hspace{0.15em}500 \text{ penguins} \hspace{1.25em}\text{and}\hspace{1.25em} L = 200 \text{ leopard seals} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore the non-zero equilibrium point is
     $\mathrm{P}(3\hspace{0.15em}500, 200)$
