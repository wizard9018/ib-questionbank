# AI1200

**Maximum mark: 15**

In certain parts of Australia, there are two common types of ant, the bull ant and the coastal brown ant. The population growth of each species is given by the coupled system

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 8x - 6y \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 5x - 5y

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $x$ is the population of bull ants in trillions, and $y$ is the population of coastal brown ants, also in trillions. $t$ is time in years.

1. 1. Write the system in the form 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \dot{x} \\[2pt] \dot{y} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) &= A\hspace{0.2em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} x \\[2pt] y \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        where $A$ is a $2\times2$ matrix.

    2. Show that the eigenvalues of matrix $A$ are $\lambda_1 = -2$ and $\lambda_2 = 5$.

    The eigenvector associated with $\lambda_1 = -2$ is $\bm{p}_1=\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 \\ 5 \end{matrix}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em}$

    3. Find the eigenvector $\bm{p}_2$, associated with $\lambda_2 = 5$.

    4. Hence state the general solution to the coupled system. [7 marks]

2. Sketch a phase portrait for the general solution to the coupled system on the following axes. [3 marks]

    ![1200a](images/1200a.JPG)

Over a long period of time, the population of each species will reach a constant ratio.

3. State the long-term ratio of bull ants to coastal brown ants.[1 mark]

A biologist is studying the growth of each population, and estimates at the start of her research that there are $7$ trillion of each species.

4. Find the exact solution to the coupled system. [3 marks]

5. On the following axes, sketch the approximate future path for this exact solution. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. The matrix $A$ will be a matrix of the coefficients from our differential equations. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        &\boxed{\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \dot{x} \\[2pt] \dot{y} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) = \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 8 & -6 \\[2pt] 5 & -5 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} x \\[2pt] y \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. To find the eigenvalues of a $2 \times 2$ matrix, we use the formula $|A - \lambda I|=0$. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \Bigg|\begin{pmatrix} 8 - \lambda & -6 \\[4pt] 5 & -5 - \lambda \end{pmatrix} \Bigg| &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[15pt]

        (8 - \lambda)(-5 - \lambda) - (-6)(5) &= 0 \\[6pt]

        \lambda^2 -3\lambda -10 &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

        &\hspace{-5.4em}\boxed{\lambda_1 = -2 \enskip \lambda_2 = 5} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    3. To find the eigenvector $\lambda_2$ of the $2 \times 2$ matrix $A$, we solve the equation

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \begin{pmatrix} 8 - \lambda_2 & -6 \\[4pt] 5 & -5 - \lambda_2 \end{pmatrix} \,\times \, \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} x \\[4pt] y \end{matrix}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em}  &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 0 \\[4pt] 0 \end{matrix}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The values we find for $x$ and $y$ will be the elements of eigenvector $\bm{p}_2$. 

        For $\lambda_2=5$, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -6 \\[4pt] 5 & -10 \end{pmatrix} \,\times \, \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} x \\[4pt] y \end{matrix}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em}  &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 0 \\[4pt] 0 \end{matrix}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        This leads to two linear equations in $x$ and $y$, which will yield the same values:

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        3x - 6y &= 0 \tag*{(1)} \\[6pt]

        5x - 10y &= 0 \tag*{(2)}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Using equation $(1)$, we find that $x=2$ and $y=1$ is a solution.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Therefore our eigenvector associated with $\lambda_2=5$ is $\bm{p}_2 = \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{matrix}\bigg)\hspace{0.1em}$ 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    4. Using the formula for the exact solution for coupled linear differential equations from the formula sheet, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \bm{x} &= Ae^{\lambda_1 t}\bm{p}_1 + Be^{\lambda_2 t}\bm{p}_2 \\[6pt]

        &\hspace{-2.6em}\boxed{\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} x \\[2pt] y \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) = Ae^{-2t}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 \\[2pt] 5 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) + Be^{5t}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 2 \\[2pt] 1 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Note: because we cannot evaluate $A$ and $B$, this is a $\textbf{general}$ solution. If we had been able to evaluate $A$ and $B$, we could have found an $\textbf{exact}$ solution.
