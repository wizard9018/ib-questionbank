# AI672

**Maximum mark: 17**

Suppose the population size of a bee colony in units of $10$ is $N$. At time $t$ weeks, the rate of change of the population can be modelled by the differential equation

$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0.4N-0.8t$

1. Given that $N=a+bt$, for $a,b \in$ $\mathbb{R}$, is a solution to the differential equation for a particular initial population, find the values of $a$ and $b$. [4 marks]

The slope field for the differential equation is shown below

![AI672](images/AI672.PNG)

2. Sketch on the slope diagram:

    1. the line $N=a+bt$

    2. the trajectory of the population if at $t=0$, $N=3$. [2 marks]

3. Find the least value for $N$ at $t=0$ that will ensure the population does not become extinct. [2 marks]

A beekeeper measuring the population $N$ determines it will reach a maximum after two and a half weeks and then will begin to decline.

4. Write down an approximation for $N$ at that time. [2 marks]

The beekeeper decides to introduce more bees at $t=2.5$.

5. If the model remains valid, find the least number of bees $N$ that needs to be added in order for the population to continue to increase in size as time increases. [2 marks]

Suppose that $N=80$ after $4$ weeks.

6. Estimate $N$ after $5$ weeks by using Euler's method with a step size of $0.2$. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting the expression for $N$ into the differential equation,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \big(a+bt\big) &= 0.4(a+bt)-0.8t \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    b &= 0.4a+0.4bt -0.8t \rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    \intertext{Using that $a+bt$ is the solution of an initial population, we get}
    b &= 0.4a \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    \intertext{substituting (2) in the left side of (1), we get}
    0.4a &= 0.4a+0.4bt-0.8t \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    0.4bt &= 0.8t \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    b &= \dfrac{0.8}{0.4} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &\hspace{-0.4em} b = 2 \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    \intertext{substituting the value of $b$ in (2), we find}
    a &= \dfrac{2}{0.4} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &\hspace{-0.5em}a= 5\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Plotting the line $N=5+2t$ and the solution curve with initial value
    $3$, we get

    ![86ee5d0d32228ca23a7384edf3bc93df776299b8.svg](images/86ee5d0d32228ca23a7384edf3bc93df776299b8.svg)

3.  We note from the sketch of the graph in part (b) that the line
    $N=2t+5$ is the first solution curve (for the minimum initial
    value), which keeps increasing as time $t$ increases. Therefore the
    minimum initial value for which population does not become extinct
    is $N = 5$

4.  From the slope diagram, we find the curve that has a maximum at
    $t=2.5$ as follows

    ![84d31f5d3ebad0f62d8028ad670d6204864cf86f.svg](images/84d31f5d3ebad0f62d8028ad670d6204864cf86f.svg)

    Therefore, we conclude the population at $t=2.5$ is
    $5$

5.  In part (b) we determine the first curve for which the population
    keeps increasing. Therefore the amount of bees to add is equal to
    the difference between the number of bees on the line $N=2t+5$ at
    $t=2.5$ and $5$:
    $$
    2(2.5)+5-5= 5
    $$
    Therefore, the beekeeper needs to
    increase $N$ by
    $5$ .

6.  Using Euler's method and rounding to two decimal places, we get

    ![33b4233637163eef86f6c49144ceb842ad223e8c.svg](images/33b4233637163eef86f6c49144ceb842ad223e8c.svg)

    Therefore, after $5$ weeks
    $N\approx113$
