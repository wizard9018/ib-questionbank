# AI986

**Maximum mark: 18**

Consider the following system of coupled dif$\text{f}$erential
equations.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 2x+3y \\[10pt]
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 2x+y\\
\end{aligned}
$$
The system can
be written in the form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \dot{x} \\[2pt] \dot{y} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg) &= A\hspace{0.2em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} x \\[2pt] y \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $A$ is a $2\times2$ matrix.

1.
    1.  Write down matrix $A$.

    2.  Find the eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors of matrix
        $A$. [7 marks]

2.  Hence write down the general solution of the system. [2 marks]

3.  Determine whether the equilibrium point E$(0,0)$ is stable or
    unstable. Justify your answer. [2 marks]

4.  Find the value of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ at point:
    1.  P$(5,0)$;

    2.  Q$(-5,0)$. [3 marks]

5.  Sketch a phase portrait for the general solution to the system of
    coupled dif$\text{f}$erential equations
    for $-8\leq x \leq 8$ and $-8\leq y \leq 8$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.
        $A = \bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} 2 & 3 \\[2pt] 2 & 1 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)$
    2.  If we solve the equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$ for
        $\lambda$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \bigg|\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} 2-\lambda & \hspace{0.65em}3 \\[10pt] \hspace{0.65em}2 & 1-\lambda \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg| &= 0 \\[10pt]
        (2-\lambda)(1-\lambda) - (3)(2) &= 0 \\[12pt]
        \lambda &= -1,\hspace{0.5em}4  \hspace{3em}{\text{$[\text{by using G.D.C.}]$}} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Let $\lambda_1 = -1$ and $\lambda_2 = 4$. Solving the equation
        $(A - \lambda_1 I)\mathbf{p}_1 = 0$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 & 3 \\[2pt] 2 & 2 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em} &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$0$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$0$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Which leads to the system of linear equations
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        3x + 3y &= 0 \\[10pt]
        2x + 2y &= 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Therefore $y = -x$. Choose $x = 1$
        and $y = -1$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{p}_1 &= \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 1 \\[2pt] -1 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Solving the equation $(A - \lambda_2 I)\mathbf{p}_2 = 0$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \bigg(\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{matrix} -2 & 3 \\[2pt] 2 & -3 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$x$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$y$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\hspace{-0.15em} &= \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} \raisebox{0.06em}{$0$} \\[2pt] \raisebox{0.175em}{$0$} \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Which leads to the system of linear equations
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        -2x + 3y &= 0 \\[10pt]
        2x - 3y &= 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Therefore $3y = 2x$. Choose $x = 3$
        and $y = 2$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{p}_2 &= \hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 \\[2pt] 2 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \\
        \lambda_1 = -1,\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{p}_1 = \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 1 \\[2pt] -1 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\phantom{\hspace{-0.15em}} \hspace{1.0em}\text{and}\hspace{1.0em} \lambda_2 = 4,\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{p}_2 = \bigg(\hspace{0.1em}\begin{matrix} 3 \\[2pt] 2 \end{matrix}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg)\phantom{\hspace{-0.15em}} \hspace{1.65em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  The equilibrium point E$(0,0)$ is
     $\text{unstable}$
    since
     $\phantom{\hspace{-0.05em}}\lambda_2 = 4 > 0\phantom{\hspace{-0.05em}}$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\raisebox{0.675em}{$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}$}}{\raisebox{-0.675em}{$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}$}} \\[10pt]
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{2x+y}{2x+3y} \hspace{3em}{(1)}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    1.  Evaluating expression $(1)$ at P$(5,0)$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\Big|_\mathrm{P} &= \dfrac{2(5)+0}{2(5)+3(0)} \\[11pt]
        &= 1
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         $\text{The value of \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) at P\((5,0)\) is \(1\).}$

    2.  Evaluating expression $(1)$ at Q$(-5,0)$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\Big|_\mathrm{Q} &= \dfrac{2(-5)+0} {2(-5)+3(0)} \\[11pt]
        &= 1
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         $\text{The value of \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\) at Q\((-5,0)\) is \(1\).}$
