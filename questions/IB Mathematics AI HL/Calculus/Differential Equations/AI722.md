# AI722

**Maximum mark: 17**

$N$ is the population size of brown bears in a forest, in units of
$100$. At time $t$ months, the rate of change of the population can be
modelled by the differential equation

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0.3N-0.72t
$$

1.  Given that $N=a+bt$, for $a,b \in$ $\mathbb{R}$, is a solution to
    the differential equation for a particular initial population, find
    the values of $a$ and $b$.[4 marks]

The slope field for the differential equation is shown below

![41e1f5e7521e2b4c9eb763071141129f6d22243a.svg](images/41e1f5e7521e2b4c9eb763071141129f6d22243a.svg)

2.  Sketch on the slope diagram:
    1.  the line $N=a+bt$

    2.  the trajectory of the population if at $t=0$, $N=6$.[2 marks]

3.  Find the least value for $N$ at $t=0$ that will ensure the
    population\
    does not become extinct.[2 marks]

A group of conservationists concerned with the brown bears extinction\
determines that the brown bear population will reach a maximum after\
six months and then begin to decline.

4.  Write down an approximation for $N$ at that
    time.[2 marks]

The conservationists decide to introduce more bears at $t=6$
months.

5.  If the model remains valid, find the least number of bears
    needed\
    to be added for the population to continue to increase in the
    future.[2 marks]

Suppose that $N=28$ after $8$ months.

6.  Estimate $N$ after $9$ months by using Euler's method with
    a step size of $0.2$.[5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting the expression for $N$ into the differential equation,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \big(a+bt\big) &= 0.3(a+bt)-0.72t \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    b &= 0.3a+0.3bt -0.72t \rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    \intertext{Using that $a+bt$ is the solution of an initial population, we get}
    b &= 0.3a \rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    \intertext{substituting (2) in the left side of (1), we get}
    0.3a &= 0.3a+0.3bt-0.72t \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    0.3bt &= 0.72t \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    b &= \dfrac{0.72}{0.3} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &\hspace{-0.4em} b = 2.4 \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    \intertext{substituting the value of $b$ in (2), we find}
    a &= \dfrac{2.4}{0.3} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &\hspace{-0.5em}a= 8\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Plotting the line $N=8+2.4t$ and the solution curve with initial
    value $6$, we get

    ![efa69428cf0b2d2a20e1797aad7af627998b79da.svg](images/efa69428cf0b2d2a20e1797aad7af627998b79da.svg)

3.  We note from the sketch of the graph in part (b) that the line
    $N=8+2.4t$ is the first solution curve (for the minimum initial
    value), which keeps increasing as time $t$ increases. Therefore the
    minimum initial value for which population does not become extinct
    is $N = 8$

4.  From the slope diagram, we find the curve that has a maximum at
    $t=6$ as follows

    ![4cf7ebe20409da71e84b39be8e6a4a4804bcaf6f.svg](images/4cf7ebe20409da71e84b39be8e6a4a4804bcaf6f.svg)

    Therefore, we conclude the population at $t=6$ is
    $14$

5.  In part (b), we determine the first curve for which the population
    continues to increase. Therefore the amount of bears to add is equal
    to the difference between the number of bears on the line $N=8+2.4t$
    at $t=6$ and $14$:
    $$
    8+2.4(6)-14= 8.4
    $$
    Therefore, the
    conservationists need to increase $N$ by
    $8.4$ , or
    equivalently, to add
    $840 \text{ bears}$.

6.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 0.3N - 0.72t \hspace{8em}\\[6pt]
    \hspace{6.25em} N(8) &= 28 \\[6pt]
    h &= 0.2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using Euler's method, we obtain

    ![15784056ddb8f158c3c1228296c541ef91d5c03f.svg](images/15784056ddb8f158c3c1228296c541ef91d5c03f.svg)

    Therefore, after $9$ months
    $N\approx 30.7$
