# AI674

**Maximum mark: 6**

Solve the differential equation
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+x^2)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2xy^2
\end{aligned}
$$
for
$y$, which satisfies the initial condition $y(0)=-\dfrac{1}{2}$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Rewriting the differential equation and integrating with respect to $x$
and $y$,\
we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{y^2} &=\dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\[8pt]
\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{y^2} &=\int \dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\[4pt]
-\dfrac{1}{y} &= \ln(1+x^2)+C \rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}\quad [\hspace{0.01em}\text{since }1+x^2>0\hspace{0.01em}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $x=0$ and $y=-\dfrac{1}{2}$ in (1), we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\dfrac{1}{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)} &= \ln(1+(0)^2)+C \rule[-5mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
2 &= \cancel{\ln(1)}+C\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
C &= 2\rule[-2mm]{0pt}{7mm}
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting the value of
$C$ in (1), we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
-\dfrac{1}{y} &= \ln(1+x^2)+2 \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\[2pt]
&\hspace{-2mm}y = -\dfrac{1}{\ln(1+x^2)+2}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}
\end{aligned}
$$
