# AI979

**Maximum mark: 8**

A particle $P$ moves is a straight line such that its displacement $x$
at time $\text{\(t \geq 0\)}$ is given by the
dif$\text{f}$erential equation
$\dot{x} = 2x\big(\hspace{-0.25em}-\hspace{-0.15em}te^{-t^2}\big)$. At
time $\text{\(t = 0\)}$,
 $x = 2$.

1.  Use Euler's method with step length $0.1$ to find an approximation
    for $x$ when $t = 0.4$, giving your answer to $4$ significant
    figures. [3 marks]

2.  By solving the differential equation, find the percentage error
    in your approximation for $x$ when $t = 0.4$.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have $f(t, x) = 2x\big[\hspace{-0.25em}-\hspace{-0.15em}te^{-t^2}\big]$
    and $h = 0.1$.

    Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    t_{n+1} &= t_n + 0.1 \\[8pt]
    x_{n+1} &= x_n + 0.1\hspace{0.1em}\Big(2x_n\big[\hspace{-0.25em}-\hspace{-0.15em}t_ne^{-{t_n}^2}\big]\Big)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using Euler's method with $t_0 = 0$ and $x_0 = 2$, we get

    ![c57bd8fa5d9ee2dce791d599a07905f0fa094322.svg](images/c57bd8fa5d9ee2dce791d599a07905f0fa094322.svg)

    Hence we find
     $x(0.4) \approx 1.782$

2.  Solving the differential equation by separating variables, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x &= \big[\hspace{-0.25em}-\hspace{-0.15em}2te^{-t^2}\big] \hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{d}t \\[11pt]
    \int \dfrac{1}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int  \big[\hspace{-0.25em}-\hspace{-0.15em}2te^{-t^2}\big]\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{d}t \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Let $u = -t^2$. Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t} &= -2t \\[8pt]
    \mathrm{d}u &= -2t\,\mathrm{d}t \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \dfrac{1}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int e^u \,\mathrm{d}u \\[11pt]
    \ln x &= e^u + C  \hspace{3em}{\text{$[\text{for some $C \in \mathbb{R}$}]$}}\\[11pt]
    \ln x &= e^{-t^2} + C \hspace{3em}{(1)} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we substitute
    $t = 0$ and $x = 2$ into equation $(1)$, we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ln 2 &= e^0 + C \hspace{1.25em} \\[11pt]
    \ln 2 &= 1 + C \\[11pt]
    C &= \ln 2 - 1\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, solving equation $(1)$ for
    $x$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ln x &= e^{-t^2} + \ln 2 - 1 \\[11pt]
    x &= e^{e^{-t^2} + \ln 2 - 1} \\[11pt]
    x &= \big[e^{\ln 2}\big]\hspace{-0.15em}\big[e^{e^{-t^2}-1}\big] \\[11pt]
    x &= 2e^{e^{-t^2}-1}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we evaluate
    $x = 2e^{e^{-t^2}-1}$  for $t = 0.4$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x(0.4) &= 2e^{e^{-(0.4)^2}-1}  \hspace{0.6em} \\[12pt]
    &\approx 1.725 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, using the percentage
    error formula, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \epsilon &= \bigg|\dfrac{1.782 - 1.725}{1.725}\bigg|\times 100\hspace{0.05em}\% \\[11pt]
    &\hspace{-0.375em}\epsilon \approx 3.30\hspace{0.05em}\%
    \end{aligned}
    $$
