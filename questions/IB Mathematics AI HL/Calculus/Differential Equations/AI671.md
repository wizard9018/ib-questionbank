# AI671

**Maximum mark: 13**

A water tank in the shape of a rectangular prism has height
4 m and a base of $3$ m
$\times$ $2$ m. Water flows out of a tap at the
bottom of the tank at a rate proportional to the square root of the
depth of the water at any given time. Let $h$ denote the depth of the
water, in metres, and $V$ is the volume of the water remaining in the
tank after $t$ minutes.

1.  Write down a differential equation for the rate of change of volume
    of water in terms of time.[2 marks]

2.  Show that the volume of water in the tank is given by
    $V=6h$ m$^3$ at time $t.$[1 mark]

3.  Hence, or otherwise, show that a differential equation for the rate
    of change of water height in terms of time is [3 marks]

    $$
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\dfrac{k\sqrt{h}}{6}
    $$

4.  Given that the tank is initially full, and the height then drops
    $3$ m after $48$ minutes, solve the
    differential equation in **part (c)**.[5 marks]

5.  Find the time it takes for the tank to empty. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  As the water flows out at a rate proportional to the square root of
    $h$, we get

    $$
    \hspace{0.5em}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = -k\sqrt{h}
    $$
    for some constant $k>0$. Note $k>0$ because the depth of the water
    is decreasing.

2.  The volume of water at time $t$ is the volume of a rectangular prism
    of base $3$ m $\times$
    $2$ m with height $h$. Thus, we obtain

    $$
    \hspace{9em}V=6h \hspace{3em}\text{[m$^3$ at time $t$]}
    $$

3.  Taking the derivative of the expression for volume found in part
    (b), using the chain rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} &= -k\sqrt{h} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= -k\sqrt{h} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{11mm}\\
    6\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= -k\sqrt{h} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{11mm}\\
    &\hspace{-1.4em}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\dfrac{k\sqrt{h}}{6} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{11mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Integrating the separable differential equation found in part (c),
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{h}} \, \mathrm{d}h &= \int -k \,\mathrm{d}t\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    12\sqrt{h} &= -kt+C \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    \intertext{Using that when $t=0$, $h=4$, we get}
    12\sqrt{4} &= -k\cdot 0+ C \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    C &= 24\rule[-2mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    \intertext{Now, using that $t=48$ when $h=1$, we get}
    12\sqrt{1} &= -48k+24 \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    k &= \dfrac{12}{48} = \dfrac{1}{4} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \therefore \hspace{0.5em} 12\sqrt{h}&=-\dfrac{t}{4}+24\rule[-2mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.35em}h=\left(2-\dfrac{t}{48}\right)^2\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Letting $h=0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0 &= \left(2-\dfrac{t}{48}\right)^2 \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &\hspace{-0.35em} t = 96 \hspace{0.2em} \text{minutes}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
