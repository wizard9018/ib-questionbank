# AI1064

**Maximum mark: 5**

A weight on a spring forces the spring to oscillate vertically about an equilibrium point. The distance $y\,\text{cm}$ of the weight below the equilibrium point is given by the differential equation

$$

\begin{aligned}

    2\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}t^2} + 6\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} + 4y = 9\cos 3t \, .

\end{aligned}

$$

Initially, $y=1.5\,$cm and $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=0.1$.

![ai1064a](images/ai1064a.PNG)

Use Euler's method with a step size of $0.1$ to estimate the maximum distance of the weight from the equilibrium point during the first second.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We will attempt to write this second order differential equation as a coupled linear system.

Let $x = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}$ and therefore $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$.

Substituting these values into our original second order equation we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    2\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} + 6x + 4y = 9\cos 3t

\end{aligned}

$$

If we rearrange this equation to make $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}$ the subject, we have the coupled linear system

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= x \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 4.5\cos 3t - 2y - 3x

\end{aligned}

$$

Using Euler's method for coupled systems with $h=0.1$, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    x_{n+1} &= x_n + 0.1 \times f_1(x_n,y_n,t_n) \\[8pt]

    y_{n+1} &= y_n + 0.1 \times f_2(x_n,y_n,t_n) \\[8pt]

    t_{n+1} &= t_n + 0.1

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $f_1(x_n,y_n,t_n) = 4.5\cos \left(3t_n\right) - 2y_n - 3x_n$ and $f_2(x_n,y_n,t_n) = x_n$. Hence,

$$

\begin{aligned}

    x_{n+1} &= x_n + 0.1\left(4.5\cos \left(3t_n\right) - 2y_n - 3x_n\right) \\[8pt]

    y_{n+1} &= y_n + 0.1 x_n \\[8pt]

    t_{n+1} &= t_n + 0.1

\end{aligned}

$$

We have initial values of $t_0=0$, $y_0=1.5$ and $x_0=0.1$. Using the G.D.C., we compile the following table up to $n=10$ (the end of the first second):

![ai1064b](images/ai1064b.PNG)

During the first second, the maximum distance of the weight from the equilibrium point is approximately $1.60\,\text{cm}$, which occurs after $0.5$ seconds.
