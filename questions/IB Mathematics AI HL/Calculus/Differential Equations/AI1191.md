# AI1191

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider a particle whose motion, relative to a fixed origin O, is described by the coupled linear system given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}&=ax+y\\[2pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}&=-x+ay

\end{aligned}

$$

1. Find, in terms of $a$, the eigenvalues for the system.      [3 marks]

2. Describe the motion of the particle in the case where $a=-2$.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Firstly we form the matrix of coefficients, $\bm{M}$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \bm{M} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r]

        a & 1 \\

        -1 & a

    \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The eigenvalues of $\bm{M}$ are found by solving the equation $|\bm{M}-\lambda \bm{I}|=0$ for the values of $\lambda$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \Bigg|\begin{pmatrix*}[c]

        a - \lambda & 1 \\

        -1 & a - \lambda

    \end{pmatrix*}\Bigg| &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the formula for the determinant of a $2\times2$ matrix, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    (a-\lambda)^2 - (1)(-1) &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    (a-\lambda)^2 &= -1 \\[6pt]

    a - \lambda &= \pm\sqrt{-1} \\[6pt]

    a - \lambda &= \pm i \\[6pt]

    \lambda &= a \pm i \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-4em}\boxed{\lambda_1 = a - i\,,\enskip \lambda_2 = a + i}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. With $a=-2$, the coupled linear system is given by

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}&=-2x+y\\[2pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}&=-x-2y

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    And the eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = -2 - i$ and $\lambda_2 = -2 + i$.

    We first note that because the eigenvalues are complex, and $a < 0$, the motion will be a $\textbf{spiral towards the origin}$. 

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We need to determine whether the spiral is clockwise or anti-clockwise. To do this, we can evaluate $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}$ at any point on the $x$-axis.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Let's choose $(1,0)$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}_{(1,0)} &= -1 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This tells us that the vertical motion from the positive $x$-axis is $\textit{downwards}$ and, in order to spiral towards the origin (as we must), the motion must be $\textit{clockwise}$.

    $$                    

    \begin{aligned}            

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}    

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$\text{When $a=-2$, the motion of the particle is a clockwise spiral towards the origin.}$$
