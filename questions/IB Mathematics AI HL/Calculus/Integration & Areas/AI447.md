# AI447

**Maximum mark: 5**

The following diagram shows part of the graph of
$f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12, x \in \mathbb{R}$. The shaded region $R$
is bounded by the $x$-axis, $y$-axis and the graph of $f$.

![1f1687a9e8e1a274ee354caa370256d3889e3626.svg](images/1f1687a9e8e1a274ee354caa370256d3889e3626.svg)

1.  Write down an integral for the area of region $R$. [2 marks]

2.  Find the area of region $R$. [1 mark]

A parallelogram ABCD has four vertices with the coordinates shown as
below.

![0efccb1c03bda9b8ce327d237a761daa3978350f.svg](images/0efccb1c03bda9b8ce327d237a761daa3978350f.svg)

3.  Find the value of $a$, the $y$-coordinate of points B and
    C, such that the area of the parallelogram is equal to the area of
    region $R$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  First, we need to find the $x$-intercept on the left hand side of
    region $R$. Setting $f(x) = 0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12 &= 0 \\[3pt]
    x = -2 \text{ or } x = 2 \text{ or } x &=3 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Thus, the point where the curve crosses the
    $x$-axis on the left hand side of $R$ is $(-2,0)$. Hence the region
    $R$ is bounded by the $x$-axis, $y$-axis and the curve for
    $-2 \leq x \leq 0$ and its area represented by the integral

    $$
    \text{[Area }R] = \int_{-2}^0 \left(x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12\right) \mathrm{d}x
    $$

2.  Evaluating the integral in part (a), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[Area }R] &= \int_{-2}^0 \left(x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12 \right) \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &=  \left[\dfrac 1 4 x^4 - 3 \left(\dfrac 1 3 x^3\right) - 4\left(\dfrac 1 2 x^2\right) +12x \right]_{-2}^0 \\[3pt]
    &= \left[\dfrac 1 4 x^4 - x^3 - 2 x^2 +12x \right]_{-2}^0 \\[3pt]
    &= \left[\dfrac 1 4 (0)^4 - (0)^3 - 2 (0)^2 +12(0) \right] \\[3pt]
    & \phantom{aa} - \left[\dfrac 1 4 (-2)^4 - (-2)^3 - 2 (-2)^2 +12(-2) \right] \\[6pt]
    &= 20 \text{ units}^2\hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Since points B and C have the same $y$-coordinate, $a$, we can
    conclude that the height of the parallelogram is $h = a$. In
    addition, we can see that $AD = BC = 5$.

    Using the formula for the area of a parallelogram, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{[Area of ABCD]} &= AD \times h \\[3pt]
    &= 5 \times a \\[3pt]
    &= 5a
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since the area of the parallelogram ABCD is
    equal to the area of region $R$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    5a &= 20 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.55em}a = 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
