# AI451

**Maximum mark: 5**

A curve $y = f(x)$ has a gradient function of $f'(x) = ax + 5$. The
diagram below shows part of the curve, with the $x$ and $y$ intercepts
labelled.

![f7403cf6716fab9c8b7128fb36203e5b76e358a7.svg](images/f7403cf6716fab9c8b7128fb36203e5b76e358a7.svg)

1.  Find the equation of the curve. [3 marks]

2.  Find the area under the curve from $0 < x < 4$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Integrating $f'(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= \int (ax + 5) \, \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= a\left(\dfrac 1 2 x^2 \right) + 5x + C \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac 1 2 ax^2 + 5x + C
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    where $C$ is a constant.

    Using the diagram, the curve passes through $(4,0)$ and $(0,12)$.
    Hence, each of these coordinates should satisfy the equation
    $y = f(x)$.

    First, substituting in $x = 0$ and $y = 12$ into $f(x)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    12 &= f(0) \\[3pt]
    12 &= \dfrac 1 2 a(0)^2 + 5(0) + C \\[3pt]
    C &= 12
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next, substituting in $x = 4$ and $y = 0$ into
    $f(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0 &= f(4) \\[3pt]
    0 &= \dfrac 1 2 a(4)^2 + 5(4) + C \\[3pt]
    0 &= 8a + 20 + 12 \\[3pt]
    0 &= 8 a + 32 \\[3pt]
    0 &= a + 4 \hspace{3em} [\div 8]\\[3pt]
    a &= -4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, the equation of the curve is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= \dfrac 1 2 (-4)x^2 + 5x + 12 \\[6pt]
    & = -2x^2 + 5x + 12
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The region shaded below represents the area under the curve from
    $0 < x < 4$.

    ![f49c2b42463c085342b6d68f2578cde4bda819cb.svg](images/f49c2b42463c085342b6d68f2578cde4bda819cb.svg)

    This area is determined by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \int_0^4 (-2x^2 + 5x + 12) \, \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= \left[-2 \left(\dfrac 1 3 x^3\right) + 5\left(\dfrac 1 2 x^2\right) + 12x\right]_0^4 \\[3pt]
    &= \left[-\dfrac 2 3 x^3  + \dfrac 5 2 x^2 + 12x\right]_0^4 \\[3pt]
    &= \left[-\dfrac 2 3 (4)^3  + \dfrac 5 2 (4)^2 + 12(4)\right] - \left[-\dfrac 2 3 (0)^3  + \dfrac 5 2 (0)^2 + 12(0)\right] \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{136}{3} \text{ units}^2 \hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
