# AI439

**Maximum mark: 17**

The diagram below shows the cross-sectional area of a mound of beach
sand created after a high tide.

![abcae07fb6cdea3f85f578f174db9e354c3201dc.svg](images/abcae07fb6cdea3f85f578f174db9e354c3201dc.svg)

The curve of the cross section can be modelled by the following equation

$$
y = \dfrac{x^2(90-x)}{1800}
$$
where $y$ represents the vertical height
of the mound in cm and $x$ denotes the horizontal width in cm, from the
start of the mound.

1.  At a horizontal width of $x = 30$, determine
    1.  The vertical height of the mound at this point;

    2.  The gradient of the mound curve at this point. [4 marks]

2.
    1.  Find the value of $x$ which corresponds to the maximum the
        vertical height of the mound.

    2.  Hence, find the maximum vertical height of the mound.
        [4 marks]

3.  Calculate the cross-sectional area of the mound, rounding your
    answer to one decimal place.  [3 marks]

A child uses a toy shovel to remove the top of sand mound, as
illustrated by the line segment MN below. Point $M$ has coordinates at
$(30,30)$.

![4416e2755cb42b15f0183898ee652b99cb3583e0.svg](images/4416e2755cb42b15f0183898ee652b99cb3583e0.svg)

4.  Determine the coordinates of point $N$. [2 marks]

The cross-sectional area removed by the child can be expressed by the
following integral

$$
\int_p^q \dfrac{x^2(90-x)}{1800} \, \mathrm{d}x - R,
$$

5.  Determine the values of $p, q$ and $R$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Substituting in $x = 30$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y &= \dfrac{(30)^2(90-30)}{1800} \\[6pt]
        &= 30 \hspace{0.25em}\text{cm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y' &= \left[ \dfrac{x^2(90-x)}{1800} \right]' \\[3pt]
        &= \left[ \dfrac{1}{1800}\left(90x^2 - x^3 \right) \right]' \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{1800}\left(90(2x) - 3x^2 \right) \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{1800}\left(180x - 3x^2 \right) \\[3pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{600}\left(60x - x^2 \right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can now substitute in $x = 30$ to find
        the gradient of the curve at this point. We get,

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y' &= \dfrac{1}{600}\left(60(30) - (30)^2 \right) \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac 3 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  To find the local maximum of the curve, we let $y' = 0$ and
        solve for $x$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{1}{600}\left(60x - x^2 \right) &= 0 \\[3pt]
        x = 0 \text{ or } x &= 60 \hspace{3em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can reject $x = 0$ as this is the
        stationary point that corresponds with the start of the mound.\
        Hence the vertical height is a maximum at
         $x = 60$

    2.  Substituting $x = 60$ into $y$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y &= \dfrac{60^2(90-60)}{1800}  \\[6pt]
        &= 60 \hspace{0.25em}\text{cm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  The cross-sectional area of the mound is calculated as
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \int_0^{90} \dfrac{x^2(90-x)}{1800} \, \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= \int_0^{90} \dfrac{1}{1800}\left(90x^2 - x^3 \right) \, \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{1800} \left[ 90 \left(\dfrac{x^3}{3} \right) - \dfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^{90} \\[3pt]
    &=  \dfrac{1}{1800} \left[ 30x^3 - \dfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^{90} \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{1800} \left[ \left(30(90)^3 - \dfrac{(90)^4}{4} \right) - \left(30(0)^3 - \dfrac{(0)^4}{4} \right)\right] \\[6pt]
    &= 3037.5 \hspace{0.25em}\text{cm}^2\hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  To find N, we let $y = 30$ and solve for $x$. We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{x^2(90-x)}{1800} &= 30 \\[6pt]
    90x^2 - x^3 &= 54000 \\[3pt]
    -x^3 + 90x^2 - 54000 &=  0 \\[6pt]
    x \approx -22 \text{ or } x = 30 \text{ or } x &\approx 82\hspace{3em} [\text{ by G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We can reject $x \approx -22$ as it's negative, and can reject
    $x = 30$ as this corresponds to point M.\
    Hence, point N is located at
     $\text{N}(82,30)$ .

5.  From part (d), we found that the $x$-coordinate of point N is
    $x = 82$. Hence the removed area (unshaded region at the top of the
    mound) can be found by subtracting the area of the rectangle $R$
    from the area of the region under the curve bounded between $x = 30$
    and $x = 82$.

    ![5ddbb6fe7004cef03a614e92275555a82c87bd6a.svg](images/5ddbb6fe7004cef03a614e92275555a82c87bd6a.svg)

    The area of rectangle $R$ can be found by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    R &= \text{length} \times \text \\[3pt]
    &= (82 - 30) \times 30 \\[3pt]
    &= 1560 \hspace{0.25em}\text{cm}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, the removed area can be expressed as

    $$
    A = \int_{30}^{82} \dfrac{x^2(90-x)}{1800} \, \mathrm{d}x - 1560
    $$
    Hence,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &p = 30 \\[6pt]
    &q = 82 \\[6pt]
    &R = 1560
    \end{aligned}
    $$
