# AI450

**Maximum mark: 5**

A curve $y = f(x)$ has a gradient function of $f'(x) = ax + 1$. The
diagram below shows part of the curve, with the $x$ and $y$ intercepts
labelled.

![5151ff4056304343e41a78f95432d391aae6588d.svg](images/5151ff4056304343e41a78f95432d391aae6588d.svg)

1.  Find the equation of the curve. [3 marks]

2.  Find the area under the curve from $0 < x < 3$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Integrating $f'(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= \int (ax + 1) \, \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= a\left(\dfrac 1 2 x^2 \right) + x + C \\[3pt]
    &= \dfrac 1 2 ax^2 + x + C
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    where $C$ is a constant.

    Using the diagram, the curve passes through $(3,0)$ and $(0,6)$.
    Hence, each of these coordinates should satisfy the equation
    $y = f(x)$.

    First, substituting in $x = 0$ and $y = 6$ into $f(x)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    6 &= f(0) \\[3pt]
    6 &= \dfrac 1 2 a(0)^2 + 0 + C \\[3pt]
    C &= 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next, substituting in $x = 3$ and $y = 0$ into
    $f(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0 &= f(3) \\[3pt]
    0 &= \dfrac 1 2 a(3)^2 + 3 + C \\[3pt]
    0 &= \dfrac 9 2 a + 3 + 6 \\[3pt]
    0 &= \dfrac 9 2 a + 9 \\[3pt]
    0 &= 9a + 18 \hspace{3em} [\times 2]\\[3pt]
    a &= -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, the equation of the curve is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= \dfrac 1 2 (-2)x^2 + x + 6 \\[6pt]
    & = -x^2 + x + 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The region shaded below represents the area under the curve from
    $0 < x < 3$.

    ![311612f4f82ae65cced9de44cee74000da1e0e89.svg](images/311612f4f82ae65cced9de44cee74000da1e0e89.svg)

    This area is determined by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \int_0^3 (-x^2 + x + 6) \, \mathrm{d}x \\[3pt]
    &= \left[-\dfrac 1 3 x^3 + \dfrac 1 2 x^2 + 6x\right]_0^3 \\[3pt]
    &= \left[-\dfrac 1 3 (3)^3 + \dfrac 1 2 (3)^2 + 6(3)\right] - \left[-\dfrac 1 3 (0)^3 + \dfrac 1 2 (0)^2 + 6(0)\right] \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{27}{2} \text{ units}^2 \hspace{3em} [\text{or by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
