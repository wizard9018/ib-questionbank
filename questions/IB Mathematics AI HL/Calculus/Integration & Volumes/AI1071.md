# AI1071

**Maximum mark: 13**

A company uses glass to make petal shaped candles as shown below.

![candle](images/candle.png)

The candle is formed when the shape shown below is rotated around the $y$-axis, the shaded area represents the glass.

 ![petal](images/petal.png)

Once the candle has been made from glass it is filled with wax.

The candles are formed using the following two functions.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    &f(x)=\sqrt{5x}+1, \hspace{3em}0\leq x\leq a\\
    &g(x)=3\sqrt{x-1}, \hspace{3em}1 \leq x\leq a
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

1. The two functions intersect at $A$, find the coordinates of $A$. [2 marks]

2. Find the volume of glass required to make a single candle. [6 marks]

Each candle should have exactly $50$\% of its usable volume filled with wax.

The usable volume of the candle is defined as the volume in the completed candle  within the limits $y_1$ and $y_2$ as shown.

![limits](images/limits.png)

3. Find the $y$-coordinate the wax should be filled up to in order to satisfy the conditions stated above. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Equate the RHS of both functions and solve for $x$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \sqrt{5x}+1&=3\sqrt{x-1}\\
        x&=5 &[\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The $y$-coordinate is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f(5)&=\sqrt{5\times5}+1\\
        &=6
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The coordinates of $A$ are $(5, 6)$.

2. As this is a revolution around the $y$-axis we need to make $x^2$ the subject for both functions $f$ and $g$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{-3em}y&=\sqrt{5x}+1 \hspace{6em} y=3\sqrt{x-1}\\
        (y-1)^2&=5x \hspace{6.6em} \left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^2=x-1\\
        x&=\frac{(y-1)^2}{5} \hspace{6em}  x=\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^2+1\\
        x^2&=\bigg(\frac{(y-1)^2}{5}\bigg)^2 \hspace{3.65em} x^2=\bigg(\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^2+1\bigg)^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Realise that for $0\leq y< 1$ we only need the volume generated by $x=\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^2+1$. The region from $1\leq y< 6$ is the difference between the volumes generated by both curves.

    $\pi  \left[\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}} \left(\left(\dfrac{y}{3}\right)^2+1\right)^2 \,dy \ + \left( \int_{1}^{6} \left(\left(\dfrac{y}{3}\right)^2+1\right)^2 \,dy - \int_{1}^{6} \left(\dfrac{(y-1)^2}{5}\right)^2 \,dy\right)\right]$

    $=50.8938... \hspace{4em}\text{[by using G.D.C.]}$

    The volume of glass required to make a single candle is $50.9 \text{ units}^3$

    Note: the calculation $\pi \int_{0}^{6} ((\frac{y}{3})^2+1)^2 \,dy - \int_{0}^{6} (\frac{(y-1)^2}{5})^2 \,dy=50.8687...$, although it correctly rounds to 3sf, is in fact not correct as it includes the value of $\pi\int_{0}^{1} (\frac{(y-1)^2}{5})^2dy=0.025133...$ which should be omitted as the function only exists when $1\leq y\leq6$.
3. First find the usable volume of the candle by integrating the inner surface of the candle using the limits described in the diagram.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &\pi\int_{1}^{6} \left(\frac{(y-1)^2}{5}\right)^2dy\\[6pt]
        &=78.5398...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The amount of wax should fill $50$\% of this.

    Therefore the wax should fill $39.26...$.

    Now we need to find the upper limit ($k$) of the integral which solves the following equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \pi\int_{1}^{k}\left(\dfrac{(y-1)^2}{5}\right)^2 \ dy&=39.26...\\[12pt]
        k&=5.3527...&\text{[by using G.D.C.]}\\[12pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The desired $y$-coordinate is $5.35 \text{ units}$.
