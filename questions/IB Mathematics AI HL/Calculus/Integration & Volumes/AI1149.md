# AI1149

**Maximum mark: 9**

The following diagram shows the curves $y=\tan{x}$ and $y=3-\tfrac{x^2}{2}$.

The intersection point of the two curves is labelled $A$.

![q1149](images/q1149.png)

1.  Find the coordinates of $A$. [2 marks]

The area enclosed by the two curves and the $y-$axis is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $y-$axis to form a volume of revolution $V$

2.  Find the value of $V$. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the G.D.C. we find the coordinates of $A$ to be $(1.16, 2.32)$.
    $$
    \tag*{\textbf{A1A1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

2.  We must find expressions for $x^2$ as we are rotating around the $y-$axis.
    $$
    \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y&=\tan{x} \hspace{13.55em}y=3-\tfrac{x^2}{2}\\
        x&=\tan^{-1}{y} \hspace{12em}\tfrac{x^2}{2}=3-y\\
        x^2&=\left(\tan^{-1}{y}\right)^2 \hspace{2em}\textbf{(A1)} \hspace{6.2em}x^2=6-2y \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\
        %& &x=\sqrt{6-2y} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We can form two regions using the point of intersection, shown below.
    $$
    \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

    ![a1149](images/a1149.png)

    Hence, we can use the formula for volume of revolution to represent $V$ by summing the following two definite integrals.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    V&=\pi \left( \int_0^{2.32} \left(\tan^{-1}{y}\right)^2 \mathrm{d}y + \int_{2.32}^{3} (6-2y)\hspace{0.1em} \mathrm{d}y\right) \tag*{\textbf{(A1A1)}}\\[6pt]
        &=6.5093...\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{6.51\,\text{units}^3}\hspace{2em}[\text{by using G.D.C.}]   \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
