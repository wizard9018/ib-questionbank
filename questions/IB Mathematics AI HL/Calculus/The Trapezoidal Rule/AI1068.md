# AI1068

**Maximum mark: 17**

An ambitious building project is nearing completion in Abu Dhabi. The front facade of the building is shown as the shaded region in the following diagram, where the $y$-axis represents height above the ground and the $x$-axis shows the distance from the left hand side of the building to the right hand side, both in metres.

![ai1068a](images/ai1068a.PNG)

The function $h(x)$ from point B$(0,60)$ to point C$(20,20)$ models the roof-line of the building. The gradient function for the roof-line, $h'(x)$, is given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

    h'(x) = 0.4x - 6\,,\,\text{for}\,0 \leq x \leq 20\,.

\end{aligned}

$$

1. Find $h(x)$.[3 marks]

2. Hence find the smallest vertical distance from the ground to the roof-line.[2 marks]

3. 1.  Use the trapezoidal rule with 4 intervals to estimate the area of the shaded region.

    2. State whether the trapezoidal rule will underestimate or overestimate the actual area. Justify your answer. [5 marks]

The actual area of the front facade of the building is $533\,$m$^2$.

4. Calculate the percentage error in the estimate from part (c)$\,$(i).[2 marks]

The engineers are planning a very large rectangular window on the front facade. The window will sit at ground level, and the height of the window will be exactly three times the width. A possible location for the window is shown in the following diagram.

![ai1068b](images/ai1068b.PNG)

The area of the window will be largest when [EF] lies on the $y$-axis.

5. 1.  Find the $x$-coordinate of point H that maximises the area of the window.

    2. Find the area of the largest possible window. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. We have that $h(x) = \int h'(x)\, \mathrm{d}x$. Therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &= \int 0.4x - 6 \, \mathrm{d}x \\[6pt]

    &= 0.2x^2 - 6x + C

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have point B$(0,60)$ lying on the graph of $h(x)$, hence $h(0)=60$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h(0) &= 0.2(0)^2 - 6(0) + C \\[6pt]

    60 &= C

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore, $\boxed{h(x) = 0.2x^2 - 6x + 60}$

2. Using the G.D.C. we can locate the minimum of $h(x)$:

    ![ai1068c](images/ai1068c.PNG)

    $$\boxed{\text{The smallest vertical distance from the ground to the roof-line is $15\,$ metres.}}$$

3. 1. The width of the building is $20\,$ metres, so with four intervals, we have $a=0$, $b=20$ and $n=4$.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        h &= \dfrac{b-a}{n} \\[8pt]

        &= \dfrac{20-0}{4} \\[8pt]

        h &= 5

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        We have been given $y_0=60\,,\,y_4=20$, and from part (b) we have that $y_3=15$. These are shown on the following diagram.

        ![ai1068d](images/ai1068d.PNG)

        Using the G.D.C. we find that $h(5)=35$ and $h(10)=20$. Hence $y_1=35$ and $y_2=20$. Using the trapezoidal rule, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \int_{a}^{b} y\,\mathrm{d}x &\approx \dfrac{1}{2}h\left((y_0+y_4)+2(y_1+y_2+y_3)\right) \\[8pt]

        &\approx 0.5(5)\left((60+20)+2(35+20+15)\right) \\[8pt]

        &\approx 550

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        $$\boxed{\text{The shaded region has an estimated area of $550\,$m$^2$ using the trapezoidal rule.}}$$

    2. The trapezoidal rule will underestimate if a curve is concave down, and overestimate if a curve is concave up.

        $$\boxed{\text{$h(x)$ is concave up, hence the trapezoidal rule will overestimate.}}$$

4. Using the percentage error formula with $v_A=550$ and $v_E=533$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \varepsilon &= \Bigg|\dfrac{v_A-v_E}{v_E}\Bigg|\times100\% \\[8pt]

    &= \Bigg|\dfrac{550-533}{533}\Bigg|\times100\% \\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-0.8em}\boxed{\varepsilon \approx 3.19\%}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. 1. The largest possible window will occur when [EF] lies on the $y$-axis, and [GH] touches the curve of $h(x)$ so that [GH]$=3x$:

        ![ai1068e](images/ai1068e.PNG)

        At point G, $x$ must satisfy the equation:

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        0.2x^2 - 6x + 60 &= 3x \\[6pt]

        0.2x^2 - 9x + 60 &= 0 \\[6pt]

        x &= 8.14 \text{ or } 36.9 \hspace{3em}[\text{using G.D.C.}]

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Given that $0 \leq x \leq 20$,

        $$\boxed{\text{When the $x$-coordinate of H is $8.14$, the area of the window is maximised.}}$$

    2. Using the formula for the area of a rectangle, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        A &= (x)(3x) \\[6pt]

        &= (8.14)(3)(8.14) \\[6pt]

        &\approx 199

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        $$\boxed{\text{The largest possible window has an area of approximately $199\,$m$^2$.}}$$
