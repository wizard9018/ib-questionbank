$\Large{\textrm{Addressing Extreme Thinness in Media and Fashion}}$

In recent years, concerns have risen over the portrayal of extreme thinness in media and the fashion industry. Specialists are not only worried about the health implications for models but also about the broader societal effects on body image. In response, Israel has passed a law prohibiting models with a body mass index (BMI) below 18.5 from appearing in advertisements and shows. This law aims to promote the acceptance of all body types and encourage healthier standards in the fashion industry. This is part of an effort to stop glorifying the ultra-thin.

Reducing the number of images in the media portraying super-skinny models is expected to have a positive impact on women, especially in reducing the pressure to conform to unrealistic beauty standards. Claire Mysko, an advocate for body diversity, supports this view, stating that media exposure to a broader range of body types would contribute to healthier perceptions of beauty.

While the law has sparked hope for change, opinions are divided. Some believe it could be the beginning of the end for the dominance of thin bodies in the media, while others are skeptical of its long-term effects. Despite the controversy, there is a clear movement toward celebrating body diversity and reducing harmful ideals.

- **B**. “Israel has passed a law prohibiting models with a body mass index (BMI) below 18.5 from appearing in advertisements and shows.”

- The law passed supports the banning of models from advertisements or shows whose BMI is less than 18.5.

- **C**. According to the text, the passing of this law is part of an effort “to promote the acceptance of all body types”. “All body types” aligns with “women of all sizes”.

- **F**. “Reducing the number of images in the media portraying super-skinny models is expected to have a positive impact on women”

- The text suggests that reducing the number of images portraying extremely thin models in the media will be beneficial for them as they will not be pressured to conform to “unrealistic beauty standards”.

- **G**. “Claire Mysko, an advocate for body diversity, supports this view” Claire Myrko states that the media could help spread information about different types of bodies, which would benefit women.

- *Incorrect options*: A, D, E, H

- **A**.  “not only worried about the health implications for models” The use of the structure “not only… but also… implies that the concerns are twofold. According to the text, experts are worried about health problems as well as possible broader societal effects.
