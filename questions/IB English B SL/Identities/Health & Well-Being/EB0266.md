# EB0266

**Maximum mark: 7**

**Food noise**

An interview with Dr. Anya Sharma's. 

**[-1-]**\

Food noise describes the intrusive thoughts about food that many people experience. It's more than just feeling hungry; it's a constant internal monologue about eating. This can include obsessing over your next meal, regretting something you ate, or intense cravings for certain foods. This “mental chatter” can be very disruptive and make it difficult to focus on other aspects of daily life.

**[-2-]**\

A combination of our biology and our modern environmental factors, almost anything, really. For example, hormones that regulate hunger can send powerful signals to our brain. When these triggers intensify, the “noise” gets louder. 

The thing is, we are constantly bombarded with food advertisements, social media posts, and the easy availability of mouth-watering processed foods. This, in turn, creates a cycle in which our brains are nonstop triggered to think about eating.

**[-3-]**\

A key strategy is to focus on balanced nutrition. Eating meals rich in protein and fiber can help regulate hunger hormones and help you feel satisfied, which quiets the noise. Additionally, mindfulness practices can help individuals recognize and manage these thoughts without actually acting on them. Finally, regular exercise helps reduce stress and can be a healthy distraction from “food noise.”
