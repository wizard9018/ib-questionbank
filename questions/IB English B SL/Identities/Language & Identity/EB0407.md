## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{north of England}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I grew up in a small town in the north of England" \

The presenter tells us directly where they grew up, establishing their personal connection to the story.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{twenty years ago}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$when I got my first job in broadcasting twenty years ago" \

The presenter gives a specific timeframe for when they began their career in broadcasting. 

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{neutral}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$a single, formal accent — clear, southern, and considered $`$neutral'" \

The traditional BBC accent was regarded as the standard or *neutral* way of speaking, against which all other accents were compared.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{unprofessional}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$anything that sounded different was seen as unprofessional" \

Presenters with regional accents were considered to lack the professional standard required by the BBC.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{regions and backgrounds}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the BBC has made great effort to include presenters from different regions and backgrounds" \

The BBC has actively worked to ensure that presenters reflect the diversity of the country, both in terms of where they come from and their personal backgrounds.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{identity}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$hearing their own accent on national broadcasts makes them feel that their identity is finally being acknowledged" \

When listeners hear an accent like their own on national radio or television, it gives them a sense that who they are - their *identity* - is recognised and valued.
