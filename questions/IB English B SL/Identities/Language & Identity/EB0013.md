$\LARGE{\textrm{The Power of Language in Shaping Identity}}$

Language is more than a means of communication; it is a powerful tool that shapes who we are and how we connect with the world. For many, speaking a native language is deeply tied to cultural identity and personal heritage. When someone grows up speaking two or more languages, they often develop what is called a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$dual identity.” This dual identity allows people to shift between cultures, adopting different customs, expressions, and ways of thinking depending on the language they are using.

Studies have shown that being bilingual or multilingual can enhance cognitive skills, such as problem-solving and creativity. However,  $\underline{\textrm{it}}$  also comes with challenges. In some communities, people may feel pressured to use a dominant language to fit in, risking the loss of  $\underline{\textrm{their}}$ original language and, consequently, part of their cultural identity.

Preserving one's native language can be a form of resistance against cultural assimilation. $\underline{\textrm{It}}$ allows individuals to stay connected to their roots and pass on traditions to future generations. In an increasingly globalised world, maintaining a strong connection to one's linguistic heritage can strengthen a person's sense of belonging and identity.

Language, therefore, is not just about words — it is about who we are and how we define ourselves.
