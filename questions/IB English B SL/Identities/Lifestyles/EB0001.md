# EB0001

**Maximum mark: 5**

$\Large{\textrm{Virtual influencers: meet the AI-generated figures posing as your new online friends}}$

Virtual influencers are transforming the way content is created, consumed, and marketed online. They represent an electrifying dance between cutting-edge technology and our desire for connection. Recent advances in technology have thrust these digital personalities into the spotlight, with examples like the virtual Japanese popstar Kyoko Date dating back to 1996. Created by 3D artists using CGI and AI tools, virtual influencers can be tailored to align with specific target audiences.

Their popularity has prompted marketing agencies to embrace them as a cost-effective promotional strategy. Unlike real influencers who demand exorbitant fees, virtual influencers like Lil Miquela charge much less, making them an attractive option for brands. However, they also raise concerns about transparency. As virtual influencers become more human-like, distinguishing them from real people becomes increasingly difficult, particularly in advertising.

This ambiguity highlights the need for clear guidelines regarding the use and disclosure of such content. Countries like India are already addressing this issue by mandating that influencers, including virtual ones, disclose promotional content. The rise of virtual influencers offers both opportunities and challenges for the future of digital marketing and content creation.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- The text discusses how virtual influencers are used by marketing agencies as a promotional strategy, highlighting their role in generating revenue for brands.

- *Incorrect options:* A, C, D

- **A**: While virtual influencers may aim to connect with audiences, their primary function in the text is tied to marketing.

- **C**: The text does not discuss health or lifestyles but focuses on marketing and engagement.

- **D**: The text indicates that virtual influencers may struggle to connect in a human way, suggesting they do not offer genuine emotional support.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- The phrase *blurred realities* indicates that as virtual influencers become more human-like, it becomes increasingly difficult to tell them apart from real people.

- *Incorrect options:* A, C, D

- **A**: While this option seems close, it oversimplifies the context by implying total indistinguishability rather than a gradual blurring.

- **C**: The text  specifically discusses virtual influencers but does not imply all influencers are virtual

- **D**: The text does not suggest that the online world is more authentic but rather highlights the complexities of authenticity in digital marketing.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{posing}$   [1 mark]

- *Posing* suggests these influencers are imitating or representing themselves as friends.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{tailored}$   [1 mark]

- Both *customized* and *tailored* convey the idea of something that has been created to target a specific audience.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{mandating}$   [1 mark]

- Both words imply that the Indian government is making it obligatory for influencers to inform the audience of promotional content.
