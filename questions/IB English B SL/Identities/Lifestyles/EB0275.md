# EB0275

**Maximum mark: 6**

**Finding Balance Between Work and Life**

I used to think that working whenever I could was the key to career progress. I worked late in the office, read e-mail during dinner, and stayed up to catch up on some unfinished tasks. I always thought I would be able to get some sleep later. But later never came, and I ended up burnt out and alienated from friends and family.

I found myself missing important moments: birthdays, weekends, even a fun dinner-table conversation. And that's when I made up my mind: I needed to change. I started setting firm boundaries, such as cutting off work at 6 p.m. and leaving my phone away from the dinner table. It was strange, at first, but gradually I could feel a change.

With more time off, I started meeting friends for coffee. I slept better, laughed harder, and even enjoyed working less.

I've learned that work-life balance is as easy as making space for life outside of work. For me, it means valuing my time, saying no when necessary, and remembering that success is about happiness and health. My life is better now, not because I'm working fewer hours, but because I'm living more.

**Choose the correct option.** 

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$What belief motivated the writer's work routine?    [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$That working at any time would help their career progress.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$That time with family was more important than work.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$That resting regularly was essential for success.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$That avoiding emails during dinner would improve productivity.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$What was the main problem the writer found before changing their routine?     [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$They didn't have enough work to do.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$They felt exhausted and disconnected from loved ones.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$They wanted to spend more time in the office.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$They disliked their job.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$What did the writer do to improve their work-life balance?    [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$They change their work.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$They set themselves limits.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$They felt strange.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$They enjoyed time with their family.

**Find the word or phrase in paragraphs 3 and 4 which means the following:**

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$free time    [1 mark]

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$appreciating    [1 mark]

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$keeping in mind    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I used to think that working whenever I could was the key to career progress.”\

The phrase $\text{\textquotedblleft}$working whenever” means the same as working at any time, which suggests being available to work as much as possible. Similarly the phrase $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the key to career progress” refers to something that would help  someone's career progress.

- *Incorrect options:* B, C, D

- **Option B** is incorrect as, according to the text, the writer neglected time with their family in favour of work. They mention becoming $\text{\textquotedblleft}$alienated from friends and family”, which shows they did not prioritize them. 

- **C**: According to the text, the writer sacrificed rest to keep working. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I always thought I would be able to get some sleep later.” This shows they did not value rest as essential for success.

- **Option D** is incorrect as the text says the opposite, the writer did read emails during dinner.  $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I worked late in the office, read e-mail during dinner, ...” as they believed constant work was the key to success.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$ended up burnt out, and alienated from friends and family.”\

The adjective *burnt out* means constantly tired, which explains why the writer feels exhausted, while the adjective *alienated*, suggesting isolation, explains why the writer feels *disconnected* from friends and family.

- *Incorrect options:* A, C, D

- **A**: The writer explains that they, in fact, had excess work: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I worked late in the office, reading e-mail during dinner, and at other times stayed up to catch up on some unfinished task.”

- **Option C** is incorrect, as in fact, the writer already spent *too much* time in the office, and this was one, among others, in the list of problems. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I worked late in the office”

- ***Option D** is not possible. There is no evidence in the text that the writer disliked their job. The main concern is the excess of work, not that he did not like it.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I started setting firm boundaries”\

*Limits* and *boundaries* are synonyms. The writer mentions they began to set boundaries, like leaving work earlier.

- *Incorrect options:* A, C, D

- **Option A** is incorrect because the writer does not mention changing their job, but making changes in their life.

- **C**: The writer mentions feeling strange, but this is not an action they took to improve their work-life balance.

- **Option D** is incorrect, as the writer mentions that they had missed time with family, but the action they took was $\text{\textquotedblleft}$setting boundaries” so they could spend more time with their family.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{time off}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$With more time off, I started meeting friends for coffee.”\

*Time off* and *free time* refer to time when the writer was not working, was free from work responsibilities. 

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{valuing}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$it means valuing my time”\

*Appreciating* is a good replacement word for *valuing*, as it means to give importance to something, to value it.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{remembering}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$remembering that success is about happiness and health”\

*Keeping in mind* and *remembering* both imply staying aware of something important, not forgetting.
