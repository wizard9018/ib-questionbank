# EB0341

**Maximum mark: 6**

**Disagreement does not equal disrespect**

**[-1-]**\

In many families, disagreements between generations are common, especially when it comes to social issues. When she was young, Leila used to avoid these conversations with her grandparents because she believed conflict could damage their relationship. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I didn't want to say the wrong thing,” she explains. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I thought disagreement automatically meant disrespect.”

**[-2-]**\

However, as she grew older, Leila learned that in her home, respect was not defined by silence or automatically agreeing with an adult's idea. Instead, her parents had gently insisted on one rule: you can disagree with someone, but you must speak and listen with care. Her grandparents followed the same principle. Even when discussions became tense, they listened closely and responded kindly and without sarcasm. They challenged her ideas, but they also took her seriously. Leila learned to listen to their points of view and, even if she didn't change her position on the issue, she learned why her grandparents held their ideas.

**[-3-]**\

Leila has learned that respect is not the same as agreement. The lesson her parents taught her - to respectfully discuss even difficult issues - now shapes the way she handles any type of disagreement inside or outside her home.

**Choose an appropriate heading from the list that matches each paragraph.**[3 marks]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Respect as a family value

- B.$\hspace{1em}$The first step: avoiding conflict at all costs

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Fear of saying the wrong thing

- D.$\hspace{1em}$A rule that guided three generations

- E.$\hspace{1em}$When agreement becomes the only goal

- F.$\hspace{1em}$Respect beyond home

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- The text tells us that Leila avoided conversations due to fear of conflict damaging the relationship.

- **Option C** is incorrect here. Although Leila says, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I didn't want to say the wrong thing,” the paragraph is not mainly about fear of speaking. It is about avoiding disagreement altogether because she thought it meant disrespect.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

- This is the correct option because this paragraph introduces the family rule and shows that the whole family follows it.

- **Option A** does not fit here. Respect is clearly important in this paragraph, but the focus is not on respect as a general family value. Instead, it is specifically about the rule that guides how they disagree.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

- This paragraph explains how the lesson shapes Leila's approach to disagreements, no matter where they happen.

- **Option E**  contradicts the main idea of the paragraph, which clearly states that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$respect is not the same as agreement,”

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{damage}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$conflict could damage their relationship.” \

*Damage* and *harm* are synonyms.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{sarcasm}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$responded kindly and without sarcasm.” \

Both *sarcasm* and *irony* refer to saying something not meant literally. They could also imply mockery.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{shapes}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$now shapes the way she handles...”   \

*Affects* and *shapes* are very close synonyms in this context.
