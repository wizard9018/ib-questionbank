# EB0009

**Maximum mark: 10**

Dear Editor,

I am deeply troubled by the behavior of many young people today, who seem to have little regard for the rules and values that keep our society functioning. I see it everywhere, on the streets, in schools, even in public spaces where simple courtesies are ignored. Young people nowadays seem to think they're above the law, whether it's speeding, ignoring traffic rules, or vandalizing property. It's as if rules have become suggestions rather than expectations.

What happened to the days when respecting authority and abiding by the law were instilled as basic values? We've gone from raising responsible citizens to nurturing a generation that tests boundaries without fear of consequences. And let's be clear: without respect for the law, chaos isn't far behind.

Parents, educators, and society as a whole need to **[-5-]** young people accountable. This lack of respect doesn't just **[-6-]** poorly on today's youth; it undermines the very fabric of our community. We can't let our values erode at the hands of a generation that refuses to take responsibility. It's time to **[-7-]** them that rules exist for a reason.

Sincerely,\

A Concerned Citizen

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- “have little regard for the rules and values that keep our society functioning" 

- The text mentions young people today do not respect the shared values that hold society together. 

- **Option D** is incorrect. The text mentions young people testing boundaries “without fear of consequences," but this sentence is about respect for values, not about fear of punishment.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- “public spaces where simple courtesies are ignored" 

- To *ignore* something means not to pay attention to it; politeness and good manners are being overlooked. 

- **Option A** is incorrect. The writer says rules have “become suggestions," not politeness and good manners; this option matches a different idea in the text.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{G}$   [1 mark]

- “What happened to the days when respecting authority and abiding by the law were instilled as basic values?" 

- The writer questions why people no longer respect authority, as they once did. 

- **Option H** is incorrect. The text refers specifically to respect for authority and the law, not to “traditional values" in general.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$   [1 mark]

- *Chaos* means complete disorder, which is what “in shambles" describes. 

- **Option E** is incorrect. The text warns of chaos, not that society will disappear or fail to survive.s, making it less suitable in this context.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$   [1 mark]

- “Hold accountable" is a fixed expression meaning to make someone take responsibility for their actions.

- **Option F** is incorrect. Keep does not collocate with “accountable"; the standard expression is “hold someone accountable," not “keep someone accountable."

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- “Reflect poorly on" is a fixed expression meaning to give a bad impression of someone or make them look bad. 

- **Option E** is incorrect. *Affect* means to influence or have an effect on something, but it does not collocate with the adverb "poorly" in this structure; we say "reflect poorly on," not "affect poorly on.

$7.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$   [1 mark]

- *Remind* means to make someone remember something they already know or have forgotten, which matches the writer's wish to “bring rules back.”

- **Option D** is incorrect. *Warn* means to tell someone about a possible danger or bad consequence, which is too strong here; the writer wants young people to be *reminded* of why rules exist, not threatened with what will happen if they break them.
