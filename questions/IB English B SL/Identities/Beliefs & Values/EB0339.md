# EB0339

**Maximum mark: 6**

**Instant opinions - not always for me!**

Hey guys - I want to talk about something that's been bothering me lately. It feels like having no opinion is almost worse than having the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$wrong” one. Teachers always say they want discussion, and I get it. But if I admit in class that I'm not sure how I feel - or what I think about the subject - someone laughs and goes, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Classic you. Always thinking.”

Last week, we discussed banning phones in school. Everyone had an instant opinion, like it was obvious. I didn't. Phones are distracting, yes, but they're an important part of our life. So when people act like it's a simple problem with a simple solution, I just don't buy it. When I said, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I need more time to think,” my teacher smiled, but I could tell some classmates were annoyed.

Online it's even worse. Chats and posts move fast, and people expect immediate reactions to every headline or social issue. If I don't reply quickly, someone will message, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$So you're not saying anything?” as if silence automatically means I don't care. But I do care. I just don't want to fake certainty about something. Am I the only one experiencing this?

**Choose the correct option.**

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$What is the writer mainly concerned about?    [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Teachers never allow students to speak in class.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$People expect instant opinions and judge uncertainty.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Phones make it impossible to have real discussions.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Students do not care about social issues anymore.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$What does the classmate's comment $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Classic you. Always thinking.” suggest?    [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$The classmate respects the writer's careful thinking.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$The classmate is surprised the writer has an opinion.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$The classmate is making fun of the writer for not choosing quickly.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$The classmate is confused about the topic.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$Why does the writer mention the phone-ban discussion?    [1 mark]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$To show that teachers ignore students' opinions.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$To give an example of an issue too complex for a quick answer.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$To argue that phones should never be banned.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$To prove that classmates are addicted to phones.

**The following statements are either true or false. Write True or False, then justify it using words that appear in the text. Both parts are required for 1 mark.**

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$The writer thinks banning phones in school is an easy decision.    [1 mark]

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$The writer also feels pressure in online spaces.    [1 mark]

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$The writer believes silence online means a person does not care.    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$having no opinion is almost worse than having the $`$wrong' one” \

The writer explains that people expect immediate responses both in class and online. This shows the main concern is pressure to have instant opinions and being judged for uncertainty.

- *Incorrect options:*  A, C, D

- **A**: The writer does not say teachers stop students from speaking. In fact, the writer says, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Teachers always say they want discussion.”

- **C**: Phones are only used as an example in one discussion, not the main concern of the text.

- **D**: The writer explicitly says they care: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$But I do care.” The issue is not a lack of interest in social issues.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

- The comment is disrespectful and impatient, showing that the classmate is making fun of the writer. The phrase $\text{\textquotedblleft}$classic you” indicates this is the typical response of the writer. 

- *Incorrect options:* A, B, D

- **A**: The mocking response, indicating the attitude of the writer is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$classic” or expected,  does not show respect.

- **B**: The writer does not share a clear opinion at that moment - they say they are unsure and need more time.

- **D**: The classmate is not confused about the topic; they react to the writer's hesitation.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Everyone had an instant opinion... I didn't”\

The writer explains that the issue is not simple: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I just don't buy it.” This shows the phone-ban discussion is used as an example of a topic that needs time and thought.

- *Incorrect options:* A, C, D

- **A**: The teacher does not ignore the writer. The writer says, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$my teacher smiled.”

- **C**: The writer does not argue phones should never be banned; they say phones are distracting but important.

- **D**: The writer does not claim classmates are addicted to phones. Addiction is never mentioned.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{False}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$when people act like it's a simple problem with a simple solution, I just don't buy it.”\

This shows the writer believes the issue is complex. The phrase $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I just don't buy it” means the writer is not convinced that this issue has a simple solution.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{True}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Online it's even worse.”\

The writer mentions that online spaces are more difficult than classes. They then continue to explain how online discussions are fast and expect $\text{\textquotedblleft}$immediate” reactions.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{False}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$But I do care”

The writer rejects the idea that silence means a lack of care. Instead, they explain how silence means careful consideration of the topic.
