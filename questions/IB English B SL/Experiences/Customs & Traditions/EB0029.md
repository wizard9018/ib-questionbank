$\Large{\textrm{The Meaning of Mardi Gras in New Orleans}}$

*Mardi Gras* in New Orleans is one of the world's most famous festivals, celebrated annually in February or March, before Lent begins. Its roots trace back to French colonial traditions, yet over centuries, it has blended with African, Indigenous, Spanish, and Creole influences to create something uniquely New Orleans. For weeks leading up to Mardi Gras day, the streets come alive with vibrant parades featuring elaborate floats, dancers in dazzling costumes, and music that fills the air. Each parade krewe (group) spends the year planning their floats, music, and performances to tell a story through art, rhythm, and color. 

For many New Orleanians, *Mardi Gras* is about freedom, joy, and community; it's a time to set aside everyday concerns and fully express oneself. The festival also celebrates the rich diversity of New Orleans society, where people from all walks of life join together to revel in the festivities. *Mardi Gras* attracts millions of tourists each year, eager to experience the sights, sounds, and lively atmosphere of this cultural celebration. For both locals and visitors, *Mardi Gras* is an unforgettable experience that captures the spirit of New Orleans.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{H}$   [1 mark]

- "it has blended with African, Indigenous, Spanish, and Creole influences"

\

*Blend* and *mix* are similar in meaning. They refer to something that combines different things, in this case, traditions and cultures from different places.

- **Option E** is incorrect. "Traditional European parades" wrongly suggests the celebration is purely European; the text makes clear that the parades come from a blend of many cultural influences.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- “vibrant parades featuring elaborate floats, dancers in dazzling costumes, and music that fills the air" \

*Vibrant* and *colorful* are similar in meaning. They refer to something bright and full of life, in this case, the parades and dancers. 

- **Option C** is incorrect. The text mentions “*Mardi Gras* attracts millions of tourists each year,” but it does not say that tourists are present in the previous weeks.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$   [1 mark]

- “it's a time to set aside everyday concerns and fully express oneself" \

“Set aside everyday concerns" and *relax* are similar in meaning. They refer to taking a break from daily worries, in this case, during the festival. 

- **Option G** is incorrect. *Mardi Gras* has its roots in French colonial traditions. However, people do not celebrate this.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$   [1 mark]

- “the sights, sounds, and lively atmosphere of this cultural celebration" 

\

*Lively* and *exciting* are similar in meaning. They refer to something full of energy and activity, in this case, the festival's atmosphere. People from all over the world come to New Orleans to enjoy the atmosphere.

- **Option A** is incorrect. Tourists come for the lively festival, not to celebrate Lent. In fact, the text mentions Mardi Gras takes place “in February or March, before Lent begins.”
