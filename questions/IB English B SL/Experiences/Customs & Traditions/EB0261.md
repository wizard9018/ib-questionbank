**Maximum mark: 5**

**Remembrance Day**

Remembrance Day takes place in Britain every year on the Sunday closest to November 11th. This commemoration marks the day the First World War ended: at 11:00 a.m. on the 11th day of the 11th month in the year 1918. The reason for this memorial service is to remember all those who died while fighting in World Wars I and II.

Prior to the actual event, many schools and organizations rehearse parades and prepare the traditional paper poppy flowers, $\underline{\textrm{which}}$ people wear on their lapels during the event. Due to $\underline{\textrm{their}}$ bright red colour, the poppy symbolizes the hardship and sacrifice of soldiers. They can also be seen, even today, in battlefields around Europe.

On the actual day, war veterans join the different parades and proudly display $\underline{\textrm{their}}$ medals. Children lay poppy wreaths on the monuments and clap at the soldiers. In the evening, a remembrance service is held at the Royal Albert Hall, where people sing hymns and give speeches.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$   [1 mark]

- The phrasal verb *take place* means to happen or *occur* when referring to an event. In this context, the commemoration *occurs* in Britain.\

\

**Option B** is incorrect. The phrase is *cancelled* means that an event is not going to occur. \

\

**Option C** is incorrect, as the phrase *is located* means that something *can be found* in a place. Taking into consideration the context, the event occurs, but it is not an item that *can be found* somewhere. \

\

**Option D** is incorrect. The word *reveal* means to make something visible that was hidden. According to the text, the commemoration is not revealed since it was not a secret. 

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$   [1 mark]

- The word *indicate* means to point out a specific date. In the context of the text, the event is commemorated on that specific moment and date because it indicates the end of World War I.\

\

**Option A** is incorrect. The word *recognize* means to acknowledge. In the context of this text, nobody denies the end of World War I, therefore, there is no need to recognize it.\

\

**Option C** is incorrect. The word *underline* means to draw a line under a word in a text. Its literal meaning can be confused with the literal meaning of the word *mark*, which also means to draw a line. \

\

**Option D** is incorrect. The word *prepare* means to get something ready for an event, but there is no connection with the idea of the word *mark* with the preparation of the event.

The word *which* refers back to the words $\text{\textquotedblleft}$paper poppy flowers”. People first prepare the flowers and then place them on their lapels since they are a symbol of this commemoration.

The word *their* refers to the word $\text{\textquotedblleft}$poppy flowers” because people connect the red colour of the poppy with the blood of the soldiers.
