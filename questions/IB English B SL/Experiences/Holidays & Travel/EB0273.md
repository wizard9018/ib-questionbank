# EB0273

**Maximum mark: 7**

June 2nd

**How can you become an eco-traveler?**

Hey guys! Today, we'll talk about how to be eco-travelers. I became one when I visited New Zealand and booked a room at one of the eco-lodges along the coastline. Since it was my first time at an eco-hotel, everything felt like an adventure: from the bamboo walls to the lack of air conditioning, from the organic breakfast in the morning to the chorus of crickets outside my window at night!

The first step to becoming an eco-traveler is to realize that mass tourism is no longer a sustainable option. Large hotels and overcrowded places are no longer a choice for me. Helping the environment, buying at a local store, and reducing my carbon footprint every time I travel are my life objectives.

Let me tell you the steps to be an eco traveler:

- Understand what ecotourism means (read the information in my previous post!)

- Pick an eco-hotel with a LEED or Green Key certification (there are other certifications, just check)

- Support local communities (Try local food, explore markets)

- Choose green transportation (Use bicycles or walk)

- Minimize waste (recycle and reuse)

I hope all this information is helpful and inspiring. Consider it next time you travel. Leave your comments!

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{booked}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I visited New Zealand and booked a room at one of the eco-lodges along the coastline.”\

To *book a room* means to make a reservation, in this case, at an eco-hotel. 

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{like an adventure}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Since it was my first time at an eco-hotel, everything felt like an adventure”\

The answer must have the word *like*, as this indicates the writer felt this experience was similar to an adventure (not exactly an adventure.)

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$*any **one** of the following answers:* 

- $\textrm{large hotels}$

- $\textrm{overcrowded places}$        [1 mark]

- “mass tourism [...] Large hotels and overcrowded places” \

The writer gives two characteristics of mass tourism that they do not want to experience anymore.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{objectives}$   [1 mark]

- The words *objectives* and *aims* are synonyms. Therefore, *aims* is a good replacement word for *objectives*.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{certification}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Pick an eco-hotel with a LEED or Green Key certification”\

The writer suggests picking a hotel that has a *certification* that assesses it as an ecological option.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{green transportation}$   [1 mark]

- The writer mentions that to become an eco-traveler, you should $\text{\textquotedblleft}$choose green transportation,” and the options mention *bicycles* or *walk*. Cycling and walking are both good examples of green transportation. 

$7.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{next time you travel}$   [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Consider it next time you travel.”\

The writer mentions that if the information in the blog is helpful, they want the readers to *consider* or *think about* it in their future trips.
