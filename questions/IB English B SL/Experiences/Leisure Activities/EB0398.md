**Cutting Edge**\

*By our motorsport correspondent*

Donna Merkley does not look like a typical racing driver. The 47-year-old from Saskatoon, Saskatchewan wears jeans and an old leather jacket, a bicycle helmet, and swimming goggles. For the last eleven years, 50 weeks a year, Donna has ruled the world of competitive lawnmower racing. She currently holds three regional titles and is described by both admirers and rivals as $`$ $\underline{\textrm{relentless}}$'.

**[-1-]**\

The machine she races - called Gerald - is a modified ride-on mower with the engine tuned for maximum speed. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$People assume these things crawl,” she says. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Mine does not.” Gerald, a 1987 Husqvarna she has rebuilt entirely on her own, can reach speeds of around 60 kilometres per hour.

**[-2-]**\

Competition in Donna's league is $\underline{\textrm{fierce}}$. Her closest competitor, a retired schoolteacher from Alberta, has finished ahead of her just twice in four seasons. Donna's comment about him: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$He's good. I'm better.” Regional events draw drivers from across Canada and the US, and audiences are surprisingly large.

**[-3-]**\

Racing is more than a hobby for Donna - it's a $\underline{\textrm{lifelong}}$ commitment. She is currently designing a training programme for junior competitors and has plans to enter her first international event next spring. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Gerald,” she says with a smile, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$is already being prepared.”

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Her current model, a 1987 Husqvarna she has rebuilt entirely on her own, can reach speeds of around 60 kilometres per hour."\

This paragraph focuses on the technical details of Donna's racing mower - its modifications, speed, and history.\

\

**Option A** sounds relevant but does not describe the paragraph's specific focus on the machine itself. 

 

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Her closest competitor, a retired schoolteacher from Alberta, has finished ahead of her just twice in four seasons."\

This paragraph describes Donna's rivals - the people she races against, race results, and the competitive atmosphere of regional events.\

\

**Option C** is incorrect. The paragraph mentions large audiences and a competitive atmosphere, which could suggest friendship, but the focus is clearly on rivalry. 

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

- "Racing is more than a hobby for Donna - it's a lifelong commitment."\

This paragraph moves beyond competition to explore what the sport means to Donna personally, including her plans for its future.\

\

**Option E** is incorrect. The word $`$speed' appears earlier in the text, making this heading plausible, but this paragraph focuses on Donna's commitment to the sport.

 

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$is described by both admirers and rivals as $`$relentless'"\

*Relentless* describes someone who never gives up or stops trying. *Persistent* has the same meaning - continuing without stopping despite meeting difficulties.\

\

**Option A** is incorrect. *Ambitious* means wanting to achieve success, which is related but does not capture the meaning of never stopping or giving up.

 

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Competition in Donna's league is fierce."\

*Fierce* describes something very strong or forceful in this context. *Intense* has a similar meaning.\

\

**Option F** is not correct here. *Powerful* could describe physical strength but does not reflect the meaning of fierce competition in this context.

 

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

- $\text{\textquotedblleft}$it's a lifelong commitment"\

*Lifelong* describes something that continues for the whole of a person's life. *Lasting* is a very close synonym meaning $`$continuing for a long time.'\

\

**Option D** is incorrect. *Permanent* suggests something that will never end or change, which is stronger than lifelong and does not fit naturally in this context.
