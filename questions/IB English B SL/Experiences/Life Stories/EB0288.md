# EB0288

**Maximum mark: 7**

**Inspiring People: Lizzie Velazquez**

Lizzie Velasquez is a motivational speaker and inspiring person. She was born with a rare condition (only two other people in the world have it!) that prevents her body from storing fat or building muscle, yet her organs and brain developed normally. Despite facing health challenges and bullying from a young age, Lizzie has become a powerful voice for kindness and resilience.

One of the key moments in her life was when she was 17. A video labeling her negatively went viral and gathered a ton of terrible comments. Instead of allowing this to define her or affect her, she used the experience to work harder on her mission to inspire and be an example to others. 

Today, Lizzie is a successful motivational speaker, author, and YouTuber. Her TEDx talks and her documentary *A Brave Heart* have reached millions. In her book *Dare to Be Kind*, she shares how compassion and self-acceptance can change lives. Lizzie promotes the idea of being $\text{\textquotedblleft}$differently-abled” rather than $\text{\textquotedblleft}$disabled,” encouraging people to see beauty in uniqueness.

Her story is a reminder that everyone deserves respect and the chance to shine.

**The following statements are either true or false. Write True or False, then justify it using words as they appear in the text. Both parts are required for [1 mark].** 

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$Two more people in the world have the same disease as Lizzie.    [1 mark]

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$Lizzie Velazquez suffered bullying, and this made her depressed.    [1 mark]

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$At 17, she went viral and this was a negative experience.    [1 mark]

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$After the viral video, she decided to become a source of hope and inspiration for others.    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{True}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$only two other people in the world have it!”\

\

The text mentions that Lizzie has a *rare condition* or *disease*. And specifies that only two other people in the world have it, too.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{False}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$has become a powerful voice for kindness and resilience”\

\

Lizzie did not get depressed after being bullied, instead she became an advocate for kindness and resilience.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{True}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$gathered a ton of terrible comments”\

\

The viral video was a bad experience for Lizzie, as it had nasty (*terrible*) comments about her. *Gathered* is a synonym of *collected*.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{True}$    [1 mark]

- Justification: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$to inspire and be an example to others”\

\

To *be an example* means *to inspire others*. The text mentions that the negative experience from the viral video made her work harder to be an inspiration.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

- *Today* refers to her current activities, to what she is doing.\

\

Option **F**, *actually*, is incorrect. *Actually* means *really* and it is used to emphasize that something someone said or did is surprising.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

- *In addition* is a connector used to add more information, in this case, about Lizzie's achievements.\

\

Option **D**, *because*, could be confusing in this space. However,  the word *because* would introduce a reason for something, not add information.

$7.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- *Instead of* shows contrast between two ideas *differently-abled* and *disabled*)\

\

Option **A**, *however*, is incorrect as it is used to show contrast between two sentences, not words or phrases.
