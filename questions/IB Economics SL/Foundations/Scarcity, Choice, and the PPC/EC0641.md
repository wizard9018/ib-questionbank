# EC0641

Though it is the world's largest vanilla producer and rich in minerals like nickel and gold, Madagascar faces persistent poverty, slow economic growth, and poor governance. Agriculture, fishing, and forestry dominate the economy: seventy per cent of the labour force works in these sectors. Eighty per cent of the country's population lives below the poverty line of US\$1.90 per day.

In contrast, Denmark is a mixed economy that enjoys high living standards and an annual per capita income of over US\$60 000. Its economy is focused on services, trade, and manufacturing, while only a small part of the population is employed in agriculture and fishing.

    

1. Explain how the economic concept of (relative) scarcity is likely to differ between the economies of Madagascar and Denmark. [3 marks]

2. Explain why Madagascar and Denmark both face the problem of scarcity despite the difference in their levels of economic development. [3 marks]

3. Explain the opportunity cost that Denmark and Madagascar would face if each government decided to build a new international airport in its capital city. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    Both the economies of Madagascar and Denmark face the economic concept of scarcity since their resources are limited. However, while scarcity in Madagascar means that people cannot access basic necessities such as food or clean water, in Denmark it means that they cannot afford all their wants, such as a new car or a second home. 

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For explaining that both countries face the economic problem of scarcity since there is a limit to their available resources (land, labour, capital) and their economic output ($\checkmark$).

    For explaining the difference in how scarcity presents itself in the two countries. 

    - For Madagascar, scarcity means a lack of basic necessities/needs such as water, staple food, shelter and/or essential medicines ($\checkmark$). 

    - For Denmark, scarcity means not being able to have access to all wants, while all basic needs are satisfied ($\checkmark$).

    

2. $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

 Scarcity occurs when the available resources cannot satisfy all needs and wants. As both countries have limited economic resources (such as land, capital and labour) and unlimited wants, each country's production is finite, which leads to scarcity despite the differences in their levels of development.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For explaining that scarcity occurs because not all needs and wants can be satisfied given the available resources ($\checkmark$).

    For explaining that both countries experience limited resources but have unlimited wants—this is the fundamental economic problem ($\checkmark$).

    For explaining that scarcity can be seen as each country's production is finite, thus not all wants can be fully satisfied ($\checkmark$).

3. $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    As Madagascar is a poor country with 80$\%$ of its population below the poverty line, building a new international airport in its capital city may be extremely costly for its government. It may have to reduce or halt other essential public services, such as education and healthcare, which would represent a high opportunity cost.

    In contrast, Denmark is a richer country with average annual incomes of over US\$60 000. Building a new international airport may not prove so challenging for its government, which could maintain all or most of the public services it provides. This means Denmark would face a much lower opportunity cost than Madagascar.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

    For explaining that the government of a poor country like Madagascar may have very limited public finances, which is supported by the fact that 80$\%$ of the population lives below the poverty line ($\checkmark$).

    For explaining that for Madagascar, building an international airport may result in the reduction or suspension of other government-funded services due to the lack of money. These services are highly important for a country, which means Madagascar would face a high opportunity cost ($\checkmark$).

    

    For explaining that the government of a rich country like Denmark may have better public finances, which can be seen by the fact that the average income in the country is over US\$60 000 ($\checkmark$).

    

    For explaining that for Denmark, building an international airport is not likely to significantly reduce government-funded services, which would result in a lower opportunity cost than in Madagascar ($\checkmark$).
