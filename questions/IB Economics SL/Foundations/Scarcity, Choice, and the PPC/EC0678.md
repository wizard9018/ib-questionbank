# EC0678

A country's government is considering upgrading the transport infrastructure connecting two large cities. While some favour the construction of a high-speed rail line due to its positive externalities, others claim that a new highway would greatly benefit local firms. Due to budget constraints, only one of the two projects can be built. 

A recent government study compared the two projects on some key features, as shown in the table below.

||High-speed rail project|Highway project|

|:--|:--|:--|

|Total cost|US\$7 billion|US\$7 billion|

|Impact on local employment|+7 000 jobs in services valued at US\$4.2 billion|+9 000 jobs in manufacturing valued at US$3.6 billion|

|Environmental impact|Reduced $\ce{CO2}$ emissions valued at US\$0.9 billion|Reduced traffic on local roads valued at US\$1.7 billion|

1.  Define the term *positive externalities*. [2 marks]

2.  Identify **two** examples of opportunity costs when choosing the highway project. [2 marks]

3.  Determine the total opportunity cost, in US dollars, when building the high-speed rail project.[2 marks]\

The country's government decides to build the high-speed rail project.

4.  Using an externality diagram, explain where the market achieves allocative efficiency. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer: }}$\

    Positive externalities are the benefits resulting from someone’s action on a third party.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation: }}$\

    For defining positive externalities as

    A beneficial impact of someone’s action ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$)

    On a third party beyond the consumer or producer ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$)

2.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer: }}$\

    Opportunity cost of funding the highway project include the 7 000 jobs in services and the reduced $\ce{CO2}$ emissions.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation: }}$\

    Opportunity costs are the benefits forgone when making a choice.

    For identifying the **two** examples of opportunity cost from the information in the table ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$)

    - The 7 000 jobs in services that would be created when building the high-speed rail 

    - The reduction in $\ce{CO2}$ emissions when building the high-speed rail

3.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer: }}$\

    The total opportunity cost when building the high-speed project will be the sum of $\textrm{US}\text{\textdollar}$3.6 billion (from the 9 000 jobs in manufacturing) and US$\text{\textdollar}$1.7 billion (from the reduction in traffic), totalling US$\text{\textdollar}3.6 + \textrm{US}\text{\textdollar}1.7 = \textrm{US}\text{\textdollar 5.3 billion.}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation: }}$\

    When determining the total opportunity cost of building the high-speed rail project, the value of the best alternative is considered. This includes the value of the 9 000 jobs in manufacturing and the value of the reduced traffic on local roads.

    Opportunity cost =$\textrm{US}\text{\textdollar}3.6 + \textrm{US}\text{\textdollar} 1.7 =$ :answer[**US$\text{\textdollar}$5.3 billion**]

    The high-speed project results in positive externalities of consumption due to the increased local employment and the reduction in 

    $\ce{CO2}$ emissions. As a consequence, the marginal social benefit (MSB) curve is above the marginal private benefit (MPB) curve. In other words, MSB exceeds MPB. Allocative efficiency occurs at $Q_{OPT}$, where $MSB=MSC$.

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation: }}$\

    For drawing a **fully labelled** externality diagram illustrating: 

    - positive externalities of consumption (the MSB curve is above the MPB curve) ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$)

    - the allocatively efficient quantity (where $MSC = MSB$) ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$)

    For explaining that the MSB curve is greater than the MPB curve as a result of the positive externalities in terms of higher local employment and diminished $\ce{CO2}$ emissions ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$).

    For explaining that allocative efficiency is achieved at $MSB=MSC$, which corresponds to the quantity $Q_{OPT}$ ($\textcolor{black}{\checkmark}$).
