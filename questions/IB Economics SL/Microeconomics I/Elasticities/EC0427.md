# EC0427

**Figure 1**

![EC0427 fig1](<images/EC0427 fig1.png>)

1.  With reference to **Figure 1**, state whether the PES value is smaller between points M and N, or if the PES value is smaller between points K and L. [1 mark]

2.  With reference to **Figure 1**, explain the difference in the PES values on $\mathrm{S_1}$ and $\mathrm{S_2}$ when the price increases from $\mathrm{P_1}$ to $\mathrm{P_2}$.  [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

    The PES value between points K and L is smaller than the PES value between points M and N.

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$: \

    Any supply curve intersecting the Y axis is relatively elastic along its entire length, therefore PES values exceed 1.

    Any supply curve intersecting the X axis is relatively inelastic along its entire length, therefore PES values fall between 0 and 1.

    All PES values on $\mathrm{S_2}$ are smaller than all PES values on $\mathrm{S_1}$.

    For stating that the PES value between points K and L is smaller than the PES value between points M and N. ($\checkmark$)

2.  $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

    The formula for PES is $\dfrac{\%\Delta Quantity\ supplied}{\%\Delta Price}$.

    The change in price is the same in respect to both curves, so it can be ignored. Similarly, because the curves are parallel, the increase in $\mathrm{Q_S}$ is the same in numerical terms. However, the increase in $\mathrm{Q_S}$ in percentage terms is different on $\mathrm{S_1}$ and $\mathrm{S_2}$. Point M represents a lesser quantity supplied than point K, so the increase in $\mathrm{Q_S}$ will be a greater percentage in respect to point M than to point K. 

    Returning to the formula for PES, it can be seen that the denominator is the same for the change on both curves, but the numerator$-$the percent change in quantity supplied$-$is greater between points M and N. Therefore, the PES values on $\mathrm{S_1}$ are greater than those on $\mathrm{S_2}$.

    $\underline{\textrm{Solution}}$:

    The formula for PES is $\dfrac{\%\Delta Quantity\ supplied}{\%\Delta Price}$.

    For an explanation clarifying that the PES values differ for the two supply curves because the increase in $\mathrm{Q_S}$ is a greater percentage on $\mathrm{S_1}$. ($\checkmark$)

    For referring to the PES formula in the explanation. ($\checkmark$)

    For stating that the PES values are greater on $\mathrm{S_1}$ than $\mathrm{S_2}$ when price increases from $\mathrm{P_1}$ to $\mathrm{P_2}$. ($\checkmark$)
