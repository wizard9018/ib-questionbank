# PH0803

A charged particle travels at a constant horizontal velocity, $v$ inside a region where electric and magnetic fields that are oriented perpendicular to one another are present as shown.

![Screenshot 2025-01-29 at 9.38.44 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-01-29 at 9.38.44 AM.JPEG>)

Gravitational forces are negligible. Which of the following is a correct expression for the velocity, $v$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{E}{B}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{B}{E}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{B}{E^2}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{E}{B}}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Electric and magnetic fields exert forces in opposite directions. (Electric force is up and magnetic force is down if the charge is positive and vice versa.)

No deflection implies net force = 0. Magnitude of magnetic and electric forces are equal.

- $F_E=E\,q$
- $F_B=Bqv\,\sin\,\theta=Bqv$  (Field is perpendicular to velocity so $\sin\,\theta=\sin\,90=1$)

- $Eq=Bqv$

Canceling $q$’s gives

- $E=B\,v$

- $v=\dfrac{E}{B}$
