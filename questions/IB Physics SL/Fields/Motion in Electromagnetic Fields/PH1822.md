# PH1822

The diagram shows the cross section of a current carrying wire in a magnetic field of flux density $0.5$ T. The only force acting on the wire is the force due to the magnetic field which causes it to accelerate to the right at a rate of $0.4$ m s$^{-2}$.

![PH1822q](images/PH1822q.JPEG)

The mass per unit length of the wire is $0.02$ kg m$^{-1}$. Which of the following correctly shows the magnitude and direction of the current in the wire?

$\\$

| | Magnitude / $\textrm{mA}$ | Direction |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| 16 | Into the page |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 16 | Out of the page |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 64 | Into the page |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 64 | Out of the page |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

**Magnitude**

The force $F$ experienced by a current carrying wire in a magnetic field is given by:

- $F=BIL\textrm{sin}\,\theta$

Where $B$ is the magnetic flux density of the field, $I$ is the current in the wire, $L$ is the length of the wire and, $\theta$ is the angle between $B$ and $I$.

Here $B$ and $I$ are perpendicular so we have $\textrm{sin}\,\theta=\textrm{sin}\,90\degree=1$

- $F=BIL$

Substituting $F=ma$ gives:

- $ma=BIL$

- $\dfrac{m}{L}a=BI$

$\dfrac{m}{L}$ is mass per unit length. Substituting our values and rearranging for $I$, we have:

- $(0.02)(0.4)=(0.5)I$

- $I=0.016$ A $=16$ mA

**Direction**

We can deduce the direction of the current using Fleming's left hand rule. According to the rule, align your left hand such that the index finger points in the direction of the magnetic field (upward), and your thumb in the direction of the force (to the right - the same as the acceleration). Your middle finger then points in the direction of the current (into the page).
