# PH0457

**Maximum mark: 8**

The diagram shows a cross-sectional view of a current carrying wire. The current direction is out of the plane of the paper.

![PH0457-1](images/PH0457-1.png)

1. Draw the magnetic field lines due to this wire.    [2 marks]

A second wire carrying current $B$ is placed near wire $A$.

![PH0457-3](images/PH0457-3.png)

The current in wire $A$ is 10 A, while in wire $B$ is 12 A. The length of wire $B$ within the magnetic field of $A$ is 1 m.

2. 1. Draw an arrow representing the direction of the force $F$ on wire $B$ due to the magnetic field of wire $A$.    [1 mark]

    The magnetic field $B$ of wire $A$ can be found using the following formula
    $$
        B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I_A}{2\pi r}
    $$

    where $r$ is the distance from the wire.

    2. If the distance between wires $A$ and $B$ is 0.1 cm, calculate the magnitude of the magnetic force on wire $B$ due to the magnetic field of wire $A$. State an appropriate unit for your answer.      [3 marks]

3. A third wire $C$ is placed between wires $A$ and $B$.

![PH0457-5](images/PH0457-5.png)

The total force on wire $C$ due to $A$ and $B$ makes it move to the right as shown.

Determine the direction of the current of wire C. Justify your answer.    [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Concentric circles with increasing spacing.$\,\checkmark$

    Anti-clockwise direction.$\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$
    The magnetic field due to a wire carrying current is concentric circles with the wire at the centre of each circle.
    The direction of the magnetic field is determined from the right-hand grip rule.

    ![PH0457-2](images/PH0457-2.png)

2. 1. Arrow directed to the right.$\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        The magnetic field of wire $A$ at wire $B$ is upwards. The direction of the current in wire $B$ is into page.

        Using the right hand rule, the force on $B$ due to the magnetic field of $A$ is to the right.

        ![PH0457-4](images/PH0457-4.png)

        This leads to the fact that wires with parallel currents attract while those with anti-parallel currents repel.

    2. $B = \dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(10)}{(2\pi)(0.1\times10^{-2})}\,\checkmark$

        $B = \ll (2.0\times10^{-3}) \, \ce{T}\gg$

        $F = (2\times10^{-3})(12)(1)\,$

        $F = 0.024\, $ $\checkmark$  :answer[N] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        The magnetic field of wire $A$ is calculated from the formula

        $\begin{aligned}
        B &= \dfrac{\mu_0 I_A}{2\pi r} \\
        B &= \dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(10)}{(2\pi)(0.1\times10^{-2})} = (2.0)(10^{-3})\, \ce{T}
        \end{aligned}$

        The magnetic force on $B$ due to magnetic field of $A$

        $F = B_A I_B L_B = (2\times10^{-3}))(12)(1) = 0.024$ N

3. Current of wire $C$ is into the page. $\checkmark$

   Current opposite direction of A / anti-parallel currents repel/ Current same as B / parallel currents attract/explanation with use of right hand or left hand rule $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$
    For wire $C$ to move to the right, it must be repelled by wire $A$ and attracted by wire $B$.

    To have a repelling force from wire $A$, the two currents must be in opposite directions. Since the current in wire $A$ is out of page, so current in wire $C$ is into page.

    Also for wire $B$ to attract wire $C$, both must have the same direction of current, which means that current in wire $C$ is into the page.
