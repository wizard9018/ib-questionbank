# PH0965

**Maximum mark: 12**

1. In a cathode ray tube (CRT) TV, an electron is accelerated from rest to a high speed by a potential difference of $3.4\times 10^3$ V. The electron then enters a region of uniform electric field horizontally, where it is deflected. The horizontal displacement of the electron in the region of the field is $5.0\times10^{-3}$ m, and the magnitude of the electric field $E$ is $7.5\times10^4\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$.

    ![PH0965aaaa](images/PH0965aaaa.png)

    1. Define net force in terms of Newton's Second Law of Motion. [1 mark]

    2. Show that the speed of the electron just before reaching the region of the electric field is around $3.5\times10^7  \, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$. [2 marks]

    3.     The electron takes 12 ns to reach the region of the electric field. Determine the average force applied to the electron during this time.  [2 marks]

2. The electron enters an electric field region and is deflected upward.

    1. Show that the acceleration of the electron in the region of the electric field is around $1.3\times10^{16} \, \ce{m\,s^{-2}}$. [2 marks]

    2.     Determine the angle of deflection of the electron as it leaves the region. [3 marks]

    3. Instead of electrons, protons are accelerated to the same speed before entering the electric field region. Deduce how the deflection path of the protons differs from that of the electrons. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. The rate of change in momentum/the product of an object's acceleration and its mass $\checkmark$

        (If the equation is given, then the symbols must be defined)

    2. Kinetic energy gained by electron $=(1.6\times 10^{-19})(3.4\times 10^3)=5.4\times 10^{-16}$ J

        $\sqrt{\dfrac{2E}{m}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(5.4\times 10^{-16})}{9.11\times 10^{-31}}}=$ :answer[$3.46\times 10^7 \, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$]

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        To kinetic energy $E$ gained by the electron can be expressed as

        - $E=qV$

        Where $q$ is the charge of the electron and $V$ is the potential difference in which the electron is accelerated.

        - $E=(1.6\times 10^{-19})(3.4\times 10^3)=5.4\times 10^{-16}\, \ce{J}$

        By using the equation of kinetic energy

        - $E=\dfrac{mv^2}{2}$

        - $v^2=\dfrac{2E}{m}$

        - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2E}{m}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(5.4\times 10^{-16})}{9.11\times 10^{-31}}}=3.46\times 10^7 \,\ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

    3. $\dfrac{mv}{t}=\dfrac{(9.11\times 10^{-31})(3.5\times 10^7)}{12\times 10^{-9}}$ $\checkmark$

        :answer[$2.6\times 10^{-15}\, \ce{N}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The force $F$

        - $F=\dfrac{\Delta p}{t}$

        Where the change in momentum $\Delta p$ is the final momentum of the electron

        - $F=\dfrac{m\Delta v}{t}=\dfrac{(9.11\times 10^{-31})(3.5\times 10^7)}{12\times 10^{-9}}=2.6\times 10^{-15}\, \ce{N}$

2. 1. $\dfrac{qE}{m}=\dfrac{(1.6\times 10^{-19})(7.5\times 10^4)}{9.11\times 10^{-31}}$

        :answer[$1.32\times 10^{16}\, \ce{m\,s^{-2}}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The net force $F$ acting on the electron in the region of the electric field:

        - $F=qE=ma$

        - $a=\dfrac{qE}{m}=\dfrac{(1.6\times 10^{-19})(7.5\times 10^4)}{9.11\times 10^{-31}}=1.32\times 10^{16}\, \ce{m\,s^{-2}}$

    2. time of motion between the plates $=\dfrac{5.0\times 10^{-3}}{3.5\times 10^7}=1.4\times 10^{-10}$ s $\checkmark$

        vertical velocity as it leaves $=(1.3\times 10^{16})(1.4\times 10^{-10})=1.9\times 10^6\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{1.9\times 10^6}{3.5\times 10^7}=$ :answer[$3.0\degree$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The horizontal velocity $v_h$ of the electron is constant during the motion between the plates. The time $t$ electron spent between parallel plates:

        - $t=\dfrac{s}{v_h}$

        where $s$ is the horizontal displacement of the electron

        - $t=\dfrac{s}{v_h}=\dfrac{5.0\times 10^{-3}}{3.5\times 10^7}=1.4\times 10^{-10}$ s

        The vertical velocity $v_v$  of the electron at the instant of leaving the plate:

        - $v_v=u+at=0+(1.3\times 10^{16})(1.4\times 10^{-10})=1.9\times 10^6\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

        The angle of deflection $\theta$ can be found by

        - $\tan{\theta}=\dfrac{v_v}{v_h}=\dfrac{1.9\times 10^6}{3.5\times 10^7}$

        - $\theta =3.0\degree$

    3. The force acting on the protons are opposite so they deflect in the opposite direction/downwards $\checkmark$

        They are heavier so they deflect less  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Protons and electrons experience the same magnitude of the force from an electric field but in opposite directions. Therefore, protons are deflected in the opposite direction to electrons. Additionally, protons have greater mass than electrons, so they accelerate slower and have lower vertical velocity as they leave the electric field region. Consequently, the angle of deflection for protons is less than for electrons.
