# PH0908

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. An initially neutral rod and cloth are rubbed together. Explain why the cloth becomes negatively charged. [2 marks]

2. 1. The rod retains its charge and is now placed near an initially neutral metal sphere that is grounded (Earthed) by a wire as shown in the diagram below.

        ![image](images/image.png)

        The sphere and the rod do not make contact. Explain why an electric current flows in the wire connecting the sphere and the ground for a short time. [2 marks]

    2. State the direction of the conventional current in the wire. [1 mark]

    3. It is given that the current has an average magnitude of $0.80\,\mu$ A and flows for a total time of $1.4\,\mu $s. Show that the total charge passing a point on the wire in this time is around $1.1$ pC. [1 mark]

    4. The ground connection is then removed, and the sphere remains charged. State the name of the phenomenon by which the sphere gained its electric charge. [1 mark]

3. The diameter of the sphere is 6.0 cm. Calculate the electric field strength at a point 40 cm away from the surface of the sphere.

     [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Friction transfers electrons.   $\checkmark$

    From the rod to the cloth.  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The objects initially are neutral because they each have the same number of electrons and protons. The chemical properties of the molecules in the cloth are such that it attracts electrons more than the rod. As the objects are rubbed together some electrons are transferred from the rod to the cloth.

    Note: A very small number of electrons is transferred compared to the total number of electrons in each of the objects. The electric force is very strong, so even a small imbalance is detectable.

2. 1. The (positively) charged rod causes negative/opposite charge to build up on sphere near to the rod.

        OR

        Opposite charges attract. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\text{Electrons}}$ flow between the ground and the sphere / from the ground to the sphere. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The rod is initially positively charged. (In part (a)(i) the cloth is negatively charged so rod must be positively charged due to conservation of charge).

        A negative charge will build up on the sphere near to the rod because opposite charges attract. This negative charge is comprised of electrons that flow from the ground to the sphere.

    2. From sphere to the ground/downwards $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The rod is initially positively charged. (In part (a)(i) the cloth is negatively charged so rod must be positively charged due to conservation of charge.)

        The sphere becomes negatively charged. Electrons must flow from the ground to the sphere. Conventional current is the rate of flow of positive charge so current flows from the sphere to the ground.

    3. $\hspace{0.5em}Q=It$

        - $=(0.80\times 10^{-6})(1.40\times 10^{-6})$

        - $=1.12\times 10^{-12} \approx 1.1$ pC $\checkmark$

        Must see formula, substitution  and extra significant figure for mark.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $Q=It=(0.80\times 10^{-6})(1.40\times 10^{-6})=1.12\times 10^{-12} \approx 1.1$ pC

        $\mu$ (micro) has a value of $10^{-6}$ and $p$ (pico) has a value of $10^{-12}$ - Remember these are given in your formula book.

    4. (Charging by electrostatic) induction. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Electrostatic induction is the process by which a neutral object is charged without touching another charged object.

3. $E=\dfrac{kq}{r^2}$

    $E=\dfrac{(8.99\times 10^9)(1.1\times 10^{-12})}{(0.43)^2}$

    $E=0.053\,\ce{N\,C^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

    (Note: Allow use of $q=1.12$ pC leading to answer of $0.054 \, \ce{N\,C^{-1}}$)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    - $F=k\dfrac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$ - $F$ is the force between two charges $q_1$ and $q_2$

    - $E=\dfrac{F}{q}$  - $E$ is the force per unit charge at a point

    We can let $q_1$ be an arbitrary unit charge at a point near a fixed charge $q_2$:

    - $E=\dfrac{F}{q_1}$

    Combining these equations gives:

    - $Eq_1=k\dfrac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$

    - $E=\dfrac{kq_2}{r^2}$

    Here our fixed charge is the charge of the sphere - this is equal to the charge passing a point in wire (see part (b)(iii)) $=1.1\times 10^{-12}$ C.

    $r$ is the distance between the point and the center of the sphere $=$ distance between point and surface of sphere $+$ radius of sphere $= 40\,\ce{cm}+\dfrac{6}{2}\,\ce{cm}=43\, \ce{cm}=0.43$ m

    Substituting these values into the equations (Coulomb constant $k$ is given in your formula book) gives:

    - $E=\dfrac{(8.99\times 10^9)(1.1\times 10^{-12})}{(0.43)^2} = 0.053\, \ce{N\,C^{-1}}$
