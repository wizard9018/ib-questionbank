# PH0923

A negatively charged rod and neutral sphere are made from the same conducting material.

![image](images/image.png)

The rod and the sphere come into contact and are separated. Which of the following correctly describes the charge on the rod and the sphere after this encounter?

Assume that no charge is transferred to the surroundings at any point.

$\\$

| | Rod | Sphere |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Neutral | Negatively charged |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Negatively charged$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Neutral |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Negatively charged | Negatively charged |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Positively charged | $\hspace{1.5em}$Negatively charged$\hspace{1.5em}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Charge is transferred from the rod to the sphere by contact. Negative charges (electrons) will be distributed between the rod and sphere until their potentials are equal.

Given that there was an excess of negative charge to begin with – there will be the same excess of negative charge distributed between the rod and sphere a short time after they come into contact (electrons will travel quickly inside conducting material).

Rod and sphere will retain this negative charge when separated so will both be negatively charged in the end.
