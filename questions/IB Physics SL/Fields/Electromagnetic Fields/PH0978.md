# PH0978

Two charges are positioned as shown, each a distance $d$ from point $A$.

![image](images/image.png)

What is the magnitude and direction of the electric field at point $A$ due to the two charges?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Magnitude$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Direction$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{2kQ\cos(60\degree)}{d^2}$ |To the left  |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{2kQ\cos(60\degree)}{d^2}$ | To the right |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{2kQ\sin(60\degree)}{d^2}$ | To the left  |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{2kQ\sin(60\degree)}{d^2}$ | To the right |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The electric field vector points in the direction of force that a positive charge would experience and the magnitude of electric field at a point due to a charge is given by

- $E=\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$

Where $k$ the Coulomb constant, $Q$ is the charge and $r$ is the separation between the charge and the point.

So, at point $A$ the electric fields from each charge may be represented as follows

![image](images/image.png)

The combination of the two vectors results in zero vertical component - the resultant electric field points to the right.

So, we consider only the horizontal components of the field vectors for the magnitude. The magnitude of the field is

- $2\Bigl(\dfrac{kQ}{d^2}\cos(60\degree)\Bigr)=\dfrac{2kQ\cos(60\degree)}{d^2}$

This is illustrated below

![image](images/image.png)

Notice the triangle is equilateral.

- $\dfrac{2kQ\cos(60\degree)}{d^2}=\dfrac{kQ}{d^2}$
