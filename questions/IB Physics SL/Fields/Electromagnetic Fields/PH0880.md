# PH0880

Three charges $-Q$, $-Q$ and $+Q$ form an equilateral triangle. Point **X** lies at the center of the triangle. The distance between **X** and the center of each of the charges is $d$ as shown in the diagram below.

![PH0880](images/PH0880.png)

What is the magnitude of the electric field strength at **X**?

Note that $\cos (60\degree) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin (60\degree) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 0

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{kQ}{d^2}$
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2kQ}{d^2}$
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{9kQ}{4d^2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The magnitude of the electric field due to each charge is given by $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{kQ}{d^2}$

![PH0880_MS](images/PH0880_MS.png)

The horizontal component of electric fields of two negative charges are equal and opposite so cancel out.

The vertical component of the electric field due to one of the negative charges is given by

- $\dfrac{kQ}{d^2}\cos 60 = \dfrac{kQ}{2d^2}$

(Angles in equilateral triangle of
$60\degree$ $\text{\textendash}$ see diagram above for illustration)

Total electric field points vertically downwards on diagram and magnitude is

- $2 \left( \dfrac{kQ}{2d^2} \right) + \dfrac{kQ}{d^2} = \dfrac{2kQ}{d^2}$
