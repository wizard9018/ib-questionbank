# PH0928

In an experiment negatively charged spherical oil drops are allowed to fall into a region between two charged parallel plates. The plates are connected to an external power supply as shown in the diagram below:

![image](images/image.png)

Each drop has a different charge and size. For a given potential difference it is observed that some of the drops are suspended stationary between the plates.

Which graph correctly shows the relationship between the charge $q$ and the radius $r$ of these stationary drops?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For the stationary oil drop in equilibrium - Electric force $=$ Weight

- $Eq=mg $

$E$ and $g$ are constant but $m$ will depend on $r$.

We can write $m$ in terms of $r$ as follows:

- $m=\rho V=\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho$

Where $\rho$ is the density of the oil drop (or more correctly (density of oil drop - density of air) due to buoyancy)

Substituting this $m$ into our initial equation gives:

- $Eq=\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho g$

So $q \propto r^3$ the correct answer is D.

Alternatively:

Drops that are stationary all have the same mass/ charge ratio.

- $\dfrac{\text{Mass}}{\text{charge}} =$ constant

And mass $\propto$ volume $\propto r^3$

So $\dfrac{q}{r^3} =$ constant

- $q \propto r^3$
