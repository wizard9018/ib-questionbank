# PH0926

The diagrams below shows two drops of the same type of oil.

Oil drop $A$ is held stationary between two parallel plates a distance $d$ apart with a potential difference $V$ between them.

Oil drop $B$ is held stationary between two parallel plates a distance $3d$ apart with a potential difference $2V$ between them.

![image](images/image.png)

The charge per unit mass of oil drop A is $\alpha$. What is the charge per unit mass of oil drop B?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{6}\alpha$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2}{3}\alpha$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3}{2}\alpha$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $6\alpha$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For the drop to be stationary, it must be in equilibrium. The two forces acting on the drop are weight $=mg$ (downwards) and electric force $=Eq$ (upwards). The free body diagram is as follows:

![FBDoildrop](images/FBDoildrop.png)

Equating weight and electric force:

- $mg=Eq$

For drop $A$:

- $m_A g = \dfrac{V}{d}q_A$

- $\dfrac{dg}{V}=\dfrac{q_A}{m_A}=\alpha\hspace{1em}$ (as $\alpha$ is charge per unit mass)

For drop $B$:

- $m_B g=\dfrac{2V}{3d}q_B$

- $\dfrac{3dg}{2V}=\dfrac{q_B}{m_B} =\dfrac{3}{2} \alpha$
