# PH0893-2

**Maximum mark: 6**

The moon orbits the Earth at a constant speed of about $1022\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$. The moon's orbit can be approximated as circular. The ratio of the mass of the earth to the mass of the moon is 81 and the average distance between the centre of the earth and the moon is around $3.8 \times 10^8$ m.

1. Deduce the mass of the Earth. [3 marks]

2. On the graph below, sketch the variation of the gravitational field strength
g with the position $x$ from the surface of the Earth to the surface of the Moon. Take the surface of the earth as the origin of the graph. [3 marks]

    *(No need to add values to the axes)*

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Identification of gravitational attraction as the centripetal force $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $M = \dfrac{rv^2}{G}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ :answer[$6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The force responsible for the circular motion of the moon around the Earth is the gravitational attraction between Earth and the Moon:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = m \dfrac{v^2}{r}$

    Where $M$ and $m$ are the mass of the Earth and the Moon respectively.

    When the relationship is simplified and rearranged to make $M$ as the subject:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $M = \dfrac{rv^2}{G}$

    By substituting the given values:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $M = \dfrac{(3.8 \times 10^8)(1022)^2}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}} = 6.0 \times 10^{24}$ kg

    2. $g$ = zero at point closer to the moon $\checkmark$

        graviational strength on the surface of the Earth is bigger than the Moon surface $\checkmark$

        gravitational field directions are opposite in both side of the zero point $\checkmark$

        ![PH2893MS](images/PH2893MS.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The gravitational fields of the Earth and the Moon point in opposite directions. Therefore, they should cancel each other at a point between Earth and the Moon. Since Earth's mass is bigger, this point should be closer to the moon. Additionally, bigger mass also results in bigger gravitational field strength on the surface of the Earth. However, the effect of the Earth's larger radius is negligible compared to the effect of its larger mass.
