# PH0838

Planet A orbits a star with orbital radius $r_A$ and orbital speed $v_A$. Planet B orbits the same star with an orbital radius $4r_A$.

![image](images/image.png)

Which of the following shows a correct expression for the speed and angular velocity of planet B?

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Speed of Planet B$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Angular velocity of Planet B$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{v_A}{2}$ | $\dfrac{v_A}{8r_A}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{v_A}{2}$| $\dfrac{v_A}{2r_A}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $4v_A$ | $\dfrac{v_A}{r_A}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $4v_A$ | $\dfrac{4v_A}{r_B}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

$v=\dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$ where $T$ is the orbital period.

$v_A=\dfrac{2\pi r_A}{T_A}$ and $v_B=\dfrac{2\pi r_B}{T_B}$

We can use Kepler's third law ($T^2 \propto r^3$) to find the orbital period of planet B, $T_B$, in terms of the orbital period of planet A, $T_A$.

- $\dfrac{(T_A)^2}{(r_A)^3}=\dfrac{(T_B)^2}{(r_B)^3}$

$r_B=4r_A$ so

$\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1em} \dfrac{(T_A)^2}{(r_A)^3} &= \dfrac{(T_B)^2}{(4r_A)^3}\\
\hspace{1em}\\
\dfrac{(T_B)^2}{4^3} &= (T_A)^2\\
\hspace{1em}\\
(T_B)^2 &= 64(T_A)^2\\
\hspace{1em}\\
T_B &= 8T_A\\
\end{aligned}$

We may therefore write:

- $v_B = \dfrac{2\pi (4r_A)}{8T_A} = \dfrac{\pi r_A}{T_A}=\dfrac{v_A}{2}$

- ${\omega}_A = \dfrac{v_A}{r_A}$

- ${\omega}_A = \dfrac{v_B}{r_B} = \dfrac{(\frac{v_A}{2})}{4r_A} = \dfrac{v_A}{8r_A}$

Alternatively, we can find the necessary proportionality relations more directly by equating the gravitational force and the centripetal force.

For $v$:

$\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1em} \dfrac{GmM}{r^2} &= \dfrac{mv^2}{r}\\
\hspace{1em}\\
\dfrac{GM}{r^2} &= \dfrac{v^2}{r}\\
\hspace{1em}\\
GM\dfrac{1}{r} &= v^2\\
\end{aligned}$

Note: this formula is in your formula booklet ($v_{orbital}=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$) under 'additional higher level'.

Where $G$ and $M$ are constant so $v^2 \propto \dfrac{1}{r}$

$\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1em} (v_A)^2 r &= (v_B)^2 (4r)\\
\hspace{1em}\\
\dfrac{(v_A)^2}{4} &= (v_B)^2\\
\hspace{1em}\\
v_B &= \dfrac{v_A}{2}\\
\end{aligned}$

For $\omega$ we can use

- $\omega_B=\dfrac{v_B}{r_B}=\dfrac{v_A}{2(4r_A)}=\dfrac{v_A}{8r_A}$
