# PH0837

Planet X and planet Y orbit the same star. Planet X has three times the orbital period and three times the mass of Planet Y.

What is $\dfrac{\text{Orbital radius of planet X}}{\text{Orbital radius of planet Y}}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{27}}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{9}}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\sqrt[3]{9}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\sqrt{27}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Kepler's third law tells us that $T^2  \propto r^3$.

So $\dfrac{T^2}{r^3}=$ constant

Where $T_x$ and $T_y$ are the orbital periods of planet X and Y respectively and $r_x$ and $r_y$ are the orbital radii of planet X and planet Y respectively.

We want $\dfrac{r_x}{r_y}$.

- $\dfrac{T_x^2}{r_x^3}=\dfrac{T_y^2}{r_y^3}$

- $\dfrac{r_x^3}{r_y^3}=\dfrac{T_x^2}{T_y^2}$

- $\dfrac{r_x}{r_y} = \sqrt[3]{\Bigl(\dfrac{T_x}{T_y}\Bigr)^2}=\sqrt[3]{\Bigl(\dfrac{3T_y}{T_y}\Bigr)^2}=\sqrt[3]{9}$

Note that the orbital period of a planet is independent of the planet's mass.
