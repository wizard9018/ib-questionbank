# PH0502

**Maximum mark: 15**

1. A space probe lands on the surface of a planet which has the same radius as the Earth, and a gravitational field strength of 6.76 N kg$^{-1}$ at its surface.

    1. The mass of Earth is given as $5.98 \times 10^{24}\ $ kg. Show that the planet's mass is approximately $4 \times10^{24} \ $kg. [2 marks]

    2. The average radius of the planet is $6.37 \times 10^3 \ $km. Calculate the planet\'s average density in SI base units. [2 marks]

    3.  The planet is at a distance of $1.43 \times 10^{11} \, $m from the center of the Earth. Calculate the gravitational force between the Earth and the planet. [2 marks]

2.
    1. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant gravitational field strength between the planet and the Earth at point $X$, which is located $\frac{1}{3}$ of the total distance between the centers of the planets when measured from the Earth. [3 marks]

    ![PH0502](images/PH0502.png)

    2. Suggest a reason why your answer above is only an estimate. [1 mark]

3.
    1. The planet has a moon with an orbital radius of $1.20 \times 10^7 \, $m. Show that the acceleration of this moon is approximately $1.9 \ $m s$^{-2}$.   [1 mark]

    2. Calculate the angular speed of this moon. [1 mark]

    3. Determine the orbital period of this moon, in hours. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. From  $g=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$  , $\hspace{1em}$   $g\propto M$

        therefore $\dfrac{m_{planet}}{m_{earth}} = \dfrac{6.76}{9.81}$

        Rearranging gives $m_{planet} = 4.12\times10^{24} \ \textrm{kg}$ $\checkmark$

        (an extra significant figure from the calculation must be shown here)

         $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
         Showing more detail,

         $\dfrac{Gm_{earth}}{r_{earth}^2}=6.76$ and $\dfrac{Gm_{planet}}{r_{planet}^2}=9.81$ , where $r_{planet}=r_{earth}$

         $\dfrac{\frac{Gm_{planet}}{r_{planet}^2}}{\frac{Gm_{earth}}{r_{earth}^2}} = \dfrac{6.76}{9.81}$

         after cancellations, this gives

         $\dfrac{m_{planet}}{m_{earth}} = \dfrac{6.76}{9.81}$

         and

         $m_{planet} = 4.12\times10^{24} \ \textrm{kg}$

    2. Volume = $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi{(6.37 \times {10}^6)}^3 = 1.08 \times {10}^{21} \, \ce{m}^3$ $\checkmark$

        Density = $\dfrac{mass}{volume}$ = $\dfrac{4.12 \ \times \ {10}^{24}}{1.08 \ \times \ {10}^{21}} = 3.81 \times 10^3\  \textrm{kg}\ \textrm{m}^{-3}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The radius given in $km$ must be converted to $m$.
        $6.37 \times {10}^3\,\textrm{km} = 6.37 \times {10}^6\,\textrm{m}$

    3. :br
    $\begin{aligned}
    \textrm{Use of }F&=\frac{GMm}{r^2}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &=\frac{(6.67 \ \times \ {10}^{-11}) (5.98 \ \times \ {10}^{24}) (4.12 \ \times \ {10}^{24})}{{(1.43 \ \times \ {10}^{11})}^2}  \checkmark\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &= 8.04 \times 10^{16}\, \textrm{N}\,\checkmark\\
    \end{aligned}$

2.
    1. :br
    $\begin{aligned}
    g_{Earth}&=\frac{GM}{r^2}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &=\frac{(6.67 \ \times \ {10}^{-11}) (5.98 \ \times \ {10}^{24})} {(\frac{1.43\times{10}^{11}}{3})^2}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &=175.5 \ \times \ {10}^{-9}\ \textrm{N\ {kg}}^{-1}\checkmark\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    g_{planet}&=\frac{GM}{r^2}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &=\frac{(6.67 \ \times \ {10}^{-11}) (4.12 \ \times \ {10}^{24})}{{(\left(1.43 \ \times \ {10}^{11}\right)(\frac{2}{3}))}^2}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &=30.2 \times {\ 10}^{-9}\ \textrm{N\ {kg}}^{-1}\checkmark\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    \end{aligned}$

Resultant $g = (175.5 \times {10}^{-9})-(30.2 \times {10}^{-9})= 1.45 \times {\ 10}^{-7}\ \textrm{N\ {kg}}^{-1}$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Each calculation should be shown for full marks. Since gravitational field strength is a vector quantity, the two vectors have opposite directions and the numbers need to be subtracted.

    2. Any of the following:     $\checkmark$

    - Assumed that there is no interference from other stars or planets
    - Planets are treated as point masses/all planet mass is assumed to be located at the center
    - Planets are assumed to be spherically symmetric

3.
    1. Equating $F= ma$ and   $\,F=\dfrac{GMm}{r^2}$ giving

        $a=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}=\dfrac{(6.67 \ \times \ {10}^{-11})\ (4.12\ \times \ {10}^{24})}{\left(1.20 \, \times \, {10}^7\right)^2}=1.908\ \textrm{m s}^{-2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Since the answer is already given, the correct substitution must be shown, or answer to at least 3 significant figures. Use of $4 \times 10^{24}$ gives 1.85 m s$^{-2}$ (also accepted).

    2. Use of $a=\omega^2r$

        $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{1.908}{1.20 \ \times \ {10}^{7\ \ }}}= 3.99 \ \times \ {10}^{-4}\ \textrm{rad\ s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    3. $T=\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$ $\checkmark$

        $T=\dfrac{2\pi}{3.99 \ \times \ {10}^{-4}}=1.579 \times {10}^4 \ \textrm{s}$ $\checkmark$

        Converting to h: $\dfrac{15747}{3600}= 4.4 \ \textrm{h}$ $\checkmark$
