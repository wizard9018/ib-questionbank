# PH1846

**Maximum mark: 5**

1. Europa is one of the moons orbiting Jupiter.

    The following data are given:

    - Mass of Europa = 4.3 $\times$ 10$^{24}$ kg
    - Mass of Jupiter = 2.0 $\times$ 10$^{27}$ kg
    - Orbital radius of Europa = 680 $\times$ 10$^3$ km

    Calculate the centripetal force acting on Europa. [2 marks]

2. 1. Point $P$ is between Jupiter and Europa. The distance between point $P$ and Jupiter is $x$.

        ![sScreenshot 2025-09-05 at 9.46.07 AM](<images/sScreenshot 2025-09-05 at 9.46.07 AM.png>)

        Sketch on the diagram **two** arrows to show the directions of gravitational fields due to Jupiter and Europa at point $P$. [1 mark]

    2. Calculate the distance $x$ at which the net gravitational field strength is zero. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $F=6.67\times10^{-11}\dfrac{(2.0\times10^{27})(4.3\times10^{24})}{(680\times10^6)^2}$ $\checkmark$

    - $F=$ :answer[$1.2\times10^{24}$ $\ll\textrm{N}\gg$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The centripetal force is the gravitational force between Jupiter and Europa is provided by the gravitational force. This can be found from the formula

    - $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

    For the radius values, we must convert to metres, which gives

    - $r=680\times10^6$ m

    Solving for gravitational force gives

    - $F=6.67\times10^{-11}\dfrac{(2.0\times10^{27})(4.3\times10^{24})}{(680\times10^6)^2}=1.2\times10^{24}$ N

2. 1. An arrow to the left **AND** an arrow to the right $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The gravitational field strength of Jupiter is directed towards Jupiter and that of Europa is towards Europa.

        ![ph1846abi](images/ph1846abi.png)

    2. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $\dfrac{2.0\times10^{27}}{x^2}=\dfrac{4.3\times10^{24}}{(680\times10^6-x)^2}$ $\checkmark$

        - $x=$ :answer[$6.5\times10^8$ $\ll\textrm{m}\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The net gravitational field strength is zero, then the two gravitational fields are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction at this point.

        - $g_J=g_E$

        - $G\dfrac{M_J}{(r_J)^2}=G\dfrac{M_E}{(r_E)^2}$

        The constant $G$ cancels here. Substituting the values, and recognising that if the distance to Jupiter is $x$ then the distance to Europa is $680\times10^6-x$ gives

        - $\dfrac{2.0\times10^{27}}{x^2}=\dfrac{4.3\times10^{24}}{(680\times10^6-x)^2}$

        - $x=6.5\times10^8$ m
