# PH0841

A planet orbits a star of mass $M$ with an orbital radius $r$. During a time $t$, an area $A$ is swept out by a line segment joining the planet and the star as shown in the diagram below.

![image](images/image.png)

Which of the following is a correct expression for the area $A$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{t}{4}\sqrt{GMr}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{t}{2}\sqrt{GMr}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{t}{4\pi}\sqrt{GMr}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{t}{2\pi}\sqrt{GMr}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Area of entire circular orbit $=\pi r^2$.

The velocity of the planet is constant so in time $t$ an area of $\dfrac{t}{T} \pi r^2$ is swept out. Where $T$ is the orbital period of the planet.

We wish to eliminate $T$ so let us write $r$ in terms of $T$.

Gravitational force is equal to centripetal force so

- $\dfrac{GmM}{r^2}=m\omega^2 r$

Where $m$ is the mass of the planet.

Cancelling $m$'s gives:

- $\dfrac{GM}{r^2}=\omega^2 r$

$\omega$ can be written in terms of the time period $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, substituting this into the equation above gives:

- $\dfrac{GM}{r^2}=\Bigl( \dfrac{2\pi}{T} \Bigr)^2 r$

- $\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r^3}}=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$

- $T=2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}$

Now substituting this into $A = \dfrac{t}{T} \pi r^2$ gives:

- $Area = \dfrac{t}{2\pi \sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}}\pi r^2 = \dfrac{t}{2} r^2 \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r^3}} = \dfrac{t}{2} \sqrt{\dfrac{GMr^4}{r^3}} = \dfrac{t}{2} \sqrt{GMr}$
