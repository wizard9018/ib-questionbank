# PH0501

1. Define gravitational field strength. [2 marks]

2. The Earth\'s average radius is given as $6.37 \times 10^3$ km. Calculate the height above the Earth\'s surface at which gravitational field strength becomes half of its surface value. [3 marks]

3. Calculate the gravitational force acting on a satellite of mass $2.63 \times 10^6$ kg, placed at the height in part (b). State your answer to the appropriate number of significant figures. [3 marks]

4. Using the available information, calculate the mass of Earth. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Gravitational force $\underline{\textrm{per unit mass}} \,\checkmark$ \
On a small/test mass placed in the field. $\checkmark$

    Do not accept “force acting on a mass.”

2. $\dfrac{g}{2}=\dfrac{GM}{{(R+h)}^2}$ ($g$ at height $h$) $\bf{and}$ $\dfrac{g}{2}\,=\dfrac{GM}{{2R}^2}$ (half of $g$ at surface) $\checkmark$

    Equating the two expressions:

    - $\left(\dfrac{GM}{2R^2}=\dfrac{GM}{{(R+h)}^2}\right)$

    - Therefore ${2R}^2={(R+h)}^2$ $\checkmark$

       Solving gives $h = 2.64 \times 10^3$ km $\checkmark$

       The first two marking points are awarded if the expression is given correctly as stated in marking point 2.
Error carried forward allowed for marking point 3.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The gravitational field strength is calculated by the formula:

- $g=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$

    where $M$ is the mass of Earth, and $r$ is the distance from the center of Earth.

    At its surface, $r$ is equal to the radius of Earth, $R = 6.37 \times 10^3$ km.

    At height $h$ above the surface, the total distance from the center of Earth becomes $R + h$.

    Since the question asks for the height where the gravitational field strength becomes $\dfrac{g}{2}$, the expression becomes $\dfrac{g}{2}=\dfrac{GM}{2R^2}$

    Equating this to the expression for the new field strength in terms of $h$ gives the answer as in the markscheme above.

    $\left(\dfrac{GM}{2R^2}=\dfrac{GM}{{(R+h)}^2}\right)$

    Substituting all values and solving for h

    gives $h = 2.64 \times 10^3$ km

3. Gravitational field strength is $\dfrac{g}{2} = \dfrac{9.81}{2} = 4.905$ N $\ce{kg^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

    $F = mg = (2.63 \times 10^6)(4.905) = 12.9 \times 10^6$ N $\checkmark$

    Answer to 3 significant figures $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The two values used for this calculation, $9.81\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-2}$ and $2.63 \times 10^6\, \ce{kg}$ are both to three significant figures, so there should be three significant figures on the answer.
