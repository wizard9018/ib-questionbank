# PH0359

The centers of three masses lie on the same line. The centers of the masses are separated as shown.

![PH0359xxx](images/PH0359xxx.png)

The net gravitational force on m$_2$ is zero when the value of $d$ is one third of $x$. What is the ratio $\dfrac{m_1}{m_3}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{4}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2}{3}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 1

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Since the net gravitational force is zero,

The gravitational force between $m_1$ and $m_2$ = The gravitational force between $m_3$ and $m_2$.

$\hspace{1em}$

$\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2cm}\frac{Gm_1m_2}{d^2} &=\frac{Gm_2m_3}{\left(x-d\right)^2}  \\
\hspace{1em} \\
\left(\frac{m_1}{d^2}\right)&=\frac{m_3}{\left(x-d\right)^2} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
\left(\frac{m_1}{m_3}\right)&=\frac{d^2}{\left(x-d\right)^2} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
&=\ \frac{{(\frac{x}{3})}^2}{{(x-\frac{x}{3})}^2} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
&=\frac{{\frac{x}{9}}^2}{{\frac{4x}{9}}^2} \\
\hspace{1em}\\
&=\frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}$
