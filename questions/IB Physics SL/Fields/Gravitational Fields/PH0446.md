# PH0446

Two stars of equal mass $M$ are shown in the diagram, distance $d$ apart. A planet is equidistant from the two stars, with distance $\dfrac{d}{2}$ across the midpoint of the line joining the stars as shown.

![PH0446](images/PH0446.png)

What is the resultant gravitational field strength at the position of the planet due to the two stars?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $2\dfrac{GM}{d^2}\cos(45\degree)$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $4\dfrac{GM}{d^2}\cos(45\degree)$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $2\dfrac{GM}{d^2}\tan(45\degree)$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $4\dfrac{GM}{d^2}\tan(45\degree)$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The distance from each star to the planet is $\dfrac{d}{2}\sqrt2$

The gravitational field strength of one star at the location of the planet is given by
$\dfrac{GM}{{(\frac{d}{2}\sqrt2)}^2}=2\dfrac{GM}{d^2}$

Since the stars are identical and distances are equal, the resultant gravitational field strength will be the sum of the components directed towards the midpoint of the two stars as shown in red.

![PH0446_MS](images/PH0446_MS.png)

Since the angle is 45$\degree$, the component of each field vector is
 $2\dfrac{GM}{d^2}\cos(45\degree)$

Therefore, the sum of the two vectors is $4\dfrac{GM}{d^2}\cos(45\degree)$
