# PH0442

Planet $A$ has a gravitational field strength $g$ at its surface. Planet $B$ has twice the density and twice the radius of planet $A$.

What is the gravitational field strength at the surface of $B$?

- A. $\hspace{1em} g$

- B. $\hspace{1em} 2g$
- C. $\hspace{1em} 4g$
- D. $\hspace{1em} 8g$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Gravitational field strength is given by $g=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$, where $M$ is the mass of the planet and $r$ is the radius.

The mass of planet $B$ is given by $mass=density \times  volume$.

The volume of a sphere is given by $\dfrac{4\pi r^3}{3}$, hence proportional to the cube of its radius, giving Planet B 8 times the volume of Planet A.

This gives $M_B= (2\rho)(8V_A) = 16\rho V_A$.

If the mass of planet A is given by $\rho V_A$,

- $M_B= 16M_A$

Substituting values into the formula,

- $\begin{aligned}
g_B &= \frac{GM_B}{r_B^2} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
&=\frac{G(16M_A)}{{(2r_A)}^2}\\
\hspace{1em} \\
&=\frac{G(16M_A)}{{4r_A}^2} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
&=4\frac{GM_A}{{r_A}^2} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
&=4g\\
\end{aligned}$
