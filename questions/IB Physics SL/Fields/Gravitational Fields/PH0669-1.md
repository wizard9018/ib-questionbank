# PH0669-1

**Maximum mark: 8**

Geostationary satellites are satellites that complete one circular orbit in the same amount of time it takes for the Earth to rotate once on its axis. A geostationary satellite is placed in an orbit at $3.6\times{10}^7$ m above the Earth\'s surface, at the equator.  The radius of the Earth is $6.4\times{10}^3$ km.

![PH0669aabb](images/PH0669aabb.png)

The satellite undergoes uniform circular motion.

1. Define gravitational field strength. [2 marks]

2. Show that the linear speed of the satellite is approximately $3.1\times{10}^3\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$. [3 marks]

3. Calculate the gravitational field strength at the point in the orbit of the satellite. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Force per unit mass $\checkmark$

    experienced by a small point mass at a location. $\checkmark$

2. ***convert km to m to give the radius of Earth to be $\bold{6.4 \times 10^6 \ \ce{m}}$***

    $\hspace{1em}$ $d=2\pi r=\left(2\right)\pi\left(3.6\times{10}^7+6.4\times{10}^6\right)$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t=\left(24\right)\left(60\right)\left(60\right)=8.6\times{10}^4$ s $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{d}{t}=\dfrac{\left(2\right)\pi\left(3.6\times{10}^7\ +6.4\times{10}^6\right)\ }{8.6\times{10}^4}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ = :answer[$3.10\times{10}^3\ \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$] $\checkmark$

    (*extra significant figures or full substitution must be shown to receive 3rd mark*)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The radius $r$ of the orbit from the centre of the Earth (converting km to m):

    $\hspace{1em}$ $r=3.6\times{10}^7\ \ce{m}+6.4\times{10}^6$ m

    The distance $d$ travelled by the satellite in one revolution:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $d=2\pi r=\left(2\right)\pi\left(3.6\times{10}^7\ +6.4\times{10}^6\right)$ m

    The time $t$ of one revolution:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t=\left(24\right)\left(60\right)\left(60\right)=8.6\times{10}^4$ s

    The linear speed $v$ of the satellite:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{d}{t}=\dfrac{\left(2\right)\pi\left(3.6\times{10}^7\ +6.4\times{10}^6\right)\ }{8.6\times{10}^4}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $=3.10\times{10}^3\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

3. Any evidence linking gravitational field strength and the centripetal acceleration $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{v^2}{r}=\dfrac{{(3.1\times{10}^3)}^2}{(3.6\times{10}^7\ +6.4\times{10}^6)}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ :answer[$2.3\times{10}^{-1}\, \ce{m\,s^{-2}}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The force $F$ acting on the satellite at that point should be the centripetal force that causes the satellite to orbit the Earth:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $F=mg=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

    Where $m$ is the mass of the satellite, $v$ is the linear speed of the satellite, and $r$ is the separation between the centre of the Earth and the satellite.

    $\hspace{1em}$ $g=\dfrac{v^2}{r}=\dfrac{{(2.6\times{10}^3)}^2}{(3.6\times{10}^7\ +6.4\times{10}^6)}=2.3\times{10}^{-1}\, \ce{m\,s^{-2}}$
