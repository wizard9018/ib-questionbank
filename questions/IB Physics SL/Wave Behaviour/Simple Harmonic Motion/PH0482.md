**Maximum mark: 11**

A  2.0 kg object is attached to a spring and vibrates vertically.

1. 

    1. State and explain whether or not the oscillation of the mass is simple harmonic motion. [3 marks]

    2. On Figure 1 above draw an arrow to show the direction of the acceleration of the mass which is displaced by $x$. [1 mark]

2. 

    1. Show that the slope of the graph equals to $\dfrac{-k}{m}$, where $k$ is the spring constant. [3 marks]

    2. Use the graph to determine the spring constant, $k$, of the spring. [2 marks]

3. For the same spring-mass system, show that the acceleration of the object is $-10\,\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-2}$ when the mass is displaced by 0.12 cm [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 

    1. It is doing SHM; $\checkmark$

        The graph shows the relationship $a \propto -x$, acceleration is proportional to negative  displacement $\checkmark$ 

        Graph is a straight line through the origin with a negative slope. $\checkmark$

    2. An arrow directing upward. $\checkmark$

        ![PH0482-2](images/PH0482-2.png)

2. 

    1.  $\ll$Hooke's law $\gg$ $F=-kx$ 

        **AND**

        $\ll$Newton's second law  $\gg$ $F=ma$  $\checkmark$

        $-kx=ma$ $\checkmark$

        slope of the graph = $\dfrac{a}{x}=-\dfrac{k}{m}$ $\checkmark$

    2. Any data on the line chosen to calculate $k$ where,  

        $k= -\dfrac{ma}{x}$ where mass, $m = 2\,\ce{kg}$

        For example 

        $a = 5\,\ce{s}^{-2}$ where $x = -0.06\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

        $k$ = :answer[$167 \ll N\,m^{-1},\,\ce{kg}\,\ce{s}^{-2}\gg$] $\checkmark$

        (accept from 165 to 170 if slope is calculated in an appropriate way)

3. Using the spring constant from b(ii) and converting its units to N cm$^{-1}$

    $\begin{aligned}

    -kx &= ma\\

    -(1.66)(0.12) &= (2) a\, \checkmark\\

    a&= -0.10\ \textrm{m s}^{-2} \approx - 10\,\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-2} \,\checkmark\\

    \end{aligned}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanaation}}$

    Our spring constant of 167 N m$^{-1}$ can be converted to 1.67 N cm$^{-1}$.

    The force from the spring $kx$ is therefore

    - $(1.66)(.12)=0.20\ $N

    We can now find acceleration

    - $a=\dfrac{F}{m}$ =$\dfrac{0.20}{2}$= 0.10 m s$^{-2} =$ 10 cm s$^{-2}$
