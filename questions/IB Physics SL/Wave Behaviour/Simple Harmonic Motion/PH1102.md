## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Motion where:

    The restoring force/acceleration is $\ll$directly$\gg$ $\underline{\textrm{proportional}}$ to the displacement from the equilibrium position;  $\checkmark$\
    The restoring force/acceleration is in the $\underline{\textrm{opposite}}$ direction to the displacement towards the equilibrium position;  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is periodic motion where the restoring force is proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position.  Since the restoring force is also proportional to the acceleration (Newton’s Second Law), it follows that the acceleration is also proportional to the displacement.  Furthermore, because the force is acting to restore equilibrium, it is in the opposite direction to the displacement from equilibrium.

    In equation form, SHM is represented as:

    - $a = -{\omega}^2x$

    Where,

    - $a$ = linear acceleration ($\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-2}$)
    - $\omega$ = angular velocity (rad$\,\ce{s}^{-1}$)
    - $x$ = linear displacement (m)

    The restoring force/acceleration is zero at the equilibrium, and is maximum when the displacement is at the maximum amplitude of the swing.

    For a pendulum, proportionality only applies for small displacement angles where $\sin{\theta}$ is approximately equal to $\theta$ radian (small angle approximation, up to $15\degree$).

2. 1. $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{12.0}{9.81}}$;  $\checkmark$

        $T = 6.95\,\ce{s}$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The wrecking ball is acting as a pendulum.

        The period of oscillation of a pendulum is given by,

        - $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$

        Substituting in values gives,

        - $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{12.0}{9.81}}$

        - $T=6.95\, \ce{s}$

        Note that the period of a pendulum is independent of mass.

        At least 2 significant figures must be shown on the calculated answer.

    2. Sinusoidal shape on positive vertical axis, with $E_p$ = maximum at $t=0$; $\checkmark$

        Maxima at $t=0$, $t=\dfrac{T}{2}$ and $T$;  $\checkmark$

        Note: Ignore any attempt to show damping.

        ![PH1102ciims](images/PH1102ciims.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Kinetic energy is given by,

        - $E_k= \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

        In a system undergoing simple harmonic motion the size of the velocity is sinusoidal with respect to time, with minima at the endpoints and a maximum at equilibrium.  Kinetic energy is therefore sinusoidal.  Because kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy are out of phase during oscillations, this means that gravitational potential energy is also sinusoidal.

        At $t=0$, the wrecking ball is released.  This is the point of maximum displacement where kinetic energy is zero and the gravitational potential energy is at a maximum.  Therefore the shape of the graph for gravitational potential energy is that of cosine.

        Graphically,
        - the motion is sinusoidal
        - is a maximum when $t=0$
        - is always positive (since proportional to the square of the velocity)
        - there are two energy cycles with each period of oscillation

        Maximum gravitational potential energy occurs at each maximum displacement.  This is also when kinetic energy is zero and note that the total energy remains the same throughout, since the effect of friction is ignored.

    3. Amplitude/Total Energy decreases;  $\checkmark$

        Period remains constant OR increase in period;  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        When friction acts on a system, energy is lost from the system as heat.  This means that the wrecking ball “system” would lose total energy if friction was experienced.

        The loss of total energy would show a decrease in $E_p$ as a decreased amplitude of the periodic motion.  Also, the maximum velocity through the equilibrium position would decrease since this is related to the decrease in $E_k$.

        In the absence of damping, the period of the motion is given by,

        - $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$

        and neither $L$ or $g$ change, the period would therefore remain constant. With light damping, the period would increase because the friction slows the motion of the pendulum. This is sometimes considered to be negligible.
