# PH0173

A simple pendulum undergoes simple harmonic motion. The graph shows the variation of a quantity $y$ with horizontal displacement $x$ from the equilibrium position.

![Screenshot 2025-04-02 at 11.00.45 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-02 at 11.00.45 AM.JPEG>)

 Which of the following quantities could be represented by quantity $y$?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The velocity of the pendulum |
  | II. | The kinetic energy of the pendulum |
  | III. | The potential energy of the pendulum |

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The y axis cannot represent the velocity of the pendulum, as a velocity-position graph would also show negative velocities to represent motion in both directions. In addition, the velocity-displacement graph for an object undergoing simple harmonic motion is elliptical in shape, and this graph is parabolic.

Using the formula for a spring (which is an example of a simple harmonic oscillator), we know that potential energy is equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$. A similar quadratic relationship between potential energy and displacement is true for a pendulum, so the potential energy will be a parabola, opening upwards.

Total energy must be constant, so the kinetic energy vs. position graph will be given by the total energy minus the potential energy, which will be a parabola opening downwards, which is what we see in the graph.

Using a Higher Level formula, the formula for kinetic energy is given by $\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(x_0^2-x^2)$, which would be parabolic in shape, opening downwards as is shown in the graph.

The potential energy of the bob should be less at the equilibrium position than in the positions where displacement is maximum.
