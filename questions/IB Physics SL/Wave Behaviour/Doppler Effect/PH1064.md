## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The Doppler Effect   $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Relative motion between an observer and a source of waves results in the Doppler Effect where waves are observed to have a different frequency (and wavelength) than those emitted.

2. 1. :::tableoptions{}
        | | $\hspace{1em}$Increased$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$Decreased$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$Constant$\hspace{1em}$ |
        | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
        | $\hspace{1em}$Frequency$\hspace{1em}$ | $\checkmark$ |  |  |
        | $\hspace{1em}$Wavelength$\hspace{1em}$ |  | $\checkmark$ |  |
        | $\hspace{1em}$Wave Speed$\hspace{1em}$ |  |  | $\checkmark$ |

        Frequency increases\
        AND\
        Wavelength decreases   $\checkmark$

        Wave speed remains constant   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        When a source of waves is moving towards an observer, the distance between wavefronts in front of the source decreases - the wavelength decreases and frequency increases.  As the car approaches, the spectator hears the sound in front of the car with the higher frequency.

        The opposite is true when a source of waves moves away from an observer.  In this case, the spectator will hear a sound with a lower frequency than is emitted by the car since the distance between wavefronts has increased.

        The relative motion between the source and the observer does not change the speed at which the wave moves through the medium.  The sound wave will still travel at the speed of sound in air.

    2. Circular wavefronts.    $\checkmark$ \
        At least three clearly shown wavefronts that are closer together in front of the car AND further apart behind the car.   $\checkmark$

        ![image](images/image.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        When a wave is emitted from a source, it will produce circular wavefronts, wavelength $\lambda$, that travel at a constant speed from a fixed point of origin.  If a wave source is moving, then successive wavefronts have a different point of origin in the direction of travel of the source.  This means that the distance between wavefronts ahead of the source is less than $\lambda$, and the distance between the wavefronts behind the source is greater than $\lambda$.  Note that, since the source is moving at constant speed, the distance between wavefronts remains constant whether ahead or behind the source.

    3. No change.   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Since the driver is in the car and moving at the same speed as the car, there is no relative motion between them.  The lack of relative motion means that the Doppler Effect will not be experienced by the driver and no change in frequency will be heard.

3. Away from the track, the change in frequency will not be as sudden. OWTTE   $\checkmark$ \
    The maximum and minimum frequencies of the car engine sound will be the same in each position.   $\checkmark$

    ![image](images/image.png)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The Doppler Effect relates to the line-of-sight (radial) velocity of a moving source or observer.

    When an observer is close to the path of a moving source, the radial velocity is approximately constant and changes direction suddenly when the observer is passed by the source.  Therefore the change in frequency is abrupt.

    When an observer is further away from the path, the radial velocity gradually decreases, is zero when the source passes, and gradually increases as the source moves away.  This means that the frequency change is less abrupt.
