# PH1900

The diagram below shows a source of sound $S$ moving to the right at constant speed, emitting wavefronts at regular intervals.

![ph1900q](images/ph1900q.png)
$\small{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra, - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

Two observers, $A$ and $B$, are at rest in the medium and placed behind and in front of the source respectively.

Consider the following statements:

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | Observer $B$ hears a lower frequency than the one emitted by the source |
  | II. | The speed at which wavefronts will reach $A$ is the same as the speed at which wavefronts will reach $B$. |
  | III. | The source is moving at a speed which is lower than the speed of sound. |

Which of the following are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

I. Incorrect. The source is moving towards $B$ which means that this observer will hear a higher frequency than the one emitted by the source.

II. Correct. The speed at which wavefronts will reach both $A$ and $B$ is determined by the medium and in this case corresponds to the speed of sound.

III. Correct. Since wavefronts can form and exist ahead of the source, the source's speed must be less than the speed of sound in the medium. The Doppler Effect (compression in front, stretching behind) is visible, but not the shockwave pattern of supersonic motion.
