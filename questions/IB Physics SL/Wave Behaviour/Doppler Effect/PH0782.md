# PH0782

**Maximum mark: 6**

1. A space probe approaching the Earth communicates with the control center on Earth via radio waves. The speed of the space probe is 24 $\ce{km}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$ and the frequency of the radio wave it emits is 150 MHz.

    Calculate, in meters, the wavelength of the radio wave sent from the space probe. [2 marks]

2. On the following diagram, the wavefronts of the emitted signal from the probe when it is stationary are shown.

    ![PH0782b](images/PH0782b.png)

    On the diagram above, sketch the wavefronts of the emitted signal by the probe when it is moving in the direction shown.      [2 marks]

3. Determine the difference between the frequency observed at the command centre and the frequency radiated from the source.  [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\dfrac {3.0 \times 10{^8}}{150\times 10{^6}}$ $\checkmark$

    :answer[$2.0\,\ce{m}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    By using the formula of wave speed:

    - $v = \lambda f$

    After rearranging the formula:

    - $\lambda = \dfrac {v}{f} = \dfrac {3.0 \times 10{^8}}{150 \times 10{^6}}$ = 2.0 m

2. Non concentric circular wavefronts $\checkmark$

    Wavefronts closer together on the right and further apart to the left $\checkmark$

    ![PH0782Ansb](images/PH0782Ansb.png)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Due to the motion of the source towards the right, wavefronts will be closer on the right and smaller on the left. After each wavefront is created, the source moves a little to the right, so that next wavefront created is centered around a different location.

3. $(150 \times 10{^6})\dfrac {24 \times 10{^3}}{3.0 \times 10{^8}}$ $\checkmark$

    :answer[$1.2 \times 10{^4}\,\ce{Hz}$]  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    By using the formula of the Doppler effect:

    - $\dfrac {\Delta f}{f} = \dfrac {v}{c}$

    - $\Delta f = f\dfrac {v}{c} = (150 \times 10{^6})\dfrac {24 \times 10{^3}}{3.0 \times 10{^8}} = 1.2 \times 10{^4}$ Hz
