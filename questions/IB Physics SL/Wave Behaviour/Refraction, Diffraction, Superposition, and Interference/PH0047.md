# PH0047

Wavefronts travel across a boundary from medium 1 to medium 2 as shown.

![refraction](images/refraction.png)

Which of the following correctly describes the change, if any, in the speed, wavelength and frequency of the waves as they pass from across the boundary?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{2em}$ Speed $\hspace{2em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ Wavelength $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ Frequency $\hspace{1em}$ |

| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$ A. $\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increases | Increases | Unchanged |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$ B. $\hspace{1.5em}$ | Unchanged | Increases | Decreases |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$ C. $\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increases | Decreases | Increases |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$ D. $\hspace{1.5em}$ | Decreases | Decreases | Unchanged |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

Note that the question states that the waves travel from Medium 1 to Medium 2, so are travelling 'upwards' on the page.

![snell](images/snell.png)

It can be seen on the diagram that when the waves cross the boundary, the wavelength decreases. At a boundary, the frequency of a wave is unchanged because the number of waves entering the boundary every second must equal the number of waves that leave the boundary every second.

From the relationship $v = f\lambda$, if $f$ remains constant and $\lambda$ decreases, the $v$ must also decrease.

Also, if rays are added at $90^o$ to the wavefronts, it can be seen that the rays are bending towards the normal which indicates a decrease in speed as the waves cross the boundary.
