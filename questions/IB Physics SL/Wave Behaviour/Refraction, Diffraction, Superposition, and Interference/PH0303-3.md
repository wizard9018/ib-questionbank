# PH0303-3

**Maximum mark: 6**

A parallel beam of red light passes through two parallel slits with negligible width and an interference pattern is formed on a distant screen.

![PH0303](images/PH0303.png)

$\theta$ is the angle that shows the diffraction angle of the light waves reaching any point on the screen.

1. Explain why an interference pattern is observed on the screen.  [3 marks]

2. On the axes below, sketch a graph to show how the intensity I of light on the screen varies with angle $\theta$.    [2 marks]
    (There is no need to add values to the axes)

![PH0303-1](images/PH0303-1.png)

3. In the arrangement, the red light source is replaced with a blue light source with the same intensity. How would the drawn graph in (a) (ii) differ?          [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. diffraction at slits $\checkmark$

    phase/path difference $\checkmark$

    waves superpose/interfere constructively or destructively $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When light passes through a slit, it undergoes diffraction and is incident on the screen. Any point on the screen receives light from two slits and the waves superpose with each other.    There are some points in which maximum and minimum intensity are observed due to the path difference between two light waves emitted from two slits.

    It is important to explain the answer in the following order;

    - diffraction
    - path difference
    - constructive or destructive superposition/interference

2.  $\hspace{1em}$

    ![PH0303-3](images/PH0303-3.png)

    Same intensity for all maxima by eye $\checkmark$

    Same width for all maxima by eye $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The slits have negligible width. Therefore, the interference pattern is not modulated with a single slit diffraction pattern. Near the centre of a double slit interference pattern, the maxima are approximately all the same height.

3.  Narrower maxima/maxima closer together $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The equation for interference;

    - $n\lambda=dsin\theta$

    By rearranging;

    - $sin\theta=\dfrac{n\lambda}{d}$

    According to the equation, the angular separation of maxima is proportional to the wavelength of light. Since blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light, maxima will be narrower.
