# PH0373

For a wave traveling in a medium, the variation of the particles' displacement with distance travelled is shown in the following graphs. The graphs represent a wave, travelling to the right, at different moments in time.

Graph I shows the wave at $t = 0\,\ce{s}$, while graph II is at $t = 1\,\ce{s}$. 

The time difference between the two graphs is less than the wave's period.

What is the wave speed?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 4 $\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 2 $\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 1 $\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 16 $\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

To calculate the wave speed, we can use the wave equation: 

- $v=\lambda f$

Since 

- $f = \dfrac{1}{T} \rightarrow$

$v = \dfrac{\lambda}{T}$

The wavelength is determined from either displacement-distance graph:

 

- $\lambda=4\,\ce{cm}$

To calculate the period, we notice that the difference between the first and second graphs means that the wave has travelled one quarter of a wavelength. This means the period is 4 times the time given between the two displacement-distance graphs.

- $T=4(1\,\ce{s}) = 4\,\ce{s}$

Therefore,

- $v=\dfrac{\lambda}{T}=\dfrac{4}{4}=1\,\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

Looking at the two graphs, we can also see a crest that is moved 1 cm in 1 second, which gives a wave speed of $1\,\ce{cm}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$.
