## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. 8.5 million waves / oscillations / vibrations per second $\checkmark$

    2. time spent in the layer of fat = $(30.3\times10^{-6}) - (2.70\times10^{-6}) = 27.6\times10^{-6}$ $\checkmark$

        distance travelled in fat = $(1.5\times10^3)(27.6\times10^{-6})=4.1\times10^{-2}\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

        thickness = $\dfrac{4.1\times10^{-2}}{2}=2.1\times10^{-2}\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

        Accept the solutions using half of the time spent in the layer of fat leading to the same answer.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The wave first reflects off the boundary between the skin and the fat layer. Then it reflects off the boundary between the fat layer and the muscle. Therefore, the difference in detection times between the first and second waves corresponds to the time the $t$ wave spends travelling through the fat layer.

        - $t=(30.3\times10^{-6}) - (2.70\times10^{-6})=27.6\times10^{-6}$

        By rearranging the equation of speed, the distance d travelled in fat:

        - $d=vt$

        - $d=(1.5\times10^3)(27.6\times10^{-6})=4.1\times10^{-2}\,\ce{m}$

        Since the thickness of the layer should be half of the distance covered by wave inside the layer:

        - thickness = $\dfrac{4.1\times10^{-2}}{2}=2.1\times10^{-2}\, \ce{\mu} \ce{m}$

3. 1. Speed remains same $\checkmark$

        $\lambda=\dfrac{v}{f}$ where $f$ increases but $v$ is constant, therefore wavelength decreases $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The speed of a wave depends solely on the medium through which it travels. Since the medium is still fat, the speed of the wave remains constant.

    2. Wavelength decreases $\checkmark$

        $\lambda=\dfrac{v}{f}$ where $f$ increases but $v$ is constant $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        According to the wave equation:

        - $v=\lambda f$

        When the frequency $f$ is increased, the wavelength  must decrease if the wave speed remains constant. Therefore, increasing the frequency results in a shorter wavelength, enhancing the resolution of the monitoring device.
