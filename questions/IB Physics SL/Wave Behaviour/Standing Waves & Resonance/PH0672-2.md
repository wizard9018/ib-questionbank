    2. The speaker oscillates in the same direction that the wave energy is transferred  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
In longitudinal waves, the vibration of the particles is along the direction of motion of the wave/energy. The sound wave created by the speaker is travelling horizontally and the air particles are also oscillating horizontally. Therefore, the wave formed in the tube is longitudinal.

    3. $\lambda=4\left(0.45\right)=$ $1.8\, m$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f=\dfrac{v}{\lambda}=\dfrac{340}{1.8}$ = :answer[$189\ Hz$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
In a tube with one closed and one open end, the fundamental harmonic standing wave has a wavelength that is four times the length of the tube:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\lambda=4\left(0.45\right)=1.8\ m$

        By using the equation of the speed of the waves:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\lambda\ f$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f=\dfrac{v}{\lambda}=\dfrac{340}{1.8}=189\ Hz$

2. Sound can be louder or quieter at different locations $\checkmark$

    where sound waves interfere constructively or destructively $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
In regions of constructive interference, where the sound waves reinforce each other, the volume and intensity of the sound will be greater. However, in regions of destructive interference, where the sound waves cancel each other out, the volume will decrease, resulting in quieter areas or even dead spots where the sound may be barely audible or absent.
