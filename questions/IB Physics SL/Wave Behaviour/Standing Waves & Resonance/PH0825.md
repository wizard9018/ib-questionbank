1. A uniform string is set up to investigate standing waves as shown in the diagram below. A mass of 500 g is suspended on the end of the string.

2. The frequency of the vibration generator is gradually increased until the next standing wave is formed at 420 Hz.

    Explain why the lowest frequency of oscillation that will produce a standing wave will be 70 Hz. [2 marks]

3. The speed of a wave in a string, $v$ is given by the following equation:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}}$

    1. Show that the displacement between A and B, $s=50\ \ce{cm}$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Wave (created by vibration generator at **A**) is reflected at **B**. Incident and reflected wave superpose/ interfere. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The outgoing waves from the vibration generator will reflect back at point **B**. The incident and reflected waves have the same amplitude, speed, and frequency so their superposition results in points where interference is always constructive (antinodes) and points where interference is always destructive (nodes).

2. Clear explanation that each harmonic frequency is a multiple of the first harmonic frequency. $\checkmark$

    Idea that difference between two successive harmonic frequencies is the first harmonic frequency so $f=70\ \ce{Hz}$\
*OR*\
$420-350=70\ \ce{Hz}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Each harmonic frequency is a multiple of the first harmonic frequency e.g.
$\text{\textquotedblleft}$For a string that is fixed at both ends, the $n^{th}$ harmonic frequency $f_n$ is given by $f_n = nf$ where $f$ is the frequency of the first harmonic.''

    Use of the fact that the difference between two successive harmonic frequencies is always equal to first harmonic frequency: $f_{n+1}-f_n=(n+1)f-(nf)=f$

    The question implies that 420 Hz and 350 Hz are successive harmonic frequencies so $f=420-350=70\ \ce{Hz}$

3. 1. $T=mg$\
        and\
        $v=f\lambda=f(2s)$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $2fs=\sqrt{\dfrac{Mgs}{m}}$ $\checkmark$

        Giving

        $\hspace{1em}$ $s=\dfrac{Mg}{4mf^2}=\dfrac{(0.5)(9.8)}{(4)(5 \times 10^{-4})(70)^2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $=(0.5\ \ce{m} =50\ \ce{cm})$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        We can use the equation given in the question to find an expression for $v$ in terms of $s$ and parameters given.

        Tension = weight of hanging mass $= Mg$ where $M$ is the hanging mass and $g$ is acceleration of free fall on Earth.

        Mass per unit length = $\dfrac{m}{s}$ where $m$ is the mass of the wire between **A** and **B**.

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{Mg}{\left (\dfrac{m}{s} \right )}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{Mgs}{m}}$

        We can use our knowledge of stationary waves to find another expression for $v$ in terms of $s$ (and the known parameter $f$).

        For the first harmonic $s=\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ so we can write

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=2sf$

        where $f$ is the frequency of the first harmonic.

        Equating our two expressions for $v$ gives:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\sqrt{\dfrac{Mgs}{m}} = 2sf$

        $\hspace{2em}$ $\dfrac{Mgs}{m} = 4s^2f^2$

        $\hspace{2.5em}$ $\dfrac{Mg}{m} = 4sf^2$

        $\hspace{4em}$ $s = \dfrac{Mg}{4mf^2}$

        Now substituting values:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $M = 0.5\ \ce{kg}$\
        $\hspace{1em}$ $g = 9.8\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$\
        $\hspace{1em}$ $m = 5 \times 10^{-4}\ \ce{kg}$\
        $\hspace{1em}$ $f = 420-350=70\ \ce{Hz}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $s = \dfrac{(0.5)(9.8)}{(4)(5 \times 10^{-4})(70)^2} = 0.5\ \ce{m} = 50\ \ce{cm}$

        Note that the same result can be obtained using different harmonic frequency and their corresponding wavelengths.

    2.  $v=f \lambda$, $\hspace{3em} \lambda = 2(0.5)=1\, \ce{m}$

        :answer[$v = 70\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        $v = 2sf = 2(0.5)(70) = 70\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

        Alternatively:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{Mgs}{m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{(0.5)(9.8)(0.5)}{(0.5 \times 10^{-3})}} = 70\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        **Q** is a node $\text{\textendash}$ the displacement of the string at **Q** is always zero, interference at point **Q** is always destructive.

    2. **P** and **R** oscillate with the same frequency/ Frequency of both **P** and **R** is $140\ \ce{Hz}$. $\checkmark$

        Amplitude of oscillation of **R** is greater than that of **P**. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Frequency of oscillation is the same at every point on the standing wave (apart from nodes where there is no oscillation).\
        Amplitude of oscillation on a standing wave is greatest at antinodes, amplitude decreases when moving from a node to an antinode. The amplitude at a node is zero (no oscillation).
