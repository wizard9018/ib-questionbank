# PH1954

A loudspeaker is placed near the open end of a pipe that is closed at the other end.

![Screenshot 2026-02-19 at 2.53.02 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-19 at 2.53.02 PM.png>)

As the frequency of the loudspeaker is increased, resonance is heard at two consecutive frequencies of
425 Hz and 595 Hz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m s$^{-1}$. What is the length of the pipe $L$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.5 m

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 1.0 m

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 1.5 m

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 2.0 m

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The pipe is closed at one end and open at the other. For resonance to occur there must be a node at one end and an antinode at the other.

The resonant frequencies are odd multiples of the first harmonic frequency,

- $f_1, 3f_1, 5f_1,$ etc

Which shows the difference between two consecutive harmonic frequencies to be $2f_1$

So, we have:

- $2f_1=595-425=170$ Hz

- $f_1=85$ Hz

Now the wavelength of the first harmonic is given by:

- $\lambda_1=\dfrac{v}{f_1}=\dfrac{340}{85}=4.0$ m

For the first harmonic the length of the pipe is one quarter of the wavelength:

- $l=\dfrac{\lambda_1}{4}=\dfrac{4.0}{4}=1.0$ m
