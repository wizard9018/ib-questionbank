# PH0402

A speaker produces a sound near a pipe that is closed at one end by a movable piston. The piston is moved in the shown direction until a sound is first heard from the pipe. At this time, the length of the part of the tube near the speaker is $L$.

![PH0402](images/PH0402.png)

$\small{\textrm{[Created with Chemix https://chemix.org/]}}$

What is the distance moved by the piston to hear the next resonant sound from the pipe?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{L}{2}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $L$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $2L$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $3L$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

For the first sound to come out of the pipe, a first harmonic is produced.

For the next sound to be produced, this means a third harmonic is produced. Remember that for a pipe opened from one end and closed from the other, odd harmonics only are formed.

The wavelength for a standing wave in a pipe closed from one end and opened from the other

- $\lambda=\dfrac{4L}{n}$

For the first harmonic, $n=1$

- $\lambda=4L$

- $L=\dfrac{\lambda}{4}$

For the third harmonic, $n=3$ 

- $\lambda=\dfrac{4L_2}{3}$

- $L_2=\dfrac{3\lambda}{4}=3L$

Where $L_2$ is the length of the pipe from the open end.

So the moved distance by the piston is $3L - L= 2L$
