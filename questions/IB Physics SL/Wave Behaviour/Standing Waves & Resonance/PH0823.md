# PH0823

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. A mass, $m$, at rest is hanging from a spring and the energy stored in the spring is $E_H$.

    ![image](images/image.png)

    Show that the spring constant, $k$, may be expressed as follows:

    $k=\dfrac{m^2 g^2}{2E_H}$

    Where $g$ is the acceleration of freefall on Earth's surface. [2 marks]

2. It is given that $m=50\, \ce{g}$ and $E_H=9.8\,\ce{m}\ce{J}$. Determine the natural frequency of oscillation of the system. [2 marks]

3. The mass-spring system is then suspended from a vibration generator that provides a driving force.

    ![image](images/image.png)

    The frequency of the driving force, $f$, is varied. For each value of the driving frequency a steady constant amplitude $A$ is reached by the mass-spring system. The oscillations of the mass spring system are lightly damped.

    On the axes below, sketch a graph to show the variation of $A$ with $f$. [2 marks]

    ![image](images/image.png)

4. Deduce the frequency at which the peak amplitude of oscillation would be achieved. Explain your answer. [2 marks]

5. The vibration generator is removed and the mass spring system is now submerged in a beaker of oil and as a result the oscillations are critically damped. Describe what is meant by $\lq$critically damped'. [2 marks]

1. ::indent
    - $E_H=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ and $F=(-)kx$ seen

    And

    - $F=(-)mg$  or $mg=kx$ seen.  $\checkmark$

    Substitution of $x=\dfrac{mg}{k}$ into $E_H=\dfrac{1}{2} kx^2$

    Or $x=\sqrt{\dfrac{2E_H}{k}}$ into $mg=kx$   $\checkmark$

    Resulting in

    - $k=\dfrac{m^2 g^2}{2E_H}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    - $E_H=\dfrac{1}{2} kx^2$ and $F=-kx$

    The elastic restoring force is equal and opposite to the weight hanging on the end of the spring so $F=-mg$

    Combining the equations $E_H=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ and $mg=kx$ gives:

    - $E_H=\dfrac{1}{2}k\Bigl(\dfrac{mg}{k}\Bigr)^2$

    - $E_H=\dfrac{m^2 g^2}{2k}$

    - $k=\dfrac{m^2 g^2}{2E_H}$

2. Frequency is given by $f=\dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$

    Or

    $k=12.25\,(\,\ce{N}\,\ce{m}^{-1})$   $\checkmark$

    $f=2.5\,\ce{Hz}$ (2sf)   $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Time period of mass spring system is given by:

    - $T=2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$

    And

    - $f=\dfrac{1}{T}$

    Therefore

    -  $f=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$

    Using the equation from part a:

    - $k=\dfrac{m^2 g^2}{2E_H}=\dfrac{(0.05)^2 (9.8)^2}{2(9.8\times 10^{-3})}=12.25\,\ce{N}\,\ce{m}^{-1}$

    Now substituting values into frequency equation:

    - $f=\dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{12.25}{0.05}}=2.49\, \ce{Hz}=2.5\, \ce{Hz}$ (2sf)

    Resonance occurs when the driving frequency is close to/equal to the natural frequency of the system (for a lightly damped system).  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Resonant frequency = natural frequency when there is no damping (not physically possible). As damping increases, resonant frequency will become smaller than the natural frequency but for light damping this difference is negligible.
