# PH0818

A standing wave of the third harmonic is formed inside a tube which is closed at both ends. The instantaneous displacements of points $P$ and $Q$ from the equilibrium position are represented in the diagram below.

![image](images/image.png)

Which row correctly identifies the phase difference between the oscillations of $P$ and $Q$ and the nature of the point $P$?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Phase difference / radians$\hspace{1.5em}$  | $\hspace{1.5em}$Nature of the point P$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ |node |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ | antinode |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\pi$ | node |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\pi$ | antinode |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Within an antinode section, all points oscillate in phase although with different amplitudes. Adjacent antinode sections oscillate opposite to one another, and so are $\pi$ radians or 180 degrees out of phase.

Because points P and Q are on adjacent antinode sections, the phase difference is $\pi$ radians.

$P$ is a point of maximum displacement so is a (displacement) antinode. In the context of sound waves maximum displacement refers to maximum longitudinal amplitude of vibration of air molecules.

Note: It is true that the points P and Q are separated by $\dfrac{3}{8}\lambda$ and in a normal progressive wave would have a phase difference of $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ in their oscillations. Because they are in adjacent antinodal sections, they are fully out of phase ($\pi$ radians).
