# PH0046

The string $X$ of length $L_X$ has two fixed ends. The string $Y$ of length $L_Y$ has one free end and one fixed end. Waves travel at the same speed in both strings.

The frequency of the third harmonic of both strings is the same.  What is $\dfrac{L_X}{L_Y}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 2

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4}{3}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3}{4}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

\

If frequencies and the speed of wave travel are the same, the wavelengths are also the same. 

For two fixed ends, the length of the third harmonic must be equal to $1.5\lambda$

![PH0046](images/PH0046.png)

With one free end, third harmonic corresponds to the frequency that is three times the first harmonic. The first harmonic frequency is given by 

- $f_1=\dfrac{v}{4L}$

The third harmonic frequency is given by 

- $f_3=\dfrac{3v}{4L}$

**note that when there is one free end and one fixed end, the convention is that there is no second harmonic frequency and the harmonic frequencies that are possible are first, third, fifth, etc. The number of the harmonic corresponds to the multiple of the fundamental frequency.

This leads to the equation 

- $\lambda=\dfrac{4L}{3}$

the length of the third harmonic must therefore be $0.75\lambda$ (as shown)

![PH0046-1](images/PH0046-1.png)

- $\dfrac{L_X}{L_Y}=\dfrac{1.5\lambda}{0.75\lambda}= 2$
