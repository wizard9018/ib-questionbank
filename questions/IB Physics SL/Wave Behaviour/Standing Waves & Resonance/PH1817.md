## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

A stationary wave on a string that is fixed at one end and free at the other will have a node (point of zero amplitude) at the fixed end and an antinode (point of maximum displacement) at the free end. The following patterns show different strings oscillating with these end conditions:

![Screenshot 2025-05-07 at 3.17.34 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-05-07 at 3.17.34 PM.JPEG>)

The top waveform shows a string oscillating at its first harmonic frequency.

The second diagram shows a string oscillating at three times its first harmonic frequency, this is referred to as the third harmonic frequency. This corresponds to option $A$ and is the correct answer.

The third diagram shows a string oscillating at its fifth harmonic frequency. Note that although it is the third lowest resonant frequency possible, it is still called the fifth harmonic because the frequency is five times the first harmonic frequency.

This arrangement is possible when the length of the string is $n\dfrac{\lambda}{4}$where $n=1$, $3$, $5...$. The third harmonic occurs when $l=3\dfrac{\lambda}{4}=\dfrac{3}{4}\lambda$.

In the image below, we see that $l=\dfrac{3}{4}\lambda$:
