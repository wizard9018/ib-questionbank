# PH1043-2

**Maximum mark: 5**

1. In an experiment, a loudspeaker is placed near a tube that is open at both ends. A sound of constant frequency is emitted from the loudspeaker and a standing wave of the first harmonic is formed inside the tube. The displacement of the particles from equilibrium at different locations along the tube is represented in the diagram below.

    ![PH2043a copy](<images/PH2043a copy.png>)

    Outline how the standing wave is formed inside the tube. [2 marks]

2. Particle $P$ has equilibrium position $\bold{P}$ labeled on the diagram.

    State whether the motion of paricle $P$ is parallel or perpendicular to the length of the tube. [1 mark]

3. The graph below shows the displacement $s$ of $P$ against time $t$ for one full cycle of the standing wave.

    ![2044bb](images/2044bb.png)
    $\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

    The speed of sound is $330\,m\,s^{-1}$. Calculate the length of the tube. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Incident wave is reflected at the open end/far end of the tube  $\checkmark$

    Incident and reflected wave interfere/superpose/ combine  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The sound wave travels through the tube and is partially reflected at the open end due to a difference in pressure inside and outside of the tube. The incident and reflected waves then superpose to form a standing wave with nodes and antinodes.

2. Parallel  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Sound waves are longitudinal. Particle motion is parallel to the direction of propagation of the wave. Here we have a stationary wave but the superposed waves are propagating parallel to the direction of the tube.

2. $\lambda=vT=(330)(0.02)=6.6$ m   $\checkmark$

    $l=\dfrac{\lambda}{2}=\dfrac{6.6}{2}=3.3\,\ce{m}$  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    One full oscillation of the standing wave occurs in the same time as a full oscillation for particle $P$. So, time period of the standing wave is $0.02$ s (from graph of $P$).

    - $v=f\lambda$ and $f=\dfrac{1}{T}$ so $v=\dfrac{\lambda}{T}$ (alternatively we can think of this equation as speed = distance/ time for a wave)

    Rearranging $v=\dfrac{\lambda}{T}$ for $\lambda$ gives

    - $\lambda=vT=(330)(0.02)=6.6$ m

    For the first harmonic of a tube open at both ends $l=\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$

    ![PH2043c copy](<images/PH2043c copy.png>)

    - $l=\dfrac{\lambda}{2}=\dfrac{6.6}{2}=3.3$ m
