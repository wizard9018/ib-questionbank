# PH0815

A loudspeaker is placed above a cylindrical tube of diameter $d$ as shown in the diagram. The loudspeaker plays a sound of constant frequency, $f$. The tube is partially filled with water and a resonant sound is heard. Water is then slowly drained from the bottom of the tube and the next resonant sound is heard when a volume of water, $V$ has been removed.

![tube](images/tube.png)

Which of the following is a correct expression for the speed of sound?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{Vf}{\pi d^2}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{2Vf}{\pi d^2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{4Vf}{\pi d^2}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{8Vf}{\pi d^2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

At the open end, vibrations are free, so this must be an antinode.
At the surface of the water, vibrations are fixed, so this must be a node.

The first resonant sound of the two sounds heard in this scenario is the $n^{th}$ harmonic; the second resonant sound must be the $(n+2)^{th}$   harmonic. The difference in height of the water between where $n^{th}$ and $(n+2)^{th}$ harmonics are heard is equal to half of the wavelength of the sound wave.

There is not enough information to determine $n$. But let us assume $n=3$ for illustrative purposes.

![image](images/image.png)

Volume of a cylinder $=\pi r^2 h$

Therefore

-  $V=\pi \Bigl(\dfrac{d}{2}\Bigr)^2 \Bigl(\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\Bigr)$

Rearranging for $\lambda$ gives

- $λ=\dfrac{8V}{\pi d^2}$

Speed of sound

- $v= f\lambda=\dfrac{8Vf}{\pi d^2}$
