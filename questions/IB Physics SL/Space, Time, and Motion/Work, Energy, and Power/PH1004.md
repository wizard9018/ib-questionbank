# PH1004

**Maximum mark: 7**

While practising, a table tennis player hits a ball of mass 2.8 g that collides with the floor and then bounces back from the wall. The speed of the ball just before reaching the floor is 8.0 m s$^{-1}$. The ball leaves the floor with an angle of 65$\degree$ to the floor as shown.

![Screenshot 2025-06-30 at 5.39.45 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-06-30 at 5.39.45 PM.png>)

1. Due to the collision with the floor, the ball loses
30$\%$ of its initial kinetic energy. Show that the leaving speed of the ball from the floor is around 7 m s$^{-1}$. [2 marks]

2. The ball strikes the wall just as it reaches the highest point of its motion. Determine the horizontal distance between the bounce point and the wall. [3 marks]

3. The ball collides elastically with the wall. The time of contact during the collision is 0.040 s. Calculate the average horizontal force exerted by the wall on the ball during the collision.     [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\textrm{Initial\ Kinetic\ Energy}=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 =\dfrac{1}{2}(2.8\times{10}^{-3})({8)}^2=0.090$ J $\checkmark$

    - $v_f= \sqrt{\dfrac{(0.7)(E_k)(2)}{m}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{(0.7)(.090\ )(2)}{(2.8\times{10}^{-3})}}=6.7\ \ce{m s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The kinetic energy $E_{ki}$ just before the collision; $E_{ki}=\dfrac{1}{2}mu^2$  where $u$ is the speed of the ball just before the collision

    - $E_{ki}=\dfrac{1}{2}(2.8\times{10}^{-3})({8)}^2=0.090$ J

    Since 30$\%$ of the $E_{ki}$ is lost, the final kinetic energy $E_{Kf}$ is

    - $E_{kf}=\left(0.7\right)\left(0.090\right)=0.063$ J

    - $E_{kf}=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$,  where $v$ is the speed of the ball just after the collision

    - $0.063=\dfrac{1}{2}(2.8\times{10}^{-3})({v)}^2$

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{(0.063)(2)}{(2.8\times{10}^{-3})}}=6.7$ m s$^{-1}$

2. Vertically,

    - $u_y = 6.7\sin(65\degree)$ $\checkmark$

    - $t\ =\ \dfrac{v-u}{a}\ =\ \dfrac{0\ -\ 6.7\sin({65\degree})}{9.8}\ = \ 0.62$ s $\checkmark$

    Horizontally,

    - $s=vt=6.7\cos({65\degree})\left(0.62\right)=1.8\ \ce{m} \ \checkmark$ (note that use of 7.0 m s$^{-1}$ gives an answer of 1.9 m)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The ball undergoes projectile motion. The collision with the wall takes place when the ball is at the highest part of its motion, so the vertical component of the motion can be used to find the time of flight.

    - $u_y = 6.7\sin(65\degree)$ and $y = 0$

    - $t\ =\ \dfrac{v-u}{a}\ =\ \dfrac{0-6.7\sin\left({65\degree}\right)}{9.8}\  =\ 0.62$ s

    Using this time in the horizontal direction, where $u_x = 6.7\cos(65\degree)$

    - $s=vt=6.7\cos\left({65\degree}\right)\left(0.62\right)=1.8$ m

3. $\Delta p = 2mv = 2(0.0028)(6.7\cos(65\degree)) =0.016$ N s $\checkmark$

    - $F\ =\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}= \dfrac{0.016}{0.040}= 0.40\ \ce{N} \ \checkmark$ (note that use of 7.0 m s$^{-1}$ gives an answer of 0.41 N)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    As the ball rebounds elastically, the final speed is equal to the initial speed.

    These speeds are equal to the horizontal component of the velocity

    - $v_x=6.7\cos(65\degree) =2.83$ m s$^{-1}$

    To find change in momentum

    $\begin{aligned}
    \Delta p&=p_2-p_1 \\
    &=mv_2-mv_1 \\
    &=0.0028\left(2.83-\left(-2.83\right)\right)
    =0.016\textrm{ N s}
    \end{aligned}$

    Note that because of the change in direction, the change in momentum is equal to twice the initial momentum.

    To find force,

    - $F\ =\ \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{0.016}{0.040}= 0.40$ N
