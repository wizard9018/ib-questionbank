# PH0559

**Maximum mark: 9**

Two blocks of masses 3.0 kg and 4.0 kg are held stationary with a thread connecting the blocks. The mass of 3.0 kg is on a rough incline that makes an angle of 30° with the ground and it starts to slip up when the other mass is slightly more than 4.0 kg. 

![PH0559MS](images/PH0559MS.png)

1. State two differences between static friction and dynamic friction [2 marks]

2. Calculate the coefficient of static friction between the mass of 3.0 kg and the rough surface. [3 marks]

3. The thread connecting masses breaks and the mass of 3.0 kg slides down. Show that the acceleration of the mass of 3.0 kg is around 1.5 m s$^{-2}$. The coefficient of dynamic friction between 3.0 kg mass and the rough surface is 0.40. [2 marks]

4. Determine the speed of the mass of 3.0 kg after it has moved through a vertical height of 1.0 m. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Static friction occurs when the object is not moving (relative to a surface) and Dynamic friction occurs when there is relative motion between the object and a surface $\checkmark$

    The magnitude of static friction is dependent on external forces and the magnitude of dynamic friction is not dependent on external forces **OR** static friction force can vary up to a maximum while dynamic friction is a constant value  $\checkmark$

2. Component of weight parallel to the incline = $\ll mg\sin(30°)=(3.0)(9.81)(0.5)=\gg 15 \ll\textrm{N}\gg$ and tension in thread $T=mg=(4.0)(9.81)=39$ N $\checkmark$

    Static friction = $\ll 39-15=\gg 24 \ll\textrm{N}\gg$ $\checkmark$

    coefficient of static friction = $\ll \dfrac{24}{(3.0)(9.81)\cos(30°)}= \gg$ :answer[0.96] $\checkmark$ (0.94 to 0.96 accepted)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    According to the diagram, the mass of 3 kg is balanced with weight $W$, tension $T$ static friction $F_f$ and normal force $N$;

    ![PH0559Q](images/PH0559Q.png)

    Weight has two components parallel to the incline $W_h$ and perpendicular to the incline $W_v$; 

    - $W_h=mg\sin(30°)=(3.0)(9.81)(0.5)=15$ N down to the slope

    The tension balances the weight of the mass of 4.0 kg; 

    - $T=mg=(4.0)(9.81)=39$ N

    The forces parallel to the incline must cancel each other; 

    - $\begin{aligned}

    W_h+F_f&=T\\

    \hspace{1em}\\

    15+F_f&=39\\

    \hspace{1em}\\

    F_f&=24\textrm{ N}\\

    \end{aligned}$

    By using the equation of static friction;

    $\begin{aligned}

    F_f&=\mu R\\

    \hspace{1em}\\

    \mu(3.0)(9.81)\cos(30°)&=24\\

    \hspace{1em}\\

    \mu&=0.96\\

    \end{aligned}$

3. The dynamic friction = $\ll\mu R=(0.40)(3.0)(9.81)\cos30°=\gg 10 \ll N\gg$ $\checkmark$

    - $a$ = $\ll\dfrac{14.7-10.2}{3}=\gg $ :answer[1.5 m s$^{-2}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    There are only two forces that act on the mass parallel to the incline. They are components of the weight $W_h$ down to the slope and dynamic friction $F_f$ up the slope. The dynamic friction; 

    - $F_f=\mu R=(0.40)(3.0)(9.81)\cos(30°)=10$ N

    - $F_{net}=ma$

    - $14.7-10.2=3.0a$

    - $a=1.5$ m s$^{-2}$

4. displacement of the mass = $\ll \dfrac{1.0}{\sin(30^o)} = \gg 2.0 \ll\textrm{m}\gg$ $\checkmark$

    - $v = \ll\sqrt{(2)(1.5)(2.0)}=\gg 2.4 \ll\textrm{m s}^{-1}\gg$ $\checkmark$

    **OR**

    Heat lost due to friction = $\ll F_f d=(10)(2.0)=\gg 20\ll\textrm{J}\gg$ $\checkmark$

    - $v$ = $\ll \sqrt{\dfrac{(2)(9.0)}{(3.0)}}=\gg$ :answer[2.4 m s$^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    The overall displacement s of the mass when it displaced 1.0 m vertically;

    - $s=\dfrac{1.0}{\sin(30^o)}=2.0$ m

    By using a suvat equation;

    - $v^2=u^2+2as$ while $u=0$, $a=1.5$ m s$^{-2}$, and $s=2.0$ m

    - $v^2=(2)(1.5)(2.0)=6.0$

    - $v=2.4$ m s$^{-1}$

    This question can be also done with the conservation of energy approach; 

    The energy dissipated due to friction is

    - $Q=F_f d=(10)(2)=20$ J

    and the change in gravitational potential energy can be found by

    - $\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h=(3.0)(9.81)(1.0)=29$ J

    The gravitational potential energy was converted to thermal energy from friction and kinetic energy, therefore

    - $29=\Delta E_k+20$

    - $\Delta E_k=9.0$ J

    Solving for speed gives

    - $\Delta E_k=\frac{1}{2} mv^2$

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\Delta E_k}{m}}$

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{(2)(9.0)}{(3.0)}}=2.4$ m s$^{-1}$
