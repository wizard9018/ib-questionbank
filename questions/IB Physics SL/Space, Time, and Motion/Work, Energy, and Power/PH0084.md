# PH0084

A system that consists of two light identical springs connected in series stores a total elastic potential energy $E_H$ when a load is added to the springs. One of the springs is then removed and the same load is added to the remaining spring.

$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Created with Chemix (https://chemix.org)]}}$

In terms of $E_H$, what is the total elastic potential energy stored in the single spring situation?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 2$E_H$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $E_H$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.5$E_H$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 0.25$E_H$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Using Hooke's law,

- $F=k\Delta x$

the elastic potential energy is

- $E_p=\dfrac{1}{2} k\Delta x^2=\dfrac{1}{2} F\Delta x$.

In the two-spring situation, both springs experience the same downward force and so they have the same extension given by the equation $F=k\Delta x$.

- $F=k\Delta x$.

When one of the springs is removed, the load remains unchanged and the remaining spring experiences the same extension. Therefore the elastic potential energy stored is also halved.
