# PH1852

The graph below represents the velocity $v$ against time $t$ of an object which is initially at rest and moves in a straight line. It is given that the areas $A_1$ and $A_2$ are 10 m and 30 m respectively.

![ph1852q](images/ph1852q.png)

At $t=10$ s the object is located at a distance $D$ from its starting point.

Which of the following correctly describes the value of $D$ and the average speed of the object from $t=0$ s to $t=10$ s?

$\\$

| | $\bf D$ / m | Average speed / m s$^{-1}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| 20 | 2  |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 40 | 2  |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{2.5em}$ 20 $\hspace{2.5em}$ | 4 |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 40 | 4  |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The area $A_1$ represents a distance travelled in a positive direction whereas area $A_2$ represents a distance travelled in a negative direction.

The total displacement of the object is then given by:

- $A_1-A_2=10\textrm{ m}-30\textrm{ m}=-20\textrm{ m}$

This tells us that at $t=10$ s the object is located at a distance of 20 m from the origin, therefore $D=20$ m.

To determine the average speed of the object we must find the ratio between the distance travelled and the time taken:

- Average speed $=\dfrac{\textrm{distance travelled}}{\textrm{time taken}}=\dfrac{10\textrm{ m}+30\textrm{ m}}{10\textrm{ s}}=\dfrac{40\textrm{ m}}{10\textrm{ s}}=4$ m s$^{-1}$
