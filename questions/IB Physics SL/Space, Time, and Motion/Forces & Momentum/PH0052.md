# PH0052

An object with a weight $W$ connected to one end of a rod of negligible mass rotates at constant speed in a vertical plane.

![Screenshot 2026-01-30 at 6.46.14 AM1](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-30 at 6.46.14 AM1.png>)

When the object is at the top position of the circular path, the tension on the rod is zero. What is the magnitude of tension at the bottom position of the circular path?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Equal to $W$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Smaller than $W$
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Equal to $2W$
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Greater than $2W$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

At the top position both the tension and the weight would act downwards, which is the same direction as the centripetal acceleration. We are told there is no tension, so all centripetal acceleration is provided by the weight.

- $T + W=F_{centripetal}$ and $T = 0$

Therefore,

- $F_{centripetal} = W$

At the bottom, tension works upwards towards the centre and weight acts downwards. Resolving for net force gives

- $T - W  =  F_{centripetal}$

Since speed and radius are constant, the centripetal acceleration at the top and bottom are equal:

- $F_{centripetal} = W$

Therefore, the tension at the bottom

-   $T = 2W$
