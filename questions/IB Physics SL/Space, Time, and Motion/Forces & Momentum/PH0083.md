# PH0083

The diagram shows the forces acting on a block as it slides down an inclined plane with constant acceleration. $W$ is the weight of the block, $F$ is the friction force, $\mu_d$ is the coefficient of dynamic friction and $g$ is the gravitational field strength. The plane is inclined at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal.

![Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 3.55.44 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 3.55.44 PM.png>)

Which expression gives the acceleration of the block?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $g\sin\theta - g\mu_d\cos\theta$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $g\cos\theta - g\mu_d\sin\theta$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $W\sin\theta - W\mu_d\cos\theta$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $W\cos\theta - W\mu_d\sin\theta$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To solve this, we should resolve the weight into components parallel and perpendicular to the slope.

![Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 7.00.51 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 7.00.51 AM.png>)

Component of weight along the slope = $W\sin \theta = mg\sin \theta$

The forces perpendicular to the slope are balanced, so the normal reaction force ($F_N$) is equal to the component of gravity opposite to it, given by

- $F_N = W\cos\theta=mg\cos\theta$

Dynamic friction

- $F= \mu_dF_N = \mu_d\,mg\cos \theta$

Apply Newton's second law:

- $F_{net}=mg\sin\theta - \mu_d\,mg\cos\theta =ma$

Dividing both sides by mass gives

- $a=g\sin\theta - \mu_d\,g\cos\theta $
