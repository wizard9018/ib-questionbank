# PH0820

Small marbles with equal mass but different diameters are released in liquids with different densities and reach terminal velocity. Which of the following combinations of marble and the density of the liquid will result in the shortest time for the marble to fall a given height?  Assume that in all cases the marbles sink in the liquid and the viscosity of the liquid is constant.

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Diameter of the marble$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$The density of the liquid$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Smaller diameter | Lower density |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Greater diameter| Lower density |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Smaller diameter | Higher density |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Greater diameter | Higher density |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For this question we can consider the terminal velocities of the two marbles. The marble with the greatest terminal velocity will reach the bottom in the shortest amount of time. It can also be shown that the marble with the greatest terminal velocity experiences the greatest acceleration in the situation where terminal velocity is not reached.

Terminal velocity takes place when the net force is zero.

Therefore

- $F_g = F_d+F_b$

- $mg = \rho V g+6\pi \eta rv$

Solving for terminal velocity gives

- $v = \dfrac{mg-\rho V g}{6\pi \eta r}$

Substituting the formula for the volume of a sphere

- $v = \dfrac{mg-\rho (\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3) g}{6\pi \eta r}$

Firstly, we see that if the density of the liquid $\rho$ decreases, the numerator increases and so the speed will increase.

Additionally, we see that as $r$ increases, the numerator will at some point reach zero. This will be the point at which the density of the marble equals the density of the fluid. We are told that the marble always sinks, and so the numerator must remain positive.

Looking at the expression, decreasing $r$ will have the effect of both increasing the numerator (thus increasing the velocity) and decreasing the denominator (also increasing the velocity).
