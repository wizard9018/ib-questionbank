# PH0968

The diagram shows the total upward force and the total downward force acting on a hot air balloon at one instant. The arrows are drawn to scale.

![Screenshot 2026-10-20 at 8.34.50 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-10-20 at 8.34.50 AM.png>)

Consider the following statements about the possible motion of the balloon.

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The balloon is accelerating   |
  | II. | The balloon is moving upwards |
  | III. | The balloon is moving downwards |

Which statements, considered independently, **could** be true?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

I is correct - The diagram shows that the upward force is greater than the downward force. So, there is a net upward force acting on the balloon. $F=ma$ so there is an upward acceleration. This statement is definitely true here.

II could be correct – Balloon could be moving upwards - acceleration is also upwards so speed of the balloon would be increasing.

III could be correct – Balloon could be moving downwards - acceleration is upwards so speed of the balloon would be decreasing (deceleration).

For linear motion, acceleration tells us nothing about the speed, just how the speed is changing. The balloon could be moving upwards, moving downwards or stationary.
