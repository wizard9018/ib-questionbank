# PH1100

**Maximum mark: 8**

In an experiment to investigate Newton's 2nd Law, a student attaches a trolley of mass $M$ to a string that passes over a pulley and is attached to a hanging mass $m$ as shown. The student measures the time taken for the cart to travel between two marks on the table when accelerating from rest.

![PH1100aaaa](images/PH1100aaaa.png)

For one of the trials, the student measured the time taken to be $1.2 ± 0.2\, $s. The distance between the markings was $1.50 ± 0.03\, $m. The acceleration can be found from the equation $a=\dfrac{2s}{t^2}$ .

1. 1. Determine the experimental value for the acceleration, with uncertainty.  [4 marks]

   2. The student increases the distance between the two marks on the table and repeats the experiment. Explain the effect of this change on the uncertainty on the calculated acceleration.    [2 marks]

2.  The student uses the equation

    - $a=\dfrac{mg}{(M+m)}$

    to predict the acceleration of the trolley. Give two reasons why the experimental value will likely be lower than the result from the calculation based on the masses.  [2 marks]

1.  1. ::indent
        - $a=\dfrac{2(1.5)}{(1.2)^2} =2.08\, \ce{m\, s^{-2}}$    $\checkmark$

       Relative uncertainty = $\dfrac{.03}{1.5}+2 \dfrac{0.2}{1.2}=0.35=35\% $  $\checkmark$

       Uncertainty = $(2.08)(0.35)= 0.72$  $\checkmark$

       $a= $:answer[$(2.1± 0.7) \, $m s$^{-2}$] $\hspace{2em}$    OR   $\hspace{2em} $:answer[$(2.08 ±  0.72) \, $m s$^{-2}$] $\checkmark$

       $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
       Substituting the measured values into the given equation

       - $a=\dfrac{2s}{t^2} =2\dfrac{1.5}{(1.2)^2}\ =2.08\, $m s$^{-2}$

       To find the uncertainty, we will need to add the relative/percentage uncertainties. Because the time is squared, this percentage uncertainty is added twice.

       - Relative uncertainty = $\dfrac{.03}{1.5}+2 \left(\dfrac{0.2}{1.2}\right)=0.35=35\% $

       The uncertainty on acceleration is found by multiplying the experimental value by the calculated relative uncertainty.

       Uncertainty = $(2.08)(0.35)= 0.72  $

        For the final statement, the number of decimal places on the acceleration and that on the uncertainty should match.

       - $a=(2.1\pm 0.7) \,$m s$^{-2}\ \ \ \ OR\ \ \ (2.08 \pm 0.72)\,$m s$^{-2} $

    2.  Uncertainty on the time and distance measurements are unchanged but the distance and time values increase.   $\checkmark$

        Relative uncertainties decrease, therefore the uncertainty on acceleration will decrease.   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Although the marks are moved further apart, we assume that the same measuring technique is used and so the uncertainty on the distance measurement will remain $± 0.03$ m. Similarly, the uncertainty on the time will likely remain $± 0.2$ s. The fractional uncertainties for both will therefore decrease, leading to a decrease in the fractional uncertainty on the acceleration. These changes should not affect the acceleration value itself, therefore the absolute uncertainty on the acceleration will also decrease.

2.  friction/air resistance. $\checkmark$

    energy goes into the rotation of the pulley/trolley wheels. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Friction will oppose the motion of the cart, reducing the net force and therefore the acceleration. Friction will also exist in the pulley

    The pulley has mass and rotates at an increasing rate as the cart accelerates. This is also true for the wheels on the cart. This takes some energy from the system that would normally go into the kinetic energy of the cart.
