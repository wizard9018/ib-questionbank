## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.
        In precise measurements the random uncertainty is small/the measurements are close to each other/there are many significant figures on the measurements. $\checkmark$

        Accurate measurements are close to the actual values. $\checkmark$

    2. Systematic uncertainty. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
A systematic uncertainty affects all recorded points in the same way. The zero error will cause all points to be either too high or too low by the same amount.

2.
    1. Evidence that emf is the y-intercept. $\checkmark$

        $\varepsilon= $ :answer[$8.9\ to\ 9.1$] $\ll\ \ce{V}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        ![Screenshot 2025-06-04 at 4.35.14 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-06-04 at 4.35.14 PM.png>)

        The formula for the terminal voltage of a cell is given by

        - $V_T = \varepsilon - ir$

        Thus the line of best fit intercepts the y-axis at the emf.

    2. Gradient=$-r$/evidence of calculating the gradient $\checkmark$

        $r= $ :answer[$1.0\,\Omega$] $\checkmark$

        uncertainty = :answer[$\pm\,0.1\ll \Omega\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The gradient of this graph is $-r$

        gradient$=\ \dfrac{6.75-9}{2.25-0}=-1$

        Then

        - $r=1.0\, \Omega$

        To determine the uncertainty in the gradient, we can the line of best fit with the maximum gradient possible with use of the uncertainty bars.

        ![Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 9.08.42 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 9.08.42 AM.png>)

        The uncertainty in gradient is:

        - $\Delta$ gradient  =best  fit  gradient - worst  fit   gradient

        - worst fit gradient=$\dfrac{6.65-9.2}{2.25-0}=-1.13$

        - $\Delta$ gradient  $= -1 -(-1.13) = 0.1 \Omega$

    3. Not affected $\checkmark$

        Error will cancel out in calculations/gradient remains the same $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The zero error will raise or lower all the measured values by the same amount, but will not affect the gradient.

        The internal resistance is calculated from the gradient of the graph of voltage variation with current.

        In these calculations, the raised values will cancel out, which means that the value of the internal resistance of the cell will not be affected.

        Alternatively, the systematic error will shift the plotted points upwards or downwards, but will not affect the gradient.
