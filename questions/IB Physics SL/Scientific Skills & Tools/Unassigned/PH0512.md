    2. The graph does not pass through the origin.

        **OR**

        Celsius cannot be used for temperature proportionality/Kelvin temperature is proportional to average kinetic energy.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To have a proportional relationship between two quantities, the graph should be straight and pass through the origin. Even though a straight line can be drawn on the graph, it does not pass through the origin.

        Furthermore, Celsius units for temperature are not directly related to a physical quantity of a substance so a proportional relationship would have little meaning. If the temperature units were in Kelvin, which is representative of the average kinetic energy of the particles of the substance, proportionality would have significant meaning. With conversion to Kelvin, the data in this experiment would not meet the criteria for proportionality.

2.
    1. $T_0=20\pm0.5$ and $T=31 \pm 0.5$ $\checkmark$

        $T-T_o=11 \pm 1 \checkmark$

        $\ll \dfrac{\Delta (T-T_0)}{T-T_0} \times 100\%= \dfrac{1}{11} \times 100\% = \gg $ :answer[$9\% $] $\checkmark$

    2. The gradient of the graph = $k$ $\checkmark$

        Correct calculation of the gradient of the graph  reference to (a)(i). *Must use a time range of at least 15s in gradient calculation* $\checkmark$

        $\ll\ k=\dfrac{35-20}{34.5-0}=\dfrac{15}{34.5}=0.43 \gg$ accept answers in the range from 0.40 to 0.50

    3. $c=\ll\dfrac{Pt}{m\left(T-T_0\right)}=\gg \dfrac{Pt}{m \Delta T}$ $\checkmark$

        $\ll\dfrac{\ce{kg}\,\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}\,\ce{s}}{\ce{kg}\ \ce{K}}=\dfrac{\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}}{\ce{K}}=\gg\ \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}\,\ce{K}^{-1}$
(*also accept if students find answer using knowledge of specific heat capacity*) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The following can be written;

        - $\dfrac{T-T_0}{t}=k=\dfrac{P}{mc}$

        When the relationship is rearranged to find c;

        - $c=\dfrac{Pt}{m\left(T-T_0\right)}= \dfrac{Pt}{m \Delta T}$

        When units are substituted;

        - $Unit\ of\ c=\dfrac{\ce{W}\,\ce{s}}{\ce{kg}\,\ce{K}}$

        Since

        - $Watts=\ce{kg} \,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-2} \ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}=\ce{kg} \ \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}$

        - $SI\ unit\ of\ c=\dfrac{\ce{kg}\,\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}\ce{s}}{\ce{kg}\,\ce{K}}=\dfrac{\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}}{\ce{K}}=\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}\,\ce{K}^{-1}$
