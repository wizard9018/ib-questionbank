# PH0578

**Maximum mark: 7**

In an experiment to determine the speed of sound in air, some students used a number of tuning forks of known frequencies and a tube with a moveable piston arranged as shown below.

For each tuning fork they used the setup shown in the diagram.

![Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 4.11.10 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 4.11.10 PM.JPEG>)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with chemix - https:// chemix.org/]}}$

The students would sound the tuning fork, then move the piston slowly downwards until a loud sound is first heard. The length $L$ was then measured. This was repeated for different tuning forks.

The graph shows the variation of $L^{-1}$ with frequency $f$.

![Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 9.03.22 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-02-18 at 9.03.22 AM.png>)

1. Draw the line of best fit. [1 mark]

2. Calculate the fractional uncertainty of $L^{-1}$ when the length is $20\, $cm. [2 marks]

3.
    1. Determine the gradient, expressing your answer in fundamental SI units. [2 marks]

    2. Use the gradient calculated in (i) to determine the speed of sound. [2 marks]

2. $\dfrac{1}{L}=0.05\ll \ce{cm}^{-1}\gg$, uncertainty is $0.001\ \ce{cm}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    Fractional uncertainty = :answer[0.02] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    From the graph, the uncertainty on the 0.05 is 0.001.

    The fractional uncertainty is therefore $\dfrac{0.001}{0.05}=0.02\ or\ 2\%$

3.
    1. Gradient = $\left(\dfrac{480-180}{5.8-2.2}\right)=$ :answer[83.33] $\checkmark$. (Accept answers from 82.5 to 86.25)

       $\ce{m s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

       $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
       Note that over half of the line should be used to find the gradient.

       The values on the horizontal axis and therefore the denominator are $\times 10^{-2}\, \ce{cm}^{-1}$. Within the gradient calculation, this becomes $\times 10^2\,\ce{cm}$ which is equvalent to m.

       The values on the vertical axis are Hz, which are equivalent to s$^{-1}$.

       Combined, this leaves the units in m s$^{-1}$.

    2. $\ll\ v=4(gradient)\gg$

        $v=\left(4\right)\left(83.33\right)$ $\checkmark$

        v= :answer[333] $\ll\ \ce{m s}^{-1}\gg$ $\checkmark$\
        *Accept answers from 330 to 345*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        This model is similar to a pipe that is open at one end and closed at the other.

        The frequency in such a pipe is given by
        $f=\dfrac{nv}{4L}$

        The first loud sound is heard at the first harmonics at $n=1$

        Then the gradient for this graph is

        $\begin{aligned}
        gradient &= \dfrac{v}{4}\\
        v &=4\left(gradient\right)\\
        v &=\left(4\right)\left(83.33\right)\\
        &=333.32\, \ce{m s}^{-1}\\
        \end{aligned}$
