**Maximum mark: 11**

1. A student investigates standing waves in strings. A string is connected to an oscillator where a function generator can adjust the frequency. The other end of the string passes through a pulley and supports a mass.

    ![PH0515aaa](images/PH0515aaa.png)
    $\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with chemix - https ://chemix.org/]}}$

    The student varied the mass $m$ and recorded the values for frequency $f$ for which the first harmonic was visible on the string. The following graph shows the recorded data.

    ![Screenshot 2025-04-15 at 11.23.36 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-15 at 11.23.36 AM.png>)

    The uncertainty of the mass is negligible.

    1. Draw the line of best fit for the plotted data on the graph. [1 mark]

    2. By referring to the plotted data, explain why it cannot be stated that frequency is directly proportional to mass.     [1 mark]

    3. Use the graph to determine the value for $f$ with its absolute uncertainty for $m=1.0\ $kg [2 marks]

2. The theory suggests that

    $f^2=km$

    where *k* is a constant.

    The variation of $f^2$ with *m* is seen on the graph below.

    ![Screenshot 2025-04-15 at 11.31.35 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-15 at 11.31.35 AM.png>)

    1.     Show that the absolute uncertainty of $f^2$ is $\pm0.01\ $kHz$^2$ for $m=1.0\ $kg. [3 marks]

    2. The absolute uncertainty of $f^2$ is $0.03\ $kHz$^2$ for $m=0.25\ $kg. Construct error bars for $f^2$ when  $m=0.25\ $kg and $m=1.00\ $kg. [1 mark]

    3. State and explain whether the theory is supported by data or not. [2 marks]

3. Determine the unit of *k*, in fundamental SI units. [1 mark]

2.
    1. Percentage uncertainty of $f = \dfrac{\Delta f}{f} \times 100\% = \dfrac{10}{530} \times 100 \% = 1.9\%$ $\checkmark$

        Percentage uncertainty of $f^2 = \dfrac{\Delta (f^2)}{f^2} \times 100 \% = 2 \times \dfrac{\Delta f}{f} \times 100 \% = 2 \times 1.9\% = 3.8\%$ $\checkmark$

        The absolute uncertainty of$\ f^2$=$f^2\times3.8\%={530}^2\times3.8\%=10674\,\ce{Hz}^2=0.01\ \textrm{kHz}^2$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The percentage uncertainty of *f* for $m=1.0\ $kg;

        - $\dfrac{\Delta f}{f} \times 100\% = \dfrac{10}{530} \times 100\% = 1.9\%$

        By using the rules of propagation of uncertainties;

        - $\dfrac{\Delta (f^2)}{f^2}\times 100\% = 2 \times \dfrac{\Delta f}{f} \times 100\% = 2 \times 1.9\% = 3.8\%$

        The absolute uncertainty of $f^2$;

        - $\Delta (f^2) = f^2 \times 3.8\% = 530^2 \times 3.8\% = 0.01 \ \textrm{kHz}^2$
