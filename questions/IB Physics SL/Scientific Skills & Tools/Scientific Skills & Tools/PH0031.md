# PH0031

A rectangular prism is obtained by combining two identical cubes as shown in the figure.

![PH0031](images/PH0031.png)

The length of one side of a cube is $10.0 \pm 0.2$ cm.

What is the ratio

 $\dfrac{\textrm{absolute uncertainty of volume of the rectangular prism}}{\textrm{absolute uncertainty of volume of one cube}}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 1

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 2

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 5

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For the rectangular prism

$V = l \times w \times h$ where $l = 20.0 \pm 0.4$, $w = 10.0 \pm 0.2$, and $h = 10.0 \pm 0.2$

The uncertainty on the volume is found from adding the fractional uncertainties of the three different dimensions.

$\Delta V=V(\dfrac{\Delta l}{l}+\dfrac{\Delta w}{w}+\dfrac{\Delta h}{h})$

$\Delta V=2000(\dfrac{0.4}{20.0}+\dfrac{0.2}{10.0}+\dfrac{0.2}{10.0})=\pm 120\,\ce{cm^3}$

For the cube

$V=l^3 \hspace{1em}$ where $l = 10.0 \pm 0.2$

$\Delta V=(V)(3)\dfrac{\Delta l}{l}$

$\Delta V=(1000)(3)(\dfrac{0.2}{10.0})=\pm 60\,\ce{cm^3}$

Therefore the ratio is $\dfrac{120}{60} = 2$
