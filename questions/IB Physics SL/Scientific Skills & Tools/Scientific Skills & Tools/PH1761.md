# PH1761

A cylinder is measured to have a length of 10.0 cm $\pm$ 0.4 cm and a diameter of 8.0 cm $\pm$ 0.4 cm.

![Screenshot 2025-02-09 at 1.21.36 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-02-09 at 1.21.36 PM.png>)

What is the percentage uncertainty in the volume of the cylinder?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ 1.2\%

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ 9\%

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ 14\%

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ 24\%

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The fractional uncertainty of a quantity $y=\dfrac{ab}{c}$ is given by

- $\dfrac{\Delta y}{y}=\dfrac{\Delta a}{a}+\dfrac{\Delta b}{b}+\dfrac{\Delta c}{c}$

Where $\Delta y$, $\Delta a$, $\Delta b$ and $\Delta c$ are the absolute uncertainties.

Note that the uncertainty on the radius $\Delta r$ is the same as the diameter $\Delta d$.

The volume of a cylinder $V$ is given by:

- $V=\pi r^2h=\pi(r)(r)(h)$

Where $r$ is the radius of the circular end and $h$ is the height. So the fractional uncertainty in the volume is given by:

- $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}+\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}+\dfrac{\Delta h}{h}$

The fractional uncertainty in $h$ is given by:

- $\dfrac{\Delta h}{h}=\dfrac{0.4}{10}=0.04$

The fractional uncertainty in $r$ is equal to the fractional uncertainty in diameter given by:

- $\hspace{0.02cm}$ $\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}=\dfrac{\Delta d}{d}=\dfrac{0.4}{8}=0.05$

- $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}+\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}+\dfrac{\Delta h}{h}=0.05+0.05+0.04=0.14$

Finally writing this a percentage:

- $(0.14)(100)=14\%$
