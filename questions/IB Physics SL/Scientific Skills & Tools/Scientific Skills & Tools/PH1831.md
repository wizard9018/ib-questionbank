# PH1831

**Maximum mark: 8**

A student wishes to investigate how the viscosity of oil is affected by its temperature. An experimental setup is designed by the student to investigate the phenomenon.

![Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 10.40.24 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 10.40.24 AM.JPEG>)

The apparatus includes an electric heater positioned at the base of a water-filled container. Inside this container, a smaller cylinder containing oil is fully immersed in the water.

A thermometer monitors the water temperature. A small metallic sphere is dropped into the oil-filled cylinder, where it descends to the bottom. The time $t$, required for the sphere to travel between two reference marks, spaced a distance $h$ apart, is measured.

1. Explain, in terms of heat transfer, the experimental reason that requires:

    1. the heating element to be placed at the bottom. [1 mark]

    2. the oil-filled cylinder to be completely immersed in water. [1 mark]

2. The metal sphere is dropped and the time taken to fall at constant speed through a distance $h=62.0\pm0.2$ cm is measured to be $t=2.0\pm0.1$ s.

    The student used these values to calculate the average speed as 0.31 m s$^{-1}$.

    Use these values to determine the absolute uncertainty on the calculated value for the speed. [2 marks]

3. The student decides to use a model for the oil viscosity $\eta$ that suggests that it is inversely proportional to the speed $v$ at which the sphere falls.

    According to this model

    - $\eta=\dfrac{k}{v}$

    where $k$ is a constant.

    State the units of $k$ in terms of fundamental SI units. [1 mark]

To investigate this phenomenon further, another student proposes to use a model for the viscosity $\eta$ of the oil at a temperature $T$ to be:

- $\eta=\alpha T^\beta$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constants to be determined.

The experiment is repeated several times. Each time, the temperature $T$ of the oil is recorded and the velocity at which the ball falls is used to calculate $\eta$.

Then, a graph of $\log\eta$ is plotted against $\log T$.

4. State and explain which features of such a graph would allow the constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$ to be obtained. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. To allow for convection currents that will lead to a constant temperature across the volume of water. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Any heating element placed at the lower part of a fluid will allow for convection currents to distribute heat uniformly through the liquid. As water in contact with the heat source increases its temperature it will expand reducing its density. This lower density will force this heated water to rise allowing for cooler water to descend and thus establishing circular flow that facilitates heat transfer by a process named *convection*.

        These currents will contribute to the uniformity of temperature across the entire volume of water within the cylinder.

    2. Conduction of heat from water into the oil will be more efficient to have a constant temperature along the entire volume of oil. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        By having the oil-filled cylinder completely immersed in water, heat transfer by conduction from the water into the oil will be more efficient thus leading to a uniform temperature along the volume of oil.

2. ::indent
    - $\Delta v=\left(\dfrac{0.002\textrm{ m}}{0.62\textrm{ m}}+\dfrac{0.1\textrm{ s}}{2.0\textrm{ s}}\right)(0.31)$ m s$^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    - $\Delta v= 0.02 \textrm{ m s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    (accept 0.016 m s$^{-1}$ or 0.017 m s$^{-1}$, rounded to 2 sf. Any more than 2 sf will not receive the mark)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Given that the speed $v$ is calculated as:

    - $v=\dfrac{h}{t}$

    where $h$ is the distance travelled by the metal sphere and $t$ is the time, its absolute uncertainty can be calculated as:

    - $\dfrac{\Delta v}{v}=\dfrac{\Delta h}{h}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{t}$

    This expression leads to:

    - $\begin{aligned}
    \ \Delta v&=\left(\dfrac{\Delta h}{h}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{t}\right)(v)\\
    \\\
    \ \Delta v&=\left(\dfrac{0.002\textrm{ m}}{0.62\textrm{ m}}+\dfrac{0.1\textrm{ s}}{2.0\textrm{ s}}\right)(0.31\textrm{ m s}^{-1})\\
    \\\
    \ \Delta v&=0.0165\textrm{ m s}^{-1}\\
    \end{aligned}$

    Because it is an uncertainty, this value should be rounded to 1 significant figure, leading to:

    - $\Delta v=0.02$ m s$^{-1}$

3. $[k]=[\eta v]=$ kg s$^{-2}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The given expression leads to the constant $k$ being equal to:

    - $k=\eta v$

    Which means that the units of $k$ are given as the product of the units of $\eta$ times the units of $v$.

    We can use the expression for the drag force to solve for $\eta v$:

    - $F_d=6\pi\eta rv$

    According to this expression

    - $[\eta v]=\dfrac{[F_d]}{[6\pi r]}$

    - $[\eta v]=\dfrac{\textrm{N}}{\textrm{m}}=\dfrac{\textrm{kg m s}^{-2}}{\textrm{m}}=$ kg s$^{-2}$

    Therefore:

    - $[k]=$ kg s$^{-2}$

4. $\eta=\alpha T^\beta$ leads to $\log\eta=\beta\log T+\log\alpha$ $\checkmark$

    $\ll$On a graph of $\log\eta$ against $\log T\gg$ $\beta$ would be the gradient of the graph $\checkmark$

    $\log\alpha$ would be the y-intercept of the graph $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    According to $\eta=\alpha T^\beta$, taking logarithms on each side leads to:

    - $\log\eta=\log\left(\alpha T^\beta\right)$

    The right-hand side of this equation can be expanded as:

    - $\log\eta=\beta\log(T)+\log\alpha$

    This equation can be interpreted as a linear one of the form $y=mx+b$ in which $\log\eta$ is the dependent variable ($y$), and $\log T$ is the independent variable ($x$).

    The constant $\beta$ would then be the gradient and $\log\alpha$ would be the y-intercept.

    On a graph of $\log\eta$ against $\log T$, $\beta$ would be the gradient and $\alpha$ would be equal to $10^{\textrm{y-intercept}}$.
