# PH1830

**Maximum mark: 9**

A student investigates the efficiency of a flask in maintaining the temperature $\theta$  of 1 kg of water over time $t$. The temperature was recorded every 10 minutes for a total duration of 24 h.

The graph below shows the trend that was recorded during the experiment.

![PH1830qa](images/PH1830qa.JPEG)

The student wishes to investigate how the rate of decrease in temperature $(v_\theta)$ is affected by the difference $\Delta\theta$ between the water temperature and the room temperature, which was recorded as 20°C.

1. State the value of $\Delta\theta$ at $t=9$ h. [1 mark]

2. By drawing a suitable tangent, determine the rate $v_\theta$ at which temperature is decreasing at $t=9$ h. [3 marks]

The student calculates the value of $v_\theta$ for different values of temperatures.

The graph below shows the **magnitude** of $v_\theta$ for different values of $\Delta\theta$.

![Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 10.41.44 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 10.41.44 AM.JPEG>)

3. Use your answer to (b) to plot the data point at $\Delta\theta=25$°C. [1 mark]

After plotting the graph, the student realises that the values of $|v_\theta|$ were calculated with a relative uncertainty of $\pm$10% and that the uncertainty in $\Delta\theta$ is negligible.

4. On the graph above, draw error bars at the points where $\Delta\theta=10$°C and $\Delta\theta=70$°C. [2 marks]

The student suggests that the rate at which temperature of the water decreases is proportional to the temperature difference between the water and the environment.

5. State and explain whether the data supports the student's suggestion. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\Delta\theta=$ :answer[25 $\ll$°C$\gg$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    From the graph, at $t=9$ h water temperature was about 45°C.

    ![PH1830aa](images/PH1830aa.JPEG)

    The difference between this and room temperature (20°C) is then:

    - $\Delta\theta=45$°C$ \ - \ 20$°C

    - $\Delta\theta=25$°C

2. Tangent at $t=9$ h traced. $\checkmark$

    Choosing (any) two points along the tangent (points at least 8 h apart). $\checkmark$

    Calculation of the gradient using $v_\theta=\dfrac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}$

    Calculated gradient must be within -3.5°C h$^{-1}$ and -2.5°C h$^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To find the instantaneous rate at which temperature is decreasing, we must first trace a tangent to the graph at $t=9$ h.

    ![PH1830ab1](images/PH1830ab1.JPEG)

    Now we must choose two points through which this tangent passes. It is recommended to choose points that are far apart from each other.

    Different students might of course choose different points leading to a slightly different estimate of the rate of decrease in temperature.

    We will use points (4, 60) and (22,5). These are points through which the tangent seems to be passing.

    ![PH1830ab2](images/PH1830ab2.JPEG)

    We can now calculate the gradient of this line as:

    - $v_\theta=\dfrac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{5\textrm{°C}-60\textrm{°C}}{22\textrm{ h}-4\textrm{ h}}$

    - $v_\theta=-3.1$ °C h$^{-1}$

3. Data point plotted correctly at (25, 3.1) $\left(\pm\dfrac{1}{2}\textrm{ square}\right)$ $\checkmark$

    ![Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 10.44.02 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 10.44.02 AM.JPEG>)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The data that corresponds $\Delta\theta=25$°C has a horizontal coordinate which is equal to 25°C and a vertical coordinate that corresponds to the magnitude of our answer to question (b): i.e. 3.1 °C h$^{-1}$.

    Point should then be plotted at (25, 3.1)

4. *Vertical* error bars plotted both points. $\checkmark$

    Error bars of the correct length for each point. $\checkmark$

    (Approximately $\pm0.1$ for the first point and $\pm0.8$ for the second one)

    ![Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 10.44.41 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 10.44.41 AM.JPEG>)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The relative uncertainty of $\pm10$% affects only $|v_\theta|$ . This means that the error bars must be represented vertically. No horizontal error bars are required given that the uncertainty in $\Delta\theta$ is negligible.

    As seen on the graph, for the data point at $\Delta\theta=$10°C, the vertical coordinate is $|v_\theta|=1$ °C h$^{-1}$. The relative uncertainty for this point is then:

    - $\Delta(|v_\theta|)_{\textrm{relative}}=0.1(1$ °C h$^{-1})=0.1$ °C h$^{-1}$

    For the data point at $\Delta\theta=70$°C, the vertical coordinate is $|v_\theta|=8$°C h$^{-1}$. The relative uncertainty for this point is then:

    - $\Delta(|v_\theta|)_{\textrm{relative}}=0.1(8$ °C h$^{-1})=0.8$ °C h$^{-1}$

    We must then represent the corresponding error bars with these lengths.

5. The data supports the student's suggestion. $\checkmark$

    A line that passes through the origin can be traced on the graph that passes through the error bars. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The student's suggestion is that the graph of $|v_\theta|$ against $\Delta\theta$ should be linear and passing through the origin (proportionality).

    The data supports this claim given that a line can be traced from the origin that passes (within the given uncertainties) through every point.
