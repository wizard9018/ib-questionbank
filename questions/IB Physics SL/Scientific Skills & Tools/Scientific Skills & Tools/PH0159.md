# PH0159

The mass and circumference of a standard volleyball are $270 \pm 10$ g and $66 \pm1$ cm respectively. What is the percentage uncertainty in the density of the ball calculated from these values?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $(\dfrac{10}{270} + (3)(\dfrac{1}{66})) \times 100\%$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $(3)(\dfrac{1}{66}) \times 100\%$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $(\dfrac{10}{270} - (3)(\dfrac{1}{66})) \times 100\%$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $(\dfrac{10}{270} - (\dfrac{1}{3})(\dfrac{1}{66})) \times 100\%$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Given, $C=2\pi r$

$\dfrac{\Delta r}{r}=\dfrac{\Delta C}{C} = \dfrac{1}{66}$

And knowing that $V=\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3$, the relative uncertainty on volume will be three times that of the radius

$\dfrac{\Delta V}{V} = 3 \dfrac{\Delta r}{r}=3(\dfrac{1}{66}) =(3)(\dfrac{1}{66})$

Using the equation for density,

$\rho =\dfrac{m}{V}$, the uncertainty is found by adding the relative uncertainties $\dfrac{\Delta \rho}{\rho}=\dfrac{\Delta m}{m}+\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}$

As percentage, that would be: $\dfrac{\Delta\rho}{\rho}=(\dfrac{\Delta m}{m}+\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}) \times 100\%$

$=(\dfrac{10}{270}+(3)(\dfrac{1}{66})) \times 100\%$
