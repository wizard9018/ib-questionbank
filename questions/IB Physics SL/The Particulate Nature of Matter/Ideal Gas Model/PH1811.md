# PH1811

The volume of an ideal gas decreases at constant temperature, with no change in the number of gas molecules.

Below are three statements about the gas:

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The average speed of the particles increases. |
  | II. | The frequency of collisions between particles and the container walls increases. |
  | III. | The pressure of the gas increases. |

Which of the statements are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

**I is incorrect:**

Absolute temperature is directly proportional to the kinetic energy of the molecules. The temperature of the gas remains constant, so $E_k$ and hence the speed remains constant.

**II is correct:**

There is less space for particles to move inside the container so collisions with the walls occur more frequently.

**III is correct:**

For an ideal gas we have:

- $PV=Nk_BT$

Where $P$ is the pressure of the gas, $V$ is its volume, $T$ is its absolute temperature, $N$ is the number of molecules, and $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant.

For constant $N$ and $T$ we have:

- $P\propto\dfrac{1}{V}$

 therefore a decrease in volume implies an increase in pressure.
