# PH0479

**Maximum mark: 8**

1. Distinguish between an ideal gas and a non-ideal gas with reference to the internal energy of the molecules. [2 marks]

2. An ideal gas of mass 20 g is heated in a sealed container. The temperature of the gas is increased from $127\degree$ C to $327\degree$ C. The specific heat capacity of the gas at constant volume is $2.5 \times 10^4\,\ce{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$.

    1. Calculate the increase in total kinetic energy of the molecules of the ideal gas. [3 marks]

    2. The average speeds of the molecules at $127\degree$ C and $327\degree$ C  are $v_i$ and $v_f$ respectively. Calculate $\dfrac{v_i}{v_f}$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Internal energy of the ideal gas = total kinetic energy of the molecules $\checkmark$

    Internal energy of the non-ideal gas = total kinetic energy of the molecules + total potential energy of the molecules $\checkmark$

2. 

    1. Mention of heat supplied = increase in internal energy = increase in total kinetic energy of the molecules of the ideal gas $\checkmark$

        Usage of $Q=mc\Delta T$ $\checkmark$

        $\ll 20 \times 10^{-3}\times 2.5\times 10^4\times 200=\gg$ :answer[$1.0\times 10^5\, \ce{J}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The change in temperature ∆T;

        $\Delta T=327-127=200\degree$ C

        The heat supplied to the gas;

        $Q=mc\Delta T=(20\times 10^{-3})(2.5\times 10^4)(200)=1.0\times 10^5$ J

        Since internal energy increase will increase only the kinetic energy of the ideal gas. 

        The increase in total kinetic energy of the ideal gas is $1.0\times 10^5$ J

    

    2. $127°C=\ll 127+273=\gg 400$ K 

        $327°C=\ll 327+273=\gg 600$ K $\checkmark$

        $\overline{\ll E}_k=\dfrac{1}{2} m\overline{v^2}=\dfrac{3}{2} k_b T\gg$    

        $\overline{v} \propto \sqrt{T}$ $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{v_i}{v_f} =\ll \dfrac{\sqrt{400}}{\sqrt{600}} = \gg $ :answer[0.82] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The temperatures are in Kelvin;

        $127\degree \ce{C}=127+273=400$ K

        $327\degree \ce{C}=327+273=600$ K

        The average kinetic energy $\overline{E}_k$ per molecule of a gas;

        $\overline{E}_k=\dfrac{1}{2} m\overline{v^2}=\dfrac{3}{2} k_b T \hspace{2em}$ where $\dfrac{3}{2} k_b$ = constant and $\frac{1}{2} m$ = constant

        So;

        $\overline{v^2} \propto T = \overline{v}\propto \sqrt{T}$

        

        According to this relationship;

        $\dfrac{\overline{v}}{\sqrt{T}}$ = constant

        When the given values are substituted;

        $\dfrac{v_i}{\sqrt{400}}$ = constant = $\dfrac{v_f}{\sqrt{600}}$

        $\dfrac{v_i}{v_f} = \dfrac{\sqrt{400}}{\sqrt{600}}=0.82$
