# PH0753

Container 1 contains $128$ g of oxygen ($32$ g mol$^{-1}$) and Container 2 contains $56$ g of nitrogen ($28$ g mol$^{-1}$). The graph shows how the pressure $P$ inside each container varies with volume $V$ at constant temperature ($R$ is the molar gas constant).

![PH0753xyx](images/PH0753xyx.png)

Which line of the table gives the temperature inside the two containers?

$\\$

| | Temperature of Container 1 / K | Temperature of Container 2 / K  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| 100 | 100 |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 200 | 50 |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 200 | 200 |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 400 | 200 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From the ideal gas law, one obtains the equation

$\hspace{1em}$ $P=nT\dfrac{R}{V}$

Hence, the gradient of the graph can be expressed as\
$\hspace{1em}$ $gradient=nT$

For Container 1,

$\hspace{1em}$ $gradient=\dfrac{20000}{50}\ \ce{K}\ \ce{mol}=400$ K mol

$\hspace{1em}$ $n=\dfrac{mass}{molar\ mass}=\dfrac{128\ \ce{g}}{32\ \ce{g\ {mol}}^{-1}}=4$ mol

$\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore\ T=\dfrac{gradient}{n}=100$ K

For Container 2,

$\hspace{1em}$ $gradient=\dfrac{12000}{60}\ \ce{K}\ \ce{mol}=200$ K mol

$\hspace{1em}$ $n=\dfrac{mass}{molar\ mass}=\dfrac{56\ \ce{g}}{28\ \ce{g}\ \ce{{mol}}^{-1}}=2\ \ce{mol}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore\ T=\dfrac{gradient}{n}=100$ K

(If one mistakenly switches the $n$ values of the two containers, answer **B** is obtained.

Answer **C** results from miscalculating both gradients by a factor of two.

Finally, wrongly assuming that both containers contain one mole of gas yields answer **D**.)
