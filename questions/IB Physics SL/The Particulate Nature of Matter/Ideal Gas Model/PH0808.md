# PH0808

**Maximum mark: 11**

The inside face of an ideal gas piston is coated in an organic film (molar mass $240\ \ce{g\ mol^{-1}}$) of negligible volume that sublimes at $200\degree$ C with a specific latent heat of vaporization of $70.0\ \ce{\ce{k}J\ mol^{-1}}$. The piston has a cross-sectional area of $100\ \ce{cm^2}$ and a mass of 1.00 kg. The gas cylinder has an initial internal length $y$ of 1.0 m and it contains gas at an initial pressure of 101 kPa held at the sublimation temperature. The pressure of the surrounding air is 100 kPa. The forces on the piston are initially in equilibrium.

![PH0808aab](images/PH0808aab.png)

1. Calculate how many moles of gas are contained in the piston prior to sublimation. [2 marks]

2.
    1. Show that the initial mass of the organic film is approximately 19 g. [3 marks]

    2. Estimate the number of moles initially contained within the organic film. [2 marks]

3. Power is supplied to the film at a rate of 10 W.

    Estimate the time required for the film to completely sublime. [2 marks]

4. As a consequence of the sublimation, the pressure inside the cylinder remains 101 kPa and the temperature is 473 K.

    Estimate the final equilibrium value of $y$. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{gas}=\dfrac{PAy}{RT}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{gas}=0.257$ mol $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
According to the ideal gas law,

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{gas}=\dfrac{PV}{RT}=\dfrac{PAy}{RT}$

    Inserting the values given yields:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{gas}=\dfrac{\left({101\times10}^3\ \ce{Pa}\right)\left({10}^{-2}\ \ce{m}^2\right)\left(1.0\ \ce{m}\right)}{\left(8.31\ \ce{J\ K^{-1}}{\ \ce{mol}}^{-1}\right)\left(473\ \ce{K}\right)}=0.257$ mol

2.
    1. $\hspace{1em}$ $Gravitational\ force=\left(m_{film}+m_{piston}\right)g$ $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        $\hspace{1em}$ $Gravitational\ force=\left(m_{film}+1.00\ \ce{kg}\right)\left(9.81\ \ce{m\ s}^{-2}\right)$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $Upward\ force=\left(P-P_0\right)A$ $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        $\hspace{1em}$ $Upward\ force=10$ N

        $\hspace{1em}$ $m_{film}=\dfrac{\left(P-P_0\right)A}{g}-m_{piston}$

        $\hspace{3.8em}$ $=\dfrac{10\ N}{9.81\ \ce{m\ s^{-2}}}-1.00\ \ce{kg}\approx$ :answer[$19\ \ce{g}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
As the forces are in equilibrium, the weight of the film plus the force exerted by surrounding air pressure must equal the upward force applied by the ideal gas. Hence,

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(m_{film}+m_{piston}\right)g+P_0A=PA$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore\ m_{film}=\dfrac{\left(P-P_0\right)A}{g}-m_{piston}$

        Inserting the values given yields:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $m_{film}=\dfrac{\left({10}^3\ \ce{Pa}\right)\left({10}^{-2}\ \ce{m}^2\right)}{9.81\ \ce{m\ s^}{-2}}-1.00\ \ce{kg}=0.01937\ \ce{kg}\approx19$ g

    2. $\hspace{1em}$ $moles=\dfrac{mass}{molar\ mass}$  $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $moles=\dfrac{19.37\ \ce{g}}{240\ \ce{g\ {mol}^{-1}}}=$ :answer[$80.7\ \ce{mmol}$] $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        $\hspace{1em}$ $moles\approx\dfrac{19\ \ce{g}}{240\ \ce{g}\ \ce{{mol}}^{-1}}\approx80$ mmol

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
The number of moles in the film is defined as

        $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{film}=\dfrac{m_{film}}{molar\ mass}$

        Inserting the mass value obtained from part b(i) yields

        $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{film}=\dfrac{19.37\ \ce{g}}{240\ \ce{g\ {mol}}^{-1}}=80.71$ mmol

3. $\hspace{1em}$ $(Power)(t)\ =n_{film}L$ $\checkmark$

    **OR**

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(10\right)t=\left(0.0801\ \ce{mol}\right)(70\times{10}^3\ \ce{Jmol}^{-1})$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t=$ :answer[$565$ s] $\checkmark$

    **OR**

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t\approx560$ s

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
From the definition of molar latent heat of vaporization, it follows that the energy involved can be expressed as

    $\hspace{1em}$ $E=Pt$

    And also by

    $\hspace{1em}$ $E=nL$

    Where in this case the latent heat is expressed in terms of moles instead of mass. Setting the equations equal to one another gives

    $\hspace{1em}$ $Pt=nL$

    Substituting the known values then gives

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(10\right)t=\left(0.0801\ \ce{mol}\right)(70\times{10}^3\ \ce{Jmol^{-1}})$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t=565$ s

    A more approximate value of 560 s (to two significant figures) is obtained if *the values* are expressed to three significant figures in the calculation rather than four.

4. $\hspace{1em}$ $P=\dfrac{\left(n_{gas}+n_{film}\right)RT}{Ay}$

    **OR**

    $\hspace{1em}$ $101\ \times{10}^3=\dfrac{\left(0.338\ \ce{mol}\right)\left(8.31\ \ce{J\ K^{-1}}\ \ce{{mol}^{-1}}\right)\left(473\ \ce{K}\right)}{\left(0.01\ \ce{m}^2\right)y}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $y=1.32=$ :answer[$1.3$ m] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
From the ideal gas law

    $\hspace{1em}$ $PV=nRT$

    The total number of moles of gas

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{total}=n_{gas}+n_{organic}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{total}=\left(0.257+0.081\right)=0.338$ mol

    The volume is given by the expression

    $\hspace{1em}$ $V=Ay$

    Therefore

    $\hspace{1em}$ $PAy=nRT$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $y=\dfrac{nRT}{PA}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $y=\dfrac{\left(0.338\right)\left(8.314\right)\left(473\right)}{\left(101000\right)\left(0.01\right)}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $y=1.32=1.3$ m
