# PH0645

**Maximum mark: 11**

1. Outline how a gas exerts pressure on a surface.  [3 marks]

2.  1. A balloon containing helium gas is released from the ground. The pressure exerted by the helium is approximately equal to the pressure exerted by surrounding air. The balloon expands at constant temperature as it rises.

        ![PH0645](images/PH0645.png)
        $\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with chemix - https://chemix.org/]}}$

        The balloon contains 16 g of helium (molar mass $4.0\, \ce{g\, mol^{-1}}$), has a temperature $T$ of 300 K and a pressure $P_0$ of 100 kPa at ground level.

        Calculate the volume $V_0$ of the balloon at ground level. [2 marks]

    2. At heights much less than 10 km, the pressure $P$ inside the balloon is      related to its height $h$ (in m) by the approximation

        $P\approx P_0\left(1-\dfrac{h}{10\ \ce{km}}\right)$

        The balloon ascends until its volume is 5% higher than the volume at ground level.

        Estimate the new height of the balloon. [3 marks]

    3.  The same balloon at 100 kPa is placed inside a sealed, rigid container of volume $3V_0$ and a temperature of 300 K. A pump is used to remove air from the container. When the air remaining in the container consists of half as many molecules as the number of molecules inside the balloon, the balloon bursts.

        Determine the pressure inside the container at the moment the balloon bursts. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Gas molecules colliding with a surface experience a change in momentum. $\checkmark$

    The change in momentum is caused by a force from the walls of the balloon. $\checkmark$

    By Newton's 3rd law, the particles also exert a force on the walls of the balloon, resulting in pressure. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The kinetic theory of gases models gas molecules as hard spheres of negligible volume that undergo elastic collisions, resulting in a pressure on any surface that is in contact with the gas.

2.  1.  $n=\dfrac{Mass}{Molar\ mass}=\dfrac{16\ \ce{g}}{4\ \ce{g}\ \ce{{mol}}^{-1}}=4\ \ce{mol}\ $ $\checkmark$

        $V=\dfrac{nRT}{P}=\dfrac{(4\ \ce{mol})(8.31\, \ce{J}\, \ce{K^{-1}}\ \ce{{mol}^{-1}})(300\ \ce{K})}{{10}^5\, \ce{Pa}}=$ :answer[$0.10\ \ce{m}^3$]   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The molar quantity $n$ of helium is given by

        -  $n=\dfrac{Mass}{Molar\ mass}=\dfrac{16\ \ce{g}}{4\ \ce{g}\ \ce{{mol}}^{-1}}=4$ mol

        Substituting this value into the ideal gas law yields

        $V_0=\dfrac{nRT}{P_0}=\dfrac{(4\ \ce{mol})(8.31\ \ce{J}\ \ce{K^{-1}}\ \ce{{mol}}^{-1})(300\ \ce{K})}{{10}^5\ \ce{Pa}}=0.10\ \ce{m}^3$

    2.  ::indent
        - $\dfrac{V}{V_0}=\dfrac{P_0}{P}$ $\checkmark$

        - $1-\dfrac{h}{10000\ \ce{m}}=\dfrac{1}{1.05}$ $\checkmark$

        - $h=$ :answer[$476\, \ce{m}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
         From the ideal gas law it follows that when the number of moles $n$ and temperature remain constant,

        - $PV$ = constant

        - $PV=P_0V_0$
        - $\dfrac{V}{V_0}=\dfrac{P_0}{P}$

        - $\dfrac{P_0}{P}=\dfrac{V}{V_0}=\ \dfrac{1}{\left(1-\dfrac{h}{10000}\right)}$

        Therefore

        - $V_0=V\left(1-\dfrac{h}{10000}\right)$

        And from the given information

        - $\dfrac{V_0}{V}=\dfrac{1}{1.05}$

        - $1-\dfrac{h}{10000\ \ce{m}}=\dfrac{1}{1.05}\ $

        - $h=(10000)\dfrac{0.05}{1.05}=\dfrac{500}{1.05}=476$ m

3.  ::indent
    - $P_{final}=\dfrac{n_{total}RT}{3V_0}$ $\checkmark$

    - $n_{total}=\dfrac{1}{RT}\left(P_0V_0+\dfrac{P_0V_0}{2}\right)=\dfrac{3P_0V_0}{2RT}$ $\checkmark$

    - $P_{final}=\dfrac{P_0}{2}=$ :answer[$50\, \ce{\ce{k}Pa}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Calculate the final pressure by first determining the moles of gas present in the container.

    The number of moles of gas in the balloon at the start is given by

    - $n_b=\dfrac{p_{0\ }V_0}{RT}$

    It is also known that the number of moles of gas that surround the balloon when it bursts is half the number of moles of gas inside the balloon

    - $n_{c\ }=\dfrac{1}{2}n_b=\dfrac{p_{0}V_0}{2RT}$

    Giving

    - $n_{total}=n_b+n_{c\ }=\dfrac{3P_0V_0}{2RT}$

    It is known that the total volume of the gas in the container is $3V_0$, therefore

    - $P_{final}=\dfrac{n_{total}RT}{3V_0}$

    - $=(\dfrac{3P_0V_0}{2RT})(\dfrac{RT}{3V_0})$

    - $=\dfrac{P_0}{2}=\ 50$ kPa
