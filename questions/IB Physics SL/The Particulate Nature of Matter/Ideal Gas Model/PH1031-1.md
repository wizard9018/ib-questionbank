# PH1031-1

**Maximum mark: 5**

1. Almost 100% of Venus' atmosphere is composed of carbon dioxide. The density of the atmosphere on the surface of Venus is around $65\,\ce{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and the molar mass of carbon dioxide is $44\,\ce{g\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$. Show that the molar density of the atmosphere on the surface of Venus is around $1500\,\ce{{mol}\,m^{-3}}$. [1 mark]

2. The average temperature on the surface of Venus is around $470\,\degree$C. Assuming that Venus' atmosphere behaves like an ideal gas, estimate Venus' surface atmospheric pressure. [2 marks]

3. State two of the conditions for which a real gas approximates an ideal gas. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\dfrac{65}{44\times 10^{-3}}=1480\,\text{mol}\,\ce{m}^{-3}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        First, we must convert mass per unit mol into SI units.

    - $44\,\ce{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}=44(10^{-3}\,\ce{kg})\text{mol}^{-1}=0.044\,\ce{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

    - $\dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}\div \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{mol}}=\dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}\times \dfrac{\text{mol}}{\text{mass}}=\dfrac{\text{mol}}{\text{volume}}$

    (We can also do a similar analysis with units to find that $\dfrac{\ce{kg\,m^{-3}}}{\ce{kg\,\text{mol}^{-1}}}$  gives us the correct units)

    - $\dfrac{65\,\ce{kg\,m^{-3}}}{0.044\,\ce{kg\,\text{mol}^{-1}}}=1477\, \text{mol} \,\ce{m}^{-3} $

2. ::indent
    - $PV=nRT \hspace{1em}$ and $\hspace{1em}470\degree \ce{C} = 743$ K $\checkmark$

    - $\dfrac{n}{V}=1480$

    - $P=\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{V}\Bigr)(R)(T)=(1480)(8.31)(743)=9.1\times 10^6\,\ce{Pa}$ $\checkmark$

    (Award full marks for use of $\dfrac{n}{V}=1500$ giving $9.3\times 10^6\,\ce{Pa}$)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    - $PV=nRT$

    Rearranging for $P$ gives

    - $P=\dfrac{nRT}{V}=\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{V}\Bigr)(R)(T)$

    - $\dfrac{n}{V}$ is moles per unit volume $= 1480\,\text{mol }\,\ce{m}^{-3}$ (calculated in part (a))

    - $R$ is the molar gas constant $= 8.31\, \ce{J \,K^{-1}}$ (given in your formula book)

    - $T$ is absolute temperature $470\degree$C= $470\,\ce{K}+273\,\ce{K} =743\,\ce{K}$

    - $P=\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{V}\Bigr)(R)(T)=(1480)(8.31)(743)=9.1\times 10^6$ Pa

3. Any two from:

    Low pressure.   $\checkmark$ \
    Low density.   $\checkmark$ \
    High temperature. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    A real gas approximates to an ideal gas at conditions of low pressure, high temperature, and low density.

    The data we are given allows us to accurately predict the pressure on Venus by assuming it is an ideal gas.
