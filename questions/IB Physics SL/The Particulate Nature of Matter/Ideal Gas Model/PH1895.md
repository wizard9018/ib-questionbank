# PH1895

A sample of $\ce{^{24}_{12}Mg}$ has the same mass as a sample of $\ce{^{56}_{26}Fe}$.

What is the ratio

 $\hspace{8em}\dfrac{\textrm{number of moles in }\ce{^{24}_{12}Mg}\textrm{ sample}}{\textrm{number of moles in }\ce{^{56}_{26}Fe}\textrm{ sample}}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3}{7}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{6}{13}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{13}{6}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{7}{3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The number of moles in a substance $n$ is given by:

- $n=\dfrac{\textrm{mass}}{\textrm{mass number}}$

- mass $=n\times$ mass number

The mass number of $\ce{^{56}_{26}Fe}$ is 56 and the mass number of $\ce{^{24}_{12}Mg}$ is 24.

The mass of each sample is the same, so we have:

- $\begin{aligned}
\ 56n_{Fe}&=24n_{Mg}\\
\\\
\ 56&=24\dfrac{n_{Mg}}{n_{Fe}}\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{n_{Mg}}{n_{Fe}}&=\dfrac{56}{24}=\dfrac{7}{3}\\
\end{aligned}$
