# PH0647

**Maximum mark: 12**

Water in a boiling flask is heated from 20$\degree$C by a constant input power $P_{in}$. The resulting steam exits the flask via a tube with an internal cross-sectional area of $30\,\ce{cm}^2$.

![PH0647a](images/PH0647a.png)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with chemix - https://chemix.org/]}}$

1. Discuss the changes that take place in the kinetic and potential energies of the water as it boils. [2 marks]

2. Estimate the mean velocity of water molecules (mass $18.0u$) in the steam. Assume that steam behaves as an ideal gas. [2 marks]

3.
    1. The burner is adjusted so that $P_{in}$ equals 5.0 kW. The steam pressure is approximately 100 kPa.

        Determine the average density $\rho$ of the steam. [3 marks]

    2. Show that the velocity $v$ of the steam through the outlet tube can be expressed as

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{1}{A \rho}\ \dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$

        where $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the outlet tube and $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$ is the mass flow rate of the steam. [2 marks]

    3. The specific latent heat of vaporization is $2.3 \ \ce{MJ \ kg^{-1}}$.

        Calculate the average flow speed of the steam as it exits the outlet tube. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    Kinetic energy of the water molecules stays constant during boiling $\checkmark$

    Potential energy increases as water changes from liquid to gas $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The kinetic energy of water molecules is directly proportional to the water temperature, which remains constant during boiling as all the energy supplied to the water is converted to an increase in intermolecular potential energy.

    The intermolecular potential energy increases during boiling due to the increased mean distance between water molecules, where the energy is being used to overcome intermolecular forces.

2.  $\hspace{1em} v=\sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{m}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $v = $ :answer[$719\ \ce{m\, s^{-1}}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Setting the molar translational kinetic energy of the water equal to the molar thermal energy of translational modes yields

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=\dfrac{3}{2}k_BT$

    where $m$ is the mass, $v$ is velocity, $T$ is temperature (373 K) and $k_B$ is Boltzmann’s constant. Rearranging this equation gives

    $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{3k_BT}{m}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3\left(1.38 \times {10}^{-23}\ \ce{J\, K^{-1}}\right)\left(373\ \ce{K}\right)}{18.0\left(1.661 \times {10}^{-27}\, \ce{kg}\right)}}=719\,\ce{m\, s^{-1}}$

3.
    1.  $\hspace{1em} \rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$ and $n=1$ $m = 18$  g $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $V\ =\dfrac{nRT}{p}$

        **OR**

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\rho=\dfrac{p}{nRT}(m)\ =\ \dfrac{(100\ 000)(18)}{(1)(8.314)(373)}$ $\checkmark$

        Giving

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\rho=$ :answer[$581\ \ce{g\ m^{-3}}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$

        Density = $\dfrac{mass}{volume}$

        We know from the fact that one molecule has a mass of 18 $u$ that the mass of one mole of steam is 18 $g$

        Using the ideal gas law
$pV\ =\ nRT$

        We can solve for volume to get
$V=\dfrac{nRT}{p}$

        Solving for density we get

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}=\dfrac{m}{\dfrac{nRT}{p}}=\dfrac{mp}{nRT}=\dfrac{(18)(100000)}{(1)(8.314)(373)}=581\ \ce{g\ m^{-3}}$

        *Alternative solution*

        The density $\rho$ of the steam can be calculated using the ideal gas law:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\rho=M_r\dfrac{n}{V}=M_r\dfrac{p}{RT}$

        where $M_r$ is the molar mass of water, $R$ is the molar gas constant, $p$ is pressure and $T$ is temperature.

        Substituting the given values into this expression for density yields

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\rho=\dfrac{\left(18.0\ \ce{g\ {mol}}^{-1}\right)\left({10}^5\ \ce{Pa}\right)\ }{\left(8.31\ \ce{J\ {K^{-1}}\ mol}^{-1}\right)\left(373\ \ce{K}\right)}=581\ \ce{g\ m^{-3}}$

    2. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{\rho} \dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = A \ v$
$\checkmark \hspace{1em} $
 giving$\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{1}{A\rho}\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In a given time interval $\Delta t$, the volume $\Delta V$ of steam that flows through the outlet tube is equal to the cross-sectional area $A$ of the tube and the distance $x$ covered by the steam in that time. As $x$ is given by:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $x = v \Delta t$

        and

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta V = Ax$

        it follows that

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = Av$

        Density $\rho$ is defined as the mass per unit volume; hence, volume flow rate can also be expressed as

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{1}{\rho} \dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$

        Combining these expressions yields
$v=\dfrac{1}{A\rho}\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$

    3. ::indent
        - $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{P_{\text{in}}}{L}=2.17 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{kg s}^{-1}\quad \checkmark$

        - $v=\dfrac{1}{A\rho}\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{1}{(30 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2)(581 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{kg m}^{-3})}(2.17 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{kg s}^{-1})\quad \checkmark$

        - $\hspace{1em}$ $v=$ :answer[$1.2\, \ce{m\, s^{-1}}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Since the energy $E$ required to boil a mass $m$ of water with specific latent heat capacity $L$ is given by

        - $E=mL$

        And energy input can also be determined from

        - $E=P t$

        it follows that

        - $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{P_{\text{in}}}{L}$

        Recalling the derived expression for $v$ where all variables are known

        - $v=\dfrac{1}{A\rho}\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$

        Substituting gives

        - $v=\dfrac{1}{A\rho}\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{1}{(30\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2)(581\times10^{-3}\ \text{kg m}^{-3})}(2.17\times10^{-3}\ \text{kg s}^{-1})$

        - $v=1.2\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
