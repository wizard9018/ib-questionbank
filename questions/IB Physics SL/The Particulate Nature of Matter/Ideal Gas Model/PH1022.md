# PH1022

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. A closed glass vessel with 5.0 L internal volume initially contains 0.20 moles of a reactive, monatomic ideal gas of molar mass $50.0\, \ce{g\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$. Inside the vessel, a plate is suspended from a spring scale. A pressure gauge is used to continuously measure the pressure $P$ inside the vessel.

    ![image](images/image.png)

    Calculate the initial gas density inside the vessel. Assume that the volume occupied by the catalyst and spring scale is negligible. [2 marks]

2. Over time the gas reacts with a substance on the metal plate to form a solid. From the spring scale measurement, the density $\rho$ of gas remaining in the vessel is calculated.

    The graph shows how $\dfrac{P}{\rho}$ changes with time $t$.

    ![image](images/image.png)

    1. Show that the gradient of the graph is proportional to the rate of change of temperature $T$ inside the vessel, following the equation

    $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=(0.166\,\ce{J\,g^{-1}\,K^{-1}})T$ [3 marks]

    2. Determine whether the reaction causes the temperature of the gas to increase or decrease over time. [2 marks]

    3. Estimate the final temperature inside the vessel. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $m_0=nM_r=10$ g  $\checkmark$

    ${\rho}_0=\dfrac{m_0}{V}=0.002\,\ce{g\,cm^{-3}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The initial mass $m_0$ of gas is related to its molar mass $M_r$ and number of moles $n$ via the equation

    - $m_0=nM_r=(0.20\, \text{mol})(50.0\,\ce{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1})=10.0$ g

    The initial density ${\rho}_0$ is in turn given by

    - ${\rho}_0=\dfrac{m_0}{V}=\dfrac{10.0\,\ce{g}}{5000\,\ce{cm^3}}=0.002\,\ce{g\,cm^{-3}}$

    The volume $V$ has been set equal to 5.0 L because the volume of the solid constituents of the vessel (the catalyst and spring scale) is stated to be negligible.

2. 1. $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{v^2}{3}$   $\checkmark$

        $v^2=\dfrac{3RT}{M_r}$    $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{RT}{M_r} =\dfrac{8.31\,\ce{J}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\ce{K}^{-1}}{50.0\,\ce{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}T=(0.166\,\ce{J\,g^{-1}\,K^{-1}})T$  $\checkmark$

        OR

        $PV=nRT$  $\checkmark$

        $V=\dfrac{nM_r}{\rho}$   $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{RT}{M_r} = \dfrac{8.31\,\ce{J}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\ce{K}^{-1}}{50.0\,\ce{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}T=(0.166\,\ce{J g^{-1}\,K}^{-1})T$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        From the ideal gas law, pressure $P$, gas density $\rho$ and mean molecular velocity $v$ are related through the expression

        - $P=\dfrac{1}{3}\rho v^2$

        - $\therefore \dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{v^2}{3}$

        In addition, the internal energy $U$ of a monatomic is given by

        - $U=\dfrac{3}{2}nRT$

        The internal energy is also the total kinetic energy of the particles in the gas, giving

        - $U=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=\dfrac{1}{2}nM_r v^2$

        Where $nM_r$ is the mass of the particles in the gas. Combining the two equations gives

        - $U=\dfrac{3}{2}nRT=\dfrac{1}{2}nM_r v^2$

        - $\therefore v^2=\dfrac{3RT}{M_r}$

        Thus, $v^2$ can be substituted in the first equation to obtain

        - $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{RT}{M_r}$

        Alternatively, one may arrive to the same equation directly from the ideal gas law:

        - $PV=nRT$

        - $\dfrac{PV}{nM_r}=\dfrac{RT}{M_r}$

        Here, the final step follows from the definition used in part a,

        - $\rho=\dfrac{nM_r}{V}$

        Giving

        - $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{RT}{M_r}$

        And

        - $\dfrac{R}{M_r} = \dfrac{8.314}{50}=0.166$

    2. $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t} \propto \dfrac{\Delta (\frac{P}{\rho})}{\Delta t} > 0$  $\checkmark$

        Reaction causes a temperature rise  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In part b(i), an equation was derived relating $\dfrac{P}{\rho}$ to the gas temperature $T$:

        - $\dfrac{P}{\rho}=\dfrac{RT}{M_r}$

        Since $\dfrac{P}{\rho}$ increases with time $t$, it follows that the reaction causes an increase in temperature, as $T$ is directly proportional to $\dfrac{P}{\rho}$.

    3. $\dfrac{P}{{\rho}_{final}}=90\,\ce{J\,g^{-1}}$   $\checkmark$

        $T_{final}=\dfrac{M_r}{R}\Bigl(\dfrac{P}{{\rho}}\Bigr)_{final}$ $\checkmark$

        $T_{final}=$ :answer[542 K]  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        From the graph, the final value of $\dfrac{P}{\rho}$ is $90\,\ce{J\,g^{-1}}$. Thus, after rearranging the relation between $\dfrac{P}{\rho}$ and $T$, one obtains

        - $T_{final}=\dfrac{M_r}{R}\Bigl(\dfrac{P}{\rho}\Bigr)_{final}=\dfrac{(50.0\,\ce{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1})(90.0\,\ce{J\,g}^{-1})}{8.31\,\ce{J\,K^{-1}}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}=542$ K
