# PH0751

Two cylindrical ideal gas pistons of equal initial volume and pressure are compressed at constant temperature by a force $F$. The cross-sectional area of Piston **A** is two times the cross-sectional area of Piston **B**.

Which graph correctly shows how the inverse of the length $\dfrac{1}{L}$ of each piston changes with increasing $F$?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
As temperature is constant, the pressure $P$ for a given volume $V$ in each piston is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $P=\dfrac{P_0V_0}{V}=\dfrac{P_0V_0}{AL}$

where $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the piston, and $P_0$ and $V_0$ are the initial pressure and volume. The volume $V$ can be found from the product of the cross-sectional area $A$ and its length $L$.

The pressure is related to applied force by the equation:

$\hspace{1em}$ $P=P_0+\Delta P=P_0+\ \dfrac{F}{A}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore\ P_0+\dfrac{F}{A}=\dfrac{P_0V_0}{AL}$

Rearranging to make $\dfrac{1}{L}$ the subject yields:

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{L}=\dfrac{A}{P_0V_0}\left(\dfrac{F}{A}+P_0\right)$

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{L}=\dfrac{F}{P_0V_0}+\dfrac{A}{V_0}$

Thus, the gradient (given by the coefficient of $F$) is independent of $A$, whilst the y-intercept is directly proportional to $A$.

Hence, the graph for Piston A intercepts the y-axis at two times the value of the Piston B graph.

As the gradient is independent of $A$, the two scenarios exhibit the same rate of increase in $\dfrac{1}{L}$ with respect to $F$.
