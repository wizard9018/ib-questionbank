# PH0620

Systems A and B both consist of an ideal gas at initial temperature $T$. In System A, the temperature of the gas is increased. In System B, gas molecules are added with the temperature of the system remaining constant.

Which of these statements about the total intermolecular potential energy of the two systems is correct?

$\\$

| | System A | System B |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Potential energy increases$\hspace{1.5em}$ |$\hspace{1.5em}$Potential energy increases $\hspace{1.5em}$|
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Potential energy is constant | Potential energy increases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Potential energy is constant |Potential energy is constant |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Potential energy increases | Potential energy is constant |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Ideal gases have no intermolecular forces between the particles, and therefore all of their internal energy consists of kinetic energy. There is no intermolecular potential energy. In both cases, the described changes will not affect the potential energy, as the potential energy is always equal to zero.
