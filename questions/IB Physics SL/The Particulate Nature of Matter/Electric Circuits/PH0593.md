# PH0593

A wire carries electrical current. If $9.0 \times 10^{12}$ electrons pass a given point in the wire every 4.5 seconds, what is the current in the wire?

- A. $\hspace{1em} 2.0 \times 10^{-7}$ A

- B. $\hspace{1em} 3.2 \times 10^{-7}$ A

- C. $\hspace{1em} 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$ A

- D. $\hspace{1em} 2.0 \times 10^{12}$ A

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From the data booklet, current $I = \dfrac{\Delta q}{\Delta t}$.

The number of electrons passing the given point in the wire every second,

- $N_e=\dfrac{9\times{10}^{12}}{4.5}=2\times{10}^{12}$.

Each electron carries a charge of $-1.6\times{10}^{-19}$ C.

Therefore the magnitude of current

- $I=\left(2\times{10}^{12}\right)\left(\left|-1.6\times{10}^{-19}\ \ce{C}\right|\right)=3.2\times{10}^{-7}$ A.
