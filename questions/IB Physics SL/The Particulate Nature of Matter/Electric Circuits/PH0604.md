# PH0604

In the following graph, the relationship between voltage $V$ and current $I$ for two different resistors $R_A$ and $R_B$ is displayed.

![PH0604aa](images/PH0604aa.png)

Which of the following statements is correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ For both curves, the resistance can be found from the inverse of the gradient of the tangent at a point.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ The resistance of *A* is never equal to the resistance of *B*
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ The gradient of the line for *B* gives the resistance of $R_B$
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The resistance of *A* is always greater than or equal to the resistance of *B*.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Resistance at any measured current value equals $\dfrac{V}{I}$. Therefore to find the resistance for any given current, the actual values of $V$ and $I$ for that current must be used in the equation.

Because for any given value of *V* the current in *A* is greater than the current in *B*, the ratio of $\dfrac{V}{I}$ will never be equal (and the resistance of *A* is always less than that of *B*). Thus the correct answer is B.

Line *B* is a straight line through the origin, therefore the inverse of the gradient would give the correct resistance from the ratio $\dfrac{V}{I}$.

Note, firstly, that the gradient of the tangent would give units of $\dfrac{Amperes}{Volts}$, which are not equivalent to the unit of resistance. Also, if the axes were switched, the gradient of the graph would only be equivalent to the resistance if the resistance was constant (the graph was a straight line through the origin).
