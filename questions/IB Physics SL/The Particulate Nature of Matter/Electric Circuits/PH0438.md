# PH0438

**Maximum mark: 5**

The graph shows the variation of current with voltage of a filament.

![PH0438](images/PH0438.png)
[Source: Created with GeoGebra, - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]

This filament is used in a light bulb, and it is made of a long tungsten wire that is wound several times. The filament is heated up when current flows and produces light.

The following data are available for the filament.

$\begin{aligned}
\text{The diameter of tungsten wire} &= 6.0\,\mu \ce{m} \\
\text{The resistivity of tungsten when it produces light} &= 2.0\,\mu\Omega \,\ce{cm}
\end{aligned}$

1. State Ohm's law.  [1 mark]

2. The filament's normal operation in a light bulb is at a potential difference of 6.0 V.

    1. Show that the resistance of the filament at a potential difference of 6.0 V is $120\,\Omega$.  [1 mark]

    2. Calculate the length of the filament.  [1 mark]

The light bulb is connected to a $100.0\,\Omega$ resistor and a cell with emf $\varepsilon$ and negligible internal resistance.

![Ph0438-2](images/Ph0438-2.png)
[Source: Created with GeoGebra, by Tom Walsh - https://www.geogebra.org/m/JbAKa82Y]

3. Calculate the required emf of the cell $\varepsilon$ to operate the bulb at the normal conditions of the filament.   [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The resistance of a conductor is constant / current proportional to potential difference if its temperature is constant. $\checkmark$

    (No mark is awarded for just “V = IR” or equivalent)

2.  1. $R = $:answer[$\dfrac{6}{50\times 10^{-3}}\,$] $\checkmark$

        $\ll R =120\,\Omega \gg\,$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        From the graph, when the potential difference is 6 V, the current is $50\,\ce{m} \ce{A}$. So,

        $R=\dfrac{V}{I} \rightarrow R=\dfrac{6}{50\times 10^{-3}}=120\,\Omega$

    2. $L =  \dfrac{(120)(\pi)(3\times 10^{-6})^2}{2\times 10^{-8}} $

        L = :answer[0.17 m ] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        $\begin{aligned}
        R &= \rho \dfrac{L}{A} \\[9pt]
        L &= \dfrac{RA}{\rho} = \dfrac{(120)(\pi)(3\times 10^{-6})^2}{2\times 10^{-8}}=0.17\, \ce{m}
        \end{aligned}$

3. $\varepsilon = (100)(50\times10^{-3})+6\,\checkmark$

    $\varepsilon = $:answer[11 V] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    The emf of the cell is the sum of the voltages of the resistors and the light bulb.
    For normal operation conditions of the filament mentioned in (b)(i), we can read off the graph that at 6V, the current is 50 mA.

    The bulb and the resistor are connected in series, which means they have the same current.
    So,

    $\varepsilon = (I)(R)+6 = (100)(50\times10^{-3})+6 = 11$ V
