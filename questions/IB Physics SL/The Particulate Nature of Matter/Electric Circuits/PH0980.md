# PH0980

The diagram below shows a circuit containing a cell, a voltmeter, a $6.0\, \Omega$ resistor and an open switch.

![image](images/image.png)

The reading on the voltmeter decreases by 20% when the switch is closed. What is the internal resistance of the cell?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.2\,\Omega$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.5\,\Omega$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $4.5\,\Omega$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $4.8\,\Omega$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

- $\varepsilon=V_T+Ir$

Where $V_T$ is the terminal potential difference, also the potential difference across the external circuit, and $Ir$ is the potential difference across the internal resistor.

When the switch is open the current in the circuit is zero (or so small that it is effectively zero - some current must be flowing for the voltmeter to give a reading). Therefore,

- $\varepsilon= V_T$

The reading on the voltmeter will be equal to the emf of the cell.

When the switch is closed, the terminal potential difference is 20% less than the emf of the cell.

- $V_T=\varepsilon-0.2\varepsilon=0.8\varepsilon$

Now

- $\varepsilon=V_T + Ir$

- $\varepsilon=0.8\varepsilon+Ir$

- $Ir=0.2\varepsilon$

Potential will drop across internal and external resistors in proportion to their resistances. So

- Internal resistance : External resistance $= 0.2\varepsilon:0.8\varepsilon=0.2:0.8=1:4=1.5:6$

- Internal resistance $= 1.5\,\Omega$
