# PH0602

The circuit below consists of a cell and two identical resistors in parallel, with an ideal ammeter in one of the branches. The current in this branch is $I_1$ and the current in the other branch is $I_2$.

![PH0602](images/PH0602.png)

If the ideal ammeter is replaced by a real ammeter, what will be the effect on currents $I_1$ and $I_2$?

$\\$

| | $I_1$ | $I_2$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Decreases $\hspace{1.5em}$| Decreases |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increases | $\hspace{1.5em}$Stays the same $\hspace{1.5em}$|
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Decreases | Increases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Decreases | Stays the same |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When the ammeter is ideal, it has zero resistance.

Kirchhoff\'s loop rule indicates that $I_1=\dfrac{V}{R}$ and $I_2=\dfrac{V}{R}$.

When the ideal ammeter is replaced with a real one, the ammeter has a non-zero resistance $R_a$.

Because the loop including $I_2$ is unchanged, it is still true that $I_2=\dfrac{V}{R}$.

Now, however, $I_1=\dfrac{V}{R+R_a}$, which is less than before.
