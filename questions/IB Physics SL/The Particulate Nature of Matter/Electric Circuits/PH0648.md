# PH0648

**Maximum mark: 7**

Many conducting wires are fastened together in a bundle to make a cable. Each wire is made of copper, with density 9.0 g cm$^{-3}$, has a diameter of 1.5 mm, and a mass of 400 kg.

1. Show that the length of the cable is approximately 25 km. [2 marks]

2.
    1. Each meter of the cable has a mass of $1.7\, $kg. Determine the number of wires that make up the cable. [2 marks]

    2. State a reason why the cable is made of many smaller wires instead of one big one. [1 mark]

    3. Each wire in the cable has a resistance of $55\, \Omega$. If the cable carries $750\,$A, calculate the electrical power lost in each kilometer of cable. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1em}$ $L=\dfrac{m}{\pi r^2\rho}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $L=\dfrac{400\ \ce{kg}}{\pi(0.75 \times  {10}^{-3}\ \ce{m})^2(9.0 \times {10}^3\, \dfrac{\ce{kg}}{\ce{m}^3})}$ = :answer[$25.1\ \ce{km}$]
$\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    Density $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$ and for each cylindrical wire, $V=\pi\ r^2L$.

    Therefore $L=\dfrac{m}{\pi r^2\rho}$.

    Substituting in quantities in base SI units,

    $\hspace{1em}$ $L=\dfrac{400\ \ce{kg}}{\pi(0.75\ \times \ {10}^{-3}\ \ce{m})^2(9.0\ \times {10}^3\dfrac{\ce{kg}}{\ce{m}^3})}$

    $\hspace{1.7em}$ $=25163\ $m

    $\hspace{1.7em}$ $=\ 25.1\ $km

         $\hspace{1em}$ $N=\dfrac{1.7\ \frac{\ce{kg}}{\ce{m}}}{0.016\ \frac{\ce{kg}}{\ce{m}}}=$ :answer[$106$] $\checkmark$

    2. *Any one of the following:*

        - - Many wires make the cable **more flexible** $\checkmark$
        - - Many wires make the cable **more durable/stronger**
        - - If one small wire fails, others can continue to carry the current

    3. $R_{1km}=\dfrac{55\ \Omega}{(\ce{25} \,\ce{km})(106)}=$ $0.021\ \Omega$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $P_{1km}=I^2R_{1km}=$ :answer[$11.7\ \ce{k}\ce{W}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The resistance of 1 km of the one wire

        $\hspace{1em}$ $R_{1km}=\dfrac{55\ \Omega}{(\ce{25}\ \ce{km})}=2.2\ \Omega$

        There are 106 wires in parallel. To find the resistance of the parallel wires, we divide the resistance of one wire by the number of wires, giving the resistance of 1 km of cable to be

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2.2\ \Omega}{106}=0.0208\ \Omega$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $P_{1km}=I^2R_{1km}=(750\, \ce{A})^2\left(0.0208\, \Omega\right)=11.7\, $kW
