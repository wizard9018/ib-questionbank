# PH1898

A cell has an internal resistance of 1.0 $\Omega$ and an emf of 15 V.

When a resistor of resistance $R$ is connected across the terminals of the cell, the terminal potential difference is 10 V.

Determine the resistance $R$.

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 1.0 $\Omega$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 2.0 $\Omega$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 3.0 $\Omega$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 4.0 $\Omega$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The electromotive force of the cell is given by:

- $\varepsilon=I(R+r)$

The equation can also be written as:

- $\varepsilon=IR+Ir$

The term $IR$ on the right-hand side corresponds to the potential difference across the resistance $R$, and given that it is the only resistance connected to the cell, it is also equal to the terminal potential difference which is 10 V.

We can now calculate the output current by substituting values as follows:

- $15\textrm{ V}=10\textrm{ V}+I(1.0\,\Omega)$

- $I=\dfrac{15\textrm{ V}-10\textrm{ V}}{1.0\,\Omega}=5.0$ A

The external resistance can now be calculated using Ohm's law:

- $V=IR$

- $R=\dfrac{10}{5}=2.0\,\Omega$
