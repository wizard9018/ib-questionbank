# PH0612-3

**Maximum mark: 5**

1. The diagram below shows a small section of wire which is a part of a closed electric circuit.

    ![PH0612aaa copy](<images/PH0612aaa copy.png>)

   Due to a potential difference across the circuit, an electric field exists in the wire, as shown. Explain, in terms of the charge carriers, why current exists in the wire. [2 marks]

2. The electric field is due to a potential difference of 120 V across the circuit. If the field performs 600 J of work moving negative charges over a time period of 2 seconds, show that approximately $3 \times 10^{19}$ electrons move through a cross-section of wire in this time.              [2 marks]

3. Calculate the current in the wire. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Charge carriers in the wire are electrons which are free to move. $\checkmark$

    The electrons move to the left due to force from the field. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The electric field exerts a force on any charged particles in the wire. Charged particles that are free to move will be accelerated in the direction of the force.

    Electric field lines point from higher to lower electric potential. Negatively charged particles are attracted to more positive regions of potential and experience a force in the opposite direction to electric field lines.

2. $V=\dfrac{W}{q} \implies q=\dfrac{W}{V}=\dfrac{600\ \ce{J}}{120\ \ce{V}}=5$   $\checkmark$

    $\dfrac{5\ \ce{C}}{\left|-1.6 \times 10^{-19}\ \ce{C}\right|}$  = :answer[$3.1 \times {10}^{19}$]   $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Using $V=\dfrac{W}{q}$, we solve for $q=\dfrac{W}{V}=\dfrac{600\ \ce{J}}{120\ \ce{V}}=5$ C.

    The number of electrons represented by this charge is $\dfrac{5\ \ce{C}}{\left|-1.6 \times 10^{-19}\ \ce{C}\right|}=3.1 \times {10}^{19}$.

3. $I = \dfrac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{5 \ \ce{C}}{2 \ \ce{s}} =  $ :answer[$2.5\ce{A}$] $\checkmark$
