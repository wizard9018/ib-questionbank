## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Electrons collide with metal atoms $\checkmark$

    Electrons transfer heat energy to metal atoms which increases the resistance of the metal $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    As the current increases, the electrons flowing through the heating element move faster and collide more frequently with the metal ions (positive ions). These collisions transfer energy to the ions, increasing the vibrations of the metal lattice. This increase in ion vibration causes more frequent collisions and greater resistance to the flow of electrons.

2. Resistance / temperature of the heater increases initially and then reaches a steady / constant value $\checkmark$

    $\ll$Total resistance of the circuit increases and then reaches a constant / steady value so$\gg$  $I=\dfrac{V}{R_{total}}$ therefore current in the circuit decreases until it reaches a steady / constant value $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    When the switch is closed the current very quickly (effectively instantaneously) reaches a value given by:

    - $I=\dfrac{V}{R_{total}}$

    Where $V$ is the voltage of the cell and $R_{total}$ is the total resistance of the circuit.

    This current causes the temperature of the immersion heater to increase. Total resistance in the circuit increases, so the current decreases as total voltage is constant and $V_{total}=IR_{total}$.

    After some time the temperature (and therefore resistance) of the heater reaches a constant value when all of the heat energy is being conducted away from it to the surroundings. Current therefore reaches a constant value.

3. Alternative 1

    - $R_{heater}=\dfrac{50}{4.2^2}=2.83$ $\Omega$ $\checkmark$

    - $R_{total}=\dfrac{15}{4.2}=3.57$ $\Omega$ $\checkmark$

    - $R=3.57-2.83=$ :answer[0.74 $\Omega$] $\checkmark$

    Alternative 2

    - $P_{total}=(4.2)(15)=63$ W $\checkmark$

    - $P_R=63-50=13$ W $\checkmark$

    - $R=\dfrac{13}{4.2^2}=0.74$ $\Omega$ $\checkmark$

    Alternative 3

    - $V_{heater}=\dfrac{50}{4.2}=11.9$ V $\checkmark$

    - $V_R=15-11.9=3.1$ V $\checkmark$

    - $R=\dfrac{3.1}{4.2}=0.74$ $\Omega$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Using $P=I^2R$, the resistance of the heater is given by:

    - $R_{heater}=\dfrac{P_{heater}}{I^2_{heater}}=\dfrac{50}{4.2^2}=2.83$ $\Omega$

    Using $V=IR$ the total resistance of the circuit is given by:

    - $R_{total}=\dfrac{V_{total}}{I_{total}}=\dfrac{15}{4.2}=3.57$ $\Omega$

    The resistors are in series, so we have:

    - $R_{total}=R+R_{heater}$

    Rearranging for $R$ and substituting our values we have:

    - $R=R_{total}-R_{heater}=3.57-2.83=0.74$ $\Omega$

4. $\hspace{1.7em}$ $A=\dfrac{\pi(0.8\times10^{-3})^2}{4}$ OR $A=5.0\times10^{-7}$ seen $\checkmark$

    - $\rho=\dfrac{RA}{L}=\dfrac{(2.83)\left(\dfrac{\pi(0.8\times10^{-3})^2}{4}\right)}{7.8}=$ :answer[$1.8\times10^{-7}$ $\Omega$ m] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The cross-sectional area of the wire is given by:

    - $A=\dfrac{\pi d^2}{4}=\dfrac{\pi(0.8\times10^{-3})^2}{4}=5.0\times10^{-7}$

    Note that $r = \dfrac{d}{2}$ and so $r^2 = \dfrac{d^2}{4}$.

    The resistance of the heater is given by:

    - $R_{heater}=\dfrac{P_{heater}}{I^2_{heater}}=\dfrac{50}{4.2^2}=2.83$ $\Omega$

    Resistivity is given by:

    - $\rho=\dfrac{RA}{L}=\dfrac{(2.83)(5.0\times10^{-7})}{7.8}=1.8\times10^{-7}$ $\Omega$ m
