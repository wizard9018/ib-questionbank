# PH1813

The circuit shown below contains three identical resistors $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ of resistance $R$ and a battery of emf $\varepsilon$ and negligible internal resistance.

![PH1813q](images/PH1813q.JPEG)

What is the ratio $\dfrac{\textrm{power dissipated by resistor X}}{\textrm{power dissipated by resistor Y}}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{4}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $2$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $4$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The power $P$ dissipated across a component is given by:

- $P=I^2R$

Where $I$ is the current through the component and $R$ is its resistance.

Because all resistors are identical, we know that the current will split evenly in the parallel branches, so

- $I_X=2I_Y$

Because $R$ is the same for all components,

- $P\propto I^2$

- $\dfrac{\textrm{power dissipated by resistor X}}{\textrm{power dissipated by resistor Y}}=\left(\dfrac{\textrm{current through resistor X}}{\textrm{current through resistor Y}}\right)^2=\left(\dfrac{2I_Y}{I_Y}\right)^2=4$

Alternatively,

The power $P$ dissipated across a component is given by:

- $P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}$

Where $V$ is the voltage across the component and $R$ is its resistance.

$X$ and $Y$ have the same resistance, so we have $P\propto V^2$. We therefore need to find the ratio of the voltages across the resistors.

The resistance of the parallel combination of $Y$ and $Z$ is given by:

- $\dfrac{1}{R_{total}}=\dfrac{1}{R}+\dfrac{1}{R}=\dfrac{2}{R}$

- $R_{total}=\dfrac{R}{2}$

Therefore, the ratio of the voltages across $X$ and $Y$ is $1:\dfrac{1}{2}=2:1$.

Therefore, we have:

- $\dfrac{\textrm{power dissipated by resistor X}}{\textrm{power dissipated by resistor Y}}=\left(\dfrac{\textrm{voltage across resistor X}}{\textrm{voltage across resistor Y}}\right)^2=\left(\dfrac{2}{1}\right)^2=4$
