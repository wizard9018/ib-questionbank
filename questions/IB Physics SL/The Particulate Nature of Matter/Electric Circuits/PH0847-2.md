2. The student took measurements of the potential difference and current in the unknown resistor for two different positions of the variable resistor:

    - $I_1 =3.5 \times 10^{-2}$ A ,  $V_1=3.0$ V

    - $I_2 =7.5 \times 10^{-2}$ A ,  $V_2=7.0$ V

    Deduce whether the resistor under study is ohmic or non-ohmic [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        To determine the I-V characteristics of the resistor, the current flowing through it and the potential difference across it must be measured. Therefore, the ammeter should be connected in series to the non-ohmic resistor while the voltmeter should be parallel.

2. $R_1 = \dfrac{3}{3.5 \times 10^{-2}}=86\, \Omega$

    $R_2 = \dfrac{7.0}{7.5 \times 10^{-2}}=93\, \Omega$ $\checkmark$

    Ratio of $\dfrac{V}{I}$ is not constant $\checkmark$

    Therefore non-ohmic $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Ohmic behaviour results in the ratio $\dfrac{V}{I}$ remaining constant

    Calculate the ratio for each position of the variable resistor

    $R_1 = \dfrac{V_1}{I_1} =\dfrac{3}{3.5 \times 10^{-2}}=86\, \Omega$

    $R_2 = \dfrac{V_2}{I_2}=\dfrac{7.0}{7.5 \times 10^{-2}}=93\, \Omega$

    Because this ratio does not remain constant, non-ohmic behaviour is exhibited
