# PH0106

A cell is connected to a variable resistor. The terminal potential difference (p.d.) and current through the cell are measured and plotted on the graph shown. What is the internal resistance of the cell?

![PH0106](images/PH0106.png)

$\small{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra, - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$]

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ $5.0\, \Omega$

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ $4.0\, \Omega$

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ $3.0\, \Omega$

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ $2.0\, \Omega$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The terminal voltage formula $\varepsilon =I(R+r)$ can be rearranged by noticing that $IR$ is the terminal p.d. of a cell $V$.

Resulting in the equation $V=\varepsilon -Ir$. Therefore, the negative of the gradient of the graph is the answer.

Gradient $\,= \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}=\dfrac{1.0-5.0}{2.0-0}=-2.0$

Because the internal resistance is the negative of the gradient, it must be equal to 2.0 $ \Omega$.
