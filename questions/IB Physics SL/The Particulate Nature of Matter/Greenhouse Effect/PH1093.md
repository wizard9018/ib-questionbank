# PH1093

A hypothetical planet, with a radius twice that of Earth, orbits the Sun at a distance twice that of the Earth's distance from the Sun. Determine the average intensity of solar radiation on the surface of the hypothetical planet in terms of the solar constant of the Earth, $S$.

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $S$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{S}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{S}{4}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{S}{16}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The intensity of the Sun on the Earth is solar constant.

- $S=\dfrac{P}{4\pi d^2}$

So the power $P$ of the Sun in terms of solar constant:

- $P=S4\pi d^2$

Where the $d$ is the distance between the Sun and the Earth.

The intensity $I$ of the Sun at the distance of the planet's orbit:

- $I=\dfrac{S4\pi d^2}{4\pi (2d)^2}=\dfrac{S4\pi d^2}{4\pi 4d^2}=\dfrac{S}{4}$

The power $P_p$ received by the planet:

- $P_p=IA=\dfrac{S}{4} \pi(2R)^2=\pi R^2 S$

Where the $R$ is the radius of the Earth.

The average intensity $I_{av}$ received by the planet:

- $I_{av}=\dfrac{P_p}{A_S}=\dfrac{\pi R^2 S}{4\pi(2R)^2}=\dfrac{S}{16}$

This last calculation is the common "solar constant divided by 4" calculation that is used to average the incident solar energy over the entire surface of the Earth.
