# PH1893

The Earth receives solar radiation at a solar constant $S$. The Earth-atmosphere system has an average albedo $a$.

What is the average power absorbed by the Earth and its atmosphere per unit area?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{Sa}{4}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{S(1-a)}{4}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $Sa$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $S(1-a)$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The solar constant $S$ is the power per unit area received at the top of Earth's atmosphere, the incoming radiative power is dependent on the projected surface of a planet along the direction of the path of the rays, resulting in a mean value of the incoming intensity being $\dfrac{S}{4}$.

Albedo $a$ is the fraction of the incident radiation that is reflected, so the fraction of the incident radiation that is absorbed is $1-a$.

Therefore, the average intensity (power per unit area) absorbed is given by:

- $\dfrac{S(1-a)}{4}$

$\underline{\textrm{Alternative approach}}: $

The solar constant $S$ is the power per unit area received at the top of Earth's atmosphere, but only the circular cross-sectional area of the Earth $\pi r^2$ (where $r$ is the Earth's radius) directly receives sunlight.

So, the total incoming power $P$ is given by:

- $P=S\pi r^2$

However, not all of this is absorbed. Albedo a is the fraction of the incident radiation that is reflected, so the fraction of the incident radiation that is absorbed is $1-a$.

- Absorbed power $=S\pi r^2(1-a)$

To find the average absorbed power per unit area over the entire surface of the Earth (which has an area of $4\pi r^2$) divide by that:

- Average absorbed power per unit area $=\dfrac{S\pi r^2(1-a)}{4\pi r^2}=\dfrac{S(1-a)}{4}$
