# PH1103

A city launches a “carbon capture” project to remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. The graph shows the relative amounts of carbon dioxide emitted by natural and human sources, plotted alongside the amount of carbon dioxide absorbed before and after the start of the project.

![PH1103](images/PH1103.png)

During the project, the city's mayor announces that the city has achieved “net zero”: the rate of carbon capture equals the city's contributions to the enhanced greenhouse effect.

1. Estimate the year in which this achievement takes place. [3 marks]

Following the announcement, scientists complain that the mayor's claim does not account for emissions of Gas $A$, which is a primary greenhouse gas.

2. 1. State which portion of the electromagnetic spectrum must be absorbed by Gas $A$ for it to behave as a greenhouse gas. [1 mark]

    2. Describe how electromagnetic absorption by Gas $A$ molecules takes place, with reference to both bond resonance and molecular energy levels. [2 marks]

3. In terms of the energy entering and leaving the Earth's atmosphere, explain how increasing the concentration of greenhouse gases raises the surface temperature. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Carbon capture at net zero = Domestic $CO_2$ + Industrial $CO_2$ $\checkmark$

    Carbon capture (2028) > Domestic $CO_2$ + Industrial $CO_2$

    Carbon capture (2027) < Domestic $CO_2$ + Industrial $CO_2$

    Year will be :answer[2027] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The enhanced greenhouse effect refers to the warming effects of greenhouse gases released by human activities, meaning that $CO_2$ resulting from natural processes may be excluded from consideration.

    Alternatively, one may determine the year in which net zero is achieved by simply noting that whilst net zero has already been achieved at the start of 2028 (as $A$ exceeds $R$), $R$(domestic) and $R$(industrial) are between two and three at the beginning of 2027, meaning that their sum is greater than $A$, which has a value of four. It therefore follows that equality between $R$ and $A$ must occur in the year 2027.

2. 1. Infra-red absorption $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Greenhouse gases contribute to the greenhouse effect by absorbing infra-red radiation emitted by the Earth's surface, preventing it from escaping the atmosphere.

    2. Radiation frequency matches molecular resonant frequencies, causing excited state  $\checkmark$

        Radiation excites molecules from one energy state to a higher energy excited state  across a quantised energy gap $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In the resonance model of absorption, molecules of Gas $A$ absorb electromagnetic radiation when the resonance frequencies of their bond vibrations match the frequencies of incident waves.

        Alternatively, an energy level model may be used to describe absorption: electromagnetic radiation is absorbed by a molecule of Gas $A$ when its energy matches the energy gap between the ground-state energy level of the molecule, and a higher “excited” energy state which corresponds to excited bond vibrations within the molecule.

3. Increased greenhouse gas concentration decreases energy radiated into space/increases incident radiation on the Earth's surface $\checkmark$

    Consequently, energy from radiation absorbed by the Earth's surface exceeds radiation emitted $\checkmark$

    Surface temperature increases until energy radiated from the Earth's surface equals energy entering it $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    In thermal equilibrium, the radiative energy entering the Earth's atmosphere is equal to the radiative energy leaving it. Greenhouse gases suppress the rate of radiative loss by absorbing infra-red radiation emitted from the surface and radiating half of it back to the Earth's surface. Therefore, increasing the concentration of these gases leads to a reduction in the rate of energy loss from the atmosphere, and in turn the radiation incident on the Earth's surface. When the Earth's surface has heated to an extent that the radiative energy emitted once again equals the radiative power incident on the surface, thermal equilibrium is reestablished.
