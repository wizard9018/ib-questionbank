# PH0646

**Maximum mark: 9**

A $100$ g chocolate bar is placed onto a hot plate. The chocolate is initially at its melting temperature of $30\degree$C.

Heat is transferred from the plate to the chocolate at a constant rate $P_{in}$ of $12$ W, causing it to melt at a constant rate of $6$ g min$^{-1}$. After all the chocolate has melted, the liquid chocolate continues to heat to a temperature of $353$ K.

Specific heat capacity of melted chocolate = $1.0$ kJ kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$

Specific latent heat of fusion of chocolate = $50$ J g$^{-1}$

![PH0646..](images/PH0646...png)

1. State how the kinetic and potential energy of the chocolate change as it melts. [2 marks]

2.  1. Determine the amount of energy required to heat the **liquid** chocolate from the melting temperature to $353$ K. [2 marks]

    2. Show that approximately 58% of the supplied energy is lost to the surroundings while the chocolate is melting. [3 marks]

3. The graph shows how the temperature of the chocolate changes with time.

    ![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.18.38 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.18.38 PM.png>)

    Draw on the same graph a new line to show how temperature would change for a larger mass of chocolate with the same starting conditions.  [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The potential energy of the chocolate increases. $\checkmark$

    The kinetic energy of the chocolate stays constant. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The potential energy of the chocolate increases as the intermolecular distance is increased.

    The kinetic energy stays constant as the temperature of the chocolate does not change during melting; all of the energy supplied by the hotplate is used to increase potential energy.

2.  1.  ::indent
        - $Q=(m_{choc})(c_{choc})\Delta T$ $\checkmark$

        - $Q=(0.1\textrm{ kg})(1\textrm{ kJ kg}^{-1}\textrm{ K}^{-1})(50\textrm{ K})=$ :answer[$5$ kJ] or :answer[$5000$ J] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The total internal energy change of the chocolate over the $50$ K temperature change ($303$ K to $353$ K) is given by the product of the mass, specific heat capacity and temperature change.

    2.  ::indent
        - $Q_L=m_{choc}L_{choc}=5\textrm{ kJ}$ $\checkmark$

        - $Q_{in}=Power\times\dfrac{mass}{melting\ rate}=(12\textrm{ W})\dfrac{100\, g}{0.1\textrm{ g s}^{-1}}=12\textrm{ kJ}$ $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{Q_{surroundings}}{Q_{in}}=1-\dfrac{Q_L}{Q_{in}}=1-\dfrac{5}{12}=0.583=58.3 \%$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The melting rate given in the question is $6$ g min$^{-1}$

        This corresponds to $6$ g every $60$ s, or $0.1$ g s$^{-1}$

        The time required for the chocolate to completely melt can be found:

        - $time=\dfrac{mass}{melting\ rate}=\dfrac{100\textrm{ g}}{0.1\textrm{ g s}^{-1}}=1000$ s

        The energy produced by the blowtorch in this time can be found from

        - $E=Pt = (12)(1000)\ =\ 12000\textrm{ J}\ =\ 12\textrm{ kJ}$

        The amount of latent heat required to melt the chocolate is given by

        - $Q_L=mL =(100\textrm{ g})(50\textrm{ J g}^{-1})=5000\textrm{ J}=5\textrm{ kJ}$

        Energy lost is

        - $12$ kJ $-5$ kJ $=7$ kJ

        As a percentage of the total energy, this gives

        - $\dfrac{7}{12} (100 \%)  = 58.3 \% $

3.  $\hspace{1em}$

    ![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.19.49 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.19.49 PM.png>)

    Plateau continues up to a higher $t$ value. $\checkmark$

    Temperature increases at a lower rate after plateau. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The total time required to melt the chocolate is given by

    - $t_{melt}=\dfrac{mass}{melting\ rate}$

    Consequently, increasing the mass of the chocolate causes the plateau to continue to a higher $t$ value.

    After melting, temperature changes at a rate given by

    - $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{P_{in}-P_{out}}{(m_{choc})(c_{choc})}$

    Thus, increasing the mass of chocolate decreases the rate at which temperature changes.
