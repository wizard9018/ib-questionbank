# PH1801

Solids $X$ and $Y$ are of equal volume and have densities of $\rho$ and $2\rho$, respectively. When heated starting from their melting points, $X$ requires an amount of energy $E$ to completely melt while $Y$ requires $3E$.

The specific latent heat of fusion of $X$ is $L$. What is the specific latent heat of fusion of $Y$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{L}{6}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2L}{3}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3L}{2}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $6L$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The substances have the same volume so using $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$ we have $\rho\propto m$. Therefore substance $Y$ has twice the mass of substance $X$.

Specific latent heat of fusion $L$, of a substance is given by:

- $Q=mL$

Where $Q$ is equivalent to $E$, the energy required to completely melt a mass $m$ of the substance.

We therefore have:

- $\dfrac{L_Y}{L_X}=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{E_Y}{m_Y}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{E_x}{m_x}\right)}=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{3E}{2m}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{E}{m}\right)}=\dfrac{3}{2}$
