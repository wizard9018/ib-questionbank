# PH1803

Opposite sides of a solid metal cube of side length 0.2 m are held at temperatures 20°C and 100°C.

![Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 1.33.27 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 1.33.27 PM.JPEG>)

The rate of heat energy transfer between the sides is $6.4$ kW. It can be assumed that there is no heat loss through the other sides of the cube. What is the thermal conductivity of the metal in W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $80$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $100$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $400$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $2000$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The thermal conductivity equation states that the rate of energy transfer by conduction $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}$ between two perpendicular surfaces is given by:

- $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}=kA\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta x}$

Where $k$ is the thermal conductivity of the material between the surfaces, $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the surfaces, $\Delta T$ is the difference between the temperature of the surfaces, and $\Delta x$ is the displacement between the surfaces.

Here we are given:

- $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}=6.4$ kW $=6400$ W

- $\Delta T=100-20=80$ K (Celsius and Kelvin are equivalent for differences in temperature).

- $A=(0.2)(0.2)=0.04$ m$^2$

- $\Delta x=0.2$ m

Substituting these values into our equation and rearranging for $k$ gives:

- $6400=k(0.04)\dfrac{80}{0.2}$

- $6400=16k$

- $k=400$ W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$
