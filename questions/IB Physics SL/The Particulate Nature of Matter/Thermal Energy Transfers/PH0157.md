# PH0157

A thin metal plate of mass $m$ is held at an absolute temperature $T$, in an enclosure of temperature $T_s<T$ . One face of the plate has an area $A$. The temperature of the plate decreases by a small value $\Delta T$ in a short interval $\Delta t$, due to radiation. Assuming the metal plate to be a perfect black body, its specific heat capacity is given by: 

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\sigma AT^4 \Delta t}{m(\Delta T)}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\sigma A(T^4-T_s\textrm{}^4 )\Delta t}{m(\Delta T)}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2\sigma AT^4 \Delta t}{m(\Delta T)}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2\sigma A(T^4-T_s\textrm{}^4 )\Delta t}{m(\Delta T)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

Specific heat capacity is defined as the thermal energy $(\Delta Q)$ gained or lost per kelvin change in temperature $(\Delta T)$ per unit mass ($m$). 

In this problem thermal energy is lost by radiation only. 

$\begin{aligned}

\therefore \Delta Q&=P_{net}(\Delta t)\\

\hspace{1em}\\

&=P_{radiated}-P_{absorbed}\\

\hspace{1em}\\

&=\sigma(2A)(T^4)(\Delta t)-\sigma(2A)(T_s\textrm{}^4)(\Delta t)\\

\hspace{1em}\\

&=\sigma(2A)(T^4-T_s\textrm{}^4)(\Delta t)\\

\end{aligned}$

                                

(considering both surfaces of the plate and using Stefan-Boltzmann equation). 

Hence, specific heat capacity $c=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{m(\Delta T)}=\dfrac{2\sigma A(T^4-T_s\textrm{}^4 )\Delta t}{m(\Delta T)}$
