# PH0694

**Maximum mark: 11**

1. A "Newton\'s cradle" contains a row of five identical metal balls, each of mass 1.0 g and specific heat capacity 400 J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$, suspended from light strings of length 30 cm. The balls hang in a straight line, touching each other.

    Ball 1 is drawn back, with its string kept taut, until it makes an angle of 60$\degree$ to the vertical, then released from rest. It swings down and collides with the remaining four stationary balls. The size of each ball may be treated as negligible.

    ![Screenshot 2026-06-01 at 1.25.59 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-06-01 at 1.25.59 PM.png>)

    Over time, the system stops moving. Assuming that all initial energy is distributed equally as thermal energy in the balls, determine the temperature increase in temperature in each ball.   [3 marks]

2. The cradle is placed inside a sealed, evacuated container. The strings and the container walls are perfect thermal insulators, so no energy is lost from the balls.

    Explain why changing the mass of each ball, while keeping the material (and hence specific heat capacity) the same, does not affect the temperature rise calculated in part (a). [2 marks]

3.  Ball 1 is removed from the cradle. It is thrown horizontally with a speed of 10.0 m s$^{-1}$ and embeds itself in a stationary ice cube of mass 9.0 g that rests on a smooth horizontal surface. The ball–ice system then slides freely along the surface.

    1. Determine the speed of the ball–ice system immediately after the collision. [2 marks]

    2. Show that the amount of energy converted to thermal energy during the collision is 45 mJ. [2 marks]

4. The ice cube is initially at 0$\degree$C. Assuming no energy is lost from the ball–ice system, calculate the percentage of the ice that melts as a result of the collision. [2 marks]

    Specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 333 kJ kg$^{-1}$

    The vertical height $h$ through which Ball 1 descends is

    - $h=l\left(1-\cos{\theta}\right)=0.30\left(1-\cos{60\degree}\right)\textrm{ m}=0.15\textrm{ m}$

    All gravitational potential energy converts to kinetic energy at the moment of collision:

    - $E_k=E_p=mgh=\left(1.0\times10^{-3}\textrm{ kg}\right)\left(9.81\textrm{ m s}^{-2}\right)\left(0.15\textrm{ m}\right)=1.47\times10^{-3}\textrm{ J}$

    This energy is shared equally among all five balls:

    - $E_k=(5m)(c)(\Delta T)$

    - $\Delta T = \dfrac{E_k}{5mc} = \dfrac{1.47\times10^{-3}\textrm{ J}}{(5\times1.0\times10^{-3}\textrm{ kg})(400\textrm{ J kg}^{-1}\textrm{ K}^{-1})} = 7.4\times10^{-4}\textrm{ K}$

    The kinetic energy at impact is $E_k=mgh$. Setting this equal to the thermal energy gained by all five balls:

    - $mgh=(5m)(c)(\Delta T)$

    Dividing both sides by $m$:

    - $\Delta T = \dfrac{gh}{5c}$

    Since $g$, $h$ (set by string length and release angle) and $c$ (set by material) are all unchanged, $\Delta T$ does not depend on mass.
