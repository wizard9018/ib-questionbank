# PH0871

Heat is supplied at a power of $10$ W to one end of a horizontal metal rod of cross-sectional area $10$ cm$^2$.

![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.06.06 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.06.06 PM.png>)

The graph shows how temperature $T$ varies with horizontal distance $x$ measured from the hot end of the rod. It may be assumed that all of the supplied energy is conducted through the rod and dissipated at the other end.

![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.06.53 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.06.53 PM.png>)

What is the thermal conductivity of the rod?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $0.5$ W m$^{-1}\,\degree $C$^{-1}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.0$ W m$^{-1}\,\degree $C$^{-1}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $50$ W m$^{-1}\,\degree $C$^{-1}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $100$ W m$^{-1}\,\degree $C$^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The rate of heat transfer through the rod is given by

- $P=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}=kA \dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta x}$

From the equation, $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{P}{kA}$

And $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta x}$ is the gradient of the graph

Using two points from the graph

- $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta x}=\dfrac{60}{30}=2\,\degree$C cm$^{-1}$

Therefore

- $\dfrac{P}{kA}=2$ K cm$^{-1}$

- $k=\dfrac{10\textrm{ W}}{(2\textrm{ K cm}^{-1})(10\textrm{ cm}^2)}$

- $=0.5\textrm{ W}\,\degree$C$^{-1}$ cm$^{-1}$

- $=50\textrm{ W}$ m$^{-1}\,\degree$C$^{-1}$
