# PH0650

**Maximum mark: 9**

Two identical buckets of water are suspended from spring scales so that their weights can be monitored. Each bucket initially contains 500 g of water at a temperature of 20$\degree$C. The water in Bucket **A** is heated at a constant power of 1.0 kW. Bucket **B** is held at a constant temperature but water leaks from it at a rate of 6.0 g min$^{-1}$. Assume that the specific heat capacity of water is 4.0 J g$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ and the specific latent heat of vaporization is 2.0 kJ g$^{-1}$.

1. Calculate the rate of change of temperature in Bucket **A** before it reaches the boiling temperature of water. [2 marks]

2. Estimate the total time required for the masses of the two buckets to equalize. [4 marks]

3. The dashed line on the graph shows how the mass difference between Bucket **A** and Bucket **B** changes with time $t$ up to the point of mass equalization.

    ![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.22.11 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 5.22.11 PM.png>)

    When the two buckets are empty, each bucket is refilled with 500 g of water. The process of heating Bucket **A** and draining Bucket **B** is repeated with the input power to Bucket **A** now doubled to 2 kW. All other parameters remain the same as in the first experiment.

    On the same graph, draw the variation of mass difference in the second experiment. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. ::indent
    - $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\textrm{power}}{\textrm{mass}  \times  \textrm{spec. \ heat \ capacity}} = \dfrac{1000\textrm{ J s}^{-1}}{(0.5\textrm{ kg})(4000\textrm{ J kg}^{-1}\textrm{ K}^{-1})}$ $\checkmark$

    - $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t} =$ :answer[$0.5\textrm{ K s}^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The energy $E$ supplied to the water in Bucket **A** is related to the temperature change $\Delta T$ by the expression

    $\hspace{1em}$ $E = mc \Delta T$

    where $m$ is the mass and $c$ is the specific heat capacity.

    Hence,

    $\hspace{1em}$ $P = \dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t} = mc \dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}$

    Rearranging for $\dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}$ yields

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{P}{mc} = \dfrac{1000\textrm{ J s}^{-1}}{(0.5\textrm{ kg})(4000\textrm{ J kg}^{-1}\textrm{ K}^{-1})} = 0.5\textrm{ K s}^{-1}$

2. Boiling rate $=\dfrac{P}{L}=0.5\textrm{ g s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    - $t_{boil}=\dfrac{373\textrm{ K}-293\textrm{ K}}{0.5\textrm{ K s}^{-1}}=160\textrm{ s}$ $\checkmark$

    - $500\textrm{ g}-\left(0.1\textrm{ g s}^{-1}\right)t=500\textrm{ g} -\left(0.5\textrm{ g s}^{-1}\right)\left(t-160\textrm{ s}\right)$ $\checkmark$

    - $t=$ :answer[$200$ s] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Using the rate value calculated in Part (a), the water reaches its boiling point after a time $t_{boil}$ given by

    - $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\frac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}} = \dfrac{373\textrm{ K} - 293\textrm{ K}}{0.5\textrm{ K s}^{-1}} = 160$ s

    After this time, mass is lost as steam at a rate $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$ that can be found from

    - $E\ =\ Pt\ =\ mL$

    where $L$ is the specific latent heat of fusion.

    This gives

    - $\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{P}{L} = \dfrac{1000\textrm{ J s}^{-1}}{2000\textrm{ J g}^{-1}} = 0.5\textrm{ g s}^{-1}$

    Finally, to calculate the time $t$ of equalization, measured from the beginning of the heating process, one must consider the equation

    - $mass\ of\ right\ bucket=mass\ of\ left\ bucket\ $

    Recalling the rates of mass loss in each case, in addition to the time required to achieve boiling, gives

    - $500\textrm{ g}-\left(0.1\textrm{ g s}^{-1}\right)t=500\textrm{ g} -\left(0.5\textrm{ g s}^{-1}\right)\left(t-160\textrm{ s}\right)$

    Rearranging for $t$ yields

    - $0.4\ \ t=80$ s

    -  $\therefore\ t=200$ s
