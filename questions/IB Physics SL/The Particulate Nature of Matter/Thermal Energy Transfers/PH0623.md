# PH0623

A thermally isolated system consists of a coin of weight $mg$ and specific heat capacity $c$ suspended in a vacuum at a vertical distance *H* above a solid block. The block has mass 4*m*, specific heat capacity 2*c* and latent heat of fusion *L*. The melting temperature of the block is an amount $\Delta T$ greater than the initial temperature of the block and the coin. The coin is dropped on to the block.

![PH0623](images/PH0623.png)

What is the minimum value of *H* required for the block to melt due to the collision?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{8c \Delta T}{g}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{8c \Delta T + 4L}{g}$
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{9c \Delta T + 4L}{g}$
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{9c \Delta T - 4L}{g}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

In order to melt the block, the kinetic energy $E_k$ supplied by the coin must exceed the total latent heat of fusion for the block ($4mL$) plus the energy required to increase the temperature of the coin and the block by an amount $\Delta T$ ($mc\Delta T+(4m)(2c)\Delta T$)=$9mc\Delta T$.

Hence,

- $E_k=9mc\Delta T+4mL$

The kinetic energy on impact equals the initial gravitational potential energy of the coin, equal to $mgH$.

Consequently,

- $mgH=9mc\Delta T+4mL$

Therefore,

- $H= \dfrac{9c\Delta T+4L}{g} $
