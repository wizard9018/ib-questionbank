# PH0622

The graph shows how the temperature of a solid wax block varies with time. The solid wax has a specific heat capacity of $2$ kJ kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ and it absorbs heat at a rate of $20$ W.

![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 4.49.13 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 4.49.13 PM.png>)

What is the specific latent heat of fusion of wax?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  50$ J g$^{-1}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  100$ J g$^{-1}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  200$ J g$^{-1}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  400$ J g$^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The heating rate of the solid can be found from the gradient of the first section of the graph.

- gradient = $\dfrac{\textrm{rise}}{\textrm{run}}=\dfrac{(310-280)}{60}=\ 0.5$ K s$^{-1}= \dfrac{\Delta T}{t}$

For the heating of the solid,

- $Pt = mc\Delta T$

therefore the mass of the solid can now be determined from

- $m=\dfrac{P}{(\frac{\Delta T}{t})(c)}$

- $=\dfrac{20\textrm{ J s}^{-1}}{(0.5\textrm{ K s}^{-1})(2000\textrm{ J kg}^{-1}\textrm{ K}^{-1})}$

- $=20$ g

The system remains at the melting temperature for 200 s;

- $E = mL$

- $Pt = mL$

Therefore

- $L=\dfrac{(P)(t)}{m}=\dfrac{(20\textrm{ W})(200\textrm{ s})}{20\textrm{ g}}=200\textrm{ J g}^{-1}$
