# PH0619

A liquid of mass $M$, temperature $4T$ specific heat capacity $S$ is held in an insulated container of negligible mass. A second liquid of mass $2M$, temperature $T$ and specific heat capacity $3S$ is added to the container.

What is the equilibrium temperature of the mixture?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{10T}{7}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{7T}{4}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  2T$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{25T}{7}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Since no energy is exchanged with the surroundings (the container is insulated), the overall internal energy change is zero. The internal energy change of the first liquid must therefore be equal in magnitude but opposite in sign to the internal energy change of the second.

Therefore, substituting into the equation $E=mc \Delta T$ for each liquid and using $T_f$ to represent the equilibrium temperature,

- $Q=mc(T_f-T_i)$

Substituting the given information,

- $Q_1=MS(T_f-4T)$

- $\Delta E_2=6MS(T_f-T)$

Using conservation of energy

$\begin{aligned}
Q_1+Q_2&=0\\
Q_1&= -Q_2\\
MS(T_f-4T)&=-6MS(T_f-T)\\
\hspace{4em}MS(T_f-4T)&=6MS(T-{T_f})\\
T_f-4T&=6T-6T_f\\
7T_f&=10T\\
T_f&=\dfrac{10T}{7}\\
\end{aligned}$
