# PH0470

**Maximum mark: 4**

1. An electric heater with a power rating of 2.8 kW heats liquid that flows through it. The temperature of the liquid increases from $18\degree$C to $38\degree$C while passing through the heater. The flow rate of the liquid $\sigma$ is $0.035$ kg s$^{-1}$. Calculate the specific heat capacity of the liquid. [2 marks]

2. Suggest whether your answer for (a) is above or below the actual value of the specific heat capacity. Justify your answer. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Use of $c=\ll \dfrac{Pt}{m\Delta T} \gg= \dfrac{P}{\sigma \Delta T}$ $\checkmark$

    - $c=\ll \dfrac{2800}{(0.035)(20)}=\gg$ :answer[$4000$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    The heat Q supplied to the liquid can be expressed as;

    - $Q=Pt$

    Specific heat capacity of the liquid;

    - $c=\dfrac{Q}{m\Delta T}=\dfrac{Pt}{m\Delta T}$ 

    It can be rearranged as;

    - $c=\dfrac{P}{\frac{m}{t}\Delta T}=\dfrac{P}{\sigma\Delta T}$

    So

    - $c=\dfrac{2800}{(0.035)(20)}=4000$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$

2. There is thermal energy lost to the surroundings during the heating process $\checkmark$

    $\ll$ Using $power \times time$ in the calculations leads to $\gg$ an overestimation of the specific heat capacity $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    There is thermal energy lost to the surroundings during the heating process. Therefore, the energy used up by the liquid to increase its temperature is less than $power \times time$. 

    

    - $c = \dfrac{Pt}{m\Delta T}$

    

    Because $power \times time$ in the calculations is an overestimation of the energy given to the liquid, it results in an overestimation of the specific heat capacity value.
