# PH0903

The diagram below shows some of the discrete energy levels in the Bohr model. The energies shown are of the fundamental and first three excited states for an electron in the hydrogen atom.

![Screenshot 2026-01-05 at 11.29.54 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-05 at 11.29.54 AM.png>)

A photon with an energy of 2.0 eV is incident on a hydrogen atom. Considering only the energy levels shown in the diagram above, which of the following correctly describes the transitions between states that would be possible for an electron?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ No transitions possible

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $n=3$ to $n=4$ only

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $n=2$ to $n=3$ only

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ Both from $n=2$ to $n=3$ and $n=3$ to $n=4$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

In Bohr's model for the hydrogen atom, an electron can transition between two states only if the photon has the exact energy required for the transition.

Given that the energy of the photon is 2.0 eV and that there are no two energy states with a difference of 2.0 eV, no transitions between energy levels shown in the diagram are possible for an electron.
