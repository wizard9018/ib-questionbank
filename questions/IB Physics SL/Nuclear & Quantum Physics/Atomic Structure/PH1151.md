# PH1151

Two different gases A and B have the same pressure and temperature conditions.
The diagram shows the emission spectrum of gas A.

![Screenshot 2026-01-01 at 12.31.45 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-01 at 12.31.45 PM.png>)

Which of the following is correct about the atomic energy levels of gas A and the emission spectrum of gas B?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Atomic energy levels of gas A$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Emission spectrum of gas B$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$ |![PH1151A](images/PH1151A.png) | Same as A |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | ![PH1151B](images/PH1151B.png) | Different from A |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | ![PH1151C](images/PH1151C.png) | Same as A |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | ![PH1151D](images/PH1151D.png) | Different from A |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The emission spectrum is unique for each element. This means that no two elements can have the same emission spectrum. Therefore the spectrum of B must be different from that of A.

The wavelengths produced depend on the differences between atomic energy levels.

- $E=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

The higher the energy difference, the lower the wavelength.

By investigating the wavelengths of the emission spectrum, we can conclude that the corresponding energy differences between energy levels varies in a way that matches the diagram in A and B.

![PH1151ms1](images/PH1151ms1.png)

As shown in the diagram above, the energy differences will emit three lines with short wavelengths and three lines with long wavelengths.

For the diagram in C and D, the energy differences for the transitions will lead to an emission spectrum different from that given in the question.

![PH1151ms2](images/PH1151ms2.png)

For example, the three transitions shown by the three arrows to the right will have almost the same wavelength.
