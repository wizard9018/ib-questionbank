# PH1069

The diagram shows the scattering of alpha particles that are directed to a thin gold foil. Which of the following scattering patterns cannot be explained by the model of the atom that existed prior to this experiment?

![Screenshot 2026-01-01 at 12.17.57 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-01 at 12.17.57 PM.png>)

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The previous model (Thomson's “plum pudding model”) assumed the atom to consist of a mass of positively charged matter with embedded electrons. According to the Thomson model, when alpha particles are directed towards an atom, the expectation is that most of them will undergo no deflection. Additionally, minor deflections can be accounted for by the interaction of alpha particles with the positive charges within the atom. However, the significant deflection of alpha particles at large angles can only be explained by the existence of a nucleus, a component not considered in the Thomson model.
