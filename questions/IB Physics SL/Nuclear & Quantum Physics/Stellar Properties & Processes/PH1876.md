# PH1876

**Maximum mark: 6**

In the early universe, the following fusion reaction occurred:

$\ce{^{1}_{1}H}+\ce{^{1}_{0}n}\rightarrow\ce{^{2}_{1}H}+\ce{^{0}_{0}\gamma}$

The following data is provided:

- The mass of $\ce{^{1}_{1}H}=1.007276$ u

- The mass of $\ce{^{1}_{0}n}=1.008665$ u

1.    The energy released in this reaction is 1.713 MeV. Determine the mass of $\ce{^{2}_{1}H}$. [3 marks]

2. State and explain the conditions needed for fusion to occur. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.85em}$ mass defect $=0.001839$ u $\checkmark$

    - mass of the reactants $=2.015941$ u $\checkmark$

    - $2.015941\textrm{ u}-0.001839\textrm{ u}=$ :answer[$2.014\textrm{ u}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The mass defect of the reaction $\Delta m$ in u:

    - $\Delta m=\dfrac{1.713}{931.5}=0.001839$ u

    The mass of the reactants $m_r$:

    - $\begin{aligned}
    \ m_r&=1.007276+1.008665\\
    \\\
    \ &=2.015941\textrm{ u}\\
    \\\
    \ \textrm{mass of }\ce{^{2}_{1}H}&=m_r-\Delta m\\
    \\\
    \ &=2.015941\textrm{ u}-0.001839\textrm{ u}\\
    \\\
    \ &=2.014102\textrm{ u}\\
    \end{aligned}$

2. high temperature $\checkmark$

    high density of the nuclei $\checkmark$

    bring nuclei close enough to overcome (electrostatic) repulsion and experience strong nuclear force $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Nuclear fusion, the process powering stars and proposed fusion reactors, requires overcoming the strong electrostatic repulsion between positively charged atomic nuclei. This is achieved by providing the nuclei with extremely high temperatures (millions of Kelvin), which gives them sufficient kinetic energy to move at speeds necessary to overcome the repulsion. Additionally, a high density of nuclei is crucial, as it increases the probability of these high-energy collisions occurring frequently enough for sustained fusion reactions. The combination of these extreme conditions allows the nuclei to get close enough for the strong nuclear force to bind them together, releasing a tremendous amount of energy.
