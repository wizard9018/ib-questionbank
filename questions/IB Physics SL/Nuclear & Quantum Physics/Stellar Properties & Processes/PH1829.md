The star Proxima Centauri is the closest star to the Sun. It is located 4.24 light years away from Earth.

1.    Calculate the parallax angle of Proxima Centauri when viewed from Earth. [3 marks]

2. Proxima Centauri is a red dwarf star that is currently in the main sequence stage of its life cycle. Describe the process by which a star like Proxima Centauri forms, from its birth to reaching the main sequence. [4 marks]

3. Proxima Centauri is currently a red dwarf star and will eventually become a white dwarf. Label on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram below the approximate location of a red dwarf (A) and a white dwarf (B). [2 marks]

1. ::indent
    - $d=\dfrac{4.24}{3.26}=1.3$ pc $\checkmark$

    Use of $d(\textrm{parsec})=\dfrac{1}{p(\textrm{arcsecond})}$ $\checkmark$

    - $p=$ :answer[0.77 arcseconds] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Since 1 pc $=3.26$ ly, then the distance to Proxima Centauri in parsecs is:

    - $d=\dfrac{4.24}{3.26}=1.3$ pc

    To find the parallax angle, we use:

    - $d(\textrm{parsecs})=\dfrac{1}{p(\textrm{arcsecond})}$

    Rearranging and substituting in the values:

    - $p=\dfrac{1}{d}=\dfrac{1}{1.3}=0.77$ arcseconds

2. Star begins as a giant molecular cloud of gas/nebula $\checkmark$

    Gravity causes the nebula to collapse and form a hot dense core known as a protostar $\checkmark$

    Temperature and pressure increase until hydrogen begins to fuse $\checkmark$

    Fusion creates outward radiation pressure that balances gravity and prevents further collapse – the star is now in its main sequence stage $\checkmark$

    OWTTE

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Proxima Centauri would have started as a cold, dense nebula — a giant molecular cloud consisting of gas and dust left over from the formation of galaxies. Nebulae are held together by gravity but are prevented from collapsing due to the outward pressure of the molecules moving in random directions. Gravity can cause a region of the nebula to collapse into a dense core. The temperature and pressure increase, creating a protostar. As the protostar gets hotter and denser, hot hydrogen starts to fuse to create helium, releasing energy. The nuclear fusion reactions create an outward radiation pressure that balances the inward force of gravity, preventing further collapse — the star enters its main sequence stage of life. The star will remain in the main sequence stage until most of the hydrogen is used up.
