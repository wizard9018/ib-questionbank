# PH1828

**Maximum mark: 7**

1. A distant star of radius $1.4\times10^{11}$ m is measured to have a peak wavelength in its spectrum of 725 nm. Calculate the surface temperature of the star. [2 marks]

2. The luminosity of the Sun is $L_\odot=3.8\times10^{26}$ W. Calculate the luminosity of the star in terms of the Sun's luminosity. [3 marks]

3. On the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram below, plot the location of the star and state the classification of this star. [2 marks]

    ![Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 9.55.20 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-03-12 at 9.55.20 AM.png>)

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Use of $\lambda_\textrm{max}=\dfrac{2.9\times10^{-3}}{T}$ $\checkmark$

    - $T=$ :answer[4000 K] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To find the surface temperature, we use Wien's law:

    - $\lambda_\textrm{max}=\dfrac{2.9\times10^{-3}}{T}$

    Rearranging for $T$ and substituting in the values:

    - $T=\dfrac{2.9\times10^{-3}}{725\times10^{-9}}=4000$ K

2. ::indent
    - $A=4\pi r^2=4\pi(1.4\times10^{11})^2=2.5\times10^{23}$ m$^2$ $\checkmark$

    - $L=\sigma AT^4=(5.67\times10^{-8})(2.5\times10^{23})(4000)^4=3.6\times10^{30}$ W $\checkmark$

    - $\dfrac{3.6\times10^{30}}{3.8\times10^{26}}=$ :answer[$9.4\times10^3\,L_\odot$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To find the luminosity of the star, we must first find its surface area:

    - $A=4\pi r^2=4\pi(1.4\times10^{11})^2=2.5\times10^{23}$ m$^2$

    We can now calculate the luminosity using:

    - $L=\sigma AT^4=(5.67\times10^{-8})(2.5\times10^{23})(4000)^4=3.6\times10^{30}$ W

    The star's luminosity as a fraction of the Sun's luminosity is given by:

    - $\dfrac{3.6\times10^{30}}{3.8\times10^{26}}=9.4\times10^3\,L_\odot$

    Use of rounded values gives $9.5\times10^3\,L_\odot$.

3. Star plotted correctly at $T=4000$ K and $L\approx10^4\,L_\odot$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The star has a temperature of 4000 K and a luminosity of $9.4\times10^3\,L_\odot\approx10^4\,L_\odot$.

    Therefore, the location of the star on the diagram is in the upper right quadrant:

    ![PH1828ac2](images/PH1828ac2.JPEG)

    A star located in this area of the Hertzsprung-Russell is classified as a **red giant**.
