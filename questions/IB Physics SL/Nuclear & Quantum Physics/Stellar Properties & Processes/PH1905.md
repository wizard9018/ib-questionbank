# PH1905

The HR diagram below shows the evolutionary track of a star starting from the main sequence (point X). The star's initial position on the main sequence corresponds to a luminosity of about $0.5L_\odot$ and a surface temperature of about 5200 K.

![Screenshot 2025-09-07 at 11.53.51 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-09-07 at 11.53.51 AM.png>)

Which of the following correctly describes the mass of the star and the final stage of its evolution?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Mass$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Final stage$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\lt12M_\odot$ | White dwarf |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\lt12M_\odot$ | Neutron star |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\gt12M_\odot$ | White dwarf |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\gt12M_\odot$ | Neutron star |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The star's initial position on the HR diagram corresponds to a luminosity $L=0.5L_\odot$ and a surface temperature $T$ = 5200 K.

Because the star's luminosity and temperature are less than that of the Sun, it must be less massive than the Sun and therefore **much less than** $12M_\odot$ which is approximately the limit above which neutron stars can be formed from the remaining core.

From its track on the HR diagram, the star evolves off the main sequence into the red giant branch, with luminosity increasing and surface temperature decreasing. Once helium fusion ends, the outer layers are ejected as a planetary nebula, leaving behind the hot core.

Because the star's initial mass is less than twelve solar masses, the core mass is insufficient to overcome electron degeneracy pressure and undergo further collapse. The remnant is a white dwarf, located in the lower left of the HR diagram.

Therefore, the star's mass is lower than 12 solar masses and its final stage is a white dwarf.
