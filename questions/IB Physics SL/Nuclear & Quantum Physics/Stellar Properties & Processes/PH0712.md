# PH0712

A main sequence star has the following possible fates in its evolution.

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | White dwarf   |
  | II. | Neutron star |
  | III. | Black hole |

Which is the possible fate of a star with a mass greater than 12 times the mass of the Sun?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ I and II only

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Stars with a main sequence mass up to 8 to 10 times the mass of the Sun will form a white dwarf. If their mass is between roughly 10 and 40 solar masses, they will form a neutron star. If the mass is greater than roughly 40 solar masses, they will form a black hole. We are told that the mass of the star is greater than 12 solar masses, so we do not know the mass of the star, only that it will not form a white dwarf $-$ depending on its mass, it could form either a neutron star or a black hole.

Note that the Chandrasekhar limit and Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit refer to the mass of the stellar remnant, the mass of the star remaining after the star has left the main sequence.
