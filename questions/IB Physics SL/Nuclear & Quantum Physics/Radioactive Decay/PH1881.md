# PH1881

One mole of deuterium nuclei $\ce{^{2}_{1}H}$ undergoes the following fusion reaction.

$\ce{^{2}_{1}H}+\ce{^{2}_{1}H}\rightarrow\ce{^{4}_{2}He}$

The total energy released in the fusion reaction is $Y$ and the binding energy per mole of deuterium is $X$.

What is the binding energy per mole of $\ce{^{4}_{2}He}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $X-Y$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $2X-2Y$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $X+Y$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $2X+2Y$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Binding energy per mole is the energy required to break all of the nuclei in one mole of a substance into its constituent nucleons.

One $\ce{^{4}_{2}He}$ nucleus is formed for every two $\ce{^{2}_{1}H}$ nuclei, so if one mole of $\ce{^{2}_{1}H}$ nuclei react, the amount of helium produced is 0.5 mol.

The energy required to break one mole of $\ce{^{2}_{1}H}$ nuclei into its constituent nucleons is $X$.

The energy **released** in the reaction is $Y$ so the energy required to break apart one mole of helium formed in the reaction is $X+Y$.

Therefore, the amount of energy required to break apart one mole of $\ce{^{4}_{2}He}$ (i.e., the binding energy per mole of $\ce{^{4}_{2}He}$) is given by:

- $\dfrac{X+Y}{0.5}=2X+2Y$
