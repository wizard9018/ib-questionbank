# PH0884

Nuclide X decays with a half-life of $T$ and nuclide Y decays with a half-life of $3T$. At $t = 0$, a sample of nuclide X has eight times the activity of a sample of nuclide Y.

When $t = 6 T$, the activity of the sample of nuclide X is $A$. What is the activity of the sample of nuclide Y at this time?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{16} A$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2} A$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $2 A$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $16 A$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Let the initial activity of nucleus Y be $A_{0}$. The initial activity of nucleus X is then $8A_{0}$.

For sample X, six half-lives have passed at $6T\left( \dfrac{6T}{T} =6 \right)$. Activity will halve six times so the activity of sample X at $t = 6 T$ is given by:

$\hspace{1em}$ $8 A_{0}\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right) ^{6} = \dfrac{8 A_{0}}{64} = \dfrac{A_{0}}{8}$

For sample Y, two half-lives have passed at $6 T \left( \dfrac{6 T}{3 T}\ =\ 2 \right)$. Activity will halve twice, so the activity of sample Y at $t = 6 T$ is given by:

$\hspace{1em}$ $A_{0} \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right) ^{2}\ =\ \dfrac{A_{0}}{4}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\textrm{Activity of sample X}}{\textrm{Activity of sample Y}} = \dfrac{A_{0}}{8} \div \dfrac{A_{0}}{4} = \left( \dfrac{A_{0}}{8} \right) \left( \dfrac{4}{A_{0}} \right) = \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Therefore, the activity of sample Y is two times that of sample X, which gives $2A$.
