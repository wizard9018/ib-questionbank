# PH1193

**Maximum mark: 10**

A 20 g pellet of Pu-239 is held in a light, closed vessel suspended from a spring. The spring constant of the spring is 0.30 N m$^{-1}$. Neutrons are fired through the wall of the vessel to initiate fission of the Pu-239 to produce Xe-134 and Zr-103. After the fission, the daughter nuclei remain within the pellet.

![PH1193](images/PH1193.png)

1. Explain why the mass of the pellet decreases during the fission reaction.[3 marks]

The binding energy per nucleon of Pu-239, Xe-134 and Zr-103 are as follows:

Binding energy per nucleon of Pu-239 = 7.56 MeV\
Binding energy per nucleon of Xe-134 = 8.41 MeV\
Binding energy per nucleon of Zr-103 = 8.43 MeV

2. 1. Show that the fission reaction yields a mass loss per Pu-239 nucleus of approximately $2u$. [3 marks]

    2. Show that the total mass of the pellet changes by about 190 mg when all the Pu-239 has undergone fission. [2 marks]

    3. All neutrons produced in the fission escape the vessel. Estimate the upward displacement of the vessel by the end of the fission reaction. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The sum of nucleons in the daughter nuclei is lower than the nucleon number of Pu-239 $\checkmark$

    OR

    There is a net loss of two neutrons from the pellet per nuclear fission $\checkmark$

    Daughter nuclei have higher binding energy per nucleon than parent $\checkmark$

    Released energy yields an equivalent mass defect (Einstein relation) $\checkmark$`

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    When a Pu-239 nucleus undergoes neutron-induced fission, it loses mass because the total mass number of its daughter nuclei ($134+103 = 237$) is lower than the initial mass number (239). This difference corresponds to the net loss of two neutrons during the fission reaction (three neutrons are released, whereas just one initiates the reaction).

    In addition to the decreased nucleon number, mass is lost as kinetic energy due to the daughter nuclei possessing higher binding energies per nucleon than the parent. This increase in binding energy is a necessary condition for the parent nucleus to undergo spontaneous fission after absorbing an incident neutron.

    Through Einstein’s equivalence relation between energy and mass ($E=mc^2$), the energy released during fission yields a corresponding mass decrease, or “mass defect”.

2. 1. ::indent
        - $\Delta BE = 188.39$ MeV $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta m(BE)=\dfrac{\Delta BE}{c^2}=\dfrac{(188.39\times10^6\,\textrm{eV})(1.60\times10^{-19}\,\ce{C})}{(3.00\times10^8\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1})^2(1.661\times10^{-27}\,\ce{kg})}u \approx 0.20 u$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta m=2.0 u+\Delta m(BE) \approx 2.2u$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        To calculate the mass defect per Pu-239 nucleus, one must first calculate the binding energy change during its fission. Using the values provided to sum the total binding energy of the daughter nuclei, then subtracting the total binding energy in the parent nucleus,

        - $\Delta BE=(134)(8.41\,\ce{MeV}) + (103)(8.43\,\ce{MeV}) - (239)(7.56\,\ce{MeV})$

        - $\Delta BE= 1,126.94\,\ce{MeV}+868.29\,\ce{MeV}-1,806.84\,\ce{MeV}$

        - $\Delta BE= 188.39\,\ce{MeV}$

        Using Einstein’s relation and converting to SI units,

        - $\Delta m(BE)=\dfrac{\Delta BE}{c^2}=\dfrac{(188.39 \times 10^6\,\textrm{eV})(1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\ce{C})}{(3.00 \times 10^8\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1})^2}$

        - $\Delta m(BE)=\dfrac{3.03 \times 10^{-11}\,\ce{J}}{9.00\times10^{16}\,\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}} \approx 3.349\times10^{-28}\,\ce{kg}=0.202 u$

        However, this value only accounts for the mass defect caused by the increased binding energy of nucleons; in addition, it is necessary to include the decrease in nucleon mass by two neutrons, or $2.017u$:

        - $\Delta m=2.017 u+0.202 u\approx2.22 u$

        Or, in SI units,

        - $\Delta m \approx 3.69\times10^{-27}\,\ce{kg}$

    2. Number of nuclei $=N_A\dfrac{20\,\ce{g}}{239\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}}=5.04\times10^{22}$ $\checkmark$

        $\Delta m_{tot}=N\Delta m=1.86\times10^{-4}\,\ce{kg}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The number of nuclei in the Pu-239 pellet is given by dividing the pellet’s mass (210 g) by the molar mass of Pu-239 (approximately 239 g mol$^{-1}$), then multiplying by Avogadro’s constant, $N_A$:

        - $N=N_A \dfrac{20\,\ce{g}}{239\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}}=6.02\times10^{23}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}\dfrac{20\,\ce{g}}{239\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}}$

        - $N = 5.04\times10^{22}$

        The total mass defect is equal to the mass loss per Pu-239 nucleus multiplied by the number of Pu-239 nuclei:

        - $\Delta m_{tot}=N\Delta m=(5.04\times10^{22})(3.69\times10^{-27}\,\ce{kg})=1.86\times10^{-4}\,\ce{kg}$

        - $\Delta m_{tot} = 186$ mg

    3. ::indent
        - $\Delta m_{tot}g=-k\Delta x$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta x = 6.1\,\ce{mm}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The change in displacement of the spring is related to the change in applied force by Hooke’s law:

        - $\Delta F=-k\Delta x$

        Substituting for the value of the mass loss yields

        - $\Delta F=\Delta m_{tot}g=(-1.86\times10^{-4}\,\ce{kg})(9.81\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-2})$

        - $\Delta F=-1.82\times10^{-3}$ N

        Finally, rearranging the equation gives

        - $\Delta x=-\dfrac{\Delta F}{k}=\dfrac{1.82\times10^{-3}\,\ce{N}}{0.3\,\ce{N}\,\ce{m}^{-1}}=6.07\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m} = 6.1\,\ce{mm}$
