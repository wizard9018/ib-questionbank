# PH1155

**Maximum mark: 10**

In a nuclear reactor, a fuel rod of U-235 is partially covered by boron control rods.

![PH1155](images/PH1155.png)

The decay of U-235 proceeds according to the equation

$\ce{_92^235{U}}+\ce{_0^1{n}} \rightarrow \ce{_56^144{Ba}}+\ce{_36^89{Kr}}+3\ce{_0^1{n}}$

1. With reference to the equation, explain the role of the control rods. [3 marks]

Each fuel rod contains 500 kg of U-235 and releases $2.23\times10^{29}$ MeV of energy if all the U-235 undergoes fission. The binding energy per nucleon of U-235 and Ba-144 are 7.59 MeV and 8.27 MeV, respectively.

2. 1. Show that one fuel rod contains approximately $1.3\times10^{27}$ nuclei. Assume that the mass of U-235 is $235u$. [1 mark]

    2. Calculate the binding energy per nucleon of Kr-89. [3 marks]

3. Show that the Kr-89 fragment receives a greater share of the released energy than the Ba-144 fragment upon fission. Assume that the U-235 nucleus is initially at rest, and that negligible energy is imparted to neutrons released in the fission reaction. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Each neutron released can lead to another fission reaction $\checkmark$

    Each fission reaction produces three times more neutrons than it uses $\checkmark$

    The control rod absorbs neutrons to prevent an uncontrolled chain reaction $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The fission of U-235 is induced when it absorbs a single neutron to yield an unstable U-236 nucleus, which subsequently breaks apart to release three neutrons and two daughter nuclei.

    The three neutrons may each induce further fission reactions, resulting in a chain reaction that could lead to a dangerous, uncontrolled increase in generated power.

    The purpose of the control rod is to absorb some of the released neutrons, preventing them from inducing fission reactions. In this way, the control rod ensures that the rate of fission reactions is maintained at a constant value.

2. 1. ::indent
        - Nuclei = $N_A\Bigl(\dfrac{\textrm{Fuel rod mass}}{\textrm{Molar mass }(\ce{_92^235{U})}}\Bigr)$

        - $\hspace{3em} =(6.02\times10^{23}\textrm{mol}^{-1})\Bigl(\dfrac{5\times10^5\,\ce{g}}{235\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}}\Bigr)=1.28\times10^{27}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The molar quantity of nuclei in each fuel rod is equal to the mass divided by the molar mass of U-235:

        - Moles = $\dfrac{\textrm{Fuel rod mass}}{\textrm{Molar mass } (\ce{_92^235{U}})}$

        Multiplying this value by Avogadro’s constant ($N_A$) gives the total number of nuclei:

        - Nuclei = $N_A\Bigl(\dfrac{\textrm{Fuel rod mass}}{\textrm{Molar mass } (\ce{_92^235{U}})}\Bigr)$

        Substituting the values provided into the equation yields $1.28\times10^{27}$ nuclei.

        - $=(6.02\times10^{23}\textrm{mol}^{-1})\Bigl(\dfrac{5\times10^5\,\ce{g}}{235\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}}\Bigr)=1.28\times10^{27}$

    2. ::indent
        - $\Delta BE$ per nucleus = $\dfrac{\textrm{Energy released}}{\textrm{ number of } \ce{_92^235{U}}\textrm{ nuclei}} = 174$ MeV $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta BE \textrm{ per nucleus} = 89BE(\ce{_36^89{Kr}}) + 144BE(\ce{_56^144{Ba}})-235BE(\ce{_92^235{U}})$

        - $89BE(\ce{_36^89{Kr}})+(144)(8.27\,\ce{MeV})-(235)(7.59\,\ce{MeV})=174$ MeV  $\checkmark$

        - $BE(\ce{_36^89{Kr}})=\dfrac{174+1.784\times10^3-1.191\times10^3}{89}\,\ce{MeV}=8.62\,\ce{MeV}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The change in binding energy per U-235 nucleus is given by the total energy released by a fuel rod divided by the number of U-235 nuclei:

        - $\Delta BE \textrm{ per nucleus}=\dfrac{\textrm{Energy released}}{\ce{_92^235{U}} \textrm{ nuclei}}=\dfrac{2.23\times10^{29}\,\ce{MeV}}{1.28\times10^{27}}=174$ MeV

        Defining $BE$(X) as the binding energy per nucleon in nucleus X, one may write

        - $\Delta BE \textrm{ per nucleus}=89BE(\ce{_36^89{Kr}})+144BE(\ce{_56^144{Ba}})-235BE(\ce{_92^235{U}})$

        Substituting for known values yields

        - $89BE(\ce{_36^89{Kr}})+(144)(8.27\,\ce{MeV})-(235)(7.59\,\ce{MeV})=174$ MeV

        Rearranging the equation to make the binding energy per nucleon of Kr-89 the subject, one obtains

        - $BE(\ce{_36^89{Kr}})=\dfrac{174+1.784\times10^3-1.191\times10^3}{89}\,\ce{MeV}=8.62$ MeV

3. ::indent
    - $m_{Kr}v_{Kr}=m_{Ba}v_{Ba}$   $\checkmark$

    - $E=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}$, $E\propto\dfrac{1}{m}$ $\checkmark$

    Kr-89 has the lower mass, therefore the greater kinetic energy $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    It is assumed that all the energy is converted to kinetic energy of the daughter nuclei. Conservation of momentum demands that these fragments possess equal and opposite momenta:

    - Momentum of $\ce{_36^89{Kr}}$ = Momentum of $\ce{_56^144{Ba}}$

    From the data booklet, $E=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}$

    We can see that because the momenta are equal, the kinetic energy is inversely proportional to the mass.

    Comparing the masses of Kr-89 and Ba-144, we see that the Kr will have the greater kinetic energy because it has the lower mass.
