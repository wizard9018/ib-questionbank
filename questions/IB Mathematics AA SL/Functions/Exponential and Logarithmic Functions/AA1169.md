# AA1169

**Maximum mark: 11**

Consider the function $f(x)=6x^2-7x+2$.

1.  Fully factorise $f(x)$. [2 marks]

\
Another function is defined as $g(x)=\ln x$.

A third function is defined as $h(x)=(g \circ f)(x)$.

2.  Hence, determine the domain of $h(x)$. [3 marks]

\
The value $a$ is added to $f(x)$, where $a \in \mathrm{R}$.

For the domain of $h(x)$ to be $x \in \mathrm{R}$ then $a>k$.

The minimum value of $k$ is $a_{\text{min}}$.

3.   Find $a_{\text{min}}$ [3 marks]

\
Now the value $b$ is added to $f(x)$, where $b \in \mathrm{R}$ and $b > a_{\text{min}}$.

4.  Find the value of $b$ such that the vertex of $h(x)$ lies on the $x-$axis. Give your answer to $4$ significant figures. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We will use the grouping method to factorise.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        a \times c &= 2 \times 6\\[6pt]
         &=12
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore we need to find two numbers that sum to $-7$ and multiply to give $12$. These are $-3$ and $-4$.

    Therefore, we can then rewrite $-7x$ as $-3x-4x$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        6x^2-7x+2&=6x^2-3x-4x+2 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We then factorise the resulting expression into two groups
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=3x(2x-1)-2(2x-1)\\[6pt]
        &=(2x-1)(3x-2) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  This is a composite function in which we form $h(x)$ by substituting $f(x)$ into $g(x)$. Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        h(x)&=\ln{\left(6x^2-7x+2\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now sketch $h(x)$ on our G.D.C.

    ![AA1169_a (2)](<images/AA1169_a (2).JPEG>)

    $h(x)$ has two vertical asymptotes and these occur when $h(x)=\ln{0}$. Hence $f(x)=0$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the answer from part (a) we can find their equations.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (2x-1)(3x-2)&=0\\[6pt]
        x&=\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{2}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The red arrows on the image above show the domain of $h(x)$.

    Therefore the $\text{domain of }h(x)\text{ is }x<\tfrac{1}{2}\text{ and }x>\tfrac{2}{3}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The following image shows $f(x)$ and $h(x)$ together on the same axes.

    ![AA1169_c (3)](<images/AA1169_c (3).JPEG>)

    Notice that $h(x)$ is undefined when $f(x)<0$, this is the dark blue part of $f(x)$.

    Hence we need to shift $f(x)$ vertically up $a$ units such that the vertex of $f(x)$ touches the $x-$axis at a single point.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{R1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    To do this we must find the minimum value of $f(x)$.

    Using a G.D.C. we can find the minimum point of $f(x)$ to be $(0.5833...,-0.04166...)$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence $f(x)+0.04166...$ will ensure $f(x)$ intersects the $x-$axis at one point. Ensuring that $f(x)\geq 0$ for all $x$. This is shown below

    ![AA1169_d](images/AA1169_d.JPEG)

    Therefore if $a>0.04166...$ then $f(x)>0$ for all $x$ and the domain of $h(x)$ is $x \in \mathrm{R}$.

    Hence, $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{a_{\text{min}}=0.0417}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Notice from the image in the previous part, that the vertex of $h(x)$ lies on the same vertical line as $f(x)$.

    Therefore the equation of the vertical line is $x=0.5833...$ and the final position of the vertex of $h(x)$ must be on the $x-$axis. This is shown below.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![AA1169_d2](images/AA1169_d2.JPEG)

    Therefore we need to solve this equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        h(0.5833...)&=0\\[6pt]
        (g \circ (f(0.5833...)+b))&=0\\[6pt]
        \ln{(f(0.5833...)+b)}&=0 \hspace{2em} (\ln{1}=0) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        f(0.5833...)+b&=1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    From part (c) we know that $f(0.5833...)=-0.04166...$ at the vertex of $f(x)$ therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        b&=1-(-0.04166...)\\[6pt]
        &=1.04166...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{b=1.042}\hspace{0.525em}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
