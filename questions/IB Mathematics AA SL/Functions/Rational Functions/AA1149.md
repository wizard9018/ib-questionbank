# AA1149

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider the function $\displaystyle f(x)=b+\frac{4}{x+a}$, $\hspace{1em}$ where $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$.

The equation of the vertical asymptote of $f(x)$ is $x=2$ and $f(\tfrac{2}{3})=0$.

1.  Write down the value of $a$.[1 mark]

2.  Hence, find the value of $b$. [3 marks]

3.  Hence, write down the equation of the horizontal asymptote.[1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The vertical asymptote occurs when the denominator of the fraction is equal to zero.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2+a&=0\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{-0.525em}\boxed{a=-2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  From the information given we know that the point $(\tfrac{2}{3},0)$ lies on the curve.

    Hence, we can create an equation and solve it for $b$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=b+\frac{4}{\tfrac{2}{3}-2} \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}\\[6pt]
        b&=-\frac{4}{-\tfrac{4}{3}}\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{-0.525em}\boxed{b=3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The horizontal asymptote occurs as $x \rightarrow \pm \infty$. We can see that as this happens the fractional part of $f(x)$ will tend to $0$.

    Hence the equation of the horizontal asymptote is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y&=3+\frac{4}{\pm \infty-2}\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{-0.525em}\boxed{y=3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The answer must be given as an equation, either $y=3$ or $f(x)=3$.
