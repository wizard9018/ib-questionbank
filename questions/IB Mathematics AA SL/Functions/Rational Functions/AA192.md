# AA192

**Maximum mark: 15**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{5}{x+1}$, for $x \neq -1$.

1.  For the graph of $f$, find the coordinates of:
    1.  the $x$-intercept;

    2.  the $y$-intercept;

    3.  the equation of the vertical asymptote. [5 marks]

Let $g(x) = x-3$, for $x \in \mathbb{R}$. The graphs of $f$ and $g$
intersect at points A and B.

2.  Find the coordinates of A and B. [5 marks]

Line $L$ is the perpendicular bisector of A and B.

3.  Find the equation of $L$, giving your answer in the form $y = mx + c$. [3 marks]

4.  Find the $x$-intercept of $L$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Since the equation $f(x) = 0$ has no solutions, there are
         $\text{no \(x\)-intercepts}$
        $\\[-20pt]$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Evaluating $f(x) = \dfrac{5}{x+1}$ for $x = 0$, we have

         $$
         \begin{aligned}
         f(0) &= \dfrac{5}{0+1} \hspace{3.6em} \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
         &= 5
         \end{aligned}
         $$
         Hence the $y$-intercept is
         $\mathrm{P}(0,5)$
        $\\[-20pt]$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        x+1 &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
        x &= -1
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the equation of the vertical
        asymptote is
         $x = -1$
        $\\[-20pt]$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  Solving the equation $f(x) = g(x)$ for $x$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{5}{x+1} &= x-3 \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}} \\[12pt]
        5 &= (x+1)(x-3) \\[9pt]
        5 &= x^2 - 2x - 3 \\[9pt]
        0 &= x^2 - 2x - 8 \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[10pt]
        0 &= (x+2)(x-4) \\[10pt]
        x &= -2,4 \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating $g(x) = x-3$
    for $x = -2$ and $x = 4$, we find
    $$
    \hspace{3.1em} \begin{array}{rcccl}
    y &=& g(-2) &=& -5 \tag*{\textbf{{A1A1}}}\\[9pt]
    y &=& g(4) &=& 1.
    \end{array}
    $$
    Hence we find
     $\mathrm{A}(-2,-5)$
    and
     $\mathrm{B}(4,1)$

3.  As A and B lie on $g$ then the gradient of line AB is the gradient of $g$, therefore $m_{AB}=1$.

    Hence, the gradient of $L$, the perpendicular bisector, is $m_L=-1$.

    The midpoint of A$(-2,-5)$ and B$(4,1)$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    M_{AB}&= \left(\frac{-2+4}{2},\frac{-5+1}{2}\right)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[12pt]
    &= (1,-2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the point-gradient form of the equation of a line (__2.1__)with  $M_{AB}(1,-2)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y-(-2) &= -1(x-1)\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[6pt]
    y+2 &= -x+1\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.8em}y=-x-1\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.
    At the $x$-intercept, $y=0$.

    Substituting this into the equation of $L$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0 &= -x-1\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
    x &= -1.\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
