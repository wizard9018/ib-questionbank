# AA1150

**Maximum mark: 4**

A geometric series has a common ratio of $3^x$.

1.  State the domain of $x$ such that the sum to infinity of the series exists. [2 marks]

The sum to infinity of the series is found to be $54$.

2. Find the value of $x$, given that the first term of the series is $52$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have $\bm{S_{\infty}}\bm{=}\dfrac{\bm{u_1}}{\bm{1-r}}\,,\,\bm{|r|<1}$ **(1.8)**.

    That is, the common ratio ($r$) must be between $-1$ and $1$ for the sum to infinity to exist. Therefore,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -1<3^x<1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $3^x$ is larger than zero for all $x$, therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0<3^x<1\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![3^x](images/3^x.png)

    From the sketch of $3^x$ we can see that when $x<0$ then $0<3^x<1$.
    $$
    \\[-2.5em]
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The domain such that the sum to infinity exists is $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{x<0}$
    $$
    \\[-2.5em]
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  Using the formula for the sum of an infinite geometric series with $S_{\infty}=54$, $r=3^x$ and $u_1=52$ we can write the equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        54&=\frac{52}{1-3^x}\\[6pt]
        1-3^x&=\frac{26}{27}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        3^x&=\frac{1}{27}\\[6pt]
        3^x&=3^{-3}\\[6pt]
        x&=-3\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
