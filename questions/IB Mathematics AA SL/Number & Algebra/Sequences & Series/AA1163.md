# AA1163

**Maximum mark: 6**

An arithmetic progression has a common difference of $d$, where $d \neq 0$.

The $7^{\text{th}}$ term is equal to the $3^{\text{rd}}$ term squared and the $5^{\text{th}}$ term is $1$.

1.  Find the common difference. [4 marks]

$S_n$ is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence.

2.  Find the largest value of $n$ such that $S_n<300$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We can use the information given to form an equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        u_7&=(u_3)^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the general term of an arithmetic progression, we can write the equation in terms of the first term, $u_1$, and $d$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        u_1+(7-1) \times d&=(u_1 + (3-1) \times d)^2\\[6pt]
        u_1+6d&=(u_1 + 2d)^2\\[6pt]
        u_1+6d&=u_1^2+4u_1d+4d^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the given fact $u_5=1$ we can write $u_1$ in terms of $d$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1&=u_1+4d\\[6pt]
    u_1&=1-4d
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now write the original equation in terms of $d$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        1-4d+6d&=(1-4d)^2+4(1-4d)d+4d^2 \tag*{\textbf{A1M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now simplify both sides of this equation and then solve the equation graphically.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        1+2d&=(1-4d)^2+4d-16d^2+4d^2\\[6pt]
        1+2d&=(1-4d)^2+4d-12d^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We do this by sketching the L.H.S. and R.H.S. of the above equation, as two seperate functions, using a G.D.C. and then finding the points of intersection
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f(x)=&=1+2x\\[6pt]
        g(x)&=(1-4x)^2+4x-12x^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![AA1163_a](images/AA1163_a.JPEG)

    Hence we can see that the intersection points tell us that $d=0$ and $d=\tfrac{3}{2}$.
    As $d\neq0$ then the common difference is $d=\tfrac{3}{2}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the formula for the sum $n$ terms of an arithmetic progression with $d=\tfrac{3}{2}$ we can write $S_n$ in terms of $u_1$ and $n$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        S_n&=\frac{n}{2}(2u_1+\tfrac{3}{2}(n-1))
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can find $u_1$ by using the 5$^{th}$ term
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        1&=u_1+4\times \tfrac{3}{2}\\[6pt]
        u_1&=-5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we can now write $S_n$ in terms of $n$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        S_n&=\frac{n}{2}(2(-5)+\tfrac{3}{2}(n-1)) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can use the G.D.C. to produce a table of values for $S_n$

    ![table_2](images/table_2.png)

    We can see that for $S_n<300$ the largest value of $n$ is $24$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
