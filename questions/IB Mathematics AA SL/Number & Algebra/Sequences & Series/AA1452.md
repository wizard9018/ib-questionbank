# AA1452

**Maximum mark: 8**

The $m$th, $n$th and $k$th terms of an arithmetic sequence are $a$, $b$ and $c$ respectively, where $a,b,c>0$. The common difference of the arithmetic sequence is $d$.

1. Show that $b-c=(n-k)d$.[2 marks]

2. Hence show that $(b-c)(m-1)+(c-a)(n-1)+(a-b)(k-1)=0$. [2 marks]

    

The $m$th, $n$th and $k$th terms of a geometric sequence, with first term $v_1>0$ and common ratio $r>0$, are also $a$, $b$ and $c$ respectively.

3. Hence show that $a^{\,b-c}\,b^{\,c-a}\,c^{\,a-b}=1$.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using **the formula for the $\bm{n}$th term of an arithmetic sequence (1.2)**, $u_n = u_1+(n-1)d$, let's first write expressions for $u_m, u_n$ and $u_k$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_m &= u_1+(m-1)d \\[6pt]

    u_n &= u_1+(n-1)d \\[6pt]

    u_k &= u_1+(k-1)d

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have that $u_m = a, u_n=b$ and $u_k=c$ and therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    a &= u_1+(m-1)d \tag*{\textbf{[1]}}\\[6pt]

    b &= u_1+(n-1)d \tag*{\textbf{[2]}}\\[6pt]

    c &= u_1+(k-1)d \tag*{\textbf{[3]}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Subtracting equation **[3]** from equation **[2]**, we can form an expression for $b-c$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    b-c &= \bigl[u_1+(n-1)d\bigr]-\bigl[u_1+(k-1)d\bigr] \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[6pt]

    &= (n-1)d-(k-1)d \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]

    &= d(n-1-(k-1))\\[6pt]

    b-c &= \boxed{(n-k)d}\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Using the method of part (a), we can form expressions for $a-b$ and $c-a$. This yields

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    a-b&=(m-n)d \\[6pt]

    c-a&=(k-m)d

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting these into the left-hand side of the given expression, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &(b-c)(m-1)+(c-a)(n-1)+(a-b)(k-1) \\[6pt]

    =&(n-k)d(m-1)+(k-m)d(n-1)+(m-n)d(k-1) \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[6pt]

    =&d\left[(n-k)(m-1)+(k-m)(n-1)+(m-n)(k-1)\right]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Expanding the brackets, we obtain

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{LHS} &= d\bigl[(mn-n-mk+k) \\

    &\quad+(kn-k-mn+m) \\

    &\quad+(mk-m-nk+n)\bigr] \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]

    &= \boxed{0} \hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Using **the $\bm{n}$th term of a geometric sequence (1.3)**, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_n &= u_1r^{n-1} \\[6pt]

    a &= v_1r^{m-1} \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[6pt]

    b &= v_1r^{n-1} \\[6pt]

    c &= v_1r^{k-1}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Substituting into the left-hand side of the given expression, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    a^{\,b-c}\,b^{\,c-a}\,c^{\,a-b} &= \left(v_1r^{m-1}\right)^{b-c}\left(v_1r^{n-1}\right)^{c-a}\left(v_1r^{k-1}\right)^{a-b} \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[6pt]

    &= v_1^{b-c}r^{(m-1)(b-c)} \cdot v_1^{c-a}r^{(n-1)(c-a)} \cdot v_1^{a-b}r^{(k-1)(a-b)}\\[6pt]

    &= v_1^{(b-c)+(c-a)+(a-b)} \cdot r^{(m-1)(b-c)+(n-1)(c-a)+(k-1)(a-b)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]

    &= v^0 \cdot r^{(b-c)(m-1)+(c-a)(n-1)+(a-b)(k-1)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Using our result from part (b), the exponent of $r$ is $0$. Therefore we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    a^{\,b-c}\,b^{\,c-a}\,c^{\,a-b} &= v_1^{0}\,r^{0}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]

    &= \boxed{1} \hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
