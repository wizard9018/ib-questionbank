# AA832

**Maximum mark: 5**

An arithmetic sequence has first term $45$ and common difference $-1.5$.

1.  Given that the $k$th term of the sequence is zero, find the value of
    $k$. [2 marks]

Let $S_n$ denote the sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence.

2.  Find the maximum value of $S_n$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The $k$th term in the sequence is given by $u_k = u_1 + (k-1)d$ with
    $u_1 = 45$ and $d = -1.5$.

    Since $u_k = 0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    45 + (k-1)(-1.5) &= 0 \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-0.5em}k = 31 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Since the common difference $d$ is negative, the terms in the
    sequence are decreasing. From part (a), we found that the $31$st
    term in the sequence is zero. Hence, any term after this would be
    negative. Therefore, the sum $S_n$ is maximised when $n = 30$ or
    $n = 31$ (as $u_{31}=0$).

    Using the formula $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}\left(u_1 + u_n \right)$ with
    $n = 31$, we find that the maximum value of $S_n$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S_{31} &= \dfrac{31}{2}(u_{1} + u_{31}) \hspace{3em}\\[10pt]
    S_{31} &= \dfrac{31}{2}(45 + 0) \hspace{3em}\\[10pt]
    &= 697.5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
