# AA1037

**Maximum mark: 15**

The arithmetic sequence has the sixth term of $u_6=\ln{2}$ and the eighth term of $u_8=\frac{3}{2}\ln{4}$.

1.  1.  Show the common difference is $\ln2$.

    2.  Find $u_1$.

    3.  Hence, show that the sum of $n$ terms is given by $S_n=\left(n^2-9n\right)\ln {\sqrt{2}} $. Where $n \in \mathbb{Z^+}$. [5 marks]

A new function, $f(x)$, is formed using $S_n$ with the discrete variable $n$ being replaced with the continuous variable $x$, such that $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence $f(x)=\left(x^2-9x\right)\ln {\sqrt{2}}$.

2.  1.  Write down the equation of the axis of symmetry of $f(x)$.

    2.  Sketch $f(x)$ on the grid below, identify the axis of symmetry and any axis intercepts.

        ![q1037](images/q1037.png)

    3.  Hence or otherwise, find the minimum value of $S_n$ in the form $a\ln b$. Where $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$ [5 marks]

Consider the linear function $g(x)=(6-3x)\ln {\sqrt 2}$.

3.  1.  Solve $f(x)=g(x)$.

    2.  Hence, solve the inequality $f(x)<g(x)$.

    3.  Hence or otherwise, find the range of values of $n$ that satisfy $S_n<g(x)$. [5 marks]

1.  1.  As the sequence is arithmetic we can write $u_8=u_6+2d$. Hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \tfrac{3}{2}\ln{4}&=\ln{2}+2d \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\
            2d&=3\ln{(\sqrt{4})}-\ln2\\
            2d&=2\ln{2}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\ln2}\hspace{2.525em} \text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.  We can use $u_6$ to find the first term
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            u_6&=u_1+5d\\
            \ln2&=u_1+5\ln2\\
            u_1&=\hspace{0.525em}-4\ln2 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    3.  Using the sum of $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence we can write
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            S_n&=\frac{n}{2}\left(2\times -4 \ln2 +(n-1)\times \ln2\right) \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\
            &=\frac{n^2}{2}\ln2-\frac{9n}{2}\ln2\\
            &=\frac{\ln2}{2}\left(n^2-9n\right) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\left(n^2-9n\right)\ln{\sqrt{2}} \hspace{2.525em} \text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  1.  Equate the RHS of each function
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \left(x^2-9x\right)\ln {\sqrt 2}&=(6-3x)\ln {\sqrt 2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Cancel out the factor $\ln {\sqrt 2}$ and solve for $x$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            x^2-9x&=6-3x\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\
            0&=x^2-6x-6
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can solve this by completing the square.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            (x-3)^2-9-6&=0\\
            x-3&=\pm\sqrt{15}\\
            x&=\hspace{0.525em}3\pm\sqrt{15} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
