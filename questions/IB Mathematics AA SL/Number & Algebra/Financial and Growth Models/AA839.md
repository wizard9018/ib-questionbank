# AA839

**Maximum mark: 14**

Alex and Julie each have a goal of saving $$\$30\hspace{0.15em}000$$ to
put towards a house deposit. They each have $$\$16\hspace{0.15em}000$$ to
invest.

1.  Alex chooses his local bank and invests his $$\$16\hspace{0.15em}000$$
    in a savings account that offers an interest rate of $5\%$ per annum
    compounded **annually**.

    1.  Find the value of Alex's investment after $7$ years, to the
        nearest hundred dollars.

    2.  Alex reaches his goal after n years, where n is an integer.
        Determine the value of n. [4 marks]

2.  Julie chooses a different bank and invests her
    $$\$16\hspace{0.15em}000$$ in a savings account that offers an
    interest rate of $r\%$ per annum compounded **monthly**, where $r$
    is set to two decimal places.

    Find the minimum value of $r$ needed for Julie to reach her goal
    after $10$ years. [3 marks]

3.  Xavier also wants to reach a savings goal of
    $$\$30 \hspace{0.15em}000$$. He doesn't trust his local bank so he
    decides to initially put his money into a safety deposit box where it does not
    earn any interest. His system is to add more money into the safety
    deposit box at the end of each year. At the end of each year he will add one third of the amount
    he added at the end of the previous year.

    1.  Show that Xavier will never reach his goal if his initial
        deposit into the safety deposit box is $$\$16\hspace{0.15em}000$$.

    2.  Find the amount Xavier needs to initially deposit in order to
        reach his goal after $7$ years. Give your answer to the nearest
        dollar. [7 marks]

    $\\[-2em]$

3.
    1.  The total amount of money in Xavier's safe after an infinite
        number of years is expressed by
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        A &= 16\hspace{0.15em}000 + 16\hspace{0.15em}000\left( \dfrac 1 3 \right) + 16\hspace{0.15em}000\left( \dfrac 1 3 \right)^2 +  16\hspace{0.15em}000\left( \dfrac 1 3 \right)^{3} + \dots
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We have an infinite geometric series with
        the first term $u_1 = 16\hspace{0.15em}000$ and the common ratio
        $r = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

        Hence, using **the formula for the sum of an infinite geometric sequence (1.8)**,
        $\text{\(S_{\infty} = \dfrac{u_1}{1-r}\)}$, we obtain

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        A &=  \dfrac{16\hspace{0.15em}000}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} \hspace{5.8em}\\[8pt]
        A &=\$24\hspace{0.15em}000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence he will never reach the
        $$\$30\hspace{0.15em}000$$ target if his initial deposit is
        $$\$16\hspace{0.15em}000$$.

    2.  Let $P$ represent the initial deposit. Note that after the initial deposit, there will be a further $7$ deposits, one at the end of each year.

        The amount of money that Xavier saves after $7$ years, $A$, is

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        A &= P + P\left( \dfrac 1 3 \right) + P\left( \dfrac 1 3 \right)^2 + \dots + P\left( \dfrac 1 3 \right)^{7} \hspace{2em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We have a geometric series with the first
        term $u_1 = P$, common ratio $r = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $8$ terms in total.

        Hence, using the formula **for the sum of $\bm{n}$ terms of a finite geometric sequence (1.3)**, $S_n = \dfrac{u_1(1-r^n)}{1-r}$
        with $\text{\(n = 8\)}$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{A} &= S_{8} \\[6pt]
        30\hspace{0.15em}000 &= \dfrac{P\left(1-\left( \frac 1 3 \right)^8\right)}{1-\frac{1}{3}} \\[12pt]
        P &= \dfrac{20\,000}{1-\left( \frac 1 3 \right)^8}\\[12pt]
        P&=\$20\hspace{0.15em}003 \hspace{1em}\text{ [to the nearest dollar]}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
