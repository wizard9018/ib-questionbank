# AA950

**Maximum mark: 5**

1.  Prove that $\dfrac{5}{x^2} = \dfrac{5}{x(x-2)}-\dfrac{10}{x^2(x-2)}$. [3 marks]

2. Determine the set of numbers $x$ for which the proof in part (a) is valid. [2 marks]
$$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. By working out the right side of the equation, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{5}{x(x-2)}-\dfrac{10}{x^2(x-2)} &= \dfrac{5x}{x^2(x-2)}-\dfrac{10}{x^2(x-2)} \\[9pt]
        &= \dfrac{5x-10}{x^2(x-2)} \\[9pt]
        &= \dfrac{5\ \cancel{(x-2)}}{x^2\ \cancel{(x-2)}} \\[9pt]
        &= \dfrac{5}{x^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $\boxed{\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}}$, as required.

2. We cannot have a denominator equal to zero, and therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x^2 &\neq 0 \Rightarrow x \neq 0 \hspace{3.95em} \text{[from L.H.S.]} \\[8pt]
        x(x-2) &\neq 0 \Rightarrow x \neq 0 , 2 \hspace{3em} \text{[from R.H.S.]} \\[8pt]
        x^2(x-2) &\neq 0 \Rightarrow x \neq 0 , 2 \hspace{3em} \text{[from R.H.S.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore the proof is valid for all values of $x$ except $x = 0$ and $x=2$.\
$x \in \mathbb{R}, x \neq 0 \text{  and  } x\neq 2$
