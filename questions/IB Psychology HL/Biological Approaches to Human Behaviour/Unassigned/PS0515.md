# PS0515

Explain one research method used in the study of the hormones and behaviour.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

The response should focus on the topic posed in the question within the first few lines (*e.g. One research method used to study hormones and behaviour is true experiments.*). As the question clearly refers to one research method, only one research method used to study hormones and behaviour should be mentioned along with a clear explanation (*e.g. Experiments are a quantitative method of research where an independent variable (IV) is clearly manipulated and its effect on the dependent variable(s) (DV) is closely measured.*).

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

There should be accurate knowledge and understanding of the topic using all or some key terms, such as:

-    True experiment - an experiment when the IV is manipulated, and participants are randomly allocated to conditions

-    Case study - involves various research methods and detailed investigation of one individual or a small group of people.

-    Natural experiment - an experiment when the IV cannot be manipulated and is a naturally occurring phenomenon without randomly allocating participants. 

-    Variables - IV, or the independent variable, is the one that is manipulated, and the DV or dependent variable is measured.

-    Random allocation - participants are placed randomly in any of the conditions

-   Quasi-experiments - random allocation of participants to conditions is not possible

 

The command term Explain is defined as $`$to provide a detailed account including possible causes or reasons'. Therefore, while explaining research methods is essential, there should also be a focus on meeting the command term. There should be a clear explanation of the topic and why it has been selected for use in the study context (*e.g. This research method would be most appropriate in studies like these as…*).

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study - including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study. *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Newcomer et al. (1999) on cortisol- True experiment

-   Kandasamy et al. (2014): cortisol and risk aversion - a true experiment

-    Goetz et al. (2014): Testosterone and aggression - a true experiment

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

There should be a clear link between the study results and the question. (*e.g. This study shows a clear manipulation of the IV - level of the hormone cortisol (high, low and placebo) to see the effect on the DV (memory). This study helps to show how and why experiments are used to study…*)

**Marking limits:**

-    Including a research study related to the brain and behaviour or genes and behaviour, rather than hormones and behaviour (*a maximum of 3 marks will be awarded*) 

-   No clear description of the research method is given. (*a maximum of 6 marks can be awarded*)

-   Not selecting an appropriate study from the unit. (*no marks will be awarded*)

-   A relevant study is described, but, no clear links to research methods are made. (*a maximum of 5 marks can be awarded*)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

One research method used to study hormones and behaviour is true experiments. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ 

Experiments are a quantitative method of research where an independent variable (IV) is clearly manipulated and its effect on the dependent variable(s) (DV) is closely measured. Furthermore, the researchers randomly allocate participants to conditions.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ True experiments allow the researcher to establish a cause-and-effect relationship between the IV and the DV and are highly standardised, increasing internal validity. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Another feature of experiments is the ability to control extraneous variables to further strengthen the cause-and-effect relationship between variables. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Researchers sometimes manipulate aspects of physiology (e.g., hormone levels) to see the effect on behaviour or vice versa. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

Newcomer et.al (1999) conducted a study to test the role of the stress hormone cortisol on verbal declarative memory. He assigned participants randomly to three different conditions - a high cortisol group who received 160 mg of cortisol each day of the condition equating to a highly stressful situation. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The second condition had a low level of cortisol around 40 mg similar to a low stressful event and the third was a placebo group. The  experiment was carried on for four days and the participants were tested thrice at different times for their memory by asking to recall a prose passage. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Baseline test was conducted to establish that there were no significant differences in their memory. Results from the study revealed that cortisol impairs memory function as the group with the highest cortisol showed significantly poorer recall when compared to the other groups. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

 

From the methodology of the study cited above it can be clearly seen how this research used a true experiment to establish a strong cause and effect relationship between stress and memory as it was able to provide evidence that stress hormone cortisol impairs memory negatively as can be seen in the performance of the participants in condition 1. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$  This cause and effect can be further strengthened as participants were randomly allocated to conditions, decreasing bias and allowing for individual participant differences to be evenly distributed across conditions. Extraneous variables were controlled as the baseline memory of all participants was also established prior to the start of the study. Furthermore, all participants completed the same tests, under the same conditions, with the only difference being the level of cortisol. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$ This helps to isolate the effect of the IV. Therefore, this study shows a clear manipulation of the IV - level of the hormone cortisol (high, low and placebo) to see the effect on the DV (memory). This study helps to show how and why experiments are used to study the effect of hormones on human behaviour such as memory. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$
