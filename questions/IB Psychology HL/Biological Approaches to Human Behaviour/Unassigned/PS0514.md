# PS0514

Explain one ethical consideration on one research study on the brain and human behaviour. 

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

The response should have a direct answer on the topic posed in the question within the first few lines. *(e.g. There are various ethical guidelines in psychological research such as …*) As the question clearly refers to ethical considerations, it should be made clear that any violation of ethical guidelines can be considered an ethical issue/consideration and should be referred to in the first few lines. A specific ethical guideline/s which has been violated in the context of the study from within the sub-topic of brain and behaviour must be mentioned along with a clear explanation. (*e.g. Therefore, we can understand how this could be considered an ethical consideration in the study of…*)

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

There should be accurate knowledge and understanding of the topic by using all or some key terms such as:

1.    Consent - giving details about the experiment to the participants and receiving permission from them 

2.    Anonymity - keeping the name and other details that might reveal the person's identity confidential.

3.    Rights to withdraw - the rights of the participant to withdraw from the experiment anytime they want to.  Even after the experiment is over, they can choose to withdraw their data

4.    Deception - Giving the participant false information about the aim of the study

5.    Undue psychological stress - psychological stress that the participant may face during the experiment

6.    Physical harm - the possibility of physical harm the participant may face during the experiment

7.    Animal welfare and protection  - Laws established to protect the welfare and life of the animals used in research.

The command term in the question is $`$explain'. The response should provide a detailed account, including possible causes or reasons. Therefore, while explaining ethical violations is essential, there should also be a focus on meeting the command term. There should be a clear explanation of the topic and why these ethical violations would occur in studies like these (*e.g. This could occur due to…*).

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study - including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study. *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Milner (1966) case study on HM

-    Antonova (2011) on the role of neurotransmitters on behaviour 

-    Squire (2002) case study on Eugene Pauly

-    Rosenzweig, Bennet $\&$ Diamond (1972) on the role of neuroplasticity on human behaviour using animal studies. 

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

There should be a clear link between the results of the study to the topic using one of the key terms mentioned in the introduction. (*e.g. Rosenzweig, Bennet & Diamond (1972) used rats to understand neuroplasticity and its possible effects on human behaviour. In order to understand the changes that had taken place in the rat's brain, they were killed, and a brain study was done which is in direct conflict with the ethical guideline on animal welfare which states that no harm should come to animals as a consequence of a study. This study had a clear ethical violation as even though there could be a justification for the use of such methods, it is still undeniably unethical*)

**Marking limits:**

-    Not selecting an appropriate study from the topic. (*no marks will be awarded*) 

-    Focussing on ethical guidelines and not ethical issues of the study. (*up to 4 marks will be awarded depending on the research cited*)

-    A relevant study is described but no clear links to ethics are made. (*up to 4 marks will be awarded*)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Ethical guidelines in psychological research are very important, and it is critical that all research follows these guidelines to be recognised and included as part of the wider database. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$  $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Various Psychological organisations around the world, such as APA (American Psychological Association) and BPS (British Psychological Society), are responsible for issuing and enforcing these guidelines.  They are created to protect the participants during and after research. One of the common ethical guidelines is informed consent - this refers to the process of receiving consent from the participants before the start of the experiment. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ It is to be noted that parental consent is also required in the case of participants younger than 16 years. This is done to ensure that the participants have complete information about the study they will participate in and agree to share their data as part of the research process. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ All necessary information regarding the study topic and its procedure should be mentioned in the form the participants must sign before the experiment starts. Additionally, the consent sheet should also include the rights of the participants, such as ensuring their confidentiality, anonymity, and reiterating their right to withdraw from the study at any time they want. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ One of the most well-known case studies conducted was on HM by Milner in 1966, which clearly shows the violation of this ethical guideline. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

HM was a 27-year-old man who was suffering from continuous seizures since the age of 7 when he was involved in a bicycle accident. After various treatments, his posterior and anterior hippocampus were surgically removed as a last attempt to treat his seizures. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Though his seizures completely stopped, it was observed that he could not convert short-term memories to long-term memories. Milner then used HM as a case study up until he died in 2008 to examine the role of the hippocampus in the conversion of memories and performed various tests and procedures on him. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ He was continually subjected to a series of MRI scans, cognitive tests, interviews, observations, etc, for an extended period of time to test the effect of his surgery. Furthermore, after his death, his brain was extracted and autopsied to study it in further detail. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The ethical guideline violated in the HM case study was informed consent. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$ This could have occurred as he was not physically capable of giving consent to participate in the experiment. Even if he did, he would not remember it after a while as he had a complete loss of episodic memories. Though there is evidence of family consent, it is still essential to note that at the time of the study, HM was a grown man, and his consent should have been present, especially as he was continually subjected to a series of tests that were quite repetitive as they wanted to test if he is able to create procedural memories without him being aware of it. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$ It can be argued that while this can be considered as a violation, it also needs to be evaluated as a cost-benefit model as findings from his case study have been used to advance researcher's knowledge of memories and help them to develop strategies and drugs that could be used help in the treatment of diseases such as Alzheimer's etc. However, the ethical concerns regarding HM are valid as he gave up his entire life to science without even being actively aware of doing so. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$
