# PS0510

Explain how one hormone may affect behaviour using one relevant study.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

There should be a direct answer to the question within the first few lines. A specific hormone affecting a behaviour which is being investigated through the study must be mentioned. (*e.g. One hormone that is linked to behaviour is cortisol which is often associated with stress and verbal declarative memory.*)

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

There should be accurate knowledge and understanding of the topic by using all or some key terms, such as:

-    Hormones - chemical substances that help pass information from one part of the body to another through the bloodstream.

-    Endocrine system - a network of glands that helps to control various bodily functions through hormones.

-    Target cells - Cells that receive the messages from the hormones as they have the specific receptors for the hormones

-    Receptors - detect signals from different hormones/neurotransmitters

The focus of the response should be on the effects of hormones on human behaviour and not its physiological functions. This should be done in a rational and easy-to-understand manner.

The command term $`$explain' seeks to provide a detailed account including possible causes or reasons. Therefore, while the explanation of the effect of hormones is essential there should also be a focus on meeting the command term. The possible reasons for a change in that behaviour due to hormone fluctuations should also be considered in the essay (*e.g. This could be due to changes in…*)

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study - including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study.\

 *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Newcomer et.al (1999) on the effect of Cortisol on Verbal declarative memory

-    Ronay and von Hippel (2010) on the effect of testosterone in risk-taking behaviour of males

-    Mc Gaugh and Cahill (1995) on the effect of adrenaline on flashbulb memories

-    Zethraeus et. al (2009) on the effect of oestrogen and testosterone on human economic behaviour

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

There should be a clear link between the results of the study and the topic using one of the key terms mentioned in the introduction. For example, using Mcgaugh and Cahill (1995) on the effect of adrenaline on flashbulb memories  a possible link would be *$\text{\textquotedblleft}$Adrenaline released during a stressful situation such as the dramatic story in the first experiment led to the formation of flashbulb memories in the participants as measured by their performance on the recognition task 2 weeks later.$\text{\textquotedblright}$*

**Marking limits:**

-    Mixing up hormones with neurotransmitters. (*no marks will be awarded*)

-    A relevant study is described but no clear links to the effect of hormones on behaviour are made (*a maximum of 5 marks will be awarded*)

-    Wrong hormone linked to the behaviour (*a maximum of 3 marks may be deducted*)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Hormones are chemicals secreted by glands in the endocrine system directly in the bloodstream. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  As they are secreted in the bloodstream and not in the synaptic gaps, it takes time to produce an effect on behaviour but they are more long-lasting than a typical neurotransmitter response. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The hormones bind themselves to specific receptor sites known as target cells bringing about a change in behaviour.Hormones affect human behaviour in myriad ways as a single hormone can have effects on multiple behaviours. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Testosterone, a hormone produced by the testes in males is known for its effect on aggression and aggressive behaviour. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ However, there is also evidence of its effect on risk-taking behaviour seen in males as suggested by the results of the study conducted by Ronay and von Hippel (2010). $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

   

Ronay and von Hippel (2010) carried out a study on 96 young adult male skateboarders to find out if testosterone affected their risk-taking behaviour. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Participants were divided into two conditions - one with a male researcher and the other with an attractive female researcher. All the participants were asked to practise two $`$types' of tricks which they could do fairly well - an $`$easy' trick and a $`$difficult' trick. After this, they were asked to attempt the same tricks either in front of the same male researcher or an attractive female researcher. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Their behaviour was coded in one of the three ways, a successful attempt, crash landing or aborted attempt. Biometric measurements of saliva samples were collected to measure the level of testosterone. Results from the study suggested that participants in the female researcher condition took greater risks as they had fewer aborted attempts in the $`$difficult' tricks. In addition, testosterone was higher in this group as measured by saliva samples. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The study concluded that testosterone had an effect on the risk-taking behaviour of males as they were willing to attempt more difficult tricks and did not abort it in the presence of an attractive female researcher. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ This can be clearly linked to the increased level of testosterone as there was a clear difference in the performance between the two groups  which occurred due to the presence of the female researcher. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$

Testosterone is a reproductive hormone that is associated with the sexual behaviour of males having clear links to the mating behaviour observed in males. As can be seen from the study, the skateboarders performed the $`$difficult' tricks with fewer aborted attempts meaning they were willing to take more risks in the presence of a female researcher as compared to when a male researcher was present indicating the mating behaviour linked to the presence of increased levels of testosterone. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$
