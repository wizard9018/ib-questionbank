# PS0779

What were the main findings of Meaney et al.'s (1988) study on glucocorticoids and memory, and what do these findings suggest about the effects of early-life stress?
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

The study found that rats exposed to high levels of glucocorticoids (stress hormones) in early life showed hippocampal cell loss and memory deficits in old age. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) Rats in the treatment group, who received daily handling and grooming, had fewer cognitive impairments and less hippocampal damage. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) These results suggest that early-life stress, marked by high glucocorticoid exposure, can lead to long-term cognitive problems by accelerating brain cell loss, particularly in the hippocampus, a region critical for memory. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) 

$\underline{\textrm{Marking Guidance}}$:

- 1 mark for describing the memory and brain structure results.

- 1 mark for explaining the correlation between early-life stress and cognitive deficits.

- 1 mark for linking the findings to long-term impacts on memory and brain function.

- Marks should be awarded for any similarly worded explanations of the findings to a maximum of 3 marks.
