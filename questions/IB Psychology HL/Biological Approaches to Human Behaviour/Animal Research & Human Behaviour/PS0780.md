# PS0780

Meaney et al.'s (1988) study on the role of stress hormones in memory provides valuable insights into how early-life stress can affect long-term cognitive functioning. The study emphasised the role of glucocorticoids in brain function. It introduced the concept of epigenetics, showing how early environmental factors, like maternal care, can influence gene expression and affect cognitive health later in life.

Discuss two strengths and two limitations of Meaney et al.'s (1988) study. 

[8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

**Strengths may include but are not limited to the following:**

**Reliability**\

The study is highly reliable due to its use of a standardised procedure. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) The treatment group of rats was groomed in a consistent and controlled manner, which allows for replication of the experiment in other settings and increases the trustworthiness of the findings. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Applications**\

The findings have practical applications in understanding the impact of stress on cognitive health. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) The study shows that stress hormones like cortisol can lead to hippocampal cell death, which has implications for disorders like Alzheimer's disease, suggesting that early stress management could help in cognitive health prevention. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Contribution to epigenetics**\

The study made an important contribution to the field of epigenetics. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) Although the concept of epigenetics was not fully understood then, the study highlighted how environmental factors (such as maternal grooming) can influence gene expression and affect the long-term stress response, which has influenced subsequent research in the field of epigenetics. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Limitations may include but are not limited to the following:**

**Lack of ecological validity**\

The study lacks ecological validity. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) The artificial nature of the laboratory setting, where rats were separated from their mothers and subjected to grooming by researchers, does not mimic real-world stressors, reducing the applicability of the findings to natural environments. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Ethical concerns**\

The study raises ethical concerns as the rats were subjected to significant stress (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) (being separated from their mothers) and ultimately killed to measure brain function, which raises questions about the ethics of using animals in research and whether the benefits of the findings justify the harm caused. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Generalisability to humans**\

The study's results are difficult to generalise to humans. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) Rats have a different brain structure and response to stress than humans. Although the study provides insight into the effects of stress, its findings may not directly apply to human cognitive functions and disorders like Alzheimer's. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Longitudinal study**\

The study has limitations in assessing long-term effects over the rats' entire lifespan. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

Although the rats were tested at two years of age, which is considered older in rat years, it is unclear if the study truly captured the full lifespan of the rats and the effects of early-life stress. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

- 1 mark for explaining and linking the strength or weakness to the study's design or findings.
