$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: 

Animal research has contributed greatly to our understanding of human diseases and behaviour. One reason animal models are used in human research is because animal physiology has the potential to mimic the characteristics or aspects of the biological process studied. Therefore, animal research may provide some insight into human behaviour.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Animal research has been utilised in psychology to study a wide range of behaviours such as memory, aggression, neuroplasticity to name a few. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

Certain species of animals are used more frequently than others when it comes to research such as mice, monkeys etc. This is due to the high percentage of similarity in genes and behaviour process that can be observed between these animals and humans.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ However, there are various considerations that need to be accounted before animal research can be undertaken such as the cost-benefit analysis that justifies the use of animals in research by measuring the possible benefit gained from the research and comparing it to the inevitable harm or loss that may come to the animal.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Research on animals is especially instrumental in understanding biological aspects of behaviour such as the effect of neurotransmitters on behaviour which will be demonstrated through the study of Rogers and Kesner (2003) and neuroplasticity as seen in the study by Rosenzweig (1972).$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ 

Rogers and Kesner wanted to investigate the role of acetylcholine on the formation of spatial memories.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ They gathered 30 rats in a lab and acclimatized them to a Hebb-William maze by placing food. The goal of the experiment was to test the rat's memory when injected with scopolamine, a drug that blocks the acetylcholine receptors in the brain. Once the rats were familiar with the maze, one group was injected with scopolamine and the other group received a placebo shot. Their behaviour was measured by the number of mistakes they made during the first five trials of the day in reaching the food signifying the formation of spatial memories.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Results showed that the experimental group took longer and made more mistakes in retrieving the food. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

These results showed that acetylcholine plays a significant role in the creation of spatial memories as in its absence the rats took longer to find the food the food that was placed. One of the most significant strengths of this study lies in its potential implications in the development of drugs that could help in the treatment of patients with memory diseases such as Alzheimer's.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ Additionally, this experiment was conducted in highly controlled conditions with the control placebo group helping to establish a strong cause and effect relationship. However, as with all animal research a weakness is the generalization of results from animals to humans.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ Though, there exists a high level of similarity between rats and humans, it cannot be said that human physiology would react in the same way as rats and not lead to other complications arising from the excessive acetylcholine given to treat memory issues. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The second behaviour studied was the effect of environment stimulation on brain changes known as neuroplasticity. Rosenzweig (1972) used rats to study the effect of environmental stimuli on the changes in the brain.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The researcher used three rats from a litter and placed them either in an impoverished condition (IC) with little to no environmental stimuli like the presence of other rats or toys, maze etc. or he placed them in an enriched condition (EC) with the other rats and having access to toys and maze.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Both the groups had equal access to food and water. The rats spent 30 - 60 days before being killed to study the effect of the condition on the physiology of their brain. Results showed that the rats in the EC had an increased thickness in the cortex and had higher activity of the neurons in the cerebral cortex associated with learning and memory. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The results of the study showed how the stimuli present in the environment had a direct impact on the physiological changes in the brain indicating that our environment plays a significant role in learning and memory. This gives further evidence to the theory that with proper care and nurture, humans can increase their ability and talents. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ A strength of the study is the possible application in the field of early childhood education. Furthermore, the results were measured through an autopsy indicating high construct validity of the results. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ However, similar to most animal research the issue of theoretical generalization is present as the study was done on rats to generalize the results to the human population may not be appropriate in spite of the similarity. Also, as the enriched condition included various aspects such as social stimulation, physical activity, and toys, it is not clear which of the above has the most impact on learning and memory. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

As seen from both the animal research cited above the implications from the research conducted are massive and can potentially impact many areas of education and drug development. While further research is required before definitive steps can be taken, it has opened up avenues of discovery for the researchers. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ On the other hand, animal research needs to be undertaken with proper justification and cost benefit analysis to avoid unnecessary harm to animals. While in the Rogers and Kesner study, there was no long-term harm to the animals, the rats were killed in the  Rosenzweig study showing the importance of cost benefit analysis as each case needs to be considered individually.  $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$Researchers must ensure they are following all regulations when considering animals for research such as the Animal Welfare Act signed in 1966 in US regulates the treatment of animals in research to abide by the ethical guidelines of research. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

In conclusion, animal research has played a significant role in helping researchers understand various biological aspects of human behaviour. While research in this field has far reaching implications, a cost benefit analysis must be conducted to ensure that researchers are following all ethical guidelines connected with this area of research to ensure there all research participants whether human or animals are receiving equitable protection. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
