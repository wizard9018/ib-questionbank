# PS0811

Friedman (2012)  and his team at the Jackson Laboratory wanted to find the signal that controls food intake and body weight. In their 1994 study (Zhang et al.), they discovered the hormone leptin by studying the OB mouse, which overeats and becomes obese due to a genetic mutation. This animal study was groundbreaking because it identified leptin as a key hormone in regulating hunger and body weight, providing new insights into obesity and metabolism.

Discuss the positive and negative impacts of using animals in this research. 

[8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

**Positive factors:**

**Scientific discovery**\

Friedman's research on leptin led to the discovery of a hormone that plays a key role in regulating hunger and satiety. This has profound implications for understanding obesity and could inform treatments for eating disorders and obesity. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) This discovery is a significant contribution to biological and medical science, helping us understand how the brain signals satiety, which could potentially lead to therapies for people with obesity. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Limited number of animals used**\

The research used only two mice, adhering to the principle of "reduction," one of the "3Rs" of animal research, which encourages minimizing the number of animals used in studies. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) This means the study was ethically mindful of the number of animals used, which can be seen as a positive aspect of the research. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Negative factors:**\

Physical harm to the animals

The study involved severe procedures, including sewing the two mice together to join their circulation systems. This caused physical harm to the animals and likely resulted in pain and distress. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) According to ethical guidelines, animal research should minimise harm, but this study clearly violated that principle by subjecting the mice to unnecessary and extreme physical stress. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Lack of refinement**\

The procedure could have been refined to reduce harm. For example, using less invasive techniques or having veterinary oversight to monitor pain and stress levels could have improved the ethical standards of the study. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) The procedure could have been refined further to ensure the mice experienced less trauma, which would have aligned better with the ethical guidelines for refinement. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)
