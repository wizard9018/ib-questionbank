$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: 

Researchers study genetics to better understand the biological basis of human behaviour. Genetic similarities help us to understand the presence of common genetic materials among humans and its correlation on the incidence of behaviour and disorders. Some of the most common ways to study genetic similarities is through twin studies and family studies. Both of these research types require rigorous research methods that will give quantifiable data for analysis. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

One of the common research methods used are natural experiments which are correlational in nature.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ In this type of experiment, the Independent variable is naturally occurring and is not manipulated by the researcher. Additionally, these are known as correlational as the premise of the study is to establish the relationship between genetic similarities and behaviour.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ One study that will be discussed in this essay demonstrating the use of correlational studies is Kendler et.al (2006). The other most commonly used research method is case study. Case studies are often longitudinal and help to ascertain the change in behaviour over a period of time. Additionally, case studies use method triangulation helping to establish the validity of the research.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ This will be shown through the Weismann et.al (2005) study. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

Kendler et.al (2006) conducted a correlational study using twins to investigate the effect of genes on depression. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ These studies use monozygotic twins (MZ) - twins who share 100% genes as they are identical and dizygotic twins (DZ) - twins who share 50\% genes between them and are non-identical. Around 15,000 twins were identified from the twin registry in Sweden and data was gathered through interviews based on the DSM IV criteria for depression. The reasoning behind using twin studies is that twins mostly share the same environment as they grow up together with the same family and are exposed to similar stressors. Therefore, any difference in the occurrence of any disorder between them can be due to the genes they share. This is measured through concordance rates which is the correlation between the similarity of inheritance and occurrence of a disorder. Results showed that female MZ twins had the highest concordance rates followed by male MZ twins. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Therefore, the results give evidence to the correlation between genes and behaviour as MZ twins had higher concordance rates indicating the more similarity in genes the higher chances of depression among twins. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ A significant strength of the study was the large sample size leading to increased generalizability as the results can now be applied to both types of twins. Furthermore, other studies have confirmed these findings increasing the reliability of the study such as Bierut et.al (1999) etc. However, a limitation of this research is the questionable validity of the diagnosis as it was a telephonic interview. Also, the symptoms were mainly self-reported which could explain the gender bias seen in the results. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ 

Weissman et.al (2005) conducted a longitudinal case study on families to test for the effect of genes on depression. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Sample was collected from three generations over a span of 20 years with the grandparents having a clinical diagnosis. The children and grandchildren were interviewed by different clinicians through surveys and observations. Results showed that if a grandparent was diagnosed with depression, the grandchild had an increased chance more than 50% to develop the disorder. Additionally, it was also found that an incidence of depression in both grandparents and parents raised the chances of the child getting the disorder. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Results demonstrate the effect of genetic similarity on depression as having the genes for depression in the family increase the chances of a person developing the disorder. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ As it was a case study, the presence of method triangulation such the use of interviews and clinical diagnosis increases the validity of the research findings.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ Additionally, the study is longitudinal increasing the reliability of the research. However, the presence of confounding variables in the study such as the amount of time spent with the parent or grandparent was not accounted for which could have affected the results as the environment could also play a role in the diagnosis of depression. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

Both studies cited above show a clear link between genetics and depression furthering the argument that depression could be inherited through genetics. As seen, true experiments help enhance the validity of the argument as the independent variable (genetic similarity) is not manipulated by the researcher and is naturally occurring.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ Furthermore, this being a correlational study, helped researchers to identify the relationship that exists between the variables of genetics and depression. However, caution must be used as correlation is different from causation, thereby it cannot be said that genetics is the sole cause of depression rather its presence only helps to ascertain the likelihood of a person developing the disorder.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ Another advantage of using this type of studies is the large sample size used as seen in both studies. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

While correlation is necessary in the research on genetic similarities, researchers should also include triangulation of data to raise the validity of the studies as seen in the second study cited. Thus, a mixed-method design is more effective in establishing the validity of the conclusions drawn linking genetics and behaviour. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
