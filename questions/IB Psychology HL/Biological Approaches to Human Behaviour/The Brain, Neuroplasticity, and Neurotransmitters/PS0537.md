# PS0537

Outline the role of one antagonist using one research study.

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question is provided in the first few sentences. The question sues the command term outline; therefore, the response should provide a summary or a brief account of the role of antagonists in relation to a research study (*e.g., $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Antagonists can be described as chemical substances introduced in the body that block receptors from passing on information . . .$\text{\textquotedblright}$*)

 

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

The answer includes a clear focus on describing one study,, including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study, where the study is linked to the topic.  Relevant studies that could be referred to include, but are not limited to:

- Study on the role of haloperidol on blocking dopamine receptors: Mirte et al. (2016)

- Study on the role of scopolamine on blocking acetylcholine receptors: Troster $\&$ Beatty (1989)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Neurotransmitters pass information from one neuron to another through the receptors located on each of the branches of the neurons known as dendrites. Antagonists are chemical substances introduced in the body that block the receptors from passing on the information, thus delaying or stopping the neurons from passing on the message in the brain. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. Antagonists are sometimes given to the patient in the form of drugs to avoid excessive reactions due to the overexcitation of the neurotransmitters.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. One such antagonist is ketamine, an excitatory neurotransmitter, which blocks the glutamate receptors known for their role in causing depression. A study was conducted by Lapidus et al. (2014) on the role of the antagonist ketamine in blocking glutamate receptors and its effect on behaviour.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Lapidus et al. (2014) conducted a lab experiment, gathering 20 participants with a diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) and randomly allocated them to 2 conditions. Group 1 was given a nasal dose of ketamine and Group 2 was given a placebo. The participants were asked to fill in a rating scale for depression one day after the treatment. Results from the scale showed that participants in Group 1 had decreased depression symptoms as compared to participants from the control group. We can thus conclude that ketamine is useful in the treatment of depression, as it reduces the symptoms of depression by lowering the amount of glutamate present in the synapses. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

From the results of this experiment, the role of ketamine as an antagonist can be clearly seen, as it blocked the glutamate receptors and reduced the symptoms of depression in the participants from the experimental group. The results are evident as the same effect was not seen in the participants of the control group. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
