# PS0508

Explain neuroplasticity by using evidence from one study.\

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the answer includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question is provided in the first few sentences. The question uses the command term explain; therefore, the response should provide a detailed account of neuroplasticity, including possible causes or reasons, in relation to a research study (*e.g., “Neuroplasticity is considered to be . . .;” “Neuroplasticity is the process of . . .”)*

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of key topics and supporting details are expressed in the answer. A minimum of 2 key terms should be used in the answer. Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define could include, but are not limited to:

- Brain plasticity: ability of the brain to reorganise its functions through changes to the neural pathways

- Dendritic branching: process of thickening of dendrites often seen when new habits/responses are formingNeural pathways: organisation of neurons that helps in communication flow

- Neural pruning: reduction or shrinkage of unused neural pathways

- Neurogenesis: process by which new neurons are formed in the brain

- Synapses: the junctions between two neurons where information passes 

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

The answer includes a clear focus on describing one study, including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study, where the study is linked to the topic. Relevant studies that could be referred to include, but are not limited to:

- Study on taxi driver's change in hippocampal volume: Maguire (2000)

- Study on the juggler's change in the grey matter of the brain: Draganski et al. (2004)

- Effect of listening to Mozart on spatial reasoning skills: Rauscher et al. (1993)

- Effect of childhood poverty on the amount of grey matter in the hippocampus and amygdala: Luby et al. (2013)

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic is present (*e.g., Maguire's (2000) study on taxi driver's change in hippocampal volume links to changes in the environment (profession as taxi drivers*).

- If the answer provides a clear explanation of one relevant study and explains key terms relevant to the topic without an explicit link between the result of the study and the topic in question, a maximum of 6 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer focuses on explaining a relevant study only, without explaining neuroplasticity, a maximum of 5 marks will be awarded. 

- If the answer focuses on a study that is not relevant to the topic, a maximum of 4 marks will be awarded.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Neuroplasticity is defined as a process that explains how the brain changes itself to suit the demands of the environment. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The brain is considered to be plastic in nature, meaning that it is malleable and can change itself depending upon the environment. This is known as brain plasticity. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The brain consists of millions of neurons that pass information to each other, forming neural pathways. These neural pathways get strengthened through dendritic branching, which occurs when neurons are firing continuously, leading to a growth in dendrites. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ This branching leads to structural plasticity or changes in the structure of the brain. However, as the brain is plastic in nature, the reverse of this also occurs, known as neural pruning, wherein synapses shrink and disappear after a period of low or no usage. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ One study that demonstrated neural pruning is Draganski et al. (2004). $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Draganski et al. (2004) wanted to investigate if learning a skill, such as juggling, would lead to changes in the brain’s structure. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ A self-selected sample of 24 young volunteers was divided into two conditions: one group who learned juggling and another who did not, as a control group. A baseline MRI test was conducted on all participants, after which the experimental group learnt the juggling routine. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ A second MRI was done once they had mastered the routine. While the first MRI showed no differences between the two groups, the second MRI showed the jugglers had a significantly larger amount of grey matter in the mid-temporal region as measured through VBM (Voxel-based morphometry), a technique used to assess the differences in the anatomy of the brain. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ After three months, once the jugglers had stopped practising their routine and could no longer perform it, a third scan was conducted. This scan showed a decrease in the grey matter in the same parts of the brain. The mid-temporal region of the brain was the part of the brain with the most growth, as juggling iss a skill involving visual-motor coordination, and it helps in visual memory. There was no change in the grey matter of the control group across the three scans. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The results from this study can be linked to the concept of neuroplasticity because the increased grey matter in the experimental group after the second scan could have occurred due to dendritic branching.  The jugglers were continuously practising a new skill until they were an expert in it, which showcases the effect of the environment on the brain. There is further evidence of plasticity in this study, as the grey matter decreased once the jugglers had stopped practising and could no longer perform the routine successfully, which shows the process of neural pruning. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$

It can be concluded that neuroplasticity is a complex phenomenon dependent upon stimulation from the environment, for example spending time practising a new skill, which can lead to structural changes in the brain.
