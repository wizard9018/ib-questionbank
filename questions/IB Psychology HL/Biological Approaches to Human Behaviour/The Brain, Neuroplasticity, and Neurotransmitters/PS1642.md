# PS1642

Describe the role of neurons in human behaviour.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

Neurons are cells that are the building blocks of the nervous system. The process of neurotransmission (and the release of neurotransmitters) relies on neurons. Neurotransmission is the process of sending electrochemical messages throughout the nervous system, via neurons that release neurotransmitters. Neurotransmission is essential for everyday functioning, including basic survival functions, movement, cognitive processes and emotions. The nervous system is made up of bundles of nerve cells called neurons. When a message travels along the axon of a neuron, it takes the form of an electrical impulse. Once the message reaches the axon terminals, chemical messengers called neurotransmitters are released from the terminal sacs into the synapse. These neurotransmitters bind with receptor sites on the dendrites of the postsynaptic neuron like a key fitting into a lock. Depending on the neurotransmitter and the receptors in the synapse, the postsynaptic neuron is either stimulated (excitatory) or inhibited (inhibitory). In doing so, neurotransmission can influence behaviour and cognition.

One study that highlights the role of neurons (and neurotransmitters) in human behaviour is Antonova et al. (2011). Antonova aimed to investigate how changes in the neurotransmitter acetylcholine affect spatial memory and brain activity. The study used a repeated measures experimental design in which participants were randomly assigned to receive either scopolamine, which blocks acetylcholine receptors (on the postsynaptic neuron), or a placebo, and then completed a virtual reality Arena Task designed to test spatial memory while undergoing fMRI scanning to measure brain activity, particularly in the hippocampus. The results showed that participants performed worse on the spatial memory task and exhibited reduced hippocampal activation in the scopolamine condition compared to the placebo condition. The study concluded that acetylcholine plays a key role in memory and hippocampal functioning, demonstrating that neurotransmitter activity influences cognitive processes, and highlighting that the process of neurotransmission (and the release of neurotransmitters) relies on neurons. The study also shows that neuron activity can directly affect human behaviour such as memory.

$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{\underline{\textrm{Possible ideas \& key terms:}}}$

Neurons: specialised cells that transmit information in the nervous system

Structure of a neuron: dendrites, soma (cell body), axon, axon terminals

Electrochemical transmission:
- electrical impulse along the axon (action potential)
- chemical transmission across the synapse

Neurotransmitters (e.g. acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin)

Synapse: gap between neurons where neurotransmitters are released

Role: neurotransmitters increase or decrease the likelihood of a neuron firing.

Receptor sites: specific binding sites on the postsynaptic neuron

Role in behaviour: neurons enable communication that allows for changes in behaviour and cognition.
