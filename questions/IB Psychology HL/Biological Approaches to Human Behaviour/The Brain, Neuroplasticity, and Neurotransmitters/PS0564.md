$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers used for communicating information with the help of an electrical impulse passed on from one neuron to another. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

 Neurons are nerve cells that are the building blocks of behaviour. Through the neuron's terminals, neurotransmitters are released and  help to send electrochemical messages to the brain so that people can respond to stimuli from the environment or changes in the body. They are stored in the neurons' terminal buttons and when the message has been passed they either break down or reabsorbed by the terminal buttons. This process is known as reuptake. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

The process of neurotransmission takes place when signals across the synapses are transferred with the help of neurotransmitters. Neurotransmitters are broadly divided into agonists and antagonists depending on the role they play. Antagonists are chemicals which counteract the action of a neurotransmitter resulting in prevention of a signal from being passed further. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Neurotransmitters influence many human behaviours such as mood, memory, sexual arousal and mental illness. For this response, the focus will be on dopamine $\&$ acetylcholine $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

Dopamine is a neurotransmitter associated with motivation, learning and pleasure. Acetylcholine plays a role in memory, learning, attention, arousal and involuntary muscle movement. Following studies explain the influence of neurotransmitters to behaviour in detail. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

The study conducted by Antonova et al in 2011 studied the effect of Acetylcholine (ACh) on memory. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

 The aim of the study was to investigate how blocking of the ACh receptor sites by scopolamine affected spatial memory in participants. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The sample were 20 adult males who were divided into two conditions. In condition one participants were injected with scopolamine and in condition two they were injected with a placebo 90 minutes before the experiment. The participants were put into an fMRI machine while completing a task to navigate and reach a pole. Once the participants reached the pole in the arena task the screen went blank for 30 seconds and started from a different location where the participants had to use their spatial memory to reach the pole again. The task was repeated six times for each participant. Participants then returned 3-4 weeks later and redid the experiment with opposite treatment to the original study. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

The results of the experiment showed that participants injected with the antagonist scopolamine made more mistakes and had trouble remembering their way to the pole compared to the participants who were in the placebo condition. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The participants had a significant reduction in the activation of the hippocampus compared to when they received a placebo. The ACh availability in the hippocampus impaired the memorization of information for participants in condition 1. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

The study has several evaluation points. The strengths of the study are there is high internal validity due to the cause and effect relationship between the independent and dependent variable. This essentially means that the changes in the DV are solely due to the change in the IV and not due to extraneous variables. Informed consent was acquired from the participants before the study. The study has high replicability as it was a lab experiment having clear and organized experimental procedures may result in ease of replicability. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The limitations of the study are that the sample is restricted to males hence it cannot be generalized to females. This also results in limited generalizability. Due to the way a lab experiment is designed, the participants may show demand characteristics like altering their natural behaviour to agree/disagree with the researchers views or suffer from stress of performing as they are not aware of the aim of the study. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The second study explaining the effect of a neurotransmitter on behaviour is Fisher, Aron and Brown (2005) study aimed to investigate the effect of dopamine on romantic love. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ The sample consisted of 17 participants with ten men and seven women who were in love with their partners. The mean age of all participants was 21 years and the mean duration of being in love was 7 months. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The procedure involved all participants being placed in a functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) scanner and they were shown photographs while their brains were being scanned. There were four parts to the experiment. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

$1.$ For 30 seconds each participant viewed a photograph of the person they loved.\

$2.$ A 40-second filler activity which counts back from a given number for all participants. \

$3.$ For 30 seconds participants viewed a photograph of an emotionally neutral acquaintance.\

$4.$ 20 seconds of counting back from a number. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

All steps were repeated six times and the procedure lasted for 12 minutes for the participants. The results of the study found that in response to the photographs of their loved ones, activation was observed in dopamine-rich neural systems, primarily the ventral tegmental area (VTA) and caudate nucleus. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ They are called the dopaminergic pathways, which means it generates and transmits dopamine and increases dopamine-related activity in the brain. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The study clearly explains the role of romantic love with dopamine-rich areas of the brain that are associated with reward and motivation. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The study has several evaluation points. The strengths of the study are there is high internal validity due to the cause and effect relationship between the independent and dependent variable. This essentially means that the changes in the DV are solely due to the change in the IV and not due to extraneous variables such as if the person has slightly had an argument with a partner or is planning to end the relationship due to unresolved issues. These points may act as an extraneous variable and affect the results. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ The study can be replicated for further studies as the procedure is standardized for all participants which makes the results reliable. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The limitations of the study are that the sample size is small which limits generalization. Extraneous factors such as issues in relationships, degree of commitment are subjective factors which are not taken into consideration and may affect the results as the changes in the DV will not be because of the IV in such cases. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

Both the studies show the influence of neurotransmitters on behaviour. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
