$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: 

Research in the field of brain and behaviour is essential for scientists to understand the functions of the brain and its impact on behaviour. One of the behaviours that is commonly observed is memory. The relationship between brain area and its impact on memory is investigated through the use of various research methods in psychology. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

While there are various research methods used in this field, the two most commonly used are case studies and true experiments.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Case studies are characterized by the in-depth study of an individual or a small group of people with the same characteristics.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Another feature of case studies is the use of methodological triangulation, this means that multiple methods of data collection such as observation, interviews, surveys are done to gain data.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The second type of research method is true experiments. True experiments are characterized by the presence of well-defined variables. An independent variable is manipulated by the researcher, in order to see the effect on a dependent variable, and participants are randomly assigned to conditions. True experiments are often conducted in a lab setting with control variables helping to establish the causality of the relationship.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Additionally, apart from this, most research also uses advanced technologies to understand the structure and function of the brain, such as MRI and fMRI.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The two studies that will be discussed in this essay are Millner (1966) case study on HM and the true experiment conducted by Antonova (2011). 

Milner (1966) conducted a longitudinal study on patient HM for a period of 50 years.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$  HM had met with an accident at age 7 and as a result had to surgically remove a part of his hippocampus to stop the seizures which started after the fall.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ However, after the surgery it was found that HM had suffered from anterograde amnesia and couldn't recall any events from after the accident for more than 15 seconds. This was an unusual opportunity for researchers to understand how memory worked and thus began the 50 year long case study on HM using various methods such as interviews, observations, cognitive testing and also MRI to study the structure of his brain specifically the hippocampus. Through this they were able to establish the importance of the hippocampus in the conversion of short term to long term memories. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

HM is a classic example of how case studies are used for research in this field as it was an in-depth study on one person and was conducted over a long period of time.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Additionally, it also used method triangulation as HM was observed in a lab setting and at his home as well as interviewed and also underwent MRI testing.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  A significant strength of this study is the role it played in establishing the understanding of the hippocampus as a key player in the conversion of memories. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$However, as it is a case study it has low generalizability as HM faced unique circumstances with his injury, thus the results cannot be generalized to the rest of the population.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ 

The second study that will be discussed in this essay was a true experiment conducted by Antonova (2011).$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ He investigated the effect of neurotransmitters on the encoding of spatial memories. Participants were  divided into two conditions - an experimental group where they were injected with scopolamine and a control group with a placebo injection. Scopolamine is a beta blocker that inhibits the neurotransmitter response and would help the researcher to understand the effect of neurotransmitter acetylcholine.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ They were then placed in an fMRI machine and were asked to play a video game in which they had to navigate to a pole. The results showed that in the experimental group there was a reduced activation of the hippocampus as compared to the placebo group signifying the importance of acetylcholine and its role on spatial memory.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

This was a true experiment as the IV ( activation of the hippocampus) was clearly measured through the use of the fMRI scanner and the DV (presence of scopolamine) was clearly manipulated as the participants were put into two groups. Further this was a double blind test meaning neither the researchers nor the participants knew which group they belonged to.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ A significant strength of the study is the high internal validity due to its ability to control variables as well as a double blind test furthering the cause and effect relationship.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ However, it has reduced ecological validity as the test was conducted in highly artificial conditions and in a real-life situation there would be many factors that could influence the formation of spatial memories.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ 

The research cited have used two different research methodologies and contribute meaningfully to gain an understanding in this field. While case studies that are conducted over an extended period of time help researchers to gain a deep understanding of how the changes in the brain structure affect the behaviour (memory) of the person and how this effect can change over a period of time as it was found that while HM could not ever remembering performing any cognitive tests his time for completing the tests did reduce overtime indicating that his procedural memory was intact.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ One can argue that case studies are more holistic in nature while true experiments can be reductionistic as they are often conducted with defined variables. However, true experiments on the other hand, have an immense value due to their high causality helping researchers to pinpoint exactly how the brain impacts behaviour.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ 

Thus we can conclude that, while both these methodologies contribute in their own way, it is essential to continue research in this field to ensure that the conclusions drawn from the research are reliable and valid.$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
