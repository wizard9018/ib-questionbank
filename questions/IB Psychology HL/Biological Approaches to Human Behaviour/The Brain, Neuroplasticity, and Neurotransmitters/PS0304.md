# PS0304

Explain neuroplasticity.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the responses include all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

The first few sentences directly answer the question (*e.g. “Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to reorganise itself by forming new neural connections throughout life.” / “Neuroplasticity refers to the brain's capacity to adapt and change in response to experiences, environmental influences, learning, and injury, allowing for forming new neural pathways and modifying existing ones.*). 

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

The answer should be focused on the command term ‘explain,’ which gives a detailed account including reasons and causes (*e.g. Defining neuroplasticity, the process of neuroplasticity*). There is accurate knowledge and understanding of the process of neuroplasticity in relation to human behaviour. Key terminology such as the following may be used to describe neuroplasticity: *long*$\text{\textemdash}$*term potentiation, neurogenesis, dendritic branching, synaptic pruning, and changes in neural connections.* 

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study – including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion. The aim should be linked to neuroplasticity.\

*Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

- Draganski et al. (2004) studied the changes in grey matter due to training

- Maguire et al. (2000) research on taxi drivers and their memory.

- Gaser & Schlaug (2003) investigated the effect of brain plasticity in musicians.

- Draganski et al. (2006) studied the effect of brain plasticity in medical students.

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the appropriate study to neuroplasticity (e.g. *‘The research clearly showed that as a result of learning a new activity like juggling, the neuroplasticity increased for the juggling group compared to the other group. There was also a correlation between the juggling performance and the amount of change in the brain plasticity.’*)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

Neuroplasticity refers to the brain's ability to reorganize and adapt by forming new neural connections, which is crucial for learning and memory.  At the level of a single neuron, it is called synaptic plasticity, and when brain area X assumes the functions of brain area Y, it is known as cortical remapping. Neuroplasticity happens on a day$\text{\textemdash}$to$\text{\textemdash}$day basis as a result of any new learning as a result of brain reshaping. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

Long$\text{\textemdash}$term potentiation strengthens synapses through repeated firing, enhancing memory formation and learning processes.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Neurogenesis, the birth of new neurons, fosters cognitive flexibility and adaptation to new information. Additionally, synaptic pruning refines neural circuits, optimizing efficiency and shaping behavior and cognitive processes such as decision$\text{\textemdash}$making and problem$\text{\textemdash}$solving. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

Research conducted by Draganski et al. (2004) aimed to find if the human brain can change structure in response to environmental demands.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ A group of self$\text{\textemdash}$selected samples was chosen and divided into two groups $\text{\textemdash}$ jugglers and non jugglers. The researchers made sure that none of the participants had any prior experience with juggling. At the start of the experiment, a brain scan was conducted for all participants.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Participants in condition 1 learned juggling for a period of 3 months, after which a second scan was conducted. Then, the participants spent another three months where they were instructed not to practice juggling. The third brain scan was performed after this. Condition 2 was the control group, where the participants were not exposed to juggling and continued with their daily lives. All 3 scans were conducted for these participants as well.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

The results showed that the first scan did not have much difference for the participants of the two groups. At the second scan, the juggler group had significantly more grey matter in some areas of the cortex,mostly seen in the mid$\text{\textemdash}$temporal area in both hemispheres. The third scan showed that the grey matter was still more in the condition 1 participants than the control group.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

The research clearly showed that as a result of learning a new activity like juggling, the neuroplasticity increased for the juggling group compared to the other group. There was also a correlation between the juggling performance and the amount of change in the brain plasticity. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
