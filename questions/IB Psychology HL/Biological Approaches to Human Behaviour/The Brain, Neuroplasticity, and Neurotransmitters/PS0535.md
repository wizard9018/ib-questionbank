# PS0535

Explain how one technique is used to study the brain in relation to human behaviour, with reference to one study. 

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

 A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences (e.g. *“Brain imaging techniques such as the fMRI scan study human behaviour by mapping brain activity through detecting changes in blood flow…”* Towards the end of the response, the focus can be redirected to the question by….. *“Thus, it can be seen how the imaging technique used was effective in helping researchers to understand…..”)* 

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study related to techniques to study the brain in relation to human behaviour $\text{\textendash}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study\

           *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Maguire's (2000) study on neuroplasticity using MRI scans. 

-    Sharot et. al (2007) used fMRI scans to understand flashbulb memories.

-    Tiernay et. al (2000) used PET scans to analyse language centres in the brain.

-    Draganski (2004) used MRI to study neural pruning in jugglers.

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of how different techniques are used to study the brain in relation to human behaviour.\

           *Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include:*

-    MRI scans (magnetic resonance imaging) - a brain scanning technique which uses magnetic waves helping to study the structure of the brain

-    fMRI scans (functional magnetic resonance imaging) -  a brain scanning technique which uses magnetic waves helping to study the function of the brain

-    PET scans (positron emission tomography) - Positron Emission Tomography uses radioactive waves which helps to study the function of the brain

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic  (e.g. *“Tiernay et.al (2000) found that when the control participants were performing language tasks in the PET scanner, the left hemisphere of the brain was getting activated. However, in the case of patient MA it was the right hemisphere that was active, leading to the conclusion that when one part of the brain gets injured another part can take over its functions. These results were found due to the detailed and colourful images that were released from the PET scans which clearly showed the areas that were active during the tasks.”)*

**Marking reductions:**

-    Techniques of studying the brain are confused with research methods (up to 5 marks may be deducted if the study used is relevant to the topic)

-    The study cited is not relevant to the topic (up to 4 marks may be deducted)

-    The response does not focus on the topic and no link is present (up to 4 marks may be deducted)

-    To meet the command term, it should also be explained why the particular technique was chosen. For example referring to Draganski (2000) - *“The MRI was selected as an appropriate scanning technique by the researchers as they wanted to investigate the structural changes in the mid-temporal region after the jugglers stopped practising the skill”*. Not meeting command term expectation may lead to a deduction of up to 3 marks. 

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Before the advent of the brain imaging techniques in research, researchers could only study the brain of dead human beings as autopsy was the most common method used. It was after the industrial revolution in the 19th century that modern medicine practices began to emerge, one of which was the use of X-rays and radioactive waves in imaging. This added immense value to the study of the brain as researchers could now understand the changes that were happening in a live brain and the factors that influence it leading to numerous discoveries in various cognitive areas such as memory $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. One such technique is known as the fMRI imaging which helps psychologists to understand the functions of the brain. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$  This technique is invasive as a contrast material is injected in the patient's vein which helps to show the level of activation in the different brain areas $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. Additionally, the participants are asked to perform certain activities while being in the scanner to identify the brain area associated with the cognitive activity performed $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$.In 1977, Brown and Kuik proposed the concept of flashbulb memories which stated that when a highly emotional event occurs we tend to have accurate and long-lasting memories of the event. While the theory was proposed in 1977, Sharot et.al in 2007 conducted a study using fMRI to give biological evidence to this phenomena. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Sharot et. al (2007) conducted a quasi experiment three years after the 9/11 attack on a sample of 24 participants from New york city. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  The participants were placed in an fMRI machine and presented a list of verbal cues along with the words "Summer” or “September”. They had to associate each of the verbal cues  with both of these words and recall a memory associated with it. The resulting brain images were analysed to see the level of activation in the amygdala known as the emotional centre of the brain. Results showed that participants who were closer to the event had a higher activation of the amygdala as well as being able to recall the event with clear and accurate details compared to the participants who were further away. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ These participants also reported having high confidence levels in their memories being accurate. It can be concluded that the level of activation of the amygdala indicates the emotional importance attached to the event which was followed by the vividness and accuracy of the memories leading to the biological evidence for flashbulb memories. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

fMRI scans were instrumental in helping the researchers understand how emotions trigger the memory in human beings as the level of activation of the amygdala was critical in understanding the intensity of the emotions associated with the memory. Further, as participants were in the fMRI while recalling the entire memory the changes in the activation of the brain could be noted from one task to another as for participants who were closer to the event it was less active when recalling an event from the summer as compared to recalling an event from September. Thereby, fMRI imaging technique was chosen as it helped researchers to discover the functions associated with different areas of the brain and give biological evidence to cognitive concepts. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
