Discuss one or more ethical considerations in research investigating the relationship between the brain and behaviour.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Research in the field of brain and behaviour is essential for scientists to understand the functions of the brain and its impact on behaviour. Some of the areas that come within this are research on brain and memory, research on neuroplasticity, localisation etc. However, as with any psychological research, protecting research participants is critical and in order to ensure this, various ethical guidelines that govern research have been imposed, such as that of the British Psychologist Association (BPA). $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

Two of the most recurring ethical concerns in this field are issues of consent and use of deception in the study. Informed consent is the act of gaining approval from the participant prior to the start of the experiment. This is essential as the participant should be aware of the aim of the experiment as well as the research methods in order to give an informed consent.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ This is done to ensure transparency between the researcher and the participants and help build a positive relationship between them. The second often recurring ethical issue is the use of deception.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Deception involves lying or hiding information pertinent to the research from the participants. This is done as sometimes sharing the true aim of the experiment might lead to demand characteristics from the participants leading to reduced validity of the results. However, it is still considered unethical as it negates the value of the consent gained from the participants as they were lied to in the beginning. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The ethical issue of consent will be explored through HM's case study and use of deception will be demonstrated through the study by Freed et.al (2002). $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Milner (1966) studied patient HM for a period of 50 years. HM had undergone an accident at age 7 and as result of which had to surgically remove a part of his hippocampus to stop the seizures which started after the fall.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ However, after the surgery it was found that HM had suffered from anterograde amnesia and couldn't recall any events from after the accident for more than 15 seconds. This was an unusual opportunity for researchers to understand how memory worked and thus began the 50 year long case study on HM through which they were establish the importance of the hippocampus in the conversion of short term to long term memories. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The ethical issue that arose out of HM's case study was informed consent. As stated above, the participant needs to be fully informed of the research prior to the start and in HM's case, even though he would be told of the study and may also comprehend some information, he wouldn't be able to recall giving the consent. Though, it can be argued that his family members had given the consent, he was still an adult at the time of the start of the experiment, thus making it imperative that his consent should be legally gained. Also, as this was a longitudinal case study, it involved long-term infringement upon his privacy that HM had no way to control. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The second study that will be discussed in this essay was conducted by Freed et al (2002). $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ He conducted a study to understand the role of dopamine on the treatment of Parkinson's disease by using a sample of 40 patients between the ages of 34 - 75. These participants were then divided into two groups, the experimental group who underwent a surgery with a novel method of dopamine neurons transplant while the control group underwent a false surgery. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Results showed that the nerve transplant had helped reduce symptoms of Parkinsons most noticeably in the younger patients. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The ethical issue of deception is clearly demonstrated in this study as the control group were deceived into thinking that they had undergone a surgery. This gives rise to immense ethical issues as the ensuing anxiety of undergoing a surgery followed by the stress of post-surgery was present. This is even more apparent as the population were slightly older participants who possibly had other chronic conditions further impacting their stress levels. This is referred to as deception of the placebo effect as all placebo groups are in effect deceived during and after the experiment. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

From the research studies cited above, the two ethical issues of deception and consent can be clearly seen. However, while both these issues endangered the rights of the participants in some ways, we also need to conduct a cost-benefit analysis to understand why it occurred and how it was unavoidable. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ While, it is easier to understand the case study of HM as at least parental and family consent was obtained and HM was not psychologically capable of giving informed consent. Apart from that full precautions were taken to ensure anonymity of his data as he was called HM through the period of the research to preserve his anonymity. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ However, the issue of deception in the second research is slightly more glaring as the participants thought they had undergone a real surgery and went through the psychological trauma associated with it for quite a while. Once again, to ensure a strong cause and effect relationship, it was essential for the psychologists to ensure that there was a placebo group in order to further strengthen the results of the study and the efficacy of the treatment.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$  It must be noted that any research in this field has massive implication in the medical industry with a possibility of the treatment being advised to other patients and thus researchers have the responsibility ensure the efficacy of the treatment being suggested with sound and valid research. One possible way to mitigate the effects of the deception would be to conduct intensive debriefing sessions with regular follow ups to ensure no long term impact was felt by the participants. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

In conclusion, we can say that while ethical guidelines established by BPA have served to streamline research conducted in this field, there are times when the rationale behind the research and its possible implications must be considered and decisions needs to be made on an individual basis. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
