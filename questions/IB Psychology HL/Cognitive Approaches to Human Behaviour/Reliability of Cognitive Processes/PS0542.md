# PS0542

Explain one study on the reliability of one cognitive process.
[9 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Cognitive processes may be unreliable for a number of reasons. One way of understanding the reliability of one cognitive process is through reconstructive memory.$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ Reconstructive memory explains that memories consist of what is encoded and stored primarily but it is also affected by prior knowledge in the form of schema.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ A schema is a mental representation of something based on prior knowledge and experience. With the help of schema, information is organized and gaps are filled in our mind, but this can be distorted.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$Memory distortion is caused by cognitive processes such as memory, thinking and decision making and these processes interfere with the encoding of the memory schema and may result in false memories.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

A study conducted by Loftus and Pickrell (1995) explains the process of reconstructive nature of memory.$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$  The aim of the research was to study if false memories of autobiographical events can be created through the power of suggestion. The sample consisted of 24 participants, 3 males and 21 females with the age between 18-53 years. The procedure consisted of asking two questions to the parent or the sibling of the participant, “Could you retell childhood memories of the participant?” and “Do you remember a time when the participant was lost in a mall?” $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The participants then received a questionnaire in the mail which consisted of 3 events that were real while 1 event was about “getting lost in the mall”. All participants were asked to write about the four events and submit their responses back to the researchers. They were also instructed that if they didn't remember either of the events, an appropriate response would be ‘I don't remember this incident'. The participants were then interviewed twice over a period of four weeks and were asked to recall information about the four events. They were also asked to rate their level of confidence about the memories on a scale of 1-10. After this, a second interview was conducted and then they were debriefed and asked if they could guess which memory was the false memory out of the four events.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

Results of the study found that 25% of the participants “recalled” the false memory but the participants also ranked this memory as less confident than the other memories. It was also found that they also wrote less information about this memory in the questionnaire.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The study concluded that people can be made to believe in certain memories even if it has not occurred in reality. It is possible for memories to be unreliable due to suggestion as explained by the reconstructive nature of memory. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
