# PS0520

Describe reconstructive memory, with reference to one study.

[9 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

The theory of reconstructive memory suggests that memory is not a passive retrieval of information from our long-term memory but is rather an active recreation of an event in the mind every time it is remembered.$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$   It recognises two pieces of information: information obtained during the perception of the event and external information obtained after the event. So, reconstructive memory proposes that memory can be changed during storage, processing, and retrieval, for example, due to schema processing.

We attempt to construct a memory using the information available to us from our schemas. This is a cognitive framework that helps organize and interpret information and allows us to make inferences about our environment. There are two processes involved with schemas: assimilation and accommodation. Assimilation is the process of a new memory or experience being added to our schemas to create a new mental framework. Accommodation is the process of altering the original memory with new information that conflicts with the previously known information.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

It has been shown that numerous variables affect reconstructive memory, one of them being the misinformation effect. This could include the phrasing of a question or information supplied after an event. This suggests that the reliability of memory recall can be quite poor, as the process of reconstructing a memory is prone to interference from previous knowledge, as well as influenced by external cues.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

A particular study that highlights this is Loftus & Palmer's (1974) study on eyewitness testimony. The aim of this study was to investigate whether memory could be altered through the use of misleading questions about an event in an eyewitness situation.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The researchers  used a sample of 45 university students, who were split into various groups and shown a short video of a car crash. The only difference between the groups was the verb in the question they were asked after they had witnessed the recording: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$hit$\text{\textquotedblright}$, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$bumped$\text{\textquotedblright}$, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$smashed$\text{\textquotedblright}$, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$collided,$\text{\textquotedblright}$ or $\text{\textquotedblleft}$contacted$\text{\textquotedblright}$ $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The participants were asked to report their estimation of the car's speed when the verb occurred.

The conclusions showed that the estimated speed reported was affected by the verb used.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ This could be because the verb implied information about the speed, which systematically affected the participants' memories of the accident.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Participants in the “smashed” condition reported significantly higher speeds than those in the “contacted” condition.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ This suggests that the verb conveyed an impression of the speed at which the car was travelling, and thus altered the participants' perception of the event. Therefore, this study suggests that leading questions act as false information, and they distort memory. This is an example of the misinformation effect and is one way to show that memories are reconstructive. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
