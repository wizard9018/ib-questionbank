# PS0308

Explain one ethical consideration in studying the reliability of one cognitive process.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the responses include all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question is provided in the first few sentences (*e.g. “An ethical consideration which is common in studying the reliability of cognitive processes is protection from physical or psychological harm.”*)

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of ethical issues that are related to the topic of reliability on cognitive processes in the cognitive approach.

Ethical considerations to be defined or focused on are some of the following: *the protection of participants from physical or psychological harm, consent, right to withdraw, confidentiality, deception, and debriefing.* 

The answer is focused on the command term ‘explain’, which gives a detailed account, including reasons and causes related to ethical considerations in research on the reliability of cognitive processes (*e.g. Defining a specific ethical consideration, stating ethical concern and its description*). This also requires giving a detailed account of one ethical consideration concerning one research study linked to the reliability of cognitive processes (e.g., *reconstructive memory, eyewitness testimony*). Ethical considerations can be positive (*what guidelines could be followed*) or negative (*what guidelines were not followed*).

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study $\text{\textemdash}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion, where the study’s aim is linked to ethical considerations on the topic of reliability of cognitive processes.\

*Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

- Loftus and Palmer (1974) investigated the role of information provided on influencing the eyewitness testimony of participants (protection from mental/physical harm) 

- Yuille & Cutshall (1986) investigated the accuracy in recall of eyewitness testimony using real eyewitnesses from a real crime  (informed consent) 

- Loftus, Miller and Burns (1978) investigated how verbal information supplied after an event influences a witness’s visual memory for that event (anonymity & confidentiality)

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the appropriate study to the topic (*e.g. Through the research conducted by Loftus and Palmer, it is seen that participants can be affected mentally as an exposure to traumatic events. Research in psychology needs to follow all ethical considerations.*).

[9 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

Ethical considerations ensure that participants are not harmed and that the research follows the standards set. Ethical standards in psychology are usually implemented by the American Psychology Association (APA) for all research done on humans and animals.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

Protection from mental or physical harm is an important ethical consideration that has to be followed to ensure the well$\text{\textemdash}$being of all participants. Protection from psychological harm is when the researchers ensure that participants are not exposed to any violence or traumatic events, which leaves them in a negative mindset after the research. On the other hand, an example of protection from physical harm is allowing participants to be ingested with any harmful substance or externally giving them anything for the research.$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

The ethical considerations include informed consent of the participants, right to withdraw anytime during the research, anonymity and confidentiality of the information shared by the participants, protection from mental and physical harm at all times, briefing and debriefing at the start and end of the research and deception to avoid social desirability effect.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

Protection from mental or physical harm is an important ethical consideration that has to be followed to ensure the well$\text{\textemdash}$being of all participants. Protection from mental harm is when the researchers ensure that participants are not exposed to any violence or traumatic events which leaves them in a negative mindset after the research. Protection from physical harm also means that the participants are not injected or ingested with anything that can harm them. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

An experiment was conducted by Loftus and Palmer in 1974. The research aimed to investigate the effect of leading questions on eyewitness testimony of an event. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 45 participants were divided into 5 groups (smashed, collided, bumped, hit and contacted). The participants were shown 7 videos of car crashes for about 5$\text{\textemdash}$30 seconds. After each video, they were asked to fill a questionnaire which had one critical question “How fast were the cars going when they?” The verb in the critical question was changed to smashed/ collided/ hit/ bumped/ contacted and participants were asked to estimate the speed of the cars. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The ethical consideration of protection from mental or physical harm was not followed. Since the participants were exposed to accidents in the videos they saw, it could have mentally affected the participants in a negative way. The car crash could have resulted in making the participants remember their own traumatic experiences with similar incidents. To avoid mental harm, participants can be asked at the beginning of the experiment if they have gone through a similar experience and if they would still like to participate in the study. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ 

The results of the experiment showed that higher intensity verbs such as smashed estimated higher speed by the participants and low intensity verbs such as contacted and hit estimated lower speed. The speed estimation in the research was as follows $\text{\textemdash}$ \

Smashed $\text{\textemdash}$ 40.5\

Collided $\text{\textemdash}$ 39.3\

Bumped $\text{\textemdash}$ 38.1\

Hit $\text{\textemdash}$ 34.0\

Contacted $\text{\textemdash}$ 31.8 $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Through the research conducted by Loftus and Palmer, it is seen that participants can be affected mentally as an exposure to traumatic events. Research in Psychology needs to follow all ethical considerations.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
