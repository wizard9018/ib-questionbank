# PS0683

To what extent does digital technology have a positive effect on cognitive processes? 

[22 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

Humans are surrounded by and engage with technology daily. Given its extensive usage, it is important to have an understanding of how technology might influence cognitive processes. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ Cognitive processes are the mental processes that are essential for everyday tasks, such as memory, thinking, and decision-making. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ While it is imperative to know whether the use of technology is harming our cognitive processes, it is equally important to understand any potential positive effects. An understanding of the positive effects can open the door for us to harness and embrace any potential benefits. The extent to which digital technology has a positive effect on cognitive processes can be considered with reference to research studies conducted by Oh et al. (2017) and Sparrow et al. (2011). $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ 

A study conducted by Oh et al. (2017) aimed to examine the effectiveness of a smartphone app (SMART) designed to improve age-related memory concerns. 53 participants ranging in age from 50-68 were randomly assigned to either the SMART app intervention condition or the waitlist (control) condition. Participants in the intervention condition used the smartphone app 5 days a week, for 15-20 minutes each day, for a total of 8 weeks. The effectiveness of the app was assessed through the use of a series of objective, standardised measures to evaluate changes in working memory (WM) and attention. Participants also completed self-report surveys to assess their subjective memory complaints. All tests took place at the start of the study and again after 8 weeks. The results showed that while there was no significant difference in self-reported subjective memory complaints, the objective working memory scores of the smartphone intervention group increased significantly compared to the wait list (control condition). This study suggests that some forms of digital technology have the potential to have positive effects on cognitive processes. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

This study benefits from its experimental nature, where the researchers randomly allocated participants to conditions and manipulated an IV (use of the SMART App or No use of the App) in order to examine the effect on the DV (objective and subjective memory scores). However, as the study was conducted in natural, real-world settings, the researchers could not control all variables that might influence memory. Therefore, the causal relationships established may not be as strong as in a laboratory experiment. While this study supports the idea that digital technology can be used to enhance cognitive abilities such as memory, it is just one study that investigated a single smartphone app. We cannot be certain that all smartphone apps aimed at improving memory are effective or that the benefits were necessarily due to the use of technology specifically. Is it possible that the same types of activities in a non-digital format, would have been just as effective? Furthermore, the study did not investigate or measure any potential negative consequences associated with smartphone usage. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

It is also worth investigating the positive and/or negative influences of other forms of digital technology on cognitive processes. A study conducted by Sparrow et al, (2011) aimed to investigate whether continual access to Google was having any impact on memory. The study used an independent groups design, and participants were asked to input recently learned trivia facts into a computer. The IV was what the participants were told would happen to the information they were typing. Some participants were told that the information would be saved and accessible later, and some were told that it would be erased. In each condition, half of the participants were explicitly asked to try and remember the information they were typing. Later, all participants were given a surprise recall test (DV). The results showed that those participants who believed the information would be erased had significantly better recall.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Being explicitly asked to remember the information had no impact on levels of recall. The researchers concluded that continuous access to search engines may impair recall due to the expectation of instant information availability. This has been referred to as the Google Effect or Digital Amnesia. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

The use of a true experiment in this study allowed the researchers to randomly allocate participants to conditions and to manipulate two different independent variables. The first was whether participants were told the data would be saved or erased, and the other was whether they were explicitly asked to remember the information. They also tightly controlled other variables, such as ensuring the newly learned trivia facts were identical across each condition. This allowed the researchers to isolate the effects of the IV and establish a cause-and-effect relationship between reliance on digital technology (as a backup storage system) and memory performance. However, tightly controlling variables in this way means that the study was conducted in a highly artificial environment that only tested recall of trivia facts. This reduces ecological validity and mundane realism, meaning the findings may not accurately predict how our memory functions in complex, real-world settings. Use of the internet in real-world settings varies widely in terms of how it is used and for how long. This means that the scope of this particular study may be too narrow to generalise to all internet use. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

At first glance, the robust findings of Sparrow’s study appear to suggest that digital technology, such as the internet, has a negative influence on cognitive processes. However, such conclusions need to be treated with caution. While it is clear from this study that reliance on internet access may mean that we are retaining less information, this may not necessarily be a completely negative thing. By relying on the internet as an external storage device, we may be able to free up important cognitive space for higher-order cognitive processes such as critical thinking. This could result in more efficient cognitive processing. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

In conclusion, humans are surrounded by and engage with technology daily. Research conducted by Oh et al. (2007) suggests that some forms of digital technology (such as memory-enhancing smartphone apps) have the potential to positively affect cognitive processes. Research conducted by Sparrow suggests that reliance on digital technology, such as the internet, may negatively impact memory. However, this may also free up space for more efficient critical thinking. While both studies are interesting and helpful, they are limited in scope. As there are so many different types of technology being used in diverse ways, it is difficult to draw a strong conclusion as to the extent to which digital technology can have a positive influence on cognitive processes. It may be that the extent to which digital technology positively or negatively influences cognition may depend on the type of technology used, how it is used, and the individual who is using it. Therefore, it would be safe to conclude that technology positively influences cognitive processes to a moderate extent.  $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
