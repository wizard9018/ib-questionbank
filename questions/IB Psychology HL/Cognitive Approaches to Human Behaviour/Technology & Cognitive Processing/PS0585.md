# PS0585

Discuss research methods used to investigate the effect of technology on cognitive processing.
[22 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\
Cognitive processing refers to the actions that take place in our mind such as the processes of memory, thinking, and decision-making.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Humans are surrounded by, and engage with, technology on a daily basis. Given its extensive usage, psychologists need to have an understanding of how technology might influence our cognitive processing. Psychologists use a variety of research methods to help them investigate the influence of technology on cognitive processes, with the choice of research method being dependent on the scope and requirements of the research. The use of true experiments and correlational studies to investigate the effect of technology on cognitive processing can be discussed with reference to research conducted by Sparrow et al., (2011)  and Mehsi et al., (2019). $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

True experiments are a quantitative method that usually aims to test a cause-and-effect relationship between two or more variables. They involve the manipulation of an independent variable to see the effect on a dependent variable and the random allocation of participants to conditions. Experiments are usually performed under highly controlled conditions where researchers attempt to control extraneous variables so they can isolate the effects of the IV. As cognitive processing is not directly observable, experiments allow for a scientific and robust method for testing hypotheses related to the impact of technology on cognitive processing. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

One research study that made use of a true experiment to investigate the effect of technology on cognitive processing is Sparrow et al. The aim was to investigate whether continual access to Google was having any impact on memory. The study used an independent groups design and participants were asked to input recently learned trivia facts into a computer. The IV was what the participants were told would happen to the information they were typing. Some participants were told that the information would be saved and accessible later and some were told that it would be erased. In each condition, half of the participants were explicitly asked to try and remember the information they were typing. Later, all participants were given a surprise recall test, with the results of the test being the dependent variable. The results showed that those participants who believed the information would be erased had significantly better recall while being explicitly asked to remember the information had no impact on levels of recall. The researchers concluded that continuous access to search engines may impair recall due to the expectation of instant information availability. This has been referred to as the Google Effect or Digital Amnesia.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The use of the experimental method allowed the researchers to manipulate two different independent variables. The first was whether participants were told that the data would be saved or erased and the other was whether they were explicitly asked to remember the information. They were also able to tightly control other variables, such as making sure that the newly learned trivia facts were identical across each condition. This allowed the researchers to isolate the effects of the IV and establish a cause-and-effect relationship between reliance on digital technology to act as a backup storage system and how memory performance. As cognitive processing is not directly observable, the experimental method allowed the researchers to make inferences about memory based on the data collected (DV). Finally, while this experiment allowed for the manipulation and control of variables, it was conducted in a highly artificial environment that only tested recall of trivia facts. This reduces ecological validity and mundane realism, meaning that the findings may not accurately predict how functions in complex, real world settings. These are limitations that are often shared by studies that make use of the experimental method.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

Another quantitative research method used to investigate the effects of technology on cognitive processing is correlational studies. When using a correlational study, the researchers do not actively manipulate an independent variable. Instead, they analyse two existing variables, to see how they might influence each other. While the numerical data collected can also be analysed for significance, cause-and-effect relationships cannot be established between variables, as the researchers have not manipulated or controlled any variables.$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

Meshi et al., (2019) made use of a correlational study to investigate the effect of technology on cognitive processing. The study aimed to examine the relationship between excessive online social networking use and decision making. The study involved 71 participants taking a Facebook Addiction Scale questionnaire (BFAS) to assess their reliance on Facebook. All participants then completed 100 trials of the Iowa Gambling Task (IGT), which is a commonly utilised measure of decision-making. Finally, the researchers examined the relationship between scores on the BFAS and performance on the IGT. The results showed a negative correlation between the BFAS score and performance in the IGT. This means the more addicted participants were to Facebook, the worse they did on the gambling task. The researcher concluded that extreme social media use is associated with worse decision-making skills (risky decision-making). Highlighting a negative influence of digital technology on cognitive processes.$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The use of the correlational method in this study allowed the researchers to quickly collect data about the long-term effects of Facebook without having to manipulate variables for an extended period of time. It also provided an ethical and practical alternative to manipulating how much time a person spends using social media. A limitation of this correlational study and correlational studies, in general, is that the researchers cannot control or manipulate variables, therefore, while the findings are interesting and not impacted by an artificial setting, no cause-and-effect relationship can be established between excessive Facebook use and diminished decision-making skills. As such bi-directional ambiguity is a significant issue. The researchers have no way of knowing whether it was pre-existing deficits in decision-making that led to excessive Facebook use or vice versa. Furthermore, there could have been additional variables that have not been accounted for that have impacted decision-making ability. Finally, data in this particular study was collected via self-reports which means that problems with honesty and memory may have negatively impacted the data collected.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

In conclusion, true experiments and correlational studies are two different quantitative research methods that allow researchers to investigate the influence of technology on cognitive processing. A true experiment, which was utlised in Sparrow's study, allow researchers to establish cause-and-effect relationships between variables, however, there are some concerns with ecological and predictive validity. On the other hand, correlational studies, as seen in Meshi's research provide a practical and ethical alternative to manipulating variables, while benefiting from being higher in ecological validity.  However, they suffer from an inability to establish causation. Finally, both research methods can be impacted by the data collection techniques that are utilised in a specific research study. Which method is best for investigating the influence of technology on cognitive processing will depend on the scope, limits, and requirements of the research. It is worth keeping in mind that both research methods are somewhat limited by the fact that cognitive processes are not directly observable and researchers will need to make inferences from the findings obtained.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
