$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

Schemas are considered as mental blocks representing our prior knowledge. One of the essential components of Schemas lies in the way it assists us to organize and recall information in our daily lives linked by our prior experience to the topic. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ As humans grow and learn, new schemas or blocks of information are created known as Assimilation. However, we are constantly being bombarded by new information which is absorbed in our existing schemas through a process known as Accommodation. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

While schemas play a crucial role in our understanding of how memory works it is especially useful in identifying one of the main errors in memory not accounted for in any of the previous models – memory distortion. This is experienced when our brain creates false memories or reconstructs memory with flaws mainly due to our memory associating schema congruent items together. Schema congruence s\is referred to the process of categorizing items together under the same category because of our association with it. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$This essay will look at how schemas affect our memory recall through the Bartlett (1932) study and further review how our memory is affected by schema congruent and incongruent items by the Brewer and Treyens (1981) study. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

 Bartlett (1932) gathered British participants and told them a Native American legend filled with unfamiliar elements. The participants were put into two groups – serial reproduction wherein they had to recall the story to another person and repeated reproduction when they had to recall the story over a period of weeks, months, and years. While there was no significant difference between the groups, Bartlett found that the story on retelling had become much shorter with some details being adapted to the participants' own cultural schemas and familiar elements in a process known as rationalization. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The results from this study give evidence to memory distortions and the role played by schemas in reconstructing memory. While the results were pivotal in how memory recall was understood, this study had significant limitations. Firstly, there was no cause and effect as the IV, separation into groups based on type of recall did not affect the DV – memory recall. Furthermore, if culture is to be considered as a variable, there was no way to verify that cultural schema played a role in memory distortion as the results were not compared to another group of Native Americans. However, in spite of this, Bartlett’s study has been known to be reliable as a follow-up study conducted by Bergman and Roediger (1999) produced similar results. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

To dive deeper into how memory gets distorted, Brewer and Treyens (1981) conducted a study to understand if we tend to recall schema-congruent items more easily rather than schema incongruent ones. The sample comprised 86 Psychology students who were asked to wait in an office before the experiment began. Unknown to them, the experiment had already begun as once they were called to another room, they were asked to recall items they saw in the office. The participants were divided into 3 conditions – free verbal recall, free drawing recall and verbal recognition condition. In condition 3, participants were presented with a list of items and asked to tick the ones they remember seeing in the office. Results showed that in condition 3 participants tended to recall more schema incongruent items whereas in condition 1 and 2 they recalled more schema congruent items. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

These results give evidence to how the way we recall memory affects the accuracy of our memory. When participants were asked to recall items freely there was a higher chance of distortion but when presented with a list there was higher accuracy. Though there were some ethical concerns with the use of deception in the study, it had to be done to avoid demand characteristics increasing the validity of the study. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

Schema theory has helped to further the understanding of the concept of memory distortions better leading to more awareness of the effect of schemas such as the formation of stereotypes. This has been one of the major implications of research in this field as it has a direct impact on understanding the basis of discrimination and bias between groups of people. However, concerns in the research field still exist as schemas cannot be observed directly and have no biological evidence lending support to the credibility of its existence. The research cited above only refers to its effects being observed but not in any biological sense. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

In conclusion, schema theory has helped to bridge the gap in understanding of memory as a whole and have provided a more holistic view of memory with an understanding of how environment could affect it.
