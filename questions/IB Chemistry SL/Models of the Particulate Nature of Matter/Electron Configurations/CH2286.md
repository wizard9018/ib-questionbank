$\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
Copper is one of the exceptions to having the $4s$ orbital completely filled before filling the $3d$ orbital.  The neutral atom has an electron configuration of [Ar]$4s^1\,3d^{10}$. When removing electrons to form cations, the $4s$ electrons are removed before the $3d$ electrons so the copper(II) ion has an electron configuration of [Ar]$3d^9$. When drawing the orbital diagrams, remember that each orbital can only fit two electrons of opposite spin and each degenerate $3d$ orbital is first singly filled before pairing the electrons up.
