# CH0265

**Maximum mark: 3**

$\ce{Cu}$ is a transition metal commonly used in wires.

1. Determine the complete electron configuration of $\ce{Cu}$.  [1 mark]

2. State the condensed electron configuration of $\ce{Cu+}.$ [1 mark]

3. Determine whether the electron configuration of $\ce{Cu}$ follows the usual rules of electron configuration.  [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Longhand/complete electron configuration of $\ce{Cu}$

    $\ce{1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^{10} 4s^1}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    $\ce{Cu}$ is one of the exceptions where a $3d$ orbital would fill before $4s$ and this is due to the fact that a full $3d$ orbital is more stable.

2.  Condensed electron configuration

    $\ce{[Ar]\,3d^{10}}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    1 electron lost from $4s$ orbital (highest energy level).

3.  There is an anomaly with the electron configuration of $\ce{Cu}$ simply because there is slightly greater stability associated with the fully filled ($3d^{10}$) subshell than the incomplete one. Hence, the $3d^{10}$ is filled first before pairing the $4s$ subshell. $\checkmark$
