# CH2092

[Maximum marks: 7]

Crude oil (also known as petroleum) is a homogeneous mixture of volatile liquid hydrocarbons. Before it can be used, it must be refined to separate it into simpler, more useful mixtures.

1.  Describe a homogeneous mixture.    [1 mark]

2.  Contrast the microscopic structure and behavior of liquids and gases using the kinetic molecular theory. [2 marks]

3. State the physical property of the components of crude oil that allows them to be separated by fractional distillation. [1 mark]

4. Kerosene, a fuel used in aviation, is one of the fractions obtained from crude oil. $\newline \ \newline$

    1.  Kerosene has a boiling point of 240.00°C. Convert this temperature to Kelvin using section 1 of the data booklet.     [1 mark]

    2.  Explain, using the kinetic molecular theory, why kerosene vaporizes when heated.      [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  uniform composition /  single$-$phase of physically$-$combined components / two or more different elements or compounds not in fixed ratios / components not chemically bonded  $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    A homogeneous mixture is one in which the composition is uniform throughout, meaning the individual components are evenly distributed and cannot be distinguished by simple observation. They appear as a single phase. Unlike compounds, the substances within a homogeneous mixture are not chemically bonded, so they retain their original chemical properties and they can combine in any ratio since they do not obey the Law of Definite Proportions.

2.  Any two of:

    -   liquid particles are close together while gas particles are far apart $\checkmark$
    -   liquid particles experience intermolecular /interatomic forces while gas particles do not $\checkmark$
    -   liquids are not compressible while gases are very compressible $\checkmark$
    -   liquids have a fixed volume while gases expand to fill a container $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The particles in a liquid move freely, sliding past each other in a closely$-$packed but disordered structure.  This allows a liquid to change its shape to fit its container while maintaining a fixed volume.  Since the particles remain in close contact, liquids are not compressible.  The particles do experience attractions to one another (intermolecular / interatomic forces) that must be overcome to enter into the gas phase.

    The particles in a gas are in constant random motion, moving rapidly in all directions with negligible forces between them.  The particles are far apart, allowing gases to expand to fill their container and making them highly compressible.

3.  Different boiling points/volatility $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The components of crude oil can be separated by fractional distillation because they have different boiling points. When crude oil is heated, the hydrocarbons with the lowest boiling points evaporate first, while those with higher boiling points remain in liquid form until reaching their respective boiling temperatures.  The fractions that are removed from the sample are groups of similarly sized hydrocarbons.

4.  1. $240.00+273.15 = 513.15 \textrm{\ K}\:\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The Kelvin scale is shifted from the Celsius scale to place the 0 at absolute zero instead of the freezing point of water:

        Temperature (K) = Temperature ($^\circ$C) + 273.15

    2. kinetic energy  increases, molecules move faster $\checkmark$ \
    boils when molecules have enough energy to overcome intermolecular forces, and transition from liquid to gas $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        According to the kinetic molecular theory, the temperature of a substance is directly related to the average kinetic energy of its particles. As kerosene is heated, its molecules absorb energy and move faster, increasing their kinetic energy. At the boiling point, the molecules have gained enough energy to break the intermolecular forces that hold them in the liquid state. This allows them to escape into the gas phase, leading to vaporization.
