# CH1104

**Maximum mark: 10**

A technician stores vaccines in a polystyrene container packed with dry ice (solid carbon dioxide). The container also holds air.

1.  Identify one element, one compound, and one mixture present
    in the container. [2 marks]

2.  1.  State the change of state that occurs as the dry ice
        keeps the vaccines cold. [1 mark]

    2.  Describe, in terms of the arrangement and movement of
         particles, what happens to the carbon dioxide during
         this change of state. [2 marks]

3.  1.  Dry air is classified as a homogeneous mixture. Explain
        what is meant by a homogeneous mixture. [1 mark]

    2.  When water vapour in the air condenses into tiny
         droplets, fog forms inside the container. State whether fog is a homogeneous or heterogeneous mixture and explain your answer. [2 marks]

4.  Pure carbon dioxide sublimes at a fixed temperature of
    $-78.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ A student finds that a sample sublimes over a range from $-80\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ to $-76\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ Suggest what this indicates
    about the sample. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  element: nitrogen **OR** oxygen \
    compound: carbon dioxide **OR** water **OR** polystyrene\
    **AND**\
    mixture: air $\checkmark$ $\checkmark$\
    *Award [1] for two correct.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    An element contains only one type of atom. Nitrogen $(\ce{N2})$ and  oxygen $(\ce{O2})$  are elements present in the air inside the container. A compound contains two or more types of atom chemically bonded in a fixed ratio. Carbon dioxide $(\ce{CO2})$ is a compound: each molecule contains one carbon atom bonded to two oxygen atoms. A mixture contains two or more substances that are not chemically bonded and can be separated by physical methods. Air is a mixture of several gases in variable proportions.

2.  1.  sublimation $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Dry ice converts directly from the solid phase to the gas phase without passing through the liquid phase. This process is called sublimation. As the dry ice sublimes, it absorbs energy from the surroundings, keeping the vaccines at a low temperature.

    2.  particles change from a regular, closely packed arrangement to a widely spaced arrangement $\checkmark$\
        particles change from vibrating in fixed positions to moving rapidly in random directions $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        In solid carbon dioxide, the molecules are held in a regular, closely packed lattice. Each molecule vibrates about a fixed position but does not move from place to place. During sublimation, the molecules gain enough energy to overcome the forces holding them in the lattice and escape directly into the gas phase. In the gas phase, the molecules are widely spaced, move rapidly in random directions, and occupy the entire volume available to them.

3.  1.  uniform composition throughout $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        A homogeneous mixture has the same composition and properties at every point within the sample. Dry air is homogeneous because the nitrogen, oxygen, argon and carbon dioxide molecules are uniformly distributed at the molecular level. Any sample of dry air taken from the container has the same proportions of each gas.

    2.  heterogeneous $\checkmark$\
        fog contains liquid water droplets dispersed in gas, so it has more than one visible phase \
        **OR**\
         its composition is not uniform throughout $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Fog is a heterogeneous mixture because it consists of tiny liquid water droplets suspended in a gas. The droplets and the surrounding air are in different phases, and the composition varies from point to point: a region containing a droplet is almost entirely liquid water, while the space between droplets is a gas mixture. The boundary between the two phases is what makes the mixture heterogeneous. By contrast, water vapour dissolved uniformly in air (humid air without visible droplets) would still be a homogeneous mixture because all the components are in the gas phase.

4.  sample is impure **OR** sample is a mixture $\checkmark$\
    a mixture changes state over a range of temperatures $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    A defining characteristic of a pure substance is that it changes state at a single, fixed temperature under constant pressure. Pure carbon dioxide sublimes at exactly $-78.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ When a sample sublimes over a range of temperatures, this indicates that other substances are present. The impurities disrupt the regular arrangement of the solid and alter the energy needed for the phase change, causing it to occur gradually over a range rather than sharply at one temperature. This principle is the basis for using melting point and boiling point measurements as tests for purity.
