# CH2573

A hydrocarbon contains $84.1\%$ carbon by mass. The molar mass of the compound is twice the empirical formula mass. What is the molecular formula of the hydrocarbon?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C2H4}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H10}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C6H14}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C8H18}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
In $100\textrm{ g}$ of the hydrocarbon, there are $84.1\textrm{ g}$ of carbon and $15.9\textrm{ g}$ of hydrogen. Converting to amount:

$n(\ce{C}) = \dfrac{84.1\textrm{ g}}{12.01\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 7.00\textrm{ mol}$

$n(\ce{H}) = \dfrac{15.9\textrm{ g}}{1.01\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 15.7\textrm{ mol}$

Dividing by the smallest gives the ratio $\ce{C} : \ce{H} = 1 : 2.24.$ The decimal $2.24$ is close to $\frac{9}{4} = 2.25,$ so multiplying through by 4 gives the whole$-$number ratio $4 : 9.$ The empirical formula is $\ce{C4H9}.$

The question states that the molar mass is twice the empirical formula mass, so the multiplier is 2 and the molecular formula is $\ce{C8H18}.$
