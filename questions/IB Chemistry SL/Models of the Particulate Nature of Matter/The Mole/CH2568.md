# CH2568

What is the amount, in mol, of oxygen atoms in 62 g of $\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}$, $M_r = 310?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}0.20$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}0.40$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}0.80$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}1.60$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The amount of $\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}$ in the sample is:

$n = \dfrac{62\textrm{ g}}{310\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.20\textrm{ mol}$

Each formula unit contains two phosphate groups, and each phosphate contains four oxygen atoms, giving $2 \times 4 = 8$ oxygen atoms per formula unit. The amount of oxygen atoms is:

$n(\ce{O}) = 0.20\textrm{ mol} \times 8 = 1.60\textrm{ mol}$

Parentheses in a chemical formula act as a multiplier. In $\ce{Ca3(PO4)2},$ the subscript 2 applies to everything inside the parentheses, so the four oxygens in each $\ce{PO4}$ group appear twice.
