# CH2566

What is the mass, in g, of water that contains the same amount of hydrogen as 32 g of methane, $\ce{CH4}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}18$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}36$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}72$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}144$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The molecular formula of methane, $\ce{CH4},$ shows four hydrogen atoms per molecule. The amount of methane in the sample is:

$n(\ce{CH4}) = \dfrac{32\textrm{ g}}{16.05\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} \approx 2.0\textrm{ mol}$

The total amount of hydrogen atoms is $2.0\textrm{ mol} \times 4 = 8.0\textrm{ mol H}.$

Each molecule of water, $\ce{H2O},$ contains two hydrogen atoms, so:

$n(\ce{H2O}) = \dfrac{8.0\textrm{ mol}}{2} = 4.0\textrm{ mol}$

$m(\ce{H2O}) = 4.0\textrm{ mol} \times 18.02\textrm{ g mol}^{-1} = 72\textrm{ g}$

Every mole of $\ce{CH4}$ supplies enough hydrogen for two moles of $\ce{H2O}.$
