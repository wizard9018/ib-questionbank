# CH0023

What mass of lead(II) carbonate would contain $20.7\, \textrm{g}$ of lead?

- A.$\hspace{1em}47.4\ \textrm{g}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}26.7\ \textrm{g}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}23.5\ \textrm{g}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}32.7\ \textrm{g}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Lead(II) has a $2+$ charge and carbonate has a $2-$ charge, so lead(II) carbonate has the formula $\ce{PbCO3}$ (a $1:1$ ratio).

$n(\ce{Pb}) = \dfrac{20.7}{207.2} \approx 0.1 \textrm{\ mol}$

Each formula unit of $\ce{PbCO3}$ contains one $\ce{Pb}$ atom, so $n(\ce{PbCO3}) = 0.1 \textrm{\ mol}.$

$M(\ce{PbCO3}) = 207.2 + 12.01 + 3(16.00) = 267.2 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$

$m(\ce{PbCO3}) = 0.1 \times 267.2 = 26.7 \textrm{\ g}$
