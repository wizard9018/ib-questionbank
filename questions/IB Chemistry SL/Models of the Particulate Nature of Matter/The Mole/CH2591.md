# CH2591

**Maximum mark: 7**

The formula of hydrated Epsom salt is $\ce{MgSO4}.x\ce{H2O}.$ An experiment is carried out to determine the value of $x.$ A sample of the salt is heated in a crucible until constant mass is reached. The following data is recorded.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| Measurement | mass / g $\pm$ 0.002 g |
|:-:|:-:|
| Mass of empty crucible | 32.184  |
| Mass of crucible $+$ hydrated salt | 33.421  |
| Mass of crucible $+$ residue after heating | 32.784|

1.  1.  Calculate the amount, in mol, of water lost during heating. [2 marks]

    2.  Calculate the amount, in mol, of anhydrous $\ce{MgSO4}$ remaining. [2 marks]

    3.  Deduce the value of $x.$ [1 mark]

2. Determine the percentage of oxygen by mass in the hydrated salt.  [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  $m(\ce{H2O})=   0.637\textrm{ g} \: \checkmark$\
    $n(\ce{H2O}) =  0.0354\textrm{ mol} \: \checkmark$\
        *Award [2] for correct answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Heating drives off the water of crystallisation:

        $\ce{MgSO4}.x\ce{H2O}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{MgSO4}\textrm{(s)} + x\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(g)}$

        The mass loss equals the mass of water evaporated. Subtracting the final mass from the initial mass of the loaded crucible:

        $m(\ce{H2O}) = 33.421\textrm{ g} - 32.784\textrm{ g} = 0.637\textrm{ g}$

        $n(\ce{H2O}) =  \dfrac{m}{M} = \dfrac{0.637\textrm{ g}}{18.02\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.0354\textrm{ mol}$

    2.  $m(\ce{MgSO4}) = 0.600\textrm{ g}$ $\checkmark$\
        $n(\ce{MgSO4}) =  4.98 \times 10^{-3}\textrm{ mol}$ $\checkmark$\
        *Award [2] for correct answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The residue after heating is anhydrous $\ce{MgSO4}.$ Its mass is the difference between the final crucible mass and the empty crucible mass:

        $m(\ce{MgSO4}) = 32.784\textrm{ g} - 32.184\textrm{ g} = 0.600\textrm{ g}$

        The molar mass of $\ce{MgSO4}$ is $24.31 + 32.07 + 4(16.00) = 120.38\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}.$

        $n(\ce{MgSO4}) = \dfrac{m}{M} = \dfrac{0.600\textrm{ g}}{120.38\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 4.98 \times 10^{-3}\textrm{ mol}$

    3.  $x = 7$ $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The mole ratio of water to anhydrous salt gives the value of $x$:

    $x = \dfrac{n(\ce{H2O})}{n(\ce{MgSO4})} = \dfrac{0.0354\textrm{ mol}}{4.98 \times 10^{-3}\textrm{ mol}} = 7.11 \approx 7$

2.  $M(\ce{MgSO4}.7\ce{H2O}) =  246.52\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$\
    $\%\textrm{O} = 71.4\%$ $\checkmark$\
    *Award [2] for correct answer.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The hydrated formula $\ce{MgSO4}.7\ce{H2O}$ contains $4$ oxygen atoms from the sulfate ion and $7$ oxygen atoms from the water molecules, giving $11$ oxygen atoms in total.

    $M = 24.31 + 32.07 + 4(16.00) + 14(1.01) + 7(16.00) = 246.52\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}$

    $\%\textrm{O} = \dfrac{11 \times 16.00\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}}{246.52\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} \times 100 = 71.4\%$
