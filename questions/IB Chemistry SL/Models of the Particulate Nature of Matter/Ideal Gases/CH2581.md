# CH2581

Which conditions favour the deviations from the ideal model for gases?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\hspace{1.1em}$  | $\hspace{0.em}$ | | $\textrm{Temperature}$ | $\textrm{Pressure}$ |
| :-- |:-:| :-- | :-: | :-:|
|| A. || Low  | Low |
|| B. || High  | Low |
|| C. || High  | High |
|| D. || Low  | High |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The ideal gas model breaks down under two conditions.

At low temperatures, gas molecules have less kinetic energy. When molecules move slowly, collisions are not always perfectly elastic because attractive intermolecular forces have a greater relative effect. The assumption that no intermolecular forces exist becomes invalid.

At high pressures, gas molecules are forced closer together and the volume occupied by the molecules themselves is no longer negligible compared to the volume of the container. The assumption that molecular volume is zero becomes invalid.

Deviations from ideal behaviour are therefore greatest at low temperature and high pressure.
