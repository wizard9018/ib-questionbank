# CH2587

The diagram shows two containers of ideal gas at the same volume and temperature.

![CH2587](images/CH2587.png)

What is the total pressure when gases A and B are mixed into a single container of the same volume at the same temperature?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}80\textrm{ kPa}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}160\textrm{ kPa}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}320\textrm{ kPa}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}640\textrm{ kPa}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
For ideal gases, the pressure depends only on the total amount of gas, the volume, and the temperature. When two ideal gases are mixed into the same container at the same temperature, the total pressure equals the sum of the individual pressures (Dalton's law of partial pressures):

$p_\textrm{total} = p_\textrm{A} + p_\textrm{B} = 120\textrm{ kPa} + 40\textrm{ kPa} = 160\textrm{ kPa}$

The identity of the gases does not matter because ideal gas particles do not interact with one another. Each gas contributes to the total pressure independently, as though the other gas were not present.
