# CH2585

Which gas would deviate the most from ideal gas behaviour?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{H2}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{O2}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH4}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{NH3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Gases deviate from ideal behaviour when intermolecular forces are significant and when the molecular volume is not negligible. Two factors increase deviation: larger molecular size and stronger intermolecular forces.

$\ce{NH3}$ is a polar molecule capable of hydrogen bonding. These strong intermolecular attractions mean that at moderate temperatures, $\ce{NH3}$ molecules interact with one another rather than behaving as independent particles.

$\ce{H2}$ and $\ce{O2}$ are nonpolar and experience only weak London dispersion forces. $\ce{CH4}$ is also nonpolar, though slightly larger than $\ce{H2}.$ Of the four gases, $\ce{NH3}$ has the strongest intermolecular forces and is therefore most likely to deviate from ideal behaviour.
