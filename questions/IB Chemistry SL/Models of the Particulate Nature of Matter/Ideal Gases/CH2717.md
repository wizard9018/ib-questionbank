# CH2717

**Maximum mark: 8**

A student determined the molar volume of hydrogen gas by reacting a weighed length of magnesium ribbon with dilute hydrochloric acid and collecting the gas in a gas burette over water.

| $\mathbf{Measurement}$ | $\mathbf{Value}$ |
:-:|:-:
| Mass of magnesium ribbon $\pm$ 0.001 / g | 0.048 |
| Volume of $\ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)}$ added, $0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ / cm$^3$ | 25.0 |
| Volume of gas collected $\pm$ 0.2 / cm$^3$ | 48.6 |
| Temperature / K | 295.2 |
| Atmospheric pressure / kPa | 101.3 |
| Vapour pressure of water at 295.2 K / kPa | 2.6 |

**Table 1:** Data for the collection of hydrogen gas over water.

$\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

1. Write a balanced equation, including state symbols, for the reaction of magnesium with hydrochloric acid. [1 mark]

2. Show, by calculation, that the hydrochloric acid is in excess. [1 mark]

3. Explain why the pressure of hydrogen in the gas burette is not equal to atmospheric pressure, and calculate the pressure of the hydrogen collected. Give your answer to an appropriate number of decimal places. [2 marks]

4. Determine the experimental molar volume of hydrogen at STP. Use section 2 of the IB Chemistry data booklet.

    If you did not obtain an answer to part (c), use $99.9 \textrm{\ kPa},$ although this is not the correct answer. [2 marks]

5. Calculate the percentage difference between the experimental molar volume and the accepted value of $22.7 \textrm{\ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}.$ [1 mark]

6. Suggest one systematic error in this method that would make the experimental molar volume smaller than the accepted value. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\ce{Mg}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{MgCl2}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)} \: \checkmark$

    *Accept $\ce{Mg}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,H^+}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Mg^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)}.$*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Magnesium is a reactive metal, so it reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to form a salt, magnesium chloride, and hydrogen gas. Magnesium chloride forms $\ce{Mg^{2+}}$ and $\ce{Cl^-}$ ions, so two chloride ions are needed for each magnesium ion; balancing the chlorine therefore requires two $\ce{HCl},$ which also supplies the two hydrogen atoms in each $\ce{H2}$ molecule. The state symbols show what happens to each substance: the solid magnesium dissolves, the magnesium chloride remains in solution, and the hydrogen leaves the solution as a gas that is collected in the gas burette.

 2. $n(\ce{HCl}) = 0.0125 \textrm{\ mol}$ **AND** $n(\ce{HCl})_{\textrm{needed}} = 2 \times n(\ce{Mg}) = 3.95 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The amount of each reactant is

    $n(\ce{HCl}) = 0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.0250 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.0125 \textrm{\ mol}$

    $n(\ce{Mg}) = \dfrac{0.048 \textrm{\ g}}{24.31 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 1.97 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

    The equation shows that each mole of magnesium reacts with two moles of hydrochloric acid, so the acid needed is

    $n(\ce{HCl})_{\textrm{needed}} = 2 \times n(\ce{Mg}) = 3.95 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

    The $0.0125 \textrm{\ mol}$ of acid supplied is more than three times this amount, so the acid is in excess and magnesium is the limiting reagent.

3. gas contains water vapour, which contributes to the total pressure $\checkmark$ \
    $P(\ce{H2}) = 98.7 \textrm{\ kPa}$ $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The hydrogen is collected over water, so the gas in the burette is a mixture of hydrogen and water vapour. When the water levels inside and outside the burette are equal, the total pressure of this mixture equals atmospheric pressure. Each gas contributes its own partial pressure to the total (Dalton's law):

    $P_{\textrm{atm}} = P(\ce{H2}) + P(\ce{H2O})$

    so the pressure of the hydrogen alone is

    $P(\ce{H2}) = 101.3 \textrm{\ kPa} - 2.6 \textrm{\ kPa} = 98.7 \textrm{\ kPa}$

    When values are added or subtracted, the answer is given to the same number of decimal places as the data. Both pressures are given to one decimal place, so the answer is given to one decimal place.

4. $V_{\textrm{STP}} = 44.4 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ $\checkmark$ \
    $V_\textrm{m} = 22.5 \textrm{\ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    *Award [2] for correct final answer.*\
    *Accept 22.8 dm${^3}$ mol$^{\,-1}$ from the value 99.9  kPa.*\
    *Accept values from 22.4 to 22.6 dm$\mathit{^3}$ mol$^\mathit{\,-1}$*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The volume of hydrogen was measured at $98.7 \textrm{\ kPa}$ and $295.2 \textrm{\ K}.$ The combined gas law converts it to standard conditions, $100 \textrm{\ kPa}$ and $273.15 \textrm{\ K}:$

    $\dfrac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2V_2}{T_2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad V_2 = V_1 \times \dfrac{P_1}{P_2} \times \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

    $V_{\textrm{STP}} = 48.6 \textrm{\ cm}^3 \times \dfrac{98.7 \textrm{\ kPa}}{100 \textrm{\ kPa}} \times \dfrac{273.15 \textrm{\ K}}{295.2 \textrm{\ K}} = 44.4 \textrm{\ cm}^3$

    The pressure factor reduces the volume slightly, because the gas is compressed from $98.7$ to $100 \textrm{\ kPa}.$ The temperature factor reduces it further, because the gas is cooled from $295.2$ to $273.15 \textrm{\ K}.$ The molar volume is the volume at STP divided by the amount of hydrogen found in part (b), with the volume converted to $\textrm{dm}^3:$

    $V_\textrm{m} = \dfrac{44.4 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ dm}^3}{1.97 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}} = 22.5 \textrm{\ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}$

5. $0.88\%$ $\checkmark$

    *Accept $0.4\%$ from the rescue value 99.9 kPa.*\
    *Accept values from 0.80\% to 1.0\%.*

     $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    $\textrm{percentage difference} = \dfrac{|22.7 \textrm{\ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}- 22.5 \textrm{\ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}|}{22.7 \textrm{\ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} \times 100\% = 0.88\%$

6. any **one** of the following:
    $\mbox{}\\[-12pts]$

    -   some hydrogen escapes before the bung is fitted $\checkmark$
    -   bubbles of gas stay attached to the apparatus $\checkmark$
    -   the magnesium is coated with oxide, so less hydrogen forms than calculated $\checkmark$

    $\mbox{}\\[-20pts]$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    A low molar volume requires the measured volume to be too small, or the calculated amount of hydrogen to be too large. Escaped or trapped gas reduces the measured volume directly. The oxide coating acts on the amount: $\ce{MgO}$ contributes to the weighed mass but produces no hydrogen, so the calculated $n(\ce{H2})$ overstates reality and the quotient falls.
