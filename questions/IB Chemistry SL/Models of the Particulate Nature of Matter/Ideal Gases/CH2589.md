# CH2589

Two different ideal gases occupy the same volume at the same pressure and temperature. Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$The mass of gas is the same in both samples.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The number of molecules is the same in both samples.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The amount of atoms is the same in both samples.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The density of the gases is the same.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
If two gas samples have the same pressure, volume, and temperature, they contain the same amount of substance and therefore the same number of molecules.

$n = \dfrac{pV}{RT} = \textrm{constant}$

The mass is not necessarily the same because different gases have different molar masses. For example, $1\textrm{ mol}$ of $\ce{H2}$ has a mass of $2.02\textrm{ g}$ while $1\textrm{ mol}$ of $\ce{O2}$ has a mass of $32.00\textrm{ g}.$

The number of atoms is not necessarily the same because gases can be monoatomic, diatomic, or polyatomic. One mole of $\ce{He}$ contains $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ atoms, while one mole of $\ce{O2}$ contains $1.20 \times 10^{24}$ atoms.

Since the masses differ but the volumes are the same, the densities also differ.
