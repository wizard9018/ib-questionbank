# CH2593

**Maximum mark: 12**

An experiment is carried out to estimate absolute zero. Air is trapped in a sealed capillary tube by a plug of oil. The tube is attached to a ruler and placed in a water bath at different temperatures. The height of the air column, $h,$ is measured at each temperature.

The graph of $h$ against temperature, $\theta,$ gives a linear fit:

$h = 0.0168\,\theta + 4.2692$

where $h$ is in $\textrm{cm}$ and $\theta$ is in $\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ The coefficient of determination is $R^2 = 0.973.$

1.  List two variables that must remain constant throughout the experiment. [2 marks]

2.  1.  State the relationship between the height of the air column and the temperature. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain, using the kinetic molecular theory, the behaviour of the trapped gas as the temperature increases at constant pressure. [3 marks]

    3.  Calculate the temperature, in $\,{}^\circ\textrm{C},$ at which the height of the trapped air column would equal zero according to the linear fit. [2 marks]

    4.  Suggest one reason why this experiment cannot give the exact value of absolute zero. [1 mark]

    5.  The accepted value of absolute zero is $-273\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ Calculate the percentage error. [1 mark]

3.  A student determines that the volume of trapped air at $4.00\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ is $0.157\textrm{ cm}^3.$ Calculate the volume of the same sample of air at $90.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C},$ in $\textrm{cm}^3.$ [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  amount / mass of air $\checkmark$\
    (external) pressure $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The independent variable is temperature and the dependent variable is the height of the air column. The amount of gas trapped in the sealed capillary tube does not change, so the mass of air is constant. Because the tube is open at one end via the oil plug, the pressure on the trapped air equals atmospheric pressure and remains constant throughout. These are the controlled variables.

2.  1. height increases linearly with temperature $\checkmark$

        *Do not accept directly proportional unless the student explicitly refers to height against absolute temperature.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The data show a positive linear relationship: as the Celsius temperature increases, the height of the air column increases. The relationship is not direct proportionality on the Celsius scale because the line does not pass through the origin. Direct proportionality holds only when height is plotted against absolute temperature.

    2.  average kinetic energy of the gas particles increases $\checkmark$\
        frequency of collisions with the walls of the tube increases $\checkmark$\
        gas expands / volume increases to maintain constant pressure $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        At constant pressure, raising the temperature increases the average kinetic energy of the gas particles. The particles move faster, striking the walls of the capillary tube more frequently and with greater force. To keep the pressure constant, the gas expands and the air column becomes taller. This is consistent with Charles's law: at constant pressure and constant amount of gas, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

    3.  $0 = 0.0168\,\theta + 4.2692$ $\checkmark$\
    $\theta = -254\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Setting $h = 0$ in the equation of the linear fit and solving for $\theta$:

        $0 = 0.0168\,\theta + 4.2692$

        $\theta = \dfrac{-4.2692}{0.0168} = -254\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

    4.  Any **one** of the following:
        $\mbox{}\\[-10pts]$

        -   gas would condense before reaching absolute zero $\checkmark$
        -   real gas particles have a finite volume,  height can never reach zero $\checkmark$
        -   linear extrapolation assumes ideal behaviour, not valid  at very  low temperature $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The linear fit assumes the gas behaves ideally at all temperatures. In reality, as the temperature decreases the gas departs from ideal behaviour: intermolecular forces become significant and the gas eventually condenses to a liquid. Absolute zero cannot be reached experimentally, and the extrapolation relies on data collected over a limited temperature range far from the actual value.

    5.  $\textrm{percentage error} =  6.96\%$ $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The percentage error compares the experimental result with the accepted value:

    $\textrm{percentage error} = \left|\dfrac{\textrm{accepted value} - \textrm{experimental value}}{\textrm{accepted value}}\right| \times 100$

    $\textrm{percentage\ error} = \left|\dfrac{-273 - (-254)}{-273}\right| \times 100 = \dfrac{19}{273} \times 100 = 6.96\%$

3.  $T_i = 277 \textrm{\ K}$ **AND** $T_f = 363 \textrm{\ K}\: \checkmark$ \
    $V_f = 0.206\textrm{ cm}^3$ $\checkmark$\
    *Award [2] for correct answer.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    At constant pressure and constant amount of gas, Charles's law applies:

    $\dfrac{V_i}{T_i} = \dfrac{V_f}{T_f}$

    The temperatures must be converted to kelvin:

    $T_i = 4.00 + 273 = 277\textrm{ K}$ and $T_f = 90.0 + 273 = 363\textrm{ K}$

    $V_f = \dfrac{V_i \times T_f}{T_i} = \dfrac{0.157\textrm{ cm}^3 \times 363\textrm{ K}}{277\textrm{ K}} = 0.206\textrm{ cm}^3$
