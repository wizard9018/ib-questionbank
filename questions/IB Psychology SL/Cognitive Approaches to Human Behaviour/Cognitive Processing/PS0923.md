# PS0923

Describe one model of thinking and decision-making.

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: 

Models of thinking and decision-making aim to explain how individuals process information and make judgments. One model of thinking and decision-making is the Dual Process Model. This model suggests that there are two distinct systems of thinking. System 1 which is fast, automatic, intuitive, and often uses mental shortcuts known as heuristics. While efficient, it can lead to errors in judgment. System 2, in contrast, is slower, more deliberate, logical, and analytical. It requires more cognitive effort and is typically used in more complex or unfamiliar situations. Finally, unlike system 1, system 2 tends to consider alternatives before reaching a final conclusion, making it less prone to error and biased thinking. A key study that helps to describe the Dual Process Model was conducted by Englich and Mussweiler (2001). 

Englich and Mussweiler (2001) investigated the influence of anchoring bias, a type of heuristic, in judicial decision-making. The researchers used an independent samples design and presented 19 young trial judges with a legal case involving alleged rape. One group was given a high anchor (a very severe sentence of 34 months), while the other group was given a low anchor (a much milder sentence of 2 months). The study found that judges were significantly influenced by the anchor provided, often recommending sentences closer to the anchor. The results confirmed that judges exposed to the high anchor (34 months) recommended an average sentence of 28.70 months, while those given the low anchor (2 months) recommended an average sentence of 18.78 months. This shows how System 1 thinking (automatic and fast) can use heuristics to overpower System 2 thinking (deliberate, analytical, and logical), leading to biased decision-making. This supports the Dual Process Model by showing how cognitive biases can affect judgments when System 1 overrides the more effortful System 2. 

Overall, the Dual Process Model helps explain why people often make quick but biased decisions, especially when under time pressure or uncertainty. By distinguishing between intuitive, heuristic-driven System 1 thinking and more deliberate, analytical System 2 thinking, the model offers valuable insight into how people think and make everyday decisions.

$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{\underline{\textrm{Possible ideas \& key terms:}}}$

 - Thinking: The process of using information and mental representations to make sense of the world, solve problems, or make decisions.

 - Decision-making: The process of choosing between alternatives, often involving reasoning and judgment.

 - Dual Process Model: A model suggesting two types of thinking: System 1: Fast, intuitive, automatic, and based on heuristics. System 2: Slow, analytical, deliberate, and logical.

 - Heuristics: Mental shortcuts used in System 1 that often lead to cognitive biases.

 - Anchoring Bias: The tendency to rely heavily on the first piece of information offered (the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$anchor”) when making decisions.

$\textcolor{#056608}{\underline{\textrm{Possible key studies:}}}$

 - Englich & Mussweiler (2001) - Anchoring bias in judicial sentencing. Judges were influenced by arbitrary sentencing recommendations, showing how System 1 overrides System 2.

 - Tversky & Kahneman (1974) - Anchoring in numeric estimation tasks. Participants gave biased estimates based on starting values (*e.g., ascending vs descending multiplication*).

 - Tversky & Kahneman (1986) - Framing effect: how positive vs. negative presentation influences decisions.

 - Alter and Oppenheimer (2007) - Difficult-to-read font can activate System 2 thinking, resulting in greater accuracy in thinking and decision-making.
