# PS0756

Describe two strengths and limitations of the dual process model of thinking and decision-making.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

**Strengths:**

- Elements of the theory are supported by empirical research. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

- The model has some explanatory power, in that it can explain some real-world phenomena. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) It can provide a potential explanation for why intelligent people can sometimes get what should be relatively simple answers wrong. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

- The model can account for how we can successfully make quick decisions (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) whilst dealing with vast amounts of information at once.(:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)

**Limitations:**

- Cognitive processes such as decision-making cannot be directly observed by researchers,  (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)  therefore, inferences must be made from observable behaviour.  (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) 

- Some psychologists suggest that the theory is overly simplistic  (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) and that we have more than two modes of thinking. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) 

- As decision-making processes cannot be directly observed,  (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) the theory can neither be verified nor falsified. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) 

- We cannot be certain that research results support the model,  (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)  therefore, the interpretation of research findings may be influenced by researcher bias.  (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)
