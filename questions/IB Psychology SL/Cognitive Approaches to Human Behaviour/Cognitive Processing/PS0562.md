$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Thinking and decision-making are cognitive processes used extensively in our daily lives. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Thinking is the process of analyzing and drawing conclusions from the information provided whereas decision-making is the process of choosing one option from available alternatives. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ There are two types of independent thinking systems, system 1 and system 2 thinking. System 1 thinking is the automatic, fast and instinct based thinking while system 2 thinking is slow, analytical and logical type of thinking. System 1 thinking is where heuristics (mental shortcuts) are used for decision making. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ These two thinking systems are based on the Dual processing model and it helps to process information with the help of system 1 and 2 thinking. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

The first study was conducted by Tversky and Kahneman (1974) with an aim to study if the order of numbers in a row would affect how participants would estimate the value if they were multiplied. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The sample consisted of high school students. All participants were divided in two conditions. In condition one, the $`$ascending condition' the numbers 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8 were presented and participants had to estimate the total value in 5 seconds. In the second condition, the $`$descending condition' the numbers were presented in the format of 8,7,6,5,4,3,2,1 where a second set of participants had 5 seconds to estimate the value. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The results were that the ascending group (condition 1) had a median estimate of 512 whereas the descending condition (condition 2) had a median estimate of 2250. The actual value was 40,320. This was because the first number seen by the participants seems to have influenced their responses. There was no time to calculate in 5 seconds and hence based on the first few multiplications they had to tell the value. When the numbers were smaller, the estimate of the value was smaller. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The study clearly shows how system 1 thinking is used as the fast, automatic response is given by the participants. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

The strengths of the study are that an independent measures design is used where different participants are taken in both conditions to avoid understanding the true aim of the study. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$  This increases the accuracy of the results. The sample consisted of high school students and can be generalized to the same age group. It was a highly controlled lab experiment which increases replicability and shows a cause and effect relationship between the IV and DV.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$  The median value was used in the results which helps exclude any outliers that can be seen if the mean value was calculated. There is no evidence of mental or physical harm to the participants. The study also has some limitations. There is deception for the participants as they were not told the true aim of the study. The participants may also have varying levels of interest and aptitude for Math which can help them to calculate faster than other participants. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The second study was conducted by Alter $\&$ Oppenheimer, 2007 with an aim to investigate how font affects thinking. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The sample consisted of 40 Princeton students who completed the Cognitive Reflections Test (CRT). The test was made up of 3 questions and measures to see whether people use fast thinking to answer the question (and get it wrong) or use slow thinking (and get it right). Half of the students were given the CRT in an easy-to-read font (condition 1), while the other half were given the CRT in a difficult-to-read font (condition 2). The results of the study found that 10$\%$ of students answered all three questions correctly in condition 1, 65$\%$ of students answered correctly in condition 2. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$  This was because difficult to read fonts caused participants to engage in system 2 thinking which is more conscious and slow. 

The strengths of the study are that it was a lab experiment which had a controlled environment hence high internal validity and less influence of extraneous variables.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ It also took informed consent from the students before the study and does not have evidence of any physical or mental harm.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ While the limitations of the study are that it only involved Princeton undergraduate students which is not representative of the general population hence the results may not generalize to other groups of participants. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ 

Both the studies show the working of system 1 and system 2 thinking and decision making is influenced by these systems. It is important to note that no one system is better than the other, it depends on the situation where an individual uses either of the thinking systems. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
