# PS0540

Describe rational or controlled thinking using one research study.

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the answer includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question is provided in the first few sentences. The question uses the command term describe; therefore, the response should give a detailed account of rational or controlled thinking in relation to a research study (*e.g., $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Rational thinking or System 2 thinking is an analytical and controlled type of thinking. One study that describes System 2 thinking was conducted by Alter & Oppenheimer in 2007.$\text{\textquotedblright}$*).

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of the key topics and supporting details are expressed in the answer. A minimum of 2 key terms should be used in the answer. Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define could include, but are not limited to:

- Decision-making: a process of choosing one option from available alternatives

- System 2/Rational/Controlled Thinking: a slow, deliberate, and analytical mode of reasoning that requires conscious effort and is used for complex decision-making or problem-solving

- Thinking: a process of analysing and drawing conclusions from the information

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

The answer includes a clear focus on describing one study, including the  aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study, where the study is linked to the topic. Relevant studies that could be referred to include, but are not limited to: 

- Study on if unconscious thinking leads to better performance than rational or controlled thinking: Bonke et al. (2014)

- Investigation of individual differences in reasoning: Stanovich $\&$ West (2000)

- Investigation on how font affects thinking: Alter $/&$ Oppenheimer (2007)

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic is present  (*e.g., $\text{\textquotedblleft}$With the help of the research conducted, controlled or rational thinking and how they affect decision-making can be understood.$\text{\textquotedblright}$*).

**Marking limits:**

- If the answer provides a clear explanation of one relevant study and explains key terms relevant to the topic without an explicit link between the result of the study and the topic in question, a maximum of 6 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer focuses on explaining a relevant study only, without explaining controlled thinking, a maximum of 5 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer focuses on a study that is not relevant to the topic, a maximum of 4 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer focuses only on describing the controlled thinking, with no mention of a relevant study, a maximum of 3 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer describes an incorrect topic (*e.g., System 1 or automatic thinking*), no marks will be awarded.

- If the answer describes two relevant studies, the marks will only be given on the first study mentioned.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Rational or System 2 thinking is a slow, analytical, and logical type of thinking, which is controlled and analytical in nature. This type of thinking is prone to fewer errors and is used to make decisions with thought. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$  Rational or controlled thinking means carefully thinking about all the information, analysing it step by step, and using logic to make decisions. It's different from intuitive thinking, which relies on quick shortcuts and can sometimes cause mistakes in judgment because of biases. System 2 thinking is a type of thinking under the dual-processing model, which was introduced by Travesky & Kahneman. Thinking and decision-making are two different cognitive processes. Thinking is the process of analyzing information and drawing conclusions from the information provided, whereas decision-making is the process of choosing one option from available alternatives.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ This answer will focus on System 2 thinking or rational thinking. It also requires more time to make decisions and is goal directed. 

A study to understand System 2 thinking was conducted by Alter & Oppenheimer in 2007. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$The aim of the study was to investigate how font affects thinking. The sample consisted of 40 Princeton students who completed the Cognitive Reflections Test (CRT). The test was made up of 3 questions and measured to see whether people use fast thinking to answer the question (and get it wrong) or use slow/controlled thinking (and get it right). $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$All the participants were divided equally into 2 conditions. In Condition 1, half of the students were given the CRT in an easy-to-read font, while in Condition 2, the other half were given the CRT in a difficult-to-read font. 

Results of the study found that 10$\%$ of students answered all 3 questions correctly in Condition 1, while 65$\%$ of students answered all 3 correctly in Condition 2.

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

There is evidence of System 2 thinking, which is controlled/rational, present in this study. Difficult to read fonts caused participants to engage in System 2 thinking, which is more conscious and slow. It made them focus more, thereby they ended up giving the right responses.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$  In Condition 1, participants used automatic thinking as a result of an easy to read font, which made them more prone to errors.
