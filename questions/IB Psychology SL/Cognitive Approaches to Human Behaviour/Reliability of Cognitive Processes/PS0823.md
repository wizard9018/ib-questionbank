# PS0823

Define the availability heuristic and explain how it can lead to errors in thinking and decision-making.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

The availability heuristic is a cognitive bias that involves making use of information or examples that most readily or easily come to mind when making judgements (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) about the likelihood of a specific event occurring in the future. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) In doing so, the more readily available examples about a specific event occurring are given more weight (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) than examples of events that are more difficult to bring to mind (or less available). (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) It is assumed that if examples of events are hard to bring to mind, (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) then they must be unlikely to occur. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$) While this logic may sometimes result in correct predictions (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$), it can in some instances, systematically lead to inaccurate predictions. (:span$\color{red}\checkmark$)
