# PS0517

Describe how and why one research method is used in the cognitive approach, with reference to one study.

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9** marks are awarded if the answer includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question is provided in the first few sentences. The question uses the command term describe; therefore, the response should provide a detailed account or description in relation to a research study (*e.g., $\text{\textquotedblleft}$One research method used in the cognitive approach is. . .$\text{\textquotedblright}$*). 

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of key topics and supporting details are expressed in the answer. A minimum of 2 key terms should be used in the answer. Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define could include, but are not limited to:

- Case study: an in-depth investigation of a single individual, family, event, or other entity; multiple types of data (psychological, physiological, biographical, environmental) are assembled, for example, to understand an individual's background, relationships, and behaviour; will usually collect qualitative, as well as, quantitative data

- Dependent variable (DV): the variable in a laboratory experiment that the researcher predicts as the effect of the phenomenon

- Empiricism: idea that research findings should be observable using the five senses

- Independent variable (IV): the variable that researchers change in a laboratory experiment; what the researcher predicts as the cause of the phenomenon

- Laboratory experiment: an experiment that uses a carefully controlled setting and standardised procedure to accurately measure how changes in the independent variable (IV) affect the dependent variable (DV) and establish causal relationships between the variables

- Reliability: idea that if a study were to be replicated, similar results should be found; established by using a standardised procedure in lab experiments

- Validity: idea that the investigation accurately measures what it intends to

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

The answer includes a clear focus on describing one study, including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study, where the study is linked to the topic. Relevant studies that could be referred to include, but are not limited to:

- Leading questions' affect on memory: Lofus $\&$ Palmer (1974)

- Schema influence on perception: Bruner $\&$ Mintern (1955)

- Acoustic and semantic similarities on memory: Baddeley (1966)

- Short-term memory (STM) maximum duration of 30 seconds: Peterson $\&$ Peterson (1959)

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic is present (*e.g., $\text{\textquotedblleft}$One of the guiding principles of the cognitive approach is that mental processes can be studied scientifically. The research method used in the cognitive approach that most reflects the scientific study of behaviour, like that of Loftus & Palmer's (1974) study is a laboratory experiment.$\text{\textquotedblright}$*)

Marking Limits:

- If the answer provides a clear explanation of one relevant study and explains key terms relevant to the topic without an explicit link between the result of the study and the topic in question, a maximum of 6 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer focuses on explaining a relevant study only, without explaining the cognitive approach, a maximum of 5 marks will be awarded. 

- If the answer focuses on a study that is not relevant to the topic, a maximum of 4 marks will be awarded.

- If the answer focuses only on describing the cognitive approach, with no mention of a relevant study, a maximum of 3 marks will be awarded. 

- If the answer describes an incorrect topic, no marks will be awarded.

- If the answer describes two relevant studies, the marks will only be given on the first study mentioned.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

One of the guiding principles of the cognitive approach is that mental processes can be studied scientifically. Perhaps the research method of the cognitive approach that most reflects this scientific study is the laboratory experiment. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ A laboratory experiment is a scientific procedure carried out with the goal of verifying, refuting, or establishing the validity of a hypothesis. All psychologists must work to accept their null hypothesis so as not to show bias in their research. Experiments are used to establish cause and effect by determining whether changes to one variable lead to changes in another. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$

One example of a laboratory experiment is Loftus & Palmer's (1974) study on how leading questions may affect one's memory of an automobile crash.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The aim of the study was to determine whether the use of leading questions could affect recall in a situation in which participants were asked to estimate speed at the time of the crash.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The researchers hypothesized that using questions with verbs associated with greater speed (e.g., smashed), in contrast with those associated with lesser speed (e.g., contacted), would result in greater estimates of speed at impact when participants were asked to recall the accident. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

In order to standardize the procedure and control the environment, participants watched the same 7 videos (in which two cars hit one another), in the same conditions. The only change to the condition came when the participants were asked to estimate the speed of a car in miles per hour (the dependent variable, orDV). After each video, the participants described what they saw and answered a number of questions about it. The independent variable (IV) was a change of verb in the critical question: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$About how fast were the cars going when they _______ each other?$\text{\textquotedblright}$ There were 5 experimental conditions associated with the verbs used: contacted, hit, bumped into, collided with, and smashed into. The researchers used an independent sample design, so each participant only experienced one condition. Otherwise, there may have been participant bias.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

By using a laboratory experiment, Loftus & Palmer (1974) were able to have high levels of variable control. Only the verb was manipulated (smashed, hit, collided, etc.) and having an objective measurement unit increased the accuracy of the response. For example, miles per hour (mph) is a very standard measurement, in that 30 mph is less than 40 mph. This increases the objectivity of the results, meaning the accuracy of measurement can be tested independently from the researcher who first reported it. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

This study is also easy to replicate, and this increases the reliability of the results. The more times an experiment can be repeated in different cognitive laboratories with different participant groups and still show robust cause-and-effect relationships in the results, the higher the validity of the cognitive theory it shows. For example, after years of experimental studies, it is now almost certain that the use of leading questions can affect the recall of an event.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ However, there are certain problems with lab experiments having highly controlled environments, including artificiality and a lack of ecological validity (when the methods, materials, and settings of the study approximate the real world that is being examined). Watching a video of a car crash is far removed from the experience of observing one in a real-life situation. Because of this difference, we cannot be certain that the same effect would be obtained in real-life circumstances.$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
