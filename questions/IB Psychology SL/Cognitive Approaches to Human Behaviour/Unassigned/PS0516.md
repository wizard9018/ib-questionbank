# PS0516

Describe one or more ethical considerations related to one research study on cognitive processing.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences *(e.g. “One example of an ethical consideration related to research on cognitive processing  is…”)*

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of ethical issues that are related to the cognitive approach

Important ethical issues to use and/or define include: 

- *$`$In psychology, ethics must be considered to ensure participants (humans and animals) are not harmed and that the research conducted is ethically valid.'*

- *$`$Researchers should always conduct research in an ethical manner and studies should always be critically evaluated for ethical issues.'* 

- *$`$Ethical standards are made by the American Psychology Association (APA) that all research done in psychology must abide by.'*

These ethical considerations are: *the protection of participants from physical or psychological harm, consent, right to withdraw, confidentiality, deception, and debriefing.* 

The command term $`$describe' is addressed. This requires giving a detailed account of one or more ethical considerations in relation to one research study linked to the cognitive approach to understanding behaviour. Ethical considerations can be positive (what guidelines could be followed) or negative (what guidelines were not followed). 

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study $\text{--}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study

*Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

- $`$Genie' Curtiss (1981) - On social deprivations long term affects.

- Clive Wearing Sacks (2007) - A case study on hippocampus damage

- Blakemore & Cooper (1970) “Kitten carrousel” - Investigated the visual pursuit of moving objects.

- Gardner and Gardner (1969) “Washoe” - Investigated if primates can learn a language

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link exploring the ethical considerations of the study in reference to the question (e.g. *$`$Protection of participants means participants should be protected from harm. Gene was protected from harm during the study, but once the study was concluded, Gene was sent to live in an adult foster home where she may have experienced feelings of abandonment and mental distress at the significant change of environment.'*)

Marking limits:

- Describing more than one study will mean marks are only given to the first study described.

- Describing more than one ethical consideration mean marks are only given to the first ethical consideration described. (see sample below)

- Describing a study but an ethical consideration is not addressed *(maximum 3 marks awarded)*

- Addressing one or more ethical considerations but does not refer to an appropriate study *(maximum 4 marks awarded)*

- Clear links between the ethical consideration and the study are needed to gain marks in the top mark band. *(If the link is only implicit a maximum of 6 marks will be awarded)*

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Ethics is the moral correctness of specific conduct - in this case, the moral correctness of animal and human research at the cognitive approach. Ethical considerations related to psychological research, which are also applied in the cognitive approach, are the following: protection of participants, consent, right to withdraw, confidentiality, deception, and debriefing. These ethical standards are made by the American Psychology Association (APA) and must be abided by. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$

The study of Genie was conducted by Curtis (1981). Genie was a young girl who had been deprived of normal exposure to language early in life. She had no apparent language skills when she was discovered at the age of 13. The researcher's aim was to investigate the sensitive period hypothesis as to whether there is a sensitive learning period (before puberty closed) during which language must be acquired to develop normally. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The researchers encouraged her to verbalize and socialise. They communicated with her, taught her sign language, and provided a caring environment for Genie, while collecting data on how her language ability developed. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

There were, however, ethical issues not abided by in this study. Participants should be protected from physical and mental harm and distress, such as humiliation, stress, and injury, and participants should not be forced to reveal personal information. Although, Genie was protected from harm during the study, when the researchers concluded the study, Genie was left to live in an adult foster home, and she may have experienced mental distress from the dramatic change in environment and carers and being abandoned by the researchers. She was also asked personal and dramatic questions, which may have caused her to relive some of her past trauma. Genie was also unable to give informed consent.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  Genie could not be fully informed or consent to the study due to language restrictions and general cognitive functioning. Genie was not in a healthy state of mind to  comprehend the nature and aims of the research, thus, it was not possible to gain fully informed consent.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Genie would also have been unable to have the right to withdraw. She would not have been able to express any desire to withdraw from the study due to her language restrictions and low levels of cognitive functioning caused by years of deprivation.$\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Alongside this, confidentiality was also breached. Although her identity was kept anonymous, as Genie is not her real name, her case was exposed to the world of psychology, and she was readily available in video footage. Genie was also unaware she was being studied (deception) and was not debriefed at the end of the study. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ 

In addition to these ethical considerations, it is largely considered that the behaviour of the researcher was inappropriate, in that they had a very personal attachment and very close relationship with Genie.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ This is inappropriate for scientific research, and one of the guiding principles of the cognitive level of analysis is that mental processes can be studied scientifically.$\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ This leads to questions about the researchers' objectivity and aims for studying Genie. 

Overall, this study does not meet the ethical requirements necessary for research into cognitive approaches to understanding human behaviour and would probably not be approved by the ethics board of universities today. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
