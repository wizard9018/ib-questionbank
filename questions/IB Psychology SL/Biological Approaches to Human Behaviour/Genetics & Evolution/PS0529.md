# PS0529

Explain the role of genes on human behaviour using one relevant study.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences (e.g. *“Genetics plays a determining role in the causation of behavior. ….”)*. This can be followed by explanations of how research on this gene is conducted. (*“With the advent of new scientific methods, techniques such as gene mapping have advanced significantly, helping scientists to…..”*)

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study$\text{\textendash}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study\

*Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Kendler et.al (2006) on the role of genes on major depressive disorder.

-    Caspi et. al (2009) on the role of epigenetics on major depressive disorder. 

-    Sorensen et.al  (1992) on the role of genes in obesity 

-    Bouchard et. al (1990) on the role of genetics on intelligence 

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of genes and their role on human behaviour.\

          *Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include some of the   

           following:*

-    Genes - Genes are made up of DNA and are the functional unit of heredity which determines factors affecting your physical and personality features. 

-    Inheritance - a process by which genetic information is passed through genes from parent to child.

-    Gene mapping - the process of understanding the position of each gene on the chromosomes

-    Epigenetics - an area of study that looks at how the interaction between genetics and environment determines the expression of genes.

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic  (e.g. *“The results of the Kendler et.al (2006) study, give evidence to the role of genetics on the occurrence of a disorder like MDD as MZ twins who share 100% identical genes had significantly higher concordance rates to MDD as compared to DZ twins. This study shows how twin studies are effective in identifying the impact of genes on human behaviour by giving further evidence to the effect of genes on MDD.”)*

**Marking limits:**

-    Focusing on the biological aspect of genetics and not its influence on behaviour. (up to 2 marks may be deducted)

-    A relevant study is described, but no clear links to the effect of genetics on behaviour are made as it can be tricky to make explicit links. (up to 5 marks will be awarded)

-    If the question is on $`$one' gene, then care should be taken to ensure that only one gene, such as the 5-HTT gene as identified by Caspi, is used( up to 2 marks may be deducted)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Genes are considered as the basic building blocks of a person which determine to a great extent their personality, their behaviour and various other aspects of living. The role of genes on human behaviour has long been an area under intensive research with continuous scientific advances that have helped further understand this field $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. Epigenetics is a branch of study focused on understanding how specific genes can exist in an individual without being actively manifested in behaviours or disorders $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$.   This exploration enhances our comprehension of the relationship between genes and the environment, shedding light on the conditions under which certain genes are activated. Consequently, epigenetics involves the targeted isolation and in-depth examination of particular genes to unravel their patterns of expression $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. One study that used epigenetics to investigate the interaction of the 5-HTT gene with the stressors in the environment leading to the occurrence of Major Depressive disorders (MDD) is Caspi et.al (2009) $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Caspi et. al (2009) used a sample of 26-year-old New Zealanders who were assessed yearly for their mental health. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  The sample was divided into three groups as per their 5-HTT allele - group 1 had two short alleles, group 2 had one long and one short allele and group 3 had 2 long alleles. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  Additionally, the participants also responded to a stressful life events questionnaire along with a depression screener. Results showed that participants with one or more short alleles demonstrated more symptoms of depression along with suicidal ideation. It was also seen that people with three or more stressful events had the strongest effect from groups 1 and 2 suggesting that continuous exposure to stressors has a role to play in gene expression. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  Therefore, it can be concluded that participants with the shorter allele were more prone to developing depression in the presence of stressful life events as compared to those with longer alleles. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

This helps us to understand that it is not enough for a gene to be present but environmental triggers are necessary for it to be expressed. Thus, epigenetics has helped us to further understand the gene environment interaction in the causation of human behaviours and offers possible reasons why two people with similar genes may or may not have the same personalities. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
