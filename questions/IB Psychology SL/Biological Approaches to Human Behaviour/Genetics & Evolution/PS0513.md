# PS0513

Describe one evolutionary explanation of one human behaviour using one study.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

There should be a direct answer linked to the question within the first few lines.

(*e.g. Evolution has had an impact on some human behaviours such as ...*) As the question clearly refers to an evolutionary explanation of human behaviour, it should be identified and referred to in the first few lines. A specific behaviour affected due to evolution must be mentioned along with a clear explanation of how evolution has played a role in its expression.

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

There should be accurate knowledge and understanding of evolutionary explanations for behaviour, by using all or some key terms, such as:

-    Theory of Evolution - the theory states that the behaviour of organisms evolves from generations 

-    Natural selection - the passing of successful behaviours from one generation to another which helps them to survive

-    Sexual selection - the process by which organisms selects mate that helps them to produce offsprings with higher chances of survival

-    Adaptive behaviour - behaviour that helps people to adapt in the current environment.

The command term outlines seeks to provide a detailed account, including possible causes or reasons. Therefore, while evolutionary explanations for behaviour is essential, there should also be a focus on meeting the command term. There should be a clear summary on how this adaptation occurs with sufficient details (*e.g. The reason for this behaviour can be traced back to…*).

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study - including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study. *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Wedekind et.al (1995) on the evolution of attraction in humans

-    Curtis et.al (2004) on how evolution plays a role in the survival of humans

-    Ronay Von and Hippel (2010) on the evolution of mate selection

-    Ahs et.al (2009) on the role of fear in the survival of humans

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

There should be a clear link between the results of the study to the topic using one of the key terms mentioned in the introduction. *(e.g. Wedekind et.al (1995)  - results from this study showed that the participants performed more risk-taking behaviour in front of the female researcher as compared to the male researcher. This could be linked to the evolutionary explanation of mate selection as since earlier times males have always had to show characteristics of bravery and courage to attract females to assure them of their capability to protect them in the wilderness*)

**Marking limits:**

-    Focusing on Genetics and not on the evolutionary aspects of behaviour. (*up to 4 marks may be awarded*)

-    A relevant study is described but no clear links to evolution is made. (*up to 5 marks may be awarded*)

-    No behaviour is mentioned specifically. (*up to 3 marks may be deducted*)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Charles Darwin - known as the father of Evolution, theorised that only behaviours that produce the fittest offspring with the highest chance of survival are repeated through generations and others slowly wane out. Evolutionary Psychologists over the years have observed human behaviour to identify behaviours that can be rooted in evolution. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The tendencies for this behaviour is said to be passed on genetically from one generation to the next and is present across the human race. One of the behaviours that has an evolutionary explanation is fear and disgust. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$  $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  Humans tend to naturally get repelled or disgusted by things that are harmful to them and will avoid it. This tendency has been known to have a protective effect as they ensure the survival of humans by protecting them from dangerous and fatal diseases. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  Another well-known behaviour is natural selection or sexual selection. According to this theory, humans will select their mate based on their chances of producing the fittest offspring for survival. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ One study that gave evidence to the evolutionary aspect of the behaviour of disgust is by Curtis et.al (2004). $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Curtis et.al (2004) hypothesised that disgust has evolved as a behaviour to protect humans from harmful diseases. A large study with over 40,000 respondents was conducted through an online platform. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ People were asked to rate 20 images of food on a scale from 1-5 with 1 being most disgusting. Among the images were also 7 foods associated with diseases. Results from the study showed that women consistently rated these 7 images as the worst as compared to men. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ This can be traced back to earlier times when knowledge of proper methods of cooking food was limited. There was not enough awareness on the dangers of uncooked food thereby evolution has helped humans to survive deadly foodborne diseases that could have been fatal. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

From the results of this study, it can be seen that women were more sensitive towards foods that were associated with diseases. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$ As women are the ones who are responsible to bear children and also play a protective role in rearing children along with being the gatekeepers of nutrition in the family, the results from this study show a direct impact of evolution on women's behaviour of disgust directly related to foods that could potentially be fatal. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$ Thus supporting the idea of the effect of evolution on human behaviour as it supports the survival of the human species. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$
