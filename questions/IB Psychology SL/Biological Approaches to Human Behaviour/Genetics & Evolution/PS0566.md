$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Evolution is the process by which organisms change from one generation to another as a result of changes in heritable characteristics. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ It assumes that organisms are driven by the need to survive and reproduce. The organisms that adapt to change are able to survive and produce their off-springs which is called survival of the fittest. The theory of evolution was introduced by Charles Darwin and was based on the explanation of differential fitness and natural selection. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Differential fitness is the process when organisms from the population have different traits as a result of adapting to the environmental changes. Traits that have not adapted to the environmental changes often wear out and are not passed to the offspring. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ Many common behaviours such as fear, attraction, mate selection and disgust may be understood from an evolutionary point. 

The study conducted by Curtis, Aunger and Rabie (2004) focuses on the behaviour of disgust as it has been evolved to a way of protecting oneself from the risk of diseases. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$ $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ There were a total of five hypotheses for the study with a total of 77,000 participants from 165 countries but only 40,000 data was selected as participants who had watched the BBC documentary on the topic were excluded. An online survey was conducted to record their responses. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The five hypotheses were: 

1.    Disgust should be felt more when faced with a disease-salient stimulus compared to a stimulus with less salience. 

2.    The behaviour of disgust should operate in a similar way across cultures. 

3.    Disgust should be more pronounced in females since they have to protect their offsprings. 

4.    Disgust should become weaker with age as the individual's reproductive potential declines.

5.    Disgust should be stronger in contact with strangers than with close relatives. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The procedure involved showing twenty images to all the participants, post which they were asked to rank them based on their level of disgust on a scale of 1 (not disgusting) to 5 (very disgusting). out of the 20 images, 14 images comprised 7 pairs of disease-salient versus less salient stimuli. All participants were asked to share their demographic details prior to the survey. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The results of the study supported all five hypotheses. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

1.    Disease-salient stimuli were rated as more disgusting than less salient ones (the image of a plate of organic-looking fluid was rated as 61$\%$ more disgusting than the image of a plate of blue fluid that looked like a chemical).

2.    Results were consistent across cultures.

3.    There were gender differences as females rated the disease-salient pictures more disgusting compared to men.

4.    There was a decrease in sensitivity to disgust as a result of aging. 

5.    Disgust was felt more for strangers. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

With the help of this study, it is evident that disgust as a behaviour has shown evolutionary changes with respect to age, and gender. Disgust has also shown to have passed down from one generation to the next. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

There are many strengths of a survey method. Data collection for a large sample size is easy to collect, conduct at the same time and cost effective compared to other research methods such as interviews or experiments. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ A survey method has low internal validity as causation cannot be established between the IV and DV whereas correlation can be established between variables. Due to the large sample size of 44,000 generalization of findings is high and makes the sample more representative of the population across cultures. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$ On the other hand, there are few limitations to the survey method. Since the data collected is self reported data, it may not always be accurate as people have a tendency to not highlight their weakness and may want to showcase a positive image of themselves. This leads to inaccurate data and there is no way of cross checking the same. It is a self selected sample where extraneous variables cannot be controlled such as subject differences with experience of disgust, having a high threshold for disgust. These variables may impact the correlation in the results$\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{CT}]}$. Another limitation is that images were shared with participants instead of actual things which may alter the level of disgust. 

The above explained study can clearly show the evolutionary explanation of disgust as a response as disgust is a biologically driven behaviour. Evolutionary theory suggests that behaviour evolves over times and through different generations by only passing off the healthy genetic material. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$
