# PS0916

Explain how twin and/or kinship studies are used to investigate the role of genetic similarities in one behaviour.

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:

\

Twin studies, especially those that compare monozygotic and dizygotic twins, assist researchers with establishing the extent to which behaviors like depression correspond with increasing genetic similarity. Monozygotic twins share 100$\%$ of their genes, while dizygotic twins share 50$\%$. If researchers find that concordance - the rate of similarity in a behavior -  correlates with the percentage of shared DNA, it indicates that a behavior is influenced by genetics. Concordance comparisons across mono and dizygotic twins may also reveal the role of the environment. 

Kendler et al. interviewed over 15,000 twins who were registered in the Swedish Twin Registry to better understand how genetic similarity would relate to the development of depression.  Through phone interviews, the researchers established whether or not participants had a history of depression, and a history of treatment for depression. In addition, researchers asked questions to reveal the extent to which each set of twins had a shared living environment in childhood. Other questions were asked to determine if events in adulthood may have exacerbated each individual’s risk of developing depression.

This study found that concordance rates for depression were higher in monozygotic twins than dizygotic twins. When comparing concordance rates by sex, female monozygotic twins had higher concordance rates than male monozygotic twins. The number of years participants spent living with their twin did not correlate with diagnosis of persistent major depression. Overall, researchers concluded heritability of depression at 0.38, in line with other completed research. 

This study demonstrates how twin research assists with understanding behavior by comparing genetic similarity. By comparing rates of depression between monozygotic and dizygotic twins, differences in concordance can support heritability, especially when the amount of time living together is taken into account.

$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{\underline{\textrm{Possible ideas \& key terms:}}}$

 - Monozygotic (identical) twins share the same DNA, while dizygotic (fraternal) share half, like other sibling types

 - Concordance rates describe the frequency at which two individuals share the same trait

 - Shared environment can also play a role in increasing concordance for some traits, and strong genetic similarity studies take measures to account for this

 - If monozygotic twin concordance rates are higher than dizygotic twin concordance rates, chances are higher that a trait is heritable 

$\textcolor{#056608}{\underline{\textrm{Possible key studies:}}}$

 - Skre et al.'s (1993) twin study investigating genetic influences on anxiety disorders

 - Holland et al.'s (1988) twin study investigating genetic influences on anorexia

 - Kendler et al.'s (2006) twin study investigating genetic influences on depression

 - Bouchard et al.'s (1990) twin study into genetic influences on intelligence

 - True et al.'s (1993) twin study investigating genetic influences on PTSD symptoms among U.S. Vietnam veterans

 - Weissman et al.'s (2005) kinship (parental) study of MDD

 - Yehuda et al.'s (2000, 1998) kinship (parental) studies investigating symptoms of PTSD among adult children of Holocaust survivors.
