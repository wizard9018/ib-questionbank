# PS0531

Explain the effect of genetic similarities on human behaviour using one  research study. 

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences (e.g. *“Genetic similarities is a significant factor which helps us to understand the reason why individuals within the same family display similar characteristics….”)*

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study $\text{\textendash}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study\

            *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Weissman et. al (2005) using family studies across generations on the incidence of depression.

-    Holland et.al (1988) uses twin studies to measure the effect of genes on eating disorders.

-    Bouchard et. al (1990) uses twin studies to measure the effect of genetics on intelligence

-    Sorensen et.al  (1992) uses adoption studies to test the role of genes in obesity 

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of hormones and its effects on human behaviour.\

           *Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include:*

-    Genetic inheritance - a process by which genetic information is passed through genes from parent to child.

-    Genetic similarities - a measure to understand the similarities in genetics among people who are related to each other

-    Twin studies - A type of research method carried out to measure genetic similarities among twins.

-    Monozygotic twins (MZ) - twins born from one single cell sharing 100% same genetic matter

-    Dizygotic twins (DZ) - twins born from two different cells sharing 50% of same genetic matter.

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic (e.g. *“Bouchard et.al (1990) used twin studies to understand the role of inheritance on intelligence - Links to results of the study as he found that MZT had a concordance rate of 86% while the MZA only had 76%. These results signify the impact of genetic factors on intelligence.”)*

**Marking reductions:**

-    No explanation on the method used to study genetics in the study cited. (up to 2 marks may be deducted)

-    Genetic similarities is not explained in detail. (up to 3 marks may be deducted)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Researchers study genetics to better understand the biological basis of human behaviour. Genetic similarities help us to understand the presence of common genetic materials among humans and its correlation on the incidence of behaviour and disorders $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. One of the earliest ways to study gene  inheritance is through twin studies using Monozygotic twins (MZ) - twins who share 100% genes as they are identical and Dizygotic twins (DZ) - twins who share 50% genes between them and are non-identical. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  The reasoning behind using twin studies is that twins mostly share the same environment as they grow up together with the same family and are exposed to similar stressors. Therefore, any difference in the occurrence of any disorder between them can be due to the genes they share.This is measured through concordance rates which is the correlation between the similarity of inheritance and occurrence of a disorder. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  One study that uses twin studies to investigate the role of inheritance in the occurrence of eating disorders such as Anorexia Nervosa  is Holland et al.'s research (1988). $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Holland et al. gathered 25 female MZ and 20 female DZ twin pairs with at least one of them diagnosed with anorexia. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  The twins were put through a structured diagnostic interview for Anorexia based on DSM III. Additionally, they were asked to complete questionnaires on eating habits and personality styles. The results from the study showed that  MZ twins had a significantly higher concordance rate of anorexia - 56% as compared to the DZ twins - only 5%. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  The results of this study give evidence  to the role of genetics on the occurrence of a disorder like anorexia nervosa as MZ twins who share 100% identical rates had significantly higher concordance rates as compared to DZ twins. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  Even though the MZ twins had a higher concordance rate when compared to the DZ twins, it is still not 100% indicating that though there is genetic predisposition it is still not necessary that if one MZ twin develops anorexia the other will also develop. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

This study shows how twin studies are effective in identifying the impact of genes on human behaviour by giving further evidence to the effect of genes on eating disorders. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
