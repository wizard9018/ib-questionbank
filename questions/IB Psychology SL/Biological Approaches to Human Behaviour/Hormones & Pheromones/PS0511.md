# PS0511

Explain how one pheromone can affect behaviour, using one study.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

There should be a direct answer to the question within the first few lines.

(*e.g. One study that explains the role of pheromones on human behaviour Doucet et a.'s research in 2009.*) A specific pheromone affecting a behaviour which is being investigated through the study must be mentioned. However, it should be pointed out that this area is still under research and all research/evidence only gives an indication of the possible role of pheromones in human behaviour. (*e.g. There is research suggesting the possibility of the pheromones playing a role in human mating behaviour.*)

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{2. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

There should be accurate knowledge and understanding of the topic by using all or some key terms, explained in a cohesive manner such as:

-    Pheromones - the focus of the response should be on the possible effects of Pheromones in human behaviour. There should be a clear explanation for why there is no complete evidence of its role in human behaviour but it is generally known to be present in animals.  This should be done in a rational and easy-to-understand manner.

-    Vomeronasal organ (VNO) - An olfactory organ not fully developed in humans said to influence mating and social behaviour.

-    Olfactory senses - to detect odours and smells 

-    Androstadienone (AND) - a pheromone secreted in male sweat that may be potentially attractive to females

-    MHC genes - major histocompatibility complex (MHC), a group of genes influencing our immune system response

The command term explain seeks to provide a detailed account including possible causes or reasons. While the explanation of the effect of pheromones is essential, there should also be a focus on meeting the command term. The types of behaviours in which pheromones are considered to be impactful must be considered. For example: *Pheromones may impact behaviours that take place between two individuals such as bonding, mating etc.*

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{3. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study - including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study. *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Wedekind et.al (1995) on the potential role of MHC genes on mating behaviour.

-    Doucet et.al (2009) on the effect of secretion of the mammary glands on an infant's sucking behaviour. 

-    Cutler, Friedman and McCoy (1998) on investigating if a synthesised pheromone can increase socio sexual behaviour of men

-    Lundstrom and Olsson (2005) on the effect of AND on women in the presence of men

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

There should be a clear link between the results of the study and the topic using one of the key terms mentioned in the introduction. For example: *Wedekind et.al (1995) on the potential role of MHC genes on mating behaviour - Females rated the t-shirts with MHC genes different from their own as highly pleasant when compared to their rating on the t-shirts with similar MHC genes. This links to the potential role of Pheromones as they are considered to affect human behaviour through their sense of smell.*

**Marking limits:**

-    Mixing up pheromones with hormones. (*no marks will be awarded*) 

-    A relevant study is described, but no clear links to the effect of pheromones on behaviour are made as it can be tricky to make explicit links. (*a maximum of 5 marks will be awarded*)

-    Jumping straight to the research without a clear explanation on how Pheromones work (*a maximum of 3 marks may be deducted*)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Pheromones are considered to be chemical messengers that work by interacting between individuals. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The olfactory senses are responsible for the transmission of pheromones as one of the ways they are secreted in the human body is through sweat.  They are theorised to work through the Vomeronasal organ (VNO)found in mammals giving them an advanced sense of smell. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The smell then triggers the hypothalamus in the brain stimulating emotions such as attraction, sexual arousal etc. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ While the role of Pheromones is observed clearly in animals, their effect on human behaviour is still an area under research, as even though this organ is present in humans, it is not as well-developed as animals. Pheromones can potentially effect human mating behaviour as suggested in the study done by Wedekind et.al (1995). $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$ $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Wedekind et al. (1995) conducted a study on 49 female and 44 male university students. He aimed to test whether MHC genes (Major Histocompatibility Complex) influence human mate preferences. MHC genes play an important role in the immune system and vary between individuals based on the alleles inherited from their parents. Evolutionary theory suggests that people may be attracted to partners with MHC genes different from their own, as this could increase the immune strength of their offspring.  $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

All participants were genetically tested to identify their MHC type. The male participants were not allowed to wear deodorant and were asked to sleep in the same plain T-shirt for two nights. They were also instructed not to engage in sexual activity or other behaviours that might affect their natural body odour. The female participants used a nasal spray to reduce the risk of colds and were tested mostly during the second week of their menstrual cycle. In the laboratory, the women were presented with seven boxes containing T-shirts — three from MHC-similar men, three from MHC-dissimilar men, and one unworn control shirt. They rated each odour for pleasantness.  $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The results showed that women generally rated the odours of MHC-dissimilar men as more pleasant than those of MHC-similar men. This suggests that biological factors, such as MHC-related body odour, may play a role in attraction.

 $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Therefore, the study provides support for the possible role of pheromone-like signals in human attraction, as women generally rated the odours of MHC-dissimilar men as more pleasant. The finding that preferences were reversed in women taking oral contraceptives suggests that hormonal factors influence MHC-based odour preferences. Since the women only smelled the T-shirts, the results indicate that body odour may act as a biological cue in attraction, although this does not prove the existence of human pheromones.  $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{L}]}$
