# PS0527

Describe one study on the effect of one hormone to human behaviour.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

 A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences (e.g. *“Hormones affect human behaviour in various ways, one of which is…… One study that demonstrates this effect is….”)*

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study $\text{\textendash}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study

            *Relevant studies include but are not limited to: *

-    *Newcomer et.al (1999,) on the effect of Cortisol on Verbal declarative memory*

-    *Ronay and von Hippel (2010), on the effect of testosterone in risk-taking behaviour of males*

-    *Mc Gaugh and Cahill (1995), on the effect of adrenaline on flashbulb memories*

-    *Zethraeus et. al (2009), on the effect of oestrogen and testosterone on human economic behaviour*

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of one hormone and its effects on human behaviour.

           Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include:

-    Laboratory experiment $\text{\textendash}$ An experiment in a lab with well defined IV and DV

-    Independent variable (IV $\text{\textendash}$ Variable that is manipulated in the experiment

-    Dependent Variable (DV) $\text{\textendash}$ Variable that is measured in the experiment.

-    Cause and effect relationship $\text{\textendash}$ The relationship between two variables with one causing another.

-    Hormones $\text{\textendash}$  chemical substances that help in passing information from one part of the body to other through the bloodstream. Provide one specific hormone, such as but not limited to the following: cortisol, adrenaline, oxytocin 

-    Target cells $\text{\textendash}$  Cells that receive the messages from the hormones as they have the specific receptors for the hormones

-    Receptors - detect signals from different hormones/neurotransmitters

-    Human behaviour - focus on a specific human behaviour related to the hormone in focus

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic (e.g. *Mcgaugh and Cahill (1995) concluded that adrenaline released during a stressful situation such as the dramatic story in the first experiment led to the formation of flashbulb memories in the participants as measured by their performance on the recognition task 2 weeks later. This result clearly suggests that adrenaline plays a role in the formation of flashbulb memories.)*

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: 

Hormones are described as chemicals secreted by glands in the endocrine system directly in the bloodstream having various effects on human behaviour$\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. As they are secreted in the bloodstream and not in the synaptic gaps, it takes time to produce an effect but they are more long-lasting than a typical neurotransmitter response. The hormones bind themselves to specific receptor sites known as target cells bringing about a change in behaviour $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. Research in this field is often carried out in lab conditions with a clearly defined IV and DV to determine a strong cause-and-effect relationship $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$.  A  lab experiment conducted by Newcomer et al in 1999 gives evidence of the effect of the hormone Cortisol on Verbal declarative memory (human behaviour) $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$.

   

Newcomer et.al (1999) carried out a study on a population of students and employees from Washington University Medical Centre. Participants were divided into three conditions - group one in a high cortisol condition, ingesting 60 mg of cortisol similar to those experiencing a highly stressful event. Group 2 ingested 40 mg of cortisol similar to a moderately stressful condition. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The last group was a control group that had a placebo pill. All participants underwent a baseline test to ensure there were no individual differences in their cognitive performance as it could become a confounding variable. The participants were asked to memorise a prose passage and were tested for the number of errors made. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Results showed that the high cortisol group performed the worst and the control group performed the best. This clearly indicates the effect of the hormone cortisol on verbal declarative memory as high levels of the hormone significantly impaired human behaviour. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

 The study had a well-defined independent variable - the different levels of cortisol ingested by the participants and an accurate measurement for the dependent variable which was the number of errors made by the participants while recalling the passage. Additionally, this was a double-blind experiment ensuring that both the participants and the experimenters were unaware of who belonged in which group thereby ensuring a strong cause-and-effect relationship $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$. Thus, the participant's memory can be linked to the increased level of cortisol as there was a clear difference in the performance between the three groups  which occurred due to the changes in the level of cortisol  $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$.
