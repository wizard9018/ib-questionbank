# PS0690

Explain one ethical consideration related to one study investigating hormones and/or pheromones and behaviour.

[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

One ethical consideration in studying the effect of hormones on behaviour is informed consent, which should be obtained in writing before a research study begins. This is evident in Newcomer et al.'s study on cortisol and memory, which ensured that participants fully understood the potential risks before agreeing to participate in the study. 

Newcomer et al.'s study aimed to investigate whether high levels of the stress hormone cortisol affect verbal declarative memory (which is the ability to recall facts and knowledge).

In the study, participants were randomly split into three groups. The first group was given a high dose of cortisol (160 mg per day) to simulate the levels experienced during a major stress event. The second group received a lower dose (40 mg per day), mimicking a minor stress event. The third group acted as a control and was given a placebo (a tablet with no active ingredient). Participants listened to and recalled parts of a prose paragraph to test their memory. 

The results showed that those who received the highest dose of cortisol performed the worst on the memory test but only after 4 days of exposure. However, their memory returned to normal after they stopped taking the hormone. The study concluded that high cortisol levels for extended periods interferes with verbal declarative memory.

To ensure that ethical standards were met, the researchers gained informed consent, which involved explaining the risks of taking cortisol, such as temporary physical side effects, including changes in blood pressure or immune response. Participants were informed about the study's purpose, the tasks they would perform, and the potential side effects. This process of providing clear information and ensuring participants voluntarily agreed is called informed consent. Gaining informed consent also involves informing participants about their rights, such as being able to withdraw at any time without penalty.

Newcomer et al.'s study highlighted the importance of acquiring scientific knowledge through ethical measures, as seen in their acquisition of consent. While the study provided valuable insights into how stress hormones like cortisol affect memory, ethical considerations such as informed consent are essential to protecting the rights and well-being of the participants involved.

 - Ethical considerations are particularly important in studies involving hormones and their physiological and behavioral effects.
