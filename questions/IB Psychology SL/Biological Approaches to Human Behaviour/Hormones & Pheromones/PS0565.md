$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Hormones are chemical messengers that are released from the endocrine glands directly into the bloodstream to influence behaviour such as aggression, mating behaviour. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$ The effect of hormones is long lasting and affects important bodily functions such as growth, metabolism and reproduction$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$There is very little voluntary control on the production and effect of hormones. Hormones alter the chances of a behaviour occurrence in response to environmental factors (gene activation or gene suppression). This essentially means that when a hormone binds to a receptor it launches a sequence of changes as a result of environmental stimuli. This explains how oxytocin promotes fidelity.

There are a variety of hormones produced in the body, a few examples are oxytocin, cortisol, adrenaline$\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. 

This answer will focus on oxytocin and its influence on human behaviour. Oxytocin is produced in the hypothalamus and is transported into the bloodstream by the pituitary gland. It plays a role in sexual reproduction (also released with every kiss or hug), childbirth (oxytocin is released from stimulation of nipples during breastfeeding) and social bonding. It is also called a love and bonding hormone. $\textcolor{#C34A2C}{[\textbf{KT}]}$The study conducted by Scheele et al (2012) on oxytocin promoting fidelity is explained below. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$

The research conducted by Scheele et al in 2012, aimed to demonstrate whether oxytocin would lead to influencing men in a relationship to keep greater distance from female strangers and whether it promotes fidelity$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$. There were a total 86 heterosexual male adults in the study, out of which some were singles and others in a stable relationship$\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$. It was a double-blind experiment using an independent measures design. A researcher administered either oxytocin or a placebo intranasally to the two groups of participants. All participants took part in 2 independent tasks, one of which was the $`$stop-distance paradigm'. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$In this scenario, participants were to be positioned at one end of the room with a mark on the floor, while an attractive female experimenter was to stand on the other end of the room. The participants were asked to move slowly towards the female experimenter and stop at a point that made them uncomfortable. A variable controlled throughout was that the experimenter maintained the same appearance over all the trials.

Results found that in the oxytocin group, men in a monogamous relationship kept a greater distance between themselves and an attractive woman compared to single men. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ It was also found that in the oxytocin group men in a relationship tend to stay away from an attractive woman who was not their partner. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

In the second part of the same experiment, a task called the $`$approach/avoidance task' was introduced in which participants viewed a series of pictures on a screen with a viewing distance of 50m. Each picture was shown for two seconds. There were a total of four types of photos, positive social or attractive women, positive non-social or beautiful landscapes, negative social or mutilations and negative non-social or dirt. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ A joystick was provided to all participants and they were instructed to pull the joystick if they liked the picture which would increase the picture's size. They were also told that if they didn't like the picture, they were to push the joystick backwards which would reduce the images' size. The results of this part of the experiment showed that the only group of pictures affected by oxytocin and relationship status was the positive social group (pictures of attractive women). The participants in the oxytocin group had slower reaction time and they were more reluctant to pull the joystick if they were in a relationship. $\textcolor{#056608}{[\textbf{S}]}$ These trials showed that oxytocin promotes fidelity in men who are in a relationship. 

The research had several evaluation points. The strengths of the research are that there was no evidence of bias as due to its use of a double-blind measure. The results also show more credibility as the experiment had two parts to show the relationship between the independent and dependent variable. The study also has high internal validity as a cause and effect relationship can be established between the IV and DV as others variables were kept constant so that they do not affect the changes in the DV. This was done to see the effect of oxytocin (IV) on fidelity (DV).  There was also less effect of extraneous variables as it was a controlled lab experiment. The study also addressed ethical considerations showing evidence of physical or psychological harm to the participants. Another strength of a lab experiment is that it has high replicability due to its standardized procedure. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

The limitations of the study are that the extraneous variables like duration of the relationship, problems in a relationship have not been taken into consideration which can alter the results as these factors also affect loyalty in a relationship. The attractiveness of the experimenter is a subjective factor which may have affected the results of the study. Another limitation of a lab experiment is that it may not always reflect real life situations as people behave differently in a lab setting versus their natural environment. $\textcolor{#1974D2}{[\textbf{CT}]}$

This study clearly supported how the hormone oxytocin has an effect on the fidelity in relationships in men. Other research methods such as semi interviews may also be used to understand factors that influence fidelity in men and women as it can help to collect qualitative data to understand the topic in depth. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{F}]}$
