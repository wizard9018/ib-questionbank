# PS0534

Describe one technique used to study the brain in relation to behaviour, using one research study.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences (e.g. *“One technique used to study the brain is through using advanced brain imaging techniques such as the magnetic resonance imaging or MRI… ”)*

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study related to the techniques to study the brain which includes the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study\

            *Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Maguire (2000) studies neuroplasticity using MRI scans. 

-    Harris and Fiske (2006) on the activation of amygdala using an fMRI machine.

-    Tiernay et. al (2000) used PET scans to analyse language centres in the brain.

-    Draganski (2004) used MRI to study neural pruning in jugglers.

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

To answer the command term describe, the response should show an accurate knowledge and understanding of at least one technique to study the brain in relation to human behaviour.\

           *Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include some of the 

           following:*

-    MRI scans (magnetic resonance imaging) - a brain scanning technique which uses magnetic waves helping to study the structure of the brain

-    fMRI scans (functional magnetic resonance imaging) -  a brain scanning technique which uses magnetic waves helping to study the function of the brain

-    PET scans (positron emission tomography) - Positron Emission Tomography uses radioactive waves which helps to study the function of the brain

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic  (e.g. *Draganski (2004) used MRI scans to study the grey matter in the mid-temporal area of the participants. The researchers further used VBM to analyse the results of the MRI and arrived at the conclusion that participants had higher grey matter in the region specific to visual memory after they learned juggling. However, a post MRI scan showed that the grey matter had reduced once they stopped practising the skill. Thus, MRI scanning are helpful to further our understanding of neural pruning..”)*

**Marking reductions:**

-    Techniques of studying the brain are confused with research methods (up to 5 marks may be deducted if the study used is relevant to the topic)

-    The study cited is not relevant to the topic such as Rogers and Kesner (2003) or Rosenzweig, Bennet & Diamond (1972) as neither use any brain imaging techniques (up to 4 marks may be deducted)

-    The response does not focus on the topic and no link is present. For eg. in the Mcgaugh and Cahill (1995) study, the response may mention an imaging technique such as MRI, fMRI etc but the focus is on the concept - flashbulb memories and not on how the technique was used to study the brain (up to 4 marks may be deducted)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

The use of brain imaging techniques in research opened up new doors for scientists to study the brain in multiple ways. The use of these methods in psychological research  are relatively recent paving the way for many discoveries. Brain imaging techniques are neuroimaging techniques that create images of the brain which help the researchers to study the changes that occur in the brain both functional and structural $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. Magnetic resonance imaging or MRI scans are one of the most widely used non-invasive techniques that help to understand the structural changes occurring in the brain including the changes in size of the grey matter in different areas of the brain, the density of the grey matter etc. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  MRI scans use the body's natural magnetic field, radioactive waves and a computer to create 2D or 3D images $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. Neuroplasticity is a concept that has been discovered through the use of these MRI scans. One study that used MRI scans to understand neuroplasticity is Maguire (2000). $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Maguire et. al (2000) wanted to investigate if the continuous practice of a skill, such as driving, could change the brain's structure leading to neuroplasticity. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  A sample of 16 right-handed male taxi drivers was used who had a driving experience of minimum of 1.5 years in addition to passing the Knowledge test - a requirement to obtain a taxi licence in London. MRI scanning was used and the participant's grey matter was scanned and later compared to 50 other right-handed males who were not taxi drivers. Additionally, the VBM (Voxel-based morphology) was used to analyse the MRI scans to determine the density of the grey matter along with pixel counting which helps to measure the size of the area being measured. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  Results from the VBM showed that the taxi drivers with many years of experience had a higher volume of right posterior hippocampus, an area in the brain associated with spatial memory. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  This indicates that dendritic branching had occurred as part of the process of neuroplasticity in the brain of the taxi drivers. The pixel counting also showed a larger area of posterior hippocampus in the taxi drivers as compared to the non-taxi drivers as a result of passing the knowledge test and driving continuously for the past few years.  $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$  The findings above suggest that neuroplasticity was evident due to changes observed in the taxi driver's brain.

The use of MRI scans in the study helped to determine the structural changes in the grey matter of the taxi drivers which was evident when compared to the images from the MRI scans of the non-taxi drivers. Further the use of VBM and pixel counting helps to give accurate results in a numerical form that can be compared and help researchers reach a logical conclusion to the research. Thereby, MRI imaging technique helped researchers to discover the concept of neuroplasticity in the brain which was not possible before as researchers had no way to study the brain of a live human being. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$
