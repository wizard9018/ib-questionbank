# PS0532

Describe one study on localisation of brain function in relation to human behaviour.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

$\textcolor{#000000}{\textbf{1. Direct Answer}\ [\textbf{DA}]}$\

A direct answer to the question provided in the first few sentences (e.g. *“One study that supports localization is Harris & Fiske's research in 2006 investigating the role of the amygdala in managing emotions..”)*

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

A clear focus on describing one study $\text{\textendash}$ including the aim, procedure, results, and conclusion of the study\

*Relevant studies include but are not limited to:*

-    Milner (1966) case study on the function of the hippocampus in the brain

-    Maguire (2000) on taxi driver's spatial ability and the hippocampus

-    Draganski (2004) on juggler's visual processing in the mid-temporal area

-    Harris and Fiske (2006) on the amygdala as the emotional centre of the brain

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of localisation of brain function in relation to human behaviour as supported by a relevant study.\

*Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include:*

-    Localization of brain function - different brain areas are associated with specific cognitive functions such as thinking, decision making etc.

-    Hippocampus - brain area associated with memory

-    Amygdala - brain area associated with managing emotions

-    Brain imaging techniques - Methods used to study the structure and functions of the brain such as MRI, fMRI etc.

-    fMRI - Functional Magnetic resonance imaging used to study the functions of the brain

$\textcolor{#4472C4}{\textbf{4. Link}\ [\textbf{L}]}$\

An explicit link connecting the results of the study to the topic  (e.g. *“Draganski (2004) found that when the participants had learned and practised a new skill of juggling the grey matter in their mid-temporal area had significantly increased. These results give evidence to the theory of localization as the mid-temporal area is associated with visual memory which would be necessary to learn the skill of juggling.”)*

**Marking reductions:**

-    No specific brain area is mentioned nor associated with a function. (up to 4 marks may be deducted)

-    The study cited is not appropriate to localization but focuses on other concepts such as neuroplasticity which are similar. (up to 4 marks may be deducted)

-    Localisation of brain function and a relevant study are both clearly described but there is no explicit link between the result of the study and localisation of brain function. (a maximum of 6 marks may be awarded)

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Localization of function in the brain was among of the earliest ways researchers tried to understand how the brain works. While many of the early theories have been discarded due to insufficient evidence, this theory still continues to hold weight. Localization of function refers to the concept that different brain areas are associated with specific cognitive functions such as thinking, decision making, impulse control, language, among other things. $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$  One of the lobes in the brain is the temporal lobe within which the amygdala is situated. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  The amygdala is known as the emotional centre of the brain known for its role in managing emotions and its associated behaviours. $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$  One study that supports localization is Harris & Fiske's research in 2006 investigating the role of the amygdala in managing emotions. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

Harris and Fiske (2006) aimed to investigate the role of the amygdala and the prefrontal cortex while being exposed to distressing images. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ They gathered 22 University undergraduates and randomly allocated them to two different conditions. This study used an fMRI - an advanced brain imaging tool that helps to identify the specific functions of the different brain areas as the patient can perform different tasks while within the machine while being connected to the scanner. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The participants in group 1 saw images of objects whereas participants in group 2 saw images of homeless people or addicts. Their brain activity was measured while viewing these images in the fMRI. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ Results from the study showed that participants in group 1 did not show any brain activity in the amygdala whereas participants who viewed homeless people and addicts did. This could be as viewing distressing images of people may have caused the participants to have an emotional reaction leading to the activation of amygdala. $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$

The results of this study give evidence to the functions of the amygdala as one that is associated with managing emotions as viewing distressing images activates this brain region among the participants as found by the fMRI scanner. $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$  This study effectively shows localization of function is present and specific brain areas are responsible for different functions of the brain.
