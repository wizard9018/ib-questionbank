# PS0528

Describe one study on neuroplasticity.
[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

A maximum of **9 marks** are awarded if the response includes all of the following:

A direct answer to the question provided in first few sentences (e.g. *“Research in the field of neuroplasticity …A study that demonstrates the process of neuroplasticity and its effect on human behaviour is ….”)*

$\textcolor{#008c3a}{\textbf{2. Study}\ [\textbf{S}]}$\

$\textcolor{#F04438}{\textbf{3. Key Terms}\ [\textbf{KT}]}$\

Accurate knowledge and understanding of neuroplasticity and its on human behaviour.

           *Important concepts and vocabulary terms to use and/or define include:*

-    Laboratory experiment - An experiment in a lab with well defined IV and DV

-    Independent variable (IV - Variable that is manipulated in the experiment

-    Dependent Variable (DV) - Variable that is measured in the experiment.

-    Correlational study - a study that looks at the relationship between two variables

-    Imaging techniques - techniques that use radioactive or magnetic waves to view the structure and functions of the brain such as MRI, fMRI etc.

-    Brain plasticity - the ability of the brain to reorganise its functions by changes to the neural pathways.

-    Neurogenesis - the process by which new neurons are formed in the brain.

-    Synapses - the junction between two neurons where information passess

-    Neural pathways - the organisation of neurons that helps in communication flow

-    Neural pruning - the reduction or shrinkage of unused neural pathways

-    Dendritic branching - the process of thickening of dendrites often seen when new habits/responses are forming.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

Research in the field of neuroplasticity is quite recent with much of it being conducted on humans in the last 40 - 50 years $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. One of the reasons for this could be that  changes in the brain are difficult to investigate and require  the use of various imaging techniques such as MRI, CT scans etc, which were developed in the mid 1900's. Neuroplasticity is described as  a process that explains how the brain changes itself to suit the demands of the environment $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$. The brain consists of millions of neurons that pass information to each other, forming neural pathways. These neural pathways get strengthened through dendritic branching, indicating that neurons are firing continuously, leading to a growth in their dendrites. This leads to structural plasticity or changes in the structure of the brain $\textcolor{#F04438}{[\textbf{KT}]}$. A study that demonstrates neuroplasticity in taxi drivers is Maguire et.al (2000) $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$.

Maguire et. al (2000) wanted to investigate if the continuous practice of a skill, such as driving, would lead to brain structure changes. A sample of 16 right-handed male taxi drivers who had a driving experience of a minimum of 1.5 years in addition to passing the knowledge test - a requirement to obtain a taxi licence in London, were gathered. An MRI scan was used, and the participant's grey matter was scanned and later compared to 50 other right-handed males who were not taxi drivers. Technology such as the VBM (Voxel-based morphology) was used, which helps to determine the density of the grey matter and pixel counting which helps to determine the size of the area being measured. Results from the VBM showed that the taxi drivers with many years of experience had a higher volume of the right posterior hippocampus, an area in the brain associated with spatial memory. This indicates that dendritic branching had occurred as part of the process of neuroplasticity in the brains of the taxi drivers. The pixel counting also showed a larger area of posterior hippocampus in the taxi drivers as compared to the non-taxi drivers as a result of passing the knowledge test and driving continuously for the past few years.  $\textcolor{#008c3a}{[\textbf{S}]}$ The findings above suggest that neuroplasticity was evident due to changes observed in the taxi driver's brain.

The study was a quasi-experiment as the independent variable (taxi drivers or non-taxi drivers) naturally occurred. Additionally, it was a correlational study as VBM results clearly showed that the more years the participants were driving caused changes to the brain structure helping to show a clear link between environment and brain plasticity $\textcolor{#4472C4}{[\textbf{L}]}$. The results from this study can thus be linked to the concept of Neuroplasticity as there is a clear indication of the changes in the grey matter of the participant's brain due to the advanced imaging techniques used. 

It can be concluded that neuroplasticity is a complex phenomenon dependent upon the stimulation from the environment - spending time practising a skill, which can lead to structural changes in the brain, as demonstrated in the study by Maguire (2000) $\textcolor{#000000}{[\textbf{DA}]}$.
