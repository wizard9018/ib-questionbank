# PH0432

**Maximum mark: 10**

A satellite of mass $m$ is launched from the surface and placed in an orbit around a planet of mass $M$ and radius $R$. The height of its orbit from the surface of the planet is $h$.

The initial speed of the satellite is equal to $\dfrac{4}{5}v_{esc}$.

The planet has no atmosphere.

1. Define gravitational potential energy    [2 marks]

2. 1. Show that the initial total energy of the satellite is given by $-\dfrac{9GMm}{25R}$. [2 marks]

    2. The satellite is placed in an orbit with orbital radius $r$.

        Determine the expression for the total energy of the satellite in its orbit in terms of $G, M, m$ and $r$.      [2 marks]

    3. Use your answers to parts b(i) and b(ii) to determine the height of orbit above the surface of the planet in terms of $R$.    [2 marks]

3.  The satellite ejects excess fuel opposite to its direction of motion. Suggest the effect of this change on the average orbital radius of the satellite. [2 marks]

2.  1. Initial total energy = gravitational potential energy + initial kinetic energy

        $= -\dfrac{GMm}{R}+\dfrac{1}{2}m{(\dfrac{4}{5}v_{esc})}^2 $    $\checkmark$

        $=-\dfrac{GMm}{R}+\dfrac{1}{2}m(\dfrac{16}{25})(\dfrac{2GM}{R})\checkmark\ll=-\dfrac{9GMm}{25R}\gg $

       $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Initial total energy = gravitational potential energy + initial kinetic energy

        Gravitational potential energy = $-\dfrac{GMm}{R}$

        Initial velocity = $\dfrac{4}{5}v_{esc}$

        Therefore initial kinetic energy = $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}m(\dfrac{4}{5}v_{esc})^2$

        Knowing that $v_{esc}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}$

        Initial kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}m(\dfrac{4}{5})^2(\dfrac{2GM}{R}) = m(\dfrac{16}{25})(\dfrac{GM}{R})$

        Total energy = $-\dfrac{GMm}{R}$+$-\dfrac{16}{25}\dfrac{GMm}{R} = -\dfrac{9}{25}\dfrac{GMm}{R}$

    2. $\ll-\dfrac{GMm}{r}+\dfrac{1}{2}m\left(v_{orb}\right)^2=\gg-\dfrac{GMm}{r}+\dfrac{1}{2}m\dfrac{GM}{r}$ $\checkmark$

         $=-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        $\begin{aligned}
            \textrm{Total energy} &= \textrm{gravitational potential energy} + \textrm{orbital kinetic energy}\\
            \hspace{1em}\\
            &=-\frac{GMm}{r}+\frac{1}{2}m\left(v_{orb}\right)^2\\
            \hspace{1em}\\
            &=-\frac{GMm}{r}+\frac{1}{2}m\frac{GM}{r}\\
            \hspace{1em}\\
            &=-\frac{GMm}{2r}\\
            \end{aligned}$

    3.  $-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}=\ -\dfrac{9GMm}{25R} $ $\checkmark$

        Solving gives $r=\dfrac{25}{18}R $ $\checkmark$

        $h=r-R=\dfrac{25}{18}R-R=\dfrac{7}{18}R $ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
 Equating the expressions from bi and bii:

        $-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}=\ -\dfrac{9GMm}{25R} $

        Solving gives $r=\dfrac{25}{18}R $

        This is the radius of the orbit, so to find the height above the surface the radius of the planet needs to be subtracted, giving

        $h=r-R=\dfrac{25}{18}R-R=\dfrac{7}{18}R $

3. ejection to the rear increases the speed due to conservation of momentum $\checkmark$

    this causes the satellite’s orbital radius to increase $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Delivering an impulse in the backwards direction to the gases will result in an impulse in the forward direction on the satellite, increasing its momentum and therefore its velocity.

    The total energy of the satellite therefore has increased. Total energy is given by the expression

    $-\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$

    For this value to increase (become less negative), the value of r must increase.
