    2. Line segment joining the asteroid and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal time intervals.  $\checkmark$

        The area swept out per radian/per degree/per unit angle is greater for points further from the sun / lower for points closer to the sun.  $\checkmark$

        Points furthest from the sun have the lowest angular speed.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Area swept out per radian is greater for points further from the sun. Kepler's second law tells us that the time taken per radian must also be greater. So, radians covered per unit time (angular speed) must be lower.

3. 1. $\dfrac{GmM}{r^2}=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ or $\dfrac{GmM}{r^2} =m{\omega}^2 r$.   $\checkmark$

        Substitution of $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ or $v=\dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$    $\checkmark$

        Clear rearranging leading to $T^2=\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{GM} r^3$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Gravitational force is equal to centripetal force so

        - $\dfrac{GmM}{r^2} = m{\omega}^2 r$

        Where $m$ is the mass of the planet.

        Cancelling $m$'s gives:

        - $\dfrac{GM}{r^2} = {\omega}^2 r$

        $\omega$ can be written in terms of the time period $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, substituting this into the equation above gives:

        - $\dfrac{GM}{r^2} = \Bigl(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\Bigr)^2 r $

        - $\dfrac{GM}{r^2} = \dfrac{4{\pi}^2 r}{T^2}$

        - $T^2=\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{GM} r^3$

    2. 225 days $\approx$ 0.62 years

        And

        $T^2≈0.37$  $\checkmark$

        $r^3≈0.38$

    3. gradient $= \dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{GM}$   $\checkmark$

        Expression or numerical value of gradient in SI units.

        gradient $= 1\times \dfrac{((365)(24)(60)(60))^2}{(1.5\times10^{11})^3} = 2.94…\times 10^{-19}\,\ce{s^2}\,\ce{m^{-3}}$   $\checkmark$

        (allow values in range 1-1.02 for gradient before change of units)

        $M=\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{(6.67\times10^{-11})(2.94…\times 10^{-19})}=2.0\times 10^{30}\, \ce{kg}$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $T^2=\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{GM} r^3$

        Comparing this to $y=mx$ tells us that $\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{GM}$ is the gradient of a graph of $T^2$ plotted against $r^3$.

        Where $G$ is the gravitational constant (found in your formula book) and $M$ is the mass of the sun.

        The gradient of the graph is approximately 1 in units $\text{(Earth years)}^2 (\ce{AU})^{-3}$ but we must convert into SI units to find the mass of the sun in kg.

        - 1 Earth year = (365)(24)(60)(60)  seconds

        Therefore

        -  $\text{(1 Earth year)}^2 (\ce{AU})^{-3}=((365)(24)(60)(60))^2 (1.5\times 10^{11})^{-3}\, \ce{s^2}\, \ce{m^{-3}}=2.94\times 10^{-19}\, \ce{s^2}\, \ce{m^{-3}}$

        - gradient $=\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{GM}$

        - $M=\dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{(G)(\text{gradient})}= \dfrac{4{\pi}^2}{(6.67\times 10^{-11})(2.94\times 10^{-19})}=2.0\times 10^{30}\, \ce{kg}$
