# PH0430

**Maximum mark: 5**

Two masses of $6.2\times10^3$ kg and $4.0\times 10^3$ kg are initially separated by a distance of 4.3 m between their centers.

1. Calculate the gravitational potential energy of the two masses when in this arrangement. [2 marks]

2. Explain why your answer to part (a) is negative.   [1 mark]

3.  With reference to the gravitational potential due to the $6.2\times10^3$ kg mass, suggest how the gravitational potential energy would change if the $4.0\times 10^3 \,$ kg mass were moved further away.   [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $GPE =-\frac{\left(6.67\,\times\,{10}^{-11}\right)\left(6.2\,\times\,{10}^3\right)(4.0\,\times\,{10}^3)}{4.3}\,\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1.0cm}=- \,3.9\,\times\,{10}^{-4}\, \ce{J}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The formula for gravitational potential energy is

    $\begin{aligned}
    Eg &= -\frac{GMm}{r}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    &= -\frac{\left(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\right)\left(6. 2\times{10}^3\right)(4.0\times{10}^3)}{4.3}\\
    \hspace{1em}\\
    \end{aligned}$
    $\hspace{1.5em} = $:answer[$\ -3.9\times{10}^{-4}\,\ce{J}$]

2. GPE at infinity is zero and it decreases as separation decreases

     **OR**

    Force and displacement vectors are 180$\degree$ apart (cos (180$\degree$)= -1 in the work formula)

    **OR**

    Because the force is attractive $\checkmark$

3. The gravitational potential energy would increase $\checkmark$

   Because the gravitational potential of the mass will become less negative/work is required to move the masses apart due to the attractive force between them $\checkmark$

   $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
   As two masses move apart, work is done against the gravitation force and so their gravitational potential energy increases, reaching a maximum of zero at infinity, which is at zero potential due to the first mass.
