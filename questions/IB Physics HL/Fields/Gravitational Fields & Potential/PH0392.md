# PH0392

A planet of mass $25M$ and its moon of mass $M$ are shown in the diagram. At point $X$ on the line joining their centers, the resultant gravitational field is zero, and the gravitational potential due to the moon is $V$.

![PH0392xxx](images/PH0392xxx.png)

What is the total gravitational potential at $X$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}2V$

- B. $\hspace{1em}3V$

- C. $\hspace{1em}6V$

- D. $\hspace{1em}26V$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

- $V=-\dfrac{GM}{r}$

At $X$, the magnitude of the gravitational field due to the planet and its moon must be equal. If the distance of $X$ from $M$ is $r$, and the distance of $X$ from $25M$ is $d$, $d = 5r$ because:

$\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2cm}\\
\frac{GM}{r^2}&=\frac{G25M}{d^2} \\
\hspace{2cm}\\
r^2&=\frac{d^2}{25}\\
\hspace{2cm}\\
d&=5r \\
\end{aligned}$

The expression for the gravitational potential of the planet at $X$ is given by:

- $V=-\dfrac{G(25M)}{5r}=-5\dfrac{GM}{r}=5V$

Potential is a scalar, therefore the total potential at X is V + 5V = 6V
