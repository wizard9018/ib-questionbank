# PH0683

**Maximum mark: 9**

A space station **X** orbits Earth with orbital radius $R$.

1. State Newton\'s universal law of gravitation. [2 marks]

2. Explain why an astronaut in the space station feels weightless. [3 marks]

3. The space station changed its position so that its orbital radius is $2R$.

    Explain how the gravitational field strength of Earth at this new position compares to that at the orbital radius $R$. [2 marks]

4. Another space station **Y** with a smaller mass orbits Earth at the same orbital radius $R$.

    Compare the orbital speed of **Y** \ to the orbital speed of **X**. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Force of gravity between two objects is inversely proportional to the square of the separation of their centers. $\checkmark$

    Force is directly proportional to the product of their masses. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Here it would also be acceptable to state the formula and define all terms within the formula

2. The centripetal force is the gravitational force between Earth and the space station $\checkmark$

    Both the astronaut and the space station have the same acceleration $\checkmark$

    The acceleration is the gravitational field strength at that position. $\checkmark$

3. Gravitational field strength is inversely proportional to the square of the distance. $\checkmark$

    The gravitational field strength will be decreased to quarter of its value at the first position. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The gravitational field strength is given by
$g=\dfrac{GM}{R^2}$

    This means that the gravitational field strength is inversely proportional to the square of the distance.
Then the gravitational field strength will be decreased to quarter of its value at the first position.

    $\hspace{1em}$ $g_2=\dfrac{GM}{\left(2R\right)^2}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $g_2=\dfrac{GM}{{4R}^2}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $g_2=\dfrac{g}{4}$

4. Same $\checkmark$

    Orbital speed is independent of mass of the space station/ only depends on the mass of the planet and the orbital radius $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The centripetal force is the gravitational force,

    Then
$\dfrac{v^2}{r}=\dfrac{GM}{r^2}$

    Then the orbital speed is
$v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$

    The orbital speed is independent of the mass of the space station; it only depends on the mass of the planet and the orbital radius.

    Since both **X** and **Y** have the same orbital radius and they both orbit the same planet, then they both have the same orbital speed
