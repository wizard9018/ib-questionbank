# PH0389

A satellite is in an orbit at a height $h$ above the surface of Earth. The gravitational potential at its location is $V$.

The satellite is transferred to a different orbit where the gravitational potential is $\dfrac{V}{2}$

What can be deduced about the new height of this satellite from the surface of Earth?

- A. $\hspace{1em}    \dfrac{h}{2}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$     Between $h$ and $2h$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $2h$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ More than $2h$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Gravitational potential is inversely proportional to the distance from the center of mass of Earth.

Initially this is $R+h$

So $V=-\dfrac{GM}{R+h}$

In the new location, the gravitational potential is $\dfrac{V}{2}$,

- $\dfrac{V}{2}=-\dfrac{GM}{2\left(R+h\right)}=-\dfrac{GM}{R+h_{new}}$

- $2(R + h) = R + h_{new}$

- $2R + 2h = R + h_{new}$

- $R + 2h = h_{new}$

Which means the new height must be more than $2h$.

This can also be solved without algebra by realizing that the radius of the planet cannot increase, therefore to double the distance from the center of the planet, $h$ must more than double.
