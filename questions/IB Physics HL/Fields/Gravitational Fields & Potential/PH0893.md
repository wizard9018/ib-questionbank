# PH0893

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. The moon orbits the Earth at a constant speed of about $1022\ \ce{ms^{-1}}$. The moon's orbit can be approximated as circular. The ratio of the mass of the earth to the mass of the moon is 81 and the average distance between the centre of the earth and the moon is around $3.8 \times 10^8$ m.

    1. Deduce the mass of the Earth. [3 marks]

    2. On the graph below, sketch the variation of the gravitational field strength
g with the position $x$ from the surface of the Earth to the surface of the Moon. Take the surface of the earth as the origin of the graph. [3 marks]

        *(No need to add values to the axes)*

        ![PH0893](images/PH0893.png)

    3. Determine the distance from the Earth's centre to a point where the combined gravitational field of the Earth and the Moon is zero. [2 marks]

2. If the radius of the Moon's orbit were larger than it is now, deduce how the gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy of the moon would compare to their current values. [2 marks]

    1. Identification of gravitational attraction as the centripetal force $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $M = \dfrac{rv^2}{G}$

        $\hspace{1em}$= :answer[$6.0 \times 10^{24}\ \ce{kg}$]

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The force responsible for the circular motion of the moon around the Earth is the gravitational attraction between Earth and the Moon:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = m \dfrac{v^2}{r}$

        Where $M$ and $m$ are the mass of the Earth and the Moon respectively.

        When the relationship is simplified and rearranged to make $M$ as the subject:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $M = \dfrac{rv^2}{G}$

        By substituting the given values:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $M = \dfrac{(3.8 \times 10^8)(1022)^2}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}} = 6.0 \times 10^{24}\ \ce{kg}$

    2. $g$ = zero at point closer to the moon $\checkmark$

        gravitational strength on the surface of the Earth is bigger than the Moon surface $\checkmark$

        gravitational field directions are opposite on both sides of the zero point $\checkmark$

        ![PH0893_MS](images/PH0893_MS.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The gravitational fields of the Earth and the Moon point in opposite directions. Therefore, they should cancel each other at a point between Earth and the Moon. Since Earth's mass is bigger, this point should be closer to the moon. Additionally, bigger mass also results in bigger gravitational field strength on the surface of the Earth. However, the effect of the Earth's larger radius is negligible compared to the effect of its larger mass.

    3. Any indication of equating gravitational field strength of Earth and Moon $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{G(81)M_M}{x^2} = \dfrac{GM_M}{\left (3.8 \times 10^8 - x\right)^2 }$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ :answer[$3.4 \times 10^8\ \ce{m}$]

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The gravitational field strength of the Earth $g_E$ at that point:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $g_E = \dfrac{GM_E}{x^2}$

        While $x$ is the distance between The Earth and the point and $M_E$ is the mass of the Earth.

        The gravitational field strength of the Moon $g_M$ at that point:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $g_M = \dfrac{GM_M}{\left (3.8 \times 10^8 - x\right)^2 }$

        $M_M$ is the mass of the Moon.

        The magnitude of the gravitational field strengths must be equal to have zero gravitational field strength.

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{GM_E}{x^2} = \dfrac{GM_M}{\left (3.8 \times 10^8 - x\right)^2 }$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $M_E = 81M_M$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{G(81)M_M}{x^2} = \dfrac{GM_M}{\left (3.8 \times 10^8 - x\right)^2 }$

        After simplification:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3.8 \times^8 - x}{x} = \dfrac{1}{9}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $10x = (9)(3.8 \times 10^8)$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $x= 3.4 \times 10^8\ \ce{m}$

2. $E_p = -\dfrac{GM_M}{r}$\
As $r$ increases gravitational potential energy increases $\checkmark$

    $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$ due to increase in $r$, $v$ decreases\
so smaller kinetic energy $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The gravitational potential energy $E_p$ of the moon:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $E_p = -\dfrac{GM_M}{r}$

    The distance $r$ between the Moon and Earth increases, the gravitational potential energy of the moon $E_p$ increases as well.

    By using the formula of orbital speed:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$

    It can be concluded that due to increase in radius of the moon, speed of the moon must be decreased.  Hence, the kinetic energy decreases.
