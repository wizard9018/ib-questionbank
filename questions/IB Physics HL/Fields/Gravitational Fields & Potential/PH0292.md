# PH0292

Satellites $A$ and $B$ orbit the same planet of mass $M$ and radius $R$. Satellite $A$ has a mass of $3m$ and it is located at a height of $R$ from the surface of the planet. Satellite $B$ has a mass of $m$ and it is located at a height of 2$R$ from the surface of the planet.

What is the ratio of the centripetal force acting on $A$ to the centripetal force acting on $B$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{27}{4}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4}{27}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 6

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 12

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The centripetal force on a satellite is given by universal law of gravitation formula $ F = \dfrac{GMm}{r^2}$

The orbital radius for each is the sum of the radius of the planet and height from the surface, thus $2R$ and $3R$. We can substitute these radii as well as the satellite masses to find the ratio:

$\begin{aligned} \\
\frac{Force\ acting\ on\ A}{Force\ acting\ on\ B}
& =\frac{\frac{GM(3m)}{{(2R)}^2}}{\frac{GMm}{{(3R)}^2}} \\
\hspace{1em} \\
 &=\frac{\frac{3}{4}}{\frac{1}{9}} \\
 \hspace{1em} \\
  \end{aligned}$

$\hspace{8em}$ = :answer[$\dfrac{27}{4}$]
