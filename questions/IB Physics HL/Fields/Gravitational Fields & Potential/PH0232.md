# PH0232

The gravitational field strength on the surface of the Moon is  $\dfrac{g_E}{6}$. Two objects of different masses $m$ and $2m$ are released with zero speed on the Moon from heights of $h$ and $2h$, respectively, where $h$ is small compared to the radius of the Moon.

What are the magnitudes of the accelerations of these objects right after release?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1em}$ **Mass m** $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$**Mass 2m** $\hspace{1em}$|
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{g_E}{6}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{12}$ |
|$\hspace{1em}$B.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{6}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{6}$ |
| $\hspace{1em}$C.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{12}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{6}$ |
| $\hspace{1em}$D.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{12}$ | $\dfrac{g_E}{12}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
The only force acting on the masses is their weight. According to Newton\'s 2nd law ($F = ma$);

$W = mg$  and $W = m \left(\dfrac{g_E}{6}\right)$

- $m \left(\dfrac{g_E}{6}\right) = ma $

- $a = \dfrac{g_E}{6}$

The acceleration of the masses is not dependent on the magnitude of mass and height. It is equal to the acceleration of freefall on the moon.

Therefore the answer is B.
