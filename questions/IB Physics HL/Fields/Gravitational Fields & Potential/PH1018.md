# PH1018

**Maximum mark: 11**

The diagram below shows part of planet $X$ and two circular orbits around it.

![PH1018](images/PH1018.png)

The radius of the planet is $R_X$ and its mass is $M_X$. Two points in the diagram are labelled $A$ and $B$. Point $A$ is on the orbit that is closest to planet $X$ at an altitude $R_X$ above the surface of the planet and point $B$ is on an orbit at an altitude $2\,R_X$ above the surface.

1. State whether the gravitational potential due to planet
$X$ is higher at $A$ or at $B$.        [1 mark]

A spaceship with a mass $m_s$ is placed in a circular orbit around planet $X$ along a path that passes through point $A$.

2. Show that the kinetic energy $E_k$ of the spaceship is given by $E_k=\dfrac{GM_Xm_s}{4R_X}$     [2 marks]

3.     Hence, for the spaceship, determine the ratio:

    - $\dfrac{\textrm{Gravitational\ potential\ energy}}{\textrm{Kinetic\ energy}}$
[2 marks]

The spaceship is to be taken into a higher orbit which passes through point $B$.

The following data is available:

- $M_X=3.50\times{10}^{22}\ \ce{kg}$

- $m_s=2500\ \ce{kg}$

- $R_X=4.70\ \times{10}^6\ \ce{m}$

4. Determine the **change** in:

    1. Gravitational potential energy    [2 marks]

    2. Kinetic energy                    [2 marks]

5. Determine the work required to take the spaceship from an orbit that passes through point $A$ to a higher orbit that passes through $B$.        [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Higher at $B$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Point $B$ is located at a further distance from planet $X$.

    The gravitational potential is zero at an infinite distance and decreases as the distance to the planet is reduced.

    The gravitational potential is then smaller at $A$ (and higher at $B$).

2. $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s\ v_{orbital}^2$ \
    $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{GM_X}{r}}\right)^2$ $\checkmark$
    $r=2R_X$ $\checkmark$

    Resulting in

    - $E_k=\dfrac{GM_X\ m_s}{4R_X}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Given that the spaceship is in circular orbit around planet $X$ its speed is given by

    - $v_{orb}=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$

    where $M$ corresponds to the mass of the planet and $r$ is the orbital radius.

    The mass of the planet is $M_X$ and the orbital radius is $2R_X$ since the spaceship is located at a height $R_X$ above the surface of the planet.

    The kinetic energy of the spaceship is given by

    - $E_K=\dfrac{1}{2}m_sv^2$.

    Combining these expressions leads to:

    - $E_K=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s{ \left(\sqrt{\dfrac{GM_X}{2R_X}}\right)}^2=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s\dfrac{G M_X}{2R_X}$

    - $E_K=\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_S}{4R_X}$

3. $\dfrac{\textrm{Gravitational\ potential\ energy}}{\textrm{Kinetic\ energy}}=\dfrac{-\frac{G M_X{ m}_s}{2R_X}}{\frac{G M_X m_s}{4R_X}}$ $\checkmark$ \
    $= -2$ $\checkmark$    (Accept an answer missing the negative sign)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The gravitational potential energy of the spaceship is given by:

    - $E_p=-G\dfrac{m_1\ m_2}{r}$

    Where $m_1$ and $m_2$ are the masses of the planet and the spaceship, and $r$ is the distance between the centres of masses which is $2R_X$.

    The gravitational potential energy is then:

    - $E_p=-G\dfrac{M_X\ m_s}{2R_X}$

    Given that the kinetic energy is given by

    - $E_K=\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_S}{4\ R_X}$

    the requested ratio becomes then:

    - $\dfrac{-G\frac{M_X m_s}{2R_X}}{\frac{G M_X m_S}{4 R_X}}$

    which simplifies to $-2$

4. 1.     ${\Delta}E_p=E_{p (B)}-E_{p (A)}=\left(-\dfrac{G M_X m_s}{3R_X}\right)-\left(-\dfrac{G M_X m_s}{2R_X}\right)$ \
        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_p=\dfrac{G M_X m_s}{6R_X}$ $\checkmark$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_p=\dfrac{(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\ \ce{N}\ \ce{m^2}\ \ce{kg}^{-2})(3.50\times{10}^{22}\ \ce{kg}\ )(2500\ \ce{kg})}{6\ (\ 4.70\ \times{10}^6\ \ce{m})\ }$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_p=2.07\times{10}^8\ \ce{J}=$ :answer[$207\ \ce{MJ}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:
        \
        The change in potential energy is to be calculated as the difference between the gravitational potential energies at the orbit that passes through $B$ and the one that passes through $A$.

        The orbital radius at $B$ is $3R_X$ and at $A$ is $2R_X$.
        These energies can be expressed as:

        - $E_{p\left(B\right)}=-\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{3R_X}$   and  $E_{p\left(A\right)}=-\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{2R_X}$

        Using a common denominator, the difference between these is

        - $\mathrm{\Delta}E_p=\left(-\dfrac{2G\ M_X\ m_s}{6R_X}\right)-\left(-\dfrac{3G\ M_X\ m_s}{6R_X}\right)=\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{6R_X}$

        Which corresponds to $\mathrm{\Delta}E_p=2.07\times{10}^8\ \ce{J}=207\ \ce{MJ}$.

    2.     $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=E_{K(B)}-E_{K(A)}=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s v_B^2-\dfrac{1}{2}m_sv_A^2$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s \left(\sqrt{\dfrac{GM_X}{3R_X}}\right)^2-\dfrac{1}{2}m_s\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{GM_X}{2R_X}}\right)^2$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=-\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{12\ R_X}$ $\checkmark$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=-\dfrac{(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\ \ce{N}\ \ce{m^2}\ \ce{kg^{-2}})(3.50\times{10}^{22}\ \ce{kg}\ )(2500\ \ce{kg})}{12(\ 4.70\ \times{10}^6\ \ce{m})\ }$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=-1.03\times{10}^8\ \ce{J}=-103\, \ce{MJ}$ $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        $\Delta E_K = -\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta E_P$ $\checkmark$

        $E_K=\ -\frac{1}{2}(2.07\times{10}^8\ )$

        $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=-1.03\times{10}^8\ \ce{J}=$ :answer[$-103 \, \ce{MJ}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:
        $E_{K(B)}=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s\ v_B^2$ and $E_{K(A)}=\dfrac{1}{2}m_s\ v_A^2$

        Where $v_B=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM_X}{3R_X}}$  and $v_A=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM_X}{2R_X}}$ \
        Substituting the expression for the speed at $B$ and at $A$ leads to:

        - $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[\left(\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{3R_X}\right)-\left(\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{2R_X}\right)\right]=-\dfrac{1}{2}\left[\dfrac{G{\ M}_X\ m_s}{6R_X}\right]=-\dfrac{G\ M_X\ m_s}{12\ R_X}$
        - $=-\dfrac{(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\ \ce{N}\ \ce{m^2}\ \ce{kg^{-2}})(3.50\times{10}^{22}\ \ce{kg}\ )(2500\ \ce{kg})}{12(4.70 \times{10}^6\ \ce{m})\ }$

        Which corresponds to $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=-1.03\times{10}^8\ \ce{J}=-103\ \ce{MJ}$.

        Alternatively

        - $E_K=\ -\dfrac{1}{2}E_P$

        Therefore

        - $\Delta E_K= -\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta E_P$

        - $E_K=\ -\dfrac{1}{2}(2.07\times{10}^8\ )$

        - $\mathrm{\Delta}E_K=-1.03\times{10}^8\ \ce{J}=-103\, \ce{MJ}$

5. 207 $\ce{MJ}\,-\,103\, \ce{MJ}$ $\checkmark$

    :answer[104 $\ce{MJ}$]  $\checkmark$ \
    (accept 103 MJ if exact values are used instead of previously calculated values)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The work required to take the spaceship into a higher orbit is given by the total increase in gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy.

    As calculated in d) gravitational potential energy increases from the orbit that passes through $A$ to the orbit that passes through $B$ by $207\ \ce{MJ}$ and the kinetic energy decreases (spaceship will be orbiting the planet at lower speed) by $103\ \ce{MJ}$.

    The work required is then given by $207\ \ce{MJ}-103\ \ce{MJ}=104\ \ce{MJ}$

    Note: if exact values were used (instead of the rounded answers to di) and dii) the calculation leads to $103\ \ce{MJ}$
