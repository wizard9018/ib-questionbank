# PH0681

Two planets **X** and **Y** have the same density however the radius of planet **X** is larger than that of **Y**. Two satellites with equal masses **A** and **B** orbit **X** and **Y** respectively with the same orbital radius.

Consider the following quantities

-    I. $\hspace{2em}$ Orbital period
-    II. $\hspace{1.7em}$ Centripetal force
-    III. $\hspace{1.4em}$ Centripetal acceleration

Which of the following quantities is smaller for satellite **A**?

-    A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

-    B. $\hspace{1em}$ II only
-    C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only
-    D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Planets **X** and **Y** have the same density, and **X** has a bigger radius than **Y**

Since

-  $Density=\dfrac{M}{V}=\dfrac{M}{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3}$

Then

-  $\hspace{1em}$ $M_X>M_y$

The centripetal force is equal to the gravitational force

-  $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

The two satellites have the same mass $m$ and the same orbital radius $r$.

Then

-  $F_A>F_B$

And through

-  $a=\dfrac{F}{m}$,

-  $a_A>a_B$

Orbital speed is given by

- $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$

Therefore

- $v_A>v_B$

And since

- $v=\dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$

Then

- $T_A<T_B$

Therefore statement I is correct.

The gravitational force at a distance r is given by the formula

- $F_g=G\dfrac{m_1m_2}{r^2}$

With the radius of orbit $r$ and mass of satellite $m_1$ the same for both satellites, the gravitational force is proportional to the planet mass $m_2$. Therefore satellite A experiences more gravitational force. The gravitational force is equal to the centripetal force, therefore A also experiences more centripetal force and therefore statement II is incorrect.

The centripetal acceleration is proportional to centripetal force, therefore A also has a greater centripetal acceleration and so statement III is incorrect.
