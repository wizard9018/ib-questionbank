# PH0636

Satellite $A$ of mass $M$ orbits a planet with an orbital speed $v$ and orbital radius $R$. Another satellite $B$ with mass $3M$ orbits the same planet with the same orbital speed.

Knowing that the ratio $\dfrac{Centripetal\ force\ of\ satellite\ A}{Centripetal\ force\ of\ satellite\ B}=\dfrac{1}{3}$, what is the orbital radius of satellite $B$?

- A.  $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{1}{2}\ R$

- B.  $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{2}{3}\ R$

- C.  $\hspace{1em}  $ $R$
- D.  $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{3}{2}\ R$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For a given planet, every different radius of orbit has an associated orbital speed that is independent of the mass of the orbiting object.

The formula for orbital speed is

$\hspace{2em}$ $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$

Where $M$ is the mass of the planet.

If the two satellites both have the same orbital velocity, then they must also have the same radius of orbit.
