# PH1846-1

**Maximum mark: 6**

Europa is one of the moons orbiting Jupiter.

The following data are given:

- Mass of Europa = 4.3 $\times$ 10$^{24}$ kg
- Mass of Jupiter = 2.0 $\times$ 10$^{27}$ kg
- Orbital radius of Europa = 680 $\times$ 10$^3$ km

 1.   Calculate the centripetal force acting on Europa. [2 marks]

2. Point $P$ lies at the point between Jupiter and Europa where there is no gravitational field.

    ![Screenshot 2025-09-05 at 9.46.07 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-09-05 at 9.46.07 AM.png>)

    The graph shows the variation of gravitational potential $V_g$ with distance $r$ from the centre of Jupiter.

    ![ph1846-1qbi2](images/ph1846-1qbi2.png)

    1.    Use the graph to explain why the distance from point $P$ to the centre of Jupiter is $6.5\times10^8$ m. [1 mark]

    2. Calculate the minimum energy required to move a probe of mass 250 kg from the surface of Jupiter to the surface of Europa. [2 marks]

    3. Explain why the work done in moving the probe to Europa is positive. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $F=6.67\times10^{-11}\dfrac{(2.0\times10^{27})(4.3\times10^{24})}{(680\times10^6)^2}$ $\checkmark$

    - $F=$ :answer[$1.2\times10^{24}$ $\ll\textrm{N}\gg$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The centripetal force is provided by the gravitational force between Jupiter and Europa. This can be found from the formula

    - $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

    For the radius values, we must convert to metres, which gives

    - $r=680\times10^6$ m

    Solving for gravitational force gives

    - $F=(6.67\times10^{-11})\dfrac{(2.0\times10^{26})(4.3\times10^{24})}{(680\times10^6)^2}=1.2\times10^{24}$ N

2. 1. $g=-\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta r}$ **and** the gradient at $r=6.5\times10^8$ m is zero $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The formula $g=-\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta r}$ tells us that the gravitational field strength at a point is the rate of change of potential with distance at that point. Where the fields are balanced, the net gravitational field is zero. At $r=6.5\times10^8$ m, the gradient of the graph is zero, which means that the rate of change of gravitational potential with distance is zero and the net field must be zero.

    2. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $W=(250)(-0.2\times10^6-(-1.8\times10^6))=(250)(1.6 \times 10^6)$ $\checkmark$

        - $W=$ :answer[$4.0\times10^8$ $\ll\textrm{J}\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The work done is given by

        - $W=m\Delta V_g$

        Considering the potentials, the probe starts on the surface of Jupiter, where the potential is $-1.8$ MJ kg$^{-1}$. In order to reach Europa, it must be moved past point $P$, which is where the potential is at a maximum. Therefore we use the potential at point $P$ ($-0.2$ MJ kg$^{-1}$) as the final potential in this situation, as this is the point at which energy will no longer need to be input

        - $W=(250)(-0.2\times10^6-(-1.8\times10^6))==(250)(1.6 \times 10^6)=4.0\times10^8$ J

    3. Probe moves from lower potential to higher potential

        **Or**

        More work is done against the gravitational field than is done by the field $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The probe is moving from a lower gravitational potential (more negative) at Jupiter's surface to that of higher gravitational potential (less negative) at Europa's surface.

        Energy is needed to transfer the probe, then the work done is positive.
