# PH0682

**Maximum mark: 13**

1.
    1. The Moon orbits the Earth in a circular orbit. Explain why a centripetal force is needed for the Moon to be in its orbit around the Earth. [2 marks]

    2. Identify the origin of this force. [1 mark]

2.
    1. Show that the orbital period $T$ of the Moon around the Earth is given by

        $ \hspace{1em}$ $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}$

        where $M$ is the mass of the Earth and $r$ is the orbital radius.                 [2 marks]

    2. Knowing that\
Mass of the Earth = $5.98 \times 10^{24} \ \ce{kg}$\
The orbital radius = $3.84 \times 10^8 \ \ce{m}$

        Calculate the orbital speed of the Moon around the Earth.                    [3 marks]

3.
    1. A total solar eclipse is seen when the Sun, the Moon and the Earth lie on the same line with the Moon in between the Sun and the Earth.

        Knowing that\
Mass of the Sun = $1.99 \times 10^{30} \ \ce{kg}$\
Distance between Sun and Moon during eclipse = $1.49 \times 10^{11} \ \ce{m}$\
        Mass of the Moon = $7.36 \times 10^{22} \ \ce{kg}$

        Determine whether the Sun or the Earth exerts a greater gravitational force on the Moon. [3 marks]

    2. Explain why the Sun doesn\'t pull the Moon away from the Earth during a total solar eclipse. [2 marks]

2.
    1. $\ll\ a=G\dfrac{M}{r^2}\gg$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}=G\dfrac{M}{r^2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $T^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2r^3}{GM}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\ll\ T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}\gg$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The centripetal force is the gravitational force

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F_c=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

        From Newton\'s second law

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F=ma$

        Then

        $\hspace{1em}$ $ma=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $a=G\dfrac{M}{r^2}$

        Since

        $\hspace{1em}$ $a=\dfrac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}$

        Then

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}=G\dfrac{M}{r^2}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $T^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2r^3}{GM}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}$

    2. $\ll\ T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\left(3.84\times{10}^8\right)^3}{\left(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\right)\left(5.98\times{10}^{24}\right)}}\gg$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $T = $ $2.37\times{10}^6\ \ll\ \ce{s}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{2\pi\left(3.84\times{10}^8\right)}{2.37\times{10}^6}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=1018.04\ =$ :answer[$1.02\ \times \ {10}^3\ll\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}\gg$]      $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Using the formula in part (b) (i)

        $\hspace{1em}$ $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r^3}{GM}}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{{(3.84\times{10}^8)}^3}{(6.67\times{10}^{-11})(5.98\times{10}^{24})}}=2.37\times{10}^6\ \ce{s}$

        The orbital speed is given by

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{2\pi(3.84\times{10}^8)}{2.37\times{10}^6}=1018\ \ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

3.
    1. Force between the Sun and the Moon:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\ll\ F_{SM}=\left(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\right)\dfrac{\left(1.99\times{10}^{30}\right)\left(7.36\times{10}^{22}\right)}{\left(1.49\times{10}^{11}\right)^2}\gg$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F_{SM}=$ $4.40\times{10}^{20}\ll\ \ce{N}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ ${\ll F}_{EM}=\left(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\right)\dfrac{\left(5.98\times{10}^{24}\right)\left(7.36\times{10}^{22}\right)}{\left(3.84\times{10}^8\right)^2}\gg$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F_{EM}=$ $1.99\times{10}^{20}\ll\ \ce{N}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        Sun\'s force is greater. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The gravitational force is given by

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F=G\dfrac{Mm}{r^2}$

        The gravitational force between the Sun and the Moon

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F_{SM}=\left(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\right)\dfrac{\left(1.99\times{10}^{30}\right)\left(7.36\times{10}^{22}\right)}{\left(1.49\times{10}^{11}\right)^2}=4.40\times{10}^{20}\ \ce{N}$

        The gravitational force between the Earth and the Moon

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F_{EM}=\left(6.67\times{10}^{-11}\right)\dfrac{\left(5.98\times{10}^{24}\right)\left(7.36\times{10}^{22}\right)}{\left(3.84\times{10}^8\right)^2}=1.99\times{10}^{20}\ \ce{N}$

        This means that the gravitational force between the Sun and the Moon is bigger than that between the Earth and the Moon.

    2. *Any two of:*

        - - Both Earth and the Moon orbit the Sun together $\checkmark$
        - - The Moon remains in gravitational orbit around the Earth as both travel around the Sun  $\checkmark$

        - - The Moon and the Earth experience the same centripetal acceleration while orbiting the Sun together

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The Moon experiences a gravitational force from the Earth that is responsible for its orbit around the Earth. The Moon-Earth orbital system is also influenced by the force of gravity from the Sun. The force of gravity from the Sun is acting on both the Earth and the Moon and due to their distances from the Sun being effectively the same, they experience the same centripetal acceleration and orbit the Sun together in a near-circular orbit. Thus the Moon and Earth stay together as they travel around the Sun.
