1. Define gravitational potential. [2 marks]

2. The graph below shows gravitational field strength against distance from the surface of the planet Venus. Distance is measured in units of $R$, where $R$ is the radius of Venus.

3. 1. Almost 100% of Venus' atmosphere is composed of carbon dioxide.     2. The average temperature on the surface of Venus is around $470\,\degree \ce{C}$. Assuming that Venus' atmosphere behaves like an ideal gas, estimate Venus' surface atmospheric pressure. [2 marks]

2. Statement or evidence that area under curve between 2 and 5 has been considered. $\checkmark$

    Area under graph 1.5 large squares (allow values in range 1.4-1.6) \
    And \
    Area of one large square is $1R \,\ce{N}\ \ce{kg^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

    - $1.5R=A=9\times 10^6 $

    - $R=6\times10^6\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

    (accept answers between 5.6 and 6.4 $\times 10^6$)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Gravitational potential is given by the magnitude of the area under graph.

    Potential difference between distances of $2R$ and $5R$ is equal to the shaded area shown below.

    ![image](images/image.png)

    This area is approximately 1.5 large squares.

    - Each large square has an area of $(1\ce{N}\ \ce{kg^{-1}})(1R)=1R\,\ce{N}\ \ce{kg^{-1}}$

    - Area = gravitational potential difference

    - $1.5\times 1R\,\ce{N}\ \ce{kg^{-1}}=9\times 10^6\,\ce{J},\ce{kg^{-1}}. $

    - $R\, \ce{N}\ \ce{kg^{-1}}=6\times 10^6\,\ce{J}\,\ce{kg^{-1}}$

    - $R=6\times 10^6\,\ce{J}\,\ce{N^{-1}}= 6\times 10^6\, \ce{m} $

3. 1. $\dfrac{65}{44\times 10^{-3}}=1480\,\text{mol}\,\ce{m^{-3}}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        First, we must convert mass per unit mol into SI units.

        - $44\,\ce{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}=44(10^{-3}\,\ce{kg})\text{mol}^{-1}=0.044\,\ce{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

        - $\dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}\div \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{mol}}=\dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}\times \dfrac{\text{mol}}{\text{mass}}=\dfrac{\text{mol}}{\text{volume}}$

        (We can also do a similar analysis with units to find that $\dfrac{\ce{kg}\,\ce{m^{-3}}}{\ce{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$  gives us the correct units)

        - $\dfrac{65\,\ce{kg}\,\ce{m^{-3}}}{0.044\,\ce{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}=1477\, \text{mol} \,\ce{m^{-3}} $

    2. ::indent
        - $PV=nRT \hspace{1em}$ and $\hspace{1em}470\degree \ce{C} = 743\,K$ $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{n}{V}=1480$

        - $P=\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{V}\Bigr)(R)(T)=(1480)(8.31)(743)=9.1\times 10^6\,\ce{Pa}$ $\checkmark$

        (Award full marks for use of $\dfrac{n}{V}=1500$ giving $9.3\times 10^6\,\ce{Pa}$)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $PV=nRT$

        Rearranging for $P$ gives

        - $P=\dfrac{nRT}{V}=\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{V}\Bigr)(R)(T)$

        - $\dfrac{n}{V}$ is moles per unit volume $= 1480\,\text{mol }\,\ce{m^{-3}}$ (calculated in part (c)(i))

        - $R$ is the molar gas constant $= 8.31\, \ce{J} \,\ce{K^{-1}}$ (given in your formula book)

        - $T$ is absolute temperature $470\degree \ce{C} =470\,\ce{K}+273\,\ce{K} =743\,\ce{K}$

        - $P=\Bigl(\dfrac{n}{V}\Bigr)(R)(T)=(1480)(8.31)(743)=9.1\times 10^6\,\ce{Pa}$

    3. Any two from:

        Low pressure.   $\checkmark$ \
        Low density.   $\checkmark$ \
        Moderate temperature. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        A real gas approximates to an ideal gas at conditions of low pressure, moderate temperature, and low density.

        The data we are given allows us to accurately predict the pressure on Venus by assuming it is an ideal gas.
