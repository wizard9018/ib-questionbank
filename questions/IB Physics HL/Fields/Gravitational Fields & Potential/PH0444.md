# PH0444

A satellite of mass $m$ is in an orbit at a height $h$ from the surface of a planet of mass $M$. The centripetal force acting on the satellite is $F$ and the linear speed is $v$.

What is the new centripetal force and linear orbital speed when it is moved to a new orbit at $2h$ from the surface of the planet?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1em}$ Centripetal force $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ Linear speed $\hspace{1em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{F}{4}$ | Less than $v$ |
|$\hspace{1em}$B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Between $F$ and $\dfrac{F}{4}$ | Less than $v$ |
| $\hspace{1em}$C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Between $F$ and $\dfrac{F}{4}$ | More than $v$ |
| $\hspace{1em}$D.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\dfrac{F}{4}$ | More than $v$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Centripetal force is given by $F=\dfrac{GMm}{r^2}$ where $r$ is the distance from the center of the planet.

In the question, height from the surface is doubled, therefore total distance $r$ is increased to a distance less than $2r$.

This means the centripetal force is varied by a factor of larger than $\dfrac{1}{4}$, giving the final force a value between $F$ and $\dfrac{F}{4}$.

Linear speed $v$  also decreases as shown in the equation $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{Gm}{r}}$
