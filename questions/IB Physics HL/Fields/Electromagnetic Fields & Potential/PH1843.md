# PH1843

**Maximum mark: 13**

1. Two parallel plates are separated by a distance $d= 3.00$ mm. A potential difference of 200 V is set between the plates as shown below. The equipotential line AB is equidistant to each plate.

    ![PH1843a11](images/PH1843a11.png)

    1. Describe what is meant by an equipotential line. [1 mark]

    2. State the electric potential along the line AB [1 mark]

2. An electron enters the region between the plates moving along the line AB.
As it enters, the electron experiences an electric force.

    ![Screenshot 2023-10-04 at 4.39.32 PM](<images/Screenshot 2023-10-04 at 4.39.32 PM.png>)

    1. State the direction of the force acting on the electron. [1 mark]

    2. Calculate the magnitude of the acceleration that the electron would experience as it enters the region. [2 marks]

3.  The electron is initially moving with a speed of $2.5 \times 10^6 \, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$.
A uniform magnetic field B is also present between the plates which causes the electron to move along the line AB without deflection.

    1.     Determine the kinetic energy of the electron in eV.    [2 marks]

    2. State the direction of the magnetic field. [1 mark]

    3. Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic field. [1 mark]

4. The experiment is repeated but the plates are removed.

    The magnetic field is still present, so the electron experiences the action of the magnetic force **only**.

    Outline the energy transformations, if any, that the electron will experience as it is accelerated by the magnetic force. [3 marks]

1.     1. Line/curve along which there is a constant electric potential.

        or

        Line/curve along which the work required to move a charge is zero

        or

        Line/curve along which $ΔV_e=0$ V between any two points.   $\checkmark$

    2.  $100 \,V$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The field is uniform between the plates, and therefore electric potential varies linearly between the plates decreasing from the upper plate, which is at $+200$ V, towards the lower plate at  0 V.

        It is given that the line AB is equidistant from the plates, its potential should be the mean value between the potentials at the plates.

2.  1. Upwards $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The electric field points from the upper plate, which is at a higher potential, towards the lower plate.
The negatively charged electron experiences a force opposite to the electric field.

    Or

    The negatively charged electron experiences an electric force which acts in the direction in which the electric potential increases.

    2.     From $E=\dfrac{F}{q}$ and $E=\dfrac{V}{d}$

        $\dfrac{F}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,  \ce{C}}=\dfrac{200\, \ce{V}}{3.00 \times 10^{-3}\,  \ce{m}}$

        $F=1.07 \times 10^{-14} \, \ce{N} $ $\checkmark$

        $a=≪\dfrac{F}{m}=\dfrac{1.07 \times 10^{-14}\,  \ce{N}}{9.110\times 10^{-31}  \ce{kg}}≫$ = :answer[$1.17 \times10^{16}$]$   \ ≪\ce{m s^{-2}} $ or $\ce{N}\, \ce{kg^{-1}}≫$ $\checkmark$

        Accept negative sign in the acceleration if -e was used for the charge.
        Award [2] for a bald correct answer

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The electric field between the plates is given by:

        - $E=\dfrac{V}{d}=\dfrac{200\, \ce{V}}{3.00 \times 10^{-3}\,  \ce{m}}=66700\, \ce{N\,C^{-1}}$

        The magnitude of the electric force experienced by the electron is then:

        - $F=qE=(1.6 \times 10^{-19}  \ce{C})(66700\, \ce{N\, C^{-1}})=1.07 \times 10^{-14}  \ce{N}$

        The elementary charge is used for the charge of the electron. There is no need to use a negative sign for the charge given that it is the magnitude of its acceleration that it is being calculated.

        Newton's second law can be used to find the magnitude of its acceleration.

        - $a=\dfrac{F}{m}=\dfrac{1.07 \times 10^{-14}  \ce{N}}{9.110 \times 10^{-31} \ce{kg}}  =1.17 \times 10^{16}  \, \ce{m \,s^{-2}}$

3.  1.  $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2} mv^2=\dfrac{1}{2}(9.110 \times 10^{-31}\,  \ce{kg})(2.5 \times 10^6 \, \ce{m\, s^{-1}} )^2$

        $E_k=2.85 \times 10^{-18}  \ce{J}$ $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{2.85 \times 10^{-18}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,  \ce{C}}$ =:answer[$17.8\, \ce{eV}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The kinetic energy of the electron is given by:

        - $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2} mv^2$

        This expression can be used to calculate the kinetic energy of the electron substituting its mass $(9.110 \times 10^{-31}  \ce{kg})$ and the speed which is given $(2.5 \times 10^6 \, \ce{m\, s^{-1}} )$, leading to:

        - $E_k=2.85 \times 10^{-18}$  J

        Each eV corresponds to $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ J. This energy can then be converted to eV by dividing its value by $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ leading to $E_k=17.8\, \ce{eV}$

    2.  Into the page/screen $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Given that the electron moves along the line AB it experiences no acceleration.

        There must then be a magnetic force acting downwards, balancing the electric force.

        According to the left-hand rule, a negative charge which is moving rightwards, will experience a magnetic force acting downwards when moving across a magnetic field that is acting into the page/screen.

    3.     $B=\ll\ \dfrac{F}{(qv\sin\ \theta}=\dfrac{(1.07\times10^{-14}\  \ce{N}) }{(1.60\times10^{-19}\ \ \ce{C})(  2.5\times10^6\  \ce{m\,s^{-1}})(  \sin\  90 \degree) }≫$ = :answer[$0.027 \, \ce{T}$] $\checkmark$

        Award [1] for a bald correct answer.
Accept ECF from (b)(ii)

         $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The magnetic force is given by

        - $F=qvB\sin \theta$.

        This expression can be used to calculate the magnitude of the magnetic field $B$.

        - $B=\dfrac{F}{qv\sin\ \theta}$

        The magnitude of the magnetic force should be equal as the previously calculated magnitude of the electric force since the total force must be zero. $\theta=90 \degree$ given that the electron moves perpendicular to the magnetic field.

4.  The magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity of the electron. $\checkmark$

    The work done by a force that is perpendicular to the particle velocity/displacement is zero. $\checkmark$

    The (kinetic) energy of the electron remains constant/does not change $\checkmark$

     $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
     As the electron enters the region where a uniform magnetic field is present, it is acted upon a magnetic force that acts along a direction that is, at all times, perpendicular to the electron's velocity.

    The work done by this force is given by $W=Fs\cos \theta$  and in this case $\theta=90\degree$ which leads to $W=0$ since $\cos\ 90\degree =0$. No work is then done on the electron which conserves its energy.

    The electron will then move in uniform circular motion. The kinetic energy will then remain constant since the velocity will not be changing in magnitude, only in direction.
