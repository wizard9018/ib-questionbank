# PH1273

**Maximum mark: 9**

1. Three small charged particles are placed at the corners of an equilateral triangle with a side length of $L$. The charges are as follows: $+4.0\,\mu$ C, $+2.0\,\mu$ C and $-3.0\,\mu$ C.

    ![PH1273q1](images/PH1273q1.png)

    The electric potential energy stored in the system of charges is $-0.18$ J.

    1. Explain what is meant by the electric potential energy of the system. [2 marks]

    2. Determine $L$. [3 marks]

2. The graph below shows the variation in the potential difference $V_e$ of another system with distance $d$.

    ![PH1273q2](images/PH1273q2.png)

    1. Using the graph, calculate the strength of the electric field at a distance of 1.0 m.  [2 marks]

    2. Determine the work done in moving a charge of $2.5\,\mu$ C from $d=0.10$ m to $d=0.50$ m.  [2 marks]

1. 1. Work done to assemble the system $\checkmark$

        From infinite separation $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When the charges are brought together from an infinite separation, work is done on the charges. The potential energy of the system is the total work done in bringing the charges together.

    2. Usage of $E_p=k\left(\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r_{12}}\right)+k\left(\dfrac{q_2q_3}{r_{23}}\right)+k\left(\dfrac{q_1q_3}{r_{13}}\right)$ $\checkmark$

        $L=\dfrac{8.99\times10^9}{-0.18}\left((2.0\times10^{-6})(-3.0\times10^{-6})+(-3.0\times10^{-6})(4.0\times10^{-6})+(2.0\times10^{-6})(4.0\times10^{-6})\right)$ $\checkmark$

        $L=$:answer[$0.50\, \ce{m}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The electric potential $E_p$ of the system can be calculated by the following formula:

        - $E_p=k\left(\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r_{12}}\right)+k\left(\dfrac{q_2q_3}{r_{23}}\right)+k\left(\dfrac{q_1q_3}{r_{13}}\right)$

        Since $r_{12}=r_{23}=r_{13}=L$

        It can be rearranged as:

        - $L=\dfrac{k}{E_p}(q_1q_2+q_2q_3+q_1q_3)$

        By substituting the given values:

        - $L=\dfrac{8.99\times10^9}{-0.18}\left((2.0\times10^{-6})(-3.0\times10^{-6})+(-3.0\times10^{-6})(4.0\times10^{-6})+(2.0\times10^{-6})(4.0\times10^{-6})\right)$

        - $L=0.50$ m

2. 1. Use of gradient of the graph to determine electric field strength $\checkmark$

        - $E=$:answer[$2.6\times10^2\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$] $\checkmark$

        Allow answers in the range of $2.4\times10^2\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$ and $2.8\times10^2\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        To calculate the electric field $E$ at a specific distance, we use the relationship between the electric field and potential difference:

        - $E=-\dfrac{\Delta V_e}{\Delta r}=-$*gradient of the graph*

        The gradient of the graph at $d=1.0$ m:

        ![PH1273a1](images/PH1273a1.png)

        - *gradient of the graph*$=\dfrac{-0.5\times10^3}{1.9}=-2.6\times10^2$

        The electric field strength $E$:

        - $E=-(-2.6\times10^2)=2.6\times10^2\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$

        The accepted range is between $2.4\times10^2\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$ and $2.8\times10^2\,\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$.

    2. $W=(2.5\times10^{-6})(0.5\times10^3-2.5\times10^{3})$ $\checkmark$

        $W=$:answer[$-5.0\times10^{-3}\, \ce{J}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Work done W in moving a charge $q$:

        - $\begin{aligned}
        \ W&=q\Delta V_e\\
        \ W&=(2.5\times10^{-6})(0.5\times10^3-2.5\times10^3)\\
        \ W&=-5.0\times10^{-3}\, \ce{J}\\
        \end{aligned}$
