# PH0387

Four point charges are placed as shown on the corners of a square. Charges of $+2Q$ and $-Q$, and unknown charges $A$ and $B$ are located as shown on the diagram.

![PH0387b](images/PH0387b.png)

At the center of the square, the total potential is zero, but the resultant electric field is not zero.

Which of the following is a possible combination for the charges $A$ and $B$?

$\\$

| | A | B |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$+2Q $\hspace{1.5em}$|$\hspace{1.5em}$ -3Q$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | +2Q | -Q |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | -Q | +2Q |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | +2Q | +Q |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Net electric potential is given by the sum of the potentials at that point due to the individual point charges. Potential is a scalar quantity and so the direction does not need to be considered.

Electric potential is given by the formula

- $V = \dfrac{kQ}{r}$

Because the center of the square is equidistant from the point charges, the sum of the potentials can be simplified to

- $V_{total} = \dfrac{k}{r}(-Q+2Q+Q_A+Q_B)$

Since the question requires the total electric potential to be zero, in the correct answer the sum of the four charges must equal zero.

In A: -Q + 2Q + 2Q+ -3Q = 0, thus electric potential is zero.

However, the resultant electric field is non-zero with this combination.

This is because the electric field is a vector and potential is a scalar quantity. As a result, the net electric field for option A is down and to the right.

B gives a resultant field zero, but the total electric potential is non-zero

C gives neither value as zero.

D gives neither value as zero.
