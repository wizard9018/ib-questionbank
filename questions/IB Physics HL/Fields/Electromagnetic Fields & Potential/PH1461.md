# PH1461

On the graphs below the solid line shows how electric field strength $E$ varies with distance $d$ from the center to a point charge embedded inside a material of permittivity $\varepsilon$.

A dotted line is also plotted on each of the graphs showing how electric field strength varies with distance for the same charge in a different material. For which of the graphs does the dotted line represent a charge embedded in a material with a permittivity $2\varepsilon$?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Electric field strength at a point is given by

- $E=\dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon r^2}$

Where $Q$ is the charge, $\varepsilon$ is permittivity, and $r$ is the distance between the point and the charge.

For constant $r$ we have that $E\propto\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$. So if $\varepsilon$ is doubled we expect $E$ to be at half the original value for every distance $d$.

The dotted line is at half the height of the solid line for every $d$ in the graph below:
