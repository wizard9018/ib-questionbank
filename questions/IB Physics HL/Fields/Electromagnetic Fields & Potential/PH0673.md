# PH0673

Two point charges are separated by a distance $r$ as shown. The negative charge has a mass $m_1$ and the mass of the positive charge is $m_2$.

![PH0673](images/PH0673.png)

The negative charge's acceleration is 3 times that of the positive charge.

What is the ratio $\dfrac{{\textrm{Gravitational\ force}}}{{\textrm{Electrostatic\ force}}}$ between the two point charges?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{G(m_1)^2}{30ke^2}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{Gm_em_p}{ke^2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{G{(m_2)}^2}{10ke^2}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{G{(m_1)}^2}{10ke^2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
By Newton's 3rd Law, the charges have equal and opposite forces on each other.

Then\
$\hspace{1em}$ $m_1a_1=m_2a_2$

Since\
$\hspace{1em}$ $a_1=3a_2$

Then\
$\hspace{1em}$ $m_2=3m_1$

The gravitational force between the two charges is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $F_g=G\dfrac{m_1m_2}{r^2}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $F_g=G\dfrac{m_1(3)m_1}{r^2}=G\dfrac{{{3m}_1}^2}{r^2}$

The electrostatic force between the two charges is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $F_e=k\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r^2}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $F_e=k\dfrac{\left(3\right)e\left(10\right)e}{r^2}=k\dfrac{30e^2}{r^2}$

The ratio
$\dfrac{Gravitational\ force}{Electrostatic\ force}=\dfrac{F_g}{F_e}
=\dfrac{G\dfrac{{{3m}_1}^2}{r^2}}{k\dfrac{30e^2}{r^2}}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{Gravitational\ force}{Electrostatic\ force}=\dfrac{G{m_1}^2}{10ke^2}$
