# PH0844

The variation of electric potential $V$ with distance $d$ from the centres was measured for each of isolated charged spheres $K$ and $L$. The resulting graphs are shown together on the axes below.

![PH1271q](images/PH1271q.png)

Which of the following statements is correct about the electric field at point $a$ and the sphere with the greatest charge?

$\\$

| | Strongest electric field at *a* | Greatest charge |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| L | K |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | L | L |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | K | K |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | K | L |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Potential outside of the sphere is given by the equation

- $V_e = k\dfrac{Q}{r}$

Where $r$ is the distance from the center of the sphere.

Since the potential of K is higher than the potential of L at the same distance, the charge of K is greater.

Electric field strength outside of the sphere is given by

- $E = k\dfrac{q}{r^2}$

where $r$ is the distance from the centre. Because $r$ is the same for both spheres and the charge of K is greater, the electric field of K must also be stronger.

Note that the electric field is also represented by the gradient of the potential graph. The gradient of the graph of the potential of K is (slightly) steeper at $a$ than that of L, indicating a stronger electric field.
