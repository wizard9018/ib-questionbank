# PH0386

The graph shows the variation of the total electric potential due to two point electric charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ located 4.0 m apart. The distance $r$ is defined to be zero at the location of $q_1$.

![PH0386](images/PH0386.png)

What is the ratio of   $\,\dfrac{q_2}{q_1}$ ?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 3

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 4

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 9

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 16

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

$q_1$ is located at the origin and each square represents 0.5 m, therefore $q_2$ is located 8 squares away on the horizontal axis.

The gradient of this graph gives electric field strength, therefore at r = 1 m$, the resultant electric field is zero as its gradient is zero. This means the electric field strength due to the two charges are equal in magnitude and oppositely directed.

That location is 1 m away from $q_1$ and 3 m away from $q_2$.

Equating the electric field strength expressions to each other gives:

- $k\dfrac{q_1}{(1)^2}=k\dfrac{q_2}{(3)^2}$

- $\dfrac{q_1}{1}=\dfrac{q_2}{9}$

- $\dfrac{q_2}{q_1}=\dfrac{9}{1}=9$
