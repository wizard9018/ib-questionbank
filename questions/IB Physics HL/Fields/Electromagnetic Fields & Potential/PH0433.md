# PH0433

**Maximum mark: 12**

A conducting sphere has a radius of $20 \,$cm and a charge of $+3.2 \, \mu $C. Point $A$ is used to describe a location $20\,$cm from the surface of the sphere.

![PH0433-1](images/PH0433-1.png)

1. Calculate the electric potential at the surface of the sphere. State the correct SI unit for your answer. [2 marks]

2. 1. On the axes given, sketch the variation of the electric potential due to the sphere with distance $\underline{\textrm{from its surface}}$. Label point $A$ on the graph you have drawn. [2 marks]

   ![PH0433-2](images/PH0433-2.png)

    2. Without calculation, outline how the electric field strength at A may be determined using the graph you have drawn [2 marks]

    3. A proton is initially held stationary at the surface of the sphere. The proton is moved from the surface of the sphere to point A. Calculate the work done on the proton. [3 marks]

3. The proton is released from point A. Outline the subsequent motion of the proton.  [3 marks]

2.  1. .

        Smooth positive curve decreasing $\checkmark$

       $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
       For a charged spherical conductor, all charge can be considered to be at the center of the sphere. The potential is constant within the radius of the sphere, but from the surface it follows the relationship

       -  $V=\dfrac{kQ}{r}$

       As Q is positive, the graph should appear above the axis with an inversely proportional relationship.

       The radius of the sphere is $20\,$cm, thus at a distance of twice the radius the potential will have dropped to half of its value at the surface, which is the vertical axis intercept on this graph.

    2. Draw a line tangent to the curve at point A and find the gradient. $\checkmark$

        The electric field strength will be the negative of the gradient. $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        Use the graph to find the value at 20 cm. $\checkmark$

        Divide this value by 0.40 m. $\checkmark$

          $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The relationship between potential and electric field strength is given by the equation

        - $E = -\dfrac{\Delta V_e}{\Delta r}$

        Thus the gradient of the tangent at point A will give the electric field strength.

        The negative sign indicates that the electric field is in the opposite direction to the change of potential with distance. In this instance, potential is decreasing with distance from the point charge. This will give a negative value for gradient, and a positive value for electric field strength. The convention of a positive electric field value is in the direction away from the point charge.

        Alternatively, because of the nature of the formulae, where

        - $V_e = \dfrac{kq}{r} \hspace{3em}$ and $\hspace{3em} E = \dfrac{kq}{r^2}$

        We can see that dividing the value for the potential by $r$ will give the electric field strength.

    3. Potential at point $A = \dfrac{144}{2} = 72$ kV $\checkmark$

        $\begin{aligned}
                W&=q\Delta \ce{V}\\
                &=(1.6\times 10^{-19})(72-144) \times10^3\hspace{1em} \checkmark\\
                \end{aligned}$

        $\hspace{1.5em}$= :answer[$-1.2 \times{ 10}^{-14}\ \ce{J}$] $\hspace{1em} \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
                Point A is double the distance from the center of the sphere compared to the surface, so its potential will be half that at the surface.

        Potential at point $A = \dfrac{144}{2} = 72$ kV

        The work done is given by the charge multiplied by the change in potential (final potential minus initial potential).

        The change in potential is $(72 - 144)$ kV

        Change in energy is work done

        $\begin{aligned}
                W&=q\Delta \ce{V}\\
                &=(1.6\times 10^{-19})(72-144) \times10^3\\
                &= -1.2 \times{ 10}^{-14}\ \ce{J} \\
                \end{aligned}$

        As the proton has moved to a lower potential, negative work is done.

3. The proton accelerates away from the sphere $\checkmark$

    The acceleration decreases as it moves further away $\checkmark$

    Eventually reaches a constant speed $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Because of $F\,=\,ma$, and the net force decreases with distance, the acceleration will also decrease with distance. At a large distance away, the net force approaches zero, therefore the acceleration will also reach zero.
