# PH0958

The diagram below shows two point charges P and Q. Both P and Q are positively charged, and Q has a greater charge than P. No other charges are present.

![PH0958](images/PH0958.png)

Below are three statements about the charges.

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The point where the electric field is zero is closer to P than Q.  |
  | II. | The electric potential at every point between the charges is positive. |
  | III. | A small positive charge placed at the midpoint between P and Q will move towards P. |

Which of the statements are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

I is correct – At the midpoint between the charges the electric field due to Q is greater than the electric field due to P. Moving towards P (and therefore away from Q) will result in the electric field due to P increasing and the electric field due to Q decreasing.

At some point the electric fields will have the same magnitude (and they point in opposite directions) so field strength will be zero (at a point closer to P than Q).

II is correct – Electric potential is zero at infinity, negative around negative charges and positive around positive charges.

(Work is done bringing a small positive charge from infinity towards a larger positive charge)

In the presence of two positive charges potential will be positive. Potentials do not cancel out like fields. Work will be done bringing a small positive charge from infinity to anywhere between the two charges.

III is correct – Small positive charge will be repelled from P and Q. Repulsion from Q is greater so charge would move towards P.
