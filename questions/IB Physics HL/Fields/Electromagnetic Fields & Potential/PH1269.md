# PH1269

Three charges, $q$, $q$, and $-q$ are held together with a separation of d, as shown in the figure. What is the work done to move the charge  $-q$ to infinity?

![PH1269q](images/PH1269q.png)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $k\dfrac{q^2}{d}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $-k\dfrac{q^2}{d}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $3k\dfrac{q^2}{2d}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $-3k\dfrac{q^2}{2d}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When the charge $q_1$ is moved to an infinite distance away, its potential energy will become zero.

In the current configuration, we can determine the electrical potential energy of $-q$ by adding the contributions from each of the other charges:

- $E_{p(total)}= \dfrac{k(-q)(q)}{d}+\dfrac{k(-q)(q)}{2d}=\dfrac{-3kq^2}{2d}$

Therefore the charge will gain $\dfrac{3kq^2}{2d}$ of energy in being brought to an infinite distance away, so this is the work done.

Alternatively:

The work done to move a charge from a system to infinity is equal to the change in the system's total electric potential energy. The electric potential energy $E_{p1}$ of the system before moving the charge $-q$:

- $E_{p1}=k\dfrac{q^2}{d}+k\dfrac{-q^2}{2d}+k\dfrac{-q^2}{d}=k\dfrac{-q^2}{2d}$

The electric potential energy
$E_{p2}$ of the system after moving the charge -q:

- $E_{p2}=k\dfrac{q^2}{d}$

The difference between the final and initial potentials:

- $W=k\dfrac{q^2}{d}-\left(k\dfrac{-q^2}{2d}\right)=3k\dfrac{q^2}{2d}$
