## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Drops have different mass and charge / mass to charge ratio.  $\checkmark$

        Drops experience weight force downwards and electric force (+ buoyancy) upwards.  $\checkmark$

        For drops moving up electric force (+ buoyancy) > weight

        OR

        For drops moving down weight < electric force (+ buoyancy)  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Weight acts downwards. From the fact that some drops are moving up, we can deduce that the electric force is directed upwards (buoyancy is much smaller than weight).

        Drops will initially be moving downwards (as they have fallen from some height). So, their free body diagram is as follows (treating buoyancy as negligible)

        ![image](images/image.png)

        If the electric force = weight, then initially there is a net force upwards (drag) causing the object to decelerate. Drag $\propto$ speed, so drop will decelerate until speed = zero (object is stationary and net force = zero)

        If weight > electric force, then the drop will reach an equilibrium where drag + electric force = gravitational force. The drop will be moving downwards at a constant velocity.

        If weight < electric force, then the drop will reach an equilibrium where electric force = drag + weight. The drop will be moving upwards at a constant velocity.

    2. Downwards / towards bottom plate OWTTE.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Electric force acting on the oil drops is directed upwards (we know this because some of the oil drops moved upwards). Oil drops are negatively charged (stated in part (a)) and electric field is directed the way a positive charge would move. So field is directed downwards.

2. 1. $Eq=mg$  $\checkmark$

        $m=\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 {\rho}_{oil}$ and $E=\dfrac{V}{d}$  $\checkmark$

        Correct substitution and rearranging clearly leading to  $q=\dfrac{4\pi r^3 gd{\rho}_{oil}}{3V}$    $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        For the stationary drop Electric force = gravitational force - drop must be stationary (not moving at a constant speed) as moving drops experience drag.

        - $mg=Eq$

        - $E=\dfrac{V}{d}$ and $m={\rho}_{oil}$(volume of oil drop) $= {\rho}_{oil} \Bigl(\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \Bigr)$

        (Oil drops are spherical so volume $=\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3$  )

        - ${\rho}_{oil} \Bigl(\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \Bigr)=\dfrac{Vq}{d}$

        - $q=\dfrac{4\pi r^3 gd{\rho}_{oil}}{3V}$

    2. The drop is very small / drop must be looked at through a microscope.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Drop is too small for radius to be measured using a ruler. Micrometer cannot be used as drop will lose its shape upon contact (it may also be too small to be identified with micrometer).

    3. Force identified as drag / $F_d=6\pi \eta rv$ seen  $\checkmark$

        - $mg=6\pi \eta rv$   $\checkmark$

        correct substitution (of $m=\dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 {\rho}_{oil})$ and rearranging clearly leading to $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{9ηv}{2g{\rho}_{oil}}}$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        At terminal velocity, weight = drag (buoyancy is also present but we are only considering two largest forces).

        - $mg=6\pi \eta rv$

        Where $m$ is the mass of the oil drop, $g$ is the acceleration of freefall on Earth,  $\eta$ is the viscosity of air, $r$ is the radius of the oil drop and $v$ is the terminal velocity of the oil drop.

        - $m={\rho}_{oil}\times$ Volume of oil

        Volume of oil drop $=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ (volume of sphere and we are assuming the drop is spherical)

        Combining the two above equations gives

        - $m=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi {\rho}_{oil} r^3$

        Substituting this $m$ into $mg=6\pi \eta rv$ gives

        - $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi {\rho}_{oil} r^3 g=6\pi \eta rv$

        - $\dfrac{4}{3}{\rho}_{oil} r^2 g=6\eta v$

        - $r^2=\dfrac{\frac{3}{4} 6\eta v}{{\rho}_{oil} g}=\dfrac{9\eta v}{2{\rho}_{oil} g}$

        - $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{9\eta v}{2g{\rho}_{oil}}}$

    4. Calculation of $r=1.0(2)\times 10^{-6}\, \ce{m}$

        OR

        Correct expression for $q$ in terms of variables given: $q=\dfrac{4\pi (\frac{9\eta v}{2g{\rho}_{oil}})^{\frac{3}{2}} gd{\rho}_{oil}}{3V}$ $\checkmark$

        $q=8.0\times 10^{-19}\, \ce{C}$  $\checkmark$

        Exact value of $r$ and use of $r$ rounded to 3sf gives an answer of $8.0\times 10^{-19}\, \ce{C} $ 2sf. Award 1 mark for answer of $7.6\times 10^{-19}\,\ce{C}$ (use of $r$ rounded to 2sf).

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{9\eta v}{2g{\rho}_{oil}}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{(9)(1.6\times 10^{-5})(1.3\times 10^{-4})}{(2)(9.8)(920)}}=1.018…\times 10^{-6}$

        It is important that we use exact value of $r$ (or $r$ correct to at least 3sf) as $q \propto r^3$ so the percentage error in $q$ will be around three times the percentage error in $r$.

        - $q=\dfrac{4\pi r^3 gd{\rho}_{oil}}{3V}=\dfrac{4\pi(1.018…\times 10^{-6)})^3 (9.8)(1.4\times 10^{-2})(920)}{(3)(700)}=8.0\times 10^{-19}$

        Note: $8.0\times 10^{-19}=5e$

3. All multiples of $1.6\times 10^{-19}$ / All have a common factor (of $e$) OWTTE  $\checkmark$

    Allow values go up in increments/steps of e/$1.6\times 10^{-19}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The term charge is quantized means that charge comes in discrete packets. This means that the charge of any object is an integer multiple of the elementary charge.

    Millikan's experiment showed that charge of oil drops is always a multiple of $1.6\times 10^{-19}$. From this it was deduced that the elementary charge (also the charge of the electron) $=1.6\times 10^{-19}$.

4. Buoyancy/Upthrust  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The oil drop is submerged in a fluid (air). A buoyancy force acts on the oil drop as there is a greater pressure at the bottom (as the weight of fluid above is greater) causing an upward force.

    Buoyancy force $F_B=\rho Vg$

    Where $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $V$ is the volume of the object submerged in the fluid and $g$ is the acceleration of freefall on Earth.

    - $F_B={\rho}_{air} V_{drop} g=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 {\rho}_{air} g$

    For the stationary oil drop in equilibrium Electric force + Buoyancy = Weight

    ![image](images/image.png)

    - $Eq+\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 {\rho}_{air} g=mg$

    - $\dfrac{V}{d} q+\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 {\rho}_{air} g=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 {\rho}_{oil} g$

    - $\dfrac{V}{d} q=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 {\rho}_{oil} g-\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 {\rho}_{air} g$

    - $\dfrac{V}{d} q=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 g({\rho}_{oil}-{\rho}_{air})$

    - $q=\dfrac{4πr^3 gd({\rho}_{oil}-{\rho}_{air})}{3V}$
