        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The field between parallel plates is uniform, but weaker near the edges of the plates. Equally spaced electric field lines represent uniform field. The curvature of the edges is due to a weaker field, represented by greater spacing between the field lines.

        Equipotential lines would be parallel to the plates and to each other between two parallel plates. The distance from each plate is proportional to the value of the potential each line represents. The total potential difference between the plates is 14 V, therefore the 0 V line is to be $\frac{1}{7}^{th}$ of the total separation from the top $(-2\, \ce{V})$ plate, judged by eye.

        Because equipotential lines are always perpendicular to electric field lines, there is curvature in the equipotential lines due the edge effects. This curvature does not need to be shown for full marks.

3.     work $=q\Delta \ce{V}=(-6.0\times10^{-6})(-2-12)$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{2.1cm} = $ :answer[$-8.4\times10^{-5}\ \ce{J}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The working above makes use of the change in electric potential, however the work done by the field can also be considered in terms of the work formula.

    - $W= Fs \cos \theta$

    The negative charge is moving from a high potential plate to a lower potential plate. For a positive charge, this would be a loss of electric potential but for the electric charge, it is a gain in electric potential. The electric force from the plates on the negative charge is opposite to the direction of motion, and so in the work formula $\theta$ will be $180 \degree$. The cosine of $180 \degree$ is $-1$ and so the electric field will be negative.
