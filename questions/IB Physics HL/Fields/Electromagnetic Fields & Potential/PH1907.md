# PH1907

The diagram below shows equipotential lines for an electric field.

![Screenshot 2025-09-06 at 6.09.43 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-09-06 at 6.09.43 PM.png>)

What is the best estimate for the electric field strength and the direction of the electric field at point $P$?

$\\$

| | Electric field strength / N C$^{-1}$ | Direction of electric field |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| 500 | To the right |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 500 | To the left |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 2500 | To the right |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 2500 | To the left |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The average electric field strength between two points is given by:

- $E=\dfrac{\Delta V}{\Delta r}$

Where $\Delta V$ is the difference in electric potential between the points and $\Delta r$ is the distance between the points.

$P$ is located between two equipotentials separated by a distance of $\Delta r=2\textrm{ cm}=0.02\textrm{ m}$. The difference is $\Delta V=30-20=10$ V. Therefore, we have:

- $E=\dfrac{10}{0.02}=500$ N C$^{-1}$

By convention, the direction of an electric field is the direction in which a positive charge would accelerate (from high to low potential) so the field points to the left.
