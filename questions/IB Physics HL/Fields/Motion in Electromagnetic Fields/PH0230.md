# PH0230

A current-carrying wire is at right angles to a uniform magnetic field. The magnetic force per unit length on the wire is $\dfrac{F}{L}$. The length of the wire, the magnitude of the magnetic field strength, and the current of the wire are all doubled. Which of the following is the magnetic force per unit length on the wire after these changes?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{F}{L}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2F}{L}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4F}{L}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{8F}{L}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The force on a current-carrying wire under the effect of a magnetic field that is perpendicular to the current;

$F = Bil$ which gives $\dfrac{F}{l} = Bi$

Both the magnetic field and the current are increased to twice their initial values;

$\text{Force/unit length} = (2B)(2i) = 4Bi = 4 \dfrac{F}{l}$

Therefore the answer is C.

Note that the changing of the length does not affect the force per unit length here.
