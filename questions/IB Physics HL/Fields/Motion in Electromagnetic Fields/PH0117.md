# PH0117

An alpha particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$, is accelerated through a potential difference $V$ into a magnetic field of flux density $B$ that is at right angles to the direction of motion. Which of the sketches below best describes the relationship between the radius of path $r$ and the potential difference of the accelerating field?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The path of a particle in the $B$-Field is a circle of radius
$$
r = \dfrac{mv}{Bq}
$$
Since kinetic energy is conserved in the field,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{kinetic energy} &= \text{work done by the field on the alpha particle.} \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2} mv^2 &= qV \\[6pt]
v &= \sqrt{\dfrac{2qV}{m}} \\[6pt]
\therefore \quad r &= \dfrac{m}{Bq} \sqrt{\dfrac{2qV}{m}} \\[9pt]
r&= \sqrt\dfrac{2Vm}{B^2q} \\[10pt]
r & \varpropto \sqrt{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence the radius varies directly as the square root of the applied potential.
