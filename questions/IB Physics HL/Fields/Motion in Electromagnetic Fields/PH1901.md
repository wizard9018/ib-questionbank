# PH1901

The diagram below shows an electron of mass $m_e$ entering a region where a constant magnetic field of magnitude $B$ is present.

![ph1901q](images/ph1901q.png)
$\small{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra, - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

Given that the electron is moving at a speed $v$, which of the following correctly describes the direction and magnitude of the acceleration experienced by the electron?

$\\$

| | Direction | Magnitude |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| up | $\dfrac{m_evB}{e}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | down | $\dfrac{m_evB}{e}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | up | $\dfrac{evB}{m_e}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | down | $\dfrac{evB}{m_e}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

According to Fleming's left-hand rule, a positively charged particle moving to the right (middle finger) in a magnetic field directed into the screen (index finger) will experience a force acting upwards (thumb).

Since the particle in question is an electron, which carries a negative charge, the force direction is reversed and will therefore act downwards. Consequently, the acceleration of the electron will also be directed downwards.

To determine the magnitude of the acceleration we can use Newton's second law.

- $F=m_ea$

The electron is initially moving with a velocity that is perpendicular to the magnetic field. The magnitude of the force will then be given by $F=eVB$.

Combining these two expressions leads to:

- $eVB=m_ea$

Which can be solved to find the magnitude of the acceleration:

- $a=\dfrac{{eV}B}{m_e}$
