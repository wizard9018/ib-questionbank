# PH0805

A wire PQ, is placed between the poles of a large magnet that is suspended freely from a Newton-meter as shown in the diagram. The wire is perpendicular to the magnetic field and 10 cm of the wire lies within the magnetic field. The magnetic flux density between the poles is 0.5 T. The newton-meter is held in place.

![805aaa](images/805aaa.png)

Initially, a current $I_1$ flows through the wire and the newton-meter reads $29.8\ $N.

The current is then changed to $I_2$

$I_{2\ }$ has three times the magnitude of $I_1$ and flows in the opposite direction. The newton-meter now reads $30.2\ $N.

What is the magnitude and direction of the initial current, $I_1$?

$\\$

| | Magnitude / A  | Direction |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| 2 | P to Q  |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 2 | Q to P |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 3  | P to Q |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 3  | Q to P |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

**Direction**

Using Fleming's left-hand rule:

A current in the direction P to Q will cause an upward force on the wire. Newton's third law (when two bodies A and B interact the force that A exerts on B is equal and opposite to the force that B exerts on A) tells us that the magnet will experience an equal and opposite force. Therefore, the magnet will experience a downward force and the reading on the newton-meter will be higher than if there were no current (if there were no current the newton-meter would simply read the weight of the magnet).

A current in the direction Q to P will cause a downward force on the wire. Newton's third law tells us that the magnet will experience an equal and opposite force. Therefore, the magnet will experience an upward force and the reading on the newton-meter will be lower than if there were no current.

$I_1$ is the smaller force reading so must be flowing in the direction Q to P.

$I_1$ and $I_2$ flow in different directions so the smaller current must correspond to a newton-meter reading smaller than the weight of the magnet; and the larger current must correspond to a newton-meter reading larger than the weight of the magnet.

**Magnitude**

Force reading on the newton-meter is the weight of the magnet ($W_m)\, \pm$ magnetic force.

$30.2=W_m+BI_2L$   (Positive sign as magnetic force in the same direction as weight for the larger newton-meter reading)

$29.8=W_m-BI_1L$  (Negative sign as magnetic force in the opposite direction of weight for the smaller newton-meter reading)

Substituting in values gives:

- $30.2=W_m+\left(0.5\right)(3I_1)\left(0.1\right)=W_m+0.15I_1$

- $29.8=W_m-\left(0.5\right)\left(I_1\right)\left(0.1\right)=W_m-0.05I_1$

We now have simultaneous equations in $W_m$ and $I_1$.

Subtracting the above equations gives

- $30.2-29.8=\left(W_m+0.15I_1\right)-(W_m-0.05I_1)$

- $0.4=0.2I_1$

- $I_1=2\ $A
