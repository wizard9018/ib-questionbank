## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The electric force ($F$) is equal to the product of the electric field strength ($E$) and the charge of the particle ($q$) $\checkmark$

    Note: Accept $F = Eq$ as long as variables are specified.

    A positive charge has the force in the same direction as the electric field / a negative charge has the force in the opposite direction to the electric field. OWTTE $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    By convention, the direction of the electric field is defined as the direction of the force acting on a positive test charge.

2. 1. One volt is a joule per coulomb and a joule is a newton metre

        ($1\ce{V} = 1\,\dfrac{\,\ce{J}}{\ce{C}}\,/\,1\,\ce{C} = 1\,\dfrac{\ce{J}}{\ce{V}}$) and ($1\,\ce{J} = 1\,\ce{N}\,\ce{m}$ / $1\, \ce{N} = 1\,\dfrac{\ce{J}}{\ce{m}}$)  $\checkmark$

        Substituting into $\dfrac{\ce{N}}{\ce{C}}$ Gives, $(\dfrac{\ce{J}}{\ce{m}})(\dfrac{V}{\ce{J}}) = \ce{V\,m^{-1}}$.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Electric field strength, $E$, is defined as the size of the electric force, $F$, acting on a unit charge, $q$:

        - $E=\dfrac{F}{q}$

        Resulting in the units of $\ce{N\,C^{-1}}$ for electric field strength.

        Work done can be found from $W=Fs=qEs$

        And work done on a charge is also $W=q\Delta V$

        Setting the two equal gives

        - $qEs=q\Delta V$

        - $E=\dfrac{\Delta V}{s}$

        which shows that the units on electric field strength can also be expressed as $\ce{V\,m^{-1}}$.

    2. $<\Delta E_k = -\Delta E_p=W>$

        $\dfrac{1}{2}m v^2=q \Delta V$

        OR

        $\Delta V=\dfrac{mv^2}{2q}$   $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{1}{2}(9.110\times 10^{-31})(8.3\times 10^6)^2=(1.60\times 10^{-19})\Delta V$

        OR

        $\Delta V= \dfrac{(9.110\times 10^{-31})(8.3\times 10^6)^2}{2(1.60\times 10^{-19})}$ $\checkmark$

        $\Delta V= 196\,(\ce{V})= \ll 200\,\ce{V} \gg$  $\checkmark$

        Note: Must show unrounded value for MP3.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The electric force, $F$, acting on an electron of charge $q$ in an electric field with field strength, $E$,

        - $F=Eq$

        The electric field, $E$, between two parallel charged plates is created by a high voltage $V$, applied across a small distance, $d$,

        - $E = \dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$

        Substituting into the expression for electric force,

        - $F=\dfrac{\Delta V}{d}q$

        The work, $W$, done by the electric field and further substitution shows,

        - $W=Fd= \dfrac{\Delta Vq}{d}d=q\Delta V$

        Conservation of Energy means that the kinetic energy gained by the accelerated electron $(E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2)$ equals the work done by the electric field.

        - $\Delta E_k= -\Delta E_p=W$

        - $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=q\Delta V$

        - $\dfrac{1}{2}(9.110\times 10^{-31})(8.3\times 10^6)^2=(1.60\times 10^{-19})\Delta V$

        - $\Delta V=\dfrac{(9.110\times 10^{-31})(8.3\times 10^6)^2}{2(1.60\times 10^{-19})}$

        - $\Delta V= 196\,(\ce{V})= 200\, \ce{V}$(2sf)

        It is assumed that that the only force acting on the electron is the electric force, or all other forces are negligible.

3. Use of

    - $F_B= F_C$

    OR

    - $qvB=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ $\checkmark$

    Substitution with correct values,

    - $<\dfrac{m}{q}=\dfrac{Br}{v}> $

    - $\dfrac{m}{q}=\dfrac{0.400(3.1\times 10^{-2})}{(3.2\times 10^4)}$ $\checkmark$

    - $\dfrac{m}{q}=3.9\times 10^{-7}\, \ce{kg\,C^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Since the ion enters the magnetic field at right angles to the field, the magnetic force acts perpendicular to the velocity of the particle ($\theta=90\degree$).

    The magnetic force is given by $F_B=qvB\sin{\theta}$ which simplifies to $F_B=qvB$ since $\sin{(90\degree)}=1$.

    This perpendicular magnetic force, $F_B$, acts as a centripetal force, $F_C$, to produce the circular path.

    Therefore,

    - $F_B= F_C$

    Centripetal force is given by,

    - $F_C=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

    So,

    - $qvB= \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

    Rearrange to determine mass-charge ratio, $\dfrac{m}{q}$, and substitute values,

    - $\dfrac{m}{q}= \dfrac{Br}{v}$

    - $\dfrac{m}{q}=\dfrac{0.400(3.1\times 10^{-2})}{3.2\times 10^4}$

    - $\dfrac{m}{q}=3.9\times 10^{-7}\, \ce{kg\,C^{-1}}$

4. 1. mass-charge ratio $\Bigl(\dfrac{m}{q}\Bigr)$ is $\underline{\text{proportional}}$ to radius ($r$) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        From part c,

        - $\dfrac{m}{q}=\dfrac{Br}{v}$

        Therefore,

        - $\dfrac{m}{q} \propto r$

    2. Magnetic field into page detects positive charge.  Reverse magnetic field direction to out of page to detect negative charge   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Applying Fleming's left-hand rule (or right-hand slap rule), the counter-clockwise motion shown in the diagram applies to a positive charge.

        Reversing the direction of the field (i.e. out of the page) will mean that a negative charge will also follow a counter-clockwise motion and so be detected.
