# PH0920

**Maximum mark: 9**

1. In a Bainbridge mass spectrometer, a charged particle passes through two regions. First it enters Region 1, which has combined magnetic and electric fields. After that, it proceeds into Region 2 where only a magnetic field exists.

    During its journey through Region 1, the charge maintains its original path without any deviation. However, once it enters Region 2, it follows a semi-circular trajectory with a radius of $r$.

    ![PH0920](images/PH0920.png)

    In Region 1, the strength of the electric field is $E$ and the strength of the magnetic flux density is $B$. Express the speed $v$ of the particle in terms of $E$ and $B$.          [2 marks]

2. Deduce how the kinetic energy of the particle changes in Region 2.    [2 marks]

3. The following data is available:

    - $v=1.0\times{10}^5\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

    - $E=1.0\times{10}^4\, \ce{V\,m^{-1}}$

    - $B=0.10$ T

    - $r=0.25$ m

    Determine the charge-to-mass ratio of the charged particle.      [3 marks]

4. In another experiment,  Helmholtz coils are used to create a uniform magnetic field. A Helmholtz coil consists of two identical circular coils with the same magnitude of current, separated by a specific distance and aligned coaxially as shown below.

    ![PH0920d](images/PH0920d.png)

    A leftward magnetic field is obtained at point A, the midpoint between the coils. Deduce the direction of the force acting on coil B.      [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $q\,v\,B=q\,E$ $\checkmark$

    $v=\dfrac{E}{B}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    As the charge passes through the first region, it experiences two forces: one magnetic $F_B$ and one electric $F_E$. To enable the charge to travel without deviating from its path, these two forces must have equal magnitudes but act in opposite directions, effectively cancelling each other out:

    - $F_B=F_E$

    - $q\,v\,B=q\,E$

    - $v=\dfrac{E}{B}$

2. No change/constant

    The resultant force/magnetic force is perpendicular to the motion of the particle $\checkmark$

    No work done

    **OR**

    No increase in speed $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    There is only one force acting on the charge, magnetic force. This force always acts at a right angle (perpendicular) to the direction of the charge's movement at any given moment. Consequently, no work is being done on the charge, meaning there's no change in its kinetic energy. Alternatively, there is no component of the resultant force on the direction of the motion of the particle. Therefore, there is no acceleration or deceleration.

3. $q\,v\,B=m\,\frac{v^2}{r}$ $\checkmark$

    $\dfrac{q}{m}=\dfrac{1.0\times{10}^5}{(0.25)(0.1)}$ $\checkmark$

    :answer[$4.0\times{10}^6\ \ce{C\,{kg}^{-1}}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The magnetic force $F_B$ acting on the charge is the provider of centripetal force in the second region:

    - $F_B=q\,v\,B=m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}$

    - $\dfrac{q}{m}=\dfrac{v}{r\,B}$

    When the given values are substituted:

    - $\dfrac{q}{m}=\dfrac{1.0\times{10}^5}{(0.25)(0.1)}=4.0\times{10}^6\ \ce{C\,{kg}^{-1}}$

4. The current directions must be the same to avoid cancellation $\checkmark$

    parallel currents attract each other/to  the left $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Because the currents in both coils have equal magnitudes, and the distance between point A and the coils is the same, the current directions must be the same to avoid cancellation of the magnetic field at point A. With parallel currents, the coils should experience an attractive force. This implies that the force acting on coil B is directed to the left.
