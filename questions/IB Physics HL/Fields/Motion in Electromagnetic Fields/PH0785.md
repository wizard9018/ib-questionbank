# PH0785

A wire and a region of uniform magnetic field lie in the same plane. The wire is positioned in two distinct ways, as illustrated in Figure **A** and Figure **B**, resulting in different angles between the direction of the magnetic field and the direction of the current in the wire

![PH0785aa](images/PH0785aa.png)

Which of the following is correct in terms of the magnitude of the force and the direction of the force acting on the wire?

$\\$

| | Magnitude of the force | The direction of the force |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Same | Same |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Same| Opposite |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Greater in **A** | Same |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Greater in **A** | Opposite |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The force $F$ acting on the wire:

$\hspace{1em}$ $F=BIL\sin\theta$

Where $B$ is magnetic flux density, $I$ is the current passing through the wire, $L$ is the length of the wire in the field and $\theta$ is the angle between the direction of the current and magnetic field.

Since $L\sin\theta$ represents the vertical component of the wire's length within the magnetic field, the value of $L\sin\theta$ is the same for both scenarios. Additionally, there is no alteration in the values of $B$ and $I$ in either position. Consequently, it follows that the magnitude of the force remains identical in both configurations.

Another way of thinking of this is that as $\theta$ increases the length of wire in the field $L$ increases. But as $\theta$ increases, $\sin\theta$ decreases and the changes to $L$ and $\sin\theta$ cancel out.

When the current in the wire is resolved into two components, one vertical and one horizontal, the horizontal component does not experience any force because it is parallel to the magnetic field. However, the vertical component does experience a force because it is perpendicular to the magnetic field. In both scenarios, the current is directed upwards and the magnetic field is directed leftwards. Therefore, the force on the wire should be the same for both.
