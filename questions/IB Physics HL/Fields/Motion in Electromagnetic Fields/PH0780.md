# PH0780

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. An electron travels into an evacuated box where a uniform electric field acts vertically downwards. The electron is travelling perpendicular to the field at a speed $v$ at the moment it enters the box.

    ![PH0780](images/PH0780.png)

    Deduce the motion of the electron inside the box. Ignore any gravitational forces. [2 marks]

2. Once the electron has exited the box, a uniform magnetic field is added to the box in addition to the electric field. The magnetic field acts into the page, perpendicular to the plane of the paper and the electric field.

    Another electron travels horizontally into the box at a speed v (initially moving perpendicular to both fields).

    ![PH0780bb](images/PH0780bb.png)

    The electron is found to exit the box along the same straight line which it entered, with the same speed, $v$. Explain this observation.      [2 marks]

3. The electric field is now removed such that the box contains the magnetic field only. Another electron enters perpendicular to the field at a speed $v$.

    This electron is found to exit the box at the same speed $v$, travelling vertically downwards.

    ![PH0780C](images/PH0780C.png)

    Explain why the speed of the electron is unchanged. [2 marks]

4. It is known that the electron in part (c) was inside the box for a time of 1.0 $\mu $s. Show that the magnetic flux density inside the box is around 9 $\mu $T.  [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Electron accelerates/experiences a net force upwards while horizontal velocity (component) remains the same  $\checkmark$

    The electron follows a parabolic path (upwards) $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Electric force acts on a positive charge in the same direction as field (downwards).

    Electron is negatively charged so force acts upwards causing an upward acceleration.

    Electron experiences no horizontal force so horizontal component of velocity remains unchanged

2. Magnetic field causes the electron to experience a force downwards. \<<Fleming's left hand rule\>> $\checkmark$

    Upward electric force and downward magnetic force have the same magnitude/ Electric and magnetic forces are equal and opposite. So net force acting on the electron in the box is zero. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Fleming's left hand rule - electron is negatively charged so acts as a negative current to the right/ positive current to the left.

    Net force = 0 implies acceleration = 0  (Newton's 1st law) so the electron remains at constant velocity.

3. Magnetic force is \<<constantly\>> perpendicular to the velocity of the electron. $\checkmark$

    So acceleration causes velocity to change direction while speed/ magnitude of velocity remains constant. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Magnetic field causes a moving charge to experience a force perpendicular to both the field and the velocity of the charge (direction can be determined using Fleming's left hand rule).

    These three vectors (velocity, magnetic field and force) remain perpendicular.

    When force is perpendicular to velocity, circular motion occurs – direction of the velocity changes while its magnitude remains the same.

4. Magnetic force = mass $\times$  acceleration

    - $\ Bqv=m\,\omega^2\,r$

    -  **OR**
    - $Bqv=\dfrac{m\,v^2}{r}$ $\checkmark$

    Substitution of $v=\omega\ r$ and \
    Rearranging for $B$:

    - $B=\dfrac{m_e\,\omega}{e}$

    **OR**

    Equation in terms of Time period:

    - $B=\dfrac{m_e\,2\pi}{T\,e}$

    - Time period = $4.0\times{10}^{-6}\,$s

    **OR**

    - $\omega=1.57\times{10}^6\ \ce{s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    Final answer:

    - $B=$ :answer[$8.9\ \mu \ce{T}$] $\checkmark$

    (extra significant figures must be shown for MP2)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Magnetic force = mass $\times$ centripetal acceleration (as the electron is moving in a circle): $B\,q\,v=m\,\omega^2\,r$ or $Bqv=\dfrac{m\,v^2}{r}$

    ($q$ is elementary charge, $e. m$ is mass of electron $m_e$)

    Substitution of $v=\omega\ r$ gives: $Be\,\omega\ r=m_e\,\omega^2\,r$

    Rearranging for $B$ gives: $B=\dfrac{m_e\,\omega}{e}$
    **OR**
    Since $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$      In terms of Time period equation may be written $B=\dfrac{m_e\,2\pi}{Te}$

    The only possible trajectory for this particular entry and exit of the box is a $^1/_4$ circle. So time given is $^1/_4$ of the time period for the circular path.

    Time period is $(4)\left(1.0\times{10}^{-6}\right)=4.0\times{10}^{-6}\ $s \
    **OR**

    - $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{(4)(1\times{10}^{-6})}=1.57\times{10}^6{\ \ce{s}}^{-1}$

    Final answer:

    - $B=\dfrac{{2\pi\, m}_e}{T\,e}$

    - $=\dfrac{(2\pi)(9.11\times{10}^{-31})}{(4\times{10}^{-6})(1.6\times{10}^{-19})}$

    - $=8.94\times{10}^{-6}\, $T or  $8.9\, \mu $T
