# PH0474

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. A mass spectrometer is a tool that uses a magnetic field to determine the mass of a moving charged particle.

    An alpha particle with a speed of $2.0\times10{^7}$ m s$^{-1}$ enters a region of magnetic field directed into the plane of the paper.

    ![Screenshot 2025-05-29 at 1.33.51 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-05-29 at 1.33.51 PM.JPEG>)

    1. Suggest why the alpha particle undergoes circular motion inside the magnetic field. [2 marks]

    2. Explain why the centripetal force does no work on the alpha particle. [2 marks]

2. 1. On the diagram, draw an arrow to represent the path of the alpha particle inside the magnetic field. [2 marks]

    2. State the charge on the alpha particle. [1 mark]

    3. The radius of the path of the alpha particle is determined to be 15 cm, and the magnetic field strength is 2.77 T.

        Calculate the mass of the alpha particle, expressing your answer to the appropriate number of significant figures. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. The alpha particle experiences a (magnetic) force $\checkmark$ \
    The force is perpendicular to the velocity (which is the condition for circular motion) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Recall that a moving charge in a magnetic field experiences a magnetic force in a perpendicular direction to the velocity and the magnetic field.

        Because the magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity at all times, it cannot either speed it up or slow it down - only change its direction. Because the magnitude of the magnetic force is proportional to the velocity, it also remains constant - and so the direction of the particle changes at a constant rate. This produces circular motion.

    2. Work = (Force)(Displacement)($\cos(\theta)$) $\checkmark$

        Force vector and displacement vector are perpendicular, therefore $\theta=90\degree$ and $\cos(\theta)$ = 0 $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        Work = $\Delta$ kinetic energy $\checkmark$

        Speed / kinetic energy of the alpha particle is constant (hence no work done) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        At any instant, the magnetic force vector is perpendicular to the velocity vector, and is thus perpendicular to the displacement vector. Thus $\theta=90\degree$ and $\cos(\theta)=0$, giving a value of zero for work done.

        We could also note that if work is done in the particle, we would expect its kinetic energy to change. Because the speed of the particle is constant in uniform circular motion, there is no change in kinetic energy and therefore no work done on the particle.

2. 1. The path must be a circular shape $\checkmark$

        Curving upwards and exiting the magnetic field at some point (cannot show a full circle) $\checkmark$

        ![Screenshot 2025-05-29 at 1.38.35 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-05-29 at 1.38.35 PM.JPEG>)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Use Fleming's or other rule to determine the force on the particle to be upwards as it enters the magnetic field. Path needs to be circular, and not parabolic in shape. Note that a full circle cannot be drawn—for small radii a semicircular path will result, with the particle exiting parallel to its entry path.

    2. :answer[$3.2\times10^{-19}$ C] $\checkmark$

        Allow $+2e$. Award no mark for $+2$.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        This is a factual question. An alpha particle is a Helium nucleus which has 2 neutrons and 2 protons, therefore +2 elementary charge.

    3. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $m=\dfrac{qrB}{v}=\dfrac{(3.2\times{10}^{-19})(15\times{10}^{-2})(2.77)}{2.0\times{10}^7}$ $\checkmark$

        - $m=$ :answer[$6.6\times{10}^{-27}\textrm{ kg}$] $\checkmark$

        Answer in 2 significant figures, correct rounding $\checkmark$

        Allow error carried forward for use of charge given in b (ii)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The magnetic force acts as the centripetal force, therefore:

        - $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}=qvB$

        Rearranging gives

        - $m=\dfrac{qrB}{v}$

        - $m=\dfrac{(3.2\times{10}^{-19})(15\times{10}^{-2})(2.77)}{2.0\times{10}^7}$

        - $m=6.6\times{10}^{-27}$ kg
