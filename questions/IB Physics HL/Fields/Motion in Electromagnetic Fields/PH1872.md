# PH1872

A current $I$ flows around a square coil of side lengths $l$. The two opposite sides of the coil are labelled $X$ and $Y$.

![Screenshot 2025-08-26 at 3.00.06 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-08-26 at 3.00.06 PM.png>)

Which of the following correctly states the magnitude of the force acting on $X$ and the nature of the force between $X$ and $Y$?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Magnitude of force on side $X$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Nature of force between $X$ and $Y$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi}$ | Attractive |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi}$ | Repulsive |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi l}$ | Attractive |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi l}$ | Repulsive |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The force $F$ per unit length $l$ acting on a pair of parallel wires of separation $r$ carrying currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ is given by:

- $\dfrac{F}{l}=\dfrac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi r}$

Where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space $=1.26\times10^{-6}$ N A$^{-2}$.

Each wire carries a current $I$ and each pair of wires is separated by a distance $l$ so we have:

- $\dfrac{F}{l}=\dfrac{\mu_0(I)(I)}{2\pi l}$

- $F=\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi}$

Now let us determine whether the force between an adjacent pair of wires is repulsive or attractive.

Consider the wire $X$, with a current flowing upwards. The wire $Y$ has a current flowing downwards.
We can determine the magnetic field produced by $X$ using the right-hand grip rule. Point your right thumb upwards in the direction of the current in $X$. Your fingers curl around the wire, showing the direction of the magnetic field. To the right of wire $X$, where wire $Y$ sits, the magnetic field points into the page.

Now apply Fleming’s left-hand rule to $Y$. The magnetic field at the position of $Y$ is into the page, and the current in $Y$ flows downwards. With the first finger pointing out of the page and the second finger pointing downwards, the thumb points to the right. This means $Y$ experiences a force pushing it away from $X$.

By the same reasoning, the left wire experiences a repulsive force from the right wire.
