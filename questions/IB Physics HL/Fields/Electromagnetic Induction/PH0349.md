# PH0349

**Maximum mark: 13**

A triangular conducting loop with the dimensions shown moves horizontally to the right with a constant velocity of $3.0\, \ce{cm\, s^{-1}}$. At $t = 0$ the loop enters a uniform 1.2 T magnetic field which is directed into the plane of the paper. There are 75 turns of the wire on the loop.

![PH0349-1](images/PH0349-1.png)

1. Define magnetic flux linkage $ \mathrm{\Phi}$ [2 marks]

2. 1. Calculate the magnetic flux linkage on the loop when the loop is entirely in the uniform magnetic field. State your answer with the appropriate unit. [3 marks]

   2. Draw, on the axes, a graph to show the variation with time of the magnetic flux linkage   in the loop, starting from the point in time where the leading edge enters the region of magnetic field. Include values for time on the horizontal axis. [3 marks]

    ![PH0349-2a](images/PH0349-2a.png)

3.  1. Calculate the magnitude of the average induced emf on the loop for the time period where it enters the magnetic field. [2 marks]

    2. Draw, on the axes, a graph to show the variation with time of the emf induced in the loop.   [3 marks]

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The induced emf is given by the rate of change of magnetic flux. This is given by the gradient of the flux-time graph from b(ii).

        When the loop enters the field, the gradient is decreasing therefore the induced emf also decreases.

        While the loop is fully inside the magnetic field, there is no change in flux and zero gradient, therefore no induced emf.

        As the loop exits the field, the magnetic flux is again changing at a decreasing rate which results in a decreasing emf.

        Note that Faraday's Law includes a negative sign to indicate the opposition of the induced emf to the change in flux. Thus the emf is the negative gradient of the flux graph, although the direction is relative and either sign on the emf is accepted here.
