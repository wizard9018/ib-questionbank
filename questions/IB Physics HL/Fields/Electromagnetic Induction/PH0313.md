# PH0313

**Maximum mark: 6**

Two identical magnets with opposite poles facing inward are placed and fixed in a circular track as shown. The magnetic field lines between magnets pass through a conducting loop which is also shown.  The circular track rotates around the conducting loop with an angular frequency of $\omega$. As the track rotates an emf is induced on the loop.

![PH0313-1](images/PH0313-1.png)

1.    Explain, with reference to the diagram, how the rotation of the generator produces an emf in the loop. [2 marks]

The graph shows how the induced emf varies with time.

![PH0313-2](images/PH0313-2.png)

2.  Comment on the effect of each of the following on the graph of induced current through the loop versus time:

    1.     An increase in angular velocity $\omega $  [2 marks]

    2. An increase in the strength of magnets [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  There is a magnetic field in the loop / as magnets rotate field lines cut the loop. $\checkmark$

    This flux linkage changes as the angle varies AND emf is induced when the magnetic flux in the loop changes. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    By Faraday's law, induced emf $\varepsilon = -N\dfrac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}$

    Where $\Phi = BA\cos(\theta)$

    The two magnets create a magnetic field $B$ between them. As the loop rotates, the angle $\theta$ changes, which generates a changing flux and therefore induces an emf.

2. 1. as angular velocity increases the rate of change of flux / max value of induced emf/current increases. $\checkmark$

        period of alternating current will decrease. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Faraday’s Law states that the induced emf is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux. In this situation, the change in flux is caused by movement of the magnets around the fixed coil. With an increased angular velocity, the rate of change of flux increases. This will increase the magnitude of the induced emf and current, and the period of the ac current will also decrease.

        Mathematically,

        $\varepsilon\ =\ -N\dfrac{\mathrm{\delta\Phi}}{\mathrm{\delta t}}=\ -NBA\dfrac{\mathrm{\delta (\cos\theta)}}{\mathrm{\delta t}} =\ -NBA\dfrac{\mathrm{\delta (\cos(\omega t))}}{\mathrm{\delta t}} = \omega NBA(\sin(\omega t)) $

         An increase in $\omega$
 therefore will increase the magnitude of induced emf and current, in addition to shortening the period of the ac current produced.

     2. An increase in the strength of the magnet only results in an increase in the change of flux/max value of induced emf/current increases. $\checkmark$

        period of alternating current will stay constant. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        By Faraday’s Law, $\varepsilon\ =\ -N\dfrac{\mathrm{\Delta\Phi}}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}=\ -NBA\dfrac{\mathrm{\Delta cos\theta}}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}$

        A larger value for $B$ will cause a proportionally larger emf.

        Alternatively, an increase in the strength of the magnets means more magnetic field lines cut the loop and as magnets rotate more lines will be replaced by another.
