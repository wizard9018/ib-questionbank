1.
    1. It is not a straight line\
    *OR*\
    $V-I$ are not directly proportional $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Ohmic devices show a proportional relationship between current and voltage. Through the relationship\
        $\hspace{1em}$ $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$\
        This implies that the resistance remains constant.

    2. Correct position of ammeter $\checkmark$\
    Correct position of voltmeter $\checkmark$

        ![PH0847](images/PH0847.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        To determine the I-V characteristics of the resistor, the current flowing through it and the potential difference across it must be measured. Therefore, the ammeter should be connected in series to the non-ohmic resistor while the voltmeter should be parallel.

    3. $\dfrac{3.0}{7.5 \times 10^{-2}} = $ :answer[$40\ \Omega$] $\checkmark$

         $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

         $\hspace{1em}$ $R = \dfrac{V}{I} = \dfrac{3.0}{7.5 \times 10^{-2}} = 40\ \Omega$

2. 1. When the switch is closed, the magnetic field is created around the wire $\checkmark$

        There is a change in magnetic flux on the loop that induces emf/current $\checkmark$

        After a short time interval, there is no change in the current $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When the switch $s$ is closed, current starts flowing through the wire. This creates a magnetic field around the wire. The magnetic flux density on the loop increases from zero to a non-zero value as the magnetic field lines pass through the loop. So flux on the coil changes. This change in flux induces an emf in the loop.

        The change in current or magnetic flux density from zero to a non-zero value happens in a very short time interval. After that, the current does not change, so the magnetic flux density does not change either. Therefore, there is no induced emf on the coil.

    2. deflects to the left then drops to zero $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
As soon as the cell is disconnected, the current flowing in the circuit decreases to zero in very short time so the magnetic flux density on the loop drops to zero. Therefore, flux on the loop changes and results in induced emf but opposite direction compared to the situation in which the switch is closed. After that, there will be no change in current so no change in flux. Hence needle deflects leftward and gets back to the centre zero position.
