# PH0023

$X$, $Y$ and $Z$ are three plane coils. They are parallel to each other and share the same central axis. There is a constant current in the fixed positioned coil $Y$.

![PH0023](images/PH0023.png)

Both coils $X$ and $Z$ are moved to the right. What are the directions of the induced currents in coil $X$ and coil $Z$ relative to coil $Y$?

$\\$

| | Coil X | Coil Z|
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Same direction as in coil Y$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Same direction as in coil Y$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Opposite direction to coil Y | Same direction as in coil Y |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Same direction as in coil Y | Opposite direction to coil Y |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Opposite direction to coil Y | Opposite direction to coil Y |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

According to Lenz' law, emf is induced such that the induced current is in a direction to oppose the change in flux. Coil $X$ is approaching coil $Y$, and magnetic flux increases; the induced current will be in the opposite direction to create the opposing magnetic field. Conversely, coil $Z$ recedes from coil $Y$, and magnetic flux decreases; that is, the induced current will be in the same direction to create a magnetic field in the same direction.
