# PH0350

**Maximum mark: 10**

A pair of vertical conducting rails are placed in a uniform magnetic field of 0.40 T directed out of the plane of the paper, as shown. A $30\, \mathrm{\Omega}$ resistor is placed on a 0.2 m long conducting rod that can slide vertically downwards on the rails under the effect of gravity.

![PH0350a](images/PH0350a.png)

1. State and explain the direction of the current passing through the ammeter when the rod moves downwards. [2 marks]

2. The rod is released from rest and moves downwards along the rails. Explain how the induced current in the rod changes over time. [5 marks]

3. 1. At a given instant, the rod's velocity reaches $0.30\, \ce{m\, s^{-1}}$. Calculate the magnitude of the induced emf in the loop formed by the rod and the rails. [2 marks]

    2. Calculate the induced current in the loop at this point in time. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. To the right on the ammeter/counter clockwise on the loop; $\checkmark$

    As the rod slides down the total area and therefore the magnetic flux decreases, «According to Lenz' law» an emf/a current is induced in such a direction to oppose the change in magnetic flux. OWTTE $\checkmark$

2. The magnitude of the current is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux / rate of change in the closed area of the loop,

    The rate of change in the closed area of the loop is proportional to the speed of the rod; $\checkmark$

    As the rod falls, its speed increases therefore the induced current increases  $\checkmark$

    As the rod falls, there is an increasing upwards force due to the interaction of the induced current with the magnetic field. $\checkmark$

    Therefore the rod experiences decreasing acceleration, so the rate at which the current increases decreases with time $\checkmark$

    Eventually the force of gravity is balanced by the upwards electromagnetic force, resulting in constant velocity and constant current  $\checkmark$
