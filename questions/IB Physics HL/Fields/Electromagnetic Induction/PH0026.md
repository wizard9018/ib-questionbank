# PH0026

A conducting rod with a length of 0.20 m is placed on two parallel conducting rails $KL$ and $MN$ as shown.

![PH0026](images/PH0026.png)

The uniform magnetic field (into the page) is normal to the plane of the conducting rails and has a magnitude of 5.0 T. A resistance of $1.5\, \mathrm{\Omega}$ is connected between the ends L and N of the rails. The copper rod moves at a constant speed of $3.0\, \ce{m \,s^{-1}}$ in the direction shown. What is the magnitude and the direction of induced current passing through the resistor?

$\\$

| | Magnitude of the current | Current direction through the resistor |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| 2.0 A | up |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 5.0 A | up |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 2.0 A | down |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 5.0 A | down |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Induced emf, $ \varepsilon=BvL\sin\theta $, where$\ \theta={90}\degree $

- $\varepsilon=\left(5.0\right)\left(3.0\right)\left(0.20\right)$

Induced current,

- $
I=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{R}\ \ \ \ \ ;\ \ \ I=\dfrac{\left(5.0\right)\left(3.0\right)\left(0.20\right)}{1.5}=2.0$ A

According to Lenz' law, as the rod moves, the magnetic flux through the loop decreases. Therefore the induced current in the clockwise direction on the loop opposes the change in flux by creating a magnetic field into the page. That is current passing through the resistor in the direction of down.
