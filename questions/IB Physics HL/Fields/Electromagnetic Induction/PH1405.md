# PH1405

**Maximum mark: 9**

1. State what is meant by magnetic flux density. [2 marks]

2. A metal framed window $ABCD$ with dimensions $1.2\,$m$\ \times \ 0.8\,$m is positioned such that, when closed, it is perpendicular to the horizontal component of the Earth's magnetic field with magnetic flux density $1.8\times10^{-5}\,$T.

    ![Screenshot 2024-11-28 at 11.48.11 AM](<images/Screenshot 2024-11-28 at 11.48.11 AM.JPEG>)

    1.    From the closed position, the window is then opened an angle of $30\degree$ using hinges connected to side $AD$. Calculate the magnetic flux passing through the frame at this time. [2 marks]

    2. The window is now subject to a periodic breeze that causes it to move back and forth in oscillatory motion. Explain how this oscillatory motion affects the magnetic flux through the frame, and describe how it results in the induction of an electromotive force ($emf$) and current in the frame. [3 marks]

3. Faraday's law of induction involves a rate of change connecting two variables. Explain an example from another area of physics where a rate of change connects two variables. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The force acting per unit current per unit length on a current-carrying conductor (wire) $\checkmark$

    Placed **perpendicular** / **at right angles** to the magnetic field $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    This is derived from the equation

    - $F=BIL\sin \theta$

    which rearranges to give

    - $B=\dfrac{F}{IL\sin\theta}$

     where $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $F$ is the force acting, $I$ is the current through the conductor, $L$ is the length of the conducting wire, and $\theta$ is the angle between the conductor and the magnetic field.

     When $\theta=90\degree$, $\sin\theta=1$ so the equation simplifies to $B=\dfrac{F}{IL}$.
2. 1.  ::indent
        - $A=0.8\times1.2=0.96\,\ce{m}^2$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Phi=BA\cos\theta=(1.8\times10^{-5})(0.96)\cos(30\degree)=1.50\times10^{-5}\,\ce{Wb}=15.0\,\mu \ce{Wb}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Assuming that the whole window is cutting the Earth's magnetic field lines, the cross-sectional area is equal to the area enclosed by the window frame:

        - $A=0.8\times1.2=0.96\,\ce{m}^2$

        The value of the magnetic flux density and the angle is given in the question and must be used with the given equation $\Phi=BA\cos\theta$.

        By substitution into the equation,

        - $\Phi=BA\cos\theta=(1.8\times10^{-5})(0.96)\cos(30 \degree)$

        The raw answer $\Phi=1.496\times10^{-5}$ which, to 3 significant figures, is $\Phi=1.50\times10^{-5}\,\ce{Wb}$, which can also be expressed using a prefix as $\Phi=1.50\,\mu \ce{Wb}$.

    2. As the window oscillates, the angle $\theta$ between the magnetic field and the normal to the window frame changes periodically. $\checkmark$

        The magnetic flux $\Phi=BA\cos\theta$ varies sinusoidally as $\theta$ changes. $\checkmark$

        According to Faraday's law of induction, a changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force ($emf$) in the frame. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The window's oscillatory motion means that the angle $\theta$ varies with time, typically in a sinusoidal manner if the motion is harmonic.

        As $\theta$ changes, the cosine of the angle $\theta$ changes, causing the magnetic flux through the window to vary sinusoidally.

3. One mark to be awarded for each identified area of physics with an example from those provided. Possible answers include:

    Kinematics:

    - - Velocity is the rate of change of displacement.
    - - Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

    Thermodynamics:

    - - The rate of change of temperature in heat transfer processes.
    - - How temperature gradients drive heat flow.

    Electric Circuits:

    - - Rate of change of current in inductive circuits.
    - - Relationship between voltage and the rate of change of current.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    In **kinematics**, velocity and acceleration are fundamental quantities that describe motion and its changes, establishing the relationship between displacement, velocity, and acceleration.

    In **thermodynamics**, temperature changes drive heat transfer, linking the rate of temperature change to energy flow and thermal equilibrium.

    In **electric circuits**, the inductive reactance depends on the rate of change of current, demonstrating how changing currents produce varying magnetic fields and influence circuit behaviour.

    Note that other answers and examples that involve rate of change would also be awarded marks here.
