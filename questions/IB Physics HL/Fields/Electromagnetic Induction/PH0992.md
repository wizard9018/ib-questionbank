# PH0992

A magnet is suspended by string. A conducting ring is placed horizontally such that its center is directly above the magnet. Point $A$ lies on the ring. This is illustrated below.

![image](images/image.png)

The ring is released from rest and does not rotate as it falls.

Which row correctly shows the position of the ring relative to the magnet and the direction of the current at point $A$ when the emf in the ring is maximum?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Position of the ring$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Direction of current at point $A$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Above the magnet |Into the page |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Above the magnet |  Out of the page |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Below the magnet | Into the page |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Below the magnet | Out of the page |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

- $\varepsilon=Blv$

Length of the coil $l$ does not change. Magnetic flux density $B$ will change with distance from the magnet but its magnitude will be symmetric above and below the magnet. Relative velocity of coil and field lines $v$ (velocity of ring) will increase as the coil falls.

So emf $\epsilon$ will be greatest when the ring is below the magnet.

Below is a diagram showing the magnetic field and the ring below the magnet.

![image](images/image.png)

Here are two ways it can be deduced that the current at point $A$ will be into the page:

1. Lenz's law states that induced emf/current acts in such a direction to produce effects to oppose the change causing it. As the ring falls the upward flux inside the ring due to the external field becomes weaker (because the ring is getting further away from the magnet). An emf is induced to restore this upward flux. Using the right-hand rule tells us that this corresponds to a current flowing into the page at $A$.

2. When a current is induced inside the ring, the field created by the current around the ring will resemble that of a bar magnet. Energy is conserved, so work must be done against magnetic force to pull the magnet and ring away from each other. The field of the ring has a north pole at the top. Again, by the right-hand rule this corresponds to a current flowing into the page at $A$.
