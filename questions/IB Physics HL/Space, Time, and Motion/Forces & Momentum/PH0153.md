# PH0153

Block $A$ of mass $m_A=3.0$ kg rests above the flat horizontal surface of block $B$ of mass $m_B=6.0$ kg, which in turn rests on a smooth horizontal surface as shown.

![Screenshot 2026-01-21 at 1.48.01 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-21 at 1.48.01 PM.png>)

The coefficients of static and kinetic friction between $A$ and $B$ are $\mu_s=0.15$ and $\mu_k=0.1$ respectively. A horizontal force of $F=7.0$ N is applied towards the right on block $A$. Assuming acceleration due to gravity $g \approx 10$ m s$^{-2}$, what is the acceleration of block $B$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}   \dfrac{1}{2}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{3}{4}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{7}{9}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{7}{6}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

If the blocks were to accelerate together with no slipping, the acceleration of the system would be

- $a=\dfrac{F_{net}}{m}=\dfrac{7}{(3+6)}=\dfrac{7}{9}$

For this to occur, the frictional force that pulls block $B$ along would need to provide the net force. Solving for this force:

- $F_{net}=ma=(6)(\dfrac{7}{9})=4.7$ N

The normal contact force on $A$, $F_{N(A)}=m_A g=(3.0$ kg) (10 m s$^{-2})=30$ N

(Contact force from $B$ supports the weight of $A$).

The maximum friction on between $A$ and $B$ is $F_{s}=\mu_s F_{N(A)}=(0.15)(30$ N) = 4.5 N

The static friction force is not enough to accelerate the bottom block at this rate. Therefore block $A$ slides across block $B$

Therefore, block $A$ slides on block $B$, reducing the final frictional force between the blocks to kinetic friction, $F_k=\mu_k F_{N(A)}=(0.1)(30)$ N = 3.0 N

Using Newton's second law, the acceleration of $B$, $a_B=\dfrac{F_{net}}{m_B} = \dfrac{F_k}{m_B} = \dfrac{3.0\textrm{ N}}{6.0\textrm{ kg}}=\dfrac{1}{2}$
