## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ Area $=\Delta p =\dfrac{1}{2}(0.04)F_{max}$ $\checkmark$

    - $\Delta p=2.32$ kg m s$^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    - $F_{max}=\dfrac{2.32}{0.02}=$ :answer[$116$ N] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The area underneath the force time graph is equal to the impulse of the collision. Impulse is also equal to change in momentum $\Delta p$.

    The area under the graph is given by the area of the triangle:

    - Area $=\dfrac{1}{2}bh=\dfrac{1}{2}(0.04)(F_{max})=0.02F_{max}$

    The mass of the ball remains constant and the change in velocity is given by:

    - $\Delta v=v-u=25-(-15)=40$ m s$^{-1}$

    Here we have assigned a negative sign to the initial velocity as the directions of the velocities are opposite. The change is momentum given by:

    - $\Delta p=m\Delta v=(0.058)(40)=2.32$ kg m s$^{-1}$

    Therefore, we have:

    - $2.32=0.02F_{max}$

    - $F_{max}=\dfrac{2.32}{0.02}=116$ N

2. 1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $t=\dfrac{9.9}{25\cos(7.0)}=0.399$ s $\checkmark$

        - $h=25\sin(7.0)(0.399)-\dfrac{1}{2}(9.8)(0.399)^2+0.55$ $\checkmark$

        - $h=0.99\textrm{ m}\gt0.93\textrm{ m}$ (allow 2sf)

        Or

        - $\Delta h=0.44\textrm{ m}\gt0.38\textrm{ m}$

        Therefore, the ball will pass over the net. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Resolving the velocity into horizontal and vertical components we have:

        - $v_h=25\cos(7.0)$

        - $v_v=25\sin(7.0)$

        ![ph1845-1abi](images/ph1845-1abi.png)

        In the horizontal direction, acceleration is zero, so the time taken for the ball to travel a horizontal distance of 9.9 m is given by:

        - $t=\dfrac{9.9}{25\cos(7.0)}=0.399$ s

        In the vertical direction we have a constant acceleration of $g=-9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ (taking upwards as the positive direction).

        We can therefore use the following suvat equation to determine the vertical distance travelled by the ball during the 0.399 s.

        - $s=ut+\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$

        - $\Delta h=25\sin(7.0)(0.399)+\dfrac{1}{2}(-9.8)(0.399)^2=0.436$ m

        The initial height of the ball from the ground was 0.55 m so the total height of the ball is given by:

        - $h=0.55+0.436=0.986$ m

        - $0.986\gt0.93$

        The height reached by the center of mass of the ball is greater than the height of the net, therefore the net will be cleared.

    2. Height of the ball will be lower due to air resistance

        Ball may not / will likely not clear the net OWTTE $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Air resistance causes a resistive force on the ball in both the horizontal and vertical directions. Therefore, the vertical kinetic energy of the ball is now being converted into work done against drag as well as the gravitational force. As a result, the ball will reach a lower maximum height and will not travel as far horizontally.

    2. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $T\cos\theta=mg$ $\checkmark$

        - $T\sin\theta=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ $\checkmark$

        - $\tan\theta=\dfrac{v^2}{gr}$ (note this alone scores 2 marks)

        - $\theta=\left(\dfrac{4.1^2}{(9.8)(1.3)}\right)=$ :answer[$53\degree$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Resolving the tension $T$ into its horizontal and vertical components we have:

        ![ph1845-1acii](images/ph1845-1acii.png)

        There is no acceleration in the vertical plane, so the net vertical force is zero. Therefore, we have:

        - $T\cos\theta=$ weight

        - $T\cos\theta=mg$ [1]

        The acceleration and hence the net force is directed towards the center of the circle. The net force is therefore $T\sin\theta$.

        - $T\sin\theta=ma$

        The ball is moving in a circle as acceleration is given by $a=\dfrac{v^2}{r}$. Therefore:

        - $T\sin\theta=\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ [2]

        Dividing equation [2] by equation [1] we have:

        - $\dfrac{T\sin\theta}{T\cos\theta}=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{mv^2}{r}\right)}{mg}$

        - $\tan\theta=\dfrac{v^2}{gr}$

        Substituting in our values we have:

        - $\theta=\left(\dfrac{4.1^2}{(9.8)(1.3)}\right)=52.8\degree$
