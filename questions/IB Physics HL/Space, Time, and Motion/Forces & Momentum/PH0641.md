# PH0641

**Maximum mark: 12**

A washing machine contains a cylinder that carries clothes and rotates at constant angular speed about a horizontal axis as shown in the diagram. Frictional forces act between the clothes and the inner surface of the cylinder.

![Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.24.54 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.24.54 AM.png>)

The radius of the cylinder is 0.27 m and the linear speed of its inner surface is 1.60 m s$^{-1}$.

1.  Draw the free body diagram for the piece of clothing at the position shown. [2 marks]

2.  The cylinder continues turning until the piece of clothing is at a position that makes an angle of $\phi$ to the horizontal as shown.

    ![Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.25.25 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.25.25 AM.png>)

    $\\$
    1.  With use of a free body diagram, show that the centripetal force on the piece of clothing at this instant is given by the expression $F_c = F_N + mg\sin\phi$. [2 marks]

    2.  Calculate the angular speed, expressing your answer to the correct number of significant figures. [2 marks]

    3.  Show that the angle $\phi$ at which the piece of clothing loses contact with the cylinder is $75\degree$. Assume the clothing does not slip relative to the cylinder. [3 marks]

    4.  Determine the time taken for a piece of clothing to move from the lowest point of the cylinder (point A) to the point where it loses contact. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  :::center

    ![Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.26.55 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.26.55 AM.png>)

    Weight vertically down **and** normal force towards the centre $\checkmark$\
    Friction vertically upwards, roughly the same length as the weight $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Weight always acts downwards and the normal force acts perpendicular to the surface. The speed is constant, so the only acceleration is the centripetal acceleration, which is horizontal at this position. There is therefore no vertical resultant force, so friction must act upwards and be equal in size to the weight.

2.  1.  :::center

        ![Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.26.59 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-11-04 at 8.26.59 AM.png>)

        Force diagram showing weight vertically down, normal force towards the centre and friction along the tangent $\checkmark$\
        Component of weight towards the centre $= mg\sin\phi$, **and** $F_N + mg\sin\phi = F_c$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The normal force always acts perpendicular to the surface and towards the centre, friction parallel to the surface and gravitational force down. Because friction has no component towards the centre, it does not contribute to the centripetal force. The component of the gravitational force that points towards the centre does contribute to the centripetal force. This can be found from $mg\sin\phi$. Adding this to the normal force gives the centripetal force.

    2.  ::indent
        - $\omega = \ll\dfrac{v}{r}\gg$

        - $\omega = \dfrac{1.60}{0.27} =$ :answer[$5.9$ $\ll$rad s$^{-1}$$\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\\$

        2 significant figures $\checkmark$

    3.  Normal force is zero $\checkmark$

        - $\sin\phi = \ll\dfrac{v^2}{rg}\gg = \dfrac{(1.60)^2}{(0.27)(9.8)}$ $\checkmark$

        - $\phi =$ :answer[$75.4\degree$] $\approx 75\degree$ $\checkmark$

        $\\$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When the piece of clothing loses contact, the normal force of the cylinder on the clothing is zero.

        The centripetal force in this case is only the component of the gravitational force towards the centre of the circular path.

        - $mg\sin\phi = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

        - $\sin\phi = \dfrac{mv^2}{mrg}$

        Then

        - $\phi = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{v^2}{rg}\right) = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{(1.60)^2}{(0.27)(9.8)}\right) = 75.4\degree$

        $\\$

    4.  The angular displacement is 2.9 rad $\checkmark$

        - $t = \ll\dfrac{\theta}{\omega}\gg = \dfrac{2.9}{5.9}$ $\checkmark$

        - $t =$ :answer[$0.49$ $\ll$s$\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\\$

        Allow ECF for use of $\omega$ value from (b)(ii)

        $\\$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Angular displacement is $90\degree + 75\degree = 165\degree$

        In radians, this gives

        - $\dfrac{(165)(\pi)}{180} = 2.9$ rad

        - $t = \dfrac{\theta}{\omega} = \dfrac{2.9}{5.9}$

        - $t = 0.49$ s

        $\\$
