# PH0614

**Maximum mark: 12**

1. 1. A spacecraft is moving in a zero gravity zone in space with constant speed. Its pilot decided to accelerate by burning fuel. The burned fuel is ejected backwards from the rocket with a constant speed of $2.5\times{10}^2$ m s$^{-1}$ relative to the rocket at a constant rate of $2.0\times{10}^2$ kg s$^{-1}$.

        Show that the net force on the rocket due to the ejection of burnt fuel is $5 \times 10^4$ N. [1 mark]

    2. The spacecraft has a mass of $4.2\times{10}^3$ kg at the instant that it starts to burn the fuel. Calculate the acceleration of the spacecraft after $1.0$ s has passed. [2 marks]

2.  The spacecraft enters the atmosphere of Mars and slows down due to friction. To decelerate its speed more to reach a safe landing speed, the operator runs a propeller that pushes against the gas in the atmosphere.

    1. By referring to Newton's Third Law, outline why there is a force acting on the spacecraft due to the propeller. [2 marks]

    2.     The average density of the Mars atmosphere is $0.020$ kg m$^{-3}$. The radius of the propeller blades is $1.2$ m.  At a given instant, the average force from the propeller to slow down the spacecraft is $1.4$ kN.

        Assuming that the propeller causes all gas that it passes through to come to rest relative to the falling spaceship, estimate the speed of the spaceship at this instant. [4 marks]

    3.  The landing legs of the spacecraft have springs that can compress as the spacecraft makes contact with the ground. Explain how this decreases the average force acting on the spacecraft as it lands. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. $F=\dfrac{\Delta(mv)}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} v = (2.0\times{10}^2\ \ )(2.5\times{10}^2) = 5.0 \times 10^4$ << N >>$\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    From Newton's 3rd Law, the force of the rocket on the gases is equal to the force of the gases on the rocket.

        For the gases, the net force is equal to the rate of change of momentum.

        Substituting in the values will lead the net force

        $\begin{aligned}
    F&=\dfrac{\Delta(mv)}{\Delta t}\\
     &= \dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} v \\
     &= (2.0\times{10}^2\ \ )(2.5\times{10}^2) \\
     &= 5.0 \times 10^4\textrm{ N}\\
     \end{aligned}$

    2. Mass of the rocket at $t=1$ s is

        - $4200-\left(2.0\times{10}^2\right)\left(1.0\right)=4.0\times{10}^3$ kg $\checkmark$

        - $a=\dfrac{F_{net}}{m}=\dfrac{5\times{10}^4}{4.0\times{10}^3} = $ :answer[$12.5$ m s$^{-2}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The total mass $m_t$ of the rocket after $1$ s:

        - $m_t=4200-\left(2.0\times{10}^2\right)\times\left(1.0\right)=4.0\times{10}^3$ kg

        To find the acceleration of the rocket:

        - $a=\dfrac{F_{net}}{m}=\dfrac{5\times{10}^4}{4.0\times{10}^3}=12.5$ m s$^{-2}$

2. 1. Propeller exerts a force on the gas $\checkmark$

        There is an equal and opposite force from the gas on the propeller $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The gas in the atmosphere experiences a force due to the propeller blades. According to Newton's Third Law, a force with the same magnitude and opposite direction acts on the spacecraft.

    2. $F_{av}=\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}{} = m\dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\, \checkmark$

        $m=\rho V=\rho Al=\rho\pi r^2vt \, \checkmark$

        $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{F_{av}}{\rho\pi r^2}}\ \, \checkmark$

        = :answer[$120$ m s$^{-1}$] $\, \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The distance $l$ covered by the spacecraft in a short time of $t$:

        $l=vt$   where $v$ is the average speed of the spacecraft

        The mass $m$ of the gas pushed during the time of $t$:

        $m=\rho V=\rho Al=\rho\pi r^2vt$ where $r$ is the radius of the blades

        The mass experiences a change of velocity of $v$, as relative to the aircraft it is initially moving at speed $v$ and comes to rest due to the propellers.

        The average force $F_{av}$ needed from the propeller;

        $\begin{aligned}
        F_{av}&=\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\\
        \hspace{1.3cm}\\
        &=\dfrac{m\Delta v}{\Delta t}\\
        \hspace{1.3cm}\\
        &= \dfrac{\rhoπr^2vt\Delta v}{\Delta t}\\
        \hspace{1.3cm}\\
        &=\rhoπr^2v^2\\
        \end{aligned}$

        Rearranging gives

        $\hspace{1.12cm}v=\sqrt{\dfrac{F_{av}}{\rho\pi r^2}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{1.4\times{10}^3}{(0.020)\pi{(1.2)}^2}}=120$ m s$^{-1}$

    3. $\Delta p=F_{av}\Delta t$   $\, \checkmark$

        duration of the collision is increased $\, \checkmark$

        Change in momentum is the same so the average force is less $\, \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The time of collision is increased with using soft material.  By using Newton’s second law;

        - $F_{av}=\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$

        For the same magnitude of the change in momentum, the average force acting on the object is less when the time of collision is increased.
