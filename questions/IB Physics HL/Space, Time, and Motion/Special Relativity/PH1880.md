# PH1880

Two inertial reference frames $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma'$ are coincident at $x=x'=0$, $ct=ct'=0$.

According to an observer in $\Sigma$, two flashes of light are separated by a distance $x=3.0$ km and a time $\Delta t$ where $c\Delta t=5.0$  km. According to an observer in $\Sigma'$ the two flashes occur at the same position.

What is the time interval $c\Delta t'$ between the flashes in frame $\Sigma'$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 2.0 km

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 4.0 km

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 5.8 km

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 8.0 km

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The spacetime interval given by:

- $\Delta s^2=(c\Delta t)^2-(\Delta s)^2$

is an invariant quantity, meaning that it is the same in all inertial reference frames.

Therefore, we have:

- $(c\Delta t)^2-(\Delta x)^2=(c\Delta t')^2-(\Delta x')^2$

From the question, we have $c\Delta t$ and $\Delta x$ and we can infer that $\Delta x' = 0$ because the events occur at the same location in that frame. We can now substitute into the equation.

- $\begin{aligned}
\ (c\Delta t)^2-(\Delta x)^2&=(c\Delta t')^2-(\Delta x')^2\\
\\\
\ (5)^2-(3)^2&=(c\Delta t')^2-(0)^2\\
\\\
\ 16&=(c\Delta t')^2\\
\\\
\ c\Delta t'&=4.0\textrm{ km}\\
\end{aligned}$
