# PH0671

**Maximum mark: 13**

1. Car **A** and Car **B** are travelling in the same direction along a straight line. In the reference frame of a stationary observer on the ground, Car **A** has a speed of 24 m s$^{-1}$ and Car **B** has a speed of 36 m s$^{-1}$.

    ![Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 4.03.33 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 4.03.33 PM.png>)

    1. Explain what is meant by a frame of reference. [2 marks]

    2. Calculate the velocity of Car **B** relative to the observer in Car **A**. [2 marks]

    3. The distance travelled by Car **B** relative to Car **A** is 60 m in a time interval of 5.0 s. Use the Galilean transformation equation to calculate the distance travelled by Car **B** relative to the stationary observer on the ground for the same time interval. [2 marks]

2. Now consider two spaceships, **C** and **D**, traveling through space towards a planet.

    ![Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 4.04.42 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 4.04.42 PM.png>)

    Both spaceships turn on their lights. The turning on of the lights of spaceship **C** and the turning on of the lights of spaceship **D** are defined as Events **C** and **D** respectively. Event **C** has coordinates $x_C = 3.456 \times {10}^5$ m, $t_C=0$ s and event **D** has coordinates $x_D = 3.443 \times {10}^5$ m,  $t_D = 1.1 \times {10}^{-3}$ s according to a stationary observer on the planet.

    A rocket flies towards the two spaceships at a speed of $0.85\ c$ according to the same observer.

    1. According to the frame of reference of the rocket, determine the space differences between events **C** and **D**. [3 marks]

    2. According to the frame of reference of the rocket, determine the space-time interval between events **C** and **D**. [2 marks]

    3. Discuss whether Event **C** and Event **D** can occur at the same time according to some other inertial frame. [2 marks]

    2.  ::indent
        - $u^\prime=36-24$ $\checkmark$

        - :answer[12 m s$^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        By using the velocity addition equation for the Galilean transformation:

        - $u^\prime=u-v$

        - $u^\prime=36-24$

        - $u^\prime=12\textrm{ m s}^{-1}$

    3. $\hspace{1.8em}\Delta x=60+(24)(5.0)$ $\checkmark$

        - $\hspace{1.1em}$ = :answer[180 m] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
By using the Galilean transformation:

        - $\Delta x^\prime=\Delta x-v\Delta t$,

        where $\Delta x$ is the distance travelled by Car **B** relative to the stationary observer and $v$ is the speed of the reference frame (in this case Car **A**) that we are transforming out of.

        - $60=\Delta x-(24)(5.0)$

        - $\Delta x=60+120=180$ m

2. 1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $\gamma=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{{(0.85)}^2c^2}{c^2}}}=1.9$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta x=(3.456\times 10^5)-(3.443\times 10^5)=1.3\times 10^3$ m $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta x^\prime = 1.9 (1.3\times 10^{3} - \left(0.85\right)\left(3.0\times10^8\right)\left(1.1\times10^{-3}\right))=$ :answer[$-5.3\times {10}^5$ m] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The Lorentz factor $\gamma$:

        - $\gamma=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{{(0.85)}^2c^2}{c^2}}}=1.9$

        Relative to the ground, space difference $\Delta x$:

        - $\Delta x = (3.456 \times 10^5)-(3.443 \times {10}^{5})=0.013 \times {10}^{5} = 1.3 \times  10^3$ m

        Space Difference $\Delta x^\prime$ can be calculated by Lorentz\'s transformation:

        - $\Delta x^\prime=\gamma(\Delta x-v\Delta t)$

        - $\Delta x^\prime=1.9(1.3\times 10^3-\left(0.85\right)\left(3.0\times10^{8}\right)\left(1.1\times{10^{-3}}\right))$

        - $\Delta x^\prime=-5.3\times 10^5$ m

    2.  Use of $\Delta x = 1.3 \times 10^3$ m and $\Delta t = 1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ s $\checkmark$

        - $(\Delta s)^2 = (3.0 \times 10^8)(1.1 \times 10^{-3}))^2 - (1.3 \times 10^3) ^2 = 1.09 \times 10^{11} \ \ce{m}^2 \checkmark$

        Note use of values for the rocket frame may lead to an answer of $1.16 \times 10^{11} \ \ce{m}^2$ if rounded values are used. Full marks would be awarded

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        For the frame of reference of the planet, the space-time interval $\Delta s^\prime$

        - $(\Delta s^\prime)^2 = (c\Delta t)^2 - (\Delta x)^2$

        - $(\Delta s^\prime)^2 = ((3.0 \times 10^8)(1.1 \times 10^{-3}))^2 - (1.3 \times 10^3)^2$

        - $(\Delta s^\prime)^2 = 1.09 \times 10^{11}$ m$^2$

        Alternatively, for the rocket frame

        Time Difference $\Delta t^\prime$ can be calculated by Lorentz\'s transformation:

        - $\Delta t^\prime = \gamma(\Delta t - v \dfrac{\Delta x}{c^2})$

        - $\Delta t^\prime = 1.9\left(1.1 \times 10^{-3} - (0.85)(\dfrac{1.3 \times 10^3}{(3.0 \times 10^8)^2})\right)$

        - $\Delta t^\prime = 2.1 \times 10^{-3}$ s

        The space-time interval $\Delta s^\prime$

        - $(\Delta s^\prime)^2 = (c\Delta t)^2 - (\Delta x)^2$

        - $(\Delta s^\prime)^2 = ((3.0 \times 10^8)(2.1 \times 10^{-3}))^2 - (-5.3 \times 10^5)^2$

        - $(\Delta s^\prime)^2 = 1.16 \times 10^{11}$ m$^2$

    3. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $(\Delta s)^2 = 0^2 - (\Delta x)^2 = -(\Delta x)^2$ $\checkmark$

        - $(\Delta s)^2$ is not negative **and** no $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $(\Delta s)^2 = 0^2 - (\Delta x)^2 = -(\Delta x)^2$

        In a particular frame $\Delta t = 0$:

        - $(\Delta s)^2 = 0^2 - (\Delta x)^2 = -(\Delta x)^2$

        $(\Delta s)^2$ can not be negative. Therefore there is no such inertial reference frame
