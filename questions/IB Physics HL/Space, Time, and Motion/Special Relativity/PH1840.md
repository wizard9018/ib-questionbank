# PH1840

**Maximum mark: 7**

Two events, $A$ and $B$, occur in an inertial frame of reference. Event $A$ is the emission of a pulse of light from a spaceship at $x=120$ m. Event $B$ is emission of a pulse of light from a different space ship 4 $\mu$s later at position $x=750$ m.

1.    State what is meant by the space-time interval and explain what it means for this interval to be invariant. [2 marks]

2. Calculate the space-time interval for the two events in (a). [2 marks]

3. On the space-time diagram below, plot and label the events $A$ and $B$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $(\Delta s)^2=c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2$ $\checkmark$

    Has the same value in all inertial frames of reference OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The space-time interval is a quantity defined by the equation:

    - $(\Delta s)^2=c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2$

    where $\Delta t$ is the time between events, and $\Delta x$ is the spatial separation between events.

    The space-time interval, $s^2$, is invariant, which means it has the same value in all inertial frames of reference. Even though observers in different frames may measure different space and time coordinates and are moving at different speeds relative to each other, they will all calculate the same spacetime interval between two events.

2. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $(\Delta s)^2=c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2=(3\times10^8)^2(4\times10^{-6})^2-630^2$ $\checkmark$

    - $(\Delta s)^2= 1.04\times10^6 \ll$ m$^2\gg$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To calculate the space-time interval, we use the equation:

    - $(\Delta s)^2=c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2$

    For the two events:

    - $\Delta t=4\times10^{-6}$ s

    - $\Delta x=x_B-x_A=750-120=630$ m

    Substituting the values:

    - $(\Delta s)^2=(3\times10^8)^2(4\times10^{-6})^2-630^2=1.04\times10^6$

3. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $ct=(3\times10^8)(4\times10^{-6})=1200$ m $\checkmark$

    - $A=(x=120, ct=0)$ $\checkmark$

    - $B=(x=750, ct=1200)$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To find the y-coordinate for event $B$, we need to calculate $ct$:

    - $ct=(3\times10^8)(4\times10^{-6})=1200$ m

    Therefore, the correct x- and y-values for events $A$ and $B$ are:

    - $A=(x=120, ct=0)$

    - $B=(x=750, ct=1200)$

4. No. $\checkmark$

    The line connecting the points has a gradient greater than 1 / the frame of reference where the events would be simultaneous would move faster than the speed of light.  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    On a spacetime diagram, points which are simultaneous in a frame of reference will lie on a line that is parallel to the $x$ axis of that frame of reference.

    On a spacetime diagram, a beam of light has a gradient of 1. Any frame of reference must have a speed less than the speed of light, and this is reflected by a gradient of less than one on the spacetime diagram. The gradient of the line that connects points A and B is

    - $\dfrac{(1200 - 0)}{(750-120)} = 1.9$

    A frame of reference where the $x$ axis passes through both points would therefore have a speed of 1.9c which is not possible according to relativity.
