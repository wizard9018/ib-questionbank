# PH0616

**Maximum mark: 15**

In a tricks competition, a snooker ball of radius $r= $ 2.5 cm is hit horizontally with a cue and rolls without slipping and then rolls up a slope. The mass of the ball is $m=$ 0.16 kg and the moment of inertia of the snooker ball is $\dfrac{2}{5} m r^2$.

![PH0616](images/PH0616.png)

1. The average acceleration of the ball during the strike is 150 m s$^{-2}$. Show that the average net torque applied on the ball during the strike is around 0.24 N m. [3 marks]

2. The contact time of the cue to the ball is 2.5 ms. Show the total kinetic energy of the ball after 2.5 ms is approximately $1.6 \times{10}^{-2}$ J. [4 marks]

3.
    1. When the cue stops applying force on the ball, the ball starts to roll up an incline placed in front of the ball. Calculate the maximum vertical height reached by the ball. Ignore friction and air resistance. [3 marks]

    2. The angle of the incline is 25$\degree$ to the horizontal. What is the minimum coefficient of static friction between the surface and the ball for it to roll down the incline without slipping? [5 marks]

1.  ::indent

    - $I=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2=\dfrac{2}{5}\left(0.16\right)\left(2.5\times{10}^{-2}\right)^2=4.0\times{10}^{-5}$ kg m$^2$ $\checkmark$

    - $\alpha=\dfrac{a}{r}=\dfrac{150}{2.5\times{10}^{-2}}=6000$ rad s$^{-2}$ $\checkmark$

    - $\tau=I\alpha=(4.0\times10^{-5})(6000)=$ :answer[$0.24$ N m] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The moment of inertia $I$ of the snooker ball:

    - $I=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2=\dfrac{2}{5}\left(0.16\right)\left(2.5\times{10}^{-2}\right)^2=4.0\times{10}^{-5}$ kg m$^2$

    Since the ball is not slipping, the average angular acceleration $\alpha$:

    - $\alpha=\dfrac{a}{r}=\dfrac{150}{2.5\times{10}^{-2}}=6000$ rad s$^{-2}$

    The average net torque $\tau$:

    - $\tau=I\alpha=(4.0\times10^{-5})(6000)=0.24$ N m

2. Linear velocity = $at = (150\textrm{ m s}^{-2})(2.5\times{10}^{-3}\textrm{ s})=0.375$ m s$^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    Translational kinetic energy = $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(0.16\right)\left({0.375}^2\right)=1.13\times{10}^{-2}$ J $\checkmark$

    Rotational kinetic energy= $\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(4.0\times{10}^{-5}\right)\left({15}^2\right)=4.5\times{10}^{-3}$ J $\checkmark$

    $(1.13\times{10}^{-2})+(4.5\times{10}^{-3})=$ :answer[$1.6\times{10}^{-2}$ J] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The linear velocity $v$ of the ball after 2.5 ms:

    - $v = at = \left(150\textrm{ m s}^{-2}\right)\left(2.5\times{10}^{-3}\textrm{ s}\right)=0.375$ m s$^{-1}$

    The translational kinetic energy $E_{Kt}$:

    - $E_{Kt}=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(0.16\right)\left({0.375}^2\right)=1.13\times{10}^{-2}$ J

    The angular velocity $\omega$ of the ball after 2.5 ms:

    - $\omega=\dfrac{v}{r}=\dfrac{0.375\ }{2.5\times{10}^{-2}}=15$ rad s$^{-1}$

    The rotational kinetic energy $E_{kr}$:

    - $E_{Kr}=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(4.0\times{10}^{-5}\right)\left({15}^2\right)=4.5\times{10}^{-3}$ J

    The total kinetic energy $E_K$:

    - $E_K=1.13\times{10}^{-2}+4.5\times{10}^{-3}=1.6\times{10}^{-2}$ J

3. 1. Any evidence of using conservation of energy $\checkmark$

        vertical displacement = $\dfrac{1.6\times{10}^{-2}}{(0.16)(9.81)}$ $\checkmark$

       $\hspace{3.3cm}=$ :answer[$1.0\times{10}^{-2}$ m] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Due to the conservation of energy, the decrease in kinetic energy $E_K$ of the ball equals to increase in the gravitational potential energy of the ball $E_p$:

        - $E_p=E_K$

        - $mgh=E_K$

        - $h=\dfrac{E_K}{mg}=\dfrac{1.6\times{10}^{-2}}{(0.16)(9.81)}=1.0\times{10}^{-2}$ m

    2. ::indent
        - $\tau=rF_F=\mu mgr\cos(25\degree)$ $\checkmark$

        - $\alpha=\dfrac{\tau}{I}=\dfrac{\mu mgr\cos(25\degree)}{\frac{2}{5}mr^2}=\dfrac{5\mu g\cos({25}\degree)}{2r}$ $\checkmark$

        - $a=\dfrac{F_{net}}{m}=\dfrac{mg\sin(25\degree)-\mu mg\cos(25\degree)}{m}$ $\checkmark$

        - $a=\alpha r=\dfrac{5\mu g\cos(25\degree)}{2}$ $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{mg\sin(25\degree)-\mu mg\cos(25\degree)}{m}=\dfrac{5\mu g\cos(25\degree)}{2}$

        - $\mu=\dfrac{2}{7}\tan(25\degree)=$ :answer[$0.13$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The friction force on the ball when it rolls without slipping is given by:

        - $F_{F}=\mu F_{N}=\mu mg\cos(25\degree)$

        The torque provided by friction is given by:

        - $\tau=rF_{F}=\mu mgr\cos(25\degree)$

        And the angular acceleration can be found from:

        - $\alpha=\dfrac{\tau}{I}=\dfrac{\mu mgr\cos(25\degree)}{\frac{2}{5}mr^2}=\dfrac{5\mu g\cos(25\degree)}{2r}$

        The linear acceleration down the slope is found from:

        - $a=\dfrac{F_{net}}{m}=\dfrac{mg\sin(25\degree)-\mu mg\cos(25\degree)}{m}$

        Linear acceleration can also be obtained from angular acceleration:

        - $a=\alpha r=\dfrac{5\mu g\cos(25\degree)}{2}$

        Setting the two equations equal gives:

        - $\dfrac{mg \sin(25\degree)-\mu mg\cos(25\degree)}{m}=\dfrac{5\mu g\cos(25\degree)}{2}$

        - $2\sin(25\degree)-2\mu\cos(25\degree)=5\mu\cos(25\degree)$

        - $2\sin(25\degree)=7\mu\cos(25\degree)$

        - $\mu=\dfrac{2}{7}\tan(25°) = 0.13$
