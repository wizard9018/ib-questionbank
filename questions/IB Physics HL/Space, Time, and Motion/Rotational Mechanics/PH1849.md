# PH1849

**Maximum mark: 9**

A solid spherical ball bearing of mass $m$ and radius $r$ is initially at rest on an inclined plane. It begins rolling without slipping down from a vertical height $h$ and reaches point $X$ having lost no energy to friction. Along the horizontal portion between points $X$ and $Y$, the ball encounters a constant resistive force $F$ opposing its motion.

![ph1849qa](images/ph1849qa.png)

1.  Show that the linear speed of the ball at point X can be expressed as $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{10g(h-r)}{7}}$

    (Moment of inertia of sphere, $I_{sphere}=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$) [4 marks]

2. The height of $h$ is 50 cm, the mass of the ball $m$ is 200 g, and the radius of the ball $r$ is 5 cm.

    1. Calculate the rotational kinetic energy at the point between $X$ and $Y$ where the ball **has lost 50%** of its total kinetic energy. [2 marks]

    2.  On the horizontal section, the ball rolls without slipping and stops at $Y$. The distance between $X$ and $Y$ is 1.57 m. Determine the average angular acceleration of the ball between points $X$ and $Y$ [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $\Delta E_p=E_k$

    - $\Delta E_p=mgh-mgr$ $\checkmark$

    - $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ $\checkmark$

    - $mg(h-r)=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

    Where $I=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$

    and $\omega=\dfrac{v}{r}$ $\checkmark$

    - $mg(h-r)=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)mr^2\dfrac{v^2}{r^2}+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

    - $mg(h-r)=\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2$

    Correct working to arrive at

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{10g(h-r)}{7}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    At the starting point, the sphere is at rest, so its initial energy is purely potential energy.

    Note that the change in height of the center of mass is given by the expression $h-r$ and so the change in gravitational energy can be expressed as

    - $\Delta E_p=mgh-mgr$

    At point $X$, the sphere has both translational kinetic energy (due to its linear velocity) and rotational kinetic energy (due to its rolling motion). The total kinetic energy therefore is

    - $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

    Where $I=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ and $\omega=\dfrac{v}{r}$

    There is no energy lost before point $X$, so we can say that

    - Change in Gravitational Potential Energy = Change in Kinetic Energy

    Equating the two energy terms and substituting in for $I$ and $\omega$ gives

    - $mg(h-r)=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)mr^2\dfrac{v^2}{r^2}+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

    - $mg(h-r)=\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2$

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{10g(h-r)}{7}}$

2. 1. Total $E_k$ at point $X=\Delta E_p=mgh-mgr$

        50% of total $E_k=\dfrac{mg(h-r)}{2}=0.44$ J $\checkmark$

        $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$, where $I=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ and $\omega=\dfrac{v}{r}$

        rotational $E_k=\dfrac{1}{5}mv^2$ and translational $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

        total $E_k=\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2$ $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2=0.44$ J

        So, rotational $KE=\ll\dfrac{1}{5}mv^2\gg=$ :answer[$0.125$ J] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The total kinetic energy at the bottom of the slope is equal to the change in gravitational potential energy.

        - $E_p=mg(h-r)$

        To find the amount of kinetic energy remaining at the location under study we can use the expression

        - $E_k=\dfrac{mg(h-r)}{2}=\dfrac{0.2(9.8)(0.50-0.05)}{2}=0.44$ J

        The total kinetic energy is the sum of the rotational and the translational kinetic energies:

        - $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$, where $I=\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ and $\omega=\dfrac{v}{r}$

        Giving

        - $E_k=\dfrac{1}{5}mv^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2=\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2$

        - $\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2=0.44$ J

        Giving

        - $mv^2=0.628$ J

        And the rotational kinetic energy

        - $E_k=\dfrac{1}{5}mv^2=\dfrac{1}{5}(0.628)=0.125$ J $\checkmark$

        - $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
