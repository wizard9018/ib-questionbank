# PH1066

**Maximum mark: 10**

Two solid cylinders with masses 3.5 kg and 2.0 kg are connected by a rigid rod of length 4.0 m and negligible mass. The system rotates around a perpendicular axis 1.0 m from the bigger mass as shown in the diagram.

![Screenshot 2025-06-03 at 4.36.02 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-06-03 at 4.36.02 PM.png>)

1. 1. Show that the moment of inertia of the system is approximately 22  kg m$^2$. [1 mark]

    2. The system accelerates from rest with an angular acceleration of $4.0$ rad s$^{-2}$.

        Calculate the kinetic energy of the system after $6.0$ s. [3 marks]

2. 1. The 3.5 kg cylinder is removed from the rod and released from rest to roll without slipping on a smooth inclined plane as shown in the diagram.

        ![PH1066_Image2](images/PH1066_Image2.png)

        The moment of inertia of the cylinder is $\dfrac{1}{2}\ mR^2$, where $R$ is the radius of the cylinder.

        Show that the angular velocity of the cylinder at the bottom of the incline is given by $\omega\ =\ \dfrac{2}{R}\ \sqrt{\dfrac{gh}{3}}$ [3 marks]

        2. The mass takes 4.2 s to reach the bottom of the incline. If the radius of the cylinder is 22 cm, and the height of the incline is 1.5 m, calculate the average torque on the cylinder as it rolls down the ramp. [3 marks]

1. 1. $\hspace{1.5em}$ $I\ =\ (3.5)(1)^2\ +\ (2)(3)^2\ =$ :answer[$21.5 \ll\textrm{ kg m}^2 \gg$ ] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $ I\ =\ \sum mr^2$

        - $I\ =\ (3.5)(1)^2\ +\ (2)(3)^2$

        - $I\ =\ 21.5\textrm{ kg m}^2\ $

    2. $\hspace{1.5em}$ $\ll\ \omega_f\ =\ \omega_i\ +\ \alpha t\ \gg$

        - $\omega_f\ =\ 0\ +\ (4)(6)  =$ $24\ \ll\ \ce{rad\ s}^{-1}\ \gg$ $\checkmark$

        - $E_k\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}(21.5)(24)^2$ $\checkmark$

        - $E_k\ =$ :answer[$6200\ \ll\textrm{ J}\ \gg$ ] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $E_k\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2} I \omega^2$

        Since

        - $\omega_f\ =\ \omega_i\ +\ \alpha t$

        - $\omega_f\ =\ 0\ +\ (4)(6)\ =\ 24\textrm{ rad s}^{-1}$

        then

        - $E_k\ =\dfrac{1}{2} (21.5)(24)^2\ =\ 6192\textrm{ J}$

2. 1. $\hspace{1.5em}$ $mgh\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}\ mv^2\ +\ \dfrac{1}{2}\ I \omega^2$ $\checkmark$

        - $v\ =\ \omega r$ $\checkmark$

        Correct work to find the expression $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Since the energy is conserved the loss of gravitational potential energy is equal to the sum of the final rotational and kinetic energies

        - $mgh\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2\ +\ \dfrac{1}{2}I \omega^2$

        and

        - $I\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}mR^2$

        then

        - $mgh\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2\ +\ \dfrac{1}{4}mR^2 \omega^2$

        - $gh\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}v^2\ +\ \dfrac{1}{4}R^2 \omega^2$

        Since

        - $v\ =\ \omega r$

        then

        - $gh\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}R^2 \omega^2\ +\ \dfrac{1}{4}R^2 \omega^2$

        - $gh\ =\ \dfrac{3}{4}R^2 \omega^2$

        - $\omega\ =\ \dfrac{2}{R}\ \sqrt{\dfrac{gh}{3}}$

    2. $\hspace{1em}$ $\omega\ = \dfrac{2}{0.22}\ \sqrt{\dfrac{(9.8)(1.5)}{3}}  =$ $20.1\ \ll\textrm{ rad s}^{-1}\ \gg$  $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $a\ = \dfrac{20.1}{4.2} =$ $4.8\ \ll\textrm{ rad s}^{-2}\ \gg$  $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\tau\ =\ \ll\ \dfrac{1}{2} (3.5) (0.22)^2 (4.8)\ \gg\ =$ :answer[$0.41\ \ll\textrm{ N m} \gg$ ] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        To find the angular speed at the bottom of the incline,

        - $\omega\ =\ \dfrac{2}{R} \sqrt{\dfrac{gh}{3}}$

        - $\omega\ =\ \dfrac{2}{0.22} \sqrt{\dfrac{(9.8)\ (1.5)}{3}}\ =\ 20.1\textrm{ rad s}^{-1}$

        To calculate that angular acceleration,

        - $\omega_f\ =\ \omega_i\ +\ \alpha t$

        - $20.1\ =\ 0\ +\ \alpha(4.2)$

        - $\alpha\ =\ \dfrac{20.1}{4.2}\ =\ 4.8\textrm{ rad s}^{-2}$

        Since

        - $\tau\ =\ I\alpha$

        then

        - $\tau\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2}mR^2\alpha\ =\ \dfrac{1}{2} (3.5) (0.22)^2 (4.8)\ =\ 0.41$ N m
