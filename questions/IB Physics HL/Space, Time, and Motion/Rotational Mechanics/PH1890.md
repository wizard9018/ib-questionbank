# PH1890

A thin uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $r$ spins around a frictionless axis at an angular speed $\omega$. The moment of inertia of the disk is $\dfrac{1}{2}mr^2$.

A small object of mass $\dfrac{m}{2}$ is dropped onto the disk, moving with the disk a distance $\dfrac{r}{2}$ from the center of rotation.

![Screenshot 2025-08-26 at 4.50.40 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-08-26 at 4.50.40 PM.png>)

What is the new angular speed of the system?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3}{5}\omega$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2}{5}\omega$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3}{4}\omega$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4}{5}\omega$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

There are no external torques, so angular momentum is conserved.

- $L_\textrm{initial}=L_\textrm{final}$

The initial angular momentum is given by:

- $L_\textrm{initial}=I_\textrm{initial}\omega_\textrm{initial}=\dfrac{1}{2}mr^2\omega$

The final moment of inertia is given by:

- $I_\textrm{final}=I_\textrm{disk}+I_\textrm{object}$

The object is small, so treating it like a point mass $\dfrac{m}{2}$ with a distance $\dfrac{r}{2}$ away from the axis of rotation, the moment of inertia $I_\textrm{object}$ is given by:

- $I_\textrm{object}=\Sigma mr^2=\left(\dfrac{m}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{r}{2}\right)^2=\left(\dfrac{m}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{r^2}{4}\right)=\dfrac{1}{8}mr^2$

- $I_\textrm{final}=\dfrac{1}{2}mr^2+\dfrac{1}{8}mr^2=\dfrac{5}{8}mr^2$

Using conservation of angular momentum we have:

- $\begin{aligned}
\ L_\textrm{initial}&=L_\textrm{final}\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{1}{2}mr^2\omega&=\dfrac{5}{8}mr^2\omega_\textrm{final}\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{1}{2}\omega&=\dfrac{5}{8}\omega_\textrm{final}\\
\\\
\ 4\omega&=5\omega_\textrm{final}\\
\\\
\ \omega_\textrm{final}&=\dfrac{4}{5}\omega\\
\end{aligned}$
