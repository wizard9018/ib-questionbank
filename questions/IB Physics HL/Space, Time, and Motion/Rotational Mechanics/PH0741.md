# PH0741

A disc of mass $m$ and radius $r$ rolls without slipping from rest down an inclined plane. A static frictional force of $F_f$ acts up the slope.

![Screenshot 2025-06-02 at 10.02.39 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-06-02 at 10.02.39 AM.png>)

The moment of inertia of the disc is $\dfrac{1}{2}mr^2$.

Which of the following is correct about the linear acceleration of the disc and the net torque?

| | Linear Acceleration | Net Torque |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{2}{3}g\sin \theta$  |$F_fr$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{2}{3}g\sin \theta$ | $mg\sin \theta$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $g\sin \theta$|$F_fr$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $g\sin \theta$ | $mg\sin \theta$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

![Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 5.26.04 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-08 at 5.26.04 PM.png>)

Using the centre as the pivot point for analysis, the net torque is due to the friction force only.

- $\tau=F_fr$

According to Newton's second law for rotation

- $\tau=I\alpha$

Then

- $F_fr=\dfrac{1}{2}mr^2\alpha$
- $F_f=\dfrac{1}{2}mr\alpha$

Since

- $a=r\alpha$

Then

- $F_f=\dfrac{1}{2}mr\bigg(\dfrac{a}{r}\bigg)$

- $F_f=\dfrac{1}{2}ma$

According to Newton's second law, the net force on the disc along the incline is given by

- $\sum F=ma$
- $mg\sin\, \theta\ -F_f=ma$

Then

- $mg\sin\, \theta\ -\dfrac{1}{2}ma=ma$
- $g\sin\, \theta =a+\dfrac{1}{2}a$
- $g\sin\, \theta = \dfrac{3}{2}a$
- $a=\dfrac{2}{3}g\sin\, \theta\ $
