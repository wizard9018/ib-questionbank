# PH1850

**Maximum mark: 10**

In the diagram above, there are two pulleys with radii $2r$ and $3r$, and masses $m$ and $3m$ respectively. Two blocks with masses $8m$ and $M$ are suspended and the system is in equilibrium. The pulleys are fixed so that they rotate together and the system can rotate around a fixed axis without friction.

1. Define torque and state the relationship between torque and angular acceleration. [2 marks]

2. The system is in equilibrium. Deduce the mass of $M$ in terms of $m$. [2 marks]

3. The string supporting the block of $M$ is then cut.

    1. Show that the acceleration of the block with mass $8m$ immediately after the string is cut is approximately 8 m s$^{-2}$.

        $\left(I_{pulley}=\dfrac{1}{2}mR^2\right)$ [4 marks]

    2. If the distance $r$ is 7 cm, find the number of revolutions completed by the pulley system during the first 2 seconds. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Torque is a measure of the rotational force/effect acting on an object. $\checkmark$

    OR

    $\tau=Fr\sin\theta$ with all terms defined.

    The greater the torque, the greater the angular acceleration of the object. $\checkmark$

    OR

    $\tau=I\alpha$ with all terms defined.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Torque is defined as the product of the force $F$ applied and the distance r from the pivot point (axis of rotation) to the point where the force is applied, in the direction perpendicular to the line of action of the force.

    Torque is responsible for causing an object to rotate about an axis. The greater the torque, the greater the angular acceleration of the object, which is related by the rotational version of Newton's second law.

2. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $\Sigma\tau=0$

    - $T_1=8mg$ and $\tau_1=8mg(3r)$ (counterclockwise)

    AND

    - $T_2=Mg$ and $\tau_2=Mg(2r)$ (clockwise) $\checkmark$

    - $24mgr=2Mgr$ gives $M=$ :answer[$12m$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Since the system is in equilibrium, the sum of the torques acting on the system must be zero.

    In each string the tension is equal to the force of gravity, so

    - $T_1=8mg$ and $T_2=Mg$

    Substituting each tension distance of application into the torque formula

    Gives a counterclockwise torque of

    - $\tau_1=8mg(3r)$

    And a clockwise torque of

    - $\tau_2=Mg(2r)$

    Setting the opposing torques equal to one another and solving for $M$ gives

    - $24mgr=2Mgr$

    - $M=12m$

3. 1. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $F_{net}=8ma=8mg-T_1$

        and

        - $\tau_{net}=T_13r=I\alpha$ $\checkmark$

        - $I_{total}=\dfrac{1}{2}m(2r)^2+\dfrac{1}{2}(3m)(3r)^2=2mr^2+\dfrac{27}{2}mr^2=\dfrac{31}{2}mr^2$

        and

        - $\alpha=\dfrac{a}{3r}$ $\checkmark$

        - $T_1(3r)=\left(\dfrac{31}{2}mr^2\right)\dfrac{a}{3r}$

        - $T_1=\dfrac{31mr^2a}{18r^2}=\dfrac{31}{18}ma$ $\checkmark$

        - $8ma=8mg-\dfrac{31}{18}ma$

        - $9.7a=8g$

        - $a=\ll\dfrac{8g}{9.7}\gg=$ :answer[8.1 m s$^{-2}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When the string supporting the block with mass $M$ is cut, the block with mass $8m$ begins to move under the influence of gravity but its acceleration is slowed by the tension from the rope attached to the pulley. The net force on the block is given by

        - $F_{net}=8ma=8mg-T_1$

        The net torque on the pulley is from the tension of the rope and this can be expressed in the rotational Newton's second law equation

        - $\tau_{net}=T_1(3r)=I\alpha$

        The combined moment of inertia of the two pulleys is given by

        - $I_{total}=\dfrac{1}{2}m(2r)^2+\dfrac{1}{2}(3m)(3r)^2=2mr^2+\dfrac{27}{2}mr^2=\dfrac{31}{2}mr^2$

        The linear/tangential acceleration and the angular acceleration are related through

        - $\alpha=\dfrac{a}{3r}$

        Substituting the expressions and solving for the tension gives

        - $T_1(3r)=\left(\dfrac{31}{2}mr^2\right)\dfrac{a}{3r}$

        - $T_1=\dfrac{31mr^2a}{18r^2}=\dfrac{31}{18}ma$

        Substituting the tension value into the net force equation for the block gives

        - $8ma=8mg-\dfrac{31}{18}ma$

        Solving for acceleration

        - $9.7a=8g$

        - $a=\ll\dfrac{8g}{9.7}\gg=8.1$ m s$^{-2}$

    2. $\hspace{1.8em}$ $\theta_f=\omega_1 t+\dfrac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$

        $\omega_1=0$, and $\alpha=\dfrac{8.1}{(3)(0.07)}=38.6$ rad s$^{-2}$

        - $\theta_f=\ll\dfrac{38.6}{2}2^2\gg=77$ radians

        - number of revolutions$=\dfrac{77}{2\pi}=$ :answer[12.2] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Using the kinematic equation, the angular displacement is found by considering the angular acceleration and the time. (ECF from (c)(i))

        - $\theta_f=\omega_1 t+\dfrac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$

        The starting angular velocity is zero and the angular acceleration can be found from the linear acceration from the previous question part.

        - $\alpha=\dfrac{8.1}{(3)(0.07)}=38.6$ rad s$^{-2}$

        Substituting into the angular acceleration formula gives the total angular displacement.

        - $\theta_f=\dfrac{38.6}{2}(2^2)=77$ radians

        One full revolution is $2\pi$ radians, so the number of revolutions is given by

        - number of revolutions$=\dfrac{77}{2\pi}=12.2$
