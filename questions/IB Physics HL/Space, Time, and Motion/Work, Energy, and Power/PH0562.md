# PH0562

**Maximum mark: 9**

A cannonball is fired horizontally from a cannon positioned at the edge of a cliff.

![PH0562zzz](images/PH0562zzz.png)

1. In the context of Newton\'s Laws, explain why the cannon recoils to the left when the cannonball is fired. [2 marks]

2. The initial recoil speed of the cannon is $1.0$ m s$^{-1}$ and its mass is 1200 kg.

    1. After the ball is fired, the cannon rolls to the left and experiences a resistive force of $8.0$ kN as it rolls. Determine the distance covered by the cannon. [2 marks]

    2. The mass of the cannonball is $9.0$ kg. Show that the magnitude of the velocity of the ball after being fired is approximately $133$ m s$^{-1}$. [2 marks]

    3. Calculate the magnitude of the displacement of the cannonball after $5.0$ s.  Air resistance can be ignored. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. By Newton\'s 3rd law, there is a force to the right acting on the cannonball $\checkmark$

    There is an equal and opposite force (to the left) on the cannon $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    There is a force acting on the cannonball during the firing. According to Newton\'s third law, there must be an equal and opposite force on the cannon.

2.
    1. $a=\dfrac{F}{m}=\dfrac{8000}{1200}=6.7$ m s$^{-2}$

        $s=\dfrac{v^2}{2a}=\dfrac{1}{(2)(6.7)}= $ :answer[0.074 m]

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The deceleration *a* of the cannon is:

        - $a=\dfrac{F}{m}=\dfrac{8000}{1200}=6.7$ m s$^{-2}$

        The distance *s* can be calculated using the uniform acceleration equations

        - $v^2=u^2+2as$

        where

        -  $v=0$
        - $u=1$ m s$^{-1}$
        - $a=-6.7$ m s$^{-2}$
        - $0=1^2+(2)(-6.7)s$
        - $s=\dfrac{1}{(2)(6.7)}=0.074$ m

    2. Any reference to the conservation of momentum $\checkmark$

        $v_2 = \dfrac{(-m_1)(v_1)}{m_2} = \dfrac{-(1200)(1.0)}{9.0} = \ll$ -133 m s$^{-1} \gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Due to the conservation of momentum, the total momentum of the objects before the firing should be equal to the total momentum of the objects after the firing.

        The total momentum of the objects before the firing is zero due to the absence of motion. Therefore the total momentum of the objects after firing is also zero

        - $0=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$

        where

        - $m_1=1200$ kg
        - $v_1=1$ m s$^{-1}$
        - $m_2=9.0$ kg
        - $v_2=\dfrac{{-m}_1v_1}{m_2}=\dfrac{-(1200)(1.0)}{9.0}=-133$ m s$^{-1}$

        The negative sign represents the direction of the velocity. Therefore it is opposite to the direction of the motion of the cannon.

    3. Horizontal displacement $=vt=\left(133\right)\left(5.0\right)=666$ m $\checkmark$

        Vertical displacement=$ut+\fdrac{1}{2}at^2=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(9.81\right)\left(5^2\right)=122$ m $\checkmark$

        Displacement of the cannonball$=\sqrt{{666}^2+{122}^2}= $ :answer[677 m]  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Due to the absence of air resistance, the horizontal velocity of the ball is constant and vertical velocity increases with the acceleration of free fall. The horizontal displacement *x* of the cannonball

        - $x=vt$  where $v=133$ m s$^{-1}$ and $t=3.0$ s

        - $x=\left(133\right)\left(5.0\right)=666$ m

        The vertical displacement *y* of the cannonball

        - $y=ut+\dfrac{1}{2}at^2$

        where

        - $u=0$
        - $a=9.81$ m s$^{-1}$
        - $t=5.0\, s$
        - $y=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(9.81\right)\left(5^2\right)=122\, $m

        The magnitude of the displacement *s* can be found by using Pythagoras\'s theorem;

        - $s=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$
        - $s=\sqrt{{666}^2+{122}^2}=677$ m
