# PH0521

**Maximum mark: 11**

A student investigates gas behaviour. A volume of gas is trapped in a capillary tube by a thin layer of oil. The tube, a thermometer and a ruler are immersed in water.

![Screenshot 2026-08-20 at 10.06.30 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-08-20 at 10.06.30 AM.png>)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with chemix - https:// chemix.org]}}$

The student slowly added boiling water and recorded the values of the temperature *T* of the water and the height *h* of the gas trapped inside the capillary tube. The recorded data is plotted on a graph as shown.

![Screenshot 2025-04-15 at 4.03.23 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-15 at 4.03.23 PM.png>)

1. Draw the best fit line for the plotted data. [1 mark]

2. The theory suggests that

    - $h=mT+C$

    where *m* and *C* are constant. Temperature *T* is in $\degree$C.

    1. Suggest whether the data are consistent with the theory. [2 marks]

    2. By using the graph drawn in (a), determine the constants *m* and *C*. [4 marks]

    3. State the units of the gradient in fundamental SI units. [1 mark]

3. Following the experiment, the student tested their thermometer in an ice bath and in boiling water. The temperatures were 2$\degree$C and 102$\degree$C respectively.

    1. Explain how this finding will affect how the student interprets their results. [2 marks]

    2. The student also goes on to find the intercept on the temperature axis. State whether this error causes their intercept value to be higher or lower than expected. [1 mark]

    1. The relationship $h = mT + C$ suggests a straight line on the graph $\checkmark$

        A straight line can be drawn that passes through all of the uncertainty bars (therefore consistent) $\checkmark$

    2. Any evidence of calculation of gradient $\checkmark$

        - $m=\ll\dfrac{11.80\ -10.64\ }{95\ -60}=\gg 0.033 \ll$ cm C$^{-1}$  $\gg \checkmark$\
        (answers between 0.030 and 0.036 accepted)

        Any evidence of substitution of correct data in the equation $\checkmark$

        - $C=8.65 \ll $C $\gg$ $\checkmark$\
        (answers between 8.60 and 8.70 accepted)

         $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The equation suggests that *m* is the gradient of the graph and *C* is the y-intercept.

        $m=gradient\ of\ the\ graph=\dfrac{11.80\ -10.64\ }{95\ -60}=0.033$

        When any data is substituted in the equation with the value of *m*;

        - For $h = 10.8$ and $T = 65$;

        - $10.8=0.033\times65+C$

        - $C=10.8-0.033\times65=8.65$

    3. SI unit of  $\ce{m} \ = \ \ll \dfrac{\ce{m}}{\degree \ce{C}}\ \gg = \ce{m}{ \ \ce{K}^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    When the equation is rearranged;

        $m=\dfrac{h-C}{T}$

        Substitute SI units of the quantities;

        SI unit of $m=\dfrac{m}{\degree C}=\ce{mK}^{-1}$

3.
    1. There is a systematic error ($\checkmark$) where all readings are 2$\degree$C too high ($\checkmark$).

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The ice water reading should be 0$\degree$C and the boiling water 100$\degree$C. This indicated that the thermometer has a zero error of 2$\degree$C. As all data points are likely affected by this fixed amount, it is a systematic error.

    2. As all points have been shifted to the right, the temperature intercept reading will be higher than expected. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The graph could be corrected by shifting data points to the left. This will shift the line of best fit to the left, decreasing the value for the temperature intercept.
