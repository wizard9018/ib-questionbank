## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Random error can be eliminated through averaging. $\checkmark$

        **Or**

        Anomalies can be avoided $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        If just one trial is taken, that value could have a random fluctuation from the actual value and there is no way of knowing this. By taking a number of readings and averaging the results, random error will cause the readings to be above and below the actual value, and averaging the readings will give a result closer to the actual value. Also, if there is a significant anomaly, this can be seen when compared to the other readings.

    2. Removes the $1.7$ cm reading as an outlier. $\checkmark$

        - Average$=\dfrac{2.4+2.3+2.2+2.3+2.1}{5}= 2.3\  \ce{cm}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The outlier is at trial 3

        If we exclude the outlier, then

        - Average$=\dfrac{2.4+2.3+2.2+2.3+2.1}{5}=2.3$ cm

2. 1. Systematic error $\checkmark$

        Zero error in measuring devices.

        **Or**

        Beam deflects under its own weight $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The graph is not passing through the origin and yet the points form a straight line. This suggests that all measurements differ from the real value by a fixed amount.

        There is a systematic error.

        This systematic error can be due to zero error in measuring devices.

        It also can be because the beam deflects under its own weight, and so would have non-zero deflection for no mass added.

    2. Substitution of units for quantities $\dfrac{(\textrm{kg})(\textrm{m s}^{-2})(\textrm{m}^3)}{(\textrm{m})(\textrm{m}^4)}$ $\checkmark$

        kg m$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $E=\dfrac{mgL^3}{48dI}$

        Unit of $E$ is found by substituting the units for all quantities in the expression. Note that $g$ is an acceleration and the units on $I$ have been given in the question.

        - $\dfrac{(\textrm{kg})(\textrm{m s}^{-2})(\textrm{m}^3)}{(\textrm{m})(\textrm{m}^4)}=\textrm{kg m}^{-1}\textrm{ s}^{-2}$

    3. At least one fractional uncertainty is correct $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{\Delta E}{E}=\dfrac{0.1}{35}+(3)\left(\dfrac{0.0005}{2}\right)+\dfrac{0.05}{5}+\dfrac{0.4}{2.5}=0.174$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta E=\dfrac{\Delta E}{E}\times E=(0.174)(4.6\times10^{11})= 8\times10^{10}\ll \ce{kg m}^{-1} \ce{s}^{-2}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Since

        - $E=\dfrac{mgL^3}{48dI}$

        Then

        - $E=\dfrac{(35)(9.8)(2^3)}{(48)(5\times10^{-2})(2.5\times10^{-9})}=4.6\times10^{11}$ kg m$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$

        The fractional uncertainty in $E$ is given by

        - $\dfrac{\Delta E}{E}=\dfrac{\Delta m}{m}+(3)\left(\dfrac{\Delta L}{L}\right)+\dfrac{\Delta d}{d}+\dfrac{\Delta I}{I}$

        - $\dfrac{\Delta E}{E}=\dfrac{0.1}{35}+(3)\left(\dfrac{0.0005}{2}\right)+\dfrac{0.05}{5}+\dfrac{0.4}{2.5}=0.174$

        The absolute uncertainty is given by

        - $\Delta E=\dfrac{\Delta E}{E}\times E=(0.174)(4.6\times10^{11})=8\times10^{10}$ kg m$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$

    4. Any instrument with lower uncertainty (micrometer/vernier caliper) $\checkmark$

        Lower fractional uncertainty/more precise $I$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        A micrometer or a vernier caliper has a lower uncertainty than a meter tape, specifically around 0.01 mm for a micrometer and 0.1 mm for a vernier caliper.

        This increases the precision of measuring $h$ and decreases its fractional uncertainty, leading to a more precise calculation of $I$.
