# PH0487

The diagram shows a cube of side length 0.010 m and a metal sphere of radius 0.5 cm. The image below is not to scale.

![PH0487](images/PH0487.png)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: OpenClipart-Vectors from Pixabay https://pixabay.com/vectors/ball-balls-glass-glow-glowing-1293319/ ]}}$

If there is a set of these spheres that could be melted down, what is the estimated number of spheres required to construct the cube from the material?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ 20

- B. $\hspace{1em}$10

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ 4

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ 2

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The side length of the cube is 0.010 m which can be written as $1\times10^{-2}$ m

The volume of the cube is

Volume of cube = side$^3$ = $(1\times10^{-2})^3 = 1\times10^{-6}\,\ce{m^3}$

The radius of the sphere is 0.5 cm which is equivalent to $0.5\times10^{-2}$ m = $(\frac{1}{2} )\times 10^{-2}$ m

Volume of the sphere = $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$

$\approx (\frac{4}{3} )( 3 ) (\frac{1}{2} \times 10^{-2})^3 \approx (4 )(\frac{1}{8} \times 10^{-6}) \approx \frac{1}{2} \times 10^{-6}\, \ce{m^3}$

*Number of spheres in the cube* $\approx \dfrac{1\times10^{-6}}{\frac{1}{2} \times 10^{-6}} \approx$ 2 spheres
