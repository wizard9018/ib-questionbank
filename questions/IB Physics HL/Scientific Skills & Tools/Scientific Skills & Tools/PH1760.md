# PH1760

At a point in space, the magnitude of the gravitational field strength in units of N kg$^{-1}$ is equal to the magnitude of the electric field strength in units of N C$^{-1}$. A proton is placed at this point.

To the nearest order of magnitude what is

- $\dfrac{\textrm{Electric force acting on the proton}}{\textrm{Gravitational force acting on the proton}}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $10^7$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $10^8$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $10^9$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $10^{10}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The gravitational force acting on an object is given by:

- $F_g=mg$

Where $m$ is the mass of the object and $g$ is the gravitational field strength.

The electric force acting on an object is given by:

- $F_e=qE$

Where $q$ is the charge of the object and $E$ is the electric field strength.

For the proton we have:

- $\dfrac{F_e}{F_g}=\dfrac{q_pE}{m_pg}$

- $m_p=1.673\times10^{-27}$ kg and $q_p=1.6\times10^{-19}$ C

Dropping units, we have $E=g$. Therefore:

- $\dfrac{F_e}{F_g}=\dfrac{q_p}{m_p}=\dfrac{1.6\times10^{-19}}{1.673\times10^{-27}}\approx\dfrac{10^{-19}}{10^{-27}}=10^{27-19}=10^8$
