# PH1762

A student investigates the motion of a trolley on a slope by measuring the time, $t$, it takes to travel down the slope. The length of the slope, $s$, is varied, and the student plots a graph of $t^2$ against $s$, as shown below.

![PH1762](images/PH1762.JPEG)

Which of the following best describes the types of error present in the experiment?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ Systematic only

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ Random only

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ Random and systematic

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ No errors are present

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The graph shows $t^2$ plotted against $s$, with data points scattered around an apparent trend. From the graph:

**Random Error:**

The data points do not lie perfectly on a straight line, indicating random fluctuations in the measurements of time or length.

These errors may arise from factors such as reaction time when starting or stopping the timer or slight inconsistencies in releasing the trolley.

**Systematic Error:**

If the best-fit line is drawn, it does not pass through the origin, which suggests a systematic error in the experiment.

This could result from a consistent offset in the measurement tools, such as a timer starting too late or an incorrect zeroing of the length.

Both random and systematic errors are present.
