# PH0882

Two cylindrical resistors **A** and **B** are made from the same material and have the same length. Resistor **A** has twice the cross-sectional area of resistor **B**.

What is the ratio $\dfrac{Power\ dissipated\ across\ resistor\ A}{Power\ dissipated\ across\ resistor\ B}$ when they are connected in series and parallel?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Series $\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Parallel$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$| 2 |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 2 | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | 2 | 2 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

$\hspace{1em}$ $\rho = \dfrac{RA}{L}$

Same material implies same resistivity. $\rho$ and $l$ are constant so $R\ \alpha\ \dfrac{1}{A}$. **A** has twice the cross-sectional area of **B**. Therefore, **A** has half the resistance of **B**.

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{Resistance\ of\ A}{Resistance\ of\ B} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

In series current is the same in both resistors so $P=I^2R$ implies that $P\ \alpha\ R$.

Therefore

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{Power\ dissipated\ across\ resistor\ A}{Power\ dissipated\ across\ resistor\ B} = \dfrac{Resistance\ of\ A}{Resistance\ of\ B} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

In parallel potential difference is the same across both resistors so
$P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}$ implies that $P\ \alpha\ \dfrac{1}{R}$. Therefore

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{Power\ dissipated\ across\ resistor\ A}{Power\ dissipated\ across\ resistor\ B} = \dfrac{1}{\left( \dfrac{Resistance\ of\ A}{Resistance\ of\ B}\right)} = \dfrac{1}{\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)} = 2$
