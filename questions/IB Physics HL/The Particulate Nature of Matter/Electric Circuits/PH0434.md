2. The cell has an internal resistance. $\checkmark$

    There is a potential difference across the internal resistance / in a real-life cell, energy is lost to the internal resistance and therefore the potential difference across the cell is not equal to the emf  $\,\checkmark$

    OWTTE.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    Current passes through the internal resistance of the cell

    $I=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{R+r}$

    And the potential difference across the internal resistance will be

    $V=Ir$

    So the emf of the battery $\varepsilon$,

    $ε=Ir+IR$

    voltmeter reading = $IR=\varepsilon-Ir$

3.  $ \varepsilon =3\, \ll \ce{V} \gg \, \checkmark$

    $ r =\, \ll \dfrac{3-0}{3-0} \gg \,=  1\, \ll \Omega \gg \checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    The emf $\varepsilon$ of the cell is when there's no current in the circuit. At $I = 0$, the potential difference is 3 V which is the emf $\varepsilon$.

    The internal resistance is the negative gradient of the graph,

    $r = -\dfrac{0-3}{3-0} = 1 \, \Omega$
