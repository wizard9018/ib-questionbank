# PH0944

The diagram below shows part of an electric circuit.

![PH0944](images/PH0944.png)

All three resistors have the same area and length. $R_2$ has twice the resistance of $R_1$. The number of electrons flowing through $R_1$ per unit time is $N$. What is the number of electrons flowing through $R_3$ per unit time?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  N$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{3N}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  2N$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  3N$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

- $I=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{ \Delta t}$

The number of electrons is proportional to charge. Therefore

- $\dfrac{N}{t} \propto I$

The area and length of the resistors are the same therefore the resistances are the same.

Because they are in parallel, the voltage is the same across $R_1$ and $R_2$ and $V=IR$ so $I \propto \dfrac{1}{R}$.

Ratio of resistances $R_1:R_2$ is 1:2 so ratio of currents $I_1:I_2$ is 2:1.

$R_2$ has half the current of $R_1 $

Therefore the number of electrons per unit time flowing through $R_2$ is $\dfrac{N}{2}$

The number of charges per unit time exiting parallel combination is

- $N + \dfrac{N}{2}=\dfrac{3N}{2}$

Giving the charge per unit time passing through $R_3$ to be $\dfrac{3N}{2}$
