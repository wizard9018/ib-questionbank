# PH0599

A light bulb is lit by 1 kJ of energy being transferred to the bulb by a potential difference of 200 V.  This transfer happens over a time of 2 seconds.

What is the current passing through the bulb?

- A. $\hspace{1em} 0.0025$ A

- B. $\hspace{1em}0.$ A

- C. $\hspace{1em}2.5$ A

- D. $\hspace{1em}5$ A

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Using $V=\dfrac{W}{q}$ from the data booklet, we find that the charge *q* passing through the bulb in 2 seconds equals

- $\dfrac{W}{V}=\dfrac{1000\ \ce{J}}{200\ \ce{V}}=5$ C.

Since $I = \dfrac{\Delta q}{\Delta t}$, the current in the bulb is $\dfrac{5\ \ce{C}}{2\ \ce{s}}=2.5$ A.
