# PH0416

An electric circuit with emf $\varepsilon$ and internal resistance $r$ is connected to a variable resistor $R$ which varies from $0\, \Omega$ to $6\, \Omega$.

The variation of the power dissipated in the resistor $R$ with the value of the resistance $R$ is shown in the graph.

![PH0416](images/PH0416.png)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra, - https://www.geogebra.org/m/JbAKa82Y}]}$

What is the value of $\varepsilon$, if the maximum dissipated power is when $R$ is equal to the internal resistance of the battery $r$ ?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ 9 V

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ 10 V

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ 12 V

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ 18 V

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The power dissipated in $R$,

- $P=I^2R$

The emf $\varepsilon$ of a cell with internal resistance $r$,

- $\varepsilon=I(R+r)$

- $I=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{(R+r)}$

So,

- $P=\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{R+r}\right)^2R$

- $P=\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(R+r)^2}R$

From the graph, the maximum power is 18 W at $R=2\, \Omega$.

Since the maximum power is at $R=r$, then $r=2\, \Omega$

- $18=\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(2+2)^2}\right)(2)=\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\right)$

- $\varepsilon=\sqrt{(18)(8)}=\sqrt{144}=12$ V
