    2. The reading on the voltmeter will increase and the reading on the ammeter will decrease. $\checkmark$

       $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
As the distance $d$ increases, so does the resistance in the potentiometer.

        Because the potentiometer is in series with the 10 Ω resistor, the overall resistance of the circuit increases and the current in the circuit will decrease as a result.

       The 12 V potential difference from the cell is shared between the potentiometer and the 10 Ω resistor, with the individual potential differences proportional to their resistances. As the resistance in the potentiometer increases, its proportion of the total resistance increases. Due to this, the potential difference across it will also increase which leads to an increase in the reading on the voltmeter.

    3. Same$\,\checkmark$

       $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    The minimum reading when $R$ is zero, will be $0$.

        The maximum is when $R$ is set at the maximum value, so the current will be the same as the case in (a)

    4. Non-linear relation because the current is not constant. $\checkmark$

        Curve such that gradient is decreasing (or sketch) $\checkmark$

         $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \

At any other choice of $d$, the total resistance contributed by the potentiometer will be less than $R$, and so the current through the potentiometer will be higher than in part b(i). As a result, the potential difference across the potentiometer will also be higher.
