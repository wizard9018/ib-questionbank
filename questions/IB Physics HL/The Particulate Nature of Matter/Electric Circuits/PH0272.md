# PH0272

The diagram below shows a circuit connected to a 12 V battery with negligible internal resistance.

![PH0272-3a](images/PH0272-3a.png)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra, - https://www.geogebra.org/m/JbAKa82Y}]}$

What is the value of the current $I$ through the $2\,\mathit{\ce{\Omega}}$ resistor?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ 1 A

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ 2 A

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ 3 A

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ 4 A

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Start by calculating the combined resistance of the circuit. Since the $6\,\Omega$ and $2\,\Omega$ resistors are in parallel and the $1.5\,\Omega$ resistor is in series with both, we have $\dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{2}}+1.5\,\Omega = 3\,\Omega.$

 Now, using the definition of resistance, the current in the circuit is $V=(I)(R)$; $I=\dfrac{12}{3}=4$ A. Current through parallel resistors distributes in the inverse ratio of the resistances, $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the current will flow through the $2\,\Omega$ resistor and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ through the $6\,\Omega$ resistor. Therefore the correct answer is $3$ A (C)

Alternatively, after calculating the resistance of the parallel combination as $1.5\,\Omega$, it can be seen that this is in series with the other $1.5\,\Omega$ resistor, which means they will share the total voltage equally, giving 6 V for the parallel combination.

Then using Ohm's Law, the current $I=\dfrac{6\,V}{2\,\Omega} = 3$ A.
