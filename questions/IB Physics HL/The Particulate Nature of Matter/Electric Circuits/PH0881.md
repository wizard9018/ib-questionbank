# PH0881

The diagram below shows part of a circuit connected to a 12 V battery. The circuit contains two resistors of resistance $R$ and $3R$ with currents $3I$ and $2I$ flowing through them respectively. Terminals **X** and **Y** are connected via an external circuit.

![PH0881](images/PH0881.png)

What is the potential difference between **X** and **Y**?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 3 V

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 4 V
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 8 V
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 9 V

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Potential difference between **X** and **Y** is the same as the potential difference across the $3R$ resistor.

Potential difference across $R$ resistor = $(3I)(R)=3IR$

Potential difference across $3R$ resistor = $(3R)(2I)=6IR$

Ratio of resistances is $3IR:6IR = 1:2 $

Voltage must be split in the same ratio (current is the same in series so $V\ \alpha\ R$)

Ratio of voltages is $1:2=4:8$

Therefore the voltage between **X** and **Y** is 8 V

$\underline{\textrm{Alternatively}}$\
Using Kirchhoff's first law: $\sum I = 0$ (junction), there must be a current of $3I-2I=I$ flowing between **X** and **Y**.

Potential difference is the same for parallel combination so $I\ \alpha\ \dfrac{1}{R}$.

Ratio of the currents is $2I:I=2:1$\
Ratio of resistances is $1:2$ so resistance between **X** and **Y** is $2 \times 3R=6R$.

Total resistance of parallel combination $R_{total}$ is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{R_{total}} = \dfrac{1}{3R} + \dfrac{1}{6R} = \dfrac{2}{6R} + \dfrac{1}{6R} = \dfrac{3}{6R}$

$\hspace{1em}$ $R_{total} = \dfrac{6R}{3} = 2R$

Ratio of resistances in whole circuit is $R:2R=1:2$. Current is the same in series so $V\ \alpha\ R$. So ratio of potential differences is $1:2$. Potential difference between **X** and **Y** is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2}{1+2} \times 12 = 8$ V
