# PH0603

The following circuit serves as a potential divider, where $V_{in}=7  V_{out}$.

![PH0603](images/PH0603.png)

If $R_1=18\ \mathrm{\Omega}$, what is the value of $R_2$?

A. $\hspace{1em} 0.1 \ \Omega$

B. $\hspace{1em} 1 \ \Omega$

C. $\hspace{1em} 3 \ \Omega$

D. $\hspace{1em} 5 \ \Omega$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From $R=\dfrac{V}{I}$ and the fact that the resistors are in series,

- ${\ V}_{in}=\left(R_1+R_2\right)I$.

Also $V_{out}=R_2I$, therefore

- $I=\dfrac{V_{in}}{R_1+R_2}=\dfrac{V_{out}}{R_2}$.

Since $V_{in}=7V_{out}$ and $R_1=18\ \mathrm{\Omega}$

-  $\dfrac{{7V}_{out}}{18+R_2}=\dfrac{V_{out}}{R_2}$

cross-multiplying

- $7R_2=18+R_2$.

Therefore

- $R_2=3\, \mathrm{\Omega}$.
