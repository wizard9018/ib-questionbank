## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Resistance of the thermistor decreases so voltage across it decreases/ the thermistor shares a greater proportion of the total voltage.

    **OR**

    Total resistance of the circuit decreases so the current increases. $\checkmark$

    Voltage across the resistor increases/voltmeter reading increases. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    From the graph, we can see that an increase in temperature will lead to a decrease in resistance.

    The total voltage of the circuit $\varepsilon$ is divided between the thermistor and the fixed resistor. As the thermistor's resistance decreases, it takes a smaller share of the total voltage. This means the voltage across the fixed resistor increases, and since the voltmeter is measuring this voltage, its reading increases.

    Alternatively, since the total resistance of the circuit is lower, from Ohm's Law $\varepsilon=IR_{total}$, we see that the current in the circuit increases. Therefore, the voltage across the fixed resistor $V=IR$ must increase.

2. $\underline{\textrm{Any two from}}$

    The cell has an internal resistance

    The voltmeter is not ideal/Voltmeter has a finite resistance OWTTE

    The connecting wires have a resistance $\checkmark$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    When the thermistor's resistance equals the fixed resistor's resistance, we would normally expect the voltmeter to read $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$ because a **potential divider** splits the total voltage evenly when both resistances are equal. However, the actual reading is **less than** $\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$. because of the following reasons:

    **1. Internal Resistance of the Cell**
    - Real batteries have some internal resistance, which reduces the actual voltage available in the circuit.

    - Some of the voltage is "lost" inside the cell, meaning the voltage across the external components (thermistor and resistor) is less than $\epsilon$

    **2. Non-Ideal Voltmeter**

    - An ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance, meaning it does not draw any current.

    - In reality, voltmeters have finite resistance, meaning they draw a small current, which slightly alters the potential difference they are measuring.

    - The resistance of the parallel combination of the resistor R and the voltmeter will be less than R which will result in a lower voltage reading

    **3. Resistance of Connecting Wires**

    - The wires in the circuit also have small resistance.

    - This means some energy is dissipated in the wires, causing a further reduction in the voltage across the fixed resistor, making the voltmeter reading slightly lower than expected.
