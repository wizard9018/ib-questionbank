# PH0436

**Maximum mark: 9**

A cell with emf $\varepsilon$ and internal resistance $r$ is connected to a variable resistor $R$, an ammeter and a voltmeter as shown.

![PH0436](images/PH0436.png)

Both meters are ideal, and the variable resistor can vary from $0$ to $9\,\Omega$.

1. Derive a relation for the power $P$ dissipated in the variable resistor in terms of the internal resistance $r$, emf $\varepsilon$ and the value of the variable resistor $R$.  [2 marks]

The graph shows the variation of the power dissipated in the resistor $R$ with the value of the resistance $R$.

The maximum dissipated power is when $R$ equals the internal resistance of the battery $r$.

2. Determine the values of the internal resistance $r$ and the emf $\varepsilon$ of the cell.  [3 marks]

3.  1. Calculate the smallest possible reading on the ammeter.   [2 marks]

    2. On the axes given, sketch the variation of the reading of the voltmeter with the reading of the ammeter for all values of the variable resistor $R$ and state the quantity represented by the gradient.   [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $P=I^2R$

    $\bold{OR}$

    $P=\dfrac{V^2}{R}$

    $\bold{OR}$

    $P=VI \,\checkmark$

    $P=\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(R+r)^2} R \,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    The power dissipated in $R$,

    $P=I^2R$

    The emf $\varepsilon$ of a cell with internal resistance $r$,

    $\varepsilon = I(R+r)$

    $I = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{R+r}$

    So,

    $P=\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{R+r}\right)^2 R$

    $P = \dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(R+r)^2}R$

    $\bold{OR}$

    If we start with

    $\begin{aligned}
    \\[-10pt]
P &= \dfrac{V^2}{R} \\[12pt]
V &= \varepsilon - Ir, \text{ and } I = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{R+r} \\[12pt]
V &= \varepsilon - \dfrac{\varepsilon r}{(R+r)} = \dfrac{\varepsilon(R+r)-\varepsilon r}{R+r} = \dfrac{\varepsilon R}{R+r} \\[14pt]
V^2 &= \dfrac{\varepsilon^2 R^2}{(R+r)^2} \\[12pt]
P &= \dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(R+r)^2}R     \\[-10pt]
\end{aligned}$

    $\bold{OR}$

    If we start with

    $\begin{aligned}
    \\[-6pt]
    P &= VI \\[10pt]
    V &= \varepsilon - \dfrac{\varepsilon r}{R+r}  = \dfrac{\varepsilon(R+r)-\varepsilon r}{R+r} = \dfrac{\varepsilon R}{R+r} \\[14pt]
    P&= \left(\dfrac{\varepsilon R}{R+r}\right)\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon }{R+r}\right) \\[12pt]
    P &= \dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(R+r)^2}R    \\[10pt]
    \end{aligned}$

2. $r = 1\,\ll \Omega\gg \, \checkmark$

    $\varepsilon = (2)(1)\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{25}{1}}\right)\,\checkmark $

    $\varepsilon = 10 \,\ll \ce{V}\gg\, \checkmark$. (allow full marks for alternative solution leading to 10 V)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    The maximum power is when the variable resistor $R$ is equal to the internal resistance of the cell $r$.

    From the graph, the maximum power is 25 W at $R = 1\,\Omega$

    The internal resistance $r$,

    $r= 1\,\Omega$

    Using the relation derived in the previous part,

    $\begin{aligned}
    P &= \dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(R+r)^2} R \\
    25 &= \left(\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{(1+1)^2}\right)(1) \\
    \varepsilon &= (2)(1)\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{25}{1}}\right) = 10\ \ce{V}
    \end{aligned}$

3.  1. $I=\dfrac{10}{1+9}\,\checkmark$

        $I =  1 \,\ll \ce{A}\gg\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        The smallest reading of the ammeter is at the maximum value of the variable resistor.

        $\begin{aligned}
        \varepsilon &= I(R+r)\\I &= \dfrac{\varepsilon}{(R+r)} \\
        I &= \dfrac{10}{9+1} = 1\,\ce{A}
        \end{aligned}$
