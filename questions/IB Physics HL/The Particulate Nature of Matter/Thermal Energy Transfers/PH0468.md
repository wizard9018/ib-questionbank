        $P = \ll \dfrac{(1400)(0.2)(60)}{140} = \gg $ :answer[$120$ W] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:  

        There are two time intervals in which temperature is kept constant. These intervals must be the time when melting and boiling takes place. The sample is in liquid form between these two time intervals. The time needed for increasing the sample's temperature from $30\degree$C to $90\degree$C is 

        - $260 - 120 = 140$ s

        The energy supplied $Q$ by the heater in the mentioned time interval can be expressed in terms of the power $P$ of the heater; 

        - $Q=Pt=(P)(140)$

        The specific heat capacity $c$;

        - $c=\dfrac{Q}{m\Delta T}=\dfrac{Pt}{m\Delta T}=\dfrac{(P)(140)}{m\Delta T}$ where $m=0.2$ kg, $\Delta T=60\degree$C, and $c=1400$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$

        - $\begin{aligned}

        1400&=\frac{(P)(t)}{(0.2)(60)}\\

        \hspace{1em}\\  

        (1400)(0.2)(60)&=140P\\

        \hspace{1em}\\

        \frac{(1400)(0.2)(60)}{140}&=P\\

        \hspace{1em}\\

        120\textrm{ W}&=P\\

        \end{aligned}$
