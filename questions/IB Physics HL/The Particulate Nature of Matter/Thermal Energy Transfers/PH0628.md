# PH0628

A hollow, insulated ring is packed with a mass $M$ of ice pellets at an initial temperature of 0$\degree$C. A mass 3*M* of steam is injected into the ring and circulates around it at an initial temperature of 100$\degree$C. Assume that the specific latent heat of vaporization is seven times the specific latent heat of fusion *L*.

*L* is also equal to the energy required to increase the temperature of a unit mass of liquid water by 80$\degree$C.

![Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 4.58.41 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-07-18 at 4.58.41 PM.png>)

What are the final states of matter in the ring?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Solid and liquid

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Liquid

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Gas

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Liquid and gas

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Firstly, consider the energy $E_{cond}$ released by total condensation of the steam and the energy $E_{melt}$ required for complete melting of the ice. Using the equations for latent heat $Q=mL$:

- $\hspace{2em}$ $Q_{cond}=3M\times7L=21ML$

- $\hspace{2em}$ $Q_{melt}=ML$

Since the energy provided by condensation of the steam is sufficient to completely melt the ice, answer **A** cannot be correct.

To convert the water from the melted ice to steam, the energy supplied by the circulating steam must be sufficient to heat the water to 100$\degree$C and then overcome the latent heat of vaporization. However, if all the water in the system is to become steam, none of this energy can be provided by condensing steam. Since the circulating steam is initially at 100$\degree$C, it follows that insufficient energy is available to convert all the water in the system to steam. Hence, answer **C** is incorrect.

After all of the ice has melted, the remaining energy available to heat the resulting water from 0$\degree$C is:

$\hspace{2em}$ $Q_{heat}=Q_{cond}-Q_{melt}=20ML$

Now consider the heat energy that then raises the temperature of the water created by the melting ice.

To find the amount of energy needed to raise the water\'s temperature to 100$\degree$C , we use the equation:

$\hspace{2em}$ $Q = mc\Delta T$

$\hspace{2em}$ $\dfrac{Q}{m}=\ c\Delta T$

From the given information, we know that *L* is the energy per unit mass  when $\Delta T = 80$.

Thus

$\hspace{2em}$ $L=c\left(80\right)$

Giving

$\hspace{2em}$ $c=\dfrac{L}{80}$

and to raise the temperature by 100$\degree$C,

$\hspace{2em}$ $Q=mc\Delta T$

$\hspace{2em}$ $Q=M(\dfrac{L}{80})(100)$

$\hspace{2em}$ $= 1.25\ ML$

As there is still $20 ML$ of heat energy in the non-condensed steam, some but not all of the steam will condense.

This will result in the final state being a mixture of liquid water at 100$\degree$C and steam at 100$\degree$C.

Therefore the answer is **D**

As an alternative approach:

The sum of this energy and the energy removed from the condensed water as it cools must balance the energy used to heat the water from the melted ice. Assuming that the ring exclusively contains liquid water when the system equilibrates, and expressing the final temperature $T_f$ in units of $\degree$C, one obtains the equation:

$\hspace{2em}$ $20mL+3mc\left(100-T_f\right)=mcT_f$

Rearranging for $T_f$ yields

$\hspace{2em}$ $T_f=\dfrac{300c+20L}{4c}$

$\hspace{2em}$ $\therefore \ T_f=75\degree C+5Lc$

Finally, as it is stated that L equals 80c,

$\hspace{2em}$ $T_f=475\degree C$

This result is greater than 100$\degree$C, which is not possible for liquid water. This contradiction means that some of the water must remain as steam in the final system, so answer **D** is correct.
