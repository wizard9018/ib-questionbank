# PH0624

An ice cube of mass $m$ at its melting temperature is placed in a mass $M$ of water at an initial temperature of $20\degree$C. Assume that the specific heat capacity of liquid water is $4$ kJ kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ and it has a specific latent heat of fusion of $320$ J g$^{-1}$.

![PH0624zzz](images/PH0624zzz.png)

Assuming that the system is thermally isolated, what is the minimum value of M required for the ice to completely melt?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  0.25m$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  4.0m$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  16m$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  80m$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Note that the units of J g$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ are equivalent to kJ kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ for specific heat capacity. Also, the units of J g$^{-1}$ are equivalent to kJ kg$^{-1}$.

The minimum amount of energy required to completely melt the ice is equal to the product of $m$ and the specific latent heat of fusion $L$. The thermal energy available for this process is given by the product of water mass $M$, the specific heat capacity $c$, and the temperature change of the water (20 K). Therefore,

- $mL=Mc\Delta T$

- $M=\dfrac{mL}{c\Delta T}$

- $M=\dfrac{m(320\textrm{ kJ kg}^{-1})}{(4\textrm{ kJ kg}^{-1}\ K^{-1})(20\textrm{ K})}$

- $M=\dfrac{320m}{80}$

- $M=4m$
