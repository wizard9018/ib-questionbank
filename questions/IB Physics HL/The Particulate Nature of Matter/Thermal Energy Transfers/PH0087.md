# PH0087

A substance, initially solid of mass 2.00 kg is heated by a 4.0 kW electric heater causing it to melt. During heating, the substance also exchanges heat with its surroundings. The substance starts melting immediately and has fully turned to liquid after 400 s. Given that the specific latent heat of fusion of the solid is 900 kJ kg$^{-1}$, what is the average rate of heat transfer between the substance and the surroundings in one second?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1em}$ Direction of heat transfer $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ Rate of heat transfer $\hspace{1em}$ |

| :-: | :-: | :-: |

|$\hspace{1.5em}$A. $\hspace{1.5em}$| from the surroundings | 500 W |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$B. $\hspace{1.5em}$| to the surroundings | 500 W |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$C. $\hspace{1.5em}$| from the surroundings | 1750 W |

| $\hspace{1.5em}$D. $\hspace{1.5em}$| to the surroundings | 1750 W |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

Heat from electric heater: (4.0)(400) = 1600 kJ 

Total heat gained to cause melting: $(2.0)(900)=1800$ kJ

Therefore the heater does not provide enough energy, and extra energy must have entered from the surroundings.

The energy that entered from the surroundings is given by

- 1800 kJ $ -$ 1600 kJ = 200 kJ

Hence power from the surrounding

- $P = \dfrac{E}{t}=\dfrac{200}{400}$ = 0.5 kW = 500 W
