# PH0510

**Maximum mark: 13**

In an experiment, a water-filled beaker is positioned on an electronic balance. An immersion heater with a power rating of $P$ is introduced into the water. Once the water begins to boil, a researcher monitored the mass $m$ of the liquid in the beaker over time $t$ once the boiling commenced.

![PH0510-4](images/PH0510-4.png)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with chemix - https://chemix.org/]}}$

The theoretically predicted relationship between time $t$ and $m$ is

- $L_v=\dfrac{Pt}{C-m}$

$C$ is the initial mass of the water when $t=0\, $s and $L_v$ is the latent heat of vaporisation of water.

$\\$

1.   Give the units of $L_v$ in fundamental SI units. [2 marks]

2. In a particular experiment, the researcher uses a heater of power $65.0\pm0.1\ $W and measures $m=0.120\pm0.001\, $kg, $C=0.125\pm0.001\ $kg, and $t=600\pm1\ $s.

    1. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the measured value of $L_v$. [3 marks]

    2. For this experiment, calculate the value of $L_v$ and its absolute uncertainty. [2 marks]

    3. The accepted value of $L_v$ is $2.3 \times 10^6 \, $J kg$^{-1}$. Explain why the experimental value does not match the accepted value. [2 marks]

3. The researcher plots a graph to show how *m* varies with *t*.

    1.     Outline why *P* must be kept constant during the experiment. [1 mark]

    2. Assuming the relationship $L_v=\dfrac{Pt}{C-m}$ is correct, sketch the expected mass-time relationship on the axes below. (There is no need to put numbers to the axes.) [2 marks]

1. $Unit\ of\ Power=\ll\dfrac{\ce{kg} \ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-2}\,\ce{m}}{\ce{s}}=\gg\ \ce{kg} \ \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}$ $\checkmark$

    $Unit\ of\ L_v=\dfrac{\ce{kg} \ \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}\,\ce{s}}{\ce{kg}}= \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}$  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    - $Power=\dfrac{W}{t}=\dfrac{Fs}{t}=\dfrac{mas}{t}$

    Substituting in the fundamental units for each of the variables gives

    - $\textrm{Unit of Power}=\dfrac{\ce{kg} \ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-2}\,\ce{m}}{\ce{s}}=\ce{kg} \ \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}$

    The unit of power is Watts. It is equal to kg m$^2$ s$^{-3}$.

    Both *m* and *C* have fundamental units of kg, and we are multiplying but time which has units of s.

    Therefore

    - $Unit\ of\ L_v=\dfrac{\ce{kg} \ \ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-3}\,\ce{s}}{\ce{kg}}=\ce{m}^2\,\ce{s}^{-2}$

    3. Heat lost to the environment $\checkmark$

        Causes the m value in the equation to be too low/means not all P contributed to the vaporisation of water $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$\
        Due to environmental losses, a portion of the heat input is not effectively utilized for boiling, resulting in a lower quantity of water actually boiling compared to the ideal scenario. As a consequence, the observed mass ($m$) is higher and the corrected mass ($C-m$) is lower, leading to a calculated latent heat of vaporization ($L_v$) that exceeds the expected value.

        Alternatively, the equation assumes that all the power supplied by the heater is utilized for boiling the water. However, in reality, there are losses, causing the actual power ($P$) to be lower than the assumed value. This leads to an overestimation of the latent heat of vaporization ($L_v$) in the equation, resulting in a higher value than the accepted value.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$\
        When the relationship is rearranged;

        $\begin{aligned}
        L_v&=\dfrac{Pt}{C-m}\\
        CL_v-mL_v&=Pt\\
        CL_v-Pt&=mL_v\\
    m&=-\dfrac{P}{L_v}t+C
\end{aligned}$

        This relationship is similar to

        - $y=mx+c$

        We can see that this should be a straight line with gradient $-\dfrac{P}{L_v}$ and a vertical axis intercept of $C$.

        Therefore, it represents a straight line with a negative gradient.
