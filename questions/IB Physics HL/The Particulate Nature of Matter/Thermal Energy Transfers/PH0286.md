# PH0286

Water of mass $m$ at an initial temperature of $20\degree$C is provided with thermal energy at a rate $P$. Its temperature rises to $30\degree$C in time $t$. 

The rate of energy transfer is doubled and the mass of the water is halved. What is the final temperature, in time $t$, if the initial temperature is again $20\degree$C?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 30$\degree$C

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 40$\degree$C

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 60$\degree$C

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 80$\degree$C

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

Using the formula for specific heat capacity:

- $Q = mc\Delta T \hspace{2em} $

We can equate it to the energy as determined from the power formula:

- $E = Pt$

- $Pt = mc\Delta T$

- $\Delta T = \dfrac{Pt}{mc}$

Doubling energy transfer rate will double the temperature rise. Halving the mass will also double the temperature rise. The change in temperature will be 4 times larger than in the initial case, which is $40\degree$C. 

This gives the final temperature as $60\degree$C.
