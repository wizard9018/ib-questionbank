# PH1844-2

**Maximum mark: 8**

An immersion heater is fully submerged inside 0.35 kg of a liquid $X$. The graph shows how the temperature $T$ of the liquid varies with time $t$.

![Screenshot 2025-09-23 at 2.20.40 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-09-23 at 2.20.40 PM.png>)

1.  Assuming the power delivered by the heater is 50 W, use the graph to determine the specific heat capacity of liquid $X$. [3 marks]

2. The actual value of the specific heat capacity of liquid $X$ is 2800 J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$. The mass and temperature of the water were measured accurately.

    Give one reason why this difference may have occurred and suggest an improvement to the experiment that will reduce this difference. [2 marks]

3. The container of liquid at 40$\degree $C is poured into a beaker with 500 g of water held at 10$\degree $C. Mixture $Y$ is created. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$. Assuming no heat is transferred to the surroundings or the beaker, determine the equilibrium temperature of mixture $Y$ in Celsius to the nearest degree. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1.7em}$ $Pt=mc\Delta T$

    OR

    - Gradient $=0.046$ $\checkmark$

    - their gradient $=\dfrac{P}{mc}$ $\checkmark$

    - $c=\dfrac{50}{(0.35)(0.046)}=$ :answer[$3100$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The change in thermal energy of a substance is given by:

    - $Q=mc\Delta T$

    Where $m$ is the mass of the substance, $c$ is the specific heat capacity of the substance in a given state, and $\Delta T$ is its change in temperature.

    By dividing both sides of the equation by a period of time $\Delta t$ we have:

    - $\dfrac{Q}{\Delta t}=mc\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}$

    - $P=mc\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}$

    $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}$ is the gradient of the graph in the question therefore we have:

    - $P=mc(\textrm{gradient})$

    The gradient of the graph is given by:

    - $\dfrac{\Delta T}{\Delta t}=\dfrac{23}{500}=0.046$

    ![ph1844-2aa1](images/ph1844-2aa1.png)

    Finally, substituting values into our equation and rearranging for $c$ we have:

    - $50=(0.35)c(0.046)$

    - $c=\dfrac{50}{(0.35)(0.046)}=3100$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$

2. One reason:

    - Heat was lost to the surroundings / container

    - The temperature of the liquid was not constant  / the energy was not evenly distributed $\checkmark$

    One corresponding improvement:

    - Insulate container (e.g., wrap container in cotton wool)

    - Use more thermally insulated container / container with low thermal conductivity / high specific heat capacity

    - Stir liquid for the duration of the experiment $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    One reason for the discrepancy is that not all of the electrical power supplied by the heater was used to raise the temperature of the liquid. Some of this heat was lost to the surroundings and to the container holding the liquid. As a result, the energy input assumed in the calculation was higher than the actual energy transferred to the liquid, leading to a calculated specific heat capacity that is larger than the true value.

    To reduce heat losses to the surroundings and improve the accuracy of the energy balance, the container could be insulated, for example by wrapping it in cotton wool or using a container made from a material with a low thermal conductivity. This would help to keep more of the supplied energy within the liquid.

    Another reason could be that the temperature of the liquid was not uniform throughout. If the liquid was not stirred continuously during heating, there may have been areas of different temperatures within the container. The thermometer would only measure the temperature at a specific location rather than the true average temperature of the entire liquid. This non-uniformity can lead to an incorrect measurement of the change in temperature, and therefore specific heat capacity.

    Stirring the liquid throughout the experiment would help ensure that the temperature is uniform throughout the liquid, giving a more accurate measurement of the average temperature change and improving the reliability of the calculated specific heat capacity.

3. Use of heat lost = heat gained. $\checkmark$

    - $(0.35)(2800)(40-T)=(0.50)(4200)(T-10)$ $\checkmark$

    - $T=19.54=$ :answer[$20\degree$C] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    From the conservation of energy and the assumption that there is no heat exchanged with the surroundings,

    - heat lost by the liquid = heat gained by the water

    Using the data booklet formula

    - $Q=mc\Delta T$

    We can use $T$ to represent the equilibrium temperature and substitute in the values

    - $(0.35)(2800)(40-T)=(0.50)(4200)(T-10)$

    Giving

    - $T=19.54=20\degree$C
