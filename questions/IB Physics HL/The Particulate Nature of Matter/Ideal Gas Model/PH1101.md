# PH1101

**Maximum mark: 8**

In an experiment the volume occupied by a fixed mass of an ideal gas is varied. The temperature of the gas is kept constant at 293 K throughout the experiment. The pressure of the gas $P$ is measured for five different volumes $V$. Below shows a graph of $P$ against $V^{-1}$. The uncertainty in $V^{-1}$ is much less than the uncertainty in P for all of the data points.

![Screenshot 2025-06-04 at 4.38.59 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-06-04 at 4.38.59 PM.png>)

1.     Determine the relative uncertainty on pressure when $\dfrac{1}{V} $ is equal to $5.0 \times 10^2\ \ce{m}^{-3}$.   [2 marks]

2.  1. Draw the line of best fit on the graph.    [1 mark]

    2. Use your line of best fit to estimate the number of moles of gas used in the experiment                     [3 marks]

    3.  It is suggested that $P$ and $\dfrac{1}{V}$ are directly proportional. Explain why the data does not support this hypothesis.                                                [1 mark]

    4.  With reference to the graph, suggest, with a reason, whether a systematic or random error is responsible for the hypothesis not being supported.         [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Absolute uncertainty +-0.2 x 105. $\checkmark$

    Relative uncertainty $=\dfrac{0.2}{2.6}=0.08=8\%.$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Reading from the graph, the uncertainty for the pressure is $0.2\times{10}^5$ Pa

    The pressure at this point is $2.6\times{10}^5$ Pa

    Relative uncertainty $=\dfrac{\Delta P}{P}=\dfrac{0.2\times{10}^5}{2.6\times{10}^5}=0.077=7.8\%$

2. 1. Straight line that passes through all uncertainty bars $\checkmark$

      ![Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 9.35.59 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 9.35.59 AM.png>)

    2. gradient $=nRT$  $\checkmark$

        Gradient of line of best fit $= 440\ (\ce{J})$ Allow values in range 420-460  (J). $\checkmark$

        Number of moles = $\dfrac{gradient}{RT}=\dfrac{440}{(8.31)(293)}=0.18\, \ce{mol}$ (Allow answers in range 0.17-0.19). $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $PV=nRT$

        Rearranging this equation, we can see

        - $P=nRT\left(\dfrac{1}{V}\right)$

        Comparing this to $y=mx\ (+c)$ tells us that gradient $=nRT $

        Finding the gradient of the line of best fit.

        - Gradient$=\dfrac{difference\ in\ P}{difference\ in\ V^{-1} }=\dfrac{1.1\times{10}^5}{2.5\times{10}^2}=440\, (\ce{J})\ $

        ![Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 3.43.33 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-16 at 3.43.33 PM.png>)

        - $n=\dfrac{gradient}{RT}=\dfrac{440}{\left(8.31\right)\left(293\right)}=0.18\ $moles

        (Note $R$ is the molar gas constant $=8.31\, $J K$^{-1}\, \textrm{mol}^{-1}$ - this value is given in your data booklet)

    3.   It is not possible to draw a straight line from the origin that passes within all uncertainty bars.  $\checkmark$

         $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
         The conditions for two variables to be directly proportional are that the graph shows a straight line that passes through the origin. For the given data and uncertainty bars, a straight line can be drawn; however, it crosses the pressure axis above the origin.

    4.  Systematic error.   $\checkmark$

        All points have been shifted by the same amount.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The points on the graph show a good amount of linearity, which implies that there is minimal random error.

        Because the line of best fit passes above the origin, it suggests that all pressure measurements are a little higher than the actual amounts, or all $\dfrac{1}{V}$ measurements are lower than they should be (or all V measurements are higher than the actual amount)
