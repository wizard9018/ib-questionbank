# PH0858

Two containers **A** and **B** contain the same ideal gas at the same temperature. The volumes of **A** and **B** are $3$ cm$^3$ and $1.5$ cm$^3$ respectively.

The pressures in **A** and **B** are $\dfrac{p}{2}$ and $p$ respectively.

The two containers are connected together and the gases are mixed together at constant temperature.

What is the change in pressure of container **B**?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{-2}{3}p$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{-1}{3}p$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{3}p$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2}{3}p$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From the ideal gas law

$\hspace{1em}$ $pv=nRT$

In container **A**,

$\hspace{1em}$ $n_A = \dfrac{p_AV_A}{RT}$

In container **B**,

$\hspace{1em}$ $n_B = \dfrac{p_BV_B}{RT}$

And after the gases have combined,

$\hspace{1em}$ $n_A + n_B = \dfrac{P\left(V_A + V_B \right)}{RT}$

Therefore

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{p_AV_A}{RT} + \dfrac{p_BV_B}{RT} = \dfrac{P\left(V_A + V_B \right)}{RT}$

Substituting in the given values gives

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\dfrac{p}{2} (3)}{RT} + \dfrac{p(1.5)}{RT} = \dfrac{P(3 + 1.5)}{RT}$

Simplifying gives

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3p}{2} + \dfrac{3p}{2} = 4.5 P$

$\hspace{1em}$ $3p = 4.5P$

$\hspace{1em}$ $P = \dfrac{3}{4.5}p = \dfrac{2}{3}p$

Therefore the pressure in **B** has changed by

$\hspace{1em}$ $ \dfrac{2}{3}p -p=-\dfrac{1}{3}p$
