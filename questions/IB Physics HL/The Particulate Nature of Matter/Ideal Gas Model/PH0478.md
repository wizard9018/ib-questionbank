# PH0478

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. Define pressure. [1 mark]

2. An ideal gas is in a container of volume $2.5 \times 10^4\,\ce{cm^3}$. The pressure of the gas is $3.0 \times 10^4$ Pa at a temperature of $127\degree$ C.

    1. Determine how many molecules there are in the gas. [3 marks]

    2. Estimate the total kinetic energy of the gas molecules. [2 marks]

3. The gas is heated at a constant rate  to reach a temperature of 800 K. The change in the volume of the container is negligible. 

    1. Determine the pressure of the gas at 800 K. [2 marks]

    2.     On the graph below, sketch how the mean square speed $\overline{c^2}$ of the gas molecules varies with temperature $T$ during the heating process. [2 marks]

    1. The temperature of the ideal gas in Kelvin / $T=273+127=400$ K $\checkmark$

        Usage of $n=\dfrac{pV}{RT}=\ll \dfrac{(3.0\, \times\, 10^4)(2.5\, \times\, 10^{-2})}{(8.31)(400)}=\gg 0.23$ mol $\checkmark$

        Number of molecules / $N=\ll nN_A=(0.226)(6.02\times10^{23})=\gg$ :answer[$1.4\times 10^{23}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The temperature of the ideal gas in Kelvin;

        - $T=273+127=400$ K

        The volume of the gas must be converted to m$^3$;

        - $V=(2.5 \times 10^4)(10^{-6})=2.5 \times 10^{-2}$

        By using the ideal gas equation, the number of mole n of the ideal gas ; 

        - $n=\dfrac{pV}{RT}= \dfrac{(3.0\, \times \,10^4)(2.5\, \times\, 10^{-2})}{(8.31)(400)}= 0.226$ mol

        Number of molecules $N$;

        - $N=nN_A=(0.226)(6.02 \times 10^{23})=1.36 \times 10^{23}$

    2. ::indent

        - $E_{k(total)}=U$

        - $U= \dfrac{3}{2}Nk_bT=\dfrac{3}{2}(1.36 \times 10^{23})(1.38 \times 10^{-23})(400) \checkmark $

        - $\hspace{1em} =1100\ \ce{J}\ \checkmark$

        

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The total kinetic energy $\overline{E}_k$ per of the molecules of an ideal gas is equal to the internal energy $U$;

        - $U= \dfrac{3}{2}Nk_bT$

        

        Substituting all of the known values gives

        

        - $U=\dfrac{3}{2}(1.36 \times 10^{23})(1.38 \times 10^{-23})(400)  $

        - $\hspace{1em} =1100\ \ce{J}\ $

3. 

    1. Usage of $P_f=\dfrac{(p_i)(T_f)}{T_i}$ $\checkmark$

        $\ll\dfrac{(3.0\, \times \,10^4)(800)}{400}=\gg$ :answer[$6.0 \times 10^4\ll \ce{Pa}\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The pressure and the temperature of an ideal gas are directly proportional to each other;

        $\dfrac{p}{T}$ = constant

        So the following relationship can be used for initial and final pressures;

        $\dfrac{p_i}{T_i} = \dfrac{p_f}{T_f}$

        By rearranging the equation;

        $P_f=\dfrac{(p_i)(T_f)}{T_i} = \dfrac{(3.0 \,\times \,10^4)(800)}{400}=6.0 \times 10^4$ Pa

    2. $\hspace{1em}$

        ![PH0478-1](images/PH0478-1.png)

        A straight line whose extension should pass through the origin $\checkmark$

        The line should be between 400-800 $K$ on the x-axis $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The average kinetic energy $\overline{E}_k$ per molecule of a gas;

        $\overline{E}_k=\frac{1}{2} m\overline{c^2} =\frac{3}{2} k_b T \hspace{2em}$ where $\frac{3}{2} k_b$ = constant and $\frac{1}{2} m$ = constant

        So ;

        $\overline{c^2} \propto T$

        This relationship can be represented with a straight line whose extension should pass through the origin. Additionally, the temperature is changed from 400 K to 800 K. Therefore, the line should be between these values on the x-axis.
