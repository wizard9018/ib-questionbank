# PH0877

Two insulated containers of ideal monatomic gas are connected via an insulated tube of negligible volume. Container $A$ initially contains two times as many molecules as container $B$. Container $A$ has an initial temperature $T_A$ and the initial temperature of the gas in Container $B$ is $\dfrac{2}{3} T_A$. The gases mix to reach equilibrium at temperature $T_F$.

What is the ratio $\dfrac{T_F}{T_A}$    ?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{4}{9}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{3}{4}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{8}{9}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  \dfrac{4}{3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Prior to connection, the internal energy in Container A can be expressed as

- $U_A=\dfrac{3}{2} N_A k_B T_A  $

We are told that

- $N_B=\dfrac{N_A}{2} \hspace{2em}$and that$\hspace{2em} T_B=\dfrac{2T_A}{3}$

Correspondingly, the internal energy in Container B is given by

- $U_B=\dfrac{3}{2} N_B k_B T_B=\dfrac{3}{2}\left(\dfrac{N_A}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{2T_A}{3}\right)$

- $∴U_B=\dfrac{1}{2} N_A k_B T_A$

Since no energy or matter is exchanged with the surroundings, the final internal energy must equal the sum of the initial internal energies and the total number of molecules must equal the sum of $N_A$ and $ N_B$ :

- $U_{total}=U_A+U_B=U_A=\dfrac{3}{2} N_A k_B T_A + \dfrac{1}{2} N_A k_B T_A =\left(\dfrac{3}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\right) N_A k_B T_A=2N_A k_B T_A  $

When combined, the total number of molecules is

- $N_a+ \dfrac{1}{2}N_A = \dfrac{3}{2}N_A$

And equating the two expressions for final internal energy gives

- $\dfrac{3}{2} \left(\dfrac{3}{2} N_A \right) k_B T_F=2N_A k_B T_A$

- $∴T_{final}=\dfrac{8T_A}{9}$
