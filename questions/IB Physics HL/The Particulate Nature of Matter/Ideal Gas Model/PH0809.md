# PH0809

**Maximum mark: 10**

1. In a game of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$tug of war'', opposing teams pull on each end of a rope. Team **A** applies a force of $1.3\ $kN to the left. Team **B** applies a force of $1.5\ $kN to the right, but a cylindrical ideal gas piston pushes against Team **B** with a force $F$. The piston is initially at the same pressure as the surrounding atmosphere (100 kPa) with a cross-sectional area of $30\ \ce{cm}^2$ and length $1.0\ $m. The piston remains at constant temperature during compression.

    ![PH0809a](images/PH0809a.png)

    Show that the pressure change in the piston, $\Delta P$, is related to the rightwards displacement of the teams, $x$ (in meters), by the equation,

    $\Delta P = 100\ \textrm{kPa} \left( \dfrac{x}{1-x} \right)$ [3 marks]

2. Once the tug of war starts, calculate the equilibrium value of $x$. [4 marks]

3. After the game, the teams are reset and the gas inside the piston is heated, but the piston is prevented from expanding. When the game commences, the piston is released, but no displacement takes place. The forces applied by the two teams are the same as in the first game.

    If the gas temperature was $27\degree$C during the first game, estimate the temperature increase inside the piston between the two games. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $P_0 + \Delta P = P_0\dfrac{V_0}{V}$ $\checkmark$

    $V = A\left(1-x\right)$ $\checkmark$

    Substitution of $V$ and $V_0$, and rearrangement to obtain final equation $ P_0 \left( \dfrac{x}{1-x} \right)$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
From the ideal gas law applied at constant temperature it follows that

    $\hspace{1em}$ $PV=constant$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $PV=P_0 V_0 $

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore P = P_0 + \Delta P = \dfrac{P_0V_0}{V}$

    where the pressure $P$ inside the piston at volume $V$ is an amount $\Delta P$ above atmospheric pressure $P_0$; in the scenario given, $P$ is equal to $P_0$ when $V$ equals the initial volume $V_0$.

    Denoting the initial length as $L_0$ ($1.0\ m$) and the displacement of Team **A** as $x$,

    $\hspace{1em}$ $V = A\left(L_0 - x \right)$

    Hence,

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta P = \dfrac{P_0V_0}{V} - P_0$

    $\hspace{2.8em}$ $= P_0 \left ( \dfrac{V_0 - V}{V} \right)$

    $\hspace{2.8em}$ $= P_0 \left ( \dfrac{AL_0 - A(L_0 - x)}{A(L_0 - x)} \right)$

    $\hspace{2.8em}$ $ = P_0\left ( \dfrac{x}{L_0 - x} \right )$

    $\hspace{2.8em}$ $ = P_0 \left( \dfrac{x}{1-x} \right)$

2. ::indent

    - $F_{piston} = (1.5 - 1.3)kN = 200\ $N $\checkmark$

    -  $\Delta P = \dfrac{200}{30 \times 10^{-4}} =$ $67 \times 10^3\ $Pa $\checkmark$

    - 67 kPa = 100k kPa $ \left( \dfrac{x}{1-x} \right)$

    giving

    - x = :answer[$0.40\ $m] $\checkmark$

$\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The force from the piston must cause Team **B** to be in equilibrium. To balance the forces of the pulling ropes, the piston must push:

- $F_{piston} = (1.5 - 1.3)\textrm{kN} = 200\ $N

The pressure in the cylinder must be atmospheric pressure plus the extra pressure, exerted to create $200\ N$ of force on the piston. The extra pressure can be found from:

- $\Delta P = \dfrac{200}{A} =  \dfrac{200}{30 \times 10^{-4}} = 67 \times 10^3\ $Pa

We can now substitute this into the given equation

- 67 kPa = (100 kPa)$\bigg( \dfrac{x}{1-x} \bigg)$

- $0.67(1-x)=x$
- $0.67=1.67x$
- $x=0.40$

3. From part b, $P=100 + 0.67=167\ $kPa

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{P_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2}{T_2}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{100}{300} = \dfrac{167}{T_2}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $T_2 = \dfrac{(167)(300)}{100} =$ :answer[500 K] $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta T = 500 - 300 =$ :answer[200 K] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
From the ideal gas law, it follows that at constant volume,

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{P}{T} = \textrm{constant}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore T_2 = T_1 \bigg(\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}\bigg)$

    where $T_2$ and $P_2$ are the gas temperature and pressure, respectively, in the new game, and $T_1$ and $P_1$ are the gas temperature and pressure, respectively, in the first game.

    Substituting gives

    $\hspace{1em}$ $T_2 = \dfrac{(167)(300)}{100} = 500\ $K

    Solving for the change in temperature:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta T = 500 - 300 = 200\ $K $\checkmark$
