# PH0729

**Maximum mark: 14**

1. An astronaut is stranded in space 2.0 km from their space shuttle. Fortunately, the astronaut's oxygen tank is fitted with a valve that allows them to propel themselves forward by ejecting oxygen gas (molar mass $32.0\, \ce{g\, mol^{-1}}$) at a velocity of $340\, \ce{m\, s^{-1}}$.

    ![729aaa](images/729aaa.png)

    The total initial mass of the astronaut and their equipment, including their oxygen tank, is 160 kg. The $0.040\, \ce{m^3}$ tank contains oxygen gas at an initial pressure of 500 kPa and a constant temperature of 298  K. The astronaut consumes oxygen at a constant rate of $0.60\, \ce{g\, min^{-1}}$.

    Show that the astronaut's breathing consumes approximately $14\%$ of the initial oxygen supply every hour. [3 marks]

2.
    1. State how Newton's third law of motion results in pressure on the wall of the oxygen tank. [3 marks]

    2. Based on the answer to part b(i), explain how the oxygen gas pressure causes the astronaut to accelerate as oxygen is ejected from the tank. [3 marks]

3.
    1. To return to the shuttle, the astronaut decides to eject $70\%$ of their oxygen supply in a very short time interval.

        Estimate the time required for the astronaut to reach the shuttle. Assume that there are no forces to oppose the astronaut's movement. [3 marks]

    2. Determine whether the astronaut reaches the shuttle before running out of oxygen. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{breath}=60\ \ce{min}\left(\dfrac{0.60\ \ce{g\ {min}}^{-1}}{32.0\, \ce{g\, {mol}^{-1}}}\right)=1.125\ \ce{mol}$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{tank}=\dfrac{\left(5\times{10}^5\ \ce{Pa}\right)\left(0.040\ \ce{m^3}\right)}{\left(8.31\ \ce{J\ {mol}}^{-1}{\ \ce{K}}^{-1}\right)\left(298\ \ce{K}\right)}=8.076$ mol $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\%\ consumed\ per\ hour=\dfrac{n_{breath}}{n_{tank}}\ \times \ 100\%=\dfrac{1.125}{8.076}\ \times \ 100\%=13.9\%$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
In one hour, the astronaut consumes $n_{breath}$ moles of gas, which can be expressed as

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{breath}=60\ \ce{min}\left(\dfrac{0.60\ \ce{g\ {min}}^{-1}}{32.0\ \ce{g\ {mol}}^{-1}}\right)=1.125$ mol

    The number of moles in the tank, $n_{tank}$, is given by

    $\hspace{1em}$ $n_{tank}=\dfrac{\left(5\times{10}^5\ \ce{Pa}\right)\left(0.040\ \ce{m^3}\right)}{\left(8.31\ \ce{J\ {mol}}^{-1}\ce{{\ K}}^{-1}\right)\left(298\ \ce{K}\right)}=8.076$ mol

    Finally, one may calculate the percentage of oxygen consumed in one hour:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $P=100\ \times \dfrac{n_{breath}}{n_{tank}}\%\ \approx\ 14\%$

2.
    1. Gas molecules colliding with a surface experience a change in momentum $\checkmark$

        The change in momentum is caused by a force from the container wall $\checkmark$

        By Newton's 3$^{\mathrm{rd}}$ law, the molecules also exert a force on the wall of the oxygen tank, resulting in pressure $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The kinetic theory of gases models gas molecules as hard spheres of negligible volume that undergo elastic collisions, resulting in a pressure on any surface that is in contact with the gas.

    2. The momentum of escaping oxygen is caused by the force exerted by colliding oxygen molecules inside the tank $\checkmark$

        By Newton's 3$^{\mathrm{rd}}$ law, an equal and opposite force acts on the astronaut $\checkmark$

        By Newton's 2$^{\mathrm{nd}}$ law, the force acting on the astronaut causes forward acceleration $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
As in part b(i), the elastic collisions between gas molecules yield a resultant force on the gas surroundings. When the valve is opened, molecules closest to the outlet of the tank experience a net force applied by colliding molecules approaching the outlet, causing them to accelerate out of the tank. This change in momentum yields an equal and opposite change in momentum of the tank and the astronaut (principle of conservation of momentum), which is a consequence of Newton's third law (the force on ejected oxygen produces an equal and opposite force on the stored oxygen) and Newton's second law (the acceleration of an object is given by the force applied to it divided by its mass).

3.
    1. $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{m_{ejected}}{M-m_{ejected}}v_{O_2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ **OR**

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v\approx\dfrac{m_{ejected}}{M}v_{O_2}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{travel}=\dfrac{x}{v}=x\dfrac{M-m_{ejected}}{m_{ejected}v_{O_2}}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ **OR**

        $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{travel}=\dfrac{x}{v}\approx\ x\dfrac{M}{m_{ejected}v_{O_2}}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{travel}=1.4$ hours $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Through conservation of momentum, the momentum of the astronaut and tank should be equal to the momentum of the ejected gas.

        Therefore:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(M-m_{ejected}\right)v=m_{ejected}v_{O_2}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{m_{ejected}}{M-m_{ejected}}v_{O_2}\approx\dfrac{m_{ejected}}{M}v_{O_2}$

        where the final approximation follows from the fact that $m_{ejected}$ is much less than the initial mass of the astronaut (including the oxygen tank), $M$.

        The oxygen tank initially contains a mass of oxygen gas given by

        $\hspace{1em}$ $m_{O_2}=\left(32.0\ \ce{g\ {mol}}^{-1}\right)n_{tank}=\left(32.0\ \ce{g\ {mol}}^{-1}\right)\left(8.076\ \ce{mol}\right)=0.258$ kg

        If the astronaut ejects $70\%$ of the oxygen, their velocity becomes

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v\approx \ 0.70\ \dfrac{m_{O_2}}{M}v_{O2}=0.70\dfrac{0.258}{160}\left(340\ \ce{m\ s^{-1}}\right)=0.384\ \ce{m\ s^{-1}}$

        Hence, the time required to reach the shuttle is given by

        $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{travel}=\dfrac{x}{v}=\dfrac{2,000\ \ce{m}}{0.384\ \ce{m\ s^{-1}}}\approx5,200\ \ce{s}=1.4$ hours

    2. $\hspace{1em}$

        $Moles consumed = (0.30)(8.076) = 2.42\ \checkmark$

        $t_{breathe}= \dfrac{8.076\, \ce{mol}}{1.125\, \ce{mol}\, \ce{h^{-1}}} ≈2.2$ hours

        $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{breathe}>t_{travel}\ \left(astronaut\ survives\right)$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
After releasing $70\%$ of the oxygen supply, the breathing time available to the astronaut is given by

        $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{breath}=\left(\dfrac{100-70}{100}\right)\dfrac{n_{tank}}{n_{breath}}=0.30\dfrac{8.076\ \ce{mol}}{1.125\ \ce{mol\ {hour}}^{-1}}\approx2.2$ hours

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\therefore\ t_{breath}>t_{travel}$

        As the travel time is less than the available breathing time, the astronaut survives.
