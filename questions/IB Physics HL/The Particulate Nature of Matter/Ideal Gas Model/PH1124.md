# PH1124

A vessel contains a mixture of helium and methane gas. 40\% of the mass of the gas is composed of helium, with the rest composed of methane.

If there is a total of 11 moles of gas in the vessel, what is the number of moles of methane in the gas?

Molar mass of helium =  $4\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}$\
Molar mass of methane = $16\,\ce{g}\,\textrm{mol}^{-1}$

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ 3

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ 4

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ 7

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ 8

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Adopting the notation He for helium and CH$_4$ for methane

- $\dfrac{\textrm{Mass of CH}_4}{\textrm{Mass of He}}=\dfrac{60\%}{40\%}=\dfrac{3}{2}$

- Mass = Molar mass $\times n$

Where $n$ is the number of moles

- $\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{(\textrm{molar mass of CH}_4)(n \textrm{ of CH}_4)}{(\textrm{molar mass of He})(n \textrm{ of He})}$

- $\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{(16)(n \textrm{ of CH}_4)}{(4)(n \textrm{ of He})}$

- $\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{4(n \textrm{ of CH}_4)}{(n \textrm{ of He})}$

- $\dfrac{3}{8}= \dfrac{n \textrm{ of CH}_4}{n \textrm{ of He}}$

So we can say that that the ratio

- $(n \textrm{ of CH}_4):(n \textrm{ of He}) = 3:8$

Splitting 11 moles into the ratio $3:8$  we have 3  moles of methane and 8 moles of helium.
