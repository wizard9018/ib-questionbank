# PH0750

An ideal gas piston of total volume $900\ $cm$^3$ contains $600\ $cm$^3$ of air and 300 cm$^3$ of water at a temperature of $27\degree$ C. The system is cooled and the piston contracts at constant pressure.

What is the approximate total piston volume at the point where the water first starts to freeze?

![PH0750aaaa](images/PH0750aaaa.png)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $273\ $cm$^3$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $546\ $cm$^3$
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $819\ $cm$^3$
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $846\ $cm$^3$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The initial temperature is $27\degree $C = 300 K

From the ideal gas law, the volume of gas at constant pressure obeys the relation

$\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{V}{T}=constant$

Hence, the final volume of the gas is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $V_{final}=\dfrac{T_{final}}{T_{initial}} \times V_{initial}=\dfrac{273}{300}(600\ {\ce{cm}}^3)=546\ {\ce{cm}}^3$

Adding in the volume of the water yields a total volume of

$\hspace{1em}$ $V_{total}=V_{water}+V_{final}=846\ {\ce{cm}}^3$

Answer **A** is obtained by switching the initial water and gas volumes, then forgetting to add the water volume to the final gas volume.

Answer **B** results from calculating the final gas volume correctly but forgetting to add the water volume.

Answer **C** is yielded by inserting the total piston volume into the ideal gas equation rather than the gas volume only.
