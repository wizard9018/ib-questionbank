# PH1858

Two sealed containers are connected by a closed valve. Both contain the same ideal gas and are kept at the same temperature.

![Screenshot 2025-08-25 at 5.49.53 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-08-25 at 5.49.53 PM.png>)

One of the containers has a volume $V$ and inside, gas is at a pressure $P$.

The second container has a volume $2V$ and inside, gas is at a pressure $\dfrac{1}{2}P$.

The valve is opened so that gas can flow freely across containers. Eventually an equilibrium pressure $P_f$ is reached.

Assuming that the temperature of both gases remains constant at all times, which of the following correctly describes the value of $P_f$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2}{3}P$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3}{4}P$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{4}{5}P$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{5}{6}P$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Initially, the number of particles $N_1$ in the first container is given by:

- $N_1=\dfrac{PV}{k_BT}$

Similarly, the number of particles $N_2$ in the second container is given by:

- $N_2=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}P\right)(2V)}{k_BT}=\dfrac{PV}{k_BT}$

This means that there is an equal number of particles in each container.

After the valve is opened, the system consists of a gas with a total number of particles

- $N=N_1+N_2=2\dfrac{PV}{k_BT}$, occupying a total volume $3V$.

We can now use the ideal gas law to find the final equilibrium pressure of the gas, substituting in the combined volume $3V$ and total particles $N$:

- $P_f(3V)=Nk_BT$

Substituting $N$ leads to:

- $P_f3V=2\dfrac{PV}{k_BT}k_BT$

That can be simplified to:

- $P_f=\dfrac{2}{3}P$
