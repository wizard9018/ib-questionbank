# PH0875

Two pistons of equal initial volume are connected so that a displacement $x$ of piston A causes an equal displacement of Piston B. The pistons contain the same number of gas molecules with the same average molecular velocity, but the molecules in Piston A are half as massive as those in Piston B. The cross-sectional area of Piston A is three times greater than the cross-sectional area of Piston B. No external forces act on either piston.

![Screenshot 2024-12-17 at 2.05.19 PM](<images/Screenshot 2024-12-17 at 2.05.19 PM.JPEG>)

How do the pistons move relative to their initial positions?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ Piston A expands and Piston B compresses.

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ Piston A compresses and Piston B expands.

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ Neither piston moves.

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ Insufficient information is provided.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The initial pressure in Piston A can be expressed as

- $P_A = \dfrac{{\rho}_A v_A^2}{3}$

Because the masses of the particles are related by a factor of 2, the densities are also related by this factor and it follows that

- $P_A = \dfrac{1}{3}\Bigl(\dfrac{{\rho}_B}{2}\Bigr)v_B^2$

Hence, the initial pressure inside Piston A is half the initial pressure inside Piston B.

The total force applied by Piston A is given by

- $F_A=P_A A_A$

We are told that $A_A=3A_B$

Expressing the pressure and cross-sectional area of Piston A in terms of the pressure and cross-sectional area of Piston B gives

- $F_A = P_A A_A = \dfrac{P_B}{2} (3A_B) = \dfrac{3}{2}F_B$

Thus, the force applied by Piston A is greater than the force applied by Piston B, meaning that Piston A expands and Piston B compresses.
