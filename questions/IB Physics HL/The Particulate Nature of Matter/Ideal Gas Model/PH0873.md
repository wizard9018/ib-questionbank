# PH0873

A vertical piston of cross-sectional area $10\,\ce{cm^2}$ contains $10^{-2}$ moles of ideal gas at a pressure of $100\, \ce{kPa}$. The piston has an equilibrium internal length of 10 cm.

![image](images/image.png)

What is the average kinetic energy per mole of gas?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $0.5\, \ce{\ce{k}J\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.0\, \ce{\ce{k}J\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.5\, \ce{\ce{k}J\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $3.0\,\ce{\ce{k}J\,\text{mol}^{-1}}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The kinetic energy of a gas is found from the equation for internal energy

- $U=\dfrac{3}{2}nRT$

The ideal gas law gives us

- $PV=nRT$

And so the internal energy equation becomes

- $U=\dfrac{3}{2}PV$

Volume can be calculated to becomes

- $V = (10\ \ce{cm}^2)(10\ \ce{cm})=100\ \ce{cm}^3 = 0.0001\ \ce{m}^3$

Giving

- $U=\dfrac{3}{2}(100000)(0.0001) =15$ J

The kinetic energy per mole of gas is therefore

- $\dfrac{E_k}{n^{-1}}=\dfrac{15}{0.01}=1500\ $J mol$^{-1}$

**OR**

The pressure $P$ inside the piston is related to gas density $\rho$ and average molecular velocity v via the equation

- $P=\dfrac{\rho v^2}{3}$

Expressing $\rho$ in terms of the molar quantity $n$ of gas, molar mass $M$, cross-sectional area $A$ and internal length $L$ yields

- $P=\dfrac{nMv^2}{3AL}$

The average kinetic energy per mole of gas is given by

- $E_k=\dfrac{mv^2}{2}$

- $\therefore P = \dfrac{2nE_k}{3AL}$

Rearranging for $E_k$ gives

- $E_k=\dfrac{3ALP}{2n}$

Inserting the values provided yields

- $\therefore E_k = \dfrac{3ALP}{2n} = \dfrac{3(0.001\,\ce{m}^2)(0.1\,\ce{m})(100000\,\ce{Pa})}{2(0.01\,\text{mol})}=1500\,\ce{J}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$
