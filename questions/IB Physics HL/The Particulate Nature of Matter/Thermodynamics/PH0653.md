## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. ::indent
        - $Q = \Delta U + W$

        Thermal energy added to the system becomes an increase in internal energy of the gas $\checkmark$

        Work done by the gas on the environment is a decrease in the internal energy of the gas $\checkmark$

    2. ::indent
        - $W=p\Delta V=\left(2.2\times{10}^5\right)\left(1.2\times{10}^{-3}-4.0\times{10}^{-4}\right)$ $\checkmark$

        - $W$ = :answer[$176\ \ce{J}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The relationship between work done $W$, pressure $p$ and change in volume $\Delta V$:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $W=p\Delta V=p(V_2-V_1)= (2.2 \times 10^5) (1.2 \times 10^{-3} -4.0 \times 10^{-4}) =176$ J

        A positive value represents the fact that the gas has done work on its surroundings.

    3. ::indent
        - $\Delta U=Q-W=810-180$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta U$ = :answer[630 J]   (Increase) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        According to the 1st law of thermodynamics;

        $\hspace{1em}$ $Q=\Delta U+W$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta U=Q-W$

        Since thermal energy is supplied to the system from the surroundings and the work is done by the system, $Q$ and $W$ are positive.

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta U=810-180=630$ J

        Since the value of change in internal energy is positive, it is an increase.

    4. ::indent
        - $\Delta T=\dfrac{2}{3} \dfrac{\Delta U}{nR}$ $\checkmark$

        - = $\dfrac{(2)(630)}{(3)(0.52)(8.31)}$ $\checkmark$

        - = :answer[$97\ \ce{K}$] $\checkmark$

        *Allow ECF for use of incorrect  answers from  (a)(iii)*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The change in temperature $\Delta T$ can be found by:

        - $\Delta U = \dfrac{3}{2} nR\Delta T$

        - $\Delta T = \dfrac{2}{3} \dfrac{\Delta U}{nR} = \dfrac{(2)(630)}{(3)(0.52)(8.31)} = 97.2$ K

    1. $A\rightarrow\ B:$ Isobaric $\checkmark$

        $B\rightarrow\ C:$ Adiabatic $\checkmark$

        $C\rightarrow\ A:$ Isovolumetric/Isochoric $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Between **A** and **B**, the pressure is constant. Therefore, it is an isobaric process.

        Between **B** and **C**, there is no exchange of heat. Hence, it is adiabatic.

        For the **C** to **A**, the volume is constant. So it is an isovolumetric (also called isochoric) process.

    2. No heat is transferred into or out of the system $\checkmark$

        Does not change $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Entropy is a measure of the amount of disorder in a system. From **B** to **C**, the change is adiabatic which means that no heat enters or leaves the system. Because there is no exchange of energy between the gas and the surroundings, there is no change in entropy.

        Alternatively,

        - $\Delta S = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$

        - As $\Delta Q = 0$, $\Delta S = 0$

    3. The work is done on the gas by the surrounding ($W$ is negative) and no heat energy is exchanged with the environment ($Q$ = 0) $\checkmark$

        - $Q=\Delta U+W$ $\checkmark$

        $\Delta U$ is positive, therefore internal energy (and therefore temperature) increases $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The work is done on the gas by the surrounding during the process

        Therefore using the IB sign convention, $W$ is negative.

        No thermal energy is exchanged with the surroundings.

        Therefore $Q$ = 0

        Rearranging $Q=\Delta U+W$

        Gives

        - $\Delta U\ =\ Q\ -\ W$

        - $\Delta U\ =\ 0\ -\ W$

        Thus $\Delta U$ is positive. Because internal energy depends on temperature, there must be an increase in temperature.

    4. An increase in temperature results in an increase in the entropy of the gas $\checkmark$

        The increase in entropy is balanced with a decrease in entropy due to a decrease in volume $\checkmark$

        Not violated $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The increase in temperature would result in an increase in the entropy of the gas but this increase is balanced by the decrease in entropy due to the decrease in the volume of the gas.
