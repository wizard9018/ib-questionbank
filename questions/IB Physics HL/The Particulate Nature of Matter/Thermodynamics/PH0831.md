# PH0831

An ideal gas, kept at a constant pressure of $5.00 \times 10^5$ Pa, has an initial volume of $0.040\,\ce{m^3}$. Work is done on the gas as it is compressed at constant pressure down to a volume of $0.020\,\ce{m^3}$. How much heat is transferred from the gas to the surroundings during the process?

![image](images/image.png)

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.5 \times 10^3$ J

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $2.5 \times 10^3$ J

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.5 \times 10^4$ J

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $2.5 \times 10^4$ J

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From the data booklet:

- $Q= \Delta U+W$

To find ($W$) the work done on the gas:

- $W= p\Delta V$

- $W=(5.00 \times10^5)(0.02-0.04) $

- $W=-1.00 \times10^4$ J

From the data booklet:

To find $\Delta U$ the change in internal energy of the gas:

- $\Delta U=\dfrac{3}{2} nR\Delta T$

and:

- $pV=nRT$

therefore:

- $\Delta U=  \dfrac{3}{2} p\Delta V$

The pressure is constant, so:

- $\Delta U=  \dfrac{3}{2}(-1.0 \times 10^4)$

- $\Delta U= -1.5 \times 10^4$ J

Finally:

- $Q= \Delta U+W$

- $Q=(-1.5 \times 10^4) +(-1.0\times10^4) $

- $Q=-2.5 \times 10^4$ J

$Q$ represents the thermal energy transfer into the gas, therefore the negative sign shows that this amount of thermal energy is transferred out of the gas to the surroundings.
