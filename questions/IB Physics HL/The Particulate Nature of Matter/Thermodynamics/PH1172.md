# PH1172

**Maximum mark: 6**

A liquid boils at $70\degree$C. The number of possible microstates of a very small sample of liquid molecules at the boiling point temperature is $6.0\times10^{93}$.

1. 1. Deduce what happens to the entropy of the sample during the boiling process. [2 marks]

    2. Show that the entropy of the molecules at the beginning of the boiling process is around $3\times10^{-21}\,\ce{J\,K^{-1}}$. [2 marks]

2. The entropy of a much larger sample of the liquid during the boiling process changes by $12\,\ce{J\,K^{-1}}$.  Determine the energy transferred to the liquid as it boils. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Molecules are less ordered / Energy is given to the sample and $\Delta S = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$ / more microstates are available $\checkmark$

        Entropy increases $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Entropy is a quantitative measure of the level of randomness or disorder in a system. Higher levels of disorder indicate higher entropy. Where a liquid changes from a more structured state to a more disorganized state, entropy increases. During boiling, the molecules in the vapour have more freedom to move than they did in the liquid, leading to an exponential increase in the number of possible microstates. This increase in potential microstates corresponds directly to a rise in entropy.

    2. ::indent
        - $S=(1.38\times10^{-23})\ln(6.0\times10^{93})$  $\checkmark$

        - $\hspace{1em}=2.98\times10^{-21}\,\ce{J\,K^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $S=k_b\ln\Omega$

        - $S=(1.38\times10^{-23})\ln(6.0\times10^{93})$

        - $S=2.98\times10^{-21}\,\ce{J\,K^{-1}}$

2. The boiling temperature of the liquid in Kelvin = $273+70=343$ K $\checkmark$

    - $(12)(343)=4.1\times10^3\, \ce{J}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The boiling temperature $T$ of the liquid in Kelvin:

    - $T=273+70=343$ K

    Change in entropy $\Delta S$ of the liquid: $12\,\ce{J\,K^{-1}}$

    The energy transferred $\Delta Q$ to the liquid:

    - $\Delta S=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$

    - $\Delta Q=\Delta ST$

    - $\Delta Q=(12)(343)=4.1\times10^3$ J
