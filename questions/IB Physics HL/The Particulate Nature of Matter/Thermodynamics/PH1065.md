# PH1065

**Maximum mark: 10**

An ideal monatomic gas undergoes three thermodynamic processes in a cycle as shown in the $pV$ diagram. The process includes an isothermal process, an adiabatic process and an isovolumetric process.

![Screenshot 2025-05-01 at 7.56.46 AM](<images/Screenshot 2025-05-01 at 7.56.46 AM.JPEG>)

1. Determine which part of the diagram represents an adiabatic process. [3 marks]

2. Calculate the volume of the gas at B. [2 marks]

3. Determine whether the entropy of the gas increases, decreases, or stays the same in the process from B to C. [3 marks]

4. Calculate how much thermal energy is added or removed in the process from C to A. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. AB $\checkmark$\
Steeper curve $\checkmark$\
Comparison to temperature change of curve BC  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    In an adiabatic process there is no heat added or removed, so a temperature change must take place. Because AC represents the isovolumetric process, we must compare curves AB and BC to determine which is isothermal and which is adiabatic.

    If AB were isothermal, then BC represents a decrease in temperature on a compression. Adiabatic compressions result in an increase in temperature.

    Therefore, BC must be the constant temperature process, with the process AB being an adiabatic expansion where there is a temperature decrease. The curve represents an expansion where the final state is on a lower temperature isotherm than the initial state.

2. ::indent
    - $\ll\ p_B V_B^{\frac{5}{3}} = p V_A^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \gg$

    - $V_B = \left(\dfrac{(4.2 \times 10^5)(1.5 \times\ 10^{-3})^{\frac{5}{3}}}{2.3 \times\ 10^5} \right)^\frac{3}{5}$ $\checkmark$

    - $V_B =$ :answer[$2.15\ \times\ 10^{-3}\ \ll \ce{m}^3 \gg$ ] $\checkmark$

    ***OR***

    - $P_BV_B = P_CV_C$

    - $(2.3 \times 10^5)V_B = (3.3 \times 10^5)(1.5 \times 10^{-3})$ $\checkmark$

    - $V_B=$ :answer[$2.15 \times 10^{-3}\ \ll \ce{m}^3 \gg$ ] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The AB curve is steeper than the BC curve, so AB must be the adiabatic change.

    Using the adiabatic formula, we can solve

    - $p_B V_B^{\frac{5}{3}} = p V_A^{\frac{5}{3}}$

    - $V_B = \left(\dfrac{(4.2 \times\ 10^5)(1.5 \times 10^{-3})^{\frac{5}{3}}}{2.3 \times 10^5} \right)^\frac{3}{5}$

    - $V_B = 2.15 \times 10^{-3}$ m$^3$

    Alternatively, the BC curve will follow the isothermal relationship

    - $P_BV_B = P_CV_C$

    - $(2.3 \times 10^5)V_B = (3.3 \times 10^5)(1.5 \times 10^-3)$

    - $V_B=2.15 \times10^{-3}\ \ce{m}^3$

3. $Q\ =\ W$ / no change in internal energy / $\Delta\ U\ =\ 0$ $\checkmark$\
Work done by the gas is negative / $W\ <\ 0$ $\checkmark$\
Entropy decreases $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The entropy is given by

    - $S = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$

    From B to C, it is an isothermal process, so the temperature is constant and there's no change in internal energy

    - $\Delta U = 0$

    From the first law of thermodynamics

    - $Q\ = \Delta U + W$

    Then

    - $Q = W$

    The gas is compressed, this means that work is done on the system and because work is defined as positive when work is done BY the gas, the work done here is negative

    - $W < 0$

    Then

    - $Q < 0$

    This leads to a decrease in entropy.

4. ::indent
    - $Q = \dfrac{3}{2} \Delta PV$ $\checkmark$

    - $Q =$ :answer[202.5 J] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The process is isovolumetric,

    - $W = 0$

    Then

    - $Q = \Delta U$

    The pressure is increasing and the gas is moving to a higher isotherm (higher temperature), therefore

    - $\Delta U > 0$

    and

    - $Q > 0$

    Since

    - $\Delta U = \dfrac{3}{2} nR \Delta T$

    and

    - $PV = nRT$

    then

    - $\Delta U = \dfrac{3}{2} \Delta PV$

    - $\Delta U = \dfrac{3}{2} (4.2 \times 10^5-3.3 \times 10^5) (1.5 \times 10^{-3}) = 202.5$ J

    - $Q = 202.5$ J
