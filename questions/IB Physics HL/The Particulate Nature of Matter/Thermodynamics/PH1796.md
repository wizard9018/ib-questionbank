# PH1796

An ideal gas undergoes an adiabatic change resulting in an increase in pressure. Below are three statements about the change.

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | There is no transfer of heat energy to or from the gas |
  | II. | Work is done on the gas |
  | III. | The temperature of the gas increases |

Which of the statements are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

**I is correct** - by definition an adiabatic change is one in which no heat is transferred into or out of the system. $Q=0$.

**II is correct** - For an adiabatic change $PV^{5/3}=\textrm{constant}$. We are told that pressure P increases, so volume must decrease. Hence work must be done on the gas (this is a compression.)

**III is correct** - The first law of thermodynamics tells us that $Q=\Delta U+W$. Where W is positive when work is done by the gas. For an adiabatic change where $Q=0$ this becomes $\Delta U=-W$. Because work is on the gas, the value of $W$ is negative, and so $U$ must be positive. Internal energy is directly proportional to temperature so temperature increases.
