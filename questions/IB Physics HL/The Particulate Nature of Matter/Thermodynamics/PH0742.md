# PH0742

The pressure-volume diagram below shows an adiabatic process from $A$ to $B$ and an isovolumetric process from $B$ to $C$.

![PH0742a](images/PH0742a.png)

Which of the following is correct about the change in internal energy $ΔU$ of processes $A→B$ and $B→C$?

$\\$

| | A→B | B→C |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Negative $\hspace{1.5em}$|$\hspace{1.5em}$Negative$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Negative | Positive |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Positive | Negative|
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Positive | Positive |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For the adiabatic process A→B, no heat enters or leaves the system

- $Q=0$

During the expansion, work is done by the gas

- $W>0$

From the first law of thermodynamics

- $Q=\Delta U+W$

Then

- $\Delta U=-W$

∆U is negative.

For the isovolumetric process B→C, there is no work done

- $W=0$

Since the pressure is decreasing at constant volume, then the temperature is decreasing and heat energy is removed

- $Q<0$

From the first law of thermodynamics

- $Q=\Delta U+W$

Then

- $\Delta U=Q$

∆U is negative
