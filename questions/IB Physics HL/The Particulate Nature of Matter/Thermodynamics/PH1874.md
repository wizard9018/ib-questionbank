    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    A Carnot cycle consists of two isotherms and two adiabats.

    From 1 $\rightarrow$ 2 we have an isothermal expansion - the volume is increased so the gas expands, and the temperature of the gas remains constant along this curve.

    From 2 $\rightarrow$ 3 we have an adiabatic expansion - the volume of the gas is increased, and no heat is transferred into or out of the system. The temperature of the gas decreases along this curve.

    From 3 $\rightarrow$ 4 we have an isothermal compression - the volume of the gas is decreased so the gas contracts, and the temperature of the gas remains constant.

    From 4 $\rightarrow$ to 1 we have an adiabatic compression - the volume of the gas is decreased, and no heat is transferred into or out of the system. The temperature of the gas increases along this curve.

2. $\hspace{1.9em}$ $P_1V_1^{\frac{5}{3}}=P_2V_2^{\frac{5}{3}}$

    - $V_2=$ :answer[$0.42$ m$^3$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    During an adiabatic process, the pressure and volume of an ideal gas are related by:

    - $PV^{\frac{5}{3}}=\textrm{constant}$

    Therefore, the pressure and volume at the start and end of the adiabatic expansion from 2 $\rightarrow$ 3 may be written as:

    - $P_1V_1^{\frac{5}{3}}=P_2V_2^{\frac{5}{3}}$

    Rearranging for the volume at the end of the adiabatic expansion, $V_2$:

    - $V_2^{\frac{5}{3}}=\dfrac{P_1V_1^{\frac{5}{3}}}{P_2}$

    - $V_2=\left(\dfrac{P_1V_1^{\frac{5}{3}}}{P_2}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}}$

    Substituting in the values:

    - $V_2=\left(\dfrac{(150 000)(0.3^{\frac{5}{3}})}{85 000}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}}=0.42$ m$^3$

3. $\hspace{1.9em}$ $T=\dfrac{PV}{nR}=\dfrac{(85 000)(0.42)}{(10)(8.31)}$

    - $T=$ :answer[$429.6\ll=430\gg$ K] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    We use the ideal-gas equation that includes the number of moles, $n$, and the gas constant, $R$:

    - $PV=nRT$

    Rearranging for $T$:

    - $T=\dfrac{PV}{nR}$

    Substituting in the values:

    - $T=\dfrac{(85000)(0.42)}{(10)(8.31)}=430$ K

4. $\hspace{1.9em}$ $\eta=1-\dfrac{T_c}{T_h}=0.21$ $\checkmark$

    - $Q_H=\dfrac{W}{\eta}=\dfrac{65}{0.21}=$ :answer[313 J] $\checkmark$

    Note -- due to variations in rounding, any answer between 310 J and 315 J is accepted.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    To find the efficiency of the Carnot cycle, we use the equation:

    - $\eta=1-\dfrac{T_c}{T_h}$

    Substituting in the values:

    - $\eta=1-\dfrac{429.6}{542}=0.207$

    Efficiency is given by the equation

    - $\eta=\dfrac{W}{Q_H}$

    Giving

    - $Q_H=\dfrac{W}{\eta}=\dfrac{65}{0.207}=313$ J
