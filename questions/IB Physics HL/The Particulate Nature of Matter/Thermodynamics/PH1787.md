1.    For one cycle, the net work done on the gas is $70$ J. Show that during process $A\rightarrow B$ $170$ J of heat energy leaves the gas. [3 marks]

2. 1. Deduce whether entropy increases, decreases or stays the same during the process $A\rightarrow B$ and explain why this does not violate the second law of thermodynamics. [2 marks]

    Process $A\rightarrow B$ occurs at a temperature of $300$ K. The number of possible microstates of the gas in states $A$ and $B$ are $\Omega_A$ and $\Omega_B$ respectively.

    2. The ratio $\dfrac{\Omega_B}{\Omega_A}$ may be written in the form $\dfrac{\Omega_B}{\Omega_A}=e^{-\alpha}$. Determine the constant $\alpha$ giving your answer to 2 significant figures. [3 marks]

1. $\ll$Work done by gas during process $C\rightarrow A\gg$ $=P\Delta V=(2.0\times10^4)(5.0\times10^{-3})=100$ J $\checkmark$

    Total work done on the gas during $A\rightarrow B$ is $100+70=170$ J, W$=-170$ J $\checkmark$

    - $Q=\Delta U+W$, $\Delta U=0$, $Q$ = :answer[$-170$ J] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Process $A\rightarrow B$ is isothermal so there is no change in temperature and hence no change in internal energy $\left(\Delta U=\dfrac{3}{2}Nk_b\Delta T\right)$. Using the second law of thermodynamics we have:

    - $Q_{AB}=\Delta U_{AB}+W_{AB}$

    - $Q_{AB}=W_{AB}$

    Process $B\rightarrow C$ is isovolumetric so there is no work done on or by the gas:

    - $W_{BC}=0$ J

    Process $C\rightarrow A$ is isobaric. The volume increases so work is done by the gas. The work done on the gas is given by:

    - $W_{CA}=P\Delta V=(2.0\times10^4)((7.0\times10^{-3})-(2.0\times10^{-3}))$

    - $W_{CA}=(2.0\times10^4)(5.0\times10^{-3})=100$ J

    The total work done by the gas is given by:

    - $W_{total}=W_{AB}+W_{BC}+W_{CA}$

    - $W_{total}=-70$ J

    - $W_{CA}=100$ J

    - $W_{BC}=0$ J

    - $W_{AB}=Q$

    Substituting these values we have:

    - $-70=100+Q$

    - $Q=-170$ J

2. 1. $V$ decreases $\ll$while $T$ remains the same$\gg$

        There is less disorder so $S$ decreases $\checkmark$

        **OR**

        Change in heat energy/ is negative

        $\Delta S=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$ is negative $\checkmark$

        The entropy of the surroundings must have increased $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Volume decreases while temperature remains constant, so the particles have less available space to move, reducing the number of possible microstates. This results in a decrease in entropy of the system.

        Alternatively, considering heat transfer, if the system loses heat during the process, the change in heat energy $\Delta Q$ is negative. Using the equation for entropy change:

        - $\Delta S=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$

        $\Delta Q$ is negative so $\Delta S$ is negative.

        The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system cannot decrease. While the system's entropy decreases, the entropy of the surroundings must increase by at least the same amount due to the heat being transferred to the surroundings. This ensures that the overall entropy of the universe either increases or remains the same, satisfying the second law of thermodynamics.

    2. ::indent
        - $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}=k_B\textrm{ln}(\Omega_A)$ $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{k_BT}=\textrm{ln}\left(\dfrac{\Omega_B}{\Omega_A}\right)$ $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{\Omega_B}{\Omega_A}=e^{\frac{\Delta Q}{T}}=e^{\frac{-170}{(1.38\times10^{-23})(300)}}=e^{-4.1\times10^{22}}$

        - $\alpha=$ :answer[$4.1\times10^{22}$] (2sf) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The entropy of a system $S$ is related to the number of microstates of the system $\Omega$ by the following equation:

        - $S=k_B\textrm{ln}(\Omega)$

        Where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant $=1.38\times10^{-23}$. The change in entropy of the system is therefore given by:

        - $\Delta S=k_B\textrm{ln}(\Omega_B )-k_B\textrm{ln}(\Omega_A)$

        The change in entropy of a system may also be expressed:

        - $\Delta S=\dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}$

        Where $\Delta Q$ is the change in heat energy and $T$ is temperature.

        Combining these equations we have:

        - $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{T}=k_B\textrm{ln}(\Omega_B)-k_B\textrm{ln}(\Omega_A)$

        - $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{k_BT}=\textrm{ln}(\Omega_B)-\textrm{ln}(\Omega_A)$

        Now using the law of subtraction for logarithms $\left(\textrm{ln}(A)-\textrm{ln}(B)=\textrm{ln}\Bigl(\dfrac{A}{B}\Bigr)\right)$:

        - $\dfrac{\Delta Q}{k_BT}=\textrm{ln}\left(\dfrac{\Omega_B}{\Omega_A}\right)$

        Now taking the exponential of both sides and substituting in our values:

        - $\dfrac{\Omega_B}{\Omega_A}=e^{\frac{\Delta Q}{T}}=e^{\frac{-170}{(1.38\times10^{-23})(300)}}=e^{-4.1\times10^{22}}$

        Finally comparing this to $e^{-\alpha}$ we have $\alpha=4.1\times10^{22}$ (2sf)
