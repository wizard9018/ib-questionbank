# PH1749

A sample of an ideal gas is expanded at a constant pressure of $2.00\times10^5$ Pa from an initial volume of $1.40\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m}^3$ to a volume of $2.40\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m}^3$.

Which of the following best describes the amount of thermal energy inserted into the system during this process?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ 200 J

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ 300 J

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ 400 J

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ 500 J

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The amount of heat inserted into the system can be calculated using the first law of thermodynamics:

- $Q=\Delta U+W$

The variation in internal energy ($\Delta U$) can be expressed as  $\dfrac{3}{2}nR\Delta T$.

From the gas law equation

- $PV=nRT$

With $P$, $n$ and $R$ constant, we can then say that

- $P\Delta V=nR\Delta T$

And so the change in internal energy is given by

- $\Delta U= \dfrac{3}{2}nR\Delta T$

The amount of work done by the gas ($W$) can be expressed as $P\Delta V$.

Inserting these two expressions into the First Law of Thermodynamics leads to:

- $Q=\Delta U+W$

- $Q=\dfrac{3}{2}P\Delta V+P\Delta V$

- $Q=\dfrac{5}{2}P\Delta V$

We can now substitute the given values to calculate the value of $Q$

- $Q=\dfrac{5}{2}(2.00\times10^5\,\ce{Pa})(2.40\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m}^3-1.40\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m}^3)$

- $Q=\dfrac{5}{2}(2.00\times10^5\,\ce{Pa})(1.00\times10^{-3}\,\ce{m}^3)$

- $Q=500$ J
