# PH1868

Pendulums $A$ and $B$ oscillate with the same mass and the same total energy. The amplitudes of oscillation are small, however the amplitude of pendulum $A$ is twice that of pendulum $B$.

What is the ratio:

- $\hspace{8em} \dfrac{\textrm{length of pendulum }A}{\textrm{length of pendulum }B}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{4}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $2$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $4$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The total energy of an oscillating object is given by:

- $E_T=\dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2x_0^2$

We can write $\omega$ in terms of length using the equation for the time period of a pendulum.

- $\begin{aligned}
\ T&=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}\\
\\\
\ \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{l}}&=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\\
\end{aligned}$

By definition $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ so we have:

- $\begin{aligned}
\ \omega&=\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{l}}\\
\\\
\ \omega^2&=\dfrac{g}{l}\\
\end{aligned}$

Substituting this into our energy equation gives:

- $E_T=\dfrac{1}{2}m\dfrac{g}{l}x_0^2$

Since $E_T$, $m$, and $g$ are the same for pendulums $A$ and $B$ we have:

- $\dfrac{(x_0)^2}{l}=$ constant

Therefore:

- $\begin{aligned}
\ \dfrac{(x_0)^2_A}{l_A}&=\dfrac{(x_0)^2_B}{l_B}\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{(x_0)^2_A}{(x_0)^2_B}&=\dfrac{l_A}{l_B}\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{l_A}{l_B}&=\left(\dfrac{x_{0A}}{x_{0B}}\right)^2\\
\end{aligned}$

Now, $x_{0A}=2x_{0B}$ so we have:

- $\begin{aligned}
\ \dfrac{l_A}{l_B}&=\left(\dfrac{2x_{0B}}{x_{0B}}\right)^2\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{l_A}{l_B}&=2^2\\
\\\
\ \dfrac{l_A}{l_B}&=4\\
\end{aligned}$
