# PH0734

In a laboratory Harry performs 2 experiments to determine the motion of a pendulum in a clock.

For experiment **A**, Harry has 2 identical pendula swinging as shown in the diagram (assume that friction is negligible). He records data for the time period after 10 oscillations of both pendulums.

![PH0734](images/PH0734.jpg)

For experiment **B**, Harry then reduces the string length of pendulum 1 by 10 cm, keeping the release angle the same as before. He does not change pendulum 2's string length but releases it from a position $5\degree$ closer to the equilibrium position than in experiment **A**.

![PH0734b](images/PH0734b.jpg)

Which of the following statements **are** correct?

- I. $\hspace{1em}$ The time period ($T$) is shorter for both pendula in experiment **B** compared to experiment **A**.
- II. $\hspace{.7em}$ Pendulum 1 has a higher frequency ($f$) in experiment **B**.
- III. $\hspace{.4em}$ Pendulum 1 has the same acceleration at the equilibrium point in experiments **A** and **B**.

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III.
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III.
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ III only.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Statement I is not correct because reducing the string length for pendulum 1 does make a shorter time period, but reducing the release angle by $5\degree$ does not significantly change the time period for pendulum 2 for 10 oscillations neglecting friction because the small angle approximation still applies.

Statement II is correct because if pendulum 1 has a shorter time period in experiment **B**, it will have a higher frequency due to $T=\dfrac{1}{f}$

Statement III is correct because the string is shorter for pendulum 1 in experiment **B**:

$\hspace{1em}$ The string length is now $\dfrac{6}{7}$ of the original

$\hspace{1em}$ Therefore, when it swings, $\Delta E_g = mgh$ is $\dfrac{6}{7}$ of the original

Because of the conversion of gravitational potential energy to kinetic energy, at the bottom of the swing:

$\hspace{1em}$ $E_k$ is $\dfrac{6}{7}$ of the original

Which means:

$\hspace{1em}$ $v^2$ is $\dfrac{6}{7}$ of the original

$\hspace{1em}$ $r$ is $\dfrac{6}{7}$ of the original

therefore:

$\hspace{1em}$ $a=\ \dfrac{v^2}{r}$ is unchanged (all quantities equate to the same as before the reduction in string length).
