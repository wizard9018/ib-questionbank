# PH0213

A body undergoes simple harmonic motion with amplitude $x_0$ and maximum potential energy $E_p$. The body is released from rest from maximum displacement. What is the magnitude of the change in potential energy of the system after the body has moved a distance of  $\dfrac{x_0}{4}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}    \dfrac{E_p}{16}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}     \dfrac{{7E}_p}{16}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}     \dfrac{9E_p}{16}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}     \dfrac{3E_p}{4}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Our general expression for the potential energy is $E_p = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.

Potential energy at maximum displacement is $E_p = \dfrac{1}{2}kx_0^2$.

The initial potential energy is therefore $E_{p_ i} = \dfrac{1}{2}kx_0^2$.

If the body has moved a distance $\dfrac{x_0}{4}$, it has moved to a position such that its displacement is  $\dfrac{3x_0}{4}$ from the equilibrium point.

The final potential energy is therefore $E_{p_f} = \dfrac{1}{2}k(\dfrac{3x_0}{4})^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}k(\dfrac{9}{16})x_0^2$

Therefore, the change in elastic potential energy

$\begin{aligned}
ΔE_p &= E_{p_f} - E_{p_ i}\\
\hspace{1em}\\
&= \frac{1}{2}k(\frac{9}{16})x_0^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_0^2\\
\hspace{1em}\\
& = -\frac{1}{2}k(\frac{7}{16})x_0^2 \\
\hspace{1em}\\
&= -\frac{7}{16}E_p
\end{aligned}$

The question asks for the magnitude which gives the correct answer of B
