# PH0732

A mass ($M$) is attached to a spring of constant ($k$) and is oscillating in simple harmonic motion across a frictionless surface.

![PH0732](images/PH0732.jpg)

Which of the following, when done together, will reduce the time period ($T$) of oscillation?

$\\$

| | Mass ($M$) | Spring constant ($k$) |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$|$\hspace{1.5em}$ Decrease by $50\%$ $\hspace{1.5em}$| Decrease by $50\%$|
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increase by $50\%$| Increase by $50\%$|
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increase by $50\%$| Decrease by $50\%$|
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Decrease by $50\%$| Increase by $50\%$|

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From the databooklet:

$\hspace{1em}$ $T=\ 2\pi\ \sqrt{\dfrac{M}{k}}$

If BOTH $M$ and $k$ are reduced or increased by 50%, the time period remains the same, therefore answers A and B are incorrect.

Increasing the mass will increase $T$ and if $k$ is decreased, that will also have the effect of increasing $T$. Hence, answer C is incorrect.

If the mass is reduced by $50\%$ and the spring constant ($k$) is also increased by $50\%$ as in response **D**, then:

$\hspace{1em}$ $T=\ 2\pi\ \sqrt{\dfrac{0.5M}{1.5k}}$ will result in a reduction of the time period ($T$)
