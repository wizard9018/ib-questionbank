# PH1107

A tennis ball is held at rest above a simple pendulum consisting of a bucket attached at the end of a rope of length $l$. The bucket and ball are released from rest with the string held taut from an elevated position. The ball subsequently lands in the bucket as the pendulum passes through its lowest point for the second time.

![PH1107](images/PH1107.png)

What is the approximate distance $h$ between the initial position of the tennis ball and the upper end of the rope?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\Bigl(\dfrac{9}{8}\pi^2 - 1\Bigr)l$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\Bigl(\dfrac{3}{2}\pi^2 - 1\Bigr)l$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\Bigl(2\pi^2 - 1\Bigr)l$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\Bigl(\dfrac{9}{4}\pi^2 - 1\Bigr)l$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

As the bucket and ball are released at the same instant, and the ball lands in the bucket as it passes through its lowest point for the second time, the transit time of the ball is equal to 0.75 periods of the bucket’s oscillation: the pendulum first passes through its lowest point after 0.25 periods, and it subsequently arrives at the same point once every half-cycle.

Hence, the transit time of the ball can be expressed as

- $t_{ball}=\dfrac{3}{4}T=\dfrac{3}{4}\Bigl(2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}\Bigr)=\dfrac{3}{2}\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$

The distance $x$ travelled by the ball is given by the equations of linear motion:

- $x=\dfrac{gt_{ball}^2}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\dfrac{3}{2}\pi\Bigr)^2l=\dfrac{9}{8}\pi^2l$

Thus, the initial height $h$ of the ball above the top of the rope is given by

- $h=x-l=\Bigl(\dfrac{9}{8}\pi^2 - 1\Bigr)l$
