# PH0169

An object undergoes simple harmonic motion. The graph shows how acceleration $a$ varies with displacement $x$ from the equilibrium position of the object. 

![PH0169](images/PH0169.png)

Which of the following gives the displacement of the object when its kinetic and potential energies are equal? 

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\sqrt{2}\,\ce{m}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $1\,\ce{m}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\ce{m}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\ce{m}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

One of the characteristics of the simple harmonic motion is the conservation of total energy. 

Additionally, the total energy of the system is equal to maximum kinetic energy;

- $E_{total}=\dfrac{1}{2} m\omega^2 x_0^2 \hspace{2em}$ where $x_0=\sqrt{2}$ according to the graph.

The kinetic energy at any position;

- $E_{\textrm{at any position}}=\frac{1}{2} m\omega^2 (x_0^2-x^2) \hspace{2em}$ where $x$ is the displacement

Due to energy conservation, the lost kinetic energy of the object must be transferred to its potential energy. When half of the maximum kinetic energy is transferred to potential, the object's kinetic energy gets equal to its potential energy. The magnitude of the  displacement of the object when it has half of the maximum kinetic energy can be found by the following relationship;

- $\dfrac{E_{total}}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2} m\omega^2 (x_0^2-x^2)$

By substituting $E_{total}=\dfrac{1}{2} m\omega^2 x_0^2$ in the equation;

- $\begin{aligned}

\frac{1}{4} m\omega^2 x_0^2&=\frac{1}{2} m\omega^2 (x_0^2-x^2)\\

\hspace{1em}\\

x_0^2&=2(x_0^2-x^2)\\

\hspace{1em}\\

x_0^2&=2x^2\\

\hspace{1em}\\

\sqrt{\frac{x_0^2}{2}}&=x

\end{aligned}$

By substituting $x_0=\sqrt{2}$

- $\begin{aligned}

\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{2}^2}{2}}&=x\\

\hspace{1em}\\

1\,\ce{m}&=x

\end{aligned}$
