# PH0663

A mass-spring system oscillates on a horizontal, frictionless surface with an amplitude of 0.10 m. At what displacement from the equilibrium position is the ratio between the potential and kinetic energies 1:3?

- A. 0.025 m

- B. 0.033 m

- C. 0.050 m

- D. 0.067 m

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Since $E_H$ and $E_K$ are in a ratio of 1:3, this means that the potential energy is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the total energy.

For a spring, total energy is equal to the maximum elastic potential energy, which occurs and maxiumum displacement $x_0$.

- $E_{max}=\dfrac{1}{2}kx_0^2$

At the desired location $x$, we know that

- $E_{x}=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2=\dfrac{E_{max}}{4}$

Therefore

- $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}kx_0^2}{4}$

Giving

- $x^2=\dfrac{x_0^2}{4}$

- $x=\dfrac{x_0}{2}=\dfrac{0.1}{2}=0.05$ m

Alternatively:

The total energy is given by:

- $E_T=\dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2x_0^2$

Divide expression of $E_T$ by 4:

- $\dfrac{E_T}{4}=\dfrac{1}{8}m\omega^2x_0^2$

- $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$

- $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$

Rearranging gives

- $k = m \Bigl(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\Bigr)^2$

- $\dfrac{E_T}{4} = \dfrac{1}{8} m \Bigl(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\Bigr)^2 x_0^2 = \dfrac{1}{8} k x_0^2$

The potential energy of a spring, at a given displacement, is given by:

- $E_H = \dfrac{1}{2} k x^2$

- $E_H = \dfrac{1}{2} k x^2 = \dfrac{1}{8} k x_0^2$

- $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} x_0^2$

- $x = \dfrac{1}{2} x_0$

- $x = \dfrac{0.1}{2}$

- $x = 0.05$ m
