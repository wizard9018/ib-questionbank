# PH0665

An object undergoes simple harmonic motion with a period of $T.$ The kinetic energy of the object as it passes the equilibrium position is given by $E$. If the amplitude of oscillations is reduced by 75%, what will be the system's resulting period and total energy?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Period$\hspace{1.5em}$ |$\hspace{1.5em}$ Total Energy$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\dfrac{T}{4}$ | $\dfrac{E}{4}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $T$ | $\dfrac{E}{4}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\dfrac{T}{4}$ | $\dfrac{E}{16}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $T$ | $\dfrac{E}{16}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Period will not change because it is independent of the amplitude of oscillation.

- $T=2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$ (No dependence on $A$ or $x_0$)

The total energy is proportional to amplitude squared, so a quarter of the original amplitude will mean 1/16 energy.

Reduction by 75% means the value is now 25%, or one quarter, of the original.

- $E_{T(1)}=\dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x_0^2 $

- $E_{T(2)}=  \dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 \Bigl (\dfrac{x_0}{4}\Bigr)^2=\dfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x_0^2\Bigl (\dfrac{1}{16}\Bigr)=\dfrac{1}{16}E_{T(1)}$
