# PH0662

A pendulum of length 40 cm is set into motion with an angular amplitude of 0.1 radians. What is the maximum speed of the mass during the oscillation?

- A. $\hspace{1em} 0.1 \textrm{ m}\textrm{ s}^{-1}$

- B. $\hspace{1em} 0.2 \textrm{ m}\textrm{ s}^{-1}$

- C. $\hspace{1em} 1 \textrm{ m}\textrm{ s}^{-1}$

- D. $\hspace{1em} 2 \textrm{ m}\textrm{ s}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Use equation $v=\omega x_0 \cos{\omega t}$ and $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ to yield the correct answer. The 40 cm must also be converted to metres, where $40\,\textrm{cm}=0.40\, \textrm{m}$.

- $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$

- $T=2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$

- $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{l}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{0.4}} = 5 \, \text{rad} \,\textrm{ s}^{-1}$

We need the horizontal displacement. This can be found by using $l \sin{\theta}=x$ , or can use small angle approximation for the amplitude as well ($l\theta=x$), yielding $(0.4)(0.1)=0.04\,\textrm{m}$.

Max speed of oscillation occurs when the cosine (or sine) term equals 1.

- $v=\omega x_0 = (5)(0.04) = 0.2\textrm{ m}\textrm{ s}^{-1}$
