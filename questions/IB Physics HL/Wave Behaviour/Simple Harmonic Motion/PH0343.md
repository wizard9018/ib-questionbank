1. A small ball of mass $m$ is oscillating on a frictionless curved surface.

   ![PH0343-1](images/PH0343-1.png)

    Outline why the ball performs simple harmonic motion. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\ll$According to the given expression, restoring$\gg$ $\underline{\textrm{force/acceleration}}$ acting on the ball is towards to $\underline{\textrm{equilibrium/ point O}} \, \checkmark$

    (OR in the negative direction, suggested by the negative sign in the given formula)

    and is proportional to its $\underline{\textrm{displacement}}$ from the equilibrium/point O $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The defining equation of simple harmonic motion;

     - $a\ \propto-x $

     When ma is substituted in the given expression instead of F;

     - $ma=\ -m(\dfrac{gx}{r})$

     - $a=-\dfrac{gx}{r} \hspace{2em}$  where $\hspace{2em}\dfrac{g}{r}\,$  is constant.

    The expression is similar to the defining equation. It means that acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement and in opposite direction to displacement. Therefore, the motion should be a simple harmonic motion. Explanations with symbols are also accepted by the mark scheme.

2.
    1. Evidence of equating $m\omega^2x=m(\dfrac{gx}{r}) \checkmark$

        - $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{r}} \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Horizontal acceleration of the ball;
$a=-\omega^2x$

        By substituting $ma \,(-{m\omega}^2x)$ in the given expression instead of F;

        $\begin{aligned}     \hspace{2em}    -m\omega^2x&=-m\frac{gx}{r}\\
        \hspace{1em}\\
\omega^2&=\frac{g}{r}\\
\hspace{1em}\\
\omega&=\sqrt{\frac{g}{r}}\\
\end{aligned}$

    2. ::indent
        - $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{r}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{9.81}{0.1}}=9.9\ \ce{rad}{\ \ce{s}}^{-1} \,\checkmark$

        - $T=\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}=\dfrac{2\pi}{9.9}=0.63\ \ce{s}\,\checkmark$

        The time needed for the motion $=\ \dfrac{T}{4}=\dfrac{0.63}{4}=\ $:answer[0.16 s] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        By substituting values of $g$ and $r$, ω is found.
Then rearrange the following relationship to find $T$.

        - $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$

        - $T=\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$

        The mentioned motion will be repeated four times to complete one oscillation. Therefore, the time needed should be one-fourth of the period.
