## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

The kinetic energy of the object during simple harmonic motion; 

- $E_k=\frac{1}{2} mv^2$         

We know that for a simple harmonic oscillator, the speed and therefore the kinetic energy is a maximum at the equilibrium position, and zero at the maximum displacement. This rules out option C.

The total energy of an oscillator is comprised of the sum of the elastic potential energy and the kinetic energy.

Using a spring for reference, we know that the potential energy stored in a spring is given by the equation

- $E_H=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$

This will be quadratic in shape. Subtracting this shape from constant total energy will give a graph that resembles half of a parabola opening downwards.  This corresponds to answer A.

Alternatively, we can substitute 

- $v=\omega \sqrt{x_0^2-x^2}$ 

into the kinetic energy equation to get 

- $E_k=\frac{1}{2} m\omega^2 (x_0^2-x^2)$  

According to this expression, the kinetic energy is maximum at $x=0$ and zero at $x=x_0$. Additionally, it decreases at an increasing rate with the square of displacement. Therefore, the answer is A.
