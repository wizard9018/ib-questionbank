# PH0738

**Calculator required**

A piece of wood is at rest vertically in a pond with 20 cm of the wood above the surface of the water. A frog jumps onto the top of the wood and it starts to oscillate vertically in simple harmonic motion (see diagram).

The wood oscillates with an amplitude ($A$) of $10.0$ cm from the equilibrium position, with a time period ($T$) of 3.0 s for one complete oscillation. The mass of the frog is negligible compared to the mass of the wood.

How far above the surface of the water will the frog be after 10.0 s?

![PH0738](images/PH0738.jpg)

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $10.3$ cm

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $11.3$ cm
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $15.0$ cm
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $18.7$ cm

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The oscillation begins from the equilibrium position at $t=0$ s and moves downwards initially, so the graph of the oscillation is a negative sine curve with the general equation:

- $x = -x_0 \sin (\omega t)$

We can find the angular frequency of the oscillation from

- $\omega = \dfrac{2 \pi}{T}=\dfrac{2 \pi}{3}$

and it is given that the amplitude of oscillation is 10 cm.

The equation for the motion of the system is therefore

- $x = -10 \sin \bigg(\dfrac{2 \pi t}{3}\bigg)$

Substituting in the time of $t$ = 10 s gives

- $x = -10 \sin \bigg(\dfrac{20 \pi}{3}\bigg)$

Solving with a calculator gives

- $x = -8.66$ cm

The system will therefore be 8.66 cm below the equilibrium position.

As the top of the wood is normally 20 cm above the surface, the distance from the frog to the surface at this point in time will be

$\hspace{1em}$ $20-8.66=11.3$ cm above the surface.
