# PH0163

A long pendulum executing simple harmonic motion is released from one end and reaches equilibrium position in 2 s.  Just after the equilibrium position, the length of the pendulum is reduced to one-fourth of its initial value because of wrapping around a nail. 

![Screenshoot 2026-04-08 at 7.54.05 AM](<images/Screenshoot 2026-04-08 at 7.54.05 AM.png>)

What is the period of the pendulum in this situation?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 8 s

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 6 s

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 4 s

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 2 s

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

The period of a simple pendulum can be found by the following equation; 

- $T=2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$

Therefore the period is directly proportional to the square root of the length.

- $T \propto \sqrt{l}$

The time needed to reach from one end of the oscillation to the equilibrium position is one-fourth of the period. Therefore, the period of the pendulum before its length is reduced is; 

- T = 8 s

Just half of the motion will be completed with the initial length. So the time for reaching the equilibrium position and returning from the equilibrium position to starting position is 4 s. 

When the length is reduced to one-fourth of the initial value; 

- $T_{new} \propto \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{4}} \propto \dfrac{1}{2} \sqrt{l}= \dfrac{1}{2} T$

So

- $T_{new} = 4$ s

But just half of the motion will be covered with the new length. Therefore time needed to complete the other half of the oscillation is 2 s.

So, the total time to complete one oscillation-period of the oscillation- is 6 s.
