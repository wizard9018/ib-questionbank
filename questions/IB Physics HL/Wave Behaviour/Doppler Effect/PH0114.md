# PH0114

A train is traveling at 30 m s$^{-1}$  towards a stationary observer. It sounds a horn of frequency 450 Hz.

Which of the following statements is true about the observed frequency compared with the emitted frequency as the train approaches?

$\\$

| | Observed frequency compared to emitted frequency | Change in observed frequency over time |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Lower | No change |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Higher | Increases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Lower | Decreases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Higher | No change |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When the source is moving towards an observer, each new wavefront is created closer to the previously created wavefront than would be the case if the source were at rest. This results in a shortening of the wavelength and an increase in frequency in this direction.

Because the source is moving at a constant speed, this change in wavelength and frequency is constant regardless of distance, as long as the source is approaching the observer.

Alternative explanation for HL students:

The formula for this motion is:

- $f^{'}=f\left(\dfrac{v}{v-u_s}\right)$

  Where $f^{'}$= frequency detected by observer

  $f$ = source frequency

  $v$ = speed of sound in still air

  $u_s$ is the speed of the source.

We can see that the result inside the brackets will give a value larger than 1, and so the observed frequency will be higher than the emitted frequency. As the train speed does not change, the calculated value for the observed frequency will remain constant, although its intensity will increase.
