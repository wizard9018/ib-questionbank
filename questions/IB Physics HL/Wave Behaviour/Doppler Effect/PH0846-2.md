    1. The red light-emitting laser is replaced with the one that emits blue light. Deduce how the intensity graph of the diffraction pattern changes. [2 marks]

    2. The student wants to try to use the Doppler effect to change the angular separation between the first minima and central maxima. The wavelength of the laser beam is $\lambda$, which is also equal to the slit width. The minimum change in angular separation that can be detected on the screen is 0.0002 rad.

        Show that moving the source away from the slit will not produce a detectable change in the angular separation of the maxima. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. A light beam with a single frequency/wavelength $\checkmark$

2.
    1. Decrease in wavelength results in decrease in angular separation of first minimum $\checkmark$

        Minima come closer/narrower maxima/smaller fringe separation $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The angular separation $\theta$ between the first minimum and central maximum:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{b}$

        Where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light and $b$ is the slit width. The angular separation between the minima in a single slit diffraction pattern is directly proportional to the wavelength of the light. Therefore, if the wavelength decreases, the angular separation will also decrease, and the minima will appear closer together.

    2. Use of $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{b}$ to find that $\Delta \lambda = 0.0002\ \lambda$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v = \dfrac{0.0002 \lambda}{\lambda}\  (3.0 \times 10^8) = 6.0 \times 10^4\ \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$ $\checkmark$

        *(This speed is too high to be obtained in the laboratory)*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Since there is a relative motion between the source and the detector (screen), the emitted wavelength and observed wavelength are different.

        According to the formula; $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{b}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta \theta = \dfrac{\lambda_1}{b} - \dfrac{\lambda_2}{b} = \dfrac{\Delta \lambda}{b}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta \lambda = \Delta \theta b = 0.0002\lambda$

        The difference between the emitted wavelength and the observed wavelength is $0.0002 \lambda$.

        By using the formula related to the Doppler effect in electromagnetic waves:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda} = \dfrac{v}{c}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{0.0002 \lambda}{\lambda} = \dfrac{v}{c}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v = \dfrac{0.0002 \lambda}{\lambda} (3.0 \times 10^8) = 6.0 \times 10^4\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

        This speed can not be obtained.
