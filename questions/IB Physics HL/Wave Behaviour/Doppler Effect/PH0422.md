1.  1. A shift in detected frequency $\checkmark$

       Due to relative motion between source and observer $\checkmark$

    2. Observed frequency/$f^\prime=21027$ Hz $\checkmark$

        Use of $f^\prime=f\dfrac{v}{v-u_s}$ $\checkmark$

        Use of $(u_s)(\cos\ (30))$  $\checkmark$

        $21027=21000(\dfrac{350}{350-u_s\cos(30)} )$

        $u_s = $:answer[$0.51 \,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$] $ \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The component of the speed of the source in the direction of the detector should be used in the Doppler effect. The source is approaching the detector at the instant seen in the figure. Therefore, the observed frequency is greater. Additionally, the frequency of the emitted wave is known. Therefore, this situation can be regarded as a moving source and stationary observer.

        - $f^\prime=f\dfrac{v}{(v-u_s\cos(30))}$

        Where $f=21000\ \ce{Hz},\ f^\prime=21027\ \ce{Hz}$ and $v=350\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

        Substitute given values in the expression;

        - $21027=21000(\dfrac{350}{350-u_s\cos(30)} )$

        - giving $u_s = 0.51\, \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$
