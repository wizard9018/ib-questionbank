# PH0955

The diagram below shows a car moving at a constant velocity $u$ towards a stationary siren.

![carsiren](images/carsiren.png)

The siren emits sound of wavelength $\lambda$. The speed of sound in air is $v$. What is the wavelength of the sound measured by a person inside the car?

$\\$

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\lambda$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\lambda \Bigl(\dfrac{v+u}{v}\Bigr)$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\lambda \Bigl(\dfrac{v}{v+u}\Bigr)$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\lambda \Bigl(\dfrac{v}{v-u}\Bigr)$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

For a stationary source, the sound waves emitted are concentric with no change in the wavelength. Although a moving observer will experience a shift in frequency, the separation of the wavefronts (the wavelengths) does not change.

The moving observer experiences a shift in the frequency because the speed of the waves relative to the observer is  higher than the speed of the waves in the air because of the relative motion of the observer and the waves. With an increased speed of the waves and constant wavelength, we from see in the formula

- $f'=\dfrac{v'}{\lambda}$

that the perceived frequency will increase when the speed of the wave increases.

Because the wavelength remains unchanged, however, the correct answer is A.
