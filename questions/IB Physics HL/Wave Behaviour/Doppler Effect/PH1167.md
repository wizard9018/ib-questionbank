# PH1167

**Maximum mark: 7**

A loudspeaker, connected to a frequency generator, produces a sound wave with a constant frequency of 2.2 kHz. Meanwhile, a microphone moves directly away from the sound source at a speed that varies between 16 and $32\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$.

![PH1167a](images/PH1167a.png)

1. Calculate the minimum frequency that will be detected by the microphone. (Take the speed of the sound wave to be $330\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$). [3 marks]

2. Making use of the wave equation $v=f\lambda$, explain the observed change in frequency of a sound wave emitted by the speaker. [2 marks]

In another case, the speaker is moving in uniform circular motion around a stationary microphone placed at the center of the circle. The speed of the speaker is $16\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$.

3. On the graph below, sketch how the frequency $f'$ recorded by the microphone varies with time $t$. (There is no need to add numbers to the axes.) [2 marks]

    ![PH1167c1](images/PH1167c1.png)

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Use of $f'=f\Bigl(\dfrac{v-u_0}{v}\Bigr)$ $\checkmark$

    $f'=(2.2\times10^3)\Bigl(\dfrac{330-32}{330}\Bigr)$ $\checkmark$

    $f' = 2.0\times10^3\, \ce{Hz}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    In this scenario, the Doppler effect causes the frequency of the wave generated from the speaker to be detected at different frequencies by the microphone. As the observer is moving further away, the moving observer formula with a negative sign for the Doppler effect should be applied.

    - $f'=f\Bigl(\dfrac{v-u_0}{v}\Bigr)$

    According to the formula, to get the minimum observed frequency, the speed of the observer should be the greatest one.

    - $f'=(2.2\times10^3)\Bigl(\dfrac{330-32}{330}\Bigr)$

    - $f'=1.987\times10^3$ Hz

2. Moving away from the source results in a decrease in the speed of sound according to the observer. $\checkmark$

    Because wavelength is unchanged, observed frequency also decreases. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    As the microphone moves away from the source at speed u, the speed of sound according to the frame of reference of the microphone decreases to $v - u$, where $v$ is the speed of sound in the air.

    Because the source is not moving relative to the medium, the spacing of the wavefronts remains $\lambda$.  With the equation $v=f\lambda$, we can see that if $\lambda$ remains constant  and $v$ decreases, there must also be  decrease in the frequency $f$.

3. Constant frequency $\checkmark$

    At 2.2 kHz $\checkmark$

    ![PH1167c2](images/PH1167c2.png)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The speaker is engaged in uniform circular motion, ensuring that its velocity is constantly perpendicular to the line connecting it to the microphone. Consequently, the speaker neither approaches nor recedes to the microphone. As a result, there is no Doppler effect, and the recorded frequency remains consistently identical to the frequency of the source.
