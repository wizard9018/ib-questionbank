# PH0066

A fire truck's siren emits a frequency of $f_0$. Starting from rest, it moves with constant acceleration and passes a stationary observer at $t = t_o$. Which graph shows the variation of observed frequency $f$ by the stationary observer with time?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When the source is moving towards an observer, each new wavefront is created closer to the previously created wavefront than would be the case if the source were at rest. This results in a shortening of the wavelength and an increase in frequency in this direction.

Because the speed is increasing, the shortening of the wavelength and increase in frequency both increase over time, so we expect an increasing frequency for the first part of the motion.

Once the source passes the observer, the wavelength will become elongated and the perceived frequency will be lower than the emitted frequency. Because the acceleration continues, this difference in frequency will increase with time and so the graph should show decreasing observed frequency once the source has passed the observer.

For HL students:

 For a moving source, the received frequency is given by the equation

- $f'=f\bigg(\dfrac{v}{v\pm u_s}\bigg)$

As the fire truck approaches the observer $f > f_o$  before $t_o$

As fire truck moves away from the observer $f < f_o$ after $t_o$

and since it is speeding up, $\Delta f$ is increasing with time, causing the observed frequency to increase as it approaches and decrease as it moves away.
