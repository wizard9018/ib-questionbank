**Maximum mark: 17**

1. Car **A** and Car **B** are travelling in the same direction along a straight line at a constant speed. Car **A** has a speed of  $24\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$ and Car **B** has a speed of $36\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$. Both of the cars are identical and have a mass of 1200 kg.

    ![PH0668](images/PH0668.png)

    The engine of Car **B** has an overall efficiency of $25\%$ and the total resistive force acting on **Car B** is 2500 N.

    1. Define power. [1 mark]

    2. Determine the average power input to the engine. [3 marks]

2.  1. A cat suddenly jumps on the road and Car **B** sounds its horn upon seeing the cat. The frequency of the sound of the horn is 120 Hz and the speed of the sound in the air is $340\ \ce{ms^{-1}}$. What is the frequency of the sound heard by Car **A**? [3 marks]

    2. Upon seeing the cat, Car **B** slows down with constant deceleration. On the graph below, sketch the variation of displacement $s$ of car **B** with time $t$ from the time deceleration is started until the car comes to a stop. [2 marks]\

3. The driver of car **A**  also slowed down but collided with stationary Car **B**. After the collision, the two cars stuck together.

    1.     The speed of car **A** just before the crash was $8.0\, \ce{{m\,s}^{-1}}$. Determine the combined speed of the cars just after the collision. [2 marks]

    2. Show that the energy lost in the collision is approximately 19000 J. [2 marks]

Assuming that the heat energy was uniformly distributed across both cars, deduce that the increase in temperature of one of the cars is not significant. [2 marks]

    2. Output power of the engine $= Fv=\left(2500\right)\left(36\right)=9.0\times{10}^4$ W $\checkmark$

        Total power in = $\dfrac{9.0\times{10}^4}{25\%}\times100\%$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ :answer[$3.6\times{10}^5 \ \ce{W}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
To keep car **B** at a steady speed of $36\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$, the horizontal resultant force acting on the car should be zero. Therefore, the engine must create a forward thrust of $2500$ N. The output power $P_{o\ }$ of the engine:

        $\hspace{1em}$ Output power of the engine $ =Fv=\left(2500\right)\left(36\right)=9.0\times{10}^4$ W

        $\hspace{1em}$ Efficiency of the engine = $\dfrac{Useful\ power\ out}{Total\ power\ in}\times100\%$

        When the equation is rearranged:

        $\hspace{1em}$ Total power in = $\dfrac{Useful\ power\ out}{Efficiency\ of\ the\ engine}\times100\%$

        $\hspace{1em}$ Total power in = $\dfrac{9.0\times{10}^4}{25\%}\times100\%=3.6\times{10}^5$  W

2.
    1. Any evidence that shows the usage of two Doppler shifts $\checkmark$

        Frequency received by car **A** due to the motion of car **B**

        - $= 120\left(\dfrac{340}{340+36}\right)=109$ Hz $\checkmark$

        Observed frequency due to motion of car **A**

        - $= 109\left(\dfrac{340+24}{340}\right)=$ :answer[$116\, \ce{Hz}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        In this question, both the observer and source are moving. But the equations in the data booklet are for one stationary and one moving object. Therefore, the situation should be adjusted for the condition in which the given equations can be applied.

        Assume that car **A** is not moving. In this case, the observed frequency $f^\prime$ (moving source and stationary observer) would be;

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f^\prime=f(\dfrac{v}{v+u_s})$

        where $f=120$ Hz, $v=340\, \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $u_s=36\ \ce{m\,s^{-1}}$

        By substituting the given values in the equation;

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f^\prime=120\left(\dfrac{340}{340+36}\right)=109$ Hz

        But due to the motion of the car, there will be another Doppler shift (stationary source and moving observer) as follows;

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f^{\prime\prime}=f^\prime(\dfrac{v+u_0}{v})$

        where $f^\prime=109$ Hz, $u_0=24\ \ce{ms^{-1}}$ and $v=340\ \ce{ms^{-1}}$

        By substituting the given and found values in the equation;

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f^{\prime\prime}=109\left(\dfrac{340+24}{340}\right)=116$ Hz

3.
    1. $v=\dfrac{(1200)(8.0)}{2400}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ :answer[$4.0\, \ce{{m\,s}^{-1}}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
Due to momentum conservation, the total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after the collision. Because the cars stick together, they both have the same velocity $v$ after the collision.

        $\begin{aligned}
        p_{A\ before}+p_{B\ before} &=p_{A\ after}+p_{B\ after}\\
        \hspace{1em}\\
        (1200)(8.0)+(1200)(0)&=1200  v+1200  v\\
        \end{aligned}$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $v=\dfrac{(1200)(8.0)}{2400}=4.0\, \ce{{m\,s}^{-1}}$

    2. $E_{Ki}=\dfrac{1}{2}m_Av_A^2+\dfrac{1}{2}m_Bv_B^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(8.0\right)^2+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(0\right)^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=38400$ J

        **AND**

        $E_{Kf}=\dfrac{1}{2}m_A{v\prime}_A^2+\dfrac{1}{2}m_B{v\prime}_B^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(4.0\right)^2+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(4.0\right)^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=19200$ J $\checkmark$

        19200 J $\checkmark$

        *Accept different answers according to the answer in (c)(i)*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The total kinetic energy of the cars just before the collision $E_{Ki}$:

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=\dfrac{1}{2}m_Av_A^2+\dfrac{1}{2}m_Bv_B^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(8.0\right)^2+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(0\right)^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=38400$ J

        The total kinetic energy of the cars just after the collision $E_{Kf}$:

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=\dfrac{1}{2}m_A{v\prime}_A^2+\dfrac{1}{2}m_B{v\prime}_B^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(4.0\right)^2+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1200\right)\left(4.0\right)^2$

        $\hspace{1.8em}$ $=19200$ J

        The difference between final and initial kinetic energies $\Delta E_K$:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta E_k=38400-19200=19200$ J

    3. $\Delta T = \dfrac{19200}{(2400)(500)}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ = :answer[$1.6 \times 10^{-2} \, \degree \ce{C}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The total mass of the cars $m$:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $m=1200+1200=2400$ kg

        The energy transferred to the bodies of the cars is equal to the energy lost during the collision and energy is transferred through thermal energy.

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta E_K = mc\Delta T$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $19200 = (2400)(500) \Delta T$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\Delta T = \dfrac{19200}{(2400)(500)} = 1.6 \times 10^{-2} \degree$ C

    4. During the collision, crumple zones increase the time of collision which results in a decrease in the force acting on the body. $\checkmark$

        Passengers and drivers are less likely to be injured. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        According to Newton\'s second law, the force acting on the body during the collision is inversely proportional to the time of collision:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $F=\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$

        During the collision, crumple zones increase the time of collision which results in a decrease in the force acting on the body.
