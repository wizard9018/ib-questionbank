# PH1818

A lightly damped mass-spring system undergoes peak resonance when a driving force $F$ with frequency $f$ is applied, resulting in a resonant amplitude $A$. If the system is more heavily damped while $F$ remains unchanged, how do the peak resonant frequency $f$ and amplitude $A$ change?

$\\$

| | $f$ | $A$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| $\hspace{1.5em}$Decreases$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Decreases $\hspace{1.5em}$|
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Decreases | Increases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increases | Decreases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Increases | Increases |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When the system experiences more damping its oscillations are more affected by energy dissipated due to resistive forces. The natural frequency of oscillation therefore decreases.

Amplitude depends on how much energy is stored in oscillations, increased damping means more energy is lost per cycle, therefore amplitude decreases.
