# PH1861

The graph below corresponds to a standing wave which is formed on a string fixed at both ends while vibrating at a frequency of 3000 Hz.

![ph1861q](images/ph1861q.png)

Which of the following corresponds to a **possible** frequency at which a standing wave could be formed on the same string?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 300 Hz

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 500 Hz

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 1200 Hz

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 1500 Hz

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The five antinodal sections tells us that the diagram shows the fifth harmonic of the string.

The given frequency then corresponds to five times the fundamental frequency of the string.

- $f_1=\dfrac{f_5}{5}=\dfrac{3000 \ce{Hz}}{5}=600$ Hz

It is given that the string is fixed at both ends, therefore a standing wave will be formed at frequencies that are multiples of 600 Hz.

Among the given answers, 1200 Hz is the only frequency that is a multiple of 600 Hz.
