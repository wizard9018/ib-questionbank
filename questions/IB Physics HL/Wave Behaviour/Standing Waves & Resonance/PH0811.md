# PH0811

A pendulum oscillates in simple harmonic motion inside a box filled with a fluid as shown in the diagram. The system is moderately damped.

![Box](images/Box.png)

A driving force is applied to the pendulum, the graph below shows how the amplitude of oscillation of the pendulum varies as the frequency of the driving force is increased.

![Damping3](images/Damping3.png)

How would the shape of the graph change if the fluid inside the box were replaced with a fluid of lower density?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ Peak lower and to the left

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ Peak higher and to the left

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ Peak lower and to the right

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ Peak higher and to the right

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

A lower density fluid means less damping (the resistive force acting on the pendulum as it moves is lower).

The amplitude of vibration at the resonant frequency is higher so the peak is higher. (Damping reduces amplitude so less damping means that a greater amplitude can be reached.)

Resonant frequency will be higher so peak moves to the right. (Resonant frequency will be slightly lower than natural frequency for non-zero damping. The less damping there is, the closer the resonant frequency is to the natural frequency. (For light damping it is assumed that natural frequency = resonant frequency.)

Note: The peak will also be thinner due to the maximum amplitude being larger.
