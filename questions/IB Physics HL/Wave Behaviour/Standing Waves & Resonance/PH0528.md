# PH0528

**Maximum mark: 6**

A speaker emits sound towards a tube that is open at both ends as shown in Figure A. The speed of sound is $340\ \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$ and a standing wave is formed in the pipe. At a particular instant, the positions of particles $X$ and $Y$ are shown.

Figure B shows the displacement of molecules of air in the pipe at this instant in time. Displacement to the right is positive.

![PH0528-1](images/PH0528-1.png)

1.  1.  Explain whether particle $X$ is in the center of a compression or a rarefaction.   [2 marks]

    2.  On Figure A, use $Y'$ to mark the equilibrium position of particle $Y$ [1 mark]

2. When the frequency of the sound is increased by 120 Hz the next harmonic of the standing wave is formed in the pipe. Calculate the length of the pipe.  [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  $X$ is in a compression. $\checkmark$

        The particles to the left of $X$ have been displaced to the right and the particles to the right of $X$ have been displaced to the left.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The displacement-position graph can be used to determine that $X$ is in the middle of a compression. On the graph, $X$ has a displacement of zero from its equilibrium position. Particles to its left, however, have a positive displacement. This means that the particles are displaced to the right, towards particle $X$. The graph also shows that particles to the right of $X$ have been displaced in the negative direction which is to the left of their equilibrium positions which is also towards particle $X$. Thus the particles either side of $X$ have been displaced closer to $X$ and so $X$ is in a compression.

    2. The graph shows that $Y$ has been displaced in the negative direction which is to the left. Thus, the equilibrium position of $Y$ must be to the right of the position shown.

2.  ::indent

    - $f = n (\dfrac{v}{2L})$ $\checkmark$

    - $f + 120 = (n+1) (\dfrac{v}{2L})$

    - $\dfrac{v}{2L} = 120$ $\checkmark$

    - $L = \dfrac{340}{240} = 1.416\, \ce{m} \approx 142\  \ce{cm}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    The pipe is seen to be vibrating at the first harmonic frequency. This is when the pipe's length is equal to half a wavelength of the sound.

    Subsequent harmonics are found at multiples of the lowest harmonic frequency.

    Because the difference between two harmonic frequencies is 120 Hz, this means that the harmonic frequency is equal to 120 Hz.

    At the first harmonic is also equal to $\dfrac{v}{2L}$

    - $\dfrac{v}{2L}= 120$ Hz     

    Which leads to 

    - $L = \dfrac{340}{240} = 1.416 \, \ce{m}  \approx 142$ cm
