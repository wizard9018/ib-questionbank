# PH0103

A taut string on a guitar is fixed at both ends. 

What is $\dfrac{\textrm{wavelength of fourth harmonic}}{\textrm{wavelength of second harmonic}}$ for the string?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 4

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ 2

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{4}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

\

Since the $n^{th}$ harmonic has frequency of $n$ times of first harmonic frequency and the ratio of frequencies is $\dfrac{4}{2}=2$. 

Using $v=f\lambda$ and knowing that the speed of the wave in the string $v$ is unchanged, the ratio of wavelengths is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Another way to consider this is to visualise the second harmonic, which would have two antinodes and the length of the string would be equal to one full wavelength. For the fourth harmonic, there will be four full antinodes and so two full wavelengths in the same distance. Therefore the wavelength of the fourth harmonic is half that for the second harmonic.
