## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. In free vibrations, the oscillating system oscillates at its own $\underline{natural frequency}$ $\checkmark$

        In forced vibrations, the oscillating system is driven by external oscillator $\checkmark$

    2. Resonance happens when the natural frequency of the bridge matches the driving frequency of the wind. $\checkmark$

        It leads to a significant increase in amplitude (by transferring the energy of the oscillator more efficiently).  $\checkmark$

        It can be the cause of failure/destruction. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Resonance happens when the natural frequency of the oscillating object matches the driving frequency of the oscillator. It leads to a significant increase in amplitude by transferring the energy of the oscillator more efficiently to the oscillating object, giving a "push" to the oscillation at just the right time to increase its amplitude. This can lead even to the point of failure.

    3. Maximum amplitude is smaller $\checkmark$

        Maximum amplitude is shifted to the left $\checkmark$

        ![PH0656_MS](images/PH0656_MS.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Damping decreases the maximum amplitude of the vibration and the resonant frequency.

    4. Any device/application that utilizes resonance $\checkmark$

        Explanation of how resonance is used  in mentioned device/application $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        A microwave oven uses resonance to quickly heat food by generating electromagnetic waves at a frequency that matches the natural frequency of water molecules. Hence, water molecules vibrate in maximum displacement and energy is transferred to them at maximum efficiency.

        The guitar is another illustration that can be used for useful resonance. The vibration of guitar strings produces a sound that is amplified using resonance. The guitar's resonant frequencies are carefully tuned to correspond with each note produced by its strings. Therefore, the produced sound is clear and amplified.

2.
    1. Over/heavily damped $\checkmark$

    2. Under/lightly damped $\checkmark$

    3. Critically damped $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    In overly or heavily damped situations, the damping force is so big that it brings the door to the rest without oscillation. But the time of closure is long. In under or lightly damped situations, the damping force is small, such that it takes several oscillations to stop. In critically damped situations, the damping force is precisely adjusted to close the door in the shortest time without oscillating.
