## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Waves superpose/interfere to form a standing wave. $\checkmark$

        At maxima/antinodes there is constructive interference. $\checkmark$

        At minima/nodes there is destructive interference. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The points of maximum and minimum intensity occur because the waves from sources $S$ and $T$ travel in opposite directions along the $x$-axis and superpose when they meet.

        At certain positions, the waves arrive in phase, so their displacements add constructively. This results in points of maximum amplitudes known as antinodes. At other positions, the waves meet completely out of phase, so their displacements cancel out and produce points of zero amplitude called nodes.

        The repeating pattern of nodes and antinodes forms a standing wave, as the two waves have the same frequency and wavelength and are travelling in opposite directions.

    2. $\hspace{1.65em}$ $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}=0.34$ m

        OR

        - $\lambda=0.68$ m $\checkmark$

        - $\dfrac{343}{0.68}=504\  \ce{Hz}$ (3sf) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The points of maximum intensity are antinodes of the stationary wave. The distance between two antinodes is equal to half the wavelength of the wave, so we have:

        - $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}=0.64-0.30=0.34$ m

        - $\lambda=2(0.34)=0.68$ m

        Now using $v=f\lambda$ we have:

        - $f=\dfrac{v}{\lambda}=\dfrac{343}{0.68}=$ $504$ Hz (3sf)

2. 1. The frequency of wave $T$ changes $\ll$ relative to wave $S$/ stationary observer $\gg$ due to the Doppler effect $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When source $T$ begins to move away from $S$, the frequency of the wave it produces decreases relative to a stationary observer or relative to source $S$. This happens because of the Doppler effect. As a result, the two waves no longer have the same frequency, so they no longer produce a stable stationary wave pattern. The nodes and antinodes shift their positions over time, and the intensity pattern between the sources changes.

    2. Values correctly substituted:

        - $504=f\left(\dfrac{343}{343+3.5}\right)$ $\checkmark$

        - $f=$ :answer[$509$ Hz] (Allow answer to 2sf) $\checkmark$

        Note: Use of $f'=500$ Hz giving $f=505$ Hz gains full marks

        Allow ecf from part (a)(i)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When source $T$ is moving away from the other source, the frequency of the wave it emits becomes Doppler shifted.

        The Doppler shift equation for a moving source is:

        - $f'=f\left(\dfrac{v}{v\pm u_s}\right)$

        Where $f'$ is the observed frequency, $f$ is the frequency in the frame of reference of the source, $u_s$ is the speed of the source, and $v$ is the speed of the wave.

        The $\pm$ in the equation refers to the direction of the source. When the source is moving away from the observer, we expect to see [a longer wavelength and therefore] a smaller frequency.

        We therefore require the fraction $\dfrac{v}{v\pm u_s}$ to be less than 1. This occurs when the sign on the bottom is positive.

        To restore the original stationary wave pattern, the frequency received from $T$, $f'$ must match the frequency from $S$, which is 504 Hz.

        - $f'=f\left(\dfrac{v}{v\pm u_s}\right)$

        - $504=f\left(\dfrac{343}{343+3.5}\right)$

        - $504=f\left(\dfrac{343}{346.5}\right)$

        - $f=504\left(\dfrac{346.5}{343}\right)=509$ Hz
