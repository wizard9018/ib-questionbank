# PH1867

Below shows a wavefront diagram of a ray of monochromatic light travelling between 2 media, $A$ and $B$. Medium $A$ has a refractive index of 2. The spacing of the wavefronts of the wave in the two different media is shown on the diagram.

![Screenshot 2025-09-06 at 5.59.46 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-09-06 at 5.59.46 PM.png>)

What is the speed of light in medium $B$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{c}{3}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{c}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2c}{3}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3c}{4}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The refractive index $n$ of a medium is defined as:

- $n=\dfrac{c}{v}$

Where $c$ is the speed of light in a vacuum and $v$ is the speed of light in the medium.

Therefore for medium $A$ we have:

- $2=\dfrac{c}{v_A}$

- $2v_A=c$

- $v_A=\dfrac{c}{2}$

When passing between media of different refractive indices, the speed and wavelength of light change while the frequency remains constant. The equation $v=f\lambda$ tells us that $v\propto\lambda$. So, we have:

- $\dfrac{v_A}{v_B}=\dfrac{\lambda_A}{\lambda_B}$

In a wavefront diagram, the distance between adjacent lines is equal to the wavelength of the light. Therefore, we have:

- $\dfrac{\lambda_A}{\lambda_B}=\dfrac{5\lambda_A}{5\lambda_B}=\dfrac{3\,\mu\textrm{m}}{2\,\mu\textrm{m}}=\dfrac{3}{2}$

Therefore:

- $\begin{aligned}
\ \dfrac{v_A}{v_B}&=\dfrac{3}{2}\\
\\\
\ v_A&=\dfrac{3}{2}v_B\\
\\\
\ v_B&=\dfrac{2}{3}v_A\\
\\\
\ v_B&=\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{c}{2}\right)\\
\\\
\ &=\dfrac{c}{3}\\
\end{aligned}$
