# PH0181

Monochromatic light of wavelength λ is diffracted while passing through a narrow slit. The three labeled light rays emerge from the top, halfway and bottom of the slit meet at a first diffraction minimum on a distant screen.

![PH0181](images/PH0181.png)

The path difference between light rays A and B is

- A. $\hspace{1em}\dfrac{1}{4}λ$

- B. $\hspace{1em}    \dfrac{1}{2}λ$

- C. $\hspace{1em}λ$

- D. $\hspace{1em}\dfrac{3}{2}λ$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To have a diffraction minimum, light rays must interfere destructively. When rays from the top and halfway superpose destructively, pairs of light rays coming from the points below in equal distance from the top and halfway interfere destructively too. This results in a minimum, as each light ray has a paired light ray that is out of phase with it.

Therefore, the path difference between light rays from the top and halfway is:

Path difference $=(n+\dfrac{1}{2})\lambda    $

n is 0 for the first minimum;

Path difference$=\left(0+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda=\dfrac{1}{2}\lambda    $
