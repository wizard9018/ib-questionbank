# PH1869

Light of wavelength 500 nm is passed through two slits separated by a distance of 0.5 mm. The graph below shows how intensity varies with diffraction angle in radians.

![Screenshot 2025-08-26 at 2.49.08 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-08-26 at 2.49.08 PM.png>)

What is the value of $x$ in radians?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $1\times10^{-3}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $2\times10^{-3}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $5\times10^{-3}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $1\times10^{-2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

On the graph, $x$ represents the first minimum of the single slit diffraction envelope. We can see that the first minimum due to the single slit pattern occurs where the fifth order maximum from the double slit pattern should occur.

We can find the angle $\theta$ of the fifth order maximum of the double slit pattern using:

- $d\sin\theta=n\lambda$

Where $d$ is the distance between the slits, $n$ is the order of the diffraction, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength.

Rearranging and substituting our values gives:

- $\sin\theta=\dfrac{n\lambda}{d}$

- $\theta=\left(\dfrac{n\lambda}{d}\right)=\left(\dfrac{5(500\times10^{-9})}{0.5\times10^{-3}}\right)=\left(\dfrac{5(500\times10^{-9})}{0.5\times10^{-3}}\right)=5\times10^{-3}$ rad

Therefore

- $x = 5\times10^{-3}$ rad
