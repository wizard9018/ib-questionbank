# PH0954

Two waves interfere at a point resulting in constant zero amplitude of vibration at that location.

Below are three statements about the waves

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The two waves are coherent    |
  | II. | The two waves have the same amplitude |
  | III. | The two waves have a phase difference of $\pi$  |

Which of the statements are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em} $ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em} $ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em} $ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em} $ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The principle of superposition states that when two waves meet the resultant displacement is the sum of the displacements of the original waves.

To have two waves completely cancel out (zero amplitude of vibration) they must

I - have the same frequency and a constant phase difference (coherent) so that the waves are always out of phase at that location.

II - have the same amplitude so that there is always full cancellation

III - Be $\pi$ radians out of phase.

This is represented in the diagram below, which shows the displacements against time that the particles at that location would have due to the different waves. Because the displacements are always exactly out of phase and of the same amplitude at any given time, there will be full cancellation.
