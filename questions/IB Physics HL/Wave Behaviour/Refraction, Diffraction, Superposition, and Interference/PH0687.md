# PH0687

In a double-slit experiment, monochromatic light with a wavelength of $600\, $nm passes through two slits spaced $1.0\, $mm apart. The resulting interference pattern is observed on a screen placed 2 meters away from the slits. What is the approximate value for the angle between the central peak and the 2$^{\ce{nd}}$ order maximum?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ degrees.

- B. $\hspace{1em}  1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ radians.

- C. $\hspace{1em}  1.2 \times 10^{-3}$ degrees.

- D. $\hspace{1em}  1.2 \times 10^{-3}$ radians.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

For the distance from the centre of the pattern to the second order maximum

- $2s=(2)\dfrac{(\lambda D)}{d}=(2)\dfrac{(600 \times 10{^{-9})(2)}}{1 \times 10{^{-3}}}=2.4 \times 10{^{-3}}\,$m

For the angle

- $\tan \theta= \dfrac{2.4 \times 10^{-3}}{2}=1.2 \times 10{^{-3}}$

- $\tan \theta \approx \theta = 1.2 \times 10{^{-3}}$ radians

Alternatively, we can use the HL equation from the data booklet:

- $n \lambda = d \sin \theta$

Giving

- $\sin \theta = \dfrac{n \lambda}{d} = \dfrac{(2)(600 \times 10{^{-9}})}{1 \times 10{^{-3}}} = 1.2 \times 10{^{-3}}$

- $\sin \theta \approx \theta = 1.2 \times 10{^{-3}}$ radians

As the small-angle approximation is valid here
