**Maximum mark: 12**

Light with a wavelength of 750 nm is incident on a single slit. The slit width $b$ is comparable to the wavelength of the incident light.

![PH0575](images/PH0575.png)

2. 

    1. On the diagram; sketch the wavefronts after passing through the slit. [2 marks]

    2. Describe the pattern observed on the screen. [2 marks]

    3. The slit width is slowly increased until it is much wider than the wavelength. Explain the effect on the observed pattern. [2 marks]

    ![PH0575-1](images/PH0575-1.png)

    1. Describe the pattern observed on the screen. [1 mark]

    2. Knowing that

       Distance between the slits $d = 150\, \ce{\mu}$ m\

    3. Explain how the widths of the slits will affect the appearance of the pattern on the screen. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The change of direction of a wave as it passes around the edge of an object. $\checkmark$

2. 

    1. At least three concentric wavefronts. $\checkmark$

        Wavelength roughly matches the wavelength of the original wave. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: 

        ![PH0575-2](images/PH0575-2.png)

    2. Bright and dark fringes with different intensities / highest intensity in center/intensity decrease at the edges. $\checkmark$

        Wide central maximum $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        A single slit diffraction pattern will have a wide central maximum, with significantly dimmer outer fringes each with width equal to half of the central maximum.

    3. Narrower maxima $\checkmark$

        At large slit width no diffraction observed $\checkmark$

3. 

    1. Successive bright and dark fringes of equal width. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        This will be the double slit diffraction pattern. Although it may be modulated by the single slit pattern, a description of this is not needed for the mark here.

    2. $\ll s=\dfrac{\lambda D}{d}\gg$

        $s=\dfrac{(750\times 10^{-9})(2.3)}{(150\times 10^{-6})}$ $\checkmark$

        $s$ = :answer[$0.0115\ll \ce{m} \gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        Use of the data booklet formula $s=\dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$ leads to the answer here.

    3. The slit width will affect the (single slit) modulation of the double slit pattern $\checkmark$

        Narrower slits will allow a number of similarly bright fringes in the centre / wider slits will cause minima close to the center of the pattern **OWTTE**. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        Very narrow slits give wide diffraction patterns, so the central maximum will contain many fringes from the double slit interference, so the modulation may not be visible. As the slits widen, the single slit pattern narrows and will begin to modulate the double slit interference pattern, and the first minima from the single slit diffraction will be closer to the center.
