# PH0688

In the double-slit experiment, the slit spacing is much larger than the widths of the slits. The distance between the slits is halved while keeping the distance between the screen and the slits and the slit widths unchanged. What happens to the maxima in terms of their spacing and relative brightness between the central peak and adjacent maxima?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Brightness$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Maxima spacing$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| No change |Decrease |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | No change | Increase |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Change | Decrease |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Change | Increase |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Because the slit width is very small compared to the slit spacing, it can be assumed that the intensities of the maxima for the double-slit pattern will not be affected by single-slit modulation, both before and after the change.

As per equation

- $n\lambda=d\sin{\theta}$

if $d$ decreases, then the angle between adjacent maxima or minima increases.

If the spacing is changed, the interference between multiple slits causes the spacing to change, but the single slit envelope remains unaffected as the slit width itself is not changed. Hence, even though the maxima are more spread out, they retain the same relative brightness.
