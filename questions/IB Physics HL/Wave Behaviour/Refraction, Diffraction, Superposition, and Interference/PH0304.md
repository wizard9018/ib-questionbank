# PH0304

Light of wavelength 400 nm and 700 nm is incident on a diffraction grating that has 700 rulings per mm. A series of bright lines are formed on a screen. The angles for different maxima for the light are shown.

![PH0304-1](images/PH0304-1.png)

1. 1.  In order to observe bright lines on the screen, the light incident on the diffraction grating must be coherent. What is meant by coherent light?  [1 mark]

    2.     Find the value for  $\dfrac {\ce{sin}\ \theta_1\ }{\ce{sin}\ \theta_2\ }\     $ [3 marks]

2. 1. Determine the maximum number of bright fringes of the same colour that can be observed on the screen.       [4 marks]

    2. For the same wavelength of light, what change can be made to the arrangement  to increase the number of bright lines observed?    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Constant phase difference $\checkmark$

    2. $\ce{sin}\ \theta\ =\frac{n\lambda}{d}\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{\ce{sin}\theta_1}{\ce{sin}\theta_2}=\dfrac{\frac{400\,\times\,{10}^{-9}}{d}}{\frac{(2)(700)({10}^{-9})}{d}}\checkmark$

        :answer[0.29] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        For a diffraction grating;

        - $d\ \ce{sin}\ \theta\ =n\lambda$

        for $\ce{sin} \theta_1\ $ when $\lambda=400\times{10}^{-9}\, \ce{m}$ and $n=1$:

        - $d\ \ce{sin}\theta_1=(1)\lambda_1$

        - $\ce{sin}{\ \theta}_1=\dfrac{400\times{10}^{-9}}{d}$

        for $\ce{sin}{\ \theta}_2$ when $\lambda=700\times{10}^{-9}\, \ce{mm}$  and $n=2$:

        - $d\ \ce{sin}\ \theta_2=(2)(700\times{10}^{-9})$

        - $\ce{sin}\theta_2=\dfrac{(2)(700\times{10}^{-9})}{d}$

        Therefore;

        - $\dfrac{\ce{sin}\theta_1}{\ce{sin}\theta_2}=\dfrac{\frac{400\times{10}^{-9}}{d}}{\frac{(2)(700\,\times\,{10}^{-9})}{d}}=0.2857\ $

2.  1.  Use of 400 nm $\checkmark$

        $d=\dfrac{{10}^{-3}}{700}=1.4\times{10}^{-6}\ \ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

        $(1.4\times{10}^{-6})(1)>(n)(400\times{10}^{-9})$ giving $ n=3$ $\checkmark$

        :answer[7] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The equation for an interference pattern of light incident on a diffraction grating;

        - $d\ce{sin}\theta=n\lambda$

        According to the equation, the highest order of maximum can be obtained on a point that has the greatest diffraction angle by using a shorter wavelength. But the diffraction angle should be less than 90° so that light can reach the screen. Therefore, $\lambda=400\times{10}^{-9} \ce{m}$ and $\ce{sin}\theta<\ce{sin}\ 90=1.$

        Additionally, slit separation $d$ can be found by:

        - $d=\dfrac{1\times{10}^{-3}}{700}=1.4\times{10}^{-6}\, \ce{m}$

        By substituting all the deduced values in the former equation;

        - $(1.4\times{10}^{-6})(1)>(400\times{10}^{-9})n$

        - $\dfrac{1.4}{4}\times{10}^1>n$

        - 3.5 > $n$

        $n$ can only be an integer. Therefore, the highest order of maximum that can be obtained is 3. But there will be 3 maxima on the other side of the central maximum and one central maximum. Hence, the total of maxima is 7. Each maximum means a bright line.

    2.  Replace diffraction grating with fewer rulings per mm $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When the highest order of maximum is increased, the number of bright lines increases. The highest order of maximum;

        - $d\ce{sin}\theta=n\lambda$

        - $\dfrac{d\ce{sin}\theta}{\lambda}=n$

        According to this relationship, $d$ and $\ce{sin}θ$ should be increased while λ may be decreased.

        $d$ can be increased by replacing diffraction grating with fewer rulings per mm.

        The number of maxima will always be limited by the maximum possible angle of 90°
