# PH0691

When monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda$ is incident on a single slit of width 0.015 mm, a diffraction pattern is formed where the central maximum has a given width as shown below.

![image](images/image.png)
$\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

A second slit of equal width is added, where the slits are separated by a distance of 0.065 mm. How many bright fringes are seen in the central diffraction maximum?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ 3

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ 7

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ 8

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ 9

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The single slit equation gives us the angle to the first minimum:

- $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{b}$

The double slit equation gives us the angle of some order of maximum (i.e. the number of fringes for a given angle):

- $n\lambda = d\sin{\theta}$

Allowing for the small angle approximation, we obtain:

- $\dfrac{n\lambda}{d}=\sin{\theta} \approx \theta$

The first single slit diffraction minimum coincides with some number of bright fringes, so we must solve for $n$:

- $\dfrac{n\lambda}{d}=\dfrac{\lambda}{b} \rarr n=\dfrac{d}{b}=\dfrac{0.065}{0.015}=4.3$

This shows that the fourth bright fringe of the double-slit pattern will just be visible before the minimum of the single-slit diffraction envelope.

Therefore, the central maximum and four fringes on either side will be visible, giving a total of 9 bright fringes.
