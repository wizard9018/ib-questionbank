# PH0685

In a Young's double-slit experiment, the observed fringes are so close together that they are just resolved. What change can improve the resolution so that the fringes can be better observed?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ Bringing the screen closer to the double-slit.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ Decreasing the distance between slits.
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ Bringing the light source closer to the double-slit.
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ Decreasing the wavelength of light.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The angle between bright fringes is given by the relationship

$\hspace{1em}$ $n \lambda=d\ sin \theta \to sin \theta =\dfrac{n \lambda}{d}$

Decreasing the distance between slits, $d$ (option **B**) will increase the angle.

Bringing the screen closer will not affect the angle, but will bring the fringes closer together.

Decreasing $\lambda$ will decrease the angle and not improve the resolution.

Increasing the distance between the light source and the slits has no effect because all diffraction effects take place after the light has passed through the slits.
