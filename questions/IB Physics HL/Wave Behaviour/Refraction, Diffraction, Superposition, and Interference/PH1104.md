# PH1104

**Maximum mark: 10**

In a double-slit experiment, light of wavelength $\lambda$ emitted from a single source is diffracted through two narrow vertical slits, $A$ and $B$, located at equal distance from the source and separated from each other by a distance $D$. A vertical screen is placed a large distance $L$ from the two slits, and the variation in light intensity $I$ is observed as a function of horizontal position $x$ along the screen.

![PH11041](images/PH11041.png)

1. 1. Explain why a line of maximum intensity appears on the screen halfway between the two slits, at $x = 0$. [3 marks]

    As $x$ goes from $x = 0$ to $x_0$, $I$ decreases to zero.

    2. State the phase angle difference between waves from the two slits at $x = x_0$. [1 mark]

    As $L$ is much larger than $D$, rays arriving at position $x_0$ on the screen are approximately parallel. The two rays are orientated at an angle of $\theta_0$ normal to the slits and the screen.

    ![PH11042](images/PH11042.png)

    3. Using the small-angle approximations $\sin{\theta_0} \approx \tan{\theta_0} \approx\theta_0$, show that

        $x_0 \approx \dfrac{\lambda L}{2D}$ [3 marks]

The position of the light source is moved so that the amplitude of waves arriving at Slit $A$ varies in direct proportion to $\sin(\omega t)$, where $t$ is time and $\omega$ is angular frequency, whilst the amplitude of waves arriving at Slit $B$ varies in direct proportion to $\sin(\omega t + \pi)$.

2. Describe how this change affects light intensity at $x = 0$ and $x = x_0$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Slits are equidistant to screen at $x = 0$  $\checkmark$

        $\therefore \ \phi(0)=0 /  $ path length difference is zero   $\checkmark$

        Hence, waves from the two slits interfere constructively  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        At $x = 0$, the path length from Slit $A$ is equal to the path length from Slit $B$. As the two slits are illuminated by the same light source, they receive coherent light. Furthermore, as they are equidistant from the source, light waves arriving at the two slits have zero phase difference between them.

    2. $ \phi (x_0) = \pi$  $\checkmark$

        OR

        $ \phi (x_0) = 180\degree$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The measured intensity is zero at $x = x_0$, indicating that waves from the two slits interfere destructively at this point. Hence, the waves must be exactly out-of-phase, with a phase angle difference of $180\degree$, or $\pi$ radians.

    3. $D\sin{\theta} = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\tan{\theta} = \dfrac{x_0}{L}$ $\checkmark$

        $D\dfrac{x_0}{L} = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The destructive interference fringe at $x = x_0$ corresponds to a phase angle difference of $180\degree$ between waves from the two slits, which is equivalent to a difference of $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ in the distance of travel. As the waves are approximately parallel as they arrive at the screen (because $L$ is much greater than $D$), trigonometry yields

        - $D\sin{\theta} = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$

        Trigonometry may be similarly used to show that

        - $\tan{\theta} = \dfrac{x_0}{L}$

        Finally, combining the two equations with the small-angle approximations provided gives

        - $D\dfrac{x_0}{L} = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ $\checkmark$

        This equation rearranges to produce the desired expression.

        - $x_0 \approx \dfrac{\lambda L}{2D}$

2. Waves from Slit $B$ shifted by half a wavelength relative to waves from Slit $A$ $\checkmark$

    Destructive interference at $x = 0$ $\checkmark$

    Constructive interference at $x = x_0$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    In the new configuration, the phase angle difference between Slits $A$ and $B$ is $\pi$ radians, which corresponds to a relative shift of half a wavelength: consequently, when the amplitude of light at Slit $A$ is maximum, the amplitude at Slit $B$ is minimum.

    At $x = 0$, the path lengths of light from Slits $A$ and $B$ are equal, meaning that the phase angle difference at the screen is the same as at the slits; therefore, the waves interfere destructively to produce a line of zero intensity.

    Conversely, as the waves initially interfered destructively at $x = x_0$, the phase angle difference in the new configuration leads to constructive interference at this position.
