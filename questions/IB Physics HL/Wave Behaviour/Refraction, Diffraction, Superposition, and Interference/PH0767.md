# PH0767

A parallel beam of monochromatic light, wavelength $\lambda$, is incident on a slit of width $b$. The diffraction pattern is then seen on a screen.

![Screenshot 2025-04-21 at 1.41.31 PM](<images/Screenshot 2025-04-21 at 1.41.31 PM.JPEG>)

What is the approximate angular separation of the 1st order maxima on either side of the central maximum, $\phi$? Note that the central maximum is twice the width of the other maxima in the pattern.

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{\lambda}{2b}$ radians

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{\lambda}{b}$ radians

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{2\lambda}{b}$ radians

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{3\lambda}{b}$ radians

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The central maximum has twice the angular width of the angle to the first minimum. If the angle at which the first minimum occurs is:

- ${\theta}_1=\dfrac{\lambda}{b}$

Then the width of the central maximum is:

- $2{\theta}_1=\dfrac{2\lambda}{b}$

The distance between corresponding minima is $\dfrac{\lambda}{b}$ (the width from central peak to first minimum).

![image](images/image.png)

Since maxima are between the minima, we add half the spacing between the next set of minima, an additional $\dfrac{\lambda}{2b}$ either side, giving a total of:

- $\dfrac{2\lambda}{b}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2b}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2b}=\dfrac{3\lambda}{b}$

The angles involved in diffraction are radians, as this allows the small angle approximation to hold.
