# PH1008

**Maximum mark: 9**

A longitudinal wave is travelling in a medium from left to right. The diagram below is a snapshot of plane wave fronts showing the position of particles X (center of compression) and Y (center of rarefaction) in the medium at $t =0 \ \ce{s}$.

![PH10008aaaaa](images/PH10008aaaaa.png)

1. Distinguish between a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave. [2 marks]

2. In the graph below, the solid line shows the variation of displacement $s$ of particle X with time, and the dotted line shows the variation of displacement of particle Y with time.

    ![PH1008bb](images/PH1008bb.png)

    1.     Show that the angular frequency $\omega$ of the oscillating particles in the medium is 3.9 $\ce{rad} \ \ce{s}^{-1}$. [1 mark]

    1. State the phase difference between particles X and Y. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. For transverse wave; the displacement of particles/ movement of particles / oscillations of particles is **perpendicular** to the direction of energy transfer/ wave movement / wave travel. $\checkmark$

    For longitudinal wave; the displacement of particles/ movement of particles / oscillations of particles is **parallel** to the direction of energy transfer/ wave movement / wave travel. $\checkmark$

2.
    1. Period of the wave from the graph, $T= 1.6 \ \ce{s}$ $\checkmark$

        < $\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ > = $\dfrac{2\pi}{1.6}=3.93\ \ce{rad} \ \ce{s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    2. $\lambda = 8 \ \ce{cm}$  and  $T =1.6 \ \ce{s}$

        So; $v=\dfrac{\lambda}{T}\ =\dfrac{8\,\ce{cm}}{1.6\,\ce{s}}\ =5.0\ \ce{cm}\ \ce{s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The wavelength is two times distance between the center of a rarefaction and the center of the next compression. The top figure shows this distance to be $4\,\ce{cm}$, therefore $\lambda =8 \ \ce{cm}$

        Period of the wave from the graph, $T= 1.6 \ \ce{s}$

        $v=\dfrac{\lambda}{T}\ =\dfrac{8\,\ce{cm}}{1.6\,\ce{s}}\ =5.0\ \ce{cm}\ \ce{s}^{-1}$

3.
    1. half cycle/ $\pi / \dfrac{1}{2}$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        **Alternative 1**\
        At any instant of time magnitudes of displacement of $X$ and $Y$ are same, but in opposite direction. That is phase difference is  half cycle/ $\pi / \dfrac{1}{2}$

        **Alternative 2**\
        $\phi=\dfrac{\Delta t}{T}\times2\pi=\ <\dfrac{0.8}{1.6}\times2\pi>\ =\ \pi$

        $v=\omega\sqrt{{x_0}^2-x^2}\ =\ 3.93\sqrt{{8.0}^2-{2.4}^2}$ $\checkmark$
