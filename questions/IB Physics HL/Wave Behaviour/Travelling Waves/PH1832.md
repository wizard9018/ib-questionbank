# PH1832

**Maximum mark: 5**

The speed of sound $v$ in a gas is hypothesized to depend on the pressure $P$ and the density of the gas $\rho$ according to

- $v=kP^a\rho^b$

where $k$ is a dimensionless constant.

1. Determine the values of $a$ and $b$. [3 marks]

A student wishes to test this hypothesis by measuring the speed
$v$ of sound in a gas at different pressures and maintaining the density $\rho$ of the gas constant.

2. Identify the independent and dependent variables [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Expressing speed, pressure and density as:

    - $[v]=$ L T$^{-1}$ (or m s$^{-1}$)

    - $[P]=$ F A$^{-1}$ (or kg m$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$)

    - $[\rho]=$ M L$^{-3}$ (or kg m$^{-3}$)

    Any 2 of these three magnitudes correctly expressed in terms of the correct dimensions (or fundamental units) $\checkmark$

    - $a=\dfrac{1}{2}$ $\checkmark$

    - $b=-\dfrac{1}{2}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    According to $v=kP^a\rho^b$, we start by expressing the dimensional forms of $v$, $P$, and $\rho$ in terms of dimensions mass $M$ (mass), $L$ (length), and $T$ (time):

    - $[v]=$ L T$^{-1}$ (speed is distance/time)

    - $[P]=$ F A$^{-1}=$ (M L T$^{-2}$)(L$^{-2}$) = M L$^{-1}$ T$^{-2}$ (pressure is force/area))

    - $[\rho]=$ M L$^{-3}$ (density is mass/volume)

    We can now substitute the dimensions of each magnitude into the equation:

    - L T$^{-1}$ = (M L$^{-1}$ T$^{-2})^a$(M L$^{-3})^b$

    By expanding the exponents we get:

    - L T$^{-1}$ = M$^a$ L$^{-a}$ T$^{-2a}$ M$^b$ L$^{-3b}$

    - L T$^{-1}$ = M$^{a+b}$ L$^{-a-3b}$ T$^{-2a}$

    Since both sides must have the same dimensions for mass $M$ (mass), $L$ (length), and $T$ (time) we can setup equation by comparing exponents:

    - For mass: $a+b=0$
    - For length: $-a-3b=1$
    - For time: $-2a=-1$

    From the third equation $a=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

    Then by substituting this value in the first equation we get $b=-\dfrac{1}{2}$.

    Thus, $v=kP^{1/2}\rho^{-1/2}$, which can be written as:

    - $v=k\sqrt{\dfrac{P}{\rho}}$

    This is consistent with the Newton-Laplace equation for the speed of sound in an ideal gas.

2. 1. IV: Pressure / $P$ $\checkmark$

        DV: Speed of sound in the gas / $v$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Pressure is the variable that would be adjusted independently and its effect of the speed would be studied.

        The speed of sound $v$ is the dependent variable given that its value is assumed to depend on the pressure.

        In this case the density of the gas is a controlled variable and must be kept at a constant value.
