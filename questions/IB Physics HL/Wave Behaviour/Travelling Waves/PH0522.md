## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. A wave where the directions of the displacement of particles and the direction of energy propagation / direction of the wave travel; $\checkmark$

    are parallel. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    The particles in a longitudinal wave oscillate parallel to the direction of energy transfer of the wave; particles in a transverse wave oscillate perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.

    Note that although this graph appears to be the shape of a transverse wave, it is a representation of a longitudinal wave, with positive values indicating displacements to the right, and negative values indicating displacements to the left.

2. 

    1. :answer[$30\,\ce{mm}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The distance between the equilibrium position and the maximum displacement is the amplitude.

    2. $T = 28\,\ce{ms}$ (from the graph) $\checkmark$ 

        $f = \dfrac{1}{T} =$ :answer[$36\,\ce{Hz}$]  $\checkmark$ 

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        On a displacement-time graph, the period of oscillation of the particle in the medium can be read off from the time from peak to peak on the graph $-$ 28 ms. 

        The period of oscillation of an individual particle is the same as the period of the wave as it travels through the medium.

        The frequency can then be calculated using the relationship $f = \dfrac{1}{T}=\dfrac{1}{0.028}$, giving a value of 36 Hz.

    3. :answer[Left] $\checkmark$ 

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        We are told that positive displacement on the graph means displacement to the right of the equilibrium for a particle, and negative displacement means displacement to the left of equilibrium. 

        We know that the particle is displaced to the right at $t = 10$ ms because the graph is positive. A short time later, at approximately $t = 14$ ms, the particle will be at equilibrium. This means that the particle must be moving to the left during this time.

3. 

    1. The wavelength of the wave from the graph is 56 cm $\checkmark$

        $v=\lambda f$ and $f=\dfrac{1}{T}$ 

        $v=\dfrac{\lambda}{T}$

        

        $v=\dfrac{56\,\times\, 10^{-2}}{28\,\times\, 10^{-3}}=$ :answer[20] $\checkmark$ :answer[$\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        The wavelength of the wave can be read from the graph as the distance peak-to-peak $-$ $56$ cm.

    2. $\hspace{1em}$

        ![PH0522-3](images/PH0522-3.jpg)

        $\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

        C at distance 14 cm **OR** 70 cm $\checkmark$

        R at distance $42$ cm $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        Particles to the immediate left of the points labelled C are displaced to the right as they are positive, and particles to the immediate right of C are displaced to the left as they are negative. As a result, C is at the center of a compression.

        Similarly, particles to the immediate left of the point labelled R are displaced to the left as they are negative, and particles to the immediate right of R are displaced to the right. As a result, R is at the center of a rarefaction.

4. $\hspace{1em}$

    ![PH0522-4](images/PH0522-4.png)

    $\footnotesize{\textrm{[Source: Created with GeoGebra - https://www.geogebra.org/graphing?lang=en]}}$

    Displacement is 30 mm at $t = 0$ ms $\checkmark$

    Graph of displacement-time is shifted to the left by 7 ms $\checkmark$

    With the same amplitude **AND** period $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    Because the second source is located in front of the first source relative to the wave direction, the particles in the medium will displace just like in the case of the first source, but a short time earlier.

    The wavelength of the wave is $56\,\ce{cm}$. Because the second source is $14\,\ce{cm}$ ahead of the first, the expected phase shift is:

    $\phi = \bigg(\dfrac{14\,\ce{cm}}{56\,\ce{cm}}\bigg)(2\pi) = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

    Therefore, the graph of the displacement of the particle solely due to the second source will show an identical oscillation of the particle with the same amplitude and period, except occurring one quarter of a period earlier, or 7 ms earlier.
