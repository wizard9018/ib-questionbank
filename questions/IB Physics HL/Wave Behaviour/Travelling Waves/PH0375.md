# PH0375

A student ties two identical ropes to a fixed wall for a fitness exercise. They then shake the ropes to form waves.

![PH0375](images/PH0375.png)

A point on the right rope oscillates with a period of $0.4 \,\ce{s}$. A point on the left rope oscillates with a period of $0.2\,\ce{s}$. 

What is the ratio $\dfrac{\textrm{wavelength of right rope}}{\textrm{wavelength of left rope}}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ 2

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{1}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ 1

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ 4

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: 

Since 

$v=f\lambda\,\,$and$\,\,f = \dfrac{1}{T}$

$v = \dfrac{\lambda}{T}$ giving

$T = \dfrac{\lambda}{v}$

Which means wavelength and period are directly proportional.

Since both ropes are made of the same material, they have the same wave speed.

Therefore:

$\dfrac{\textrm{period of right rope}}{\textrm{period of left rope}}=\frac{0.4\,\ce{s}}{0.2\,\ce{s}}=2$

meaning that:

 

$\dfrac{\textrm{wavelength of right rope}}{\textrm{wavelength of left rope}} = \dfrac{\textrm{period of right rope}}{\textrm{period of left rope}}=2$
