# PH0695-3

\[Maximum marks: 8\]

1. A thorium-234 ($\ce{Th-234}$) nucleus undergoes beta decay and transforms into a protactinium-234 nucleus ($\ce{Pa-234}$).  The binding energies per nucleon for thorium-234 and protactinium-234 are $7.84\  $MeV and $7.92\ \ $MeV respectively. An electron has a rest mass energy of $0.020\  $MeV.

    The reaction can be represented as follows:

    $\ce{^{234}_{90}Th}  \rightarrow\ \ce{^{234}_{91}Pa}+\ce{^{0}_{-1}e}+\overline{\nu_{e }}$

    1. Explain what is meant by binding energy per nucleon. [2 marks]

    2.     Calculate, in joules, the total energy released in the decay. [3 marks]

2. A cubic container is filled with pure $\ce{thorium-234}$. The container does not contain any other elements. After 62 days, the ratio of the number of nuclei of decay product to $\ce{thorium-234}$ nuclei is 5:1.

    Calculate, in days, the half-life of the $\ce{thorium-234}$ nuclei. [3 marks]

1.
    1. The minimum energy required/work to (completely) separate the nucleons in a nucleus  $\checkmark$

        Divided by the number of nucleons in the nucleus $\checkmark$

    2. The difference between binding energies = Energy released $\checkmark$

        The difference between binding energies = $1853.3-1834.56=$ $18.74\ \ \ce{MeV}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(18.74\right)\left({10}^6\right)\left(1.6\ \times\ {10^{-19}}\right)=$ :answer[$3.0\ \times\ {10}^{-12}\ \ce{J}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The sum of the binding energies of the products:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(234\right)\left(7.92\right)+0.020=1853.3\ \ce{MeV}$

        The binding energy of the mother nucleus:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(234\right)\left(7.84\right)=1834.56\ \ce{MeV}$

        The total energy released equals the difference in binding energies of the products and mother nucleus:

        The difference between binding energies:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $1853.3-1834.56=18.74\ \ce{MeV}$

        The total energy released in Joules:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\left(18.74\right)\left({10}^{6}\right)\left(1.6\ \times\ {10}^{-19}\right)=3.0\ \times\ {10^{-12}}\ \ce{J}$

2. $\dfrac{N_0-N_0e^{-62\lambda}}{N_0e^{-62\lambda}}=$ $5$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\lambda=\dfrac{ln6}{62}=$ $0.029$ $\checkmark$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{\frac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{ln2}{\lambda}=\dfrac{ln2}{0.029}=$ :answer[$24\ \ce{days}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
After 62 days, the number of the nucleus $N$ of thorium-234:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$

    Where $t=62$ days

    $\hspace{1em}$ ${N=N}_0e^{-62\lambda}$

    After 62 days, the number of the nucleus $N_p$ of protactinium-234:

    $\hspace{1em}$ ${N}_p=N_{0}-N$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $N_p=N_0-N_0e^{-62\lambda}$

    The ratio of protactinium-234 nuclei to thorium-234 nuclei:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{N_0-N_0e^{-62\lambda}}{N_0e^{-62\lambda}}=5$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $N_0-N_0e^{-62\lambda}=5N_0e^{-62\lambda}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $N_0=6N_0e^{-62\lambda}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $1=6e^{-62\lambda}$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $e^{62\lambda}=6$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $62\lambda=ln6$

    $\hspace{1em}$ $\lambda=\dfrac{ln6}{62}=0.029$

    The half-life $t_{1/2}$ of the thorium-234 nuclei:

    $\hspace{1em}$ $t_{\frac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{ln2}{\lambda}=\dfrac{ln2}{0.029}=24\ $days
