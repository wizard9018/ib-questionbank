**Maximum mark: 12**

When a nuclide undergoes beta-minus decay $({\beta}^- )$, the observed energy spectrum shows that the energy gained by the emitted beta particles is not the same for every beta particle.

1. Discuss whether the energy spectrum for the emitted beta particles is discrete or continuous. [2 marks]

Tritium $(\ce{^{3}_{1}H})$  decays into helium $(\ce{^{3}_{2}He})$ by the emission of a ${\beta}^-$ particle.
The graph below shows the energy distribution for the ${\beta}^-$ particles emitted during this decay.

![image](images/image.png)

The graph intersects the horizontal axis at 18.6 keV. This point is labelled as M.

2. Use information from the graph above to calculate the maximum speed of the emitted ${\beta}^-$ particles (ignore relativistic effects). [3 marks]

The peak of the ${\beta}^-$ energy spectrum shown on the graph above is shifted towards the lower energy side.

3. Explain, in terms of Coulomb's law, why the ${\beta}^+$ spectrum is expected to be shifted towards the higher energy side. [2 marks]

4. Tritium $(\ce{^{3}_{1}H})$  has a half-life of 12.3 years.
The activity of a sample of tritium is initially measured to be $36\,000\,\dfrac{\text{counts}}{\ce{s}}$.
    Determine:

    1. The initial number of atoms in the sample. [3 marks]

    2. The activity of the sample after 4 years. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Continuous $\checkmark$

    There is an infinite number of ways in which energy can be shared between the ${\beta}^-$/beta-minus particle/electron and the $\overline{\nu}$/antineutrino. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    As lead undergoes beta-minus decay two particles are emitted from the nucleus. These particles are an electron $({\beta}^-)$ and an antineutrino ($\overline{\nu}$).

    The energy that is released during this decay can be shared between these two particles in any of an infinite number of ways which are only restricted by the conservation of energy (and momentum).

    Therefore, the emitted beta-particles will carry an amount of energy that might take any value within a well-defined range.

    The energy spectrum is then continuous.

2. ::indent
    - Energy $=\ll 18.6\,\ce{keV}=(18.6\times 10^3)(1.60\times 10^{-19}\,\ce{J})\gg = 2.976\times 10^{-15}\,\ce{J}$  $\checkmark$

    - $\dfrac{1}{2}m_e v^2=2.976\times 10^{-15}\,\ce{J}$

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(2.976\times 10^{-15}\,\ce{J})}{9.110\times 10^{-31}\,\ce{kg}}}$         $\checkmark$

    - $8.08\times 10^7  \ll \ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}\gg$  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The highest energy of the emitted ${\beta}^-$ particles is  18.6 keV.

    This corresponds to:

    - $(18.6\times 10^3)(1.60\times 10^{-19}\,\ce{J})=2.976\times 10^{-15}\,\ce{J}$

    which represents the highest kinetic energy of the emitted ${\beta}^-$ (electrons).

    Given that $E_k=\dfrac{1}{2} mv^2$ we can solve for $v$ and use the electron rest mass to find the maximum speed.

    - $v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2E_k}{m_e}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(2.976\times 10^{-15}\,\ce{J})}{9.110\times 10^{-31}\,\ce{kg}}}=8.08\times 10^7\,\ce{m}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

3. ${\beta}^+$ are $\underline{\text{repelled}}$ by the nucleus due to Coulomb's force $\checkmark$

    ${\beta}^+$ will increase their kinetic energy after emission $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Unlike the negatively charged ${\beta}^-$ particles (electrons) which are attracted towards the nucleus after being emitted due to the electric force (Coulomb's law), the ${\beta}^+$ particles (positrons) are repelled by the positively charged nucleus.

    This causes the electrons to slow down after the emission thus losing some of their kinetic energy, and positrons to speed up thus increasing their kinetic energy.

        - $\lambda = \ll \dfrac{\ln{2}}{T_{\frac{1}{2}}} = \dfrac{\ln{2}}{(12.3365)(24)(60)(60)}\gg=1.79\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$  $\checkmark$

        - $\ll A=\lambda N \gg$

        - $N=\dfrac{A}{\lambda}=\dfrac{36\,000\,\ce{s}^{-1}}{1.79\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{s}^{-1}}$   $\checkmark$

        - $N=2.01\times 10^{13}$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        We first need to find the value of the decay constant $\lambda$. This is defined in terms of the half-life which should be expressed in seconds.

        - $\lambda = \dfrac{\ln{2}}{T_{\frac{1}{2}}} = \dfrac{\ln{2}}{(12.3)(365)(24)(60)(60)}=1.79\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

        This value is related to the activity of a sample according to

        - $A=\lambda N$

        Where $A$ is the activity and $N$ is the number of atoms.

        To find our answer, we solve for $N$

        - $N=\dfrac{A}{\lambda}=\dfrac{36\,000\,\ce{s}^{-1}}{1.79\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{s}^{-1}} = 2.01\times 10^{13}$

    2. $\underline{\text{Method 1}}$

        - $A=\lambda N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$

        - $A=(1.79\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{s}^{-1})(2.01\times 10^{13})e^{-(1.79\times 10^{-9}\, \ce{s}^{-1})[(4)(365)(24)(60)(60\ce{s})]}$ $\checkmark$

        - $A=28\,700\,\ce{s}^{-1}$  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\text{Method 2}}$

        - $A=A_0  2^{-\frac{t}{T_{\frac{1}{2}}}}$

        - $A=(36\,000\,\ce{s}^{-1})(2^{-\frac{4}{12.3}})$   $\checkmark$

        - $A=28\,700\,\ce{s}^{-1}$   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The activity of the sample after 4 years can be calculated in two different ways:

        $\underline{\text{Method 1}}$\
        Use the expression given in the formula booklet:

        - $A=\lambda N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$

        Substitute the value for each magnitude.

        - $A=(1.79\times 10^{-9}\,\ce{s}^{-1})(2.01\times 10^{13})e^{-(1.79\times 10^{-9}\, \ce{s}^{-1})[(4)(365)(24)(60)(60\ce{s})]}$

        Which leads to

        - $A=28\,700\,\ce{s}^{-1}$

        $\underline{\text{Method 2}}$\
        Consider that after 4 years the fraction $\dfrac{4}{12.3}$ represents the number of half-lives that have passed.

        The activity will then be reduced by a factor $2^{-\frac{4}{12.3}}$, thus it can be calculated using:

        - $A=A_0 2^{-\frac{t}{T_{\frac{1}{2}}}}$ (not in formula booklet)

        - $A=(36\,000\,\ce{s}^{-1})(2^{-\frac{4}{12.3}})$

        - $A=28\,700\,\ce{s}^{-1}$
