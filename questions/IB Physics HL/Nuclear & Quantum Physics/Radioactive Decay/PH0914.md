# PH0914

The continuous spectrum of beta decay can be explained by

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ Emission of neutrinos with varying energies from the nucleus

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ Absorption of neutrinos by the nucleus

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ Emission of gamma radiation with varying energies from the nucleus

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ Absorption of gamma radiation by the nucleus

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

During beta decay, beta particles are observed to possess a continuous spectrum of energies. To justify this with the existence of discrete nuclear energy levels, it was proposed that beta particles must be accompanied by another particle, and the combined energy of the two particles should be discrete but the proportion in which this energy is shared between the particles varies. Consequently, the suggestion arose that a chargeless and extremely small uncharged particle should accompany beta decay. The following experimental evidence identifying the neutrino confirmed this proposal.
