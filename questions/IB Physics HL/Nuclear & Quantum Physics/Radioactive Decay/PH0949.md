# PH0949

The graph below shows a graph of binding energy per nucleon against mass number for some nuclei. Nuclei P, Q, R, S, T and U are labelled.

![PH0949](images/PH0949.png)

Which of the following nuclear reactions involving nuclei P, Q, R, S, T and U will result in the largest amount of energy released per reaction?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ P + Q $\rarr$ R

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ R $\rarr$ P + Q

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ S + T $\rarr$ U

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ U $\rarr$ S + T

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Energy will be released when binding energy per nucleon increases so we can disregard B and C (these require the input of energy, as binding energy per nucleon decreases). Note that B represents the fission of a small nucleus and C represents the fusion of large nuclei.

Comparing the difference in binding energy per nucleon (difference in height on graph), we can see that it is slightly greater for reaction P + Q $\rarr$ R compared to U $\rarr$ S + T. So energy released per nucleon is greater for the reaction P + Q $\rarr$ R.

**However, we have been asked for energy released per reaction.**

Energy released per reaction = (Difference in binding energy per nucleon) $\times$ (number of nucleons per reaction)

The number of nucleons involved in each reaction U $\rarr$ S + T is much greater than the number of nucleons involved in each reaction P + Q $\rarr$ R (around 10 times greater) So energy released per reaction is greater for U $\rarr$ S + T.
