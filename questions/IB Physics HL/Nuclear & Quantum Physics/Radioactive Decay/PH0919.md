# PH0919

Two radioactive samples of different nuclides, A and B, have decay constants of ${\lambda}_A$ and $2{\lambda}_A$, respectively. Both samples initially have $N_0$ undecayed nuclei. What is the number of undecayed nuclei in sample A when the number of undecayed nuclei in sample B is $\dfrac{N_0}{4}$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{N_0}{8}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{N_0}{6}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{N_0}{4}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{N_0}{2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The relationship between $T_{\frac{1}{2}}$ and $\lambda$:

- $T_{\frac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{\ln{2}}{\lambda}$

Therefore, it can be deduced that half-life of the sample A, $T_{\frac{1}{2}A}$, is twice the half-life of the sample B, $T_{\frac{1}{2}B}$.

- $T_{\frac{1}{2}A}$=2$T_{\frac{1}{2}B}$

The time $t$ needed for sample B to have $\dfrac{N_0}{4}$ undecayed nuclei:

- $t=2 T_{\frac{1}{2}B}$

Therefore:

- $t=T_{\frac{1}{2}A}$

After the time equivalent to one half-life of sample A, the number of undecayed nuclei of sample A is $\dfrac{N_0}{2}$.
