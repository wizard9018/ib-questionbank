# PH0508

A pure radioactive sample of mass $m$ has a decay constant of $\lambda$.  Its activity is given by $A$. Another pure sample of the same material has a mass $2m$. Consider the following statements about the sample of mass $2m$ after an interval of two half lives.

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The decay constant is $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$.  |
  | II. |     The activity is $\dfrac{A}{2}$.  |
  | III. |     The mass of decayed nucleons in the sample is $\dfrac{m}{2}$. |

Which of the statements is/are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The decay constant depends on the radioactive material. It does not change for a given nuclide. In this case it is $\lambda$ for both samples as they are the same nuclide. Hence, I is incorrect.

Activity depends on the mass of the sample. If it is $A$ for mass $m$ at $t=0$, it will be $2A$ for $2m$. But after two half lives, it will be halved twice and become $\dfrac{A}{2}$. So, II is correct.

After one half life, half of $2m$ decays, and will leave a mass $m$ decayed, while another $m$ is active. After the second half life, half of the remaining $m $ decays leaving just another $\dfrac{m}{2}$ active. So the total mass of decayed material is $\dfrac{3m}{2}$.  Hence III is incorrect.
