## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The Compton scattering equation states that the difference between the wavelength of the incoming photon and scattered photon $\Delta\lambda$ is given by:

- $\Delta\lambda=\dfrac{h}{m_ec}(1-\textrm{cos}\,\theta)$

Where $h$ is Planck's constant, $m_e$ is the mass of the electron, $c$ is the speed of light, and $\theta$ is the scattering angle (i.e., the angle between the momentum vector of the incoming photon and the momentum vector of the scattered photon).

We see that:

- $\Delta\lambda\propto(1-\textrm{cos}\,\theta)$

Option A shows the graph of $1-\textrm{cos}\,\theta$.

We know that when the change is wavelength is zero the scattering angle should be zero, and when the scattering angle is
180$\degree$ the change in wavelength is maximum. Once the scattering angle is 360$\degree$, the wavelength shift is again zero.
