# PH0517

**Maximum mark: 9**

1. De Broglie related the wavelength of a particle to its momentum. State what is meant by matter waves.     [1 mark]

2.  1. Show that the wavelength $\lambda$ of an electron of mass $m$ and kinetic energy $E$ can be found from

        $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mE}}$             [2 marks]

    2. Assuming that the magnitude of the energy of a given energy level of an atom is equal to the kinetic energy of the electron, show that the wavelength of the electron in the second energy level in a hydrogen atom is approximately $6.7 \times 10^{-10}$ m.   [3 marks]

    3. Use the wavelength from b(ii) to estimate the orbital radius of this electron.      [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any appropriate statement $\checkmark$

    - de Broglie relationship with definition of the symbols;
    - particles have a wavelength that can be determined from their momentum

2. 1. ::indent
        - $E=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}$

        - $p=\sqrt{2mE}\ \checkmark$

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}\ \checkmark$

        - $\ll\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mE}}\gg$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        - $E=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}$

        Therefore,

        - $p^2=2mE$

        - $p=\sqrt{2mE}$

        According to de Broglie, wavelength can be determined from the electron’s momentum

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$

        Then,

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mE}}$

    2. ::indent
        - $E=\ll-\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}\gg$

        - $E=-\dfrac{13.6}{2^2}\ =-3.4\ll \text{eV}\gg \checkmark$

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{6.63\times{10}^{-34}}{\sqrt{(2)(9.11\times{10}^{-31})(3.4)(1.6\times{10}^{-19})}} \checkmark$

        - $\lambda=\ 6.66\times{10}^{-10}\,\ce{m}\ \checkmark  \ll= 6.7 \times 10^{-10}\, \ce{m}\gg \checkmark$

        (extra significant figure should be shown for final calculation mark)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        To find the energy of an electron in a hydrogen atom

        - $E=-\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$

        For $n=2$ (second energy level)

        - $E=-\dfrac{13.6}{2^2}=-3.4\,\text{eV}$

        Then, the wavelength of this electron is

        $\begin{aligned}
     \lambda&=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2mE}}\\
    \lambda&=\frac{6.63\times{10}^{-34}}{\sqrt{(2)(9.11\times{10}^{-31})(3.4)(1.6\times{10}^{-19})}}\\
    \hspace{0.8cm}\\
    &=6.66\ \times{\ 10}^{-10}\,\ce{m}\\
    \end{aligned}$

    3. ::indent
        - $r=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi mv}$ and $\ \lambda=\dfrac{h}{mv}   \checkmark$

        - $r=\dfrac{(2)(6.66\times{10}^{-10})}{2\pi} \checkmark$

        - $r=2.11\times{10}^{-10}\,\ll \ce{m}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        According to Bohr, the angular momentum of the electron is given by

        - $mvr=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

        Then the orbital radius $r$ is found from

        - $r=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi mv}$

        Since

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$

        Then

        - $r=\dfrac{n\lambda}{2\pi}$

        - $r=\dfrac{(2)(6.66\ \times{\ 10}^{-10})}{2\pi}=2.11\ \times{\ 10}^{-10}\ \ce{m}$
