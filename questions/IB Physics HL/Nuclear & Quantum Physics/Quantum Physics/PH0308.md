# PH0308

In a vacuum chamber, electromagnetic radiation of frequencies $f_A<f_B$ just manage to produce a non-zero photocurrent from metals A and B respectively. When illuminated by an electromagnetic radiation of frequency $f>f_B$, the stopping voltage for the photocurrents from plates A and B are recorded to be $V_A$ and $V_B$ respectively.

Which of the following is true about $V_A$ and $V_B$?

- A. $\hspace{1em}      V_A<V_B$

- B. $\hspace{1em}      V_A>V_B$

- C. $\hspace{1em}      V_A=V_B$

- D. $\hspace{1em} $Information provided is insufficient to compare $V_A$ and $V_B\ \ \ $

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

We are given information about the threshold frequencies of A and B

- $f_A<f_B$

The energy of the threshold frequency photons are equal to the work functions, therefore

- $\phi_A < \phi_B$

From the photoelectric equation

- $E_{max}=hf-\phi$

And we can replace $E_{max}$ with the stopping potential times the elementary charge $eV_s$ to get

- $eV_s=hf-\phi$

Because we know that

- $\phi_A < \phi_B$

This means that

- $(eV_s)_A>(eV_s)_B$

And so

- $(V_s)_A>(V_s)_B$

In words, we can say that the work function of A is less than the work function of B. This means that when both surfaces are exposed to the same energy photon, the electrons emitted from A will have more kinetic energy than those from B, and so will require a higher stopping potential.
