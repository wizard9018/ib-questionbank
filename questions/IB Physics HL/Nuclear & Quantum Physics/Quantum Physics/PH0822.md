# PH0822

**Maximum mark: 10**

X-rays with energy 250 keV are scattered from a carbon block, losing energy in the process.

1. Explain the change in energy of the photons due to scattering. [2 marks]

2. A scattered photon is detected at an angle $45\degree$.

    1. Show that the wavelength shift at this angle is approximately $7 \times 10^{-13}$ m. [2 marks]

    2. Determine the energy of the scattered photon at this angle. [3 marks]

3. Determine the minimum energy of a photon that could be detected after scattering off the carbon block. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Incident photon collides with a free electron. $\checkmark$

    The electron gains energy or momentum from the photon/the photon momentum decreases. $\checkmark$

    (The scattered photon has less energy.)

2.
    1.

        - $≪\Delta \lambda=\dfrac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos \theta)≫$

        - $\Delta \lambda=\dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{(9.11 \times 10^{-31})(3\times 10^8)}(1-\cos 45\degree)$ $\checkmark$

        - $\Delta \lambda=$ :answer[$7.11\times 10^{-13}\,\ce{m}$] $\checkmark$

    2.

        - $\lambda_i = ≪\dfrac{hc}{E_i}≫$

        -  $\lambda_i = \dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3\times 10^8)}{(250\times 10^3)(1.6\times 10^{-19})}=4.97\times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

        - $\lambda_f = \Delta \lambda + \lambda_i$

        - $\lambda_f = 0.711 \times 10^{-12} + 4.97\times 10^{-12}=5.68 \times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

        - $E_f=≪\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3\times 10^8)}{5.68\times 10^{-12}}≫=$ :answer[$3.50\times 10^{-14}\,\ce{J}\,\,$] (accept $219\,\ce{keV}$) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The wavelength of a photon is given by

        - $E=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{hc}{E}$

        The initial wavelength

        - $\lambda_i = \dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3\times 10^8)}{(250\times 10^3)(1.6\times 10^{-19})}=4.97\times10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$

        Since

        - $\Delta \lambda = \lambda_f - \lambda_i$

        Then

        - $\lambda_f = \Delta \lambda + \lambda_i$

        - $\lambda_f = 0.711\times 10^{-12} + 4.97\times 10^{-12} = 5.68\times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$

        Then, the scattered energy is

        - $E_f=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_f}=\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3\times 10^8)}{(5.68\times 10^{-12})}=3.50\times 10^{-14} \, \ce{J}=219\,\ce{keV}$

3.

    - $\Delta \lambda_{max} = 2\dfrac{h}{m_e c}$

    - $\Delta \lambda_{max} = \dfrac{(2)(6.63\times 10^{-34})}{(9.11 \times 10^{-31})(3\times 10^8)}=4.85\times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$ $\checkmark$

    - $\lambda_{f_{max}}=\Delta \lambda_{max} + \lambda_i = 4.85\times 10^{-12}+4.97\times 10^{-12}=9.82\times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$

    - $E_{min} = ≪\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_{max}}≫=\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3\times 10^8)}{(9.82\times 10^{-12})}$ $\checkmark$

    - $E_{min} = $ :answer[$2.03 \times 10^{-14}\,\ce{J}$]  (accept $\,127\,\ce{keV} $) $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The minimum energy of a scattered photon is at the maximum Compton shift, which is at $180\degree$.

    - $\Delta \lambda=\dfrac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos \theta)$

    - $\Delta \lambda_{max}=\dfrac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos (180\degree))=2\dfrac{h}{m_e c}$

    - $\Delta \lambda_{max}=2\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})}{(9.11\times 10^{-31})(3\times 10^8)} = 4.85 \times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$

    Then

    - $\lambda_{f_{max}}=\lambda_{max} + \lambda_i = 4.85 \times 10^{-12} + 4.97 \times 10^{-12} = 9.82\times 10^{-12}\,\ce{m}$

    The minimum energy of a scattered photon is

    - $E_{min}=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_{max}}=\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3\times 10^8)}{(9.82\times 10^{-12})}=2.03\times10^{-14}\,\ce{J}=127\,\ce{keV}$
