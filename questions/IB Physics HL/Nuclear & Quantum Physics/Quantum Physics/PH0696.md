# PH0696

**Maximum mark: 12**

1. On a sunny day, a house is powered by a solar panel. In order to run home appliances, the incident power of sunlight on the solar panel must be 10 kW.

    1. The average surface temperature of the Sun is 5850 K. Show that the peak wavelength of the radiation from the Sun is around 500 nm. [2 marks]

    2. It can be assumed that 7.5\% of the energy from incident sunlight consists of peak wavelength photons that are converted to electrical energy by the panel. Calculate the number of 500 nm photons that must strike the solar panel per second in order to meet the power requirement of the appliances. [3 marks]

2. Solar panels make use of the photoelectric effect in order to function. The graph below shows how the maximum kinetic energy of an emitted photoelectron $E_{max}$ varies with the frequency $f$ of light in the photoelectric effect.

    ![Screenshot 2026-01-11 at 7.18.58 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-11 at 7.18.58 AM.png>)

    1. Explain how the value for Planck\'s constant could be obtained from the above graph.  [2 marks]

    2. The material in the solar panel has a threshold frequency of $2.7 \times {10}^{14}$ Hz.  Calculate the frequency of light that causes the emission of an electron with a maximum kinetic energy of 3.4 eV. [3 marks]

    3. State two observations of the photoelectric effect that were inconsistent with the wave model of light.   [2 marks]

1. 1. ::indent
        - $\dfrac{2.90\times{10}^{-3}}{5850}$ $\checkmark$

        - :answer[496 nm] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
By using Wien\'s displacement law,

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\lambda_{max}=\dfrac{2.90\times{10}^{-3}}{5850}=496$ nm

    2. Energy of the 500 nm photons in one second $=\left(10000\right)\left(0.075\right)\left(1\right)=750\, \ce{J}$ $\checkmark$

        Energy per 500 nm photon = $\dfrac{(6.63\times{10}^{-34})(3.0\times{10}^8)}{500\times{10}^{-9}}=3.98\times{10}^{-19}\, \ce{J}$ $\checkmark$

        $\hspace{1em}$ $n=\dfrac{750}{(3.98\times{10}^{-19})}=$ :answer[$1.9\times{10}^{21}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Total power of the 500 nm photons = $\left(10000\right)\left(0.075\right)=750\, \ce{W}$

        The energy $E$ received by the solar panel due to 500 nm photons in one second:

        - $E=P\,t=\left(750\right)\left(1\right)=750\, \ce{J}$

        The energy $E_p$ of one 500 nm photon:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $E_p=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}=\dfrac{(6.63\times{10}^{-34})(3.0\times{10}^8)}{500\times{10}^{-9}}=3.98\times{10}^{-19}\ \ce{J}$

        The number $n$ of 500 nm photons:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $n=\dfrac{750}{(3.98\times{10}^{-19})}=19\times{10}^{20}$

2. 1. ::indent
        - $E_{max}=hf-\phi$ $\checkmark$

        - $h$  will be the gradient of the graph $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The equation $E_{max}=hf-\phi$ is in slope-intercept form $y = mx + b$

        Given that $E_{max}$ and $f$ are the $y$ and $x$ variables respectively, $h$ will be the gradient of the graph.

    2. ::indent

        - $\mathrm{\phi}=\left(6.63\times{10}^{-34}\right)\left(2.7\times{10}^{14}\right)=$ $1.8\times{10}^{-19}\ \, \ce{J}$ $\checkmark$

        - $E_{max}=\left(3.4\right)\left(1.6\times{10}^{-19}\right)=$ $5.4\times{10}^{-19}\ \ \ce{J}$ $\checkmark$

        - $f=\dfrac{5.44\times{10}^{-19}+17.9\times{10}^{-20}}{6.63\times{10}^{-34}}=$ :answer[$1.1\times{10}^{15}\ \ce{Hz}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
The work function $\mathrm{\phi}$ of the solar panel:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $\mathrm{\phi}=hf_c=\left(6.63\times{10}^{-34}\right)\left(2.7\times{10}^{14}\right)=17.9\times{10}^{-20}$ J

        The maximum kinetic energy $E_{max}$ of emitted electrons in joules:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $E_{max}=\left(3.4\right)\left(1.6\times{10}^{-19}\right)=5.44\times{10}^{-19}$ J

        The frequency $f$ of the light can be found by rearranging the formula of kinetic energy of emitted photoelectron:

        $\hspace{1em}$ $f=\dfrac{E_{max}+\mathrm{\phi}}{h}=\dfrac{5.44\times{10}^{-19}+17.9\times{10}^{-20}}{6.63\times{10}^{-34}}=1.1\times{10}^{15}$ Hz

    3. *Any two from:*

        - The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons is independent of light intensity $\checkmark$
        - The ejection of photoelectrons is instantaneous when light is incident  $\checkmark$
        - There is a certain minimum frequency below which no photoelectrons are emitted   $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
In wave theory it is thought that energy is continually delivered to the electrons. A consequence of this would be that the energy could build up over time, leading to eventual photoemission regardless of the light\'s frequency.

        In addition, the wave model predicted that higher intensity light was associated with higher amplitude waves and would therefore result in higher energy in the photoelectrons.

        Replacing the wave model with the particle/photon model resolved the contradictions between the wave theory and the observations.
