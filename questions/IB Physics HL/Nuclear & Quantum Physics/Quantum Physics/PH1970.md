# PH1970

**Maximum mark: 10**

In an electric circuit used to investigate the photoelectric effect, monochromatic light of wavelength 300 nm is incident on a metal surface in a vacuum. A collecting plate is connected to a variable power supply, an ammeter and a voltmeter.

![Screenshot 2026-08-27 at 5.16.05 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-08-27 at 5.16.05 PM.png>)

The ammeter detects a current when the power supply is turned off.

1. Explain why the reading of the ammeter is zero at large negative potential difference of the power supply. [2 marks]

2. The graph shows the variation of the reading of the ammeter with the potential difference of the power supply.

    ![Screenshot 2026-08-27 at 5.24.41 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-08-27 at 5.24.41 PM.png>)

    1. Complete the graph for positive potential difference. [1 mark]

    2. Explain the variation of the reading of the ammeter when the supply provides a high positive potential difference. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the work function of the metal surface in eV. [3 marks]

The light source is replaced with another one with longer wavelength and higher intensity. The ammeter reads zero regardless of the potential difference.

4. Explain how this supports the particle nature of light. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Electrons are repelled by the collecting plate $\checkmark$\
    Electrons with highest kinetic energy/most energetic don't have enough energy to reach the collecting plate $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    When light hits the metal surface, electrons are emitted.

    When the power supply is off, some of the electrons emitted reach the collecting plate and the ammeter detects current.

    When the collecting plate is connected to negative potential difference it becomes negatively charged and repels the electrons emitted from the metal surface.

    The electrons that have high kinetic energy can overcome this repulsion and reach the collecting plate.

    When the potential difference is increased in the negative direction, the collecting plate will become more negatively charged and fewer electrons will reach the plate.

    When the highest energy electrons don't have enough energy to reach the collecting plate, the current will be zero. The potential difference at this point is the stopping potential difference.

    The current will remain zero for further increase in the negative potential difference.

    2.  Electrons are attracted by the collecting plate. $\checkmark$\
        All emitted electrons reach the collecting plate at high positive potential difference. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        When the collecting plate is connected to positive potential difference, it will be positively charged and attracts electrons emitted from the metal surface.

        As the positive potential difference increases, the collecting plate carries more positive charge and attracts more electrons.

        When the collecting plate carries enough positive charge so that it attracts all electrons emitted from the metal surface the current will reach its maximum value.

        Any further increase in the positive potential difference will give the same maximum reading of current since all electrons are attracted by the collecting plate and the number of electrons emitted is governed by the number of incident photons.

3.  ::indent
    - $E = V_{s} = 0.8$ eV $\checkmark$

    - $\phi = \dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{300 \times 10^{-9}} - 0.8$ $\checkmark$

    - $\phi =$ :answer[$3.33$ eV] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The highest energy of an emitted electron is the same as the stopping voltage in eV.

    From the graph,

    - $E = V_{s} = 0.8$ eV

    Since

    - $E = hf - \phi$

    And

    - $f = \dfrac{c}{\lambda}$

    Then

    - $E = h\dfrac{c}{\lambda} - \phi$

    - $0.8 = \dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{300 \times 10^{-9}} - \phi$

    - $\phi = \dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{300 \times 10^{-9}} - 0.8$

    - $\phi = 3.33$ eV

    $\\$

4.  Energy of a photon depends on its frequency/wavelength $\checkmark$\
    Photon energy is not enough to release an electron $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Particle nature of light suggests that light consists of particles, which are photons.

    The energy of a photon depends on its frequency or wavelength.

    - $E = hf = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

    The energy of a photon is independent of the intensity of light.

    A photon must have enough energy to release an electron from a metal surface.

    When the wavelength of the source increases, the energy of a photon decreases. Then, a photon's energy is not enough to release an electron from the surface and no current will be detected by the ammeter.
