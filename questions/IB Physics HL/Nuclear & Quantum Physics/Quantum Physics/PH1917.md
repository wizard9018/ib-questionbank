# PH1917

**Maximum mark: 8**

In an experiment, electrons are emitted from a heated cathode and accelerated through a potential difference $V$ towards a thin film of carbon atoms.

![Screenshot 2026-03-18 at 3.59.10 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-03-18 at 3.59.10 PM.png>)

After passing through the film, the electrons produce a circular pattern on a fluorescent screen.

1. Explain why these patterns provide evidence of de Broglie's hypothesis. [2 marks]

2. Show that the de Broglie wavelength $\lambda$ is given by

    $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_eeV}}$ [2 marks]

3. Explain the effect of decreasing the potential difference on the width of the central maximum of the diffraction pattern. [3 marks]

4. Other experiments gave evidence that light behaved like a particle.  Identify **one** of these experiments. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Electrons undergo diffraction with carbon nuclei $\checkmark$\
    Diffraction is a wave phenomenon / electrons behave like a wave $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    de Broglie's hypothesis states that particles such as electrons have wave properties with a wavelength $\lambda$ that depends on the particle's momentum.

    The circular pattern produced in this experiment is a diffraction pattern.

    Since diffraction is a wave phenomenon, then the electrons behave like waves. This observation confirms de Broglie's hypothesis.

2. ::indent
    - $E_k=eV$ $\checkmark$

    Correct work leading to $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_eeV}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The energy gained by the electron through the potential difference is

    - $E=q\Delta V=eV$

    Then the kinetic energy of the electron

    - $E_k=eV$

    Since

    - $E_k=\dfrac{p^2}{2m_e}$

    Then

    - $eV=\dfrac{p^2}{2m_e}$
    - $p^2=2m_eeV$
    - $p=\sqrt{2m_eeV}$

    The de Broglie wavelength is given by

    - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$

    Then

    - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_eeV}}$

3. de Broglie wavelength increases / is inversely proportional to the square root of the potential difference / $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$ $\checkmark$\
    More spreading of electrons\
    OR\
    Increase in $\theta$ $\checkmark$\
    The width of the central maximum increases $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Since

    - $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_eeV}}$

    Then the de Broglie wavelength is inversely proportional to the square root of the potential difference

    - $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$

    When the potential difference decreases, the wavelength increases.

    A larger wavelength passing through the same carbon atoms leads to more spreading of electrons which leads to a wider central maximum of the diffraction pattern.

    OR

    Since

    - $\lambda=d\sin{\theta}$

    Where $d$ is the atomic spacing, then the increase in wavelength leads to an increase in $\theta$ which means an increase in the width of the central maximum.

4. Photoelectric effect \
    OR\
    Compton effect $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The photoelectric effect described in Einstein's work involves the interaction between particles of light (photons) and electrons which are emitted from a surface. This experiment could not be explained by a wave-model for light.

    Compton scattering involves a decrease in frequency (increase in wavelength) of photons which are incident on electrons. Due to this collision, there is an energy transfer from the photons to the electrons causing a decrease in frequency on the photons. This represents a collision where momentum is conserved. This change in frequency could not be explained by a wave-model for light.
