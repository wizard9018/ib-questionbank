# PH0311

Red and blue light beams of equal intensities illuminate a photosensitive plate and produce photocurrents $I_R$ and $I_B$ respectively. The respective stopping voltages against the two photocurrents are $V_R$ and $V_B$.

Consider the following statements:

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | $I_R>I_B$  |
  | II. | $V_R<V_B$ |
  | III. | Photoelectrons with higher kinetic energy result in a higher current. |

Which statements are correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Intensity of a beam increases with:

1. The energy of photons ($E=hf$, each blue photon carries higher energy than a red photon).

2. Number of photons delivered by the beam per second (since red photons pack less energy than the blue ones, their number needs to be greater to have the same intensity as the blue beam).

More photons eject more electrons, and hence the red beam causes higher photocurrent (I is true).

Higher energy photons impart higher kinetic energy to electrons, and thus the blue beam needs a higher stopping voltage (II is also correct).

The kinetic energy of photoelectrons does not increase the photocurrent in the circuit as current is dependent on the number of electrons passing a point in a given time and not their individual speeds (III is incorrect).
