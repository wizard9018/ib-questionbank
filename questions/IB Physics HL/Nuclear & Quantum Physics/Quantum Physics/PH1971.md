# PH1971

**Maximum mark: 9**

A photon with a wavelength of $43\times10^{-12}$ m collides with an electron at rest. The photon scatters with an angle $\theta$.

![Screenshot 2026-08-27 at 5.09.35 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-08-27 at 5.09.35 PM.png>)

The electron recoils with an energy of 0.62 keV.

1. Outline why Compton scattering is evidence of the particle nature of light. [1 mark]

2. 1. Show that the energy of the scattered photon is 28 keV. [3 marks]

    2. Calculate the scattering angle $\theta$. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the maximum scattered wavelength for this incident photon. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Photons have momentum/transfers momentum to the electron $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    In Compton scattering, a photon collides with an electron and transfers energy and momentum to it. This cannot be explained by the wave nature of light.

    Considering that light consists of particles, photons that have energy and momentum help explain Compton scattering.

2.  1.  ::indent
        - $E_{i} = \dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{43 \times 10^{-12}}$ $\checkmark$

        - $E_{i} = 28.8$ keV $\checkmark$

        - $E_{f} = 28.8 - 0.62$ $\checkmark$

        - $\ll E_{f} = 28.2$ keV $\gg$

        $\\$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The initial energy of the photon

        - $E_{i} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_{i}}$

        - $E_{i} = \dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{43 \times 10^{-12}} = 28.8$ keV

        The energy of the scattered photon

        - $E_{f} = E_{i} - E_{\textrm{electron}}$

        - $E_{f} = 28.8 - 0.62 = 28.2$ keV

        $\\$

    2.  ::indent
        - $\dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{28.2 \times 10^{3}} - 43 \times 10^{-12} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{(9.110 \times 10^{-31})(3 \times 10^{8})}(1 - \cos\theta)$\
          **OR** $9.72 \times 10^{-13} = 2.43 \times 10^{-12}(1 - \cos\theta)$ $\checkmark$

        - $\ll \cos\theta = 1 - 0.4 = 0.6 \gg$

        - $\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.6) =$ :answer[$53\degree$] $\checkmark$

        Accept $0.93$ rad

        $\\$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Since

        - $\lambda_{f} - \lambda_{i} = \dfrac{h}{m_{e}c}(1 - \cos\theta)$

        And the scattered wavelength is given by

        - $\lambda_{f} = \dfrac{hc}{E_{f}}$

        Then

        - $\dfrac{hc}{E_{f}} - \lambda_{i} = \dfrac{h}{m_{e}c}(1 - \cos\theta)$

        - $\dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}}{28.2 \times 10^{3}} - 43 \times 10^{-12} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{(9.110 \times 10^{-31})(3 \times 10^{8})}(1 - \cos\theta)$

        - $9.72 \times 10^{-13} = 2.43 \times 10^{-12}(1 - \cos\theta)$

        - $1 - \cos\theta = \dfrac{9.72 \times 10^{-13}}{2.43 \times 10^{-12}}$

        - $\cos\theta = 1 - 0.4 = 0.6$

        - $\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.6) = 53\degree$

        $\\$

3.  ::indent
    - Recognition that $\theta = 180\degree$ (or $\pi$ rad) $\checkmark$

    - $\lambda_{f} = 43 \times 10^{-12} + (2.43 \times 10^{-12})(2)$ $\checkmark$

    - $\lambda_{f} =$ :answer[$4.79 \times 10^{-11}$ m] $\checkmark$

    $\\$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The maximum wavelength of a scattered photon is when the scattering angle is $180\degree$.

    Then

    - $\lambda_{f} - \lambda_{i} = \dfrac{h}{m_{e}c}(1 - \cos\theta)$

    - $\lambda_{f} = \lambda_{i} + \dfrac{h}{m_{e}c}(1 - \cos 180\degree)$

    - $\lambda_{f} = 43 \times 10^{-12} + \left(\dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{(9.110 \times 10^{-31})(3 \times 10^{8})}\right)(1 - (-1))$

    - $\lambda_{f} = 43 \times 10^{-12} + (2.43 \times 10^{-12})(2) = 4.79 \times 10^{-11}$ m

    $\\$
