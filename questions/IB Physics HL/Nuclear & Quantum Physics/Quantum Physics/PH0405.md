# PH0405

In a laboratory experiment, $n$ moles of photons of wavelength $\lambda$ are normally incident every second per square meter on a solar sail. Which of the following expressions correctly states the photo-pressure on the solar sail, given that the photons are reflected elastically?

- A. $\hspace{1em}      \dfrac{h}{\lambda}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}      \dfrac{2h}{\lambda}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}      (n)(N_A)\dfrac{h}{\lambda}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}    (n) (N_A)\dfrac{2h}{\lambda}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From de Broglie’s equation, the momentum of each photon is $p=\dfrac{h}{\lambda}$.

Since it is scattered normally (according to law of reflection, light bounces off normal to the surface), the change in momentum per photon is $p-(-p)=2p=\dfrac{2h}{\lambda}$.

So, the force, or the total change in momentum per second, is, $(n)(N_A)\dfrac{2h}{\lambda}$.

Since this force acts on a unit area, it also equals the pressure on the solar sail.
