# PH1267

A monochromatic light source is directed onto a metal surface, and electrons are emitted from the surface in a photoelectric effect experiment.  The wavelength of the light is decreased while the number of incident photons is increased, as shown in the diagram below, where$\lambda_1>\lambda_2$.

![Screenshot 2026-01-07 at 4.21.44 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-07 at 4.21.44 PM.png>)

Which of the following statements is **always true** about the number of electrons emitted and their kinetic energy when the wavelength decreases and incident rate of photons increases?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$ Rate of electron emission $\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$ Maximum kinetic energy $\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| increases | decreases |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | stays the same | increases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | increases | increases |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | decreases | increases |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Decreasing the wavelength of the light increases the energy of the incident photons, since $E=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$.  This will cause the maximum kinetic energy of the electrons emitted to increase, according to the photoelectric equation:

- $E_{max}=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}-\phi$

Where $\phi$ is the work function of the metal.

Increasing the intensity of the light increases the number of photons hitting the surface, which increases the number of electrons emitted.

Therefore, when both the wavelength is shortened (increasing energy) and the intensity is increased (increasing photon count), **more electrons** are emitted and their **maximum kinetic energy increases**.
