# PH0866

An electron in a certain energy state orbits a proton with a radius $r$ and speed $v$ as shown in the diagram below.

![Screenshot 2026-01-04 at 12.34.39 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-04 at 12.34.39 PM.png>)

According to the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom, which of the following is a correct expression for the energy state of the electron in eV?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $-13.6 \left( \dfrac{h}{2\pi m_e vr}\right)^2$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $-13.6 \left( \dfrac{2\pi m_e vr}{h}\right)^2$
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $-13.6 \left( \dfrac{h}{2\pi m_p vr}\right)^2$
- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $-13.6 \left( \dfrac{2\pi m_p vr}{h}\right)^2$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

According to the Bohr model the electric potential energy of the electron is given by

$\hspace{1em}$ $E = - \dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV

Where $n$ refers to the energy level of the electron $\text{\textendash}$ with $n=1$ being the lowest energy level (ground state).

The Bohr model also states that

$\hspace{1em}$ $mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

This equation states that the angular momentum of the electron
$mvr$ is quantized (specifically that it goes up in steps of $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$). Because we are referring to the angular momentum of the electron, the mass is $m_e$.

Rearranging this equation for
$n$ gives

$\hspace{1em}$ $n = \dfrac{2 \pi m_e vr}{h}$

Substituting this into $E =- \dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ gives

$\hspace{1em}$ $E =- \dfrac{13.6}{\left( \dfrac{2 \pi m_e vr}{h} \right)^2} = -13.6 \left ( \dfrac{h}{2 \pi m_e vr} \right)^2$
