# PH0898

The Bohr model for hydrogen predicts discrete energy levels $n$ for the electron. When an electron transitions energy level $n=3$ to level $n=1$ it emits a photon of energy $E.$

Which of the following correctly describes the energy $E$ of the emitted photon?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{1}{9}(13.6\,\text{eV})$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{1}{4}(13.6\,\text{eV})$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{2}{3}(13.6\,\text{eV})$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{8}{9}(13.6\,\text{eV})$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

According to Bohr's model, the energy of an electron occupying an energy level with $n=3$ is given by:

- $E_{(n=3)}=-\dfrac{13.6}{3^2}\,\text{eV}=-\dfrac{1}{9}(13.6\,\text{eV})$

The energy at $n=1$ is given by:

- $E_{(n=1)}=-\dfrac{13.6}{1^2}\,\text{eV}=-13.6\, \text{eV}$

The difference between these energies corresponds to the energy of the emitted photon.

- $E_{photon}=-\dfrac{1}{9}(13.6\,\text{eV})-(-13.6\, \text{eV})=\dfrac{8}{9}(13.6\,\text{eV})\,$
