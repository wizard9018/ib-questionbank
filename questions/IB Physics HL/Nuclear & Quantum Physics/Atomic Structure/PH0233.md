# PH0233

Some of the energy levels of electrons of a mercury atom are as shown. 

![Screenshot 2026-01-06 at 6.00.27 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-06 at 6.00.27 PM.png>)

Electrons and photons are incident on a mercury vapour causing energy level transitions. Photons emitted from the vapour due to the transition of electrons between energy levels have an energy of 4.9 eV. 

Which of the following could be incident into mercury vapour? 

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}

  | | |

  | :-- | :-- |

  | I. | An electron with an energy of 5.1 eV |

  | II. | A photon with an energy of 4.9 eV |

  | III. | A photon with an energy of 5.1 eV |

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The energy difference between the ground state and the first excited state of an electron is given by 

- $-5.5 - (-10.4) = 4.9$ eV

In order to have emitted photons with an energy of 4.9 eV, electrons must be excited. Incident electrons can excite electrons in orbits with more than sufficient energy while photons need to have the exact amount of energy for moving an electron from one state to another. Therefore a photon with an energy of 5.1 eV cannot excite the electrons but others can. So, the answer is C.
