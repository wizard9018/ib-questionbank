    1. Determine the distance of closest approach for alpha particles that have been accelerated through a potential difference of 5.0 MV. [2 marks]

    2. At the distance of closest approach, the alpha particle stops  $\checkmark$

        Kinetic energy reaches zero  $\checkmark$

        Kinetic energy has been converted into electrical potential energy  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        An alpha particle that is scattered with a very large angle is repelled by the positive nucleus.

        This particle has an initial kinetic energy which transfers into electric potential energy as it moves towards the atom.

        The kinetic energy reaches zero and the alpha particle stops and the electrical potential energy is at its maximum, with all of the kinetic energy having been converted into electrical potential energy.

        This happens at the closest distance from the atom that the alpha particle can reach before being repelled by the positive nucleus and change its direction.

        - $E=qV=(2)(5\times 10^6 )=10\times 10^6\,$eV  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The energy given to the alpha particle by the electric field can be found through

        - $E=qV=(2)(5\times 10^6 )=10\times 10^6\,$eV

        All of this energy becomes electrical potential energy at the distance of closest approach (because kinetic energy is zero at this point)

    2. Rutherford's model uses electromagnetic force to predict interaction  $\checkmark$

        In the Rutherford model the distance of closest approach is very small at high energies/very small atomic radius  $\checkmark$

        Over very small distances, the strong force applies to the interaction  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The initial energy of an alpha particle is transferred to electrical potential energy at the distance of closest approach.

        According to the relation given in part b(i) the initial energy is inversely proportional to the distance of closest approach.

        This leads to a very small distance at high energies and a very small atomic radius which is not true.

        Another experimental results for higher energies shows that the Rutherford model is not valid at high energies.

        At high energies, the very close approach distance results in strong nuclear forces affecting the alpha particles in addition to the electrostatic forces.
