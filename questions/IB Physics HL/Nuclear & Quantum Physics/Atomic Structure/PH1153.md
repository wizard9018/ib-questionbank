## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Emission spectrum is when electrons transition from a higher energy level to a lower one. $\checkmark$\
    Absorption spectrum is when electrons transition from a lower energy level to a higher one. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    When white light hits an atom, electrons will absorb energy that’s equivalent to the difference between the ground state and another energy level. The electron will transition to the higher energy level.\
    Since

    - $E=hf$

    The energy gained by the electron corresponds to a frequency of light which represents a certain color. This will leave a dark region at the position of the absorbed frequency.\
    The white light leaving the atom will represent an absorption spectrum.

    On the other hand, when an excited electron transitions to a lower energy level, it will emit a photon with energy equal to the difference between the two energy levels.\
    The frequency of this photon depends on this energy difference.\
    The spectrum emitted from this atom is an emission spectrum.

2. Atoms absorb/emit energy that is exactly equal to the difference of energies between its energy levels. $\checkmark$\
    Wavelengths of absorbed/emitted photons have discrete values. $\checkmark$\
    The wavelengths emitted are unique for each element. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Energy levels have discrete values that are unique for each element.

    An electron transitions from one energy level to another by absorbing or emitting energy that is equal to the energy difference between the two energy levels.

    This energy difference corresponds to a certain wavelength of a photon.
    The wavelengths emitted or absorbed are unique for each element.

    This means that we can identify an element from its absorption or emission spectrum.
