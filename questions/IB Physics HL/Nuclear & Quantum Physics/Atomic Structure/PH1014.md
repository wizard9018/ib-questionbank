# PH1014

**Maximum mark: 8**

1.  An electron in a hydrogen atom moves from energy level $n$ to the ground state and a photon with energy approximately 12.09 eV is emitted.

    1. Show that $n = 3$. [3 marks]

    2. Determine the wavelength of the emitted photon. [1 mark]

2. Calculate the angular momentum of this electron at its excited state. [2 marks]

3. Bohr assumed that an electron is in an orbit due to the electrostatic force between the electron and the proton. Use this assumption to calculate the orbital radius of the electron at $n = 3$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. $E_1=-13.6 \ll\text{eV}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $E_n=12.09+(-13.6)=-1.51\ll\text{eV}\gg$ $\checkmark$

        $n=\sqrt{\dfrac{-13.6}{-1.51}}=$ :answer[3] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        According to Bohr's model of hydrogen atom, the energy in an energy level is given by

        - $E=-\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$

        The energy of the ground state is

        - $E_1=-13.6\, \text{eV}$

        The energy of the emitted photon equals the difference in energy between the level $n$ and the ground state

        - $E_{Photon}=E_n-E_1$

        - $E_n=12.09+(-13.6)=-1.51\,\text{eV}$

        Then

        - $E_n=\dfrac{-13.6}{n^2}$

        - $n=\sqrt{\dfrac{-13.6}{-1.51}}=3$

    2. $\lambda=\dfrac{hc}{E}=$ :answer[$1.03\times 10^{-7}\,  \text{m}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Using the data booklet value of

        - $hc = 1.24 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{eV}\,\text{m}$

        wavelength can then be determined

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{hc}{E}=\dfrac{1.24 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{eV}\,\text{m}}{12.09\,\text{eV}} = 1.03 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$

2. $L=\dfrac{(3)(6.63\times 10^{-34})}{2\pi}$   $\checkmark$

    $L=$ :answer[$3.17\times 10^{-34}\,\ll\text{J}\,\text{s}\gg$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The angular momentum is given by

    - $L=mvr$

    The angular momentum is quantized according to Bohr's model

    - $mvr=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

    Then

    - $L=\dfrac{(3)(6.63\times 10^{-34})}{2\pi}=3.17\times 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$

3. $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}=k \dfrac{e^2}{r^2}$

    $\ll r= \dfrac{n^2 h^2}{4\pi^2m_e ke^2}\gg$

    $r= \dfrac{(3)^2 (6.63 \times 10^{-34})^2}{4\pi^2 (9.11 \times 10^{-31}) (8.99 \times 10^9) (1.60 \times 10^{-9})^2}$ $\checkmark$

    **OR**

    - $r=\dfrac{L^2}{m_e ke^2}$

    - $r=\dfrac{(3.17 \times 10^{-34})^2}{(9.11 \times 10^{-31})(8.99 \times 10^9)(1.60 \times 10^{-19})^2}$ $\checkmark$

    - $r=$ :answer[$4.78 \times 10^{-10}  \ll \text{m}\gg $] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The electrostatic force is the centripetal force

    - $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}=\dfrac{ke^2}{r^2}$

    - $r=\dfrac{m_ev^2r^2}{ke^2}$

    - $r=\dfrac{(m_evr)^2}{m_eke^2}$

    Since

    - $mvr=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

    Then

    - $r=\dfrac{(\frac{nh}{2\pi})^2}{m_eke^2}$

    - $r=\dfrac{n^2h^2}{4\pi^2m_eke^2}$

    - $r=\dfrac{(3)^2(6.63\times 10^{-34})^2}{4\pi^2(9.11\times 10^{-31})(8.99\times 10^9)(1.60\times 10^{-19})^2}=4.78\times 10^{-10}\,\text{m}$

    **OR**

    - $r=\dfrac{(m_evr)^2}{m_eke^2}=\dfrac{L^2}{m_eke^2}$

    - $r=\dfrac{(3.17 \times 10^{-34})^2}{(9.11\times 10^{-31})(8.99\times 10^9)(1.60\times 10^{-19})^2}=4.78\times 10^{-10}\,\text{m}$
