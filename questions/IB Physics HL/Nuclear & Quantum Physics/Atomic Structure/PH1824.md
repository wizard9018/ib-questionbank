# PH1824

Rutherford’s gold foil experiment involves firing alpha particles at a thin sheet of gold. For high alpha particle energies, the number of alpha particles detected at a given scattering angle deviated from the expected trend as shown in the graph below.

![Screenshot 2026-01-05 at 11.03.51 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-05 at 11.03.51 AM.png>)

Which of the following explains this deviation?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ The repulsive strong nuclear force is more significant at short distances.

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ The attractive strong nuclear force is more significant at short distances.

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ The repulsive electrostatic force is more significant at short distances.

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The attractive electrostatic force is more significant at short distances.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

In Rutherford's gold foil experiment, alpha particles are fired at a thin sheet of gold. At low to moderate energies, the observed scattering is well explained by the Coulomb (electromagnetic) force, since the positively charged alpha particles are repelled by the positively charged gold nucleus.

At higher energies, the number of backscattered alpha particles drops towards zero because they get close enough to the gold nucleus to experience the attractive strong nuclear force. Since the strong force is short-range and attractive, it pulls the alpha particles into the nucleus instead of scattering them back.

Note that although the repulsive electrostatic force also grows stronger at short distances, this is not the reason for the deviation from the expected trend.
