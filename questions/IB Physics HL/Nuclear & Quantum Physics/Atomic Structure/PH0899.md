# PH0899

A proton is moving with kinetic energy $E_k$ towards a distant gold ($\ce{^{197}_{79}Au}$) nucleus. Given that the proton is on a path that would lead to a head-on collision, which of the following expressions correctly describes the distance of closest approach between the proton and the nucleus?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\sqrt{\dfrac{79ke^2}{E_k}}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\sqrt{\dfrac{197ke^2}{E_k}}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{79ke^2}{E_k}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $\dfrac{197ke^2}{E_k}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Given that the proton is initially distant from the nucleus, the initial potential energy can be considered as zero.

As the proton moves towards the nucleus, it will be slowed down due to the electrical repulsion between the positively charged proton and the nucleus.

Eventually, the proton will reach a point of closest approach where all its kinetic energy would have been converted into potential energy and it will be instantaneously at rest.

- $E_{k}$(initial)$=E_p$ (at the point of closest approach)

- $E_k=\dfrac{kq_1q_2}{r}$

- $E_k=\dfrac{k(e)(79e)}{r}$

Where $e$ is the charge of the proton, $79e$ is the charge of the nucleus and $r$ is the distance of closest approach.

Solving the previous equation for $r$ leads to:

- $r=\dfrac{79ke^2}{E_k}$
