# PH1904

An alpha particle is emitted from a radioactive source and directed on a head-on collision course towards the nucleus of an atom with atomic number $Z$.

If the kinetic energy of the alpha particle is $E_k$​, a classical electrostatic calculation gives the distance of closest approach as:

$r=\dfrac{2kZe^2}{E_k}$

Consider the following statements

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The nucleus is treated as a point charge. |
  | II. | The nucleus is treated as an immobile point mass (it does not recoil). |
  | III. | A short-range attractive nuclear force acts between the $\alpha$-particle and the nucleus. |

Which of the statements are underlying assumptions in the derivation of this expression?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ II and III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The derivation of the expression for the distance of closest approach

- $r=\dfrac{2kZe^2}{E_k}$

assumes that all the kinetic energy of the alpha particle is converted into electrostatic potential energy. The nucleus is also treated as a fixed (infinitely massive) point charge, meaning it does not recoil, and no kinetic energy is transferred to it.

The short-ranged attractive force (strong nuclear force) is ignored in the derivation given that the interaction is assumed to be purely electrostatic.
