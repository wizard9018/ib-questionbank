# PH0901

Deviations from the Rutherford model were observed for highly energetic alpha particles. These deviations could not be explained by an electric repulsion based on Coulomb's law.
Which of the following correctly describes a conclusion that was drawn from these deviations?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ The atom consists of a very small nucleus and orbiting electrons.

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ A short-range and attractive nuclear force exists.

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ Nuclear decays have discrete energy emissions.

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ Electrons orbit the nucleus in discrete energy levels.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Rutherford's experiment on the scattering of alpha particles through a thin sheet of gold could be explained using Coulomb's law for the repulsion between alpha particles and the nuclei.

However, for highly energetic alpha particles deviations from the predicted behaviour were observed. This observation led to hypothesize the existence of an attractive nuclear force that was only experienced by the alpha particles with greater energies.

This force had to be short-range which would explain why only the particles that could get close enough to the nucleus (the most energetic ones) would experience its effects.
