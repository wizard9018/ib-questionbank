# PH0896

A beam of alpha particles is aimed towards a thin sheet of gold and a detector is placed behind the foil at an angle of $60\degree$ to the original path of the particles. The relative intensity of alpha particles deviates from that predicted by Coulomb's law for the highly energetic particles.

Which of the following correctly describes the relative intensity of the observed alpha particles with high energies and the conclusion to which this led?

$\\$

| | $\hspace{1.5em}$Relative Intensity$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1.5em}$Conclusion$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$A.$\hspace{1.5em}$| Less than predicted |$\hspace{1.5em}$An attractive short-range force was also acting$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
|$\hspace{1.5em}$B.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | More than predicted | $\hspace{1.5em}$An attractive short-range force was also acting$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$C.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | Less than predicted | $\hspace{1.5em}$A repulsive short-range force was also acting$\hspace{1.5em}$ |
| $\hspace{1.5em}$D.$\hspace{1.5em}$ | More than predicted | $\hspace{1.5em}$A repulsive short-range force was also acting$\hspace{1.5em}$ |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Rutherford's observations on the scattering of alpha particles through a thin sheet of gold could be modelled using Coulomb's law.

For different scattering angles, the observed relative intensity of alpha particles could be predicted and explained in terms of the electric repulsion between the particles and the nuclei. As the alpha particles pass very close to the nucleus, the electric repulsion increases in magnitude deviating the particles from their original path. Those alpha particles with a 60$\degree$ diffraction angle will have passed quite close to the nucleus to have scattered at such a relatively large angle.

However, as the kinetic energy of the alpha particles was increased, deviations from this predicted behaviour were observed. The relative intensity was less than predicted by Rutherford's model for the most energetic alpha particles. Thus, the alpha particles that were previously deflected by 60$\degree$ will experience a smaller overall repulsive force and deflect by a smaller angle. This will decrease the number of alpha particles detected at this angle for high energy particles.

This deviation led scientists to hypothesize the existence of a short-range attractive force that would balance the repulsive electric force but would only be experienced by the most energetic alpha particles. These would be the ones that would get closer to the nucleus.
