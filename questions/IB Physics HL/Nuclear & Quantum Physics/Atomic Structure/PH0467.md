    1. Downward arrow. $\checkmark$ \

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

        To have the shortest wavelength this means the highest energy photon.

        - $\lambda=\dfrac{hc}{E}$

        To emit such a photon, the electron will go from the last energy level to the ground state.

    2. ::indent

        - $E=\ll -0.38-(-13.60)\gg$

        - $\lambda=\ll \dfrac{hc}{E}\gg$

        The energy difference from $n=6$ to $n=1$

        - $E=-0.38-(-13.60)=13.22$ eV

        To calculate the shortest wavelength

        Remember to convert eV to joules.

        - $\lambda =\dfrac{(6.63 \,\times\, 10^{-34})(3 \,\times \,10^8)}{(13.22)(1.6\, \times\, 10^{-19})} = 9.40 \times 10^{-8}$ m

3. ::indent

    - $\ll E= hf\gg$

    - $E=\dfrac{(6.63\, \times\, 10^{-34})(460 \,\times \,10^{12})}{(1.6\, \times\, 10^{-19})}$ $\checkmark$

    - $E=1.9\ll \text{eV}\gg$ $\checkmark$

    - From $n=3$ to $n=2$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \

    - $\begin{aligned}

    &=3.05 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}\\

    &=1.9\,\text{eV}
