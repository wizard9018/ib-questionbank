# PH1886

In the Geiger-Marsden-Rutherford experiment alpha particles were directed at a thin gold foil to investigate atomic structure.

Below are three statements about the experiment.

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | Most of the alpha particles passed straight through the foil because the atom is mostly empty space. |
  | II. | Most of the alpha particles that did not pass through the foil were absorbed by the gold atoms. |
  | III. | Some of the alpha particles were deflected as they were repelled by the electrons in the gold atoms. |

Which of the statements is correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ I and II only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

**Statement I is correct.**
In the Geiger-Marsden-Rutherford experiment, most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil, which led to the conclusion that atoms are mostly empty space.

**Statement II is incorrect.**
Alpha particles are massive and carry a positive charge, and they were not absorbed by the gold atoms. Instead, the few that didn't pass through were scattered, often at large angles due to repulsion from the dense, positively charged nucleus.

**Statement III is incorrect.**
Deflections were caused by the strong electrostatic repulsion from the nucleus, not the electrons.
