# PH1903

The table below shows the lowest four energy levels for the electrons around an atom.

$\begin{array}{c|c}
\textrm{Level} & \textrm{Energy / eV} \\
\hline
1 & -10 \\
\hline
2 & -4.0 \\
\hline
3 & -2.8 \\
\hline
4 & -1.6 \\
\end{array}$

The energy of a violet photon is approximately 3.1 eV.

Given that the wavelength of a red photon is about twice the wavelength of a violet photon, which of the following electronic transitions of an electron in the atom shown would result in the emission of a visible photon?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $4\rightarrow3$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $4\rightarrow2$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $3\rightarrow2$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $2\rightarrow1$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

It is given that the wavelength of a red photon is about twice that of a violet one. This means that its frequency (and thus its energy) must be half that of a violet photon.

Therefore, visible photons will have energies ranging between 1.5 eV (red) and 3.1 eV (violet).

The only transition that falls between these values is the one between the energy levels 4 to 2.

- $E_{4\rightarrow2}=E_2-E_4=(-4.0\textrm{ eV})-(-1.6\textrm{ eV})=-2.4$ eV

When an electron transitions between the 4$^\textrm{th}$ energy level to the 2$^\textrm{nd}$ energy level, a photon with 2.4 eV will be emitted.

This photon will be a visible photon.

No other transitions shown fall within the energy range for visible light.
