## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. The energy of a photon is related to its wavelength $\checkmark$

        Emitted wavelength is quantized/discrete $\checkmark$

        Photon energies correspond to differences in electron energy levels (therefore electron energy levels are discrete) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Photons are emitted when electrons transition between energy levels. The wavelength of the emitted photon is directly related to its energy. Because the emitted wavelengths are quantized, the energy of a photon is also quantized. Therefore, there are distinct energy differences between energy levels.

    2. The energy difference should be smallest

        656 nm is the longest wavelength that gives lowest energy difference

        - $\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3.00\times 10^8)}{656\times 10^{-9}}=3.03\times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The energy difference between the excited states corresponds to the energy of the emitted photon when electrons transition between those states. Energy $E$ of the electron:

        - $E=hf=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

        The photon possesses its minimum energy when the wavelength is at its maximum. Specifically, the energy difference between the 3rd  and the 2nd energy states should be the smallest among all other transitions from various excited states to the 2nd state. Consequently, a photon with a wavelength of 656 nm is expected to be emitted during this particular transition.

        - $E=\dfrac{hc}{\lambda}=\dfrac{(6.63\times 10^{-34})(3.00\times 10^8)}{656\times 10^{-9}}=3.03\times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$

2. 1. shift to the right $\checkmark$

        The greater shift for longer wavelengths $\checkmark$

        ![Screenshot 2026-01-06 at 5.56.24 PM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-06 at 5.56.24 PM.png>)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        Due to the Doppler effect, the observed wavelength of the receding sources is getting longer than the wavelength of the source. Due to the following formula:

        - $\dfrac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}=\dfrac{v}{c}$

        Change in wavelength depends on the wavelength of the source. For the longer wavelengths, change is greater. Therefore, the spectral lines should be shifted to the right with an increasing amount of shift.

    2. $v=\dfrac{c\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}$ $\checkmark$

        $\dfrac{(3.00\times 10^8)(10\times 10^{-9})}{410\times 10^{-9}}=$ :answer[$7.3\times 10^6\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \hspace {1em} \textrm{away from Earth} $] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The change in wavelength:

        - $\Delta \lambda = 420 \times 10^{-9}-410\times 10^{-9}=10\times 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$

        The speed of the galaxy can be found by the following formula:

        - $\dfrac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}=\dfrac{v}{c}$

        - $v=\dfrac{c\Delta \lambda}{\lambda}=\dfrac{(3.00\times 10^8)(10\times 10^{-9})}{410\times 10^{-9}}=7.3\times 10^6 \, \text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

        Because there is an increase in the wavelength, the star must be moving away from the Earth.
