    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    The energy released from the reaction is found by subtracting the mass of the products from the mass of the reactants:

    - $\Delta m=\textrm{mass(reactants)}-\textrm{mass(products)}$

    - $\Delta m=(2.014101+3.016049)-(1.008664+4.002603)=0.018883$ u

    The mass difference is positive i.e. the mass of the reactants is greater than the mass of the products. This mass difference represents the mass than has been converted into energy.

    Since 1 u$=931.5$ MeV, then the energy released is:

    - $E=(0.018883)(931.5)=17.6$ MeV

2. Nuclei repel each other due to being positively charged OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    High temperatures give nuclei greater kinetic energies to overcome repulsion OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    High density will increase frequency of collisions, leading to more fusions OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Due to the deuterium and tritium nuclei both being positively charged, there will be a repulsive electrostatic force between them. To overcome this force and allow the nuclei to fuse, a fusion reactor needs to bring these nuclei to very high speeds under very high pressures for the nuclei to become close enough to experience the strong nuclear force and fuse. High temperatures are used in fusion reactors to supply the nuclei with large amounts of kinetic energy, allowing them to travel at very high speeds and overcome the repulsive force. Since the nuclei need to be in very close proximity to one another to fuse, fusion reactors also use very high pressures to create high density, bringing the nuclei closer together and increase the frequency of collisions between the nuclei. This results in a greater number of fusion reactions occurring.

3. Any two of the following

    Confining the plasma is difficult due to the extreme temperatures (millions of degrees) required for fusion and the need for strong magnetic fields OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    Current reactors require more energy input than they output, making the reactors inefficient OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    Tritium as a fuel source is rare and difficult to produce OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Confining and controlling the plasma that exists within tokamak fusion reactors is very difficult for several reasons.

    To sustain the plasma and allow fusion to occur, the plasma must be heated to very high temperatures (approximately 150 million degrees Celsius).

    The plasma must also be confined and shaped to prevent the plasma making contact with the vessel walls, which would damage the fusion reactor and cool the plasma down. Today's current fusion reactors require more energy input than they output, and thus, they are inefficient. Fusion reactors of the future, such as ITER, will produce more energy that is required to be put in, making them efficient and suitable for power production.

    One of the main fuels used in fusion reactors is radioactive tritium. Tritium is rare (approximately only 10 kg exists on Earth at any given time), and so it must be created to supply future fusion reactors. One of the methods for this involves absorbing the neutrons produced from fusion via a lithium blanket surrounding the reactor. When lithium is combined with a neutron, tritium is produced which can be used as fuel.
