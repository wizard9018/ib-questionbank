# PH1851

**Maximum mark: 9**

1. 1. Alpha Centauri A is a main sequence star with radius $8.5\times10^8$ m and surface temperature of 5790 K. Calculate the luminosity of Alpha Centauri A. [2 marks]

    2. A planet at a distance of 0.9 AU from Alpha Centauri A has an albedo of 0.3. Calculate the average intensity of radiation from Alpha Centauri A absorbed by the surface of the planet. [3 marks]

2. Alpha Centauri A maintains a constant radius because it is in equilibrium. Explain how this equilibrium occurs. [2 marks]

3. Star X has the same luminosity as Alpha Centauri A and it is located to the right of Alpha Centauri A on a Hertzsprung-Russell diagram. Deduce how the radius of Star X compares to that of Alpha Centauri A. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. $\hspace{1.9em}$ $L=(5.67\times10^{-8})(4)(\pi)(8.5\times10^8)^2(5790)^4$ $\checkmark$

        - $L=$ :answer[$5.8\times10^{26}$ $\ll\textrm{W}\gg$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The luminosity is given by

        - $L=\sigma AT^4$

        The surface area of a sphere can be calculated from the formula

        - $A=4\pi r^2$

        Substituting this value and all formulae gives

        - $L=(5.67\times10^{-8})(4)(\pi)(8.5\times10^8)^2(5790)^4=5.8\times10^{26}$ W

    2. $\hspace{1.9em}$ $b=\dfrac{L}{4\pi r^2}=\dfrac{5.8\times10^{26}}{4\pi(0.9(1.5\times10^{11}))^2}=2526$ W m$^{-2}$ $\checkmark$

        - $b_\textrm{avg}=\dfrac{2526}{4}=632$ W m$^{-2}$ $\checkmark$

        - $I_\textrm{abs}=(1-0.3)(632)=$ :answer[$442$ W m$^{-2}$] $\checkmark$

        Allow ECF from (a)(i)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        First we can find the intensity of the light from Alpha Centauri A at a distance of 0.9 AU, where the value for 1 AU can be found in the data booklet.

        - $b=\dfrac{L}{4\pi r^2}=\dfrac{5.8\times10^{26}}{4\pi(0.9(1.5\times10^{11}))^2}=2526$ W m$^{-2}$

        The average intensity over the entire surface of the planet is given by

        - $b_\textrm{avg}=\dfrac{b}{4}=\dfrac{2526}{4}=632$ W m$^{-2}$

        The albedo gives the fraction of the intensity that is reflected, so the fraction absorbed can be found from

        - Fraction absorbed $=(1-\textrm{albedo})=1-0.3=0.7$

        Therefore the absorbed intensity is

        - $I_\textrm{abs}=(1-0.3)(632)=442$ W m$^{-2}$

2. Outward radiation/thermal pressure/force due to fusion reactions $\checkmark$

    is balanced by inward gravitational pressure/force. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    There is an inward gravitational force due to the mass of the star and an outward force due to the pressure of radiation/thermal energy released from the fusion reactions taking place in the core. In a main sequence star, these forces/pressures balance each other out an an equilibrium is maintained.

3. $T_X<T_A$ / Temperature of Star X is lower

    AND

    $\dfrac{R_X}{R_A}=\dfrac{(T_A)^2}{(T_X)^2}$ / radius is inversely proportional to the square of temperature for the same luminosity $\checkmark$

    Bigger radius $\checkmark$

    **OR**

    Lines of constant radius slope upwards to the left   $\checkmark$

    X lies on a line of larger radius $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
    Since Star X is to the right of Alpha Centauri A, then it has a lower surface temperature.

    Since the luminosity is given by

    - $L=\sigma AT^4$

    And both stars have the same luminosity, then

    - $\begin{aligned}
    \ L_X&=L_A\\
    \ \\
    \ \sigma A_X(T_X)^4&=\sigma A_A(T_A)^4\\
    \ \\
    \ 4\pi (R_X)^2(T_X)^4&=4\pi (R_A)^2(T_A)^4\\
    \ \\
    \ (R_X)^2(T_X)^4&=(R_A)^2(T_A)^4\\
    \ \\
    \ \dfrac{(R_X)^2}{(R_A)^2}&=\dfrac{(T_A)^4}{(T_X)^4}\\
    \ \\
    \ \dfrac{R_X}{R_A}&=\dfrac{(T_A)^2}{(T_X)^2}\\
    \end{aligned}$

    Since

    - $T_X<T_A$

    Then

    - $R_X>R_A$

    The radius of Star X is bigger than that of Alpha Centauri A.

    Alternatively we can consider an H-R diagram and the lines of constant radius. The lines of constant radius are angled upwards to the left, with the radius increasing as we move upwards on the graph. Thus X lies on a line of greater radius than Y.
