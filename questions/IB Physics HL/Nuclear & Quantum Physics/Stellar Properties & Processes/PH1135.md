# PH1135

In the second stage of the proton-proton cycle a nucleus of hydrogen fuses with deuterium to form $\ce{^{3}_{2}He}$. The equation below describes this process:

$\ce{^{1}_{1}H} + \ce{^{2}_{1}H \rightarrow \ce{^{3}_{2}He^} + \gamma}$

It is given that the binding energy per nucleon for $\ce{^{2}_{1}H}$ is $1.112$ MeV and for $\ce{^{3}_{2}He}$ is $2.573\,$MeV.

Which of the following best describes the energy released in the reaction?

- A. $\hspace{1em}  $ $1.461\,$MeV

- B. $\hspace{1em}  $ $2.573\,$MeV

- C. $\hspace{1em}  $ $5.495\,$MeV

- D. $\hspace{1em}  $ $7.719\,$MeV

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To split the reactants into its constituent parts, the energy required is given by:

- $2(1.112\,\ce{MeV})=2.224\,\ce{MeV}$

Then, when $\ce{^{3}_{2}He}$ is formed the energy released can be calculated as follows:

- $3(2.573\,\ce{MeV})=7.719\,\ce{MeV}$

The energy released is then:

- $7.719\,\ce{MeV}-2.224\,\ce{MeV}=5.495\,\ce{MeV}$
