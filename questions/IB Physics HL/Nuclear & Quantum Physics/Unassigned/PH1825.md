# PH1825

Two identically charged particles move directly towards each other. When the particles are separated by a distance $d$, the magnitude of the kinetic energy of each particle is equal to the magnitude of the electric potential energy of the system.

![Screenshot 2026-01-05 at 11.06.14 AM](<images/Screenshot 2026-01-05 at 11.06.14 AM.png>)

What is the distance of closest approach of the particles?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{d}{3}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{d}{2}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{2d}{3}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ $\dfrac{3d}{4}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The total energy of the system when the separation is $d$ is equal to the sum of the kinetic and potential energies of the particles given by:

- $E_{total}=KE_{particle\,1}+KE_{particle\,2}+E_P$

For the electrical potential energy, we know that

- $E_P=\dfrac{kq^2}{d}$

Where $q$ is the charge of the particles. From the information given in the question, the magnitude of each of the kinetic energies is also $\dfrac{kq^2}{d}$ so we have:

- $E_{total}=3\dfrac{kq^2}{d}$

Energy is conserved so we know that that total energy at the distance of closest approach is $3\dfrac{kq^2}{d}$. At the distance of closest approach, the kinetic energy of the particles is zero, the energy of the system is purely potential, so the distance of closest approach $D$ is given by:

- $3\dfrac{kq^2}{d}=\dfrac{kq^2}{D}$

- $\dfrac{3}{d}=\dfrac{1}{D}$

- $3=\dfrac{d}{D}$

- $3D=d$

- $D=\dfrac{d}{3}$
