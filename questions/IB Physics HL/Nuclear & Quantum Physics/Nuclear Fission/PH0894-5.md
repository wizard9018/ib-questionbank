# PH0894-5

**Maximum mark: 12**

In the nuclear reactor, the fission reaction is represented by:

$\hspace{8em}$ $\ce{^{1}_{0}n}+\ce{^{235}_{92}U} \to \ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}$ +$\ce{^{141}_{56}Ba}+3\ce{^{1}_{0}n}$

The following data is available for the reaction:

- Binding energy per nucleon of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U} =\ 7.59$ MeV
- Binding energy per nucleon of $\ce{^{141}_{56}Ba} =\ 8.33$ MeV
- Binding energy per nucleon of $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr} =\  8.51$ MeV

1. 1. Define binding energy. [1 mark]

    2. Show that energy released in each reaction is around 170 MeV. [3 marks]

    3. Molar mass of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$ is around 235 g mol$^{-1}$. Determine the energy released from 1.0 kg of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$. [3 marks]

    4. Explain the purpose of control rods in a nuclear reactor. [2 marks]

2. The half-life of $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}$ is 3.0 s. Determine the time it takes for the activity of a $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}$ sample to decrease to 10\% of its initial activity. [3 marks]

    2. The binding energy of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U} = (235)(7.59) = 1784$ MeV

        The binding energy of $\ce{^{141}_{56}Ba}= (141)(8.33) = 1174$ MeV

        The binding energy of $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}= (92)(8.51) = 783$ MeV   $\checkmark$

        $(1174 + 783) - 1784$ $\checkmark$

        = :answer[$173 \text{MeV}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        The binding energy of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}= (235)(7.59) = 1784$ MeV

        The binding energy of $\ce{^{141}_{56}Ba}= (141)(8.33) = 1174$ MeV

        The binding energy of $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}= (92)(8.51) = 783$ MeV

        The released energy can be found by calculating the difference in binding energies between reactants and products.

        The energy $\Delta E$ released:

        - $\Delta E = (1174 + 783) - 1784 = 173$ MeV

    3. $\dfrac{1.0}{235 \times 10^{-3}} = 4.25$ mol $\checkmark$

        $(4.25)(6.02 \times 10^{23}) = 2.56 \times 10^{24}$ $\checkmark$

        $(2.56 \times 10^{24})(170 \times 10^{6})(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) $= :answer[$6.96 \times 10^{13} \text{J}$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        The molar mass of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$ is 235 g mol$^{-1}$

        Therefore, to find the number of moles in 1 kg:

        - $\dfrac{1.0}{235 \times 10^{-3}} = 4.25$ mol

        The number of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$ atoms in one mole is $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ and the number of the $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$ atoms in 4.25 mol:

        - $(4.25)(6.02 \times 10^{23}) = 2.56 \times 10^{24}$

        The energy $E$ released, in J,  from 1 kg of $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$:

        - $E = (2.56 \times 10^{24})(170 \times 10^{6})(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) = 6.96 \times 10^{13}$ J

    4. Control rods absorb neutrons $\checkmark$ \
        To control reaction rate $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        Nuclear fission reactions produce two or three neutrons per neutron consumed. These extra neutrons can trigger further fission reactions, creating a chain reaction that releases a tremendous amount of energy. If this chain reaction is not controlled, it can lead to a meltdown or explosion.

        To prevent this, control rods are used to absorb excess neutrons. By inserting or withdrawing the control rods, the rate of fission reactions can be controlled, and the energy released can be kept at a safe level.

2.  Decay constant $= \dfrac{\ln{2}}{t_{1/2}} = \dfrac{\ln{2}}{3.0} =0.23\ \text{s}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    $\dfrac{1}{10} = e^{-(0.23)t}$ $\checkmark$

    $\dfrac{\ln{10}}{0.23} =$ :answer[$10 \text{s}$] $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    The decay constant $\lambda$ of the $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}$:

    - $\lambda = \dfrac{\ln{2}}{t_{1/2}} = \dfrac{\ln{2}}{3.0} = 0.23 \text{s}^{-1}$

    By using the activity formula:

    - $A = A_{0}e^{- \lambda t}$

    - $\dfrac{A_{0}}{10} = A_{0}e^{-(0.23)t}$

    - $\dfrac{1}{e^{-(0.23)t}} = \dfrac{10A_0}{A_{0}}$

    - $e^{0.23t} = 10$

    - $\ln(e^{0.23t}) = \ln{10}$

    - $0.23t = \ln{10}$

    - $t = \dfrac{\ln{10}}{0.23} = 10$ s
