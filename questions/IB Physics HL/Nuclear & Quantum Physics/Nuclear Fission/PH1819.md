# PH1819

What is the primary role of the moderator in a uranium-235 nuclear reactor?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ To absorb excess neutrons

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ To protect workers from radiation

- C. $\hspace{1em}$ To transfer heat away from the reactor core

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ To slow down neutrons to sustain the chain reaction

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When uranium-235 undergoes fission, it releases fast-moving neutrons. However, these neutrons have too much kinetic energy to efficiently cause further fission in other uranium-235 nuclei.

The moderator is a material (such as water, heavy water, or graphite) that slows down these fast neutrons through elastic collisions with its atoms. Slower neutrons (called thermal neutrons) have a much higher probability of being absorbed by uranium-235, allowing the chain reaction to continue at a controlled rate.

A is incorrect. This is the role of control rods, which absorb neutrons to regulate the reaction.

B is incorrect. Radiation shielding (such as thick concrete and lead layers) provides protection, not the moderator.

C is incorrect. This is the job of the coolant, such as water or liquid sodium.
