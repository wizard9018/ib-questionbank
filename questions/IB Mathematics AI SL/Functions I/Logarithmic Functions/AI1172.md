# AI1172

**Maximum mark: 8**

The Mohs scale of mineral hardness is a qualitative, discrete scale characterizing the scratch resistance of various minerals through the ability of harder materials to scratch softer ones. It ranges from talc (1), the softest mineral, to diamond (10), the hardest.  Minerals are assigned a whole number value on the Mohs scale.

Absolute hardness of a mineral quantifies its resistance to scratching on a continuous scale, providing a more precise measurement than the Mohs scale. 

The relationship between Mohs scale score, $M$, and absolute hardness, $H$, is given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

0.319M &= \log_{10}H-0.117

\end{aligned}

$$

1. Calculate the Mohs scale score for quartz, which has an absolute hardness of 100. Give your answer to the nearest whole number.     [2 marks]

2. Find $H$ as a function of $M$.[2 marks]

3. Calculate the absolute hardness of fluorite, which has a Mohs scale score of 4.[2 marks]

4. Hence determine how many times greater the absolute hardness of quartz is than the absolute hardness of fluorite.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. For quartz, we have $H=100$. If we substitute this value into the Mohs scale equation, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    0.319M &= \log_{10} (100) - 0.117 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    M &= \dfrac{1.883}{0.319} \\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-1.4em}\boxed{M = 6 \text{ [Nearest whole number]}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    0.319M &= \log_{10}H-0.117 \\[6pt]

    0.319M + 0.117 &= \log_{10}H

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now use the log equivalence statement, $a^x=b \Leftrightarrow x = \log_a b$, with $a=10$, $b=H$ and $x = 0.319M + 0.117$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\boxed{H = 10^{0.319M + 0.117}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. For fluorite, we have $M=4$. If we substitute this value into the expression we formed in part (b), we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    H &= 10^{0.319\times 4 + 0.117} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

    &= 24.71...\\[6pt]

    &\hspace{-1.25em}\boxed{H = 24.7\,(3\,\mathrm{s.f.})} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. We have 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\text{Hardness of quartz}}{\text{Hardness of fluorite}} &= \dfrac{100}{24.71...} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= 4.045...\\[6pt]

    &= 4.05\,(3\,\mathrm{s.f.})

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Quartz is approximately 4.05 times harder than fluorite.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
