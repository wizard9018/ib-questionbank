# AI1124

**Maximum mark: 7**

The brightness of a star can be measured in terms of its _apparent magnitude_, $M$, as seen from Earth.

The amount of energy received on Earth by a star is called the the _observed flux density_, $F$.

$M$ and $F$ are related by the following formula
$$
\frac{M}{2.5} = \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{F}\right)
$$

1. If a star has a value of $F=2500$, find the value of $M$.[2 marks]

2. Find $F$ as a function of $M$.[2 marks]

The apparent magnitude of the sun is known to be $M_s=-26.8$ and the apparent magnitude of the full moon is known to be $M_m=-12.7$.

3. Find their _brightness factor_, $\dfrac{F_s}{F_m}$, to three significant figures in the form $a\times10^k$, where $1<a<10$ and $k\in \mathbb{Z}$.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the numerical solver on the G.D.C.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{M}{2.5} &= \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{2500}\right)\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\
        M&=-8.49485...\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-8.49} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  First we raise both sides of the equation to the power of $10$.

    The R.H.S. is such that $10^{\log_{10}\tfrac{1}{F}}=\frac{1}{F}$.

    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        10^{\tfrac{M}{2.5}}&=\frac{1}{F} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        F&=\hspace{0.525em}10^{-\tfrac{M}{2.5}} \hspace{2em} \text{or } \hspace{2em} \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\frac{1}{10^{\tfrac{M}{2.5}}}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We can use the formula from part (b) to find $F_s=F(-26.8)$ and  $F_m=F(-12.7)$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{F_s}{F_m}&=\frac{F(-26.8)}{F(-12.7)} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{10^{-\tfrac{-26.8}{2.5}}}{10^{-\tfrac{-12.7}{2.5}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Notice the negative signs cancel out.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{5.248...\times10^{10}}{120226.44..} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\
        &=436515.8...\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{4.37 \times 10^5} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
