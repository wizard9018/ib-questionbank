# AI120

**Maximum mark: 17**

The line $L_1$ has equation $2y - x - 10 = 0$ and is shown on the
diagram.

![de606d5be573f475a578c89b32ed5f6e2d4e6f74.svg](images/de606d5be573f475a578c89b32ed5f6e2d4e6f74.svg)

The point A has coordinates $(2,6)$.

1.  Show that A lies on $L_1$. [2 marks]

The point C has coordinates $(8,18)$. M is the midpoint of \[AC\].

2.  Find the coordinates of M. [2 marks]

3.  Find the length of \[AC\]. [2 marks]

The straight line, $L_2$, is perpendicular to \[AC\] and passes through
M.

4.  Show that the equation of $L_2$ is $2y + x - 29 = 0$.
    [5 marks]

The point D is the intersection of $L_1$ and $L_2$.

5.  Find the coordinates of D. [2 marks]

The length of \[MD\] is $\dfrac{9\sqrt{5}}{4}$.

6.  Write down the length of \[MD\] correct to three
    significant figures. [1 mark]

The point B is such that ABCD is a rhombus.

7.  Find the area of ABCD. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting $x = 2$ & $y = 6$ in the left hand side of the equation
    of $L_1$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2(6) - 2 - 10 &= 12 - 2 - 10 \\[6pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point A$(2,6)$ lies on the line
    $L_1$.

2.  We have
    $$

    \hspace{2.8em} \begin{array}{rcccl}
    \dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2} &=& \dfrac{2+8}{2} &=& 5 \\[12pt]
    \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2} &=& \dfrac{6+18}{2} &=& 12 \\[3pt]
    \end{array}
    $$
    Hence the coordinates of M are
     $(5,12)$

3.  Using the distance b/n two points formula with A$(2,6)$ and
    C$(8,18)$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathrm{AC} &= \sqrt{(8-2)^2 + (18-6)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{6^2 + 12^2} \\[9pt]
    &\approx 13.4
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The gradient of line segment AC is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{12em} m_{\mathrm{AC}} &= \dfrac{18-6}{8-2} \hspace{3em} [\text{by gradient formula}] \\[8pt]
    &= 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the gradient
    of $L_2$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.7em} m_{L_2} &= -\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathrm{AC}}} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $L_2 \perp L_1$}] \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the point-gradient formula with M$(5,12)$ and
    $m = -1/2$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[6pt]
    y - 12 &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\hspace{0.05em}(x-5) \\[6pt]
    \hspace{0.3em}  2y - 24 &= -x + 5 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    2y + x - 29 = 0 \hspace{5.6em}
    $$

5.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    L_1&:\hspace{1.0em} 2y - x = 10  \\[6pt]
    L_2&:\hspace{1.0em} 2y + x = 29
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving
    the system of linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$ for $x$ and $y$, we
    obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}

    \hspace{11.3em} \begin{array}{rcl}
    x &=& 9.5 \\[6pt]
    y &=& 9.75 \\[2pt]
    \end{array} \hspace{3.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the coordinates of D are
     $(9.5,9.75)$

6.
    $\mathrm{MD} \approx 5.03$

7.  If we sketch the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$, and the points A, B, C, D
    and M, we have

    ![77fb9723b552ee7da7594cc46fb5967dcf76c511.svg](images/77fb9723b552ee7da7594cc46fb5967dcf76c511.svg)

    Using the area of a rhombus formula, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{11.8em} A &= \dfrac{1}{2}[\mathrm{AC}\times\mathrm{BD}] \\[6pt]
    &\approx \dfrac{1}{2}[13.4][2(5.03)] \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\mathrm{BD} = 2\mathrm{MD}$}] \\[12pt]
    &\approx 67.4 \text{ unit}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
