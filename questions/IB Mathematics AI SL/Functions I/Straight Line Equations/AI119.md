# AI119

**Maximum mark: 15**

The equation of the line $L_1$ is $3y - x - 5 = 0$. The line $L_1$ is
shown on the diagram below.

![f4ed7a05bbd56bbe84b8725c56c6d6ec6699d794.svg](images/f4ed7a05bbd56bbe84b8725c56c6d6ec6699d794.svg)

The point K has coordinates $(4,3)$. The point T has coordinates
$(2,-3)$. The point M is the midpoint of \[TK\].

1.  Calculate the coordinates of the point M. [2 marks]

2.  Show that the point K lies on the line $L_1$. [2 marks]

3.  Calculate the length of \[TK\]. [2 marks]

The line $L_2$ passes through the point M and is perpendicular to $L_1$.

4.  Find the gradient of the line $L_2$. [2 marks]

5.  Find the equation of $L_2$. Give your answer in the form
    $ax + by + d = 0$, where $a, b$ and $d$ are integers. [4 marks]

The point N is the intersection of $L_1$ and $L_2$.

6.  Calculate the coordinates of N. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$

    \begin{array}{rcccl}
    \dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2} &=& \dfrac{4+2}{2} &=& 3 \\[12pt]
    \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2} &=& \dfrac{3+(-3)}{2} &=& 0
    \end{array} \hspace{3em}
    $$
    Hence the coordinates of the midpoint of
    the line segment \[TK\] are
     $(3,0)$

2.  Substituting $x = 4$ & $y = 3$ in the left hand side of the equation
    of $L_1$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3(3) - 4 - 5 &= 9 - 4 - 5 \\[6pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point K lies on the line $L_1$.

3.  Using the distance b/n two points formula with T$(2,-3)$ and
    K$(4,3)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.6em} \mathrm{TK} &= \sqrt{(4-2)^2 + (3-(-3))^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{2^2 + 6^2} \\[9pt]
    &\approx 6.32
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  If we rewrite the equation of $L_1$ in the form $y = mx + c$, we
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3y - x - 5 &= 0 \hspace{5.2em} \\[12pt]
    3y &= x + 5 \\[6pt]
    y &= \dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{5}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the gradient
    of $L_1$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m_{L_1} &= \dfrac{1}{3} \hspace{2.3em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore the
    gradient of $L_2$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.5em} m_{L_2} &= -\dfrac{1}{m_{L_1}} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $L_2 \perp L_1$}] \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{1/3} \\[12pt]
    &= -3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using the point-gradient formula with M$(3,0)$ and $m = -3$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[6pt]
    y - 0 &= -3\hspace{0.05em}(x-3) \\[6pt]
    \hspace{2.8em} y &= -3x + 9 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    3x + y - 9 = 0 \hspace{5.2em}
    $$

6.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    L_1&:\hspace{1.0em} 3y - x = 5 & \\[6pt]
    L_2&:\hspace{1.0em} 3x + y = 9 &\hspace{2em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving the
    system of linear equations $[1]-[2]$ for $x$ and $y$, we obtain

     $$
     \begin{aligned}

     \hspace{11em} \begin{array}{rcl}
     x &=& 2.2 \\[6pt]
     y &=& 2.4
     \end{array} \hspace{4em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
     \end{aligned}
     $$

    Hence the coordinates of N are
     $(2.2,2.4)$
