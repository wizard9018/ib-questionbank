# CH2094

What happens when magnesium reacts with chlorine?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Magnesium | Chlorine |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | One magnesium atom loses two electrons | One chlorine atom gains two electrons |
    | B. | Two magnesium atoms lose two electrons each | One chlorine atom gains one electron |
    | C. | One magnesium atom loses two electrons |Two chlorine atoms gain one electron each |
    | D. | Two magnesium atoms lose two electrons each | Two chlorine atoms gain one electron each |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Magnesium and chlorine will react to form an ionic compound: the metal will lose electrons to form a cation isoelectronic with the nearest noble gas and the nonmetal will gain electrons to form an anion isoelectronic with the nearest noble gas.

Magnesium has an electron configuration of $1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^6\ 3s^2.$ When it loses two electrons to form $\ce{Mg^2+}$ it gets the electronic configuration of neon, $1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^6.$

Chlorine has an electron configuration of $1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^6\ 3s^2\ 3p^5.$ When it gains an electron to form $\ce{Cl-}$ it gets the electronic configuration of argon, $1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^6\ 3s^2\ 3p^6.$

Since one Mg atom loses two electrons, but each Cl atom can only accept one electron, two chlorine atoms are needed to balance the charge. The overall reaction is:

$\ce{Mg + Cl2 -> MgCl2}$
