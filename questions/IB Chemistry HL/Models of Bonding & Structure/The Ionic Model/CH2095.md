# CH2095

Which element is not likely to form ions during a chemical reaction with sulfur?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Chlorine

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Yttrium

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Strontium

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Sodium

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Sulfur is a nonmetal from Group 16. It has six valence electrons and typically gains two electrons to form $\ce{S^2-}$ ions when reacting with metals. Furthermore, it generally forms ionic bonds with metals (Y, Sr, Na) and covalent bonds with nonmetals (N, O, Cl).

Chlorine is a nonmetal, just like sulfur. Instead of forming an ionic bond, chlorine, and sulfur would likely form a covalent bond in compounds like $\ce{SCl2}.$ By looking at the electronegativity difference and average of the $\ce{S-Cl}$ bond, we get:

| Electronegativity difference | | Electronegativity average |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $3.2 - 2.6 = 0.6$ | $\hspace{1em}$ | $\dfrac{3.2 + 2.6}{2} = 2.9$ |

This value, when referenced on the Van Arkel$\text{--}$Ketelaar triangle, indicates that $\ce{SCl2}$ is a polar covalent compound. This means that the bonding electrons are shared between the atoms but with unequal distribution due to the difference in electronegativity.
