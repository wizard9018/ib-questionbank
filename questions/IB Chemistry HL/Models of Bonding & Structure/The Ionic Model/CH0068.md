# CH0068

**Maximum mark: 4**

The following table provides some information about four unknown pure substances, labelled W, X, Y, Z.
$\newline \ $

| $\mbox{\enskip\textbf{Substance}}\enskip$ | Melting $\mbox{\enskip\textbf{point / K}}\enskip$ | Boiling $\mbox{\enskip\textbf{point / K}}\enskip$ | Electrical $\mbox{\enskip\textbf{conductivity}}\enskip$ | Solubility $\mbox{\enskip\textbf{in water}}\enskip$ |
| :-: | :-:  | :-:  | :-:                                            | :-:       |
| W   | 371  | 1156 | Good in solid and liquid state                | Insoluble |
| X   | 1074 | 1738 | Conducts in liquid state and aqueous solution | Soluble   |
| Y   | 91   | 111  | Does not conduct electricity                  | Insoluble |
| Z   | 3870 | 5100 | Good in the solid state                       | Insoluble |

1. Identify which substance is a gas at room temperature and pressure. [1 mark]

2. Identify the substance that contains ionic bonding. [1 mark]

3. Magnesium oxide is an ionic substance. Give the formula for the ions forming this compound and explain why molten magnesium oxide can conduct electricity. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Substance Y is a gas at room temperature $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
   Room temperature is 298 K. Boiling point is the temperature at which a substance changes from liquid to gas. Substance $Y$ has a boiling point below 298 K.

2. Substance X $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
   Ionic compounds have high melting points and do not conduct electricity when solid as the ions are in fixed positions. When molten or aqueous, the ions are free to move and carry a charge.

3. $\ce{Mg^2+ and O^2-}$  $\checkmark$ \
   Molten magnesium oxide has mobile ions / charge carriers $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$ \
   Magnesium has two valence electrons which it loses to form an ion with a $2+$ charge. Oxygen has six valence electrons and so gains two to form an ion with a $2-$ charge.

   When molten, the ions move independently so electricity can be conducted.
