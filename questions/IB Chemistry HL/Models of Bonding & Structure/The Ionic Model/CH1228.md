# CH1228

What are the correct names for these compounds?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | $\mathbf{(NH_4)_2CO_3}$ | $\mathbf{CuHCO_3}$ | $\mathbf{Fe_2(SO_4)_3}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A. | Ammonium carbonate | Copper (II) hydrogen carbonate | Iron (III) sulfate |
    | B. | Ammonium carbonate | Copper (I) hydrogen carbonate | Iron (III) sulfate |
    | C. | Ammonium carbonate | Copper (II) hydrogen carbonate | Iron (III) sulfide |
    | D. | Ammonium hydrogen carbonate | Copper (I) hydrogen carbonate | Iron (III) sulfide |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
When naming ionic compounds, the cation is always named first, followed by the anion. For transition metals, the oxidation state of the metal must be specified in Roman numerals.

For $\ce{(NH4)2CO3}$, the cation is ammonium, $\ce{NH4+}$, and the anion is carbonate, $\ce{CO3^{2-}}$. The correct name is ammonium carbonate.

For $\ce{CuHCO3}$, the anion is hydrogen carbonate, $\ce{HCO3-}$. Since there is only one anion present, the copper cation must carry a $+1$ charge, so the name is copper(I) hydrogen carbonate.

For $\ce{Fe2(SO4)3}$, the sulfate ion has a $2-$ charge. Three sulfate ions give a total charge of $-6$, which means the two iron atoms together must contribute $+6$. Therefore, each iron has a $+3$ oxidation state, and the compound is iron(III) sulfate.

It is important to note that “sulfide” refers only to $\ce{S^{2-}}$, not to polyatomic anions such as $\ce{SO4^{2-}}$.

This question is straightforward, as it requires careful identification of cations, anions, and the proper use of Roman numerals in naming.
