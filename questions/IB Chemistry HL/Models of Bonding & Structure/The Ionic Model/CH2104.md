# CH2104

[Maximum marks: 3]

Magnesium chloride melts at 718$^\circ\text{C}$ and aluminium chloride melts at 192$^\circ\text{C}.$

1.  Deduce the molecular formulas for each compound. [1 mark]

2.  Explain the significant difference in melting points. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{MgCl2}$ **AND** $\ce{AlCl3}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    Magnesium is a Group 2 metal, and its ion has a 2+ charge, as shown in Section 10 of the IB Chemistry data booklet. Chlorine is a halogen that forms a $1-$ ion. To form a neutral compound, two chloride ions are needed to balance the charge of one magnesium ion. Therefore, the formula of magnesium chloride is $\ce{MgCl2}.$

    Aluminum is a Group 13 element and forms a $3+$ ion. Like before, each chloride ion carries a $1-$ charge. To balance the 3+ charge of one aluminum ion, three chloride ions are required. This results in the neutral compound $\ce{AlCl3}.$

2.  $\ce{MgCl2}$ is an ionic solid **AND** $\ce{AlCl3}$ is a covalent molecular solid $\checkmark$

    ionic bonds and much stronger than intermolecular / dispersion forces  $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    Analysis using the Van Arkel$\text{--}$Ketelaar diagram in Section 17 of the IB Chemistry data booklet.

    | Bond | $\mathbf{\Delta \chi}$ | $\mathbf{\sum \chi}$ | Bond Type | Solid Type |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | $\ce{Mg-Cl}$ | $\ \ 3.2 -1.3 = 1.9\ \ $ | $\ \ \dfrac{3.2 + 1.3}{2} = 2.3\ \ $ | Ionic | Ionic |
    | $\ce{Al-Cl}$ | $3.2 -1.6 = 1.6$ | $\dfrac{3.2 + 1.6}{2} = 2.4$ | Polar covalent | Molecular |

    Magnesium chloride is an ionic compound, whereas aluminum chloride is polar covalent. These bonding differences result in $\ce{MgCl2}$ forming a strong ionic lattice, where $\ce{Mg^2+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions are held together by strong electrostatic forces. In contrast, $\ce{AlCl3}$ in its solid state is molecular with weak dispersion forces between the molecules. Much less energy is needed to separate the neutral molecules than the ions in the lattice, so the melting point of $\ce{AlCl3}$ is much lower than that of $\ce{MgCl2.}$

    ***Additional information:***\
    A common mistake in this type of question is assuming that $\ce{AlCl3}$ is an ionic compound. This leads to incorrectly comparing lattice enthalpy trends instead of focusing on the difference between intermolecular forces in molecular compounds and electrostatic forces in ionic compounds.
