# CH0071

**Maximum mark: 6**

 The diagram below represents an expanded view of a sodium chloride crystal lattice.

![4.1-71](images/4.1-71.png)

1. Write the name of the species found at location $A$ and at location $B$. [1 mark]

2. Describe the nature of the bonding found within this crystal lattice. [1 mark]

3. Explain why sodium chloride does not conduct electricity in the solid state, yet it does conduct electricity when molten. [2 marks]

4. The following table shows three of the oxides of elements found within period 3. These are labelled $A$, $B$, and $C$. [2 marks]

    | $\quad \textbf{Sodium oxide} \quad$ | $\quad \textbf{Magnesium oxide} \quad$ | $\quad \textbf{Aluminium oxide} \quad$ |
    | :-:          | :-:             | :-:             |
    | $A$            | $B $              | $C $              |

    By considering the ions contained within the three substances, place them in order of melting point, from lowest to highest, using the labels $A, B$, and $C$.

    Explain how you chose the substance with the highest melting point.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\textrm{A} = \ce{Cl-}$ and $\textrm{B} = \ce{Na+}\: \checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$
    The chloride ion is larger than sodium ion.

2. electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions; ionic bonding $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$
    $\ce{Na}$ is a metal (group 1), and $\ce{Cl}$ is a nonmetal (group 17).

3. ***solid:*** ions occupy fixed positions $\checkmark$\
    ***molten:***  ions can move freely, allowing conduction $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Substances that conduct must contain mobile charge carriers.  The ions in the molten liquid can move so can conduct charge.  In the solid, the ions are in a fixed position so cannot conduct charge.

4. $A<B<C\,\checkmark$\
    metallic bonding is strongest for higher charge and smaller ionic radius $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The strength of the attraction is proportional to the charge on the cation (and inversely proportional to its size).  Aluminium ion has the greatest charge and smallest radius so forms the oxide with the strongest bonds and the highest melting point.  Sodium, by contrast, has the lowest charge and largest size.
