# CH2103

**Maximum mark: 4**

Strontium chloride is the most common strontium compound found in dietary supplements. Strontium is commonly used to treat tooth sensitivity, osteoporosis, and other bone$\text{--}$related disorders.

1.  State the chemical formula of strontium chloride. [1 mark]

2.  State and explain the conditions under which strontium chloride acts as an electrical conductor. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{SrCl2}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Strontium is in group 2 of the periodic table so has a charge of $+2$ as a cation and chloride is in group 17 so has a charge of $-1$ as an anion.  The two ions must come together in a $1:2$ ratio to balance charge.

2.  molten / $\ce{SrCl2(l)}\ \checkmark$\
    aqueous / $\ce{SrCl2(aq)}\ \checkmark$\
    electrical conductivity requires mobile charge carriers $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    In its solid form, $\ce{SrCl2}$ exists as an ionic lattice, where $\ce{Sr^2+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions are held in fixed positions by strong electrostatic forces (ionic bonds). Since the ions cannot move freely, there are no mobile charge carriers, so electricity cannot be conducted.

    When $\ce{SrCl2}$ is heated to its melting point, the ionic lattice breaks down, and the $\ce{Sr^2+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions become free to move.

    $\ce{SrCl2}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Sr^2+}\textrm{(l)} + 2 \ce{\ Cl-}\textrm{(l)}$

    These mobile ions can carry electric charge, allowing electricity to flow.

    When $\ce{SrCl2}$ is dissolved in water, it dissociates into its ions:

    $\ce{SrCl2}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Sr^2+}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ Cl-}\textrm{(aq)}$

    The free$\text{--}$moving $\ce{Sr^2+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions in the solution act as charge carriers, enabling the solution to conduct electricity. the degree of electrical conductivity depends on the solubility of the ionic compound; poorly soluble compounds will produce fewer dissolved ions, resulting in lower conductivity compared to highly soluble salts.
