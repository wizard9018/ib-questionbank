# CH2101

[Maximum marks: 3]

1.  Halogens react readily with lead to form lead halides.

    Using Section 10 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, write the chemical formulas of the compounds formed when lead reacts with fluorine. [1 mark]

2.  Explain why lead (II) fluoride melts at $824^\circ\text{C}$ while lead (II) chloride melts at $501^\circ\text{C}.$ [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{PbF2}$ **AND** $\ce{PbF4}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    Lead can form lead (II) and lead (IV) compounds (see Section 10 in the IB Chemistry data booklet), meaning it has two ions, $\ce{Pb^2+}$ and $\ce{Pb^4+}.$  Fluorine is a Group 17 (halogen) element, forming the $\ce{F-}$ ion. To balance charges, the two compounds are $\ce{PbF2}$ and $\ce{PbF4}.$

2.  $\ce{PbCl2}$ has weaker ionic bonds / lower lattice enthalpy than $\ce{PbF2}\ \checkmark$

    $\ce{Cl-}$ has a larger ionic radius

    **OR**

    $\ce{Cl-}$ has a lower charge density $\checkmark$

    *Accept the opposite statement about fluoride.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The melting point of an ionic compound depends on the strength of the electrostatic attraction between its ions. This strength can be modeled using Coulomb's Law:

    $F \propto \dfrac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$

    where $q_1$ and $q_2$ are the charges on the cation and anion, and $r$ is the distance between the centers of the ions (the sum of their ionic radii).

    When comparing $\ce{PbF2}$ and $\ce{PbCl2},$ the charges on the ions are the same: lead forms a $2+$ ion, and both fluoride and chloride are $1-$ ions. The key difference lies in the ionic radius. Fluoride is smaller than chloride, so the distance between $\ce{Pb^2+}$ and $\ce{F-}$ is shorter than that between $\ce{Pb^2+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}.$ According to Coulomb's Law, a smaller distance increases the attractive force between the ions, resulting in stronger ionic bonds in PbF₂. Because stronger ionic bonds require more energy to break, $\ce{PbF2}$ has a higher melting point than $\ce{PbCl2}.$
