# CH0966

**Maximum mark: 8**

Calcium phosphide is used in explosives and fireworks.                                       

1.  Describe the bonding that exists between calcium and phosphorus in calcium phosphide. [2 marks]

2.  State the chemical formula for calcium phosphide. [1 mark]

3.  Calcium can also bond with thiocyanate to form calcium thiocyanate, $\ce{Ca(SCN)2}$. Deduce the ionic charge on thiocyanate. [1 mark]

4.  Calcium fluoride $\ce{(CaF2)}$ and calcium oxide $\ce{(CaO)}$ are two examples of ionic compounds of calcium. Explain why the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{CaO}$ is higher than the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{CaF2}$. [2 marks]

5.  Predict, with a reason, whether calcium oxide $\ce{(CaO)}$ or calcium fluoride $\ce{(CaF2)}$ will have a higher melting point. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  ionic  $\checkmark$\

    electrostatic attraction between (metal) cations and (nonmetal) anions in a (crystal) lattice $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    Ionic bonds exist between atoms with a large difference in electronegativity, where the more electronegative atom (the nonmetal) takes an electron from the less electronegative atom (the metal), resulting in an anion and a cation. These ions are attracted electrostatically in what is called an ionic bond. In this case, phosphorus (the non-metal) takes electrons from calcium (the metal).

2.  $\ce{Ca3P2}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    Calcium has a charge of 2+ in ion form, while phosphorus has a charge of 3$-.$ Therefore, the resulting compound will require three calcium ions and two phosphorus atoms to form a neutral compound. There are two strategies to arrive at this answer:

    Swap and drop:

    ![S2.1-966](images/S2.1-966.png)

    Mathematical: The sum of the charges must be equal to zero.

    $\ce{Ca^2+ }: 3 \times (+2) = +6$\

    $\ce{P^3- }: 2 \times (-3) = -6$\

    $+6 - 6 = 0$

3.  1$-\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    Calcium has a charge of 2+ in ion form. Therefore to form $\ce{Ca(SCN)2}$, the charge on the thiocyanate must be 1$-$:

    $\ce{Ca^2+ }: 1 \times (+2) = +2$\

    $\ce{SCN- }: 2 \times (-1) = -2$\

    $+2 - 2 = 0$

4.  $\ce{O^2-}$ has a higher charge than $\ce{F-}$ $\checkmark$\

    higher charges result in greater lattice enthalpy $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    Higher charges result in larger lattice enthalpies because of stronger electrostatic attraction between the ions. This can be explained with Coulomb's Law, where the force that exists between the ions is proportional to the product of the ion charges and inversely proportional to the distance between the ions (hence smaller ions result in stronger forces and larger lattice enthalpies):

    $F \propto \dfrac{q_1q_2}{r_2}$        

    where:

    -   $F$ = force of attraction

    -   $q$ = charge on the ions

    -   $r$ = distance between the ions

    -   $\ $

6.  CaO / calcium oxide $\checkmark$\

    larger lattice enthalpy / stronger ionic bond / greater attraction between ions $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    To melt an ionic compound, the electrostatic attraction between the ions must be overcome. When the bond is stronger, the lattice enthalpy is higher; therefore, it takes more energy to break the bonds.

    Since the lattice enthalpy of calcium oxide $\ce{(CaO)}$ is higher than calcium fluoride $\ce{(CaF2)}$, CaO will have a higher melting point.
