# CH2099

Which of the following compounds is the least volatile?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{BCl3}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{SiCl4}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{BaCl2}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HCl}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Volatility refers to how readily a substance vaporizes, which depends on how easily its particles can overcome the attractive forces holding them together. Substances with stronger interparticle forces have lower volatility because more energy is required for the particles to escape into the gas phase.

To determine which compound has the lowest volatility, it is crucial to analyze the chemical bond types using electronegativity differences (see Section 9 of the IB Chemistry data booklet):

| Compound | $\mathbf{\Delta \chi}$ | Bond type |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\ce{BaCl2}$ | $3.2 - 0.9 = 2.3$ | Ionic |
| $\ce{BCl3}$ | $3.2 - 2.0 = 1.2$ | Polar covalent |
| $\ce{SiCl4}$ | $3.2 - 1.9 = 1.3$ | Polar covalent |
| $\ce{HCl}$ | $3.2 - 2.2 = 1.0$ | Polar covalent |

$\ce{BaCl2}$ is an ionic compound, composed of $\ce{Ba^2+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions arranged in a strong three$\text{--}$dimensional ionic lattice. These ions are held together by strong electrostatic forces (ionic bonds), resulting in a high melting point and extremely low volatility. Ionic compounds do not vaporize  under standard conditions because breaking the lattice requires significant energy.

$\ce{BCl3},$ $\ce{SiCl4},$ and HCl are all molecular compounds held together by weak intermolecular forces. $\ce{BCl3}$ and $\ce{SiCl4}$ are nonpolar covalent molecules with only London dispersion forces between them. HCl is polar, so in addition to dispersion forces, it experiences dipole$\text{--}$dipole interactions, which are moderately strong but still much weaker than ionic bonds.
