# CH2100

A mineral has the formula $\ce{LiAlSiO4}.$ Based on this formula, which is the correct chemical formula of the compound formed between magnesium and the silicate anion?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{Mg2(SiO4)4}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{Mg3(SiO4)2}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{Mg2SiO4}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{Mg(SiO4)2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

$\ce{LiAlSiO4}$ is a mineral formed by two monatomic cations and one silicate ion of unknown charge.  Lithium is a Group 1 metal with a fixed charge of 1+ and aluminum is a Group 13 metal with a common charge of 3+ (as indicated in Section 10 of the IB Chemistry data booklet). According to the principle of electrical neutrality, the algebraic sum of all charges in an ionic compound must be zero.

Charges of the cations + charges of the anions = 0\
$1 + 3 + x = 0\ ; \qquad x = 4-$

The anion is $\ce{SiO4^4-}.$ When forming a salt with magnesium, the charges must balance. Since magnesium is a Group 2 metal with a fixed charge of 2+, the compound is $\ce{Mg2SiO4}.$

$2(2) + -4 = 0\ \checkmark$
