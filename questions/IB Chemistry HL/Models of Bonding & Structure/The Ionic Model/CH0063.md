# CH0063

Which of the following statements correctly describes what happens when magnesium fluoride is formed?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$One magnesium atom gains two electrons to achieve a full outer shell of electrons

- B.$\hspace{1em}$For every magnesium ion formed, two fluorine atoms each gain one electron to form two fluoride ions, each with a single negative charge

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Two fluorine atoms each gain one electron, to form a fluorine molecule where each atom has a full outer shell

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Electrons are transferred from the fluorine atoms to the magnesium atoms

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
Magnesium has two electrons in its outermost shell and loses both to achieve a full outer shell (Mg$^{2+}$). Fluorine has seven electrons in its outermost shell; it needs one more electron to achieve a full outer shell (F$^-$).

Two fluorine atoms are therefore required for each magnesium atom in order to form a neutral ionic compound (where the charges of the positive and negative ions cancel out).
