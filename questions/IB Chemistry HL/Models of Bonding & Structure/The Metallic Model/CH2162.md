# CH2162

Which of the following explains why aluminium has a higher melting point than magnesium?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | Al contains more delocalized electrons per ion than magnesium. |
    | II. | The ionic radius of $\ce{Al^3+}$ is smaller than that of $\ce{Mg^2+}.$ |
    | III. | The charge of an aluminium ion is greater than that of a magnesium ion. |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Aluminium has a higher melting point than magnesium because metallic bonding in aluminium is stronger.

This greater strength arises from several factors:
-   Aluminium $(\ce{Al^3+})$ has three delocalized electrons per atom, compared to only two from magnesium $(\ce{Mg^2+}).$ A greater number of delocalized electrons results in stronger metallic bonding because there are more mobile electrons to hold the structure together. Statement I is correct.

-   The ionic radius of $(\ce{Al^3+})$ is smaller than that of $(\ce{Mg^2+}).$ A smaller radius means the positive charge is concentrated over a smaller volume, increasing the electrostatic attraction between the cations and the delocalized sea of electrons. Statement II is correct.

-   The charge on $(\ce{Al^3+})$ is greater (+3 compared to +2 for $\ce{Mg^2+}),$ leading to a stronger attraction between the ions and the delocalized electrons. Statement III is correct.

Thus, all three statements are correct, and they collectively explain why aluminium has a higher melting point than magnesium.
