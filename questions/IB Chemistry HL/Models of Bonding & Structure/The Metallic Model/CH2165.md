# CH2165

Which metal has the strongest metallic bonding?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Li

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$K

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Rb

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Cs

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Metallic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and a sea of delocalized electrons. The strength of metallic bonding depends on the charge of the ions, the number of delocalized electrons, and the size of the ions.

In the Group 1 metals, all ions have a 1+ charge. However, $\ce{Li+}$ is the smallest ion among the options. A smaller ion leads to stronger electrostatic attraction between the positive ion and the delocalized electrons, hence stronger metallic bonding.

The ionic radius increases as the atomic number increases down a group. As the ionic radius becomes larger, the distance between the positive cations and the delocalized electrons increases. This greater separation reduces the strength of the electrostatic attraction between them, leading to weaker metallic bonding.
