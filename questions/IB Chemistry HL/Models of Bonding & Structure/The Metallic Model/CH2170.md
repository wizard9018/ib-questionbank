# CH2170

Metals are good electrical and thermal conductors.

1.  Explain why sodium can conduct electricity in the solid state, whereas solid sodium chloride cannot. [2 marks]

2.  Explain why the electrical conductivity of metals decreases with increasing temperature. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  delocalized electrons in sodium are free to move under an applied potential difference/voltage $\checkmark$

    anions and cations in solid sodium chloride occupy fixed positions in the lattice, therefore no mobile charge carriers can move under applied potential difference/voltage $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

    Sodium is a metal with a structure consisting of positive cations arranged in a regular lattice and surrounded by a sea of delocalized electrons. These delocalized electrons are mobile and can move freely throughout the solid, allowing electrical current to flow when a voltage is applied.

    In contrast, solid sodium chloride is an ionic compound in which positive sodium ions and negative chloride ions are held in fixed positions within a rigid lattice by strong electrostatic forces. In the solid state, the ions are not free to move and therefore cannot conduct electricity. Electrical conductivity in ionic compounds only occurs when the lattice is disrupted, such as when molten or dissolved in water.

2.  metal cations vibrate more vigorously at high temperature $\checkmark$

    vibrations scatter the delocalized electrons, reducing electrical conductivity $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

    As temperature increases, the metal cations vibrate more vigorously due to the greater thermal energy. The increased vibrations lead to more frequent collisions between the delocalized electrons and the vibrating cations, resulting in increased resistivity. As resistivity increases, the electrical conductivity of the metal correspondingly decreases.

    From a particulate perspective, these collisions scatter the delocalized electrons, impeding their flow through the metallic lattice and thereby reducing the overall electrical conductivity of the metal at higher temperatures.
