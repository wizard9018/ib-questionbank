# CH0703

Potassium and calcium are both solids at room temperature.

Which of the following statements is true?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$Calcium melts at a higher temperature because more valence electrons are delocalized

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$Calcium melts at a lower temperature because more valence electrons are delocalized

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$Calcium melts at a higher temperature because fewer valence electrons are delocalized

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$Calcium melts at a lower temperature because fewer valence electrons are delocalized

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

The melting point of a metal is determined by the size and charge of the metal ions and the number of delocalized electrons surrounding them in the solid lattice.

Potassium is in group 1, so each ion contributes one valence electron to the sea of delocalized electrons, producing a lattice of larger 1+ ions.

Calcium, however, is in group 2, so each atom contributes two valence electrons and forms smaller 2+ ions. The radius of the calcium ion is also smaller and more strongly attracted to the delocalized electrons because it has the same number of inner electron shells as sodium (i.e., the same number of shielding core electrons) but a higher nuclear charge. The stronger attraction between the ions and the sea of delocalized electrons in solid calcium results in stronger metallic bonding.

More thermal energy is needed to separate the atoms/ions, and the melting point is higher.

This question is moderately difficult, as the student must determine that the question is about metallic bonding, correctly compare the electron configurations of the two metals, and recall the link between delocalized electrons and melting point without any additional information given.
