# CH2166

The melting point of calcium, $839^\circ\text{C},$ is much higher than the melting point of lithium, $180.5^\circ\text{C}.$ Which statement is most relevant in explaining this difference?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The calcium atom has a larger radius than the lithium atom.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The calcium atom has a higher molar mass than the lithium atom.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The calcium ion has a higher charge than the lithium ion.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The calcium ion has more electrons than the lithium ion.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The melting point in metals is strongly influenced by the strength of the metallic bonds, which depend on the charge of the metal cations, their size, and the number of delocalized electrons.

The best explanation for calcium having a higher melting point than lithium is that lithium forms $\ce{Li+}$ ions (1+ charge) while calcium forms $\ce{Ca^2+}$ ions (2+ charge). A higher cation charge leads to stronger electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the sea of delocalized electrons, making the metallic bonding much stronger in calcium compared to lithium. Thus, the higher charge on the calcium ion explains why calcium has a significantly higher melting point.

Calcium has a larger atomic radius than lithium, a larger radius would actually weaken electrostatic forces, not strengthen them. Thus, this does not explain the higher melting point.
