# CH2171

Metals and alloys have unique physical properties that make them useful.

1.  Explain why metals are malleable and ductile. [2 marks]

2.  Explain why alloys are harder than pure metals. [1 mark]

3.  Magnesium has an electrical conductivity of $2.3\times 10^7\text{ Sm}^{-1},$ while aluminum has an electrical conductivity of $3.5\times 10^7\text{ Sm}^{-1}.$ Suggest a reason for the difference in their electrical conductivities. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  metals are composed of positive cations and delocalized electrons $\checkmark$

    nondirectional bonding allows cations to slide without breaking bonds $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

    Metals consist of a lattice of positive cations surrounded by a sea of delocalized electrons. Because metallic bonding is nondirectional, when stress is applied the layers of cations can slide over one another without breaking the metallic bonds. This explains why metals are both ductile (can be drawn into wires) and malleable (can be hammered into sheets).

2.  atoms of different size disrupt the regular arrangement of the metal lattice and make it more difficult for layers of atoms to slide $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

    In pure metals, atoms are arranged in highly ordered and uniform lattices. This regular structure allows layers of atoms to slide relatively easily over one another when a stress or force is applied. However, alloys consist of a mixture of different elements, where the atoms typically differ in size from the atoms of the main metal. These differences disrupt the regular arrangement of the metal atoms, introducing distortions and irregularities in the lattice structure. Alloys generally have greater hardness, strength, and resistance to deformation than the pure metals from which they are made.

3.  aluminum contributes three delocalized electrons per atom to the electron sea, while magnesium atoms contribute only two $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

    In metals, electrical conductivity primarily depends on the number of delocalized electrons (charge carriers) and the ease of their movement through the metallic lattice. Each aluminum atom contributes three delocalized electrons to the electron sea, while each magnesium atom contributes only two. The greater number of delocalized electrons provided by aluminum increases the density of charge carriers available to move through the metallic lattice when a potential difference (voltage) is applied. As a result, aluminum exhibits a higher electrical conductivity compared to magnesium.
