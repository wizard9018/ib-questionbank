# CH2177

Why are alloys usually stronger than the pure metals from which they are made?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$They have stronger bonds between the molecules they contain.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$They combine the properties of the elements from which they are made.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$They have atoms of different sizes in their lattices.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$They are made by mixing a molten metal with another metal or element.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Alloys are typically stronger than pure metals because the atoms of different sizes disrupt the regularity of the metal lattice. This irregular arrangement makes it more difficult for layers of atoms to slide over each other, which increases the strength and hardness of the material. Unlike pure metals, where atoms are uniform and can shift easily, the mixed sizes in an alloy hinder dislocation movement and reinforce the structure.
