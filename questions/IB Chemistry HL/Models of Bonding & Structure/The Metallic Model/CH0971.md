# CH0971

**Maximum mark: 5**

Vanadium and titanium are both common metals in aerospace engineering because they have a high strength to weight ratio.        

1.  Explain why vanadium has a higher melting point than titanium. [2 marks]

2.  Titanium and vanadium are both able to conduct electricity. Explain how this is possible. [1 mark]

3.  Tin is not a good choice for use in aerospace engineering. Predict, with a reason, why this is so. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Vanadium exhibits stronger metallic bonding (therefore a higher melting point), $\checkmark$

    (This is because) vanadium has more delocalized electrons/more unpaired valence electrons. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Vanadium's electron configuration is $[Ar]\,4s^2\,3d^3$ while titanium's is $[Ar]\,4s^2\,3d^2$.

    The additional electron in vanadium's valence shell enables it to exhibit stronger interactions between the vanadium ions and the delocalized electrons resulting in stronger metallic bonding. Stronger metallic bonding means that more energy is required to break the cohesive forces in the crystal lattice, therefore it has a higher melting point.

2.  (both metals have) delocalized electrons that are able to move freely through the metal lattice. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    In order to conduct electricity, there must be electrons that are able to move freely through the material. In metals, unpaired valence electrons are not bound to any particular atom and instead are able to form a sea of delocalized electrons. These delocalized electrons are responsible for a metal's conductivity.

3.  (Tin) has weaker metallic bonds/fewer delocalized electrons resulting in:  $\checkmark$

    One of: $\checkmark$

    -   Lower melting point (compared to V and Ti);

    -   Softer metal;

    -   Low strength to weight ratio/low mechanical strength.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    

    To be effective in aerospace engineering, metals must be strong and able to withstand high heat. The weaker metallic bonds in tin resulting from fewer delocalized electrons leads to a lower melting point, higher malleability and lower strength to weight ratio - all undesirable attributes for aerospace engineering.
