# CH0545

\[Maxiumum mark: 6\]

Titanium, lithium, and potassium have melting points of 1670 $^\circ$C, 181 $^\circ$C, and 63 $^\circ$C, respectively. 

1.  Outline two characteristics of a metallic element that affect the strength of the metallic bond. [2 marks]

2.  In terms of atomic structure, explain the observed melting points of titanium, lithium, and potassium listed above [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The charge on the ion/number of valence electrons, higher correlates to a stronger metallic bond. $\checkmark$

    The ionic radius, a smaller ionic radius correlates to a stronger metallic bond. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Higher charge and smaller ionic radii lead to a greater charge density of the metal cations. This results in stronger electrostatic forces of attraction between the cations and the delocalized electrons

2.  Melting points of Li and K are lower than Ti because Li and K ions carry a charge of 1+. In contrast, Ti ions carry a charge of 2+ or 4+. $\checkmark$  $\checkmark$ $\checkmark$

    *(The reverse statement is acceptable, Ti > Li, K, with proper explanation)*

    The melting point of K is lower than Li because K$^{+}$ has 3 complete energy levels and, therefore, a larger ionic radius than Li$^{+}$ with only one complete energy level. $\checkmark$  $\checkmark$ $\checkmark$

    *(The reverse statement is acceptable Li$^{+}$ > K$^{+}$ with proper explanation)*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Higher charge and smaller ionic radii lead to a greater charge density of the metal cations. This results in stronger electrostatic forces of attraction between the cations and the delocalized electrons.
