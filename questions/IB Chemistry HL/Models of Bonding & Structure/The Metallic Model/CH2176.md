# CH2176

Which statement best explains why chromium has stronger metallic bonds than aluminum?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Chromium has a smaller atomic radius.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Chromium has more delocalized electrons available for bonding.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Chromium has a higher atomic number.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Chromium is in a different period.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Chromium forms stronger metallic bonds than aluminum because it has more delocalized electrons contributing to the metallic bonding.

As a transition metal, chromium has six electrons is both its $4s$ and $3d$ orbitals that can delocalize, increasing the electron density in the sea of electrons. This leads to stronger electrostatic attraction between the positive metal ions and the delocalized electrons, resulting in stronger metallic bonds and a higher melting point.

Aluminum has the electron configuration $[\ce{Ne}]\ 3s^2\ 3p^1,$ so it has only three valence electrons to contribute to the electron sea.
