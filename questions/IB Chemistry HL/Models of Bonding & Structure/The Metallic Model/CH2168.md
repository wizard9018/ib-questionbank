# CH2168

Which is the correct description of the lattice in the alloy brass, composed of copper and zinc?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Delocalized cations and anions in fixed positions

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Delocalized anions and cations in fixed positions

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Cations in fixed positions and delocalized valence electrons

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Molecules in fixed positions and delocalized valence electrons

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

An alloy is a mixture of two or more elements, where at least one is a metal. It may consist of a combination of metals or a metal mixed with small amounts of nonmetals such as carbon, phosphorus, silicon, or boron. Alloys generally possess different physical properties compared to their component elements.

In metallic structures and alloys like brass, metal cations (such as $\ce{Cu^2+}$ and $\ce{Zn^2+})$ are held in fixed positions in a lattice, while their outer valence electrons are delocalized and free to move throughout the structure. This delocalization gives metals their conductivity and malleability.

Furthermore, alloys are harder and stronger than pure metals. This increased hardness arises because the different-sized atoms disrupt the regular arrangement of metal cations, making it more difficult for layers of atoms to slide over each other. This hinders the movement of dislocations and enhances the strength of the material.
