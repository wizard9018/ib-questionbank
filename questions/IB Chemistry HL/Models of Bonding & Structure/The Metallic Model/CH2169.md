# CH2169

Consider the melting points of some Period 3 elements shown in the table below
$\mbox{}\\[10pts]$

| Element | Melting point / K |
| :-: | :-: |
| Sodium | 371 |
| Magnesium | 922 |
| Aluminium | 933 |
| Silicon | 1683 |

1.  Explain why metals generally have high melting points. [2 marks]

2. State two reasons why the melting point of aluminium is higher than that of sodium. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  strong electrostatic attraction between cations and delocalized electrons $\checkmark$\
    overcoming electrostatic attraction requires heat/energy to break the metallic lattice $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$\
    The melting point is the temperature at which a substance changes from the solid to the liquid phase. This phase change requires overcoming the electrostatic interactions between particles; therefore, stronger electrostatic interactions lead to higher melting points, as more energy is needed to break these forces.

    Metallic bonding can be described as a lattice of positive cations surrounded by a sea of delocalized electrons. This structure results in strong electrostatic attraction between the cations and the negatively charged electrons. Metals typically have higher melting points than many ionic or covalent compounds because the electrostatic attractions in metallic bonding are often stronger than the forces present in those other types of bonding.

2.  Any two of:

    $\ce{Al^3+}$ ions have a higher charge than $\ce{Na+}$ ions $\checkmark$\
    $\ce{Al^3+}$ ions are smaller than $\ce{Na+}$ $\checkmark$\
    Al has more delocalized electrons per atom than sodium (3 *vs.* 1) $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$\
    Melting point and boiling point trends can be used to compare the strength of metallic bonding in different metals. As the strength of the metallic bond increases, the melting and boiling points also increase, due to stronger electrostatic forces requiring more energy to overcome.

    Several factors influence metallic bond strength:
    -   Ionic radius: A smaller ionic radius decreases the distance between cations and delocalized electrons, resulting in stronger electrostatic attraction.
    -   Ionic charge: A higher positive charge on the cation increases the strength of the electrostatic attraction between the cations and the delocalized electrons.
    -   Electron density of the delocalized sea of electrons: A greater number of delocalized electrons per cation strengthens the metallic bond. For example, each aluminium atom contributes three delocalized electrons, whereas each sodium atom contributes only one.

    Together, smaller ionic size, higher ionic charge, and a greater number of delocalized electrons lead to stronger metallic bonding and, consequently, higher melting and boiling points.
