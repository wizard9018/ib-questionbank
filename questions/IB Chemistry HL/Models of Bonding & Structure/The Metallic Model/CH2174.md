# CH2174

Which one of the following does not contain delocalized electrons?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Propene

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Methyl benzene

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Graphene

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Sodium

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Propene contains a localized $\ce{C=C}.$ While a double bond includes a $\pi$ bond formed from side$\text{--to--}$side $p$ orbital overlap, in simple alkenes like propene, the $\pi$ electrons are localized between the two carbon atoms and do not extend over multiple atoms. Therefore, it does not have a system of delocalized electrons.

Methyl benzene has a benzene ring. Benzene, with its two equivalent resonance structures, has a  delocalized system of $\pi$ electrons spread over the six carbon atoms in the ring.

Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice. Each carbon atom forms three $\sigma$ bonds, leaving one $p$ orbital per carbon atom that overlaps side$\text{--by--}$side with its neighbors. This creates a giant delocalized $\pi$ electron system across the entire sheet, allowing graphene to conduct electricity.

Sodium is a metal. In metallic bonding, the outermost (valence) electrons are delocalized and form a sea of electrons around the positively charged metal ions. These delocalized electrons are responsible for sodium's electrical conductivity and other metallic properties.
