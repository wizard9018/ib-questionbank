# CH2164

The melting and boiling points of an element are $1550^\circ\text{C}$ and $3000^\circ\text{C},$ respectively. It is an excellent conductor of electricity in the solid and molten states. What type of bonding is present in the element?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$ionic

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$polar covalent

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$coordination bonding

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$metallic

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The element described has very high melting and boiling points and is an excellent conductor of electricity both in the solid and molten states. These are hallmark properties of metallic bonding. In metallic bonding, atoms release their valence electrons to form a sea of delocalized electrons that can move freely throughout the lattice, allowing metals to conduct electricity efficiently in both solid and liquid forms.

The following table summarizes some physical properties with the bonding type:

| Bonding Type | Melting/Boiling Points | Electrical Conductivity | Key Features |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Metallic | Very high | Conducts in solid and molten states | Delocalized electron sea |
| Ionic | High | Conducts only when molten or in solution | Solid ionic crystals are brittle |
| Covalent (polar) | Low to moderate | Generally nonconductive | Molecules held by intermolecular forces |
| Covalent (network) | Very high | Generally nonconductive (except graphite) | Giant covalent lattices (e.g., diamond, $\ce{SiO2})$ |
| Coordination | Variable | Usually conductive in solution | Metal ions bonded to ligands |
