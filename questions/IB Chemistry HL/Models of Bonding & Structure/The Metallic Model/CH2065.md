6.  1. electrons flow from the impure copper to the pure copper $\checkmark$\
    pure copper is the cathode $\checkmark$

        ![CH2065cellans](images/CH2065cellans.png)

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        This process is known as electrorefining. An impure sample of copper is oxidized at the anode (left) and reduced at the cathode (right). As the process occurs, impurities separate from the initial sample, and pure copper is produced at the negative electrode (cathode). Electrons flow from the anode to the cathode.

    2. *Any two of the following:*

        -   less active impurities fall to the bottom of the cell, do not plate on cathode $\checkmark$\
        more active impurities are oxidized, do not plate on cathode $\checkmark$\
        side reactions, like the oxidation of water at the anode, would not change the mass of the anode $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The mass loss at the anode occurs due to the oxidation of copper and the detachment of impurities at the electrode. However, the mass gained at the cathode corresponds only to the pure copper deposited at the electrode. As a result, the change in the mass of the electrodes is not the same.

        If the impurities at the anode are easy to oxidize the mass will be lost but the impurity will stay in the electrolyte and not reduce onto the cathode.  If the impurity in the cathode is difficult to oxidize, it will be collected as solid “anode mud” and will not deposit at the cathode. If side reactions occur, like the oxidation of water at the anode, copper could plate at the cathode without a matching loss of mass at the anode.
