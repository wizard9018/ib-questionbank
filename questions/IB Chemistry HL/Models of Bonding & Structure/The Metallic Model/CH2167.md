# CH2167

Which statements are correct for metals?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | They are good thermal conductors because they have free moving cations. |
    | II. | They often consist of a close packed lattice of cations with delocalized valence electrons. |
    | III. | They are malleable and ductile because the layers of cations can slide across each other and still remain bonded. |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

Metals consist of positive cations arranged in a regular lattice structure, surrounded by a sea of delocalized electrons. These delocalized electrons are responsible for electrical and thermal conductivity, as well as the strong bonding in metals. The ability of the cations to slide past one another without breaking bonds (nondirectional bonding) explains the malleability and ductility of metals.

The cations vibrate around fixed lattice positions and do not move freely. Statement I is false.

Cations are arranged in fixed positions within the lattice, while valence electrons are delocalized and move freely throughout the structure. Statement II is true.

Malleability and ductility of metals are explained by the electron sea model: when stress is applied, layers of cations can slide over each other without disrupting the metallic bonding. This allows the metal to change shape without fracturing. In contrast, applying stress to an ionic crystal causes it to shatter, due to repulsive forces between like$-$charged ions. Statement III is true.

Malleability reflects the nature of metallic bonding, which acts uniformly in all directions. The cations are equally attracted to the surrounding sea of electrons in nondirectional bonds, allowing them to shift within the lattice without breaking the metallic bond.
