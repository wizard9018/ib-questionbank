# CH0386

The electron configuration of carbon is $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^2$.

Why is hybridization helpful for explaining the bonding of carbon in ethene ($\ce{C2H4}$)?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Hybridization allows the paired $2s$ electrons to participate in separate $\sigma-$bonds

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Hybridization allows the paired $2s$ electrons to participate in separate $\pi-$bonds

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Hybridization allows the paired $2p$ electrons to participate in separate $\sigma-$bonds

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Hybridization allows the paired $2p$ electrons to participate in separate $\pi-$bonds

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Each carbon atom in ethene forms three $\sigma-$bonds and one $\pi-$bond. The $s$ electrons are originally paired but are conceptually unpaired during the formation of the three $sp^2$ orbitals, allowing the $\sigma-$bonds to form. The $p$ electrons are unpaired in the original atomic orbitals and remain unpaired in the hybrid orbitals. One $p$ orbital in each carbon atom is used to produce the $\pi-$bond.

This question is moderately difficult as the student must recall the concept of electron unpairing/promotion in hybridization and differentiate between $\sigma-$ and $\pi-$orbitals without the aid of a diagram.
