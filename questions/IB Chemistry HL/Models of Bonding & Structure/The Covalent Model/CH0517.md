# CH0517

**Maximum mark: 7**

Phosphorus reacts with oxygen to form two different oxides, phosphorus(III) oxide $\ce{(P4O6)}$ and phosphorus(VI) oxide $\ce{(P4O10)}$.

1.  The oxides of phosphorus consist of cage-like molecules with the following structural formulas.

    ![14.1-CH0517a](images/CH0517a.png)

    The $\ce{O-P-O}$ bond angles in the two molecules are labelled.

    1.  State the geometries of the bonds around each phosphorus atom in $\ce{P4O6}$. [1 mark]

    2.  State the geometries of the bonds around each phosphorus atom in $\ce{P4O10}$. [1 mark]

    3.  Suggest why the bond $\ce{O-P-O}$ bond angles of the two molecules are similar despite the different arrangements of bonding and non-bonding electrons around the phosphorus atoms. [2 marks]

    4.  Explain how the ability of phosphorus to form both $\ce{P4O6}$ and $\ce{P4O10}$ demonstrates that the octet rule is not always obeyed. [2 marks]

2.  $\ce{P4O6}$ and $\ce{P4O10}$ react with an aqueous base to produce phosphite ($\ce{HPO3^2-}$) and phosphate ($\ce{PO4^3-}$) ions, respectively. The structural formulas of $\ce{HPO3^2-}$ and $\ce{PO4^3-}$ are shown.

    ![14.1-CH0517b](images/CH0517b.png)

    State why resonance structures are needed to accurately represent the bonding in $\ce{HPO3^2-}$ and $\ce{PO4^3-}$. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  $\ce{P4O6}$: trigonal pyramidal. $\checkmark$

        *Do **not** award M1 for trigonal;*\
        *Do **not** award M1 for pyramidal.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        There are 4 electron domains around each $P$ atom and so, the shape is based on the tetrahedral geometry.The existence of 1 lone pair gives it the trigonal pyramidal geometry.

    2.  $\ce{P4O10}$: tetrahedral. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        There are 4 electron domains around each $P$ atom and no lone pairs.

    3.  Repulsion between non-bonding / lone and bonding electron pairs is stronger than between two bonding pairs; $\checkmark$

        but, $\ce{P-O}$ single bonds in $\ce{P4O10}$ are repelled by two pairs of bonding electrons (in $\ce{P=O}$ bond) **OR** additional repulsion of second / $\pi$ / double bond matches repulsion of non-bonding / lone pair. $\checkmark$

       *Award M1 if stronger repulsion of non-bonding electron pair is implied in rest of answer.*

    4.  ($\ce{P}$ atom in) $\ce{P4O10}$ does not obey octet rule (whereas $\ce{P4O6}$ does); $\checkmark$

        as it forms 5 bonds **OR** shares 5 bonding pairs **OR** has expanded octet **OR** 10 / more than 8 valence / outer shell electrons. $\checkmark$

        *Accept answers that describe $\ce{P4O10}$ only;*\
    *Award M1 if statement is implied in rest of answer;*\
    *Do **not** award M2 if reference is not made to an expanded octet or number of valence, bonding or outer shell electrons specifically*;\
    *Award [0] for answers that do not explicitly describe the bonding or electron configuration in $\ce{P4O10}$.*
