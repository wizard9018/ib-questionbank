# CH0999

Which intermolecular forces cause the boiling point of ethanol to be greater than that of methoxymethane, $\ce{CH3OCH3}$?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$Covalent bonds

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$London dispersion forces

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$Hydrogen bonds

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$Ionic bonds

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The structures of ethanol and methoxyethane are shown below.

|![CH0999a](images/CH0999a.png)|![CH0999b](images/CH0999b.png)
:-:|:-:

These molecules are structural isomers; they have the same molecular formula, $\ce{C2H6O}$ but different functional groups.

Both are polar molecules. However, ethanol has an $\ce{O-H}$ bond within its structure, and methoxyethane does not. As a result, they show different intermolecular forces. Ethanol has London dispersion forces, dipole-dipole forces, and hydrogen bonding. Methoxymethane has London dispersion forces and dipole-dipole forces only. The relative strength of the intermolecular forces is shown below.

London dispersion forces < dipole-dipole forces < hydrogen bonds.

Ethanol has stronger intermolecular forces, so more energy is needed to overcome these forces of attraction and turn liquid ethanol into gaseous ethanol. The boiling point of ethanol is greater.

This question is categorized as medium as the student must be able to compare the structural formulas of ethanol and methoxymethane, deduce the intermolecular forces, and compare their strength.
