# CH2257

Which of the following substances is correctly matched with its type of solid and characteristic physical properties?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

|$\hspace{0.95em}$||$\hspace{0.1em}$|Substance|Type of Solid|Structure and Properties
:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
||A.||Diamond|Giant covalent|Three$-$dimensional network structure with high melting point; hard, electrical insulator
||B.||Sodium Chloride | Ionic|Two$-$dimensional lattice with high melting point; brittle, electrical conductor in aqueous solution
||C.||Copper | Metallic|Three$-$dimensional lattice with low melting point;  brittle, electrical insulator
||D.||Iodine | Simple molecular|Discrete molecular units with high melting point, conductor in aqueous solution

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

**Diamond** is a giant covalent solid. Each carbon atom is covalently bonded to four others in a continuous three$-$dimensional network. Melting requires breaking strong covalent bonds throughout the structure, giving a very high melting point, and the same bonds make diamond hard. All four valence electrons on each carbon are held in covalent bonds, so there are no mobile charge carriers and diamond does not conduct electricity.

*Graphite and graphene are also covalent network solids, but are built from layers rather than a three$-$dimensional network. Each carbon bonds to three others, leaving one delocalized electron per atom, so they conduct along the sheets. The layers are held together only by London dispersion forces, which is why graphite is soft.*

**Sodium chloride** is ionic, but the structure in option B is wrong: ionic solids form three$-$dimensional lattices, not two$-$dimensional ones. The remaining properties are correct. They are brittle because displacing one layer brings like charges into contact, and they conduct when molten or dissolved because the ions become mobile, but not in the solid state.

**Copper** is metallic, and option C reverses its properties. A lattice of cations in a sea of delocalized electrons gives high electrical and thermal conductivity in both the solid and liquid states, and most metals have high melting points. Metallic bonding is non$-$directional, so cations slide past one another without breaking the attraction, making metals malleable rather than brittle.

**Iodine** is simple molecular, and option D is wrong on the melting point. Discrete $\ce{I2}$ molecules are held together only by London dispersion forces, which are weak, so molecular solids melt at low temperatures. The covalent bonds within each molecule are strong, but it is the forces between molecules that are overcome on melting.

Diamond and iodine both contain only covalent bonds, yet diamond melts above $3500\,^\circ\text{C}$ and iodine at $114\,^\circ\text{C}.$ The difference is whether the covalent bonding extends throughout the solid or stops at the edge of each molecule.
