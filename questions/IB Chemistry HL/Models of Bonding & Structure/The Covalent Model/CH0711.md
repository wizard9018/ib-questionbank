        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        The Lewis structure shows the positions of all valence electrons in the molecules, as well as the formal bonds between the atoms. N is in group 15 so has five valence electrons, while H is in group 1 so has 1. A single covalent bond is formed between the N atom and each H atom is formed by the sharing of the H valence electron and one N valence electron. Thus, there are two unshared valence electrons remaining on the N atom; these form a non-bonding lone pair.

    2.  N has a higher electronegativity than H; $\checkmark$

        so, N has a partial negative / $\delta -$ charge

        **OR**

        H has a partial positive / $\delta +$ charge

        **OR**

        the (bonding) electron density is higher on / near N

        **OR**

        the (bonding) electron density is lower on / near H. $\checkmark$

        *Award M2 for a diagram of the bond with a dipole or partial charges correctly drawn;\
        Do **not** award M1 if the electronegativities of the elements are stated but not compared.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Electronegativity is the ability of an atom in a bond to attract a bonding pair of electrons towards itself. The N atom contains one more electron shells than the H atoms and the inner shell electrons repel the valence electrons, shielding the nuclear charge. However, the nuclear charge of N (7+) is much greater than that of H (1+) so the valence electrons are much more strongly attracted to the nucleus, resulting in a higher electronegativity. The bonds are covalent because the electronegativity difference ($3.0-2.2 = 0.8$) is not sufficiently large for the H valence electrons to be donated to the N atom: ions do not form. Nonetheless, the electronegativity difference does result in a higher bonding electron density near the N atom, so the N atom has a small negative charge ($\delta -$) and the H atoms a small positive charge ($\delta +$): the bond is polar.

    3.  Trigonal pyramidal. $\checkmark$

        *Do **not** accept “trigonal”;\
        Do **not** accept “pyramidal”.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        The N atom has four electron domains: a lone (non-bonding) electron pair and three bonds, each featuring a single bonding electron pair. The geometry of four electron domains is tetrahedral. However, the arrangement of the bonds alone is trigonal pyramidal, as the four atoms in the molecule form the vertices of a triangle-based pyramid.

    4.  The bond angle of ammonia is $107\degree$; $\checkmark$

        The bond angle of the ammonium ion is $109.5\degree$ / $109\degree 28'$ ; $\checkmark$

        (Ammonia has a smaller bond angle because) lone pairs repel (bonding pairs) more strongly than bonding pairs (repel each other). $\checkmark$

        *Award M2 for a bond angle of $109\degree 30'$;\
        Award M2 for a bond angle of $109.46\degree$ **OR** $109.47\degree$;\
        Award M1 but not M2 if the sizes of the bond angles are correctly compared but neither value is stated.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        $\ce{NH4+}$ contains no lone pairs and four bonds, each featuring a single bonding electron pair. There are four electron domains, so the geometry is tetrahedral. The bond angle for a tetrahedral molecule is $109\degree 28'$, which is approximately $109.5\degree$ ($1\degree$ contains 60 “minutes”, expressed as $60'$, therefore $0.5\degree$ = $30'$).

        $\ce{NH3}$ also has a tetrahedral electron domain geometry but the bonding geometry is trigonal pyramidal, as one electron domain is a lone pair. The lone pair repels the bonding pairs more strongly than they repel each other, so the bonds are pushed closer together than in $\ce{NH4+}$ and the bond angle decreases to $107\degree$.

2.  Ammonia / $\ce{NH3}$ has a higher boiling point due to hydrogen bonding; $\checkmark$

    The electronegativity (of elements) decreases down the group (from 3.0 to 2.0); $\checkmark$

    so the other molecules form London / dispersion / induced dipole-induced dipole forces **AND** the interactions of these molecules are weaker than hydrogen bonds; $\checkmark$

    The boiling points increase from $\ce{PH3}$ to $\ce{SbH3}$ / down the group because the central atom increases in size / contains more electrons **AND** this makes the atom more polarizable / allows induced dipoles to form more easily / allows the electron cloud to change shape more easily (so the London forces are stronger). $\checkmark$

    *Do **not** award M1 if the link between boiling point and hydrogen bonding is not stated explicitly;\
    Do **not** award M1 if the term “hydrogen bond” is not used;\
    Do **not** award M2 if the electronegativities are stated but not compared;\
    Do **not** award M3 if the interactions are referred to ONLY as dipole-dipole (forces) OR van der Waals forces;\
    Do **not** award M3 if the relative strengths of London and hydrogen forces are not explicitly compared;\
    Do **not** award M4 if the link between polarizability and atom size / number of electrons is not explicitly stated.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    An N atom has a much higher electronegativity (3.0) than an H atom (2.2) so the N-H bond is polar, with a partial positive charge on the H atom and a partial negative charge on the N atom. The partial charges of neighboring molecules are electrostatically attracted to each other, and this interaction is very strong due to the small size and large charge density of the H atom. This type of intermolecular interaction is called a hydrogen bond; it results in a high boiling point, as much thermal energy is needed to separate the molecules. The other elements in group 15 have electronegativities similar to that of H (2.2 for P and As, 2.0 for Sb) so the bonds are much less polar and the main interactions are London forces, which occur when a random imbalance in electron density in one molecule repels electrons in another molecule to produce an induced dipole. Induced dipoles are stronger for larger atoms with larger, more polarisable electron clouds, so the interaction strength and boiling point increases in the order P < As < Sb.
