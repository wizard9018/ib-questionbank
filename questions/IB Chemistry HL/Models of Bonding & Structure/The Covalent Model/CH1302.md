# CH1302

$\ce{BrF}$ has a higher boiling point than $\ce{BrCl}.$

Which row accurately represents the intermolecular differences between $\ce{BrF}$ and $\ce{BrCl}?$

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Dipole-dipole attractions | London dispersion forces| Strongest intermolecular force |

    | C. | Stronger in $\ce{BrCl}$ | Stronger in $\ce{BrF}$ | Dipole-dipole |
    | D. | Stronger in $\ce{BrF}$ | Stronger in $\ce{BrCl}$ | London |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Both molecules are polar.

The electronegativity is a measure of the ability of an atom to attract a bonding pair of electrons to itself.

The strength of the attraction depends on the size of the atom and its nuclear charge. The attraction gets stronger with increasing nuclear charge and smaller size.

Fluorine is the element with the greatest electronegativity in the periodic table. It has a small size and a high nuclear charge.

Electronegativity decreases down a group as the atomic radius increases, so the $\ce{Br-Cl}$ bond is less polar than $\ce{Br-F}.$

A greater electronegativity difference in the $\ce{Br-F}$ bond results in stronger dipole-dipole intermolecular forces.

The relative molecular mass of these two molecules is different.

Relative molecular mass of $\ce{BrF} = 79.90 + 19.00 = 98.90\,\text{g mol}^{-1}$

Relative molecular mass of $\ce{BrCl} = 79.90 + 35.45 = 115.35\,\text{g mol}^{-1}$

$\ce{BrCl}$ has a greater relative molecular mass, so its London dispersion forces are stronger than those in $\ce{BrF}.$

$\ce{BrF}$ has a higher boiling point, meaning the intermolecular forces between these molecules are stronger than those in $\ce{BrCl},$ That implies that the dipole-dipole attractions are the determining factor here as they are stronger.

This question is categorized as hard. Students are expected to understand the London dispersion forces as non-permanent attractions that depend on the relative molecular mass and the dipole-dipole forces as attractions that depend on the net polarity in the molecule. They should also deduce the trend in the electronegativities down a group to predict how the polarity varies here.
