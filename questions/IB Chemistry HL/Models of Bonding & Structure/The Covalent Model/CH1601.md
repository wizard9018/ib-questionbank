Which option is in decreasing order of their boiling points?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$X > Y > Z > W

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Y > X > W > Z

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$W > Z > Y > X

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Y > W > X > Z

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To answer this question, we need to consider the factors that influence boiling points in order of importance.

***Molecular Mass and Polarizability:*** Compounds with iodine (I) generally have higher boiling points than those with chlorine (Cl) because iodine is heavier and more polarizable, leading to stronger London dispersion forces.

***Branching:*** Branched compounds like 2$\text{--}$chloro$\text{--}$2$\text{--}$methylpropane and 2$\text{--}$iodo$\text{--}$2$\text{--}$methylpropane generally have lower boiling points than their straight-chain counterparts due to decreased surface area for intermolecular interactions.

Thus, 1$\text{--}$iodobutane has the highest boiling point due to its high molecular mass and lack of branching, followed by 2$\text{--}$iodo$\text{--}$2$\text{--}$methylpropane. 1$\text{--}$chlorobutane comes next, and 2$\text{--}$chloro$\text{--}$2$\text{--}$methylpropane has the lowest boiling point due to its lower molecular mass and branched structure
