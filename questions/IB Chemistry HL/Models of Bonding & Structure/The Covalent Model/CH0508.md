# CH0508

Molecular oxygen $\ce{(O2)}$ and ozone $\ce{(O3)}$ in the atmosphere undergo the following decomposition reactions.

$\begin{aligned}
\ce{O2 &-> O. + O.} \\
\ce{O3 &-> O2 + O.}
\end{aligned}$

The reactions occur when the reactant molecules absorb a specific wavelength of light.

Which of the following statements is true?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{O3}$ contains weaker bonds than $\ce{O2}$, so a shorter wavelength of light breaks it down

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{O3}$ contains weaker bonds than $\ce{O2}$, so a longer wavelength of light breaks it down

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{O3}$ contains stronger bonds than $\ce{O2}$, so a shorter wavelength of light breaks it down

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{O3}$ contains stronger bonds than $\ce{O2}$, so a longer wavelength of light breaks it down

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The bonds in $\ce{O3}$ have bond order 1.5, as the $\pi$ bonding pair is delocalized across the two bonds.

The bond in $\ce{O2}$ has bond order 2. The bonds in $\ce{O3}$ are therefore weaker than those in $\ce{O2}$ and broken by a longer wavelength of light (331 nm) than the bond in in $\ce{O2}$ (241 nm).

The student must provide two pieces of information: the relative bond strengths of ozone and oxygen and the correlation between energy and wavelength. Nonetheless, this question is of only moderate difficulty since it requires recall of two aspects of a key topic covered explicitly in the syllabus.
