# CH2154

What are the electron domain and molecular geometries of $\ce{IF4+}?$

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Electron Domain Geometry | Molecular Geometry |
    | :- | :-: | :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Trigonal bipyramidal | Trigonal bipyramidal |
    | B. | Trigonal bipyramidal | Seesaw |
    | C. | Octahedral | Square planar |
    | D. | Tetrahedral | Tetrahedral |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

To determine the molecular geometry in any molecular arrangement is crucial to draw the Lewis formula and then to deduce the molecular geometry based on the number of electron domains around the central atom using the VSEPR Theory.

The central atom in $\ce{IF4+}$ is iodine because fluorine is more electronegative and typically forms single bonds. To draw the Lewis structure, we need to calculate the valence electrons:

Total valence electrons = $7 + (7)(4) - 1 = 34$ electrons

We now distribute electrons by forming single bonds between the central iodine and each of the  four fluorine atoms, using $4\times 2 = 8$ electrons. The octet on the fluorine atom can be completed with nonbonding pairs. This requires $6\times 4 = 24$ electrons. The two remaining electrons are placed as a lone pair on the central iodine atom.

![CH2154a](images/CH2154a.png)

Since iodine has five regions of electron density (bonding and nonbonding), the electron domain geometry is trigonal bipyramidal. Iodine has one lone pair, the molecular geometry is seesaw shape. The lone pair occupies an equatorial position to minimize electron$\text{--}$electron repulsion.
