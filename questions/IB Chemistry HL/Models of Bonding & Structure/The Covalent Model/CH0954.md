# CH0954

Which intermolecular forces are present between the amino acid, serine, and water as the mobile phase in paper chromatography?

![CH0954](images/CH0954.png)

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$London dispersion forces

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$Hydrogen bonds

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$Dipole$-$dipole forces and hydrogen bonds

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$London dispersion forces, dipole$-$dipole forces, and hydrogen bonds

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
To answer this question, the student must first assess the polarity of serine and recognize that this molecule is polar and therefore, it is capable of forming dipole$-$dipole forces.

Additionally, serine is able to form hydrogen bonds with water. These hydrogen bonds are the strongest of the intermolecular forces, and they are formed between the highly electronegative oxygen atom in water with the less electronegative hydrogen atoms of serine's hydroxyl and carboxyl groups.

Since water is also polar, it can form the same intermolecular bonds. All molecules are capable of London dispersion forces regardless of polarity, and therefore all three types of intermolecular forces are present between serine and water.

This question is of medium difficulty since the student must carefully assess the polarity of serine and then recall the types of intermolecular forces that are possible based on structure.
