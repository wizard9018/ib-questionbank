# CH0341

Which of the following statements regarding the structure of ozone, $\ce{O3}$, is correct when compared to the structure of  molecular oxygen, $\ce{O2}$?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The bond order of $\ce{O3}$ is less than $\ce{O2}$, but the bond enthalpy of $\ce{O3}$ is greater than $\ce{O2}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Both the bond order and the bond enthalpy of $\ce{O3}$ are greater than that of $\ce{O2}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Both the bond order and the bond enthalpy of $\ce{O3}$ are less than that of $\ce{O2}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The bond order of $\ce{O3}$ is greater than $\ce{O2}$, but the bond enthalpy of $\ce{O3}$ is less than $\ce{O2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\

Ozone has two equivalent resonance structures, each with one $\ce{O=O}$ double bond and one $\ce{O-O}$ single bond. 

![CH0341](images/CH0341.png)

The true structure is a hybrid, giving each bond an order of $1.5.$ Molecular oxygen has a double bond, so its bond order is $2.$

Since bond order and bond strength are directly related, a higher bond order means a greater bond enthalpy. The bond enthalpy of the $\ce{O-O}$ bond in $\ce{O3}$ $(364 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1})$ is therefore less than that of $\ce{O2}$ double bond, $(498 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}).$ Both the bond order and bond enthalpy of $\ce{O3}$ are less than those of $\ce{O2}.$
