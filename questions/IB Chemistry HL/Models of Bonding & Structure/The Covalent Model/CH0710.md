# CH0710

Some of the electrons in molecule **A** are delocalized, giving rise to resonance structures. Molecule **A** features a bond angle $\theta = 119\degree.$

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\
Resonance occurs when a double bond is shared over two or more positions.

Molecules in option A and B show resonance, as each $\ce{S=O}$ and $\ce{N=O}$ bonds, contain a second pair of electrons that can be shared across the $\ce{S-O}$ and $\ce{N-O}$ bonds formally drawn as single bonds. Resonance makes the $\ce{S-O}$ bonds in A and N-O bonds in B equivalent; they are partway between single and double bonds (bond order 1$\frac{1}{2}$ in $A$ and 1$\frac{2}{3}$ in B).

There is no resonance in C, as it contains no double bonds, while D shows no resonance because $\ce{C-H}$ double bonds cannot form.

In option A, $\theta$ is close to 120$\degree$ because the S has one lone pair and two bonding domains, giving a trigonal planar structure. However, the lone pair repels more strongly than the bonding pairs, causing θ to be less than 120$\degree$. The value of $\theta$ in D is also slightly less than 120$\degree$ since the second bonding pair in the $\ce{C=O}$ bond provides additional repulsion, while $\theta = 120\degree$ in B because there are three equivalent bonding domains.

Molecule C has two lone pairs and two bonding pairs around the central O atom, so it has the same geometry as water: $\theta$ should be close to 104.5$\degree$ (the true value is 103$\degree$).

This question is categorized as very difficult, as the student must deduce the compounds' electronic structures and draw on their knowledge of both resonance and VSEPR to identify the molecule with the features described.
