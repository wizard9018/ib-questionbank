# CH0380

Ethene has the following structural formula:

![CH0380](images/CH0380.png)

Which orbitals of the carbon atoms are used to form the $\ce{C-H}$ and $\ce{C=C}$ bonds of ethene?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}

  | | $\ce{C-H}$ bonds|$\ce{C=C}$ bond

  | :-: | :-: |:-: |

   A.$\hspace{1em}$|$sp^2$ orbitals|$p$ orbitals

   B.$\hspace{1em}$|$p$ orbitals|$sp^2$ and $p$ orbitals

   C.$\hspace{1em}$| $sp^2$ orbitals| $sp^2$ and $p$ orbitals

   D.$\hspace{1em}$| $p$ orbitals| $sp^2$ orbitals

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The $sp^2$ orbitals in ethene are used to form $\sigma$ bonds, while the non$-$hybridized $p$ orbitals overlap laterally to form the $\ce{C-C}\ \pi$ bonds.

The $\ce{C=C}$ bond contains one $\sigma$ and one $\pi$ bond, so it involves both $sp^2$ and $p$ orbitals.

This question is moderately difficult as the student must recognize the difference between $sp^2$ and $p$ orbitals and understand how they contribute to both $\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds without the aid of orbital diagrams.
