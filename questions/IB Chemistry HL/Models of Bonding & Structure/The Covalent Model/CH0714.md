## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Hydrogen bond(ing). $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    The $\ce{O-H}$ bond of ethanoic acid is polar, because the O atom is more electronegative than the H atom and attracts the bonding electron density more strongly towards itself. The $\delta +$ H atom of the $\ce{O-H}$ bond is electrostatically attracted to the $\delta -$ O atoms of neighboring molecules, either in the $\ce{O-H}$ bonds or the similarly polar $\ce{C=O}$ bonds. This interaction is stronger than other dipole-dipole interactions, such as the interactions between two $\ce{C=O}$ groups, or the weaker London forces between non-polar bonds such as $\ce{C-C}$ and $\ce{C-H}$. The strength of hydrogen bonds is is due to the small size and high charge density of the H atom and the large polarity of the $\ce{O-H}$ bond. Molecules linked by hydrogen bonds, therefore, have much higher melting and boiling points than molecules with less polar covalent bonds, or polar bonds that do not involve an H atom.

2.
    1.  The boiling points (of **A** and **B**) are lower (than the value for ethanoic acid), $\checkmark$

        because only London / dispersion / induced dipole forces can form between the molecules **AND** these are (much) weaker (than hydrogen bonds). $\checkmark$

        *Do **not** award M2 if the interactions formed by molecules of **A** and **B**  are not named;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the interactions are labeled van der Waals forces only;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the strength of the interactions of **A** and **B** are not compared with those of ethanoic acid.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Compounds **A** and **B** are alkanes and do not contain polar bonds. Thus, the molecules can only interact via London forces, the weakest type of van der Waals force. London forces occur when a small random shift in the electron density of an atom or molecule creates transient regions of partial positive and negative charge. Electrons in neighboring molecules are repelled by the transient $\delta -$ charge or attracted by the transient $\delta +$ charge, so shift to produce a new induced dipole that can interact with other molecules. Since these interactions involve smaller dipoles and less dense accumulations of charge than hydrogen bonds, less thermal energy is needed to overcome them, and the boiling point of the material is lower.

    2.  **A** has a higher boiling point, $\checkmark$

        because molecules of **A** are linear / less branched/unbranched, **AND** this allows them to pack more tightly together / a larger area is accessible for (London) interactions / branching reduces the number of (London) interactions that can form / branching increases the (average) separation of molecules. $\checkmark$

        *Accept converse statements for **B**;\
        Award M2 for a diagram clearly illustrating the closer molecular packing of **A**;\
        Award M2 for similar descriptions indicating that branching reduces the ability of molecules to form close interactions;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the structures of the two molecules are not compared;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the effect of the structural differences on intermolecular forces is not stated.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        The strength of London forces increases with the size of the molecule, as a larger electron cloud can be polarized more easily, and there is a larger area over which interactions can form. The shape of the molecule is also important, however, as molecules that can pack together more tightly can form shorter interactions over a larger area. Branched molecules have lower boiling points because they are less able to fit together to form a close-packed structure, so their atoms are forced to remain further apart and cannot interact as strongly.

    3.  Interactions between water molecules must be broken when a solute is added / a solution is formed; $\checkmark$

        Water forms hydrogen bonds **AND** (dipole-induced dipole/van der Waals) interactions of the solutes/alkanes / **A** and **B** with water are much weaker. $\checkmark$

        *Award M1 for a diagram illustrating the loss of water-water interactions when a solute is added;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the interactions formed between water molecules are not named;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the strengths of the hydrogen bonds in water and the water-solute interactions are not compared.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Like ethanoic acid, water contains polar $\ce{O-H}$ bonds. Thus, molecules of water interact through hydrogen bonding. When a material dissolves in water, some of these hydrogen bonds must be broken and replaced by interactions between water and the solute. Dissolution of **A** and **B** is impossible because they do not contain dipoles, so they can only form weak dipole-induced dipole interactions with the water molecules.

3.
    1.  Lone pairs drawn correctly on all O atoms of at least one molecule; $\checkmark$

        Structures linked by double-headed resonance arrow; $\checkmark$

        Change in positions of C=O bond, **AND** negative charge indicated clearly. $\checkmark$

        ![CH0714-MS](images/CH0714-MS.png)

        *Award [1] if only one resonance structure is drawn;
        Accept answers in which the $\ce{CH3}$ group is abbreviated or omitted from one or both structures;\
        Accept answers in which the two resonance structures are drawn in different orientations, but the two O atoms are clearly labeled;\
        Do **not** award M1 if lone pairs are drawn incorrectly for one structure;\
        Do **not** award M3 if charges are omitted or incorrectly positioned in either structure.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Resonance occurs when a double bond can occur in two or more positions. In ethanoic acid, either O atom can form a double bond with the C atom, but only one C=O bond is possible overall. Therefore, the second pair of bonding electrons is shared between the two bond positions, forming two bonds halfway between a single and double bond (the order of each bond is 1.5). Likewise, the negative charge is shared between the two O atoms, such that the charge on each atom is -0.5. This resonance is represented by drawing both possible structures linked by a double-headed arrow, which indicates that the real bonding is a mixture of the versions drawn.

    2.  The electrical conductivity increases (when ethanoic acid dissolves), $\checkmark$\
because the ions in solution can move/carry a current (in response to an applied voltage). $\checkmark$

        *Do **not** award M2 if the ion is not identified as the source of increased conductivity;\
        Do **not** award M2 if the mobility of the ion is not described.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        An electrical current can be conducted through a solution only if it contains mobile ions. The ions move between the electrodes in response to the applied potential difference. The dissolution of ethanoic acid increases the number of mobile ions in the solution, increasing the electrical conductivity.
