# CH0276

Which row(s) show(s) the correct interactions between the substances and water?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Hydrogen bonding is an intermolecular force that occurs when a hydrogen atom directly bonded to a highly electronegative atom (such as oxygen, nitrogen, or fluorine) interacts with the lone pair of electrons on another electronegative atom. For hydrogen bonding to occur, there must be:

-   A hydrogen bond donor, which is a hydrogen atom directly attached to an electronegative atom.
-   A hydrogen bond acceptor, which is an electronegative atom with a lone pair of electrons capable of interacting with the hydrogen.

$\ce{H2O},$ water:

Water serves as both a hydrogen bond donor and acceptor. Its two hydrogen atoms are bonded to oxygen, allowing each to act as a hydrogen bond donor. Additionally, the oxygen atom in water has two lone pairs of electrons, making it a hydrogen bond acceptor. This dual capability enables water to form extensive hydrogen$-$bonding networks with itself and other molecules capable of hydrogen bonding.

$\ce{CH3CH2COOH},$ propanoic acid:

Propanoic acid has a carboxyl group $-\ce{COOH}$, which contains both a hydrogen atom bonded to oxygen (making it a hydrogen bond donor) and a lone pair on the oxygen atom (making it a hydrogen bond acceptor). As a result, propanoic acid can form hydrogen bonds both with itself and with water. The claim that it cannot form hydrogen bonds with itself is incorrect, as intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the hydroxyl and carbonyl groups are common in carboxylic acids.

$\ce{CH3OCH3},$ methoxymethane:

Methoxymethane has an oxygen atom with lone pairs, making it a hydrogen bond acceptor. However, it lacks a hydrogen atom bonded to an electronegative atom, so it is not a hydrogen bond donor. Thus, methoxymethane cannot form hydrogen bonds with itself. However, it can form hydrogen bonds with water, where water acts as the hydrogen bond donor, and the lone pairs on the oxygen atom in dimethyl ether act as the acceptor. The claim that it cannot form hydrogen bonds with water is incorrect.

$\ce{CH3CH2OH},$ ethanol:

Ethanol contains a hydroxyl group, where the hydrogen atom is a hydrogen bond donor and the oxygen atom is a hydrogen bond acceptor. This allows ethanol to form hydrogen bonds both with itself and with water. The claims in the table that ethanol forms hydrogen bonds with both water and itself are correct.
