# CH1159

Which statements about silicon dioxide are correct?

- I.$\hspace{1.em}$It has a high melting point.
- II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Covalent bonds hold atoms together in a three$-$dimensional covalent network.
- III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Every silicon atom is bonded to two oxygen atoms in its structure.

- A.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Silicon dioxide, $\ce{SiO2}$ is a three$-$dimensional covalent solid. Statement II is correct.

![CH1159](images/CH1159.png)

Every Si is covalently bonded to four oxygens in a tetrahedral structure, and every oxygen is covalently bonded to two silicon atoms in a bent arrangement due to the presence of two lone pairs in every oxygen. Statement III is incorrect.

Covalent network solids have higher melting points than molecular structures, as atoms are strongly held together in a giant three$-$dimensional lattice structure. Statement I is correct.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must describe silicon dioxide's structure to determine the validity of these statements.
