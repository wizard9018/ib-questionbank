---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

A $\pi$ bond is formed by the sideways overlap of unhybridized $p$ orbitals on adjacent atoms. Unlike $\sigma$ bonds, which result from head$\text{--to--}$head overlap, $\pi$ bonds provide weaker overlap and contribute to double and triple bonds in molecular structures.

The most common orbital interaction that leads to the formation of a $\pi$ bond is the $p\text{--}p$ overlap observed in organic compounds that have double or triple bonds. For example, the  $\ce{C=C}$ bond in ethene $(\ce{C2H4}).$ In this case, each carbon forms three $\sigma$ bonds using the $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals, while unhybridized $p$ orbitals on each carbon overlap side$\text{--to--}$side to form a $\pi$ bond.
