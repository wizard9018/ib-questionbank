# CH0280

\[Maxiumum mark: 6\]

Chloroalkanes can be produced in substitution reactions between alkanes and chlorine in the presence of UV light. The reaction between methane and chlorine produces a mixture of four chloroalkanes $\ce{CCl4}$, $\ce{CHCl3}$, $\ce{CH2Cl2}$, and $\ce{CH3Cl}.$

The table below shows the boiling points for $\ce{CCl4}$, $\ce{CHCl3}$, $\ce{CH2Cl2}$, and $\ce{CH3Cl}.$

| Substance | Boiling Point ($\mathbf{^\circ}$ C) |
| :---: | :---: |
| $\ce{CCl4}$ | 76.7 |
| $\ce{CHCl3}$ | 61.2 |
| $\ce{CH2Cl2}$ | 39.6 |
| $\ce{CH3Cl}$ | $-24.2$ |

1.  Outline with a reason the relative strengths of the London forces from $\ce{CCl4}$ to $\ce{CH3Cl}$.   [2 marks]

2.  A student predicted that the boiling point for $\ce{CCl4}$ would be significantly lower than that of the other chloroalkanes. Suggest the reasoning for the student's prediction.  [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  London forces get weaker $\checkmark$

    $M_r$/size of molecule/number of electrons decreases $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    The higher the boiling point, the stronger the London forces.

2.  $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is polar$.\,\checkmark$

    $\ce{CHCl3}$, $\ce{CH2Cl2}$, and $\ce{CHCl3}$ are all asymmetrical AND have dipole-dipole interactions/a permanent dipole/a net dipole moment$\,.\checkmark$

    $\ce{CCl4}$ is symmetrical AND only has London forces between molecules$\,.\checkmark$

    Dipole-dipole interactions are stronger than London forces; therefore the student would expect a lower boiling point for $\ce{CCl4}\,.\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    All the above reasons justify the student's reasoning. But, $\ce{CCl4}$ has the highest boiling point, and that is due to its high molecular mass, which explains very strong London forces that overcome the dipole$-$dipole interactions in the other 3 chloroalkanes.
