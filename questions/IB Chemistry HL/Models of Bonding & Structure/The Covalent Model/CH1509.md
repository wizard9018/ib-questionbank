# CH1509

The formula and boiling points of two derivatives of methane are given in the table below:

|Substance|Formula|Boiling point /$^\circ$C|
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
methanol | $\ce{CH3OH}$ | 65
difluoromethane | $\ce{CH2F2}$ | $-52$

Which of the statements below explains the difference in boiling point between methanol and difluoromethane?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The bond dipoles cancel in difluoromethane but not in methanol.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Hydrogen is attached directly to oxygen in methanol.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Difluoromethane is a symmetrical molecule.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Difluoromethane has weaker dispersion forces than methanol.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To solve this question, we need to identify the relative strengths of intermolecular forces in the two compounds. We also need to recall the nature of intermolecular forces in each compound. Even though difluoromethane has the stronger London dispersion forces due to its higher relative molecular mass, ethanol has the stronger intermolecular interaction, namely hydrogen bonding.

![CH1509](images/CH1509.png)

Hydrogen attached directly to the electronegative oxygen atom in methanol allows for hydrogen bonding between the molecules. This is not the case for difluoromethane. Both compounds have a net dipole moment as the bond dipoles do not cancel.
