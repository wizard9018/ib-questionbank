# CH2037

Which chemical species has the highest boiling point?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$hexane, $\ce{C6H14}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$ethanoic acid, $\ce{CH3COOH}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$propanone, $\ce{CH3COCH3}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$chloromethane, $\ce{CH3Cl}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The boiling point of a molecule is determined by assessing the intermolecular forces between the molecules. To change from a liquid to a gas, the intermolecular forces must be overcome. Therefore, the stronger the forces, the higher the boiling point. There are three main intermolecular forces to consider:

*London dispersion forces:* These are weak forces caused by instantaneous dipoles resulting from the temporary shifts in electron density. They are present in all molecules, regardless of polarity.

*Dipole$-$dipole forces:* These are attractive forces between molecules with a permanent dipole (polar molecules). These are stronger than London dispersion forces.

*Hydrogen bonding:* These are strong dipole$\text{--}$dipole interactions that occur when a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to a highly electronegative atom (fluorine, oxygen, or nitrogen) interacts with a lone pair on an F, O, or N atom in a neighboring molecule.  Hydrogen bonds are the strongest type of intermolecular force.
