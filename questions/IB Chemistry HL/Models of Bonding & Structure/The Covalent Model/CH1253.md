# CH1253

Which combination is correct for the molecular geometries below?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
 | | $\mathbf{TeCl_4}$ | $\mathbf{ICl_3}$ | $\mathbf{BrF_5}$ |
 | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
 | A. | tetrahedral | pyramidal | Trigonal bipyramidal |
 | B. | tetrahedral | T-shaped | Trigonal bipyramidal |
 | C. | See-saw | T-shaped | Square pyramidal |
 | D. | See-saw | pyramidal | Square pyramidal |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The molecular geometries can be deduced from the Lewis structures of these molecules.

Lewis structures should be built considering valence electrons only. Electrons are shared so that atoms attain stability. Mostly, atoms will achieve an octet in their outer shell by sharing, although some exceptions apply (electron-deficient species and expanded octets).

For example, in $\ce{TeCl_4},$ tellurium is in group 16, so its atom has 6 valence electrons, whereas chlorine is in group 17, presenting 7 valence electrons.

The total number of valence electrons is $6 + (4 \times 7) = 34.$

Tellurium is the central atom in the molecule, and chlorine atoms will bond to it.

Each chlorine atom shares a pair of electrons with the tellurium atom. This makes a total of 8 electrons out of the 34 used.

Then, for each chlorine atom, we add 3 non-bonding pairs of electrons so that each achieves their octet.

This makes a total of 8 + 24 = 32 electrons used. Two electrons out of 34 are left.

We add them as a non-bonding pair of electrons for Te. Te now has an expanded octet of 10 electrons.

The Lewis structures of all molecules can be observed below.

![CH1253](images/CH1253.png)

The VSEPR theory can be applied, and the electron domains can be determined to figure out the molecular shape.

$\ce{TeCl_4}$ has five electron domains (4 bonding and 1 non-bonding) $\rarr$ See-saw

$\ce{ICl_3}$ has five electron domains (3 bonding and 2 non-bonding) $\rarr$ T-shaped

$\ce{BrF_5}$ has six electron domains (5 bonding and 1 non-bonding) $\rarr$ Square pyramidal

This question is categorized as hard as students must deduce the Lewis structures of molecules, analyze the number of electron domains, and label them bonding/non-bonding to assign the molecular geometry.
