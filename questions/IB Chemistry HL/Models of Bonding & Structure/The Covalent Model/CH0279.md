# CH0279

**Maximum mark: 6**

The graph below shows the boiling points of some of the group 15 hydrides.

![4.4-279](images/4.4-279.png)

1. State the weakest intermolecular force present in all of the hydrides. [1 mark]

2. Explain the trend in boiling point from $\ce{PH3}$ to $\ce{SbH3}$. Refer to all intermolecular forces present. [2 marks]

3. Predict the boiling point for $\ce{NH3}$ by drawing a bar on the graph. [1 mark]

4. Justify your predicted boiling point for $\ce{NH3}$ in part (c).[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. London (dispersion) forces $\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

 All simple molecular structures have London forces. These forces are the weakest ones among intermolecular forces.

2. London forces AND dipole-dipole interactions present $\textit{(ALLOW van der Waal's)}$ $\,\checkmark$

 As $M_r$ increases/molecule becomes larger/number of electrons increases, London forces/van der Waal’s forces become stronger/increase.$\,\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

 Boiling point increases from $\ce{PH3}$, to $\ce{SbH3}$. While the hydrides have London forces and dipole-dipole interactions, the increasing strength of London forces causes this trend.

3. Bar drawn shorter than $\ce{SbH3}$ $(> - 90\,{}^\circ \text{C}).\,\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

 In addition to London forces and dipole-dipole force, $\ce{NH3}$ can form hydrogen bonds with other ammonia molecules. Therefore, it has a greater boiling point than the rest of the hydrides.

4. $\ce{NH3}$ can form hydrogen bonds/hydrogen bonding present$.\,\checkmark$

 Hydrogen bonds are stronger than/require more energy to overcome than London forces/dipole-dipole interactions/van der Waal's forces$.\,\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

 $\text{Hydrogen bonding} > \text{dipole-dipole} > \text{London forces}$.
