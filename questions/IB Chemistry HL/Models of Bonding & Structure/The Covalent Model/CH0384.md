# CH0384

The structural formula of pyrrole is shown.

![14.2-384](images/14.2-384.png)

What does the labelled bond angle suggest is most likely about the hybridization of the N atom?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The trigonal planar molecular geometry indicates the hybridization is $sp^2$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry indicates the hybridization is $sp^2$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The trigonal planar molecular geometry indicates the hybridization is $sp^3$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry indicates the hybridization is $sp^3$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The bond angle is close to 120° so the geometry is trigonal planar and the hybridization is $sp^2.$

If the molecule were trigonal pyramidal and the hybridization $sp^3$, a bond angle would be close to 107 $^\circ$, as in ammonia.

The $sp^2$ angle is slightly larger than expected as the $\ce{C-N-C}$ angle (110 $^\circ$) must be close to the natural internal angle of a planar pentagon (108 $^\circ$) to minimize ring strain. The sum of the angles around the N atom remains 360 $^\circ$, as expected for a planar geometry.

This question is hard as the student must recall the geometry and bond angle of an $sp^2$ species and apply this knowledge to an unfamiliar molecule with a slightly distorted geometry.
