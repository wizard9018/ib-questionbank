# CH0506

The molecule $\ce{BH2F}$ contains a polar $\ce{B-F}$ bond. One atom in the bond has a partial positive electrostatic charge $(\delta+)$ and the other has a partial negative charge $(\delta-)$.

One representation of the bonding in $\ce{BH2F}$ is shown. In addition to possessing partial electrostatic charges, the $\ce{B}$ and $\ce{F}$ atoms in this structural formula have non$-$zero formal charges.

![14.1-CH0506](images/CH0506.png)

What are the formal and partial electrostatic charges of the $\ce{B}$ atom in this structural formula?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
  | | $\enskip$Formal charge of B$\enskip$ | $\enskip$Partial charge of B$\enskip$ |
  | :-: | :-: | :-: |
  | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | $+1$ | $\delta +$ |
  | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | $+1$ | $\delta -$ |
  | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | $-1$ | $\delta +$ |
  | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | $-1$ | $\delta -$ |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To calculate the formal charge use the expression:

$FC$ = valence $\ce{e-}$ $-$ nonbonding $\ce{e-} - \dfrac{1}{2} \times $ bonding $\ce{e-}$

Boron is in Group 13 of the periodic table and has 3 valence electrons. In the molecule $\ce{BH2F}$ boron forms 4 bonds (or has 8 bonding electrons) and has no lone pairs.

$FC = 3 - 0 - \dfrac{1}{2} \times 8 = -1$

Fluorine is in Group 17 of the periodic table and has 7 valence electrons. In the molecule $\ce{BH2F}$ fluorine forms 2 bonds (or has 4 bonding electrons) and has two lone pairs (or 4 nonbonding electrons). The formal charge of F is

$FC = 7 - 4 - \dfrac{1}{2}\times 4 = + 1$.

$\ce{F}$ is more electronegative than $\ce{B},$ so $\ce{B}$ has a partial positive charge and $\ce{F}$ a partial negative charge.

$\ce{BX3}$ compounds are the key examples of incomplete octets in this course, so should be familiar to most students. The question is of moderate difficulty; however, comparing formal and partial charges may confuse some students.
