Itaconic acid has the following skeletal structural formula.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    Ethane-1,2-diol is a chain of two $\ce{CH2}$ groups (indicated by the eth- suffix) with a hydroxyl group on each end of the chain (indicated by the 1,2-diol suffix). Each hydroxyl group can react with a carboxylic acid group of itaconic acid to produce an ester group, with water as the by-product:

    ![CH1069_Image4](images/CH1069_Image4.png)

    Since the alcohol contains two hydroxyl groups and the acid contains two carboxylic acid groups, the condensation reaction produces a polymer. The structure of the polymer is indicated by brackets surrounding the repeat unit structure, with bonds extending beyond the brackets to indicate the continuation of the structure. A subscript after the brackets denotes the number of units in the polymer chain.

    In practice, this simple repeat unit is unlikely, as itaconic acid can be incorporated in two different orientations. Many polymer chains are possible, including those without a repeating structure.

2.  (Esterification is a) reversible / equilibrium reaction $\checkmark$

    because the product/ester groups can be hydrolyzed/react with water (to regenerate the starting materials) $\checkmark$

    *Award M2 for equation showing the reaction of an ester group with water; \
    Do **not** award M1 unless words “reversible” or “equilibrium” used.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    In the presence of an acid catalyst, an ester group can react with water to produce a carboxylic acid and alcohol.

    This hydrolysis reaction reverses the esterification reaction, regenerating the starting materials.

    During the polymerization of itaconic acid and 1,2-ethanediol, both reactions happen at the same time: the reactants and products form a dynamic equilibrium, with some polymer chains becoming longer as others becoming shorter. When the position of equilibrium is reached, some of the itaconic acid molecules may not be incorporated into a polymer.

3.  The alkene group can undergo addition polymerization / (free) radical addition **OR** the $\ce{C=C}$ bonds react together to produce $\ce{C-C}$ single bonds $\checkmark$

    where the polymers are linked together (by $\ce{C-C}$ bonds), **AND** this makes the polymers less flexible / less able to deform (under an applied force) $\checkmark$

    *Award M1 for equation showing the addition reaction of two alkene groups; \
    Award M2 for diagram showing response of polymer to applied force before and after crosslinking; \
    Do **not** award M2 unless answer describes effect of addition on polymer size / connectivity; \
    Do **not** award M2 unless answer links addition reaction to behavior of polymer under applied force.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    A material is brittle if its molecules cannot move or deform in response to an applied force: the force instead causes bonds between the molecules to break, so the material shatters. Polymer **P** consists of long chains of monomers with no branches, so the molecules can easily deform and move over each other when a force is applied. However, the alkene groups can undergo addition reactions, wherein the $\ce{C=C}$ bonds are replaced by $\ce{C-C}$ bonds:

    ![CH1069_Image6](images/CH1069_Image6.png)

    These addition reactions cause the polymer chains to become linked together by new single bonds. The chains are therefore more rigidly held in position and less able to deform in response to a force: the material becomes more brittle.
