# CH0962

**Maximum mark: 4**

Iron (II) oxide, $\ce{FeO}$, is one of the most abundant compounds in Earth's crust at nearly 9% by mass. 

*Refer to Sections 9 and 17 of the data booklet for this question.*

1.  Calculate the electronegativity difference between iron and oxygen in $\ce{FeO}$. [1 mark]

2.  Use $\Delta \chi$ and the average $\chi$ to identify the bond type that exists between iron and oxygen in $\ce{FeO}$ [2 marks]

3.  Iron can also exist as a $\ce{Fe^3+}$ ion, resulting in iron (III) oxide when bonded to oxygen. Describe how the bond type differs in $\ce{FeO}$ compared to $\ce{Fe2O3}$. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.6  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    The student must first collect the following information from the data booklet:

    -   $\chi$ (Fe) = 1.8

    -   $\chi$ (O) = 3.4

    Next, the student must calculate the electronegativity difference between the atoms and the average electronegativity:

    $\text{Electronegativity difference} = \Delta \chi = \vert \chi (\ce{O}) - \chi (\ce{Fe}) \vert = 3.4 - 1.8 = 1.6 $

2.  Average electronegativity = 2.6  $\checkmark$

    Polar covalent  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    The student must use the $\Delta \chi$ from the previous question and calculate the average electronegativity:

    $\text{Average electronegativity} = \sum \chi = \dfrac{\chi (\ce{O}) + \chi (\ce{Fe})}{2} = \dfrac{3.4 + 1.8}{2} = 2.6$

    Using the van Arkel-Ketelaar triangle, this correlates to a polar covalent bond.

3.  bond type would not differ/would be the same/is also polar covalent $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Bond type is dependent on the type of atoms that are bonded, not the quantity of those atoms.

    Therefore, only the bond between a single Fe and O needs to be analyzed for both $\ce{FeO}$ and $\ce{Fe2O3}$  resulting in the same difference in electronegativity and average electronegativity. This results in the same bond type.
