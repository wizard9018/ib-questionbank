# CH0590

**Maximum mark: 7**

Compound **A** has the empirical formula $\ce{CH2O}$ and the same molecular mass as propan$-$1$-$ol, to 1 decimal place.
Compound **A** reacts with propan$-$1$-$ol to produce an ester **B**. The reaction is performed in the presence of sulfuric acid.

1.  Draw an equation for this reaction, including the full structural formulas of compounds **A** and **B**. [4 marks]

2.  State the role of sulfuric acid in the reaction. [1 mark]

3.  Suggest why the percentage yield of the reaction is much lower than 100$\%$, even if neither reactant is in excess. [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The empirical formula of compound A is $\ce{CH2O}$ $(M_r = 30.03).$ Since A has the same molar mass as propan$-$1$-$ol $(Mr = 60.11),$ its molecular mass is about $60 \textrm{\ g\,mol}^{-1},$ so

    $n = \dfrac{M_{molecular}}{M_{empirical}} \approx \dfrac{60}{30} = 2$

    giving a molecular formula $\ce{C2H4O2.}$ The carboxylic acid with this formula is ethanoic acid, $\ce{CH3COOH},$ which is consistent with $\ce{CH2O}$ as the empirical formula.

    In an esterification reaction, the $\ce{-OH}$ group from the carboxylic acid and an H from the alcohol combine to form water, and the remaining fragments join through a $\ce{-COO-}$ linkage. Thus, propan$-$1$-$ol $(\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH})$ and ethanoic acid $(\ce{CH3COOH})$ react to form propyl ethanoate $\ce{(CH3CH2CH2OCOCH3})$ and water, as shown in the structural equation above.

2. catalyst $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Sulfuric acid accelerates the reaction by donating hydrogen ions to the $\ce{C=O}$ group of ethanoic acid. This makes it easier for the $\ce{C=O}$ bond to break when the acid reacts with propan$-$1$-$ol, and for water to be eliminated as a byproduct. However, the products are the same regardless of whether acid is present, so the acid is not consumed in the reaction. A compound that increases the rate of reaction without undergoing a net chemical change itself is termed a catalyst.

3.  reaction achieves equilibrium\
    **OR**\
    ester can be hydrolysed / react with water\
    **OR**\
    the equilibrium constant is (relatively) small $\checkmark$

    some ester is converted back to reactants\
    **OR**\
    backward reaction rate is significant \
    **OR**\
    yield increases until the forward and backward reaction rates are equal $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    In a reversible reaction, the product molecules can react to regenerate the starting materials. Esterification is usually a reversible process, as the ester product can react with water to produce the original alcohol and carboxylic acid. This backward reaction, known as hydrolysis, is also catalysed by sulfuric acid. The yield only increases until the reaction reaches its position of equilibrium, where the rates of the forward and backward reactions are equal.
