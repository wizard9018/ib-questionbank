# CH1062

The skeletal structural formulas of glycerol and ethanoic acid are shown.

![CH1062_Image1](images/CH1062_Image1.png)

Glycerol and ethanoic acid both have melting points above $15\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$, but they react to produce a compound with a melting point of $-7\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

Why does the product of this reaction display a lower melting point than the reactants?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The product is more branched, so the molecules pack together less easily

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The product contains no $\ce{O-H}$ bonds, so it cannot form hydrogen bonds

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The product is larger, so it has a smaller surface-area-to-volume ratio for dispersion forces

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The product contains no acid or base groups, so it cannot form ionic bonds

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The melting point of a material depends on the amount of heat energy needed to break bonds between the molecules.

The hydroxy groups in glycerol and the carboxyl group in ethanoic acid are both capable of forming strong hydrogen bonds, so the melting points of these materials are relatively high.

The carboxyl group of ethanoic acid can react with the hydroxy groups in glycerol to form an ester via a condensation reaction. The triester, drawn below, contains no $\ce{O-H}$ bonds and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds. It only exhibits weaker dipole-dipole interactions:

![CH1062_Image2](images/CH1062_Image2.png)

Although more branched than the reactants, the product remains highly symmetrical, so the molecules can still pack together effectively to form a solid.

The product molecule is larger than the reactant, so it displays stronger dispersion forces. However, these forces cannot compensate for the loss of hydrogen bonding. Thus, the melting point of the product is lower.
