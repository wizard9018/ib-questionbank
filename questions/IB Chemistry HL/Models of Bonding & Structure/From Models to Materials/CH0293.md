# CH0293

**Maximum mark: 6**

1. Explain how the size of the atoms in a covalent bond can affect the relative attraction to electrons and hence the predict the relative polarity of the $\ce{C-Cl}$ and $\ce{C-I}$ bond. [3 marks]

2. Calculate the electronegativity differences and determine the covalent character of the bonding in $\ce{Cl2,}$  $\ce{HCl,}$ and $\ce{NaCl}.$  [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. as size increases, attraction between nucleus and valence electrons (electronegativity) decreases $\checkmark$\
    bond polarity is associated with difference in electronegativity, so polarity decreases $\checkmark$ \
    $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is more polar than $\ce{C-I}$ bond $\checkmark$

   $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
   Electronegativity is the relative attraction between the nucleus and a bonding pair of electrons.  As the size of the atom increases, the bonding electrons are more weakly attracted to the nucleus and the electronegativity decreases.  Iodine is less electronegative than chlorine.

   When bonding to carbon, the polarity of the bond is due to the difference in electronegativity of the atoms.  The difference in electronegativity between carbon and chlorine will be larger than the difference in electronegativity between carbon and iodine so the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is more polar than the $\ce{C-I}$ bond.

2. $\ce{Cl2}$ 100 \% covalent, $\Delta \chi = 0  \: \checkmark$\
    $\ce{HCl}$ $\approx 75\%$ covalent, $\Delta \chi = 3.2-2.2=1.0 \: \checkmark$\
    $\ce{NaCl}$ $\approx 25\%$ covalent, $\Delta \chi=3.2-0.9=2.3 \: \checkmark$

   $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
   The van Arkel$-$Ketelaar triangle in Section 17 of the data booklet can be used to estimate the covalent / ionic nature of a compound.
