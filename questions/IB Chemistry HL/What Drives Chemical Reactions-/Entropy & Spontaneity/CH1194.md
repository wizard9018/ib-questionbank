# CH1194

Consider the chemical reaction below.

$\ce{2 KClO3(s) -> 2 KCl(s) + 3 O2(g)}$

Which combination is correct?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | $\mathbf{\Delta S^{\minuso}_{system}}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta H^{\minuso}_{R}}$ | Bonding in $\mathbf{KClO_3}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A. | Positive | Positive | Ionic |
    | B. | Positive | Negative | Ionic |
    | C. | Negative | Positive | Covalent |
    | D. | Negative | Negative | Covalent |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Breaking up the solid will require energy, resulting in an endothermic reaction (positive enthalpy change). When bonds are broken, a lot of energy is needed. This reaction is endothermic.

In addition, from a solid, a gas is released, increasing the disorder in the system and, thus, increasing the entropy of the system.

Moreover, the reactant is made of potassium and chlorate ions ($\ce{K+}$ and $\ce{ClO3-}).$ These ions form a crystal lattice structure in an ionic structure.

This question is categorized as hard as students should identify the nature of the bonding in the reactant, understand that the reaction is endothermic, and deduce the entropy change.
