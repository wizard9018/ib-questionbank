# CH1263

What are the signs for $\Delta H^{\minuso}$ and $\Delta S^{\minuso}$ for a spontaneous reaction at any given temperature?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
 | | $\mathbf{\Delta H^{\minuso} / kJ\, mol^{-1}}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta S^{\minuso} / J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$ |
 | :-: | :-: | :-: |
 | A. | Negative | Positive |
 | B. | Negative | Negative |
 | C. | Positive | Positive |
 | D. | Positive | Negative |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The change in standard Gibbs free energy, $\Delta G^{\minuso},$ is calculated as $\Delta G^{\minuso} = \Delta H^{\minuso} - T \Delta S^{\minuso}.$

At a constant pressure, any chemical change is spontaneous if $\Delta G^{\minuso} < 0.$

Negative value at any temperature results from the combination of a negative enthalpy change $(\Delta H^{\minuso})$ and positive entropy change $(\Delta S^{\minuso}).$
