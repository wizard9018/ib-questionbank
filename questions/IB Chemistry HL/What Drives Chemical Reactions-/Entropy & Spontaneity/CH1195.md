# CH1195

The standard entropy change for the complete combustion of an unknown compound is *x* in $\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1},$ and its standard enthalpy change is *y* in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}.$

What is the Gibbs free energy change, in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1},$ for this chemical reaction at $10\,{}^\circ \text{C}?$

-   A. $\hspace{1em}y - 10x$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}y - 283 x$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}y - \dfrac{283 x}{1000}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}x - \dfrac{283 y}{1000}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = \Delta H^{\minuso} - T\Delta S^{\minuso}$

The temperature should be expressed in Kelvin $(273 + 10 = 283\,\text{K})$ and the entropy change should be converted from $\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ to $\text{kJ K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ by dividing by 1000.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students should apply the Gibbs equation, including a correct unit conversion in its procedure.
