# CH2135

Consider the chemical reaction below.

$\ce{CaCO3}\textrm{(s)}\longrightarrow\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)}$

Which combination is correct for this thermal decomposition?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta S>0$ and the reaction is spontaneous at any given temperature.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta S>0$ and the reaction is spontaneous at high temperatures only.
-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta S>0$ and the reaction is spontaneous at low temperatures only.
-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\Delta S<0$ and the reaction is spontaneous at any given temperature.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The student must identify this reaction as a thermal decomposition, meaning that energy is required for the reaction to occur, so $\Delta H>0.$ In addition, the amount of gas produced increases the entropy of, so $\Delta S>0.$

The spontaneity of the reaction depends on the change in the Gibbs free energy which must be negative:

$\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S < 0$

As $\Delta S>0$ and the absolute temperature is positive, we can conclude that $T\Delta S<0.$ But as $\Delta H > 0$, the temperature must be high to obtain a negative $\Delta G.$

This question is categorized as hard as the student must work out relationships from the given equation.
