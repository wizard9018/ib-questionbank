# CH0181

**Maximum mark: 5**

Ethanol can be produced through the fermentation of glucose:

$\ce{C6H12O6(aq) -> 2 C2H5OH(l) + 2 CO2(g)}$

1.
    1.  Predict whether the entropy change for this reaction is positive or negative. Explain your reasoning. [1 mark]

    2.  The absolute entropy for glucose is +209.2 J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$. Using section 13 (*Section 12$-$2016 Syllabus*) of the data booklet, calculate the entropy change for this reaction. [1 mark]

2.  The enthalpy of formation of glucose is $-1273.3\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$. Using section 13 (*Section 12$-$2016 Syllabus*) of the data booklet, calculate the enthalpy change for this reaction. [1 mark]

3.  Using your answers to (a)(ii) and (b), calculate the Gibbs free energy change at $298\,\text{K}$. [2 marks]

*Important note:\
The section numbers and values mentioned in the video solution are specific to the 2016$-$2024 syllabus. The written question has been updated to the current syllabus.*

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Positive: the reaction produces 2 mol of gas from an aqueous
        reactant $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        Entropy predictions run on the moles of gas before and after. Here an
        aqueous reactant, with no gas at all, becomes two moles of carbon
        dioxide gas, and gases have far more accessible microstates than
        liquids or solutions, so this gain dwarfs every other change in the
        equation. The two moles of liquid ethanol barely move the total.
        Saying only "more moles of product" does not earn the mark; the
        reasoning must name the gas.

    2.  $\Delta S = {+540.8\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        The entropy change is the sum for the products minus the sum for the
        reactants, with each absolute entropy multiplied by its coefficient:

        $\Delta S = 2S^{\minuso}(\ce{C2H5OH}) + 2S^{\minuso}(\ce{CO2}) - S^{\minuso}(\ce{C6H12O6})$

        $\Delta S = 2(+161) + 2(+214) - 209.2 = +540.8\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

2.  $\Delta H = {-70.7\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    The enthalpy change follows the same products-minus-reactants structure,
    now built on enthalpies of formation:

    $\Delta H = 2\Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\ce{C2H5OH}) + 2\Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\ce{CO2}) - \Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\ce{C6H12O6})$

    $= 2(-278) + 2(-394) - (-1273.3) = -70.7\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

3.  $\Delta S = +0.5408 \textrm{\ kJ K}^{-1} \textrm{\,mol}^{-1}$ / units must match $\checkmark$\
    $\Delta G = -232\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\:\checkmark$

    *Award [2] for correct answer.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Convert $\Delta S$ to kJ before combining: $+540.8$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$
    becomes $+0.5408$ kJ K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$, matching the kJ mol$^{-1}$ of
    $\Delta H$. Then substitute into the Gibbs equation from section 1:

    $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S = -70.7 - (298)(0.5408) = -232\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$
