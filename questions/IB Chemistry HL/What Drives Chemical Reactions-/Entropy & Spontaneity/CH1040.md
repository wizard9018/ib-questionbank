# CH1040

What are the signs of entropy changes associated with this reaction?

$\ce{CO2(g) -> CO2(s)}$

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | $\mathbf{\Delta S_{surroundings}}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta S_{system}}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A. | $+$ | $+$ |
    | B. | $+$ | $-$ |
    | C. | $-$ | $+$ |
    | D. | $-$ | $-$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
When considering entropy changes, it is important to separate what happens to the system from what happens to the surroundings. In this reaction, carbon dioxide is deposited from the gas phase into the solid phase. Gases have very high entropy because their particles move freely in many directions, while solids are much more ordered. Moving from gas to solid therefore represents a large decrease in the entropy of the system, so $\Delta S_{\text{system}}$ is negative.

The surroundings, however, respond to the enthalpy change of the process. Deposition of carbon dioxide is exothermic, releasing heat into the surroundings. Entropy change in the surroundings is proportional to $-\Delta H_{\text{system}}/T.$ For an exothermic reaction, the surroundings become more disordered and $\Delta S_{\text{surroundings}}$ is positive.

Thus, for $\ce{CO2(g) → CO2(s)}$, the system loses entropy while the surroundings gain it. The correct signs are $\Delta S_{\text{system}} < 0$ and $\Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} > 0$.

This question is categorized as hard. Students must interpret the deposition in terms of entropy changes. In addition, they are expected to relate the entropy change of the system and that of the surroundings.
