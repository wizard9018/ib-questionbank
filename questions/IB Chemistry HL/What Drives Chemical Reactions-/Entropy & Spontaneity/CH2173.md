# CH2173

[Maximum marks: 12]

Isooctane (2,2,4$\text{--}$trimethylpentane) is a reference fuel used to determine the octane ratings of gasoline. Its complete combustion reaction is given below.

$\ce{C8H18(l) + \frac{25}{2} O2(g) -> 8 CO2(g) + 9 H2O(l)}$

1.  The table lists standard enthalpies of formation, $\Delta H_f^{\minuso},$ for some species in the reaction above.

    | Substance | $\ce{C8H18(l)}$ | $\ce{O2(g)}$ | $\ce{CO2(g)}$ | $\ce{H2O(l)}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | $\Delta H_f^{\minuso}$ / kJ mol$^{-1}$ | $-54.4$ | $\text{---}$ | $-394$ | $-286$ |

    1.  Outline why no value is listed for $\ce{O2(g)}.$ [1 mark]

    2.  Using the values in the table above, determine the value for the standard enthalpy change of complete combustion of isooctane in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}.$ [3 marks]

2.  The table lists standard entropy, $S^{\minuso},$ values:

    | Substance | $\ce{C8H18(l)}$ | $\ce{O2(g)}$ | $\ce{CO2(g)}$ | $\ce{H2O(l)}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    $S^{\minuso}$ / J mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ | $329$ | 205 | 214 | 70 |

    1.  Outline why the absolute entropy of $\ce{O2(g)}$ is not zero. [1 mark]

    2.  Calculate the standard entropy change, $\Delta S,$ for the complete combustion of isooctane in $\text{J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}.$  Justify the sign for the change in entropy. [3 marks]

3.  Based on your answers above, calculate the standard free energy change, $\Delta G^{\minuso}$ in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1},$ for the combustion of isooctane at 25$^\circ\text{C}.$ [2 marks]

4.  Deduce the temperature, in $^\circ\text{C},$ above which the reaction becomes nonspontaneous. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  $\Delta H_f^{\minuso}$ of elements in their standard state is zero $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The standard state enthalpy of formation is the enthalpy change when one mole of a substance is formed from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions (298 K, 100 kPa).  Since diatomic oxygen gas is the standard state of oxygen at standard atmospheric temperature and pressure, its $\Delta H_f^{\minuso}$ value is zero.

    2.  $\Delta H_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{CO2}) + 9\times  \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{H2O})\big] - \big[\Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{C8H18}) + 12.5\times \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{O2})\big]\ \checkmark$

        $\Delta H_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times (-394) + 9\times (-286)\big] - \big[-54.4 + 12.5\times 0\big]\ \checkmark$

        $\Delta H_{comb}^{\minuso} =  -5672\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\ \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The enthalpy change for a reaction can be calculated from the standard enthalpies of formation using the relationship

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = \sum \Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\text{products}) - \sum \Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\text{reactants})$

        where each enthalpy of formation is multiplied by the coefficient in the balanced chemical equation. For the combustion of isooctane,

        $\Delta H_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{CO2}) + 9\times  \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{H2O})\big] - \big[\Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{C8H18}) + 12.5\times \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{O2})\big]$

        $\Delta H_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times (-394) + 9\times (-286)\big] - \big[-54.4 + 12.5\times 0\big]$

        $\Delta H_{comb}^{\minuso} =  -5672\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

2.
    1.  Absolute entropy is zero at 0 K for a pure crystalline solid $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        Entropy is a measure of disorder, and $\ce{O2(g)}$ has disorder due to its molecular motion even in its standard state.

    2.  $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times 214 + 9\times 70\big] - \big [329 + 12.5\times 205\big]\ \checkmark$

        $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} = -549.5\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}\ \checkmark$

        $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} < 0$ because there are fewer moles of gas on the product side (12.5 *vs.* 8) $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The entropy change for a reaction can be calculated from the absolute entropies using the relationship

        $\Delta S^{\minuso} = \sum S^{\minuso}(\text{products}) - \sum S^{\minuso}(\text{reactants})$

        where each absolute entropy is multiplied by the coefficient in the balanced chemical equation.  For the combustion of isooctane,

        $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times S^{\minuso}(\ce{CO2}) + 9\times  S^{\minuso}(\ce{H2O})\big] - \big[S^{\minuso}(\ce{C8H18}) + 12.5\times S^{\minuso}(\ce{O2})\big]$

        $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} = \big[8\times 214 + 9\times 70\big] - \big [329 + 12.5\times 205\big]$

        $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} = -549.5\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$

        The $\Delta S_{comb}^{\minuso} < 0$ because there are fewer moles of gas on the product side; there are 12.5 moles of oxygen gas on the reactant side and only 8 moles of carbon dioxide gas on the product side. Gases have much higher absolute entropies than other states of matter because  their particles are widely spaced and move freely, resulting in a greater number of possible microstates and therefore higher disorder.

3.  $\Delta G_{comb}^{\minuso} = -5672 - (298)(-0.5495)\ \checkmark$

    $\Delta G_{comb}^{\minuso} = -5508\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The Gibbs free energy change of the reaction can be calculated using

    $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$

    The temperature must be converted to Kelvin and the entropy change must be converted to $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$ for the units to work out.

    $\Delta G_{comb}^{\minuso} = -5672\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} - (25 + 273)\text{ K}\left(-549.5\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}\times \dfrac{1\text{ kJ}}{1000\text{ J}}\right)$

    $\Delta G_{comb}^{\minuso} = -5508\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

4.  $\Delta H^{\minuso} - T\Delta S^{\minuso} < 0 $
 **OR** $T > \dfrac{\Delta H^{\minuso}}{\Delta S^{\minuso}}\ \checkmark$

    $T > 10049^\circ\text{C}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The reaction becomes nonspontaneous with $\Delta G^{\minuso} > 0.$ Rewriting $\Delta G^{\minuso}$ in terms of the enthalpy and entropy changes allows us to determine the temperature at which the reaction will becom nonspontaneous.

    $\Delta G^{\minuso} > 0$

    $\Delta H^{\minuso} - T \Delta S^{\minuso} > 0$

    $T < \dfrac{\Delta H^{\minuso}}{\Delta S^{\minuso}}$

    Note that we reverse the inequality when dividing by $\Delta S^{\minuso}$ because $\Delta S^{\minuso}$ is negative. Plugging in the values,

    $T < \dfrac{5672\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}{0.5495\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}} = 10322\text{ K}$

    $T = 10322 - 273 = 10049^\circ\text{C}$
