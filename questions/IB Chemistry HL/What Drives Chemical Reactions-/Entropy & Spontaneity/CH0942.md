# CH0942

**Maximum mark: 10**

Propanoic acid is commonly used as an antifungal medicine because of its mild acidic nature. The unbalanced equation of the combustion reaction is:

$\ce{\_\_\_ C2H5COOH(l) +  \_\_\_ O2(g) -> \_\_\_ CO2(g) + \_\_\_ H2O(l)}$

| | Standard entropy, $\\$ $\mathbf{S^{\minuso}}$ / J mol$\mathbf{^{-1}}$ K$\mathbf{^{-1}}$ |
| :-: | :-: |
| $\ce{O2(g)}$| $205$ |
| $\ce{C2H5COOH(l)}$ | $191$ |

1.
    1.  Balance the reaction for one mole of propanoic acid. [1 mark]

    2.  How many moles of propanoic acid will react with 3.50 g of oxygen? [2 marks]

2.  The standard enthalpy change for the combustion of propanoic acid is $-1528$ kJ mol$^{-1}$.

    1.  Using data from Section 13 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, calculate the standard enthalpy of formation, $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f$, of propanoic acid. [3 marks]

    2.  Using data from Section 13 of the IB Chemistry data booklet and part b(i), calculate the standard entropy change, $\Delta S^{\minuso}$ for the reaction at 25 $^\circ$C. [2 marks]

    3.  Calculate $\Delta G^{\minuso}$ of the reaction and deduce if the reaction is spontaneous at 25 $^\circ$C. [2 marks]

    $\ $

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  $\ce{C2H5COOH + \frac{7}{2} O2 -> 3 CO2 + 3 H2O}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Step 1: balance the equation with whole numbers.

        $\ce{2 C2H5COOH + 7 O2 -> 6 CO2 + 6 H2O}$

        Step 2: Divide the equation by 2 to get the reaction for 1 mole of propanoic acid.

        $\ce{\frac{2}{2} C2H5COOH + \frac{7}{2} O2 -> \frac{6}{2} CO2 + \frac{6}{2} H2O}$

        $\ce{C2H5COOH + \frac{7}{2} O2 -> 3 CO2 + 3 H2O}$

    2.  $n(\ce{O2}) = 0.109\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

        $0.031\,\text{mol } \ce{C2H5COOH}$ reacts with 3.50 g $\ce{O2}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Step 1: Convert the mass of oxygen to moles using the relationship $n = \dfrac{m}{M}$.

        $n(\ce{O2}) = \dfrac{3.50\,\text{g}}{32.00\,\text{g mol}^{-1}} = 0.109\,\text{mol}$

        Step 2: Use the molar ratio of propanoic acid to oxygen $(1 : \frac{7}{2})$ to find the moles of $\ce{C2H5COOH}$.

        $n(\ce{C2H5COOH}) =  0.109\,\text{mol }\ce{O2}\times \dfrac{1\,\text{mol }\ce{C2H5COOH}}{\frac{7}{2}\,\text{mol }\ce{O2}} = 0.031\,\text{mol }\ce{C2H5COOH}$

2.
    1.  $\Delta H^{\minuso} = \left[3\Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(CO2)} + 3\Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(H2O)}\right] - \left[ \Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(C2H5COOH)} + \frac{7}{2} \Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(O2)} \right]\,\checkmark$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f (\ce{C2H5COOH}) = \left[3(-393.5) + 3 (-285.8) \right] - (-1528)\,\checkmark$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{C2H5COOH}) = - 509.9\,\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Step 1: Write the formula for the standard enthalpy of formation and substitute values from the question.

        $\begin{aligned}
        \Delta H^{\minuso} &= \sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_f (products) - \sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_f (eactants) \\
        &= \left[3\Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(CO2)} + 3\Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(H2O)} \right] - \left[ \Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(C2H5COOH)}  + \frac{7}{2}  \Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(O2)} \right]
        \end{aligned}$

        Step 2: Rearrange the equation to isolate $ \Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(C2H5COOH)}.$ The enthalpy of formation of oxygen is zero because it is an element in its standard state.

        $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{C2H5COOH}) = \left[ 3\Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(CO2)} + 3\Delta H^{\minuso}_f \ce{(H2O)} \right] - \Delta H^{\minuso}$

        Step 3: Substitute in the values and solve for the standard enthalpy of formation of propanoic acid, $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{C2H5COOH}).$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{C2H5COOH}) = \left[ 3(-393.5) + 3 (-285.8) \right] - (-1528) = - 509.9\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

    2.  $\Delta S^{\minuso} = \left[ 3(214) + 3(70) \right] - \left[ 191  + \frac{7}{2} (205) \right]\,\checkmark$

        $\Delta S^{\minuso} = - 56.5\,\text{J mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Write the formula for the standard entropy change, substitute values from the question, and solve.

        $\begin{aligned}
        \Delta S^{\minuso} &= \sum S^{\minuso} (products) - \sum S^{\minuso} (reactants) \\
        &= \left[ 3S^{\minuso} \ce{(CO2)} + 3S^{\minuso} \ce{(H2O)} \right] - \left[ S^{\minuso} \ce{(C2H5COOH)} + \frac{7}{2} S^{\minuso}\ce{(O2)} \right] \\
        &= \left[ 3(214) + 3(70) \right] - \left[ 191 + \frac{7}{2} (205) \right] \\
        &= -56.5\,\text{J mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}
        \end{aligned}$

    3.  $\Delta G^{\minuso} = -1528 - 298\times 0.0565 = -1511\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

        spontaneous;  $\Delta G^{\minuso} < 0$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Step 1: Convert the temperature to Kelvin and the entropy to kJ mol$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$.

        $T = 25 + 273 = 298\,\text{K}$

        $\Delta S^{\minuso} = -56.5\,\text{J mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}\times \dfrac{1\,\text{kJ}}{1000\,\text{J}} = -0.0565\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}$

        Step 2: Write the formula for Gibbs free energy, substitute known and calculate the unknown.

        $\begin{aligned}
        \Delta G^{\minuso} &= \Delta H^{\minuso} - T \Delta S^{\minuso} \\
        &= -1528\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1} - 298\,\text{K}\times - 0.0565\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1} \\
        &= -1511\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}
        \end{aligned}$

        The reaction is spontaneous because $\Delta G^{\minuso}$ is negative.
