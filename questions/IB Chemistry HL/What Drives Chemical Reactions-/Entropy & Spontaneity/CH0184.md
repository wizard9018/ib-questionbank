# CH0184

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the following reaction:

$\ce{C2H4(g) + H2(g) -> C2H6(g)}$

| $\Delta H$         | $-136\, \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ |
| :-:                | :-: |
| $\Delta S$         | $-121\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ |
| $\enskip \Delta G$ at $298\,\text{K}\enskip$ | $-99.9\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ |

1.
    1.  Explain why the enthalpy of formation of $\ce{H2}$ is $0\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain why the entropy change for this reaction is negative. [1 mark]

2.  Explain how a reaction can be spontaneous with a negative entropy change. [2 marks]

3.  Calculate the temperature at which this reaction is no longer spontaneous. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  Enthalpy of formation is the enthalpy change when a compound is formed from its constituent elements. Since $\ce{H2}$ is an element, its enthalpy of formation is therefore $0\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. $\checkmark$

    2.  The reaction goes from two moles of gas to one mole of gas. This means there is a decrease in disorder, and therefore, a decrease in entropy. $\checkmark$

2.  $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$

    For a reaction to be spontaneous, the $\Delta G$ must be negative. $\checkmark$

    $\Delta H < T\Delta S$

    **OR**

    $-136\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1} < -36.1\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    In this reaction, we see that the $\Delta H$ and the $\Delta S$ are both negative. The entropy of the surroundings will increase as heat is released from the system (exothermic) but the entropy of the system will decrease ($\Delta S < 0$).  For the reaction to be spontaneous, the effect of releasing heat to the surroundings must be larger than the effect of decreasing the entropy of the system.  We require that $\Delta G < 0$ so $\Delta H - T\Delta S  < 0$, which can be rearranged to find that $\Delta H < T \Delta S$.

    Numerically, solve for the value of  $T\Delta S$,

    $T\Delta S = \left(298\,\text{K}\right)\left(-121\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\times \dfrac{1\,\text{kJ}}{1000\,\text{J}}\right) = -36.1\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

    According to our inequality above, $\Delta H(-136\, \text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1})$ must be more negative.  As it is, $\Delta G$ is negative.

3.  The reaction is no longer spontaneous when $\Delta G\geq 0$ $\checkmark$

    Consider the point $\Delta G = 0$

    $\begin{aligned}
    \Delta H - T\Delta S &= 0 \\
    T &= \dfrac{\Delta H}{\Delta S} = \dfrac{-136\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1} - 0}{\left(\dfrac{-121\text{J mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}}{1000\,\textrm{J kJ}^{-1}}\right)}= 1124\,\text{K}\,\checkmark \\
    \end{aligned}$

    The reaction will be non-spontaneous above 1124 K.
