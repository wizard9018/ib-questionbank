# CH2120

Which of the following are correct statements about fuels?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | A fuel stores chemical potential energy. |
    | II. | Fuel combustion produces carbon dioxide and water only. |
    | III. | Fuels must be liquids at room temperature and pressure. |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$II only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The student must understand that fuels can be burned to produce energy. Thermal energy can be produced from the chemical potential energy stored in the bonds of the fuel, so statement I is correct.

The combustion of a hydrocarbon can be complete, producing carbon dioxide and water, or incomplete, leading to carbon soot or carbon monoxide formation rather than carbon dioxide only.  The combustion of hydrogen gas (as in a fuel cell) produces only water. As a result, statement II is incorrect.

Fuels can be gases (hydrogen, methane), liquids (octane, diesel), or solids (coal, wood). Statement III is incorrect.

This question is categorized as medium as the student must understand the concept of fuel, biofuel, the types of combustions and the conversion of chemical energy into another type of energy while burning a fuel.
