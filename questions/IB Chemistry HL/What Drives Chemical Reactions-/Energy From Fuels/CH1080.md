# CH1080

Based on the experimental data below, collected by calorimetry, which fuel will have the largest molar enthalpy of combustion? Assume 10 g of each fuel was burned and the initial temperature of water was $25.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ for all trials.

$\textbf{Final Temperature of Water} \mathbf{\ ({}^\circ C)}$

| Trial | Fuel A | Fuel B | Fuel C | Fuel D |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $1$ | $38.2$ | $37.5$ | $39.0$ | $36.8$ |
| $2$ | $39.8$ | $37.9$ | $40.5$ | $37.1$ |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Fuel C

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The molar enthalpy of combustions cannot be determined, but Fuel D has the highest energy density.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The molar enthalpy of combustions cannot be determined, but Fuel C has the highest specific energy.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Fuel D

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

In this experiment, 10 g of each fuel is burned, and the temperature change of the water reflects the heat released by combustion. However, without knowing the molar masses of the fuels, it is impossible to calculate the molar enthalpy of combustion $\Delta H_c$ which is defined as the energy released per mole of fuel burned. Instead, the data allows us to determine which fuel has the highest specific energy (energy released per gram of fuel burned), based on the observed $\Delta T.$

$\textbf{Final Temperature of Water} \mathbf{\ ({}^\circ C)}$

| Trial | Fuel A | Fuel B | Fuel C | Fuel D |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $1$ | $38.2$ | $37.5$ | $39.0$ | $36.8$ |
| $2$ | $39.8$ | $37.9$ | $40.5$ | $37.1$ |
| Average | $39.0$ | $37.7$ | $39.8$ | $37.0$ |
| $\Delta T$ | $14.0$ | $12.7$ | $14.8$ | 12.0 |

Fuel C produces the highest average temperature change, indicating it releases the most energy per gram burned; it has the highest specific energy. Calculating the energy density would require the density of the fuels; it measures heat released per unit volume.
