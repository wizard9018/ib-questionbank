# CH2131

What is a similarity between fuel cells and rechargeable cells?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Both use oxygen in the air as the oxidizing agent that drives the chemical reaction.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Both require a constant supply of fuel to work properly.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Both involve reversible reactions.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Both can convert chemical energy into electrical energy.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The student must understand the differences between fuel and rechargeable cells.

In fuel cells, fuel is supplied at the anode for oxidation. Electrons flow through an external circuit and produce electric current while move toward the cathode. At the cathode, oxygen from the air is reduced to water.

Rechargeable batteries involve a reversible reaction so that the electron flow from anode to cathode can be reversed if electric current is supplied to recharge the battery. The electrolyte in rechargeable cells keeps the electrodes separate but allows ion flow in both directions. Rechargeable cells do not require oxygen as the oxidizing agent.

The similarity is that both can convert chemical energy into electrical energy as electrons flow from anode to cathode.

This question is categorized as hard as the student must understand the differences between fuel and rechargeable cells in detail.
