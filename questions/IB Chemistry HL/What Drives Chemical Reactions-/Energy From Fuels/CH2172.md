# CH2172

[Maximum marks: 13]

Biogas is a fuel produced by the anaerobic digestion of animal waste. It contains approximately 60% methane and 40% carbon dioxide by volume.

1.  Define the term biofuel and explain why biogas is classified as one. [2 marks]

2.  State two advantages and one disadvantage of using biogas instead of fossil fuels. [3 marks]

3.  Biogas is often referred to as carbon neutral.

    1.  Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of methane, including state symbols. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain why biogas is considered carbon neutral. [2 marks]

4.  State one condition that would favor the incomplete combustion of methane, and list two products of incomplete combustion. [2 marks]

5.  The energy released by combustion of biogas and coal was measured under standard conditions:

    | Fuel | Mass burnt / g | Energy released / J | Density / kg m$^{\mathbf{-3}}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | Biogas | 1000 | 42500 | 1.15 |
    | Coal | 200.0 | 6200 | 1500 |

    1.  Calculate the specific energy (in $\text{J g}^{-1})$ of each fuel. [1 mark]

    2.  Calculate the energy density (in $\text{J m}^{-3})$ of each fuel. Report your answer in scientific notation using three significant figures. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  biofuels are derived directly from living matter $\checkmark$

    biogas is produced from the decay of animal waste $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

    A biofuel is a fuel derived from recently living biological material, typically plants or animal waste, that can be used as a renewable source of energy. Biogas is classified as a biofuel because it is produced through the anaerobic decomposition of organic matter such as animal manure.

2.  Any two of the following advantages:
    -   Renewability $\checkmark$
    -   Energy security $\checkmark$
    -   Waste reduction $\checkmark$
    -   Lower environmental impact $\checkmark$
    -   Reduced greenhouse emissions $\checkmark$

    Any one of the following disadvantages:
    -   Lower energy density $\checkmark$
    -   Less existing infrastructure $\checkmark$
    -   Higher cost $\checkmark$
    -   Impurities $\checkmark$
    -   Limited scalability $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    Biofuels are renewable, produced from animal or plant waste, and minimize pollution. They offer many advantages over petroleum.

    -   ***Renewability:*** Biogas is produced from organic waste, which is continuously generated, making it a renewable energy source unlike finite fossil fuels.
    -   ***Energy security:*** Biogas can be produced locally, reducing dependence on imported fossil fuels. Biogas digesters can provide clean energy in rural areas, reducing reliance on wood or kerosene.
    -   ***Waste reduction:***  Using animal manure or food waste to produce biogas helps reduce the volume of waste that would otherwise contribute to environmental pollution.
    -   ***Lower emissions:*** Biogas combustion generally releases fewer pollutants (like sulfur dioxide or particulates) compared to coal or diesel.
    - ***Carbon neutrality:*** The carbon dioxide released during biogas combustion was originally absorbed by plants, meaning it does not contribute significantly to net greenhouse gas emissions.

    There are also disadvantages compared to petroleum$\text{--}$based fuels.

    -   ***Lower energy density:*** Biogas has a lower specific energy than fossil fuels like natural gas or gasoline, meaning more is needed to produce the same amount of energy.
    -   ***Less existing infrastructure:*** Biogas is a gas at room temperature and can be harder to compress, store, or transport compared to liquid fuels.  Much existing infrastructure relies on solid and liquid fuels.
    -   ***Higher cost:***  Setting up biogas digesters and infrastructure may require significant upfront investment.
    -   ***Impurities:*** Raw biogas may contain impurities like hydrogen sulfide or moisture, which must be removed to prevent corrosion and inefficiency
    - ***Limited scalability:***  Biogas production may not be sufficient to meet large-scale industrial energy demands without vast feedstock supplies.

3.
    1.  $\ce{CH4(g) + 2 O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2 H2O(l)}\ \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The complete combusiton of a hydrocarbon produces carbon dioxide and liquid water.

    2.  $\ce{CO2}$ released during combustion is equal to $\checkmark$

        $\ce{CO2}$ absorbed from atmosphere by plants during photosynthesis $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$

        Biogas is considered carbon neutral because it participates in a closed carbon cycle. When organic matter is formed, carbon dioxide from the atmosphere is absorbed and converted into organic compounds through photosynthesis. This organic matter then undergoes anaerobic digestion to produce biogas containing methane. During combustion of the biogas, the same amount of $\ce{CO2}$ that was originally removed from the atmosphere is released back into it. Therefore, there is no net increase in atmospheric $\ce{CO2}$ concentration, making biogas carbon neutral over its complete lifecycle.

4.  ***condition:*** limited oxygen/air supply $\checkmark$

    ***products:*** **TWO** of

    -   C(s) / soot $\checkmark$
    -   CO(g) / carbon monoxide $\checkmark$
    -   $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ / water $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    Incomplete combustion occurs when there is insufficient oxygen available for complete oxidation of the fuel. Under these conditions, methane cannot be fully oxidized to carbon dioxide and water. Instead, the carbon in methane is only partially oxidized, leading to the formation of carbon monoxide (CO) and solid carbon (soot). Water is still produced from the hydrogen atoms in methane.

5.
    1.  Specific energy of biogas = $42.50\text{ J g}^{-1}$

        **AND**

        Specific energy of coal = $31.00\text{ J g}^{-1}\ \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The student must understand how to calculate a fuel's specific energy.

        $\text{Specific energy} = \dfrac{\text{energy released from fuel}}{\text{mass of fuel consumed}}$

        $\text{Specific energy of biogas} = \dfrac{42500\text{ J}}{1000\text{ g}} = 42.50\text{ J g}^{-1}$

        $\text{Specific energy of coal} = \dfrac{6200\text{ J}}{200.0\text{ g}} = 31.00\text{ J g}^{-1}$

    2.  Energy density of biogas = $4.89\times 10^4\text{ J m}^{-3}\ \checkmark$

        Energy density of coal = $4.65\times 10^7\text{ J m}^{-3}\ \checkmark$

        *Award M1 for correct values and M2 for correct significant figures and scientific notation.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The student must understand how to calculate a fuel's specific energy.

        $\text{Energy density} = \dfrac{\text{energy released from fuel}}{\text{mass of fuel consumed}}\times \text{density of fuel}$

        $\text{Energy density of biogas} = \dfrac{42500\text{ J}}{1.000\text{ kg}}\times 1.15\text{ kg m}^{-3}= 4.89\times 10^4\text{ J m}^{-3}$

        $\text{Energy density of coal} = \dfrac{6200\text{ J}}{0.2000\text{ kg}}\times 1500\text{ kg m}^{-3} = 4.65\times 10^7\text{ J m}^{-3}$
