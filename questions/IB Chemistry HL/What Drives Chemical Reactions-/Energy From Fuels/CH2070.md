# CH2070

[Maximum marks: 8]

Alcohols are an important alternative fuel source.

1.  Outline two features of a homologous series. [2 marks]

2.  Ethanol, a widely used biofuel, can be produced from various plant sources such as corn and sugarcane.

    1.  Explain why biofuels are carbon neutral. [1 mark]

    2. Outline another advantage and one disadvantage of biofuels. [2 marks]

3.  Methanol can be used in fuel cells.

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

    1.  State the reactions that occur at the anode and cathode in a direct methanol fuel cell. [2 marks]

    2.  Describe an advantage of the methanol fuel cell over the hydrogen fuel cell. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Any ***two*** of the following:

    -   same general formula $\checkmark$
    -   differ by a $\ce{CH2}$ $\checkmark$
    -   same chemical reactivity $\checkmark$
    -   gradual trend in their physical properties $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    Members of a homologous series have the same general formula, differ by a $\ce{CH2},$ have the same chemical reactivity because they contain the same functional groups, and show gradation in their physical properties which scale with molecular mass.

2.
    1.  carbon in the fuel is from $\ce{CO2}$ via photosynthesis, no net change in $\ce{CO2}\ \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Biofuels are carbon neutral because the carbon in the fuel came from carbon dioxide in the air. In the first step, photosynthesis converts water and carbon dioxide into a simple sugar. The sugar is then fermented into a biofuel, like ethanol. When the ethanol is combusted, the carbon is returned to the atmosphere as $\ce{CO2}.$

    $\ce{\textcolor{red}{\textbf{6 } \ce{CO2}} + 6 H2O ->[photosynthesys] C6H12O6 + 6 O2}$

    $\ce{C6H12O6 ->[fermentation] \textcolor{red}{\textbf{2 } \ce{CO2}} + 2 CH3CH2OH}$

    $\ce{2 CH3CH2OH + 6 O2 ->[combustion] \textcolor{red}{\textbf{4 }\ce{CO2}} + 6 H2O}$

    2.  Any ***one*** of the following advantages:

        -   renewable energy source $\checkmark$
        -   sustainable $\checkmark$
        -   many raw materials can produce it $\checkmark$
        -   biodegradable $\checkmark$
        -   reduce dependence on imported oil / fossil fuels $\checkmark$
        -   can use existing infrastructure $\checkmark$

        Any ***one*** of the following disadvantages:

        -   land and food competition $\checkmark$
        -   high water and resource (fertilizer, pesticide) use $\checkmark$
        -   monocultures / less diversity in crops $\checkmark$
        -   deforestation $\checkmark$
        -   high cost of production compared to fossil fuels $\checkmark$
        -   lower energy density than fossil fuels $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        Biofuels offer a promising alternative to fossil fuels due to their renewability, lower carbon emissions, and ability to be integrated into existing fuel infrastructure. They can help reduce dependence on non$\text{--}$renewable energy sources while offering biodegradability and reduced toxicity, making them more environmentally friendly in certain applications. However, biofuel production comes with significant challenges, including competition for farmland, high water consumption, and potential deforestation, which can offset their environmental benefits. Additionally, lower energy density and high production costs can make biofuels less efficient and economically viable compared to fossil fuels.

3.
    1.  anode: $\ce{H2O + CH3OH -> CO2 + 6 H+ + 6 e-}\ \checkmark$\
        cathode: $\ce{4 H+ + 4 e- + O2 -> 2 H2O}\ \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further explanation:}}$\
        The net reaction is the combustion of methanol. The overall balanced chemical equation can be determined by balancing the electrons lost at the anode in the oxidation of methanol with the electrons gained at the cathode with the reduction of atmospheric oxygen gas.

        $\begin{aligned}
        ( & \ce{H2O + CH3OH &-> CO2 + 6 H+ + 6 e-} & & )\times 2 \\
        ( & \ce{4 H+ + 4 e- + O2 &-> 2 H2O} & & )\times 3 \\ \hline
        & \ce{2 CH3OH + 3 O2 &-> 2 CO2 + 4 H2O}
        \end{aligned}$

    2.  Any ***one*** of

        -   higher energy density $\checkmark$
        -   existing infrastructure for liquid fuel $\checkmark$
        -   easier transport / storage of liquid fuel $\checkmark$
        -   easy to produce from biomass $\checkmark$
        -   safer to handle $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        A key advantage of the methanol fuel cell over the hydrogen fuel cell is that methanol is a liquid at room temperature, making it easier to store, transport, and handle compared to hydrogen, which requires high$\text{--}$pressure tanks or cryogenic storage. This makes methanol fuel cells more practical for portable and transportation applications, such as in electric vehicles and backup power systems.
