# CH2487

Which reaction has an enthalpy change equal to the standard enthalpy change of formation, $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{{\minuso}},$ of propanone, $\ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C3H4}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}3\ce{\,C}\textrm{(s)} + 3\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}6\ce{\,C}\textrm{(s)} + 6\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} + 3\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)} + \frac{9}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The standard enthalpy of formation is defined as the enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions $(298\textrm{ K},$ $100\textrm{ kPa}).$

Three requirements must be met simultaneously: the reactants must be elements in their standard states, exactly one mole of product must be formed, and the equation must be balanced.

The reaction $\ce{C3H4}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}$ fails because $\ce{C3H4}$ is a compound, not an element. The reactants in a formation equation must all be elements.

The equation $6\ce{\,C}\textrm{(s)} + 6\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}$ has the correct elements as reactants, but it produces two moles of propanone. Formation equations are always written per mole of product.

The equation involving $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ and $\ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)}$ as reactants also fails because these are compounds, not elements.

The correct formation equation uses carbon as graphite $\ce{C}\textrm{(s)},$ hydrogen gas $\ce{H2}\textrm{(g)},$ and oxygen gas $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)}$ to form exactly one mole of $\ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)}.$

$3\ce{\,C}\textrm{(s)} + 3\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{C3H6O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{2em}\Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\ce{C3H6O})$
