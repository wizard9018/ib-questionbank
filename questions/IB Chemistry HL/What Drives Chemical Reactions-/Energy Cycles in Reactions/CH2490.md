# CH2490

**Maximum mark: 8**

The manufacture of hydrogen cyanide, known as the Andrussow process, is shown below.

$2\ce{\,NH3}\textrm{(g)} + 3\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,CH4}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,HCN}\textrm{(g)} + 6\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

It is a catalytic reaction that occurs over a platinum$-$rhodium catalyst.

1. The table lists the standard enthalpies of formation, $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso},$ for some of the species in the reaction above.

    $\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

    | $\mathbf{Substance}$ | $\ce{NH_3}\textrm{(g)}$ | $\ce{O_2}\textrm{(g)}$ | $\ce{{CH_4}\textrm{(g)}}$ | $\ce{{HCN}\textrm{(g)}}$ | $\ce{{H_2O}\textrm{(l)}}$ |
    |:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
    | $\mathbf{\Delta H_\textbf{f}^{\minuso}\textbf{ / kJ mol}^{-1}}$ | $-46.0$ | | $-74.0$ | $+130$ | $-286$ |

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

    1. Outline why no value is listed for $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)}.$ [1 mark]

    2. Determine the value of $\Delta H^{\minuso},$ in $\textrm{kJ},$ for the reaction using the values in the table. [2 marks]

    3. Sketch the energy profile for the process, including the enthalpy change labelled on the graph. [2 marks]

2.  Some enthalpies of combustion are given below.

    | $\mathbf{Substance}$ | $\ce{NH3}\textrm{(g)}$ | $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)}$ | $\ce{CH4}\textrm{(g)}$ | $\ce{HCN}\textrm{(g)}$ | $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ |
    |:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
    | $\mathbf{\Delta H_\textbf{c}^{\minuso}\textbf{ / kJ mol}^{-1}}$ | $-383$ | $0$ | $-891$ | $-667$ | $0$ |

    $\mbox{}\\[-10pts]$

    1. Outline why the enthalpy of combustion of $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ is zero. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1. $\Delta H_f^{\minuso} = 0$ for elements in their standard state $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The enthalpy of formation is defined as the enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states. Since $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)}$ is already an element in its standard state, no formation process occurs and $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso} = 0\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

    2. $\Delta H^{\minuso} = [2(+130) + 6(-286)] - [2(-46.0) + 3(0) + 2(-74.0)] \: \checkmark$\
    $\Delta H^{\minuso} = -1216\textrm{ kJ}$ $\checkmark$

        *Award [2] for correct final answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The enthalpy change of a reaction is calculated from standard enthalpies of formation:

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of products}) - \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of reactants})$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = [2(+130) + 6(-286)] - [2(-46.0) + 3(0) + 2(-74.0)]$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = -1216\textrm{ kJ}$

2. 1. water cannot be oxidised further $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The standard enthalpy of combustion is the enthalpy change when one mole of a substance is completely burned in oxygen under standard conditions. Water is already a product of combustion. The hydrogen in water is already combined with oxygen, so no further combustion can occur. Therefore $\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}(\ce{H2O}) = 0.$

    2.  $\Delta H^{\minuso} = [2(-383) + 3(0) + 2(-891)] - [2(-667) + 6(0)]$ $\checkmark$\
    $\Delta H^{\minuso} = -1214\textrm{ kJ}$ $\checkmark$

        *Award [2] for correct final answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        When using combustion data, the enthalpy change formula is reversed compared to formation data:

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of reactants}) - \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of products})$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = [2(-383) + 3(0) + 2(-891)] - [2(-667) + 6(0)]$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = [-2548] - [-1334]$

        $\Delta H^{\minuso} = -1214\textrm{ kJ}$
