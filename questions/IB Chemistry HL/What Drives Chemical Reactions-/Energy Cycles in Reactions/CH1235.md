# CH1235

Consider the chemical reactions below.

$\begin{aligned}
\ce{2 B(s) + 3 H2(g) &-> B2H6(g)}\qquad &\Delta H &= - 36\,\text{kJ} \\
\ce{2 H2(g) + O2(g) &-> 2 H2O(l)}\qquad &\Delta H &= - 572\,\text{kJ} \\
\ce{4 B(s) + 3 O2(g) &-> 2 B2O3(s)}\qquad &\Delta H &= - 2548\,\text{kJ}
\end{aligned}$

What is the enthalpy change $\Delta H$ for the chemical reaction:
$\\[10pts]$

$\ce{B2H6(g) + 3 O2(g) -> B2O3(s) + 3 H2O(l)}$

-   A. $\hspace{1em}-3154\,\text{kJ}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}-3084\,\text{kJ}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}-1810\,\text{kJ}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}-2096\,\text{kJ}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Hess's law is used to figure out the unknown enthalpy as a combination of the other chemical equations.

$\begin{aligned}
\ce{2 B(s) + 3 H2(g) &-> B2H6(g)}\qquad &\Delta H &= - 36\,\text{kJ} \\
\ce{2 H2(g) + O2(g) &-> 2 H2O(l)}\qquad &\Delta H &= - 572\,\text{kJ} \\
\ce{4 B(s) + 3 O2(g) &-> 2 B2O3(s)}\qquad &\Delta H &= - 2548\,\text{kJ}
\end{aligned}$

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide" col3="hide"}
  | | | |
  | :-- | :-- | :-- |
  | Reverse the first reaction  | $\ce{B2H6(g) -> 2 B(s) + 3 H2(g)}$ | $+36\,\text{kJ}$ |
  | Multiply the second reaction by $\frac{3}{2}$ $\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{3 H2(g) + \frac{3}{2}O2(g) -> 3 H2O(l)}$ | $-858\,\text{kJ}$ |
  | Divide the third reaction by two | $\ce{2 B(s) + \frac{3}{2}O2(g) -> B2O3(s)}$ |  $-1274\,\text{kJ}$ |

$\begin{aligned}
\ce{B2H6(g) &-> 2 B(s) + 3 H2(g)}\qquad & & +36\,\text{kJ}\\
\ce{3 H2(g) + \tfrac{3}{2}O2(g) &-> 3 H2O(l)}\qquad & & -858\,\text{kJ} \\
\ce{2 B(s) + \tfrac{3}{2}O2(g) &-> B2O3(s)}\qquad & & -1274\,\text{kJ} \\\\
\ce{B2H6(g) + 3 O2(g) &-> B2O3(s) + 3 H2O(l)}
\end{aligned}$

$\Delta H = + 36\,\text{kJ} - 858\,\text{kJ} - 1274\,\text{kJ} = - 2096\,\text{kJ}$

This question is categorized as hard as the Hess's law should be applied to combine three chemical reactions.
