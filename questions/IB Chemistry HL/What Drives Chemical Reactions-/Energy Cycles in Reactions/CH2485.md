# CH2485

The Born$-$Haber cycle for $\ce{CaO}$ is shown below.

![CH2485](images/CH2485.png)

Which statement is correct?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$The lattice enthalpy of $\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)}$ is $3409\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$The enthalpy of formation of $\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)}$ is the same as the enthalpy of combustion of calcium.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}(590 + 1145)\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ corresponds to the second ionization energy of calcium.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The lattice enthalpy is calculated by summing the alternative route from $\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)}$ to $\ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O^{2-}}\textrm{(g)}:$

| $\mathbf{Step}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta H\textbf{ / kJ mol}^{-1}}$ |
|:--|:-:|
| $\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca}\textrm{(s)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)}$ | $+635$ |
| $\ce{Ca}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca}\textrm{(g)}$ | $+178$ |
| $\frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{O}\textrm{(g)}$ | $+249$ |
| $\ce{Ca}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-}$ | $+590$ |
| $\ce{Ca+}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-}$ | $+1145$ |
| $\ce{O}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{O-}\textrm{(g)}$ | $-141$ |
| $\underline{\ce{O-}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{O^{2-}}\textrm{(g)}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }$ | $\underline{+753\ \ \ }$ |
| ${\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O^{2-}}\textrm{(g)}}$ | $\Delta H_{latt}^{\minuso}$ |

Summing all steps:

$\Delta H_{latt}^{\minuso} = (635 + 178 + 249 + 590 + 1145 - 141 + 753)\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1} = 3409\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

The positive value confirms that breaking the lattice is endothermic, as expected. Statement I is correct.

The standard enthalpy of formation of $\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)}$ is the enthalpy change for $\ce{Ca}\textrm{(s)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)}.$ The standard enthalpy of combustion of calcium is the enthalpy change for the same equation — the "combustion" of a metal is its reaction with oxygen to form the oxide, which is identical to the formation of the metal oxide from its elements. Statement II is correct.

The second ionization energy of calcium is the energy required to remove the second electron:

$\ce{Ca+}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-}$

This corresponds to $\textrm{IE}_2 = +1145\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ alone. The sum $(590 + 1145) = 1735\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ represents the total energy needed to remove both electrons from $\ce{Ca}\textrm{(g)}$ to produce $\ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(g)},$ which is the sum of the first and second ionization energies, not the second ionization energy by itself. Statement III is incorrect.
