# CH2482

Which enthalpy change is the most negative?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Cl}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{Cl-}\textrm{(g)}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{K}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{K+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}2\ce{\,Cl}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cl2}\textrm{(g)}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{K+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Cl-}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{KCl}\textrm{(s)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Each option represents a step in the Born$-$Haber cycle for $\ce{KCl}.$ Their signs must be determined from the nature of each process.

The electron affinity of chlorine, $\ce{Cl}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{Cl-}\textrm{(g)},$ is exothermic. Adding an electron to a chlorine atom releases energy because the incoming electron is attracted to the nuclear charge. The value is $-349\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

The ionisation of potassium, $\ce{K}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{K+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-},$ is endothermic. Removing an electron requires energy to overcome the nuclear attraction, so all ionisation energies are positive $(+418\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ for potassium$).$ This option can be eliminated immediately.

The combination $2\ce{\,Cl}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cl2}\textrm{(g)}$ is the reverse of bond dissociation: two chlorine atoms combine to form a $\ce{Cl-Cl}$ bond. Bond formation releases energy, giving $-242\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

The process $\ce{K+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Cl-}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{KCl}\textrm{(s)}$ is the reverse of the lattice enthalpy. The lattice enthalpy of $\ce{KCl}$ is $+717\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1},$ so the reverse process releases $717\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$ This is far more negative than either the electron affinity or the bond formation because the electrostatic attractions in an ionic lattice involve many ion pairs in a three$-$dimensional array, collectively releasing much more energy than a single bond formation or a single electron capture.
