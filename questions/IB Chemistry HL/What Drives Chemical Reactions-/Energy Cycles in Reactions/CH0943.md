# CH0943

**Maximum mark: 5**

Below are enthalpy change values for the multiple steps associated with the formation of iron(II) iodide.

  | Equation| Enthalpy change $\\$ (kJ mol$\mathbf{^{-1}}$) |
  | :-: | :-: |
  | Formation of iron(II) iodide | $- 113$ |
  | Electron affinity of iodine| $- 295$ |
  | First ionization energy of iron | $+ 759$ |
  | Second ionization energy of iron | $+ 1561$ |
  | Atomization of iodine| $+ 107$ |
  | Atomization of iron| $+ 416$ |

1.  Define lattice enthalpy. [1 mark]

2.  Calculate the lattice enthalpy of iron (II) iodide. [2 marks]

3.  Discuss how the lattice enthalpy of iron (II) chloride would compare to iron (II) iodide. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Lattice enthalpy is the minimum amount of energy associated with the breaking apart of one mole of solid ionic compound into its gaseous ions. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Lattice enthalpy is a measure of the strength of the forces of attraction between the ions in the solid state. When the lattice is being broken, the ions are forced apart and energy is required. This is an endothermic process.

    $\ce{M_aX_n}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow a \ce{M^{+n}}\textrm{(g)} + n \ce{X^{-a}}\textrm{(g)}$

2.  $\Delta H ^{\minuso}_{latt} = 416 + ((2) (107)) + ((2) (- 295)) + 759 + 1561 - (- 113)$\
    $\Delta H ^{\minuso}_{latt} = 2473\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Hess's Law states that the total energy involved in a reaction is the same regardless of the path and intermediate steps, as long as the initial and final stages are the same. When we apply this to the Born-Haber cycle we can obtain the lattice enthalpy of iron (II) iodide.

    Step 1: Apply Hess's Law to the Born$-$Haber cycle.

    $\Delta H^{\minuso}_f = \Delta H^{\minuso}_{at}(\ce{Fe}) +  2 \Delta H^{\minuso}_{at}(\ce{I}) + 2 EA_1(\ce{I}) + IE_1(\ce{Fe}) + IE_2(\ce{Fe}) - \Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt}$

    Step 2: Rearrange the equation to determine the lattice enthalpy of iron (II) iodide.

    $\Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt} = \Delta H^{\minuso}_{at}(\ce{Fe}) + 2 \Delta H^{\minuso}_{at}(\ce{I}) + 2 EA_1(\ce{I}) + IE_1(\ce{Fe}) + IE_2(\ce{Fe}) - \Delta H^{\minuso}_f$

    Step 3: Substitute and calculate the lattice enthalpy of iron (II) iodide.

    $\begin{aligned}
    \Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt} &= 416 + ((2)(107)) + ((2)(-295)) + 759 + 1561 - (-113) \\
    &= 2473\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}
    \end{aligned}$

3.  lattice enthalpy of iron(II) chloride would be greater than that of iron (II) iodide $\checkmark$\
    chloride ion has a smaller radius than iodide ion $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The lattice enthalpy is a measure of the strength of the forces of attraction between the ions in the solid. The smaller the ions, the closer they can get to each other and the stronger the forces of attraction between them. This is because the smaller ions have a higher charge density, which means that there is more charge per unit volume. The greater the charge on the ions, the stronger the forces of attraction between them. This is because the larger charges have a greater potential energy when they are separated. Therefore, the smaller the ions and the higher their charge, the greater the lattice enthalpy of the ionic compound will be.
