# CH0729

**Maximum mark: 12**

Liquid ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol to produce the liquid ester ethyl ethanoate. The equation for this esterification reaction is shown.

![CH0729](images/CH0729.png)

When equal molar quantities of ethanol and ethanoic acid react, complete conversion to ethyl ethanoate can be achieved by removing the water from the reaction mixture as it is formed.

Under these conditions, the standard enthalpy change of the esterification reaction, $\Delta H_r^{\ominus}$, can be calculated from the enthalpies of combustion of the reactants and products.

1.  Write an equation for the complete combustion of ethyl ethanoate under SATP conditions, including the molecular formulas and state symbols of the reactants and products. Sections 2, 13 and 14 of the data booklet may be used for guidance. [3 marks]

2.  State why it is possible to calculate $\Delta H_r^{\ominus}$ from the enthalpies of combustion of the reactants and products. [1 mark]

3.  The enthalpies of combustion of the reactants and products are listed in Section 14 of the data booklet.

    1.  Draw an enthalpy cycle from which $\Delta H_r^{\ominus}$ can be calculated, representing each reaction as an arrow labelled with the corresponding enthalpy change in kJ mol$^{-1}$. [2 marks]

    2.  Calculate the value of $\Delta H_r^{\ominus}$ in kJ mol$^{-1}$. [2 marks]

4.  Ethyl ethanoate can be used as a fuel.

    An insulated flask of water at $20.0\,{}^\circ$C was heated by burning $0.0350 \textrm{\ mol}$ ethyl ethanoate in excess oxygen. The water reached a maximum temperature of $74.5\,{}^\circ$C.

    Calculate the mass of water in the flask, using Section 1, 2, and 14 of the data booklet and showing your working. [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    In the complete combustion of a compound containing only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, the only products should be carbon dioxide and water. All of the carbon is incorporated into $\ce{CO2}$, so the number of moles of $\ce{CO2}$ is given by the number of carbon atoms in the combusted fuel. Likewise, all of the hydrogen is incorporated into $\ce{H2O}$, so the number of moles of $\ce{H2O}$ is equal to the number of hydrogen atoms in the fuel divided by 2. Summing the number of atoms of oxygen in the $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{H2O}$ products, and subtracting the number of atoms of oxygen in the fuel, gives the total number of oxygen atoms that must be added to complete the combustion. This value divided by 2 is equal to the number of $\ce{O2}$ molecules reacted. The reaction takes place under SATP conditions, meaning that the pressure is 100 kPa and the temperature is 298 K. Ethanoic acid and water are liquids under these conditions, while oxygen and carbon dioxide are gases. These standard states are indicated by state symbols in the reaction equation.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        An enthalpy cycle shows several paths by which a set of reactants can be converted to products. One path is typically the reaction under investigation, while the other path is a theoretical set of reactions for which the enthalpy changes have been measured or calculated. In the theoretical reaction pathway, the reactants may first be broken down into combustion products, individual atoms or elements in their standard states, before being converted to the final products. The enthalpy change for each reaction step is next to the reaction arrow, and the sign of the enthalpy change is chosen depending on the direction of the arrow. For example, combustion is usually exothermic, so reaction arrows drawn from an organic compound to its combustion products must be associated with a negative enthalpy change.

    2. $\Delta H_r = \Delta H_c(\ce{CH3COOH}) + \Delta H_c(\ce{CH3CH2OH}) - \Delta H_c(\ce{CH3COOCH2CH3}) \: \checkmark$\
        $\Delta H_r = -874 - 1367 + 2238 = {-3\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}}\:\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In the enthalpy cycle, the esterification reaction is shown to have the same enthalpy change as the combustion of ethanoic acid and ethanol followed by the reverse combustion of ethyl ethanoate. Both reaction pathways produce water in the same net amounts, so a separate enthalpy change for the formation or reaction of water is not needed. Based on this analysis, the value of $\Delta H_r$ is given by the enthalpies of combustion of the reactants (forward processes) minus the enthalpies of combustion of the product (reverse process). This calculation can only be performed because the reactants are completely converted to products and the water is removed from the reaction mixture. If this were not the case, the enthalpy change for the reaction would also involve the enthalpy changes for mixing of the reactants and products, which are not accounted for in the enthalpies of combustion.

4.  $\Delta T = 74.5 - 20.0 = 54.5\,{}^\circ$C $\checkmark$\
    $q = -n \Delta H_c^{\ominus} = 0.0350 \times 2238 = {78.33\,\text{kJ}}\,\checkmark$\
    $m = \dfrac{q}{c\Delta T} = \dfrac{78330}{4.18 \times 54.5}\,\checkmark$\
    $m = 344\:\text{g}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The temperature change $\Delta T$ is equal to the final temperature minus the initial temperature. Each mole of reactant releases an amount of heat energy equal to $-\Delta H_c^{\ominus}$, so the amount of energy released, $q$, by $n$ moles of reactant is given by $-n\Delta H_c^{\ominus}$. The minus sign appears in this equation because enthalpy changes describe the amount of energy absorbed; exothermic reactions are therefore associated with negative enthalpy changes, and must be multiplied by $-1$ to determine the amount of energy released. Dividing both sides of the equation q$ = mc\Delta T$ by $c\Delta T$ produces an equation from which the mass of solution, $m$, can be calculated. It should be noted that $m$ is the mass of solution, rather than the mass of reactant, as it is the solution which absorbs the energy $q$ and undergoes the change in temperature $\Delta T$.
