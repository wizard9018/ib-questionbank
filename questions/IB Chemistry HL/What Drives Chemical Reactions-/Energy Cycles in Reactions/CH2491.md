# CH2491

**Maximum mark: 10**

Consider the Born$-$Haber cycle for $\ce{Li2O}$ below.

![CH2491](images/CH2491.png)

1. State the name of the enthalpy change I, V and VI. [3 marks]

2. Deduce the enthalpy of atomization of lithium, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}.$ [1 mark]

3. Calculate the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{Li2O}$ using the information given above and sections 9 and 12 of the chemistry data booklet. [4 marks]

4. Comment on why the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{BeO}$ is greater than that of $\ce{Li2O}.$ [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. I: enthalpy of formation $\checkmark$\
    V: first electron affinity $\checkmark$\
    VI: second electron affinity $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The enthalpy changes in the Born$-$Haber cycle are:

    | $\mathbf{Step}$ | $\mathbf{Name}$ | Equation
    |:-:|:--|:-:
    | I | Enthalpy of formation |$2\ce{\,Li}\textrm{(s)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Li2O}\textrm{(s)}$ |
    | II | Enthalpy of atomization / sublimation of $2\ce{\,Li}$  | 2 Li(s) $\longrightarrow$ 2 Li(g)
    | III | Enthalpy of atomization / $\frac{1}{2}$ bond enthalpy of $\ce{O2}$ | $\frac{1}{2}\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{O}\textrm{(g)}$
    | IV | First ionization energy of $2\ce{\,Li}$ | $2 \ce{\,Li}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Li^+}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,e-}$
    | V | First electron affinity | $\ce{O}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{O-}\textrm{(g)}$ |
    | VI | Second electron affinity | $\ce{O-}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{O^{2-}}\textrm{(g)}$ |
    | VII | Lattice enthalpy | $\ce{Li2O}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Li^+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O^{2-}}\textrm{(g)}$

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

2. $159\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Step II involves the atomization of two moles of lithium: $2\ce{\,Li}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Li}\textrm{(g)},$ $\Delta H = +318\textrm{ kJ}.$ Since the enthalpy of atomization is defined per mole, the value is halved: $318 \div 2 = 159\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

3.  Step III $= 249\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$\
    Step IV $= 1040\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$\
    Step V $= -141\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1},$ Step VI $= +753\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$\
    $\Delta H_\textrm{latt} = 2854\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    *Award [4] for correct final answer with working shown.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The lattice enthalpy corresponds to step VII. By Hess's law, the alternative route from $\ce{Li2O}\textrm{(s)}$ to $2\ce{\,Li+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O^{2-}}\textrm{(g)}$ gives:

    $\textrm{Lattice enthalpy (VII)} = -\textrm{I} + \textrm{II} + \textrm{III} + \textrm{IV} + \textrm{V} + \textrm{VI}$

    The values from the data booklet are: bond enthalpy of $\ce{O=O} = 498\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ (section 12), $\textrm{IE}_1(\ce{Li}) = 520\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ (section 9), $\textrm{EA}_1(\ce{O}) = -141\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ and $\textrm{EA}_2(\ce{O}) = +753\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ (section 9).

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

    | $\mathbf{Step}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta H\textbf{ / kJ mol}^{-1}}$ |
    |:--|:-:|
    | $-\textrm{I: reverse formation}$ | $+635$ |
    | $\textrm{II: atomization of }2\ce{\,Li}$ | $+318$ |
    | $\textrm{III: }\frac{1}{2} \times 498\textrm{ (bond enthalpy of }\ce{O2}\textrm{)}$ | $+249$ |
    | $\textrm{IV: }2 \times 520\textrm{ (IE}_1\textrm{ of Li)}$ | $+1040$ |
    | $\textrm{V: EA}_1\textrm{ of O}$ | $-141$ |
    | $\textrm{VI: EA}_2\textrm{ of O}$ | $+753$ |

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

    $\Delta H_\textrm{latt} = 635 + 318 + 249 + 1040 + (-141) + 753 = 2854\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

4.  $\ce{Be^{2+}}$ has a greater charge than $\ce{Li+}$ $\checkmark$\
    electrostatic attractions are stronger, lattice enthalpy is larger $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The lattice enthalpy depends on the product of the ionic charges and is inversely proportional to the sum of the ionic radii. Beryllium forms $\ce{Be^{2+}}$ ions with a $2+$ charge, while lithium forms $\ce{Li+}$ ions with only a $1+$ charge. The stronger electrostatic attraction between $\ce{Be^{2+}}$ and $\ce{O^{2-}}$ means more energy is required to separate the ions, giving $\ce{BeO}$ a larger lattice enthalpy. In addition, $\ce{Be^{2+}}$ is smaller than $\ce{Li+},$ which further increases the strength of the attraction.
