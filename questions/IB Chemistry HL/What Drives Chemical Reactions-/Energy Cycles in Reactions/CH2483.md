# CH2483

Which statement is correct?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$The enthalpy of atomization of sodium is the same as the enthalpy of sublimation of sodium.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$The enthalpy of atomization of chlorine is the same as the bond enthalpy of $\ce{Cl2}\textrm{(g)}.$
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Ionization energies are always positive.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The enthalpy of atomization of sodium is the enthalpy change for $\ce{Na}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Na}\textrm{(g)}.$ Since sodium is a monatomic metal, converting the solid to gaseous atoms is exactly the same process as sublimation. Both refer to the transition from the solid phase directly to the gas phase. Statement I is correct.

The enthalpy of atomization of chlorine refers to the formation of one mole of gaseous atoms:

$\frac{1}{2}\ce{\,Cl2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cl}\textrm{(g)}$

The bond enthalpy of $\ce{Cl2}$ refers to breaking one mole of $\ce{Cl-Cl}$ bonds:

$\ce{Cl2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Cl}\textrm{(g)}$

The atomization enthalpy is therefore half the bond enthalpy, not equal to it. Statement II is incorrect.

Ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom or ion. Removing an electron from the attraction of the nuclear charge always requires an input of energy, so all ionization energies are endothermic and therefore positive. Statement III is correct.
