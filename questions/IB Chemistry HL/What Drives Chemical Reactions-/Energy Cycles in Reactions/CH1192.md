# CH1192

What is the enthalpy change of the complete combustion of propane gas in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}?$

|Substance|Enthalpy of formation (kJ mol$\mathbf{^{-1}})$ |
| :-: | :-: |
| Propane | $-105$ |
| Carbon monoxide | $-111$ |
| Water | $-286$ |
| Carbon dioxide | $-394$ |
| Steam | $-242$ |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}  -3 (- 394) - 4 (- 286) - 105$

-   B. $\hspace{1em} 3 (- 394) + 4 (- 286) + 105$

-   C. $\hspace{1em} - 111 + 3 (- 394) + 4 (- 286) - 105$

-   D. $\hspace{1em} - 111 + 3 (- 394) + 4 (-242) + 105$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Propane is $\ce{C3H8},$ and its complete combustion balanced chemical equation is shown below.

$\ce{C3H8(g) + 5 O2(g) -> 3 CO2(g) + 4 H2O(l)}$

$\begin{aligned}
\Delta H^{\minuso} &= \sum \Delta H_{f}^{\minuso}\, (products) - \sum \Delta H_f^{\minuso}\, (reactants) \\
\Delta H^{\minuso} &= 3\Delta H_f^{\minuso}\, (\ce{CO2(g)}) + 4\Delta H_f^{\minuso}\, \ce{H2O(l)} - \Delta H_f^{\minuso}\, \ce{C3H8(g)} - 5\Delta H_f^{\minuso}\, \ce{O2(g)}
\end{aligned}$

The enthalpy of formation of $\ce{O2}(g)$ is equal to zero, as it is zero for all elemental substances in their standard states.

$\Delta H^{\minuso} = 3(-394) + 4(-286) - (-105) - (5 \times 0)$

This question is categorized as hard since students must figure out the **balanced** chemical equation of the complete combustion of propane to deduce the enthalpy change of reaction using the approppriate enthalpies of formation.
