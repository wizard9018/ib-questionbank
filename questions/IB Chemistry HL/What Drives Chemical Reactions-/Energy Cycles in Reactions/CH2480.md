# CH2480

The standard enthalpies of formation and the standard enthalpy of sublimation are given below.

| $\mathbf{Quantity}$ | $\mathbf{Value}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\textrm{A(s)})$ | $X$ |
| $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\textrm{B(l)})$ | $Y$ |
| $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\textrm{C(s)})$ | $Z$ |
| $\Delta H_\textrm{sub}^{\minuso}(\textrm{C})$ | $W$ |

What is the enthalpy change for $\textrm{A(s)} + 2\textrm{\,B(l)} \longrightarrow 3\textrm{\,C(g)}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}X + 2Y - 3Z + 3W$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}X - 2Y - 3Z - 3W$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}-X - 2Y + 3Z - 3W$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}-X - 2Y + 3Z + 3W$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The product $\textrm{C}$ is in the gaseous state, but the formation data is given for $\textrm{C(s)}.$ The standard enthalpy of formation of $\textrm{C(g)}$ must first be constructed using Hess's law:

$\textrm{Elements} \xrightarrow{\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}} \textrm{C(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta H_\textrm{sub}^{\minuso}} \textrm{C(g)}$

$\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\textrm{C(g)}) = Z + W$

The enthalpy change of the reaction is then calculated from formation data:

$\Delta H = \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of products}) - \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of reactants})$

$\Delta H = 3(Z + W) - (X + 2Y)$

$\Delta H = -X - 2Y + 3Z + 3W$

The key step is recognising that the formation data for $\textrm{C(s)}$ cannot be used directly for $\textrm{C(g)}.$ The sublimation enthalpy bridges the two phases: $\textrm{C(s)} \longrightarrow \textrm{C(g)}.$ Without adding $W,$ the calculation uses the wrong reference state for the product.
