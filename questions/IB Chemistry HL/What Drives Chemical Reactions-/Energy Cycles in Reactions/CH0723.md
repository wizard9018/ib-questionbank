# CH0723

Liquid dinitrogen tetroxide ($\ce{N2O4}$) is converted to gaseous nitrogen dioxide when heated, as shown in the diagram. No other molecules react or form during this reaction.

![CH0723](images/CH0723.png)

The standard enthalpy change of the reaction, $\Delta H_{rxn}^{\minuso}$, is +85.8 kJ mol$^{-1}$. The standard enthalpy of formation of gaseous $\ce{NO2}$, $\Delta H_f^{\minuso}$ ($\ce{NO2}$), is +33.1 kJ mol$^{-1}$.

What is the standard enthalpy of formation of liquid $\ce{N2O4}$, $\Delta H_f^{\minuso}$ ($\ce{N2O4}$)?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}-52.7$ kJ mol$^{-1}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}-19.6$ kJ mol$^{-1}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}+19.6$ kJ mol$^{-1}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}+52.7$ kJ mol$^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The reaction equation is $\ce{N2O4(l) -> 2 NO2(g)}$.

The enthalpy change of the reaction can be determined from the enthalpies of formation as

$\Delta H^{\minuso}_{rxn} = \sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{products}) - \sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{reactants})$

For this reaction,

$\Delta H^{\minuso}_{rxn} = 2\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{NO2}) -  \Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{N2O4})$

Rearranging the equation, we can solve for the enthalpy of formation of $\ce{N2O4}:$

$\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{N2O4})  = 2\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{NO2}) -  \Delta H^{\minuso}_{rxn}$

$\Delta H^{\minuso}_f(\ce{N2O4})  = 2\times 33.1 -  85.8 = -19.6\,\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}$

The alternative options are obtained if the wrong signs are given to the enthalpy changes in the equation and/or $\Delta H_f^{\minuso}(\ce{NO2})$ is not multiplied by 2.

This question is categorised as very hard, as the student must deduce the stoichiometry of the reaction, sum the enthalpy changes with the correct signs, and obtain the correct value for the unknown quantity, without the aid of a reaction equation or enthalpy cycle.
