# CH2452

Consider the chemical equations below.

|$\ce{A} + \ce{B} \longrightarrow \ce{C}$| $\Delta H^{\minuso} = +137\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
:-:|:--
$2\ce{\,C} \longrightarrow \ce{D} + 2\ce{\,E}$ | $\Delta H^{\minuso} = -543\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

What is the correct expression for the enthalpy change calculation, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1},$ for $2\ce{\,A} + 2\ce{\,B} \longrightarrow \ce{D} + 2\ce{\,E}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}+137 - 543$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}2 \times 137 - 543$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}543 - 137$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}542 - 2 \times 137$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Hess's law states that the enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the pathway between the initial and final states. The target reaction can be constructed by combining the two given reactions.

The target has $2\ce{\,A}$ and $2\ce{\,B}$ as reactants, but the first equation produces only $1\ce{\,C}$ from $1\ce{\,A}$ and $1\ce{\,B}.$ Multiplying the first equation by $2:$

$2\ce{\,A} + 2\ce{\,B} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,C} \hspace{3em} \Delta H = 2 \times (+137 \textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1})$

The second equation uses $2\ce{\,C}$ and can be added directly:

$2\ce{\,C} \longrightarrow \ce{D} + 2\ce{\,E} \hspace{3em} \Delta H = -543\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

The $2\ce{\,C}$ cancels as an intermediate, giving:

$2\ce{\,A} + 2\ce{\,B} \longrightarrow \ce{D} + 2\ce{\,E}$

$\Delta H = 2 \times 137  -543 = -269\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
