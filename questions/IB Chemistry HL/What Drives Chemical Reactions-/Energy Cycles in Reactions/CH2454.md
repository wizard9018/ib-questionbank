# CH2454

Consider the cycle below.

![CH2454](images/CH2454.png)

Which combination is correct?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_1 = \Delta H_2 - \Delta H_3 - \Delta H_4$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}\Delta H_4 = \Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2 - \Delta H_3$
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}\Delta H_2 = -\Delta H_3 - \Delta H_4 + \Delta H_1$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
By Hess's law, any two routes between the same initial and final states have the same total enthalpy change. The cycle provides two routes from $\ce{MX}\textrm{(s)}$ to $\ce{M}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{X}\textrm{(g)}:$

|$\textrm{Route 1 (top then right):}$| $\Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2$
:--|:-:
Route 2 (left then bottom):| $\Delta H_4 + \Delta H_3$

Setting these equal:

$\Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2 = \Delta H_4 + \Delta H_3$

This is the master equation from which all three statements can be tested.

Statement I claims $\Delta H_1 = \Delta H_2 - \Delta H_3 - \Delta H_4.$ From the master equation, $\Delta H_1 = \Delta H_4 + \Delta H_3 - \Delta H_2.$ These are not equivalent, so Statement I is incorrect.

Statement II claims $\Delta H_4 = \Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2 - \Delta H_3.$ This is consistent with the master equation, so Statement II is correct.

Statement III claims $\Delta H_2 = -\Delta H_3 - \Delta H_4 + \Delta H_1.$ From the master equation, $\Delta H_2 = \Delta H_3  + \Delta H_4 - \Delta H_1.$ These are not equivalent, so Statement III is incorrect.
