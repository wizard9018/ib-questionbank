# CH2488

The enthalpy change for the following reaction is given.

$2\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,CH3OH}\textrm{(l)} + 3\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = +1452\textrm{ kJ}$

What is the standard enthalpy change of combustion of methanol, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}-726$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}726$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}-1452$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}1452$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The standard enthalpy of combustion of methanol corresponds to:

$\ce{CH3OH}\textrm{(l)} + \frac{3}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

The given equation is the reverse of the combustion of two moles of methanol. Two manipulations are needed: reversing the equation changes the sign of $\Delta H,$ and halving it adjusts the stoichiometry to one mole.

Reversing:

$2\ce{\,CH3OH}\textrm{(l)} + 3\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = -1452\textrm{ kJ}$

Halving:

$\ce{CH3OH}\textrm{(l)} + \frac{3}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso} = -726\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
