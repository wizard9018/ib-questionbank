# CH2479

Which statement is correct about the standard enthalpy of combustion of a substance?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$It is tabulated at $273\textrm{ K}$ and $100\textrm{ kPa.}$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$It corresponds to $1\textrm{ mol}$ of substance undergoing combustion.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$It is always positive.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Standard conditions are $298\textrm{ K}$ $(25\,{}^\circ\textrm{C})$ and $100\textrm{ kPa},$ not $273\textrm{ K}$ $(0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}).$ The value $273\textrm{ K}$ is the standard temperature used in gas law calculations at STP, which is a different convention. This is a common point of confusion.  Statement I is incorrect.

The standard enthalpy of combustion is defined per mole of substance. This is reflected in its unit, $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1},$ and means the balanced equation must show exactly one mole of the substance being combusted. Statement II is correct.

Combustion reactions are exothermic, so $\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}$ is negative. Exothermic processes release energy to the surroundings. Statement III is incorrect.
