# CH2478

The equation for the complete combustion of phenylethanone is shown below.

$\ce{C6H5COCH3}\textrm{(l)} + \frac{19}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 8\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = -4149\textrm{ kJ}$

The table shows standard enthalpy of formation data.

| $\mathbf{Substance}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textbf{ / kJ mol}^\mathbf{-1}}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| $\ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)}$ | $-394$ |
| $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ | $-286$ |

What is the standard enthalpy of formation of liquid phenylethanone, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}147$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}-147$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}-8445$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}8445$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The enthalpy change of a reaction can be expressed in terms of standard enthalpies of formation:

$\Delta H = \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of products}) - \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of reactants})$

The standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state is zero, so $\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)}) = 0.$

Substituting:

$-4149\textrm{ kJ} = [8(-394\textrm{ kJ}) + 4(-286\textrm{ kJ})] - [\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{C6H5COCH3})]$

$-4149 = -4296 - \Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{C6H5COCH3})$

$\Delta H_\textrm{f}^{\minuso}(\ce{C6H5COCH3}) = -4296 + 4149 = -147\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Because phenylethanone is a stable organic liquid, a negative enthalpy of formation is expected, the compound is lower in energy than its constituent elements.
