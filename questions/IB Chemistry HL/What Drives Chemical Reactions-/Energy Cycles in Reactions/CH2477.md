# CH2477

The hydrogenation of but$-$2$-$yne is shown below.

$\ce{CH3C#CCH3}\textrm{(l)} + 2\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CH3CH2CH2CH3}\textrm{(g)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = -360\textrm{ kJ}$

The table shows standard enthalpy of combustion data.

| $\mathbf{Substance}$ | $\mathbf{\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}\textrm{ / kJ mol}^{-1}}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| $\ce{H2}\textrm{(g)}$ | $-286$ |
| $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH3}\textrm{(g)}$ | $-2878$ |

What is the enthalpy of combustion of but$-$2$-$yne, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}-2952$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}-2666$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}2952$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}2666$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The enthalpy change of a reaction can be expressed in terms of enthalpy of combustion data:

$\Delta H = \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of reactants}) - \Sigma(\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}\textrm{ of products})$

Substituting:

$-360\textrm{ kJ} = [\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}(\textrm{but--2--yne}) + 2(-286\textrm{ kJ})] - [(-2878\textrm{ kJ})]$

$-360 = \Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}(\textrm{but--2--yne}) - 572 + 2878$

$\Delta H_\textrm{c}^{\minuso}(\textrm{but--2--yne})  = -2666\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

The hydrogenation reaction converts a triple bond in but$-$2$-$yne to single bonds in butane. Because the more unsaturated molecule stores more chemical energy, its enthalpy of combustion has a larger magnitude than that of butane.
