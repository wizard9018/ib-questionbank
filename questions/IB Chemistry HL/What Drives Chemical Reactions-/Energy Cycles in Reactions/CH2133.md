# CH2133

Which combination is correct for the combustion of propane at 298 K?

$\ce{C3H8}\textrm{(g)}+5 \ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 3 \ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} +4 \ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

|$\hspace{0.95em}$||$\hspace{0.1em}$|$\mathbf{\Delta H}$|$\mathbf{\Delta S}$|$\ $Spontaneous$\ $
:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:
||A.||$\ \ $negative$\ \ $|$\ \ $positive$\ \ $|yes
||B.||negative|negative|yes
||C.||positive|positive|no
||D.||positive|negative|no

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The student must understand that the combustion of hydrocarbons is exothermic, so its change in enthalpy is negative.

The combustion of propane converts six moles of gas into three moles of gas, decreasing the amount of gas, so decreasing the system's entropy, a negative $\Delta S.$

Finally, the combustion of hydrocarbons is spontaneous at room temperature. The reaction is very exothermic, overcoming the modest decrease in entropy.  The change in Gibbs free energy accounts for both the change in enthalpy and entropy of the system, $\Delta G= \Delta H-T\Delta S < 0.$  The first term is much larger in magnitude than the second term so the reaction is spontaneous.

This question is categorized as medium as the student must interpret the characteristics of a complete combustion reaction in terms of entropy, enthalpy changes, and spontaneity.
