# CH2020

Which enthalpy changes of the Born$-$Haber cycle are endothermic?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | Ionization energy |
    | II. | Enthalpy of atomization |
    | III. | Lattice enthalpy |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The student must understand the ionization energy, enthalpy of atomization, lattice enthalpy, and the associated chemical reactions.

*Ionization energy:*

$\ce{M(g) -> M+(g) + e-}$

Removing an electron requires energy. As a result, ionization energy is always endothermic.

*Enthalpy of atomization:*

$\ce{Z(s) -> Z(g)}$

Separating atoms in an element requires energy. Atomization is the same as sublimation for metals. In the case of diatomic gases, the enthalpy of atomization is one half of the bond dissociation enthalpy. This process requires energy because bonds are broken, it is endothermic.

*Lattice enthalpy:*

$\ce{M_aX_b(s) -> a M^{b+}(g) + b X^{a-}(g)}$

This is a process that requires energy as it describes the break up of the ionic solid into separated ions in the gaseous state. This process is endothermic because energy is required to overcome the strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the oppositely charged ions in the solid lattice.

This question is categorized as medium as the student must identify the enthalpy change for each.
