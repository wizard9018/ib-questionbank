# CH0717

Ozone ($\ce{O3}$) can be broken down by the absorption of ultraviolet light. An enthalpy cycle diagram for this reaction is shown.

![5-717](images/5-717.png)

Which equation would give the correct value for the standard enthalpy change of this reaction, $\Delta H_r^\ominus$?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_r^\ominus = \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O2}) + \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O3})$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_r^\ominus = \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O2}) - \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O3})$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_r^\ominus = \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O}) + \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O3})$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_r^\ominus = \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O}) - \Delta H_f^\ominus(\ce{O3})$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

Hess's Law states that the reaction enthalpy is equal to the enthalpies of formation of the products minus the enthalpies of formation of the reactions.

The enthalpy change can be calculated in this way because it is a state function: its value depends only on the initial and final states and not on the path taken.

Gaseous diatomic oxygen ($\ce{O2}$) is already in its standard state so its enthalpy of formation is zero. Thus, $\Delta H_r^\ominus$ is given by the enthalpy of formation of O minus the enthalpy of formation of $\ce{O3}$.

  

This question is of moderate difficulty as the process of combining enthalpies of formation is covered directly in R1.2, though the student must also recognize that $\ce{O2}$ does not feature in the calculation because its enthalpy of formation of elements in their standard state is 0.
