# CH1261

Consider the Born-Haber cycle below.

*All enthalpies shown on the diagram are values for **one** mole of substance.*

![CH1261](images/CH1261.png)

Which change represents the lattice enthalpy of magnesium chloride?

- A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{H1 + H2 + H3 + H4 - H5 + 2 H7}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{H1 + H2 + H3 + H4 - H5 + 2 H7 - H6}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{H1 + H2 + H3 + H4 - H5 + H7}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{-H1 - H2 - H3 - H4 + H5 - 2 H7}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The lattice enthalpy is the enthalpy change when one mole of a solid ionic compound, such as magnesium chloride, is separated into gaseous ions under standard conditions. In this case:

$\ce{MgCl2(s) -> Mg^{2+}(g) + 2 Cl-(g)}$

This corresponds to $\ce{H6}.$

An alternative route to determine the magnitude of $\ce{H6}$ is to go the other way around, as shown in red below.

![CH1261Ans](images/CH1261Ans.png)

This step includes reversing $\ce{H5}$ (so a negative sign is added to it).

Since one electron is added to each of the 2 gaseous Cl atoms, we multiply $\ce{H7}$ (electron affinity) by 2.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students are expected to distinguish lattice enthalpy from the other processes in the Born-Haber cycle.
