# CH0169

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider the following reaction:

$\ce{2Mg}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{2MgO}\textrm{(s)}$

1.  Suggest two reasons why bond enthalpies cannot be used to calculate the enthalpy of formation of $\ce{MgO}$. [2 marks]

2.  Using sections 8 and 11 of the data booklet and the following data, construct a Born$-$Haber cycle and calculate the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{MgO}$. [3 marks]

| $\enskip \text{Enthalpy of formation of }\ce{MgO} \enskip$   | $- 602\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ |
| :-:                            | :-:                 |
| $\text{Enthalpy of atomization of }\ce{Mg}$  | $148\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$   |
| $\text{Second ionization energy of }\ce{Mg}$ | $1451\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$  |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{MgO}$ has ionic bonds not covalent bonds $\checkmark$\
    $\ce{MgO}$ and $\ce{Mg}$ are solids $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The tabulated bond dissociation enthalpies describe the energy required to break covalent bonds in the gas phase, averaged over representative compounds.  The reaction here breaks ionic bonds in the solid MgO.

2.  $\Delta H^{\minuso}_{f} = \Delta H^{\minuso}_{atom}(\ce{Mg}) + \Delta H^{\minuso}_{IE_1}(\ce{Mg}) + \Delta H^{\minuso}_{IE_2}(\ce{Mg})\  +$\
    $\hspace{8em} \frac{1}{2}\Delta H^{\minuso}_{bond}(\ce{O=O}) + \Delta H^{\minuso}_{EA_1}(\ce{O}) + \Delta H^{\minuso}_{EA_2}(\ce{O}) - \Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt} \: \checkmark$\
    $\Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt} = +602 + 148 + 738 + 1451 + \frac{1}{2}498 - 141 + 753 ={+ 3800\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}} \: \checkmark $

    Born$-$Haber cycles can be used to calculate lattice enthalpy through indirect means. Hess's law is applied where the enthalpy change is independent of the route taken. Understanding atomisation, ionisation energy, electron affinity, and formation is required.
