# CH2481

Which statement is correct about the lattice enthalpy?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$It is always positive.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$It is the enthalpy change when $1\textrm{ mol}$ of an ionic solid is separated into its gaseous ions.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$It increases as the charge on the cation increases.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Lattice enthalpy is defined as the enthalpy change when one mole of an ionic compound is separated into its gaseous ions. For example, for sodium chloride:

$\ce{NaCl}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Na+}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Cl-}\textrm{(g)}$

Separating ions in a crystal lattice requires energy to overcome the electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions. The process is endothermic, so lattice enthalpy is always positive. Statement I is correct.

Statement II is correct. This is the definition itself: one mole of ionic solid is converted entirely into gaseous ions.

The electrostatic attraction between ions is proportional to the product of their charges. A divalent cation such as $\ce{Mg^{2+}}$ attracts anions more strongly than a univalent cation such as $\ce{Na+},$ so more energy is required to separate the ions. For example, the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{MgCl2}$ $(2523\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1})$ is much larger than that of $\ce{NaCl}$ $(787\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}).$ Statement III is correct.
