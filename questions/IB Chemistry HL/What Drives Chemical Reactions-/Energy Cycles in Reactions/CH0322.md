# CH0322

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the formation of water: $\ce{H2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2} \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

1.  Using Section 12 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, calculate the enthalpy change for this reaction. [3 marks]

2. 1. Compare the theoretical value of enthalpy of formation of water found in Section 13 of the IB Chemistry data booklet to your answer in part (a). Suggest two reasons why they differ. [2 marks]

   2.   State the enthalpy change required to make your answer in part (a) more accurate. [1 mark]

1. $\Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\ce{H-H}) + \frac{1}{2} \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\ce{O=O}) = 436 + \frac{1}{2} \times 498 = {685\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}}\,\checkmark$\

    $2 \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\ce{O-H}) = 2 \times 463 =  926\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$\

    $\Delta H = 685 - 926 = -241\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    $\Delta H =\sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\text{bonds broken}) - \sum \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\text{bonds formed})$

    Drawing out the structures of the molecules reveals the bonds that must be broken and formed.

    $\Delta H = \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\ce{H-H}) + \frac{1}{2} \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\ce{O=O}) - 2 \Delta H^{\minuso}_{BE}(\ce{O-H}) $

    $\Delta H = 436 + \frac{1}{2} \times 498 - 2 \times 463 =  -241\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

2.  1.  average bond enthalpies are  averages across a range of compounds $\checkmark$

        bond enthalpy calculations assume all species are gases, in this reaction $\ce{H2O}$ is a liquid $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$ \

        The calculated value, $-241 \textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$, is less exothermic than the literature value $-285.8 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$ because the calculation in (a) assumed all species were in the gas phase.  Condensing water vapor to liquid water would release more energy (about $-40 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1})$.  This is the major source of error.  

        A smaller source of error is the assumption that all bonds between two atoms have the same bond enthalpy.  The table reports the average across representative compounds, but the values differ slightly across molecules.
