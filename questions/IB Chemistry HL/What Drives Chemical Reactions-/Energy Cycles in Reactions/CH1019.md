# CH1019

**Maximum mark: 6**

Hydrazine $\ce{(N2H4)}$ is a commonly used liquid rocket fuel.

The rocket is propelled by the decomposition of liquid hydrazine into nitrogen and hydrogen gases. The equation and enthalpy change of this decomposition reaction are shown.

:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:

1.  Hydrazine may also decompose via the following reactions:

    |$\textrm{Reaction 2:}$ | $\ce{3 N2H4 -> 4 NH3 + N2}$ | $\qquad \Delta H_2$

    :--|:-:|:--

    Reaction 3:$\qquad$|  $\ce{N2H4 + 4 NH3 -> 3 N2 + 8 H2}$|$\qquad \Delta H_3$

    Express $\Delta H_1$ in terms of $\Delta H_2$ and $\Delta H_3$, explaining your reasoning. [2 marks]

2.  Using Section 12 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, calculate $\Delta H_1$ in kJ mol$^{-1}$. [2 marks]

3.  Suggest two reasons why the value calculated in (b) differs from the measured value of $\Delta H_1$. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  use enthalpy cycle / Hess's Law $\checkmark$\

    $\Delta H_1 = \frac{1}{4}(\Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3)\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    In Reaction 1, hydrazine is converted to nitrogen and hydrogen in one step. Hydrazine can also be converted to the same gases via two steps, reactions 2 and 3, with ammonia $\ce{(NH3)}$ as an intermediate.

    Hess's law states that the enthalpy change for the one$-$step reaction is the same as that for the two$-$step reaction, given that the initial reactants and final products are the same.

    Summing reactions 2 and 3 gives the following overall equation:

    |$\textrm{Reaction 2:}$ | $\ce{3 N2H4 -> 4 NH3 + N2}$ | $\qquad \Delta H_2$

    :--|:-:|:--

    Reaction 3:$\qquad$|  $\ce{N2H4 + 4 NH3  -> 3 N2 + 8 H2}$|$\qquad \Delta H_3$

    ***

    $\ce{3 N2H4 + N2H4 + \cancel{4 \ce{\ NH3}} -> \cancel{4 \ce{\ NH3}}  + N2 + 3 N2 + 8 H2}$

    Simplifying:

    $\ce{4N2H4 -> 4 N2 + 8 H2} \qquad \qquad \Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3$

    This equation is the same as reaction 1 multiplied by 4. Therefore, the sum of $\Delta H_2$ and $\Delta H_3$ is $4\times \Delta H_1$, or 

    $\Delta H_1 = \frac{1}{4} \left( \Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3 \right)$

2.  $\Delta H_1 = 4(391) + 158 - 945 - 2(436)\,\checkmark$\

    $\Delta H_1 = -95\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    When a reaction takes place, energy is absorbed to break bonds in the reactants and released when bonds in the products are formed. The enthalpy change of the reaction is, therefore, equal to the sum of the enthalpies of the  broken bonds in the reactants minus the sum of the enthalpies of the formed bonds in the products.

    In reaction 1, 4 $\ce{N-H}$ bonds and 1 $\ce{N-N}$ bond are broken, and 1 $\ce{N#N}$ and 2 $\ce{H-H}$ bonds are formed.

    $\Delta H_1 = 4 D\ce{(N-H)} + D\ce{(N-N)} - \left [D(\ce{N#N}) - 2D\ce{(H-H)}\right]$\

    $\Delta H_1 = 4(391) + 158 - 945 - 2(436) = -95 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

3.  bond enthalpies differ from  average values $\checkmark$\

    hydrazine is a liquid, bond enthalpies are for gases / do not include intemolecular forces $\checkmark$

  

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\

    Tabulated bond enthalpies are average values calculated from the enthalpies of bonds in representative molecules in the gas phase.

    The strength of a bond is affected by the other bonds in the molecule, so the $\ce{N-N}$ and $\ce{N-H}$ bonds in hydrazine will not display enthalpies exactly equal to the average value. By contrast, the bond enthalpies of $\ce{H2}$ and $\ce{N2}$ must be equal to the average values, as these molecules provide the only possible examples of $\ce{H-H}$ and $\ce{N#N}$ bonds.

    When enthalpy changes are calculated from bond enthalpies, it is further assumed that only covalent bonds are broken or formed. Nonpolar gases such as $\ce{H2}$ and $\ce{N2}$ can be treated as ideal gases, as they do not form significant intermolecular interactions.

    Hydrazine, however, contains polar $\ce{N-H}$ bonds and can form hydrogen bonds with neighboring molecules. Overcoming these interactions requires a relatively large amount of heat energy, so hydrazine is a liquid at room temperature. Breaking hydrogen bonds between hydrazine molecules lowers the amount of heat energy released during its decomposition reaction, causing $\Delta H_1$ to be less negative.
