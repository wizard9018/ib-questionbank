# CH1003

What is the correct expression for the enthalpy change of the following reaction?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$\ce{N2(g) + 3 F2(g) -> 2 NF3(g)}$

$\\$

| Bond | Bond Enthalpy |
| :-: | :-: |
| $\ce{N#N}$ | $x$ |
| $\ce{F-F}$ | $y$ |
| $\ce{N-F}$ | $z$ |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}6z-3y-x$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}3z-3y-x$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}-6z+3y+x$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}-3z+3y+x$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Breaking bonds is an endothermic process, and forming bonds is an exothermic process. Students should use the average bond enthalpies given to calculate the enthalpy change. Below is the process taking place.

![CH1003v2](images/CH1003v2.png)

One  $\ce{N#N}$ and three $\ce{F-F}$ bonds are broken. Six $\ce{N-F}$ bonds are formed.

$\Delta H = \sum BE\textrm{(broken)}- \sum BE\textrm{(formed)}$

$\Delta H = \ce{(N#N)} + 3\ce{(F-F)} - 6\ce{(N-F)}$

$\Delta H = x + 3y - 6z$

This question is categorized as medium difficulty students must figure out the Lewis (electron dot) structures and calculate the enthalpy change.
