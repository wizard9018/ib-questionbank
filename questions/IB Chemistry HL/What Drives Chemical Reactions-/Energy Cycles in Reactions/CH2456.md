# CH2456

**Maximum mark: 7**

Catalytic converters in vehicles reduce pollutant emissions. Exhaust fumes enter the catalytic converter, where a reduction occurs, and the resulting exhaust is released into the atmosphere.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$2\ce{\,CO}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,NO}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{N2}\textrm{(g)}$

1. Determine the enthalpy change, $\Delta H,$ for the reaction, in $\textrm{kJ},$ using section 12 of the data booklet. Assume that $\ce{NO}$ contains a double bond. [3 marks]

2. Calculate the enthalpy change, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1},$ for the formation of $\ce{NO}$ using the previous result and the data below. [2 marks]

$\ce{CO}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)}$|$\Delta H = -282.5\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
:-:|:--
$\frac{1}{2}\ce{\,N2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{NO}\textrm{(g)}$|$ \Delta H = \textrm{\ ?}$

3. Sketch the energy profile for the formation of $\ce{NO}.$ Label the change in enthalpy on the graph. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. bonds broken:  $2(\ce{C#O}) + 2(\ce{N=O}) = 3328\textrm{ kJ} \: \checkmark$\
    bonds formed: $4(\ce{C=O}) + (\ce{N#N}) = 4161\textrm{ kJ} \: \checkmark$\
    $\Delta H = -833 \textrm{\ kJ} \: \checkmark$

    *Award [3] for correct final answer.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The enthalpy change of the reaction is calculated as

    $\Delta H^{\minuso} = \sum (\textrm{bonds broken})  - \sum (\textrm{bonds formed})$

    First draw the Lewis structure of the reactants and products:

    ![CH2456Lewis](images/CH2456Lewis.png)

    Bonds broken: $2(\ce{C#O}) + 2(\ce{N=O})$

    $\Sigma(\textrm{bonds broken}) = 2(1077\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}) + 2(587\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}) = 3328\textrm{ kJ}$

    Bonds formed: $4(\ce{C=O}) + (\ce{N#N})$

    $\Sigma(\textrm{bonds formed}) = 4(804\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}) + 945\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1} =  4161\textrm{ kJ}$

    The overall enthalpy change is

    $\Delta H = 3328 - 4161 = -833\textrm{ kJ}$

2. $\Delta H_f^{\minuso} = -\frac{1}{2}(-833 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}) + (-282.5 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}) \: \checkmark$\
    $\Delta H_f^{\minuso} = +134 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1} \: \checkmark$

    *Award [2] for correct final answer.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Reverse and halve the first equation:

    $\ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,N2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{NO}\textrm{(g)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = -\frac{1}{2} (-833 \textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}) \: \checkmark$

    Add the second equation:

    $\ce{CO}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = -282.5\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

    The enthalpy of formation of NO is the sum of these reactions:

    $\frac{1}{2}\ce{\,N2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{NO}\textrm{(g)} $

     The enthalpy change is $\Delta H = -\frac{1}{2} (-833 \textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}) + (-282.5\textrm{ kJ}) = +134\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
