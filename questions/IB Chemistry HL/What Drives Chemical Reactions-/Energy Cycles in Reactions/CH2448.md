# CH2448

Which equation represents the bond enthalpy of the $\ce{X-Y}$ bond in $\ce{XY5}?$

All species are in the gaseous state.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{XY5}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{X}\textrm{(g)} + 5\ce{\,Y}\textrm{(g)}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{XY5}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{XY4}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Y}\textrm{(g)}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\frac{1}{5}\ce{\,XY5}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \frac{1}{5}\ce{\,X}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Y}\textrm{(g)}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{XY5}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{XY4}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{3}\ce{\,Y3}\textrm{(g)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The bond enthalpy (bond energy) of a particular bond is defined as the energy required to break one mole of that bond in gaseous molecules under standard conditions. For a molecule like $\ce{XY5},$ which contains five $\ce{X-Y}$ bonds, the total energy needed to break all five bonds and atomise the molecule is five times the individual bond enthalpy. The equation for breaking one mole of $\ce{X-Y}$ bonds must therefore show $\frac{1}{5}$ of the atomisation:

$\frac{1}{5}\ce{\,XY5}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \frac{1}{5}\ce{\,X}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Y}\textrm{(g)}$
