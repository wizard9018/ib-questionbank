# CH0168

**Maximum mark: 5**

Many instant ice packs contain the ammonium nitrate salt, $\ce{NH4NO3}$, which undergoes an endothermic reaction when dissolved in water:

$\ce{NH4NO3}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{NH4^+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{NO3-}\textrm{(aq)}$

1. When $30 \textrm{\ g}$ of $\ce{NH4NO3}$ dissolves in $100 \textrm{\ cm}^{3}$ of water, $9.00 \textrm{\ kJ}$ of energy is absorbed.
    1.  Calculate the change in temperature of the solution. Assume the specific heat capacity of the solution is the same as that of water. Use Sections 1 and 2 of the data booklet. [1 mark]

    2.  Calculate the molar enthalpy of reaction. [1 mark]

    3.  Using your answer to (a)(ii), calculate the percentage error and state the major source of this error. The literature value is $25.69 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}.$ [2 marks]

2.  If your answer to (a)(ii) was then used to calculate the lattice enthalpy of $\ce{NH4NO3}$, would you expect the theoretical lattice enthalpy of $\ce{NH4NO3}$ to be higher or lower than what you calculate? [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  $\Delta T  = \dfrac{9000\,\text{J}}{(100\,\text{g})(4.18\,\text{J}\,\text{g}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1})} = {21.53\,\text{K}}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        The temperature change can be determined by using the relationship

        $q = mc \Delta T$

        The specific heat capacity $c$ is considered the same as that of water, $c = 4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\textrm{K}^{-1}$.  Assuming the density of the solution is the same as that of water $(d = 1.00 \textrm{\ g cm}^{-3}),$ the $100 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ solution has a mass of 100 g.   Rearranging the equation to solve for the temperature change, and substituting in the values gives:

        $\Delta T = \dfrac{q}{mc} = \dfrac{9000\,\text{J}}{(100\,\text{g})(4.18\,\text{J}\,\text{g}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1})} = 21.53\,\text{K}$

    2.  $\Delta H = \dfrac{9.00\,\text{kJ}}{30 \textrm{\ g} \div 80.06  \textrm{\ g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}} = {+24.0\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        To calculate the standard enthalpy change, use the relationship $q = n\Delta H.$ Given that 30 g of ammonium nitrate dissolved, the moles can be calculated using the molar mass of the salt.

        $\ce{M(NH4NO3)} = 2 \times 14.01 + 4 \times 1.01 + 3 \times 16.00 = 80.06\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

        $n = \dfrac{m}{N} = \dfrac{30\,\text{g}}{80.06\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}} = 0.375\,\text{mol}$

        The standard enthalpy of reaction is

        $\Delta H = \dfrac{q}{n} = \dfrac{9.00\,\text{kJ}}{0.375 \textrm{\ mol}}= +24.0\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

    3.  percentage error = $\left(\dfrac{25.69-24.0}{25.69}\right)\times 100 = {6.6\, \%}\,\checkmark$\
        heat absorption from the surroundings $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        The formula for percentage error is:

        $\textrm{percentage error} = \dfrac{\vert \textrm{theoretical} - \textrm{experimental}\vert}{\textrm{theoretical}} \times 100\%$

        Substituting the values:

        $\textrm{percentage error} = \left(\dfrac{25.69-24.0}{25.69}\right)\times 100 = 6.6\, \%$

        Less heat is absorbed in the experiment than expected from the literature value.  This is likely due to limited insulation of the solution allowing heat absorption from the surroundings.  Random error in the thermometer readings would not account for this error so we can assert that the error is systematic.

        $\textrm{percentage uncertainty} = \dfrac{0.2 \textrm{\ K}}{21.53 \textrm{\ K}} \times 100 = 0.93\%$

2.  lattice enthalpy will be higher / more endothermic $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    The enthalpy cycle described in the problem can be written as

    $\Delta H_\textrm{a(ii)}^{\minuso} = \Delta H_\textrm{lattice}^{\minuso} + \Delta H_\textrm{hydration}^{\minuso}$

    If the enthalpy in a(ii) is too positive, the lattice enthalpy will be overestimated.
