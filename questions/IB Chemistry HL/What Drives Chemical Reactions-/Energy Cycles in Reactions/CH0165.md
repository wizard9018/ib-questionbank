# CH0165

The lattice enthalpy values for calcium chloride and potassium chloride are shown below:

$\Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt}(\ce{KCl}) = +720\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$\
$\Delta H^{\minuso}_{latt}(\ce{CaCl2}) = +2271\,\text{kJ}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

Which of the following are reasons why the magnitude of the lattice enthalpy of calcium chloride is higher than potassium chloride?

- I.$\hspace{1.em}$The potassium ion radius is greater than the one of calcium ion.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$The calcium ion has a higher charge than the potassium ion.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$There are two chloride ions in $\ce{CaCl2}$ and only one in $\ce{KCl}.$

- A.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Lattice enthalpy measures the energy required to separate an ionic lattice into separated ions in the gas phase.

$\ce{M_aX_b}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow a \ce{\ M^{b+}}\textrm{(g)} + b \ce{\ X^{a-}}\textrm{(g)}$

The magnitude of the attraction between the ions depends on their charge and size.
$\ce{Ca^2+}$ has the higher charge and will form ionic compounds with a higher lattice enthalpy.

$\ce{Ca^2+}$ has a greater nuclear charge with the same number of shells (same shielding effect) as $\ce{K+}$, so $\ce{Ca^2+}$ has a smaller ionic radius.
