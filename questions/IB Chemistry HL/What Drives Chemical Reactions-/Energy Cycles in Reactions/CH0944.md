# CH0944

**Maximum mark: 8**

Copper oxide can form via two pathways, as shown in the Born-Haber cycle below.

![CH0944_Image1](images/CH0944_Image1.png)

1.  Write the electron configuration of the copper ion formed in step S. [1 mark]

2.  State and explain the entropy change for step Q in the cycle. [2 marks]

3.  Write the equation for step V in the cycle. [1 mark]

4.  Explain why the enthalpy change for step S is greater than that for step R. [2 marks]

5.  Use the data in the cycle to calculate the energy associated with step W.     [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6\,3d^9$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Copper exists as $\ce{Cu^2+}$ ions in this compound and has a total number of 27 electrons.

    -   $\ce{Cu } : 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6\,3d^10\,4s^1$

    The first electron is lost from the $4s$ shell.

    -   $\ce{Cu+ } : 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6\,3d^{10}$

    The second electron is lost from the $3d$ shell.

    -   $\ce{Cu^2+ } : 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^6\,3d^9$

2.  The entropy [of the system] increases $\checkmark$

    because copper changed from a solid to a gaseous state. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Since the particles of a solid are more ordered than those of a gas, the solid has a lower entropy than the gas. When a solid changes into a gas, the particles spread and the system becomes more disordered, increasing the system's entropy.

3.  $\ce{O-(g) + e- -> O^2-(g)}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Step V refers to the energy required to add an electron to each ion in one mole of gaseous oxygen $\ce{O-}$ ions under standard conditions i.e. the enthalpy of second electron affinity.

4.  The electron removed is much closer to the nucleus $\checkmark$

    and therefore is strongly attracted to the positively charged nucleus. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    When an electron is removed from an atom, the remaining electrons' level of repulsion towards one another decreases. They are drawn in closer to the nucleus and are more strongly attracted by the nucleus; hence, it requires more energy to remove them.

5.  $- 155 = + 339 + 745 + 1960 + 249 - 141 + 753 - \Delta H_W\,\checkmark$

    $\Delta H_W = 4060\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Step 1: Apply Hess's law to equate both steps and substitute the known values.

    $\begin{aligned}
    \Delta H_P (\Delta H_f) &= \Delta H_Q (\Delta H_{at}(\ce{Cu})) + \Delta H_R (IE_1(\ce{Cu})) + \Delta H_S (IE_2(\ce{Cu})) + \Delta H_T (\Delta HH_{at}(\ce{O2})) + \Delta H_U (EA_1(\ce{O})) + \Delta H_V (EA_2(\ce{O})) - \Delta H_W (\Delta H_{latt}) \\
    -155 &= + 339 + 745 + 1960 + 249 + (-141) + 753 - \Delta H_W \\
    -155 &= + 339 + 745 + 1960 + 249 - 141 + 753 - \Delta H_W
    \end{aligned}$

    Step 2: Rearrange and calculate unknown.

    $\begin{aligned}
    \Delta H_W &= (+339 + 745 + 1960 + 249 - 141 + 753) + 155 \\
    &= 4060\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}
    \end{aligned}$
