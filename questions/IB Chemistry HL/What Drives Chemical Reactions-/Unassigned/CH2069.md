## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $K_c = \dfrac{\ce{[CH3OH]}}{\ce{[CO]}\ce{[H2]}^2}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The equilibrium constant expression includes the equilibrium concentrations of products on the numerator and reactants on the denominator, so the coefficients of the balanced equation appear as exponents.

    $\ce{a A + b B <=> c C + d D} \quad  K_c = \dfrac{\ce{[C]}^c \ce{[D]}^d}{\ce{[A]}^a \ce{[B]}^b}$

    In this case:

    $\ce{CO(g) + 2 H2(g) <=> CH3OH(g)} \quad K_c = \dfrac{\ce{[CH3OH]}}{\ce{[CO]}\ce{[H2]}^2}$

2.  increase in pressure shifts reaction to side with fewer moles of gas $\checkmark$\
    increase in the yield of methanol $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    This chemical reaction has fewer gas on the reactant and product side. As a result, changes in pressure affect the equilibrium. An increase in pressure is counteracted by shifting the equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas, favouring methanol formation. The yield of methanol will increase.

    This can also be explored algebraically. To increase the pressure of all of the gases, the volume needs to be lowered. Concentration is defined as the ratio of moles to volume, $C = \dfrac{n}{V}.$ Substituting this into the equilibrium constant expression gives

    $K_c = \dfrac{\frac{n_\ce{CH3OH}}{V}}{\frac{n_\ce{CO}}{V} \left({\frac{n_\ce{H2}}{V}}\right)^2} = \dfrac{{n_\ce{CH3OH}}}{{n_\ce{CO}} \left({{n_\ce{H2}}}\right)^2}\times V^2$

    Since the equilibrium constant does not change at constant temperature, if the volume decrease (because the pressure increased) the molar ratio must increase. The reaction will shift forward and there will be more methanol produced.

4.  $\Delta S < 0\ \checkmark$\
    more moles $\underline{\text{of gas}}$ on the reactant side / fewer moles $\underline{\text{of gas}}$ on the product side $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The chemical equation is given below.

    $\ce{CO(g) + 2 H2(g) <=> CH3OH(g)}$

    The reaction has 3 moles of gas on the reactant side and one mole on the product side. There will be a decrease in the system's disorder, and so the entropy change is negative.

5.  at low $T,$ $\Delta H$ dominates $\Delta G < 0,$ spontaneous\
    **AND**
    at high $T,$ $-T\Delta S$ dominates $\Delta G > 0,$ not spontaneous $\checkmark$\
    $\Delta G$ increases as $T$ increases $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further information:}}$\
    Using the expression $\Delta G = \Delta H - T \Delta S$, the temperature dependence of $\Delta G$ can be explored. For this reaction,  $\Delta H < 0,$ and $\Delta S < 0.$

    At very low temperatures, the first term dominates so $\Delta G \approx \Delta H < 0$ and the reaction is spontaneous. At very high temperatures, the second term dominates so $\Delta G \approx -T \Delta S > 0$ and the reaction is not spontaneous. As temperature increases, $\Delta G$ increases, reducing the reaction's spontaneity.
