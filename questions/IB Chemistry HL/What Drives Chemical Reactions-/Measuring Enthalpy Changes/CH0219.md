# CH0219

Which reaction is exothermic?

- A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{NH4NO3}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{NH4+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{NO3-}\textrm{(aq)}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{6CO2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{6H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{C6H12O6}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{6O2}\textrm{(g)}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH4}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{2O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{2H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{N2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{2H2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{N2H4}\textrm{(g)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The combustion of $\ce{CH4}$ with $\ce{O2}$ is exothermic $(\Delta H < 0).$ The energy released in forming the strong $\ce{C=O}$ and $\ce{O-H}$ bonds in the products exceeds the energy required to break the $\ce{C-H}$ and $\ce{O=O}$ bonds in the reactants.

The dissolution of $\ce{NH4NO3}$ in water is endothermic, this is why ammonium nitrate is used in cold packs. Photosynthesis (the reaction of $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{H2O}$ to form glucose and $\ce{O2}$) is endothermic. The formation of hydrazine $(\ce{N2H4})$ from its elements is also endothermic, as energy is needed to break the strong $\ce{N#N}$ triple bond $(945 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}).$
