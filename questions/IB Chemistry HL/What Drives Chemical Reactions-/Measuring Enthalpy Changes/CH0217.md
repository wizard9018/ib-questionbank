# CH0217

A block of nickel of mass $70\text{\ g}$ whose initial temperature $T_1 = 18.70\,{}^\circ \text{C}$, absorbs 350 J of heat. Given that the specific heat capacity of $\ce{Ni}$ is approximately 0.50 J g$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$, calculate the final temperature of the block $T_2$, in ${}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

- A.$\hspace{1em}+ 28.7\,{}^\circ \text{C}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}- 28.7\,{}^\circ \text{C}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}+ 11.3\,{}^\circ \text{C}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}- 11.3\,{}^\circ \text{C}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
To determine the final temperature of the nickel block, we use the relationship between heat absorbed, mass, specific heat capacity, and the change in temperature, $Q = mc\Delta T.$ Rearranging to isolate $\Delta T$ and substituting the given values:

$\Delta T = \dfrac{Q}{m  c} = \dfrac{350\,\text{J}}{70\,\text{g} \times 0.5\,\text{J g}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}} = 10.0 \textrm{\ K}$

Because the block absorbs heat, its temperature increases by this amount. Since a change of one kelvin is equal in magnitude to a change of one degree Celsius, the temperature increase of 10.0 K can be added directly to the initial temperature expressed in degrees Celsius. Starting from an initial temperature of $18.7\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, the final temperature is $18.7 + 10.0 = 28.7\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$
