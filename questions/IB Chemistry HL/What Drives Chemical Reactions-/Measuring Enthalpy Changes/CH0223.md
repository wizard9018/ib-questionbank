# CH0223

**Maximum mark: 5**

When a 2.80 g of anhydrous copper sulfate sample, $\ce{CuSO4}$ was added to 20.0 g of water in a calorimeter, a rise in temperature of $15.0\,{}^\circ \text{C}$ was recorded.

Given that the specific heat capacity of water is 4.18 J g$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$;

1.  Define specific heat capacity. [1 mark]

2.  Calculate the change in enthalpy in kJ.  [1 mark]

3.  Calculate the number of moles of copper Sulfate.   [1 mark]

4.  Calculate the molar enthalpy of reaction.  [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The amount of heat needed to raise the temperature of $1 \textrm{\ g}$ of a substance by $1\,{}^\circ \textrm{C}$ or $1 \textrm{\ K}.$ $\checkmark$

2.  $\ \\[-13pt]$
    $\mbox{\hspace{0mm}}
    \begin{aligned}
    q &= mc\Delta T \\
    &= (20.0 \textrm{\ g} )(4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1} \textrm{K}^{-1}) (15.0\textrm{\ K})\\
    &= 1254 \textrm{\ J} = 1.25\, \textrm{kJ}\, \checkmark
    \end{aligned}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Our solution assumes that the heat of the reaction was absorbed by the water.  In the $q = mc\Delta T$ equation, the mass considered is that of the water.  The density is assumed to be that of water. The heat capacity is assumed to be that of water.  These are approximations used to solve the problem as the copper sulfate solution would have a different heat capacity and a different density.  Some students choose to take the mass of the water plus the mass of the solid as $m$ in the equation above, that is also acceptable.

3.  $\ \\[-15pt]$
    $\mbox{\hspace{0mm}}
    \begin{aligned}
    n(\ce{CuSO4}) &= \dfrac{m}{M}\, \\
    &= \dfrac{2.80\textrm{\ g}}{159.62\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} \\
    &= 0.0175\textrm{\ mol}\, \checkmark
    \end{aligned}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    $\ce{M(CuSO4}) = 63.55 + 32.07 + 4(16.00) = 159.62 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$

4.  $\dfrac{q}{n} = \dfrac{1254\textrm{\ J}}{0.0175\textrm{\ mol}}\,\checkmark = 71657.1\textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}$ or $71.7\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

    $\Delta H = -71.7 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    As the temperature increased, the reaction is exothermic. $\Delta H$ must therefore be negative.
