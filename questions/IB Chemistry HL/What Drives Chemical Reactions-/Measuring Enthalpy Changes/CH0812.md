# CH0812

**Maximum mark: 12**

The enthalpy of neutralization for the reaction between sulfuric acid and potassium hydroxide can be determined experimentally.

$\ce{H2SO4(aq) + 2KOH(aq) -> K2SO4(aq) + 2H2O(l)}$

A student recorded the temperature of 20.0 cm$^3$ of 0.500 mol dm$^{-3}$ sulfuric acid every minute. At four minutes, 25.0 cm$^3$  $\ce{KOH(aq)}$ is added to the solution, stirred immediately, and temperature recorded for another four minutes. The results are represented in the graph below.

![CH0812](images/CH0812.png)

1.  Calculate the lowest concentration of aqueous KOH that must be used to ensure it is in excess. [2 marks]

2.  Use the graph above to estimate a value for the maximum temperature change, $\Delta T$. [2 marks]

3.  State one assumption you made in part (b). [1 mark]

4.  Identify the anomalous result and suggest a possible type and source of error. [2 marks]

5.  In another experiment with the same reactants, the student measured the initial and final temperatures and presented their results below:

    $\mbox{}\\[-20pts]$

    |$\text{Initial Temperature }(^\circ\text{C} \pm 0.1\,{}^\circ\text{C})$ | $24.8$ | $25.1$ | $24.9$ |
    |:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
    |Final Temperature ($^\circ$C $\pm$ 0.1 $^\circ$C) | 26.4 | 26.6 | 26.4

    $\mbox{}\\[-10pts]$

    1.  Calculate the average change in temperature ($\Delta T$) giving its percentage uncertainty. [3 marks]

    2.  Use Sections 1 and 2 of the data booklet to calculate the energy released and its uncertainty. You may assume that the only source of uncertainty is from the temperature change. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $n_\ce{H2SO4} = 0.500\times 20.0\times 10^{-3} = 0.0100\,\text{mol}$ **AND** $n_{\ce{KOH}} = 0.0100\times 2 = 0.0200\,\text{mol}$ $\checkmark$\
    $C_{\ce{KOH}} > \dfrac{0.0200}{0.0250} = 0.800\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$    $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    $\ce{H2SO4}$ and KOH react in a $1:2$ ratio. To be in excess, more moles of KOH must be present than required to exactly neutralize the $\ce{H2SO4}$.

2.  best$-$fit line extrapolated to 4 minutes $\checkmark$\
    $\Delta T = 26.6 - 25.0 = 1.6\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} \: \checkmark$

    ![CH0812-MS](images/CH0812-MS.png)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Extrapolation assumes that the maximum temperature would have been reached immediately after mixing. Subtraction of values means the least number of decimal places must be maintained.

3.  linear cooling $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Drawing a line of best fit and extrapolating to minute 4 assumes that temperature decreased linearly from the time of mixing.

4.  $t = 7 \textrm{\ min} \: \checkmark$
    random error **AND** fluctuations in electronic thermometer readings $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    The error is random and not systematic as it is an individual anomalous result. The temperature reading is higher than it should be and so any reasonable explanation for that can be considered.

5.  1.  Average = $\dfrac{1.6 + 1.5 + 1.5}{3} = 1.53 = 1.5\,{}^\circ$C $\checkmark$\
        Absolute uncertainty =  $\pm 0.2\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} \: \checkmark$\
        Percentage uncertainty = $\dfrac{0.2}{1.5} \times 100 = 13.3\% \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In subtracting the values to calculate each change in temperature, the least number of decimal places (1) must be maintained. The average can not be given to a higher level of precision than each individual value and so must also be to 1dp.

        Uncertainties for each measurement are added giving $\pm 0.2\,{}^\circ$C.

        Percentage uncertainty is calculated as $\dfrac{\textrm{absolute uncertainty}}{\textrm{measured value}} \times 100$

    2.  $q = mc\Delta T = 45.0 \times 4.18 \times 1.5 = 282.15\,\text{J} \Longrightarrow 280 \textrm{\ J} \: \checkmark$\
        absolute uncertainty = $282.15 \times \dfrac{13.3}{100} = 37.62 \Longrightarrow \pm 40 \textrm{\ J} \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        $q = mc\Delta T$ where $m$ is the total mass of the solutions (20.0 g + 25.0 g assuming a density of $1 \textrm{\ g cm}^{-3}).$. The least number of significant figures must be used as it is a multiplication operation. Two significant figures considering 1.5 from the temperature change.  The precision of the absolute uncertainty must match the precision of the calculated value.
