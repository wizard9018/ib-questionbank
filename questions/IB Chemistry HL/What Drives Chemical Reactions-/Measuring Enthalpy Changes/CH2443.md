# CH2443

Four metal samples are each supplied with $75.0\textrm{ kJ}$ of energy. The mass and specific heat capacity of each metal are shown below.

| $\hspace{0.95em}$ || $\hspace{0.1em}$ |  Metal Sample  | c / J g$^\mathbf{-1}\,{}^\mathbf{\circ}$C$^\mathbf{-1}$ |
| :-- |:-:| :-- | :-: | :-: |
|| A. || $1.0\textrm{ g Mg}$ | $1.02$ |
|| B. || $7.5\textrm{ g Pb}$ | $0.13$ |
|| C. || $5.0\textrm{ g Al}$ | $0.90$ |
|| D. || $5.0\textrm{ g Fe}$ | $0.41$ |

Which metal has the largest increase in temperature?

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Rearranging $q = mc\Delta T$ gives $\Delta T = \dfrac{q}{mc}.$ Since every sample receives the same energy $(q = 75.0\textrm{ kJ}),$ the sample with the smallest product $m \times c$ will undergo the largest temperature change.

Estimating $m \times c$ for each sample:

$\textrm{Mg: } 1.0\textrm{ g} \times 1.02\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} = 1.02 \textrm{\ J}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$

$\textrm{Pb: } 7.5\textrm{ g} \times 0.13\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} = 0.975 \textrm{\ J}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$

$\textrm{Al: } 5.0\textrm{ g} \times 0.90\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} = 4.50\textrm{\ J}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$

$ \textrm{Fe: } 5.0\textrm{ g} \times 0.41\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} = 2.05 \textrm{\ J}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$

Lead has the smallest $mc$ product, so lead undergoes the largest temperature increase.

This question rewards the estimation strategy of comparing $mc$ products rather than computing all four $\Delta T$ values individually. On a timed paper, identifying that $\Delta T \propto \dfrac{1}{mc}$ and comparing the denominators is a more efficient approach.
