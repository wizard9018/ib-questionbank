# CH1001

When 40.0 cm$^3$ of 0.200 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{NaOH(aq)}$ is mixed with 40.0 cm$^3$ of 0.200 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{HNO3(aq)}$ at 25.0 $^\circ$C, the temperature increases to 55.0 $^\circ$C.

What is the expression, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}$, for the enthalpy of this neutralization reaction?

*Assume the solution has the density and specific heat capacity of pure water.*

-   A. $\hspace{1em}-\dfrac{80.0\times 4.18\times 30.0}{80.0\times 0.200}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}-\dfrac{80.0\times 4.18\times 30.0}{40.0\times 0.200}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}-\dfrac{40.0\times 4.18\times 30.0}{80.0\times 0.200}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}-\dfrac{40.0\times 4.18\times 30.0}{40.0\times 0.200}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Students must interpret the neutralization reaction, as shown below.

$\ce{NaOH(aq) + HNO3(aq) -> NaNO3(aq) + H2O(l)}$

Neutralization reactions are exothermic. The enthalpy change is negative for this process, $\Delta H_{neut} < 0.$

To determine the enthalpy of neutralization, the heat absorbed by the solution was measured using $q_{sol} = mc\Delta T.$ The heat gained by the solution was lost by the neutralization reaction, assuming no heat loss, $q_{neut} = -q_{sol}$.  Finally, the enthalpy of neutralization is the ratio fo the heat of neutralization and the moles that reacted.

$\Delta H_{neut} = - \dfrac{mc \Delta T}{n}$

***Heat capacity:***

The specific heat capactiy of the aqueous solution is assumed to be that of water:

$c = 4.18\,\text{kJ kg}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}$

Since the question asks for $\Delta H$ in kJ, the heat capacity in kJ was selected.

***Mass of solution:***

The acid and base are mixed so that 40.0 cm$^3$ are mixed with 40.0 cm$^3$. The volume of the aqueous mixture is approximately 80.0 cm$^3$. The density of the aqueous solution is assumed to be that of water:

$m = d\times V = 1.00\,\text{g cm}^{-3}\times 80.0\,\text{cm}^3 = 80.0\,\text{g}$

$m = 80.0\,\text{g}\times \dfrac{1\,\text{kg}}{1000\,\text{g}}$

The mass is reported in kg to match the units of the heat capacity.

***Change in temperature:***

The temperature of the solution increases from 25.0 $^\circ$C to 55.0 $^\circ$C.

$\Delta T = 55.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 25.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} = 30.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}\longrightarrow 30.0\,\text{K}$

Recall that the size of Kelvin and Celsius degrees are the same so $\Delta T$ can be expressed in either unit.  Alternatively, convert both temperatures to Kelvin and subtract: $\Delta T = 328\,\text{K} - 298\,\text{K} = 30\,\text{K}.$

***Number of moles:***

40.0 cm$^3$ of 0.200 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{NaOH(aq)}$ is mixed with 40.0 cm$^3$ of 0.200 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{HNO3(aq)}$.  The number of moles of the acid and base are equal and the reactants combine in a $1:1$ ratio:

$n = C\times V = \dfrac{0.200\,\text{mol}}{1\,\text{dm}^3}\times 40.0\,\text{cm}^3\times \dfrac{1\,\text{dm}^3}{1000\,\text{cm}^3}$

***Enthalpy of neutralization:***

$\Delta H_{neut} = - \dfrac{mc \Delta T}{n}$

$\Delta H_{neut} = - \dfrac{80.0\,\text{g}\times \dfrac{1\,\text{kg}}{1000\,\text{g}}\times 4.18\,\text{kJ kg}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}\times 30.0\,\text{K}}{\dfrac{0.200\,\text{mol}}{1\,\text{dm}^3}\times 40.0\,\text{cm}^3\times \dfrac{1\,\text{dm}^3}{1000\,\text{cm}^3}}$

$\Delta H_{neut} = - \dfrac{80.0\times 4.18\times 30.0}{0.200\times 40.0}$

This question is categorized as hard as students must calculate the heat and the neutralized amount, evaluate the sign of the enthalpy and analyze the calculations and conversions to get the requested unit.
