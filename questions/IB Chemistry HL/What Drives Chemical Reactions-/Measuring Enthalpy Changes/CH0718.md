# CH0718

The accurate enthalpy change for the reaction of aqueous hydrochloric acid with solid sodium carbonate is $\Delta H = -36\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. 

 

A student tried to determine $\Delta H$ by adding 3.00 g of sodium carbonate to 100 cm$^3$ aqueous hydrochloric acid and measuring the temperature change of the solution, $\Delta T$. They obtained an experimental value $\Delta H_{exp} = -38\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}.$

The table shows the values used by the student in their calculation, using $q = mc\Delta T$.

$\textrm{Solution density,\ } d$| $\textrm{1.00 g cm}^{-3}$

|:-:|:-:|

Solution mass, $m$ |100 g

Heat capacity, $c$ |4.186 J g$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$

Temperature change, $\Delta T$|2.6 K

Which of the following could account for the difference between $\Delta H$ and $\Delta H_{exp}$?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The solution density was greater than 1.00 g cm$^{-3}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The solution mass was increased by the addition of the sodium carbonate

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The heat capacity was less than 4.186 J g$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The solution lost heat to the surroundings during the reaction

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

The accepted value is $\Delta H = -36 \textrm{\ kJ\,mol}^{-1},$ while the student obtained $\Delta H = -38 \textrm{\ kJ\,mol}^{-1}.$ The experimental value is therefore more exothermic than the accepted value.

Using

$q=mc\Delta T \hspace{2.em} \textrm{\ and\ } \hspace{2.em}  \Delta H = -\dfrac{q}{n}$

a value of $\Delta H$ that is too negative must result from an overestimate of $q.$

Errors that make $m$ or $\Delta T$  too small would lead to an underestimate of $q$ and therefore to a value of $\Delta H$that is less exothermic, the opposite of what was observed. Examples include assuming a density that is too low, ignoring the extra mass from the dissolved solid, or losing heat to the surroundings (which lowers $\Delta T).$

By contrast, assuming a specific heat capacity larger than the true value causes $q = mc\Delta T$

to be overestimated. This leads directly to a value of $\Delta H$ that is more negative than the accepted value, matching the experimental result.
