# CH2564

**Maximum mark: 9**

An alcohol burner is used to calculate the enthalpy of combustion of pentanol, $\ce{C5H11OH}$ $(M = 88.15\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}).$

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

|$\textrm{Mass of pentanol combusted / g}$| $1.55$ |
:--|--:
Mass of water heated / g |  100.0
Initial temperature / $^\circ\textrm{C}$ | 20.0
Final temperature / $^\circ\textrm{C}$  | 85.5
Specific heat capacity of water/ $\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$ | 4.18

1. State the balanced chemical equation for the complete combustion of pentanol. [1 mark]

2. Calculate the change in temperature and use this to explain whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the heat absorbed by the water, in $\textrm{kJ}.$ [1 mark]

4. Calculate the moles of pentanol combusted. [1 mark]

5. Calculate the molar enthalpy of combustion of pentanol, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}.$ [1 mark]

6. The literature value for the standard enthalpy of combustion of pentanol is $-3331\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$ Calculate the percentage error between the experimental and literature values. [1 mark]

7. Suggest two reasons why the experimental value is less exothermic than the literature value. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $2\ce{\,C5H11OH}\textrm{(l)} + 15\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 10\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 12\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Complete combustion of any alcohol (or hydrocarbon) always produces $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{H2O}.$ Balance carbon first $(10\ce{\,CO2}),$ then hydrogen $(12\ce{\,H2O}),$ then count the oxygen atoms on the product side $(20 + 12 = 32)$ and balance with $\ce{O2}.$ The two $\ce{OH}$ groups in the reactant supply $2$ oxygen atoms, leaving $30$ to come from $\ce{O2},$ giving $15\ce{\,O2}.$

2. $\Delta T = 65.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}\:\checkmark$\
    exothermic, energy released to surroundings $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The water temperature rises:

    $\Delta T = 85.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} - 20.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} = 65.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

    The system (the combustion reaction) releases energy, that energy is transferred to the surroundings and is observed as the water temperature rising.     An exothermic process is defined as one in which energy flows from the system to the surroundings, corresponding to a negative $\Delta H.$

3. $q = 27.4 \textrm{\ kJ}\: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The heat absorbed by the water is

    $q = mc\Delta T = 100.0\textrm{ g} \times 4.18\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} \times 65.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} = 27\,379\textrm{ J}
    $

    In kJ, the heat absorbed by the water is $q = 27.4\textrm{ kJ}$

4. $n  = 0.0176\textrm{ mol}$ $\checkmark$

     $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
     The moles of pentan$-$1$-$ol  is the mass divided by the molar mass:

    $n = \dfrac{m}{M} = \dfrac{1.55\textrm{ g}}{88.15\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.0176\textrm{ mol}$

5. $\Delta H =  -1560\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The heat released by the reaction is

    $q_{rxn} = -q_{water},$

    where we make the implicit assumption that all of the heat from the combustion was absorbed by the water.  The enthalpy of the reaction is the heat of the reaction divided by the number of moles that reacted.

    $\Delta H = -\dfrac{q}{n} = -\dfrac{27.4\textrm{ kJ}}{0.01758\textrm{ mol}} = -1560\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

6. $\textrm{\% error} =  53.2\% \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Percentage error compares the absolute difference between the experimental and literature values to the literature value.

    $\textrm{\% error} = \left \vert \dfrac{-3331 - (-1558)}{-3331} \right \vert \times 100 = 53.2\%$

    The large percentage error $(>50\%)$ is typical for alcohol burner experiments due to the systematic errors described in part (g).

7. Any two of the following:

    -   heat loss to the surroundings $\checkmark$
    -   incomplete combustion of pentanol  $\checkmark$
    -   evaporation of pentanol from the wick  $\checkmark$
    -   heat absorbed by the equipment  $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Heat loss, incomplete combustion, and heat absorbed by equipment each cause the measured $\Delta T$ to be smaller than it would be under ideal conditions, giving a smaller $q$ from $q = mc\Delta T.$ Evaporation increases the apparent mass of pentanol combusted, increasing $n$ in the denominator of $\Delta H = -q/n$ and reducing $|\Delta H|.$
