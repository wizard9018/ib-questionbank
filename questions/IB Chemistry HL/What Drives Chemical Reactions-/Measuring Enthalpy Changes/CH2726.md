# CH2726

**Maximum mark: 9**

A student determined the concentration of a sodium hydroxide solution by thermometric titration. A $25.00 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ portion of $\ce{NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}$ was placed in a polystyrene cup and $2.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ hydrochloric acid was added $1.00 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ at a time from a burette, stirring and recording the steady temperature after each addition.

![CH2726a](images/CH2726a.png)

**Graph 1:** Temperature of the mixture *vs.* volume of $\ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)}$ added.

1. Explain why a polystyrene cup is used rather than a glass beaker. [1 mark]

 2. On **Graph 1,** draw a line of best fit through the rising temperatures and another through the falling temperatures, and extend them until they meet. Use your lines to determine the equivalence volume and the temperature change, $\Delta T,$ at the equivalence point. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution. [1 mark]

4. Outline why the temperature decreases when acid is added beyond the equivalence point. [2 marks]

5. Determine the enthalpy of neutralization, in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}.$ Assume the mixture has the density and specific heat capacity of water; use sections 1 and 2 of the IB Chemistry data booklet.

    If you did not obtain values in part (b), use $11.00 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ and $8.0 \textrm{\ K},$ although these are not the correct answers. [2 marks]

6. Suggest why the extrapolated temperature rise gives a more valid value of the enthalpy of neutralization than the highest recorded temperature. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. polystyrene is a  thermal insulator / reduces heat loss to the surroundings $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    A calorimeter must keep the released heat in the solution, where the thermometer records it. Polystyrene conducts little heat outward and absorbs almost none itself, so the assumption that only the solution warms is nearly true. A glass beaker conducts heat to the surroundings and absorbs some heat itself; both losses make the reaction appear less exothermic.

2. $V_{\textrm{eq}} = 11.5 \textrm{\ cm}^3$  $\checkmark$ \
    $\Delta T = 8.4 \textrm{\ K} \: \checkmark$

    *Accept $V_{\textrm{eq}}$ from 11.2 to 11.8  cm$^\mathit{3}$ and $\Delta T$ from 8.2 to 8.6  K.*

    ![CH2726ms](images/CH2726ms.png)

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Neither set of points peaks at the true equivalence point, because heat is lost throughout the titration and the temperature is recorded only after each $1.00 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ addition. Each set of points lies close to a straight line, so the two lines are drawn separately and extended until they meet. The intersection gives the equivalence volume, $11.5 \textrm{\ cm}^3,$ and the temperature the mixture would have reached with no heat loss, $28.4\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ The starting temperature was $20.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C},$ so

    $\Delta T = 28.4\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} - 20.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} = 8.4 \textrm{\ K}$

    A change of $1\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ is the same as a change of $1 \textrm{\ K},$ so $\Delta T$ can be given in either unit.

3. $c(\ce{NaOH}) = 0.920 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ $\checkmark$

    *Accept values consistent with $V_{\textrm{eq}}$ from part (b), 0.896 to 0.944  mol dm$^\mathit{\,-3}$.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    At the equivalence point the amount of acid added equals the amount of base present, because $\ce{NaOH}$ and $\ce{HCl}$ react in a $1 : 1$ ratio:

    $n(\ce{HCl}) = 2.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.01150 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.0230 \textrm{\ mol}$

    $c(\ce{NaOH}) = \dfrac{0.0230 \textrm{\ mol}}{0.02500 \textrm{\ dm}^3} = 0.920 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$

4. no further reaction occurs, so no more heat is released $\checkmark$\
    same heat is shared by a larger mass of solution, so the temperature falls $\checkmark$

    *Accept the added acid is cooler than the mixture for M2.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Heat is released only while acid reacts with base. Once the base is used up at the equivalence point, the added acid has nothing to react with, so no more heat is produced. Each further addition increases the mass of solution in the cup, and the same amount of heat is now spread over more solution, so the temperature falls. The added acid is also at room temperature, cooler than the warm mixture, so each addition cools the mixture further.

5. $q = 1.28 \textrm{\ kJ}$ $\checkmark$ \
    $\Delta H = -55.7 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    *Award [2] for correct final answer.*\
    *Accept $-$54.7 kJ mol$^\mathit{\,-1}$ from the values 11.00  cm$^\mathit{3}$ and 8.0 K.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The heat released warms the whole mixture at the equivalence point. Assuming a density of $1.00 \textrm{\ g cm}^{-3},$ its mass is

    $m = \left(25.00 \textrm{\ cm}^3 + 11.50 \textrm{\ cm}^3\right) \times 1.00 \textrm{\ g cm}^{-3} = 36.5 \textrm{\ g}$

    The heat absorbed by the mixture is determined with $q = mc\Delta T.$

    $q = 36.5 \textrm{\ g} \times 4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\textrm{ K}^{-1} \times 8.4 \textrm{\ K} = 1.28 \times 10^3 \textrm{\ J} = 1.28 \textrm{\ kJ}$

    The amount of water formed equals the amount of acid added at the equivalence point:

    $n(\ce{H2O}) = n(\ce{HCl}) = 2.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.01150 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.0230 \textrm{\ mol}$

    The reaction released the heat that the mixture absorbed, so $\Delta H$ is negative:

    $\Delta H = -\dfrac{q}{n} = -\dfrac{1.28 \textrm{\ kJ}}{0.0230 \textrm{\ mol}} = -55.7 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

6. extrapolation estimates the temperature the mixture would have reached with no heat loss, whereas the recorded maximum has already lost heat $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    By the time the highest temperature is recorded, the cup has been losing heat for several minutes, so the reading is below the rise that would occur with no heat loss. The intersection of the two ruled lines estimates the temperature at the instant of complete reaction, as if all the heat had been released before any was lost.
