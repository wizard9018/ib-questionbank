# CH0720

A 4.30 g sample of butane is combusted, and the released energy is used to heat 750 g of water. The temperature of the water increases by 68 $^\circ$C.
  What is the enthalpy of combustion of butane, $\Delta H_c$, in $\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$?

$c_\ce{H2O} = 4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\textrm{K}^{-1}$;

$M_r(\ce{butane}) = 58.1$;

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_c = \dfrac{-750 \times 4.18  \times 68  \times 58.1} {1000 \times 4.30}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_c = \dfrac{-750 \times 4.18  \times 68  \times 4.30} {1000 \times 58.1}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_c = \dfrac{-750 \times 4.18  \times (68  + 273) \times 58.1} {1000 \times 4.30}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\Delta H_c = \dfrac{-750 \times 4.18  \times (68  + 273) \times 4.30} {1000 \times 58.1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The student must recognise that $q = -n\Delta H_c$, where $n$ is the number of moles of butane combusted, and that $n = \dfrac{m}{M_r}$.

Therefore,

 $\Delta H_c = \dfrac{-q}{n} = \dfrac{-mc\Delta T}{n}$

 $\Delta H_c = -mcΔT \times \dfrac{M_r}{m}$

The final answer must be given in units of $\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$ but $q$ is calculated in J, the SI unit, so a division by 1000 is needed. The value of $\Delta T$ is a temperature difference, not an absolute temperature, so the units of $^\circ$C and K are equivalent: the value is not increased by 273.

This question is categorised as hard as the student must recall how to convert $q$ to an enthalpy change and the mass of a reactant to the number of moles, correctly rearrange an equation involving many terms, and understand when an additive term is needed to convert between different temperature units.
