# CH2445

When $10.0\textrm{ cm}^3$ of $0.100\textrm{ mol dm}^{-3}$ $\ce{HNO3}$ is mixed with $10.0\textrm{ cm}^3$ of $0.100\textrm{ mol dm}^{-3}$ $\ce{LiOH},$ the temperature of the solution changes from $18.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ to $20.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

If the volumes of both the acid and the base are doubled to $20.0\textrm{ cm}^3$ at the same concentrations, what is the expected final temperature? Assume the initial temperature is $18.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}20.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}20.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}21.8\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}22.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Doubling the volume of each solution at the same concentration doubles the number of moles of both acid and base. Twice as many moles react, so twice as much heat is released: $q_2 = 2q_1.$

However, the total mass of solution also doubles (from $20.0\textrm{ g}$ to $40.0\textrm{ g},$ assuming density $= 1.00\textrm{ g cm}^{-3}).$ The temperature change depends on $\Delta T = \dfrac{q}{mc}.$ Because both $q$ and $m$ double while $c$ remains constant, $\Delta T$ is unchanged:

$\Delta T = \dfrac{2q_1}{2m_1 \times c} = \dfrac{q_1}{m_1 \times c} = 2.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

The final temperature is $18.0\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}+ 2.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} = 20.5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C},$ the same as the original experiment.
