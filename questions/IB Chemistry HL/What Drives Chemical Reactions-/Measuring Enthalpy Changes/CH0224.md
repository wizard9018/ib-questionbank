# CH0224

**Maximum mark: 5**

An uknown mass of magnesium was added to $500\textrm{\ cm}^3$ of aqueous hydrochloric acid in a styrofoam (polystyrene) cup to form magnesium chloride. The temperature at the start was $18^\circ\textrm{C}$ and after all of the magnesium had dissolved, the temperature rose to $20\,{}^\circ \textrm{C}$.

1.  State a balanced chemical equation for this reaction. [1 mark]

2.  The literature value for the enthalpy change for this reaction is $\Delta H = -143 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}.$ Using the specific heat capacity in Section 2 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, calculate the mass of magnesium added to the hydrochloric acid. Assume that the density of the solution is $1 \textrm{\ g cm}^{-3}.$[2 marks]

3.  Identify one systematic error, other than the approximations used in part (b), in this experimental set$-$up and comment on its effect. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{Mg}\textrm{(s)} + 2 \ce{\ HCl}\textrm{(aq)} → \ce{MgCl2}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)}\,\checkmark$
    *State symbols not required for the mark.*

2.  $q_\ce{H2O} = m C \Delta T = 500 \textrm{\ g} \times 4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} \times 2^\circ\textrm{C} \:\checkmark$\
    $m = \dfrac{q_\ce{H2O}M}{\Delta H} = \dfrac{-4180 \textrm{\ J} \times 24.31 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}}{-143000 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}} =  0.711 \textrm{\ g Mg}\:\checkmark$\
    *Award both marks for final correct answer*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    First determine the heat absorbed by the solution using the relationship $q = mC\Delta T.$. The mass will be that of the solution, $500 \textrm{\ cm}^3 = 500 \textrm{\ g}$, the heat capactiy is $4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$ and the change in temperature is from 18$^\circ$C to 20 $^\circ$C, or 2 $^\circ$C.

    $q_\ce{H2O} = m C \Delta T = 500 \textrm{\ g} \times 4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} \times 2\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

    $q_\ce{H2O} = 4180 \textrm{\ J}$

    The solution gained the heat from the exothermic chemical reaction.

    $q_{rxn} = -q_\ce{H2O}$

    The enthalpy change of the reaction is known to be $\Delta H = -143 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$.  So, the relationship, $q = n\Delta H$ will allow us to deduce how much Mg reacted by comparing the heat released during this chemical reaction to the heat released when one mole reacts. Make sure both values are in J. Rearrange the relationship to isolate $n$.

    $n = \dfrac{q_{rxn}}{\Delta H} = \dfrac{-4180 \textrm{\ J}}{-143000 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}}$

    $n = 0.0292 \textrm{\ mol}$

    The mass of magnesium can finally be determined using the molar mass:

    $m = n M = 0.0292 \textrm{\ mol} \times 24.31 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$

    $m = 0.711 \textrm{\ g Mg}$

3.  heat lost to the surroundings$\:\checkmark$\
    calculated mass will be smaller than the actual mass$\: \checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    The amount of heat measured will be smaller than expected due to heat loss to the environment, so the calculation will indicate that less Mg has reacted.
