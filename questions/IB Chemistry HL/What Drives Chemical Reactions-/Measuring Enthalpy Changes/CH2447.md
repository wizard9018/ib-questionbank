# CH2447

The standard enthalpy change of neutralisation between a strong acid and a strong base is approximately $-57\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1},$ regardless of which strong acid and strong base are used.

Which statement best explains this observation?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Strong acids and strong bases are fully dissociated in solution, so the net reaction is always the same.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The enthalpy change depends on the specific acid$-$base pair.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$All neutralisation reactions are exothermic.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Water is always formed as a product.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Strong acids and strong bases dissociate completely in aqueous solution. Regardless of the identity of the acid or base, the ions that participate in the neutralisation reaction are always $\ce{H+}\textrm{(aq)}$ and $\ce{OH-}\textrm{(aq)}.$ The spectator ions (such as $\ce{Na+},$ $\ce{K+},$ $\ce{Cl-},$ or $\ce{NO3-})$ do not participate in bond formation or breaking. The net ionic equation is therefore always

$\ce{H+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{OH-}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{2em} \Delta H = -57\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Because the same bond $(\ce{O-H}$ in water$)$ is formed in every case, the same amount of energy is released.

The statement that the enthalpy change depends on the specific pair contradicts the observation given in the stem. While it is true that all neutralisation reactions are exothermic and that water is always a product, neither of these facts explains why the enthalpy change is the same constant value. Many different exothermic reactions release different amounts of energy, and many different reactions produce water with different $\Delta H$ values. The distinguishing feature is that the net ionic equation is identical for all strong acid$-$strong base combinations.

When a weak acid or weak base is involved, the enthalpy of neutralisation differs from $-57\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ because additional energy is required to dissociate the weak electrolyte. For example, the neutralisation of ethanoic acid by $\ce{NaOH}$ has $\Delta H \approx -55\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1},$ reflecting the endothermic dissociation of $\ce{CH3COOH}.$
