# CH2446

The standard enthalpy change for the following reaction is $-286\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

$\ce{H2}\textrm{(g)} + \frac{1}{2}\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

What is the standard enthalpy change for this reaction?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$2\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}-143\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}-286\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}-572\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}+572\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The second equation is exactly twice the first: every coefficient has been multiplied by $2.$ When a balanced equation is multiplied by a factor $n,$ the enthalpy change is also multiplied by $n.$ This is because the enthalpy change is an extensive property that depends on the amount of substance reacting.

$\Delta H = 2 \times (-286 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}) = -572\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

The general rule: multiplying a balanced equation by a factor $n$ multiplies $\Delta H$ by $n.$ Reversing an equation changes the sign of $\Delta H.$
