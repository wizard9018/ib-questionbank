# CH2560

A $4.00\textrm{ g}$ sample of $\ce{NaOH}$ $(M = 40.00\textrm{ g mol}^{-1})$ is dissolved in $100.0\textrm{ cm}^3$ of water in a polystyrene cup calorimeter. The temperature increases from $22.8\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ to $32.8\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

What is the molar enthalpy of solution of $\ce{NaOH}?$

Specific heat capacity of water $= 4.18\textrm{ J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1}$\
Density of solution $= 1.00\textrm{ g cm}^{-3}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}+41.8\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}-4.18\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}+4.18\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}-41.8\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The temperature of the solution rises, so the dissolution of $\ce{NaOH}$ is exothermic and $\Delta H$ must be negative. This immediately eliminates the two positive options.

The heat absorbed by the solution is

$q = mc\Delta T = 100.0 \textrm{\ g} \times 4.18 \textrm{\ J g}^{-1}{}^\circ\textrm{C}^{-1} \times (32.8 - 22.8)^\circ\textrm{C}$

$q =  4180\textrm{ J} = 4.18\textrm{ kJ}$

The amount of $\ce{NaOH}$ dissolved is

$n = \dfrac{4.00 \textrm{\ g}}{40.00 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.100\textrm{ mol}$

The molar enthalpy of solution is

$\Delta H = -\dfrac{q}{n} = -\dfrac{4.18 \textrm{\ kJ}}{0.100 \textrm{\ mol}} = -41.8\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

The negative sign reflects the exothermic nature of the process: the system loses energy to the surroundings.
