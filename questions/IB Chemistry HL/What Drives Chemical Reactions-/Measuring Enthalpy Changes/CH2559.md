# CH2559

Which of the following processes is endothermic?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Freezing water

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Combustion of methane

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Boiling water

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Neutralisation of an acid with a base

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
An endothermic process requires a net transfer of energy from the surroundings to the system. Boiling (vaporisation) requires energy to overcome the intermolecular forces holding molecules in the liquid phase. This energy is absorbed from the surroundings, so the process is endothermic.

Freezing is the reverse of melting. When a liquid freezes, intermolecular forces form, releasing energy to the surroundings. Freezing is exothermic.

Combustion reactions break bonds in the fuel and oxygen and form stronger bonds in the products $(\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{H2O}).$ The energy released in forming the product bonds exceeds the energy required to break the reactant bonds, so combustion is always exothermic.

Neutralisation releases energy as the ionic reaction $\ce{H+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{OH-}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ proceeds. The standard enthalpy of neutralisation for strong acid$-$strong base reactions is approximately $-57\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

A useful test: if energy must be continuously supplied for the process to occur (a flame under a beaker to boil water, an oven to bake a cake), the process is endothermic. If the process releases heat on its own once initiated, it is exothermic.
