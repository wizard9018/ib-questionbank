# CH0716

Hydrogen chloride gas can be produced from the reaction from gaseous hydrogen and chlorine. Approximate bond enthalpies of these molecules are given below.

-   :::tableoptions{}
    | Molecule | Bond enthalpy $\newline$ (kJ mol$^\mathbf{-1}$) |

    $\ce{H-Cl}$ | 430 |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

In an energy profile, the separation of the reactants and products on the $y-$axis is equal to the enthalpy change during the reaction.

The equation for this reaction is

$\ce{H2 + Cl2 -> 2 HCl}$

For this reaction, the enthalpy change can be determined as

$\begin{aligned}
\Delta H &= \sum \text{bonds broken} - \sum \text{bond formed} \\
\Delta H &= D(\ce{H-H}) + D(\ce{Cl-Cl}) - 2 D(\ce{H-Cl}) \\
\Delta H &= (+440 + 240) - (2 \times  430) \\
\Delta H &= +680 - 860 = -180\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}
\end{aligned}$

The reaction is exothermic, as the products have a more negative enthalpy (larger total bond enthalpy) than the reactants.

This question is difficult, as the student must deduce the reaction equation and calculate the change in enthalpy correctly, noting that two $\ce{H-Cl}$ bonds are formed and that the sign of the answer is negative.
