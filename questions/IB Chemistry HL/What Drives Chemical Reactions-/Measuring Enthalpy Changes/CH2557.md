# CH2557

Calcium hydroxide reacts with hydrochloric acid in aqueous solution.

$\ce{Ca(OH)2}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{CaCl2}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

Which row correctly shows the expected change in temperature of the solution and the classification of the reaction?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\hspace{1.1em}$ | $\hspace{0.em}$ | | Temperature of solution | Classification |
| :-- |:-:| :-- | :-: | :-: |
|| A. || increases | exothermic |
|| B. || increases | endothermic |
|| C. || decreases | exothermic |
|| D. || decreases | endothermic |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Neutralisation is an exothermic process. The net ionic reaction in any acid$-$base neutralisation is

$\ce{H+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{OH-}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

Energy is released when the $\ce{O-H}$ bonds in water form, and this energy is transferred to the surrounding solution. Because the enthalpy of the products is lower than the enthalpy of the reactants, $\Delta H$ is negative, and the temperature of the solution increases.

![CH2557exo](images/CH2557exo.png)

The connection between the sign of $\Delta H$ and the observed temperature change is direct: in an exothermic reaction $(\Delta H < 0),$ the surroundings gain energy and the temperature rises. In an endothermic reaction $(\Delta H > 0),$ the surroundings lose energy and the temperature falls. A temperature decrease with an exothermic label, or a temperature increase with an endothermic label, is an internally contradictory combination.
