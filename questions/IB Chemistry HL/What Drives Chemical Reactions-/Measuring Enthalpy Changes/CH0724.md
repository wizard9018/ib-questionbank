# CH0724

Which of the following statements are true?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | $T$ is a measure of the maximum kinetic energy of the water molecules |
    | II. | A change in $T$ of 1 $^\circ$C is equal to a change in $T$ of 1 K |
    | III. | The theoretical maximum change in $T$ is proportional to the enthalpy change of the reaction |

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The temperature of a material follows a Boltzmann distribution, a function that only tends towards zero as the kinetic energy increases. Thus, there is no definite maximum kinetic energy, although the proportion of molecules with energies above a given value will increase as the material is heated.

The temperature is actually a measure of the average kinetic energy of the water molecules: for an ideal gas, the average kinetic energy is proportional to the temperature. For an absolute temperature, changing from $^\circ$C to K causes the value to increase by 273, but changes in temperature are unaffected since increments in the two unit systems are the same. The maximum change in temperature is measured when there is no loss of heat to the surroundings. This change in temperature is proportional to the absorbed energy $q$, which in turn is proportional to enthalpy change of reaction.

This question is moderately difficult as the student must recall multiple pieces of information from the syllabus and recognise the distinction between average and maximum kinetic energies.
