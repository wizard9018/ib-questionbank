# CH2562

Which reaction is endothermic?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH4}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}2\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CaCO3}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{NaOH}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{NaCl}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate (limestone) requires a sustained input of energy. Industrially, limestone is heated to above $800\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ in a lime kiln for the decomposition to proceed. The products $(\ce{CaO}$ and $\ce{CO2})$ are at a higher enthalpy than the reactant, so $\Delta H > 0$ and the reaction is endothermic.

The combustion of methane and the combustion of hydrogen are both exothermic. Combustion reactions release energy because the bonds formed in the products $(\ce{C=O}$ in $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{O-H}$ in $\ce{H2O})$ are stronger than the bonds broken in the reactants, giving a net release of energy.

Neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong base is exothermic $(\Delta H \approx -57\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}).$ The net ionic reaction $\ce{H+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{OH-}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ releases energy as the strong $\ce{O-H}$ covalent bond forms.

A useful classification: combustion and neutralisation reactions are reliably exothermic. Thermal decomposition and many dissolution processes are typically endothermic because they require energy to break bonds or lattice structures without forming comparably strong new bonds.
