# CH2284

A 0.100 mol sample of $\ce{NO}\textrm{(g)}$ is placed in a $5.00 \textrm{\ dm}^3$ closed container and heated to $2500 \textrm{\ K}.$ The equilibrium below is established.

$2 \ce{\,NO}\textrm{(g)} \rightleftharpoons \ce{N2}\textrm{(g)}+ \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \qquad        K =2450$

1. Write the equilibrium constant expression, $K,$ for this reaction. [1 mark]

2. Calculate the equilibrium concentration, in $\textrm{mol dm}^{-3},$ of $\ce{N2}.$ [2 marks]

3.  1. Distinguish between the terms reaction quotient, $Q,$ and equilibrium constant, $K.$ [1 mark]

    2. Explain the change in the value of $Q$ from the initial conditions until equilibrium is reached. [2 marks]

4. Calculate the standard Gibbs free energy change, $\Delta G^{\minuso},$ in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}$ for this reaction at $2500 \textrm{\ K}.$ Use Sections 1 and 2 of the IB Chemistry data booklet. [1 mark]

5. At $2000 \textrm{\ K},$ the equilibrium constant has a value of 1610. Deduce, giving a reason, the sign of the standard enthalpy change, $\Delta H^{\minuso},$ for the reaction. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $K=\dfrac{[\ce{N2}][\ce{O2}]}{[\ce{NO}^2]} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The equilibrium constant expression takes the equilibrium concentrations of the products over those of the reactants, each raised to its stoichiometric coefficient.

    For the generic reaction

    $a \ce{\,A} + b \ce{\,B}  \rightleftharpoons  c \ce{\,C} +d \ce{\,D}$

    The equilibrium constant expression is

    $K=\dfrac{[\ce{C}]_{eq}^c [\ce{D}]_{eq}^d}{[\ce{A}]_{eq}^a [\ce{B}]_{eq}^b}$

    where only aqueous and gas phase compounds are included in the expression.

2. $2450= \dfrac{x^2}{(0.0200-2x)^2} \: \checkmark$\
    $0.00990 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The initial concentration of NO can be deduced from the amount and volume using the relationship $C = n/V.$

    $[\ce{NO}]_0 = \dfrac{n}{V} = \dfrac{0.100 \textrm{\ mol}}{5.00 \textrm{\ dm}^3} = 0.0200 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$

    A RICE table can be used to deduce the change in the concentration of NO required to reach equilibrium.

    Reaction:| $2\ce{\,NO}\textrm{(g)}$ |  $\rightleftharpoons$   |   $\ce{N2}\textrm{(g)}$ |     $+$ |     $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)}$
    :--|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:
    $\textbf{Initial:}$|            0.0200        ||    0    ||    0
    $\textbf{Change:}$|    $-2x$ ||    $+x$    ||    $+x$
    $\textbf{Equilibrium:}$|    $0.0200 - 2x$ ||        $x$ ||        $x$

    At equilibrium:

    $K= \dfrac{[\ce{N2}][\ce{O2}]}{[\ce{NO}]^2}=\dfrac{x^2}{(0.0200-2x)^2}$

    Solving for $x$, the equilibrium concentration of nitrogen:

    $2450=\dfrac{x^2}{(0.0200-2x)^2}$

    $\sqrt{2450} = \dfrac{x}{0.0200-2x}$

    $\sqrt{2450} \times (0.0200-2x) =x$

    $0.0200 \sqrt{2450}= x + 2\sqrt{2450} x$

    $x = \dfrac{0.0200 \sqrt{2450}}{1 + 2\sqrt{2450}} $

    $x=0.00990$

    The nitrogen concentration at equilibrium is $0.00990 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$

3.  1.  $Q$ approaches $K$ as the reaction occurs, revealing the direction of equilibrium \
    **AND**\
    $K$ is a constant $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$ \
        The equilibrium constant is determined by the ratio of equilibrium concentrations, whereas the reaction quotient can be determined using the same ratio of concentrations at any  time during the reaction (non$-$equilibrium concentrations).  When $Q < K$, the reaction proceeds forward to achievel equilibrium.  When $Q > K$, the reaction proceeds in reverse to achieve equilibrium.  When $Q=K$ the reaction is at equilibrium.

    2. $Q$ increases  $\checkmark$\
        until $Q=K$ at equilibrium $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
         At non$-$equilibrium conditions, $Q≠K.$ However, as the system achieves equilibrium, $Q$ changes until it equals $K.$ In this case, there are only reactants at the beginning, meaning that $Q=0$ initially. As time goes by, $Q$ increases until $Q=K.$

4.  $\Delta G = -8.31 \textrm{\ J K}^{-1}\textrm{mol}^{-1} \ \times 2500 \textrm{\ K} \ln 2450 \: \checkmark $

    $\Delta G = -162 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}\: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$ \
    Using the relationship  $\Delta G^{\minuso}=-RT\ln K,$ we can substitute the known values to determine $\Delta G$ in $\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}.$

    $\Delta G= -(8.31 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}\textrm{K}^{-1})(  2500 \textrm{\ K}) \ln 2450 = -162125 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}$

    $\Delta G = -162 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

5.  $\Delta H > 0 \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    When the temperature is decreased, $K$ decreased and the reaction became less favorable.  According to Le Chatelier's principle, the reaction shifts in the exothermic direction when the temperature is decreased. If the reverse reaction is exothermic, the forward reaction is endothermic and $\Delta H > 0.$
