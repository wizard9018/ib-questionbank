# CH0909

Water can reversibly dissociate into a hydroxide and hydronium ion as shown.

![CH0909_Image1](images/CH0909_Image1.png)

The enthalpy change for the forward reaction is $+57.7\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$.

How does a **decrease** in temperature affect the pH of water?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The pH increases because the position of equilibrium shifts to the left

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The pH increases because the position of equilibrium shifts to the right

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The pH decreases because the position of equilibrium shifts to the left

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The pH decreases because the position of equilibrium shifts to the right

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

According to Le Chatelier's principle, a change to a system in equilibrium causes the position of equilibrium to shift to counteract the change. The self-ionization of water has a positive enthalpy change, so it is endothermic, absorbing heat energy to break the O-H bond. If the system is cooled, the reverse exothermic reaction is favored to produce an increase in temperature. Therefore, the position of equilibrium shifts to the left. This reduces the concentration of $\ce{H3O+}$ ions, so the pH increases.
