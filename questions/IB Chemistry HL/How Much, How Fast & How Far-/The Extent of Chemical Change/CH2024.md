# CH2024

The following equilibrium reaction is investigated.

$\ce{A(aq) + 2 B(aq) <=> 3 C(aq)}$

The concentrations are plotted against time at a constant temperature, as shown below.

![CH2024](images/CH2024.png)

Which combination is correct?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | At $\mathbf{t}_\mathbf{0}$ | At $\mathbf{t}_\mathbf{1}$ | At $\mathbf{t}_\mathbf{2}$ |
    | :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | $Q_c < K_c$ | $Q_c = K_c$ | $Q_c > K_c$ |
    | B. | $Q_c < K_c$ | $Q_c = K_c$ | $Q_c < K_c$ |
    | C. | $Q_c = K_c$ | $Q_c < K_c$ | $Q_c = K_c$ |
    | D. | $Q_c > K_c$ | $Q_c = K_c$ | $Q_c > K_c$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The student must analyze how the reaction quotient $Q_c$ compares to the equilibrium constant $K_c$ at different times using the concentration vs. time graph.

The relationship between $Q_c$ and $K_c$ determines how a system will shift to reach equilibrium. If  $Q_c < K_c,$ the reaction shifts forward to produce more products. On the graph, the concentration of C will increase while the concentrations of A and B decrease. If $Q_c > K_c,$ the reaction shifts backward to consume C and form A and B. If  $Q_c = K_c,$ the system is at equilibrium with no net change in concentrations; the gradient of the curves will be 0.

At $t_0,$ the system has not yet achieved equilibrium. C is being produced and the reactants A and B are being consumed; $Q_c < K_c.$ At $t_1,$ the concentrations are constant, indicating equilibrium is reached and $Q_c = K_c.$ At $t_2,$ after a sudden increase in the conecntration of A, the system must shift forward to return to equilibrium, $Q_c < K_c.$

This question is categorized as medium as the student must be able to interpret the graph and deduce the relationship between the reaction quotient and the equilibrium constant from the observed trend.
