# CH0766

**Maximum mark: 9**

Esterification is a reversible process that occurs between alcohols and carboxylic acids.

One example of esterification is the reaction between ethanol and ethanoic acid to produce ethyl ethanoate and water.

$\ce{CH3COOH(l) + CH3CH2OH(l) <=> CH3COOCH2CH3(l) + H2O(l)}$

1.  Ethanol and ethanoic acid were added to the reaction vessel and $Q$ was calculated before equilibrium was reached. State the relationship between the values of $Q$ and $K_c$. [1 mark]

2.  Use section 13 (*Section 14- 2025 Syllabus*) of the data booklet to calculate the enthalpy change for the forward reaction ($\Delta H_c(\ce{H2O}) = 0\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$). [2 marks]

3.  Use your answer to (b) to predict and explain how the value of $K_c$ and the equilibrium yield of the ester will change with increasing temperature. [3 marks]

4.  An acid catalyst is often used in esterification reactions. State and explain the effect of the catalyst on the equilibrium yield of the ester. [1 mark]

5.  To separate the ester from the equilibrium mixture, an organic solvent is often added to produce two distinct layers. Ethanol and ethanoic acid are both soluble in water and found in the aqueous layer. State and explain which of these is more soluble in water. [2 marks]

1.  $\ce{Q < K_c}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    The reaction quotient (*Q*) measures the relative amount of products and reactants present during a reaction at a particular point in time. As equilibrium has not yet been reached and is being approached from the reactants, there must be a higher concentration of reactants present at this point in time than at equilibrium.

    $K_c = \dfrac{[products]}{[reactants]}$

3.  $K_c$ decreases **AND** yield decreases; $\checkmark$

    (Reaction is) exothermic; $\checkmark$

    Equilibrium shifts to the left/towards the reactants/backwards reaction is favored/endothermic reaction is favored OWTTE. $\checkmark$

    *ALLOW reverse argument as ecf from (b)*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    $K_c$ is affected by temperature only. Le Chatelier's principle dictates that the position of the equilibrium will shift to oppose any change to the system. As temperature is increased, the equilibrium shifts to reduce the temperature and so favors the endothermic reaction (backwards).
