# CH1199

Consider the chemical equilibrium below.

$\ce{H2(g) +  Br2(g) <=> 2 HBr(g)}\qquad K = 3.80\times 10^{-4}\ \text{ at }\ 1000\,{}^\circ \text{C}$

$1.00\times 10^{-2}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ of hydrogen, $1.00\times 10^{-2}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ of bromine and $2.00\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ of hydrogen bromide are mixed at $1000\,{}^\circ \text{C}.$

Which statement about this chemical reaction is correct?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The reaction shifts to produce bromine

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The reaction shifts to consume hydrogen

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The reaction shifts to produce hydrogen bromide

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$There is no shift as the reaction is at equilibrium

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The reaction quotient, $Q,$ can be deduced at this particular time to determine the direction in which the reaction will proceed to reach equilibrium.

$Q = \dfrac{[\ce{HBr}]^2}{[\ce{H2}][\ce{Br2}]} = \dfrac{(2.00\times 10^{-3})^2}{(1.00\times 10^{-2})(1.00\times 10^{-2})} = 0.04$

$Q >K;$ the reaction is not at equilibrium. As a result, $Q$ should decrease by decreasing the concentration of products and increasing the concentration of reactants. The reaction shifts to the left.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must calculate $Q$ and compare it with $K$ at $1000\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$
