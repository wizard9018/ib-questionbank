# CH1542

Consider the equilibrium below.

$\textrm{M}_2\textrm{(g)} + \textrm{N}_2\textrm{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2 \textrm{\ MN(g)}$

1.00 mol of MN is placed in a sealed 2.00 dm$^3$ container and allowed to come to equilibrium at 300 K. At equilibrium, 0.400 mol of $\textrm{M}_2$ was present in the reaction vessel.

What is the value of the equilibrium constant at 300 K?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$0.250

- B.$\hspace{1em}$4.00

- C.$\hspace{1em}$0.50

- D.$\hspace{1em}$2.25

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

The student must work out the calculation of equilibrium constants.

We can use a ***RICE*** table to organize the work.

 Recall that in an equilibrium constant expression, the concentration of the reactants and products are required in units of mol dm$^{-3}$.  Since values in the problem statement are given in moles, we must include the volume of the system, 2.00 dm$^3$.  The ***initial*** concentration of MN is 

$C = \dfrac{n}{V} =  \dfrac{1.00 \textrm{\ mol}}{2.00 \textrm{\ dm}^3} = 0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ 

Using the stoichiometric coefficients we can deduce what ***changes*** will occur to the concentrations of the reactants and products. The $1:1:2$ ratio of the coefficients will be matched by the coefficients of $x$.  For every 2 MN that decompose, 1 N$_2$ and 1 M$_2$ will form.  Finally, expressions for the ***equilibrium*** concentrations of each compound can be determined by adding the changes to the initial concentrations.

|$\textrm{Reaction:}$| $\textrm{M}_2\textrm{(g)}$ | $\textrm{+}$ | $\textrm{N}_2\textrm{(g)}$ | $\hspace{1em} \rightleftharpoons \hspace{0.5em}$ | $\textrm{2 MN\textrm{(g)}}$|

| :-- | :-:  |:-: |:-: |:--| :-:| 

|Initial:|0||0 || $0.500$

|Change:|$+x$||$+x$ || $-2x$|

|Equilibrium:| $x$ ||$x$||  $0.500 -2x$|

At equilibrium, the problem states that there are 0.400 moles of $\textrm{M}_2$.  The equilibrium concentration of $\textrm{M}_2$ is

$C = \dfrac{n}{V} =  \dfrac{0.400 \textrm{\ mol}}{2.00 \textrm{\ dm}^3} = 0.200 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ 

So, $x=0.200 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$.

We can use this information to deduce all the concentrations at equilibrium and deduce $K.$

$[\textrm{M}_2]_{eq} = [\textrm{N}_2]_{eq} = x = 0.200 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$

$[\textrm{MN}]_{eq}$ = $0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} - 2 x = 0.100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$

$\ K$ = $\dfrac{[\textrm{MN}]^2}{[\textrm{M}_2][\textrm{N}_2]}$ = $\dfrac{0.100^2}{0.200 \times 0.200} = \dfrac{0.0100}{0.0400} = 0.250$

This question is categorized as hard as the student must work out many relationships to deduce $K$ and work out the equilibrium constant magnitude.
