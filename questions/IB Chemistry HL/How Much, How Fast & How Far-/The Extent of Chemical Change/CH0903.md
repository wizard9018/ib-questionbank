# CH0903

**Maximum mark: 13**

Hexaaquacobalt (II) complexes react with concentrated hydrochloric acid and undergo a ligand substitution reaction.

$\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+(aq) + 4 Cl-(aq) <=> [CoCl4]^2-(aq) + 6 H2O(l)}$

1.  $\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+}$ produces a red$-$pink solution. Chloride is a weaker ligand than water.  Use Section 15 of the IB Chemistry data booklet to predict the possible color of $\ce{[CoCl4]^2-}$ solutions. [4 marks]

2.  The equilibrium constant expression for the reactoin is written as

    $K = \dfrac{[\ce{[CoCl4]^2-}]}{[\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+}][\ce{Cl-}]^4} = 1.02\times 10^3$ at 298 K

    Explain why water is not included. [1 mark]

3.  A total volume of 50.0 cm$^3$ $\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+}$ and $\ce{HCl}$ were placed in a flask. At equilibrium,

    $\ce{[[CoCl4]^2-] = [Cl-]} = 0.100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$

    Calculate the initial concentration of $\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+}$ in the 50 cm$^3$ reaction mixture. [5 marks]

4.
    1.  Calculate $\Delta G$ in $\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$ and give your answer to three significant figures. [1 mark]

    2.  This reaction becomes less spontaneous as temperature decreases. Deduce signs for $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$. Justify your choices. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Violet **OR** Blue **OR** Green; $\checkmark$

    $\ce{Cl-}$ is lower in the spectrochemical series *AND* smaller $\Delta E$ between d orbitals. $\checkmark$

    The **wavelength absorbed is longer than the one for green color absorption** (Red OR Orange OR Yellow); $\checkmark$

    Complementary color observed. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    In transition metal complexes, the d-subshell is split into two energy levels. The difference in energy between the orbitals ($\Delta E$) depends on the ligand. The higher in the spectrochemical series, the larger $\Delta E$.

    $\Delta E$ decreases as the ligand is swapped from $\ce{H2O}$ to $\ce{Cl-}$. The excited electron therefore absorbs a longer wavelength of light, and the complementary color in the color wheel is observed.

2.  Water is in large excess/water. Its concentration is effectively constant. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    As this is a heterogeneous equilibrium, only the aqueous species are included in the $K_c$ expression.

3.  $\ce{[[Co(H2O)6]^2+]}_{eq} = \dfrac{\ce{[[CoCl4]^2-]}}{K_c\ce{[Cl-]}^4} = \dfrac{(0.100)}{1.02\times 10^3(0.100)^4} = 1.00\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\checkmark$

    $n(\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+}_{eq}) = 1.00\times 0.05 = 0.0500\,\text{mol}$

    *AND*

    $n(\ce{[CoCl4]^2-}_{eq}) = n(\ce{Cl-}_{eq}) = 0.100\times 0.05 = 0.005\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

    Change in moles $\ce{[CoCl4]^2-} = +0.005\,\checkmark$

    $n(\ce{[Co(H2O)6]^2+}_{initial})  = 0.0500 + 0.005 = 0.055\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

    $\ce{[[Co(H2O)6]^2+]}_{initial} = \dfrac{0.055}{0.05} = 1.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    | | [Co(H$\mathbf{_2}$O)$\mathbf{_6}$]$\mathbf{^{2+}}$ | 4 Cl$\mathbf{^-}$ | [CoCl$\mathbf{_4}$]$\mathbf{^{2-}}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | Initial moles | $x = equilibrium\ moles + 0.005$ | $y = 0.025$ (not needed) | $0$ |
    | Change in moles | $x - 0.005$ | $(+ 4) (0.005)$ (not needed) | $+ 0.005$ |
    | Equilibrium moles | Determine using $K_c$ | $0.005$ | $0.005$ |

4.
    1.  $\Delta G = -RT \ln K = -(8.31)(298) \ln(1.02\times 10^{3}) = -17200\,\text{J mol}^{-1} = -17.2\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Using $\Delta G = -RT \ln K$, $\Delta G$ is in J mol$^{-1}$ (as units for $R$ are J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$).

    2.  $\Delta S$ positive AND more product molecules than reactant molecules OWTTE $\checkmark$

        $\Delta H$ positive AND $- T\Delta S < \Delta H$ as temperature decreases. $\Delta G$ becomes positive. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        Entropy describes the disorder in the system. The increase in molecules from left to right increases entropy, so a positive $\Delta S$.

        $\Delta G = \Delta H - T \Delta S$.

        Reactions are spontaneous when $\Delta\ G < 0$.

        As $\Delta S$ is positive, if $\Delta H$ is positive, as temperature decreases, the $- T \Delta S$ term becomes smaller until $- T \Delta S < \Delta H$ and $\Delta G$ becomes positive. This would not happen for a negative $\Delta H$ as $\Delta H - T \Delta S$ is negative at all temperatures.
