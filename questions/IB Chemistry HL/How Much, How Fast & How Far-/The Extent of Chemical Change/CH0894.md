# CH0894

0.150 mol of $\ce{SO3}$ was placed in a sealed 1 dm$^3$ flask and allowed to reach equilibrium.  The number of moles of $\ce{SO3}$ at equilibrium can be expressed as $(0.150 - x)$.  Which of the below gives the correct expression for $\ce{K_c}$?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$\ce{2 SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2 SO3(g)}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{(0.150 - x)^2}{2 x^3}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{(0.150 - x)^2}{0.5 x^3}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{0.5 x^3}{(0.150 - x)^2}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{2 x^3}{(0.150 - x)^2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\
For the general equation,

$\ce{a A + b B <=> c C + d D}$

The equilibrium constant is written as

$K_c = \dfrac{[\ce{C}]^{c}[\ce{D}]^{d}}{[\ce{A}]^{a}[\ce{B}]^{b}}$

For this reaction,

$\ce{2 SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2 SO3(g)}  \qquad K_c = \dfrac{[\ce{SO3}]^2}{[\ce{SO2}]^2[\ce{O2}]}$

We are told that the concentration of $\ce{SO3}$ at equilibrium is $0.150 - x,$ meaning $x$ moles of $\ce{SO3}$ have decomposed.  Since $\ce{SO3}$ is a product, the reaction must shift to the left forming $\ce{SO2}$ and $\ce{O2}$.  The stoichiometric coefficients tell us how much of each forms.  The ratio of $\ce{SO3}$ to $\ce{SO2}$ is $2:2$, so if $\ce{SO3}$ decreases by $x$, then $\ce{SO2}$ increases by $x$.  The ratio of $\ce{SO3}$ to $\ce{O2}$ is $2:1$, so $\ce{O2}$ increases by half as much, $0.5x$.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| Reaction| 2 SO$\mathbf{_2}$| +| O$\mathbf{_2}$ |$\rightleftharpoons$ | 2 SO$\mathbf{_3}$ |
| :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: |:-:|:-:|
| Initial| 0 || 0 | |0.150 |
| Change | $+ x$|| $+ 0.5 x$ || $- x$ |
| Equilibrium  | $x$ || $0.5 x$ || $0.150 - x$ |

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$
Substituting the equilibrium concentrations into the $K_c$ expression:

$K_c = \dfrac{(0.150 - x)^2}{x^2(0.5x)}$
