# CH0731

For the mechanism proposed below, what would be the rate law?

$\begin{aligned}

\ce{I2(g) &-> 2 I(g)}\qquad &\textrm{fast} \\

\ce{H2(g) + I(g) &<=> H2I(g)}\qquad &\textrm{fast} \\

\ce{H2I(g) + I(g) &-> 2 HI(g)}\qquad &\textrm{slow}

\end{aligned}$

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$rate = k$\ce{[I2]}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$rate = k$\ce{[H2I][I]}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$rate = k$\ce{[H2][I]}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$rate = k$\ce{[H2][I2]}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

The overall equation is $\ce{I2(g) + H2(g) -> 2 HI(g)}$, so the rate law needs to be expressed in terms of the reactants in the overall equation.

The slow step determines the rate of the reaction; therefore, the rate law would be rate = k$\ce{[H2I][I]}$; however, both of the reactants of this slow step are intermediates and cannot be in the rate law as they do not appear in the overall reaction.

The presence of $\ce{H2I}$ depends upon the concentration of $\ce{H2}$ and I; furthermore, the presence of 2 I depends upon $\ce{I2}$. 

  

Therefore, the overall rate law is first order with respect to $\ce{I2}$ and first order with respect to $\ce{H2}$.

- $\textrm{rate} = k\ce{[H2I][I]}$; from the slow step, but $\ce{[H2I]}$ depends upon $\ce{[H2]}$ and $\ce{[I]}$.

- $\textrm{rate} = k\ce{[H2][I][I]}$; $\ce{2 I}$ depends upon the presence of $\ce{I2}$;

- $\textrm{rate} = k\ce{[H2][I2]}$, overall rate law.
