# CH0208

**Maximum mark: 10**

The enzyme carbonic anhydrase catalyzes the reaction of dissolved carbon dioxide with water to form carbonic acid.

$\ce{CO2}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{H2CO3}\textrm{(aq)}$

1.  Explain how an enzyme increases the rate of reaction. [3 marks]

2.  The rate constant was measured over a range of temperatures. Two of the measurements are shown in the table.

    | $\enskip$Temperature / $\mathbf{^\circ C}\enskip$ | k / s$\mathbf{^{-1}}$ |
    | :---: | :---: |
    | 20 | $\enskip 6.0 \times 10^5 \enskip$ |
    | 40 | $1.5 \times 10^6$ |

    Explain why the rate increases as the temperature increases. [3 marks]

3.  Consider the plot of $\ln k$ against $\dfrac{1}{T}$ for this reaction.

    ![CH0208](images/CH0208.png)

    Use the graph and sections 1 and 2 of the data booklet to calculate the activation energy, $E_a$, in kJ mol$^{-1}$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  provides alternate pathway with lower activation energy $\checkmark$\
    more molecules have enough energy to overcome the activation barrier $\checkmark$\
    higher fraction of successful collisions per unit time $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Enzymes speed up reactions by stabilizing the transition state. By lowering the activation energy, they allow reactant molecules to react under milder conditions. Even though the frequency of collisions remains similar, a much higher percentage of those collisions are successful because more molecules exceed the lowered activation threshold.

2.  increased kinetic energy $\checkmark$\
    increased speed, more frequent collisions $\checkmark$\
    more molecules have $\textrm{KE} > E_a,$ more successful collision $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    As the temperature increases from $20\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ to $40\,{}^\circ\textrm{C},$ the average kinetic energy of the particles in solution increases. This means the particles are moving more rapidly, and so the frequency of collisions between reactant molecules also increases. More importantly, the energy distribution of molecules shifts so that a greater proportion of the molecules now have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy of the reaction. Because more collisions exceed this activation barrier, a higher fraction of collisions are successful. The combined effect is that the rate increases with temperature.

3.  $E_a = -R \times \textrm{gradient} \,\checkmark$\
    $\mbox{}\\[-12pts]$
    gradient =   $ \dfrac{14.26-13.24}{0.00319-0.00343} = -4250 \textrm{\ K} \: \checkmark$\
    $\mbox{}\\[-12pts]$
    $E_a =   35.3\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1} \: \checkmark$

    *Award full marks for final answer.*\
    *Accept answers in range 34.7 $-$ 35.7 kJ mol$^\textit{\,-1}.$*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Using the linear form of the Arrhenius equation, $\ln k  = \dfrac{-E_a}{R} \left(\dfrac{1}{T}\right) + \ln A$, the activation energy can be determined from the gradient of the line of best$-$fit: $m = \dfrac{-E_a}{R}.$

    The points in part (b) give the following data:

    | $\enskip$T / $\mathbf{^\circ C}\enskip$ |  1/T / K$^{\mathbf{-1}}$ |  k / s$\mathbf{^{-1}}$ | ln k
    | :-: | :-: | :-:|:-:
    | 20 | 0.003413 | $\enskip 6.0 \times 10^5 \enskip$ | 13.305
    | 40 | 0.003195| $1.5 \times 10^6$ | 14.221

    The gradient of the line is

    $m = \dfrac{14.221-13.305}{0.003195-0.003413} = -4202 \textrm{\ K}$

    Estimating from the line of best fit gives a slightly different value:

    $m = \dfrac{14.26-13.24}{0.00319-0.00343} = -4250 \textrm{\ K}$

    The activation energy is

    $E_a = 8.31 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}K^{-1} \times 4250 \textrm{\ K} \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ kJ}}{1000 \textrm{\ J}} = 35.3 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$
