# CH2395

The Maxwell$-$Boltzmann distribution curve for the reaction below is shown in the diagram.

$\ce{O_2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,H_2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,H_2O}\textrm{(l)}$

![CH2395](images/CH2395.png)

$\mbox{}\\[-5pts]$

The reaction is catalysed by platinum. $E_{a,cat}$ represents the activation energy with the catalyst and $E_a$ represents the activation energy without the catalyst.

Which of the following factors would result in a decrease in the number of particles in the Y region of the diagram?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$Increasing the pressure of oxygen gas
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Removing half of the catalyst
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Decreasing the temperature of the reaction

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The Maxwell$-$Boltzmann distribution describes how kinetic energy is distributed among gas particles at a given temperature. Region Y on the diagram represents particles with kinetic energy equal to or greater than the uncatalysed activation energy. Only changes that alter the shape of the distribution curve will change the number of particles in this region.

Temperature is the only factor that changes the shape of the Maxwell$-$Boltzmann distribution. Decreasing the temperature shifts the peak to a lower energy and compresses the curve, reducing the proportion of particles in the high$-$energy tail. Fewer particles have energy above the uncatalysed activation energy, so the number in region Y decreases.

Increasing the pressure of oxygen gas increases the total number of gas particles in a given volume, but it does not change the energy distribution. The proportion of particles at each energy remains the same, so the fraction in region Y is unchanged. (The absolute number in Y would actually increase, not decrease, because there are more particles overall.)

Removing half of the catalyst does not affect the Maxwell$-$Boltzmann distribution at all. A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy, but it does not change how kinetic energy is distributed among the particles. The number of particles in region Y remains the same whether the catalyst is present or not.
