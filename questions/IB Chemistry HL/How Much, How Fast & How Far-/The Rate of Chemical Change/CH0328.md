# CH0328

Why does a reaction occur faster at a higher temperature?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}

    | | |

    | :- | :- |

    | I. | The number of molecules with energy equal to or greater than the activation energy increases |

    | II. | The molecules collide more frequently |

    | III. | The activation energy of the reaction is lowered |

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

When the temperature increases, the number of particles with enough kinetic energy to overcome the activation energy will increase. Therefore, more successful collisions will occur, increasing the frequency of collisions. This results in an increase in the rate of reaction.

Catalysts lower activation energy.
