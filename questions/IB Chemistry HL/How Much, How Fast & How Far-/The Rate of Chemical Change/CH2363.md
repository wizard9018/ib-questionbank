# CH2363

**Maximum mark: 12**

Acidified potassium manganate(VII), $\ce{KMnO_4}$, is a vibrant purple solution that absorbs strongly at 525 nm. It oxidises ethanedioic acid, $\ce{H_2C_2O_4}$, to form carbon dioxide and a nearly colourless manganese(II) salt. The net ionic equation is shown below.

$2\ce{\,MnO_4^-}\textrm{(aq)} + 16\ce{\,H^+}\textrm{(aq)} + 5\ce{\,C_2O_4^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 8\ce{\,H_2O}\textrm{(l)} + 10\ce{\,CO_2}\textrm{(g)}$

A student investigates the rate of this reaction at $50\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ using a spectrophotometer. A plot of absorbance at 525 nm against time is shown below.

![CH2363](images/CH2363.png)

1. Explain why absorbance at 525 nm can be used to monitor the rate of this reaction. [1 mark]

2. Determine the initial rate of decrease of absorbance. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the average rate of change of absorbance over the first four minutes. [1 mark]

4. Explain, with reference to collision theory, why the rate of reaction decreases as the reaction proceeds. [2 marks]

5. The student repeats the investigation at $70\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$.
    1. Sketch the shape of the curve obtained on the axes above. [2 marks]

    2. Explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the rate of the reaction. [2 marks]

6. The $\ce{Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$ ions produced in this reaction further react with ethanedioate ions in a two$-$step mechanism.

    Step 1: $\hspace{1em}4\ce{\,Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{MnO_4^-}\textrm{(aq)} + 8\ce{\,H^+}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 5\ce{\,Mn^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 4\ce{\,H_2O}\textrm{(l)}$

    Step 2: $\hspace{1em}2\ce{\,Mn^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{C_2O_4^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,CO_2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$

    State the role of $\ce{Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$ ions in this reaction, giving a reason for your answer. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  absorbance   directly proportional to concentration    $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The Beer$-$Lambert law states that $A = \varepsilon c l$, where $A$ is absorbance, $\varepsilon$ is the molar absorptivity, $c$ is the concentration, and $l$ is the path length. Since $\varepsilon$ and $l$ are constant throughout the experiment, absorbance is directly proportional to the concentration of $\ce{MnO_4^-}$. As $\ce{MnO_4^-}$ is consumed, its concentration falls and so does the absorbance at 525 nm. Monitoring this decrease therefore tracks the progress of the reaction.

2.  $\textrm{tangent drawn at}\ t = 0 \: \checkmark$\
    $\textrm{rate} \approx 0.76\textrm{\ a.u.\ min}^{-1} \: \checkmark$\
    *Accept 0.70–0.82 a.u. min$^{-1}$.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    By the Beer$-$Lambert law, absorbance is directly proportional to the concentration of $\ce{MnO_4^-}$. The initial rate is the instantaneous rate at $t = 0$, found by drawing a tangent to the curve at the $y-$intercept and calculating its gradient.

     ![CH2363a](images/CH2363a.png)

    Two points on the tangent, for example $(0,\, 1.10)$ and $(1.44,\, 0)$, give:

$\textrm{rate} = \left|\dfrac{\Delta A}{\Delta t}\right| = \left|{\dfrac{0 - 1.10}{1.44 - 0}}\right| = 0.764\textrm{\ a.u.\ min}^{-1}$

3.  $\textrm{average rate} \approx 0.24\textrm{\ a.u.\ min}^{-1} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The average rate is the total change in absorbance divided by the total time.

     ![CH2363b](images/CH2363b.png)

    From the graph, the absorbance falls from 1.10 at $t = 0$ to approximately 0.07 at $t = 4.0\textrm{\ min}$:

$\textrm{average rate} = \left|\dfrac{0.07-1.10}{4.0}\right| = 0.26\textrm{\ a.u.\ min}^{-1}$

4.  $\textrm{concentration of } \ce{MnO_4^-} \textrm{ decreases as it is consumed} \: \checkmark$\
    $\textrm{lower frequency of collisions between reactant particles, so rate decreases} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    As the reaction proceeds, $\ce{MnO_4^-}$ and $\ce{C_2O_4^{2-}}$ are consumed and their concentrations fall. Fewer reactant particles per unit volume means the frequency of collisions between $\ce{MnO_4^-}$ and $\ce{C_2O_4^{2-}}$ decreases. Since rate depends on collision frequency, the rate of reaction decreases over time, producing the characteristic curve that is steep initially and gradually levels off.

    2.  $\textrm{greater frequency of collisions} \: \checkmark$\
        $\textrm{greater proportion of particles with } E \geq E_a \: \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        At a higher temperature, particles have greater average kinetic energy. This has two effects on the rate. First, particles move faster, so they collide more frequently. Second, and more significantly, a greater proportion of particles have kinetic energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, $E_a$. Both effects increase the rate of reaction, but the second effect is the dominant one.

6.  $\ce{Mn^{2+}} \textrm{ acts as a catalyst} \: \checkmark$\
    $\textrm{consumed in Step 1 and regenerated in Step 2} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    A catalyst is a species that increases the rate of a reaction without being consumed overall. In Step 1, $\ce{Mn^{2+}}$ is a reactant, and in Step 2, it is regenerated as a product. Because $\ce{Mn^{2+}}$ appears on both sides of the overall process, it is not consumed overall and meets the definition of a catalyst. It provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy than the uncatalysed route.
