# CH2052

Which of the following factors would increase the rate of reaction for calcium carbonate reacting with hydrochloric acid?

$\ce{CaCO3(s) + HCl(aq) -> CaCl2(aq) + CO2(g) + H2O(l)}$

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$increased pressure

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$increased particle size of calcium carbonate

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$decreased concentration of hydrochloric acid

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$increased surface area of calcium carbonate

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The rate of reaction is increased by any factor that increases the probability of successful collisions between the reacting particles.

***Temperature:*** increased temperature increases the average molecular speed of the particles which increases the number of collisions per unit time and therefore the rate.  It also increases the probability of success of each collision because more molecules will have enough energy to overcome the activation barrier.

***Pressure:*** For gaseous reactants, an increase in pressure causes an increase in the rate of reaction because the reacting particles are closer together so collide more frequently.

***Particle size/surface area:*** When the surface area of a solid is increased (by decreasing the particle size), there are more reactant particles available, so there are more frequent collisions, so the rate increases.

***Concentration:*** When the concentration of a reactant increases, there are more particles available to react so the rate increases.
