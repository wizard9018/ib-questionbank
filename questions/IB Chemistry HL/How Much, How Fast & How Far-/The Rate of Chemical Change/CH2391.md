# CH2391

A student added $50\textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $1.0\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ hydrochloric acid to excess calcium carbonate at $25\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ and measured the volume of carbon dioxide gas collected over time. The results are shown as the dashed curve.

The experiment was then repeated with one change. The solid curve shows the new results.

![CH2391-1](images/CH2391-1.png)

Which change to the original investigation would produce the solid curve?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Using a greater volume of hydrochloric acid

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Using a more concentrated hydrochloric acid

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Using smaller pieces of calcium carbonate

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Using a lower concentration of hydrochloric acid

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The solid curve has two key features compared to the dashed original: a steeper initial slope (faster rate) and the same final volume of $\ce{CO2}$ (same plateau). Any correct answer must account for both observations.

The final volume of $\ce{CO2}$ depends on the moles of the limiting reactant, hydrochloric acid. Because the plateau is unchanged, the total moles of $\ce{HCl}$ must be the same as in the original experiment. Smaller pieces of calcium carbonate have a greater surface area, increasing the frequency of collisions between acid particles and the solid surface. This raises the initial rate without changing the amount of acid available, so the curve rises more steeply but levels off at the same volume.

A greater volume of the same $1.0\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ acid means more moles of $\ce{HCl}.$ The final volume of $\ce{CO2}$ would be higher than the original, not the same. Using $50\textrm{\ cm}^3$ of a more concentrated acid increases both the rate and the total moles of $\ce{HCl},$ producing a curve that is steeper and reaches a higher plateau. A lower concentration in the same $50\textrm{\ cm}^3$ means fewer moles of $\ce{HCl}$ and a lower collision frequency, producing a curve that is both shallower and levels off at a lower volume.
