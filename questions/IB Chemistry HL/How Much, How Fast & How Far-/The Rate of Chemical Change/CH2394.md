# CH2394

Which factor does **not** affect the rate of a homogeneous reaction?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Increasing the temperature

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Adding a catalyst

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Increasing the concentration of a reactant

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Increasing the surface area of a reactant

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A homogeneous reaction is one in which all reactants and products are in the same phase, for example all dissolved in aqueous solution or all in the gas phase. Because there are no phase boundaries, there are no exposed surfaces where one phase meets another. Surface area is only relevant in heterogeneous reactions, where the rate depends on the area of contact between phases (such as a solid reactant in a liquid or gas). In a homogeneous system, increasing the surface area of a reactant has no meaning.

Temperature affects the rate of all reactions by changing the proportion of particles with kinetic energy equal to or greater than the activation energy. A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy, increasing the rate regardless of whether the reaction is homogeneous or heterogeneous. Increasing the concentration of a reactant increases the frequency of collisions between reacting particles. All three of these factors influence the rate of a homogeneous reaction.
