# CH0742

**Maximum mark: 10**

A student adds excess zinc pellets to 100.0 cm$^3$ of  0.100 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{HNO3}$ in a beaker. The student tracks the reaction's progress by monitoring the mixture's pH in the beaker over time. The data is collected and graphed. The results are shown below:

$\ce{Zn(s) + 2 HNO3(aq) -> Zn(NO3)2(aq) + H2(g)}$

![CH0742](images/CH0742.png)

1.  Draw a line on the graph showing how the pH would change if a solid strip of zinc with equal mass was added to the nitric acid rather than zinc pellets. [1 mark]

2.
    1.  The change of pH over time is related to the disappearance of the $\ce{HNO3}$. If the rate of disappearance of $\ce{HNO3}$ is determined to be $5.53\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, what is the rate of appearance of $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ ions? [2 marks]

    2.  Propose an observation of a different substance in this chemical reaction that could be used to determine the rate of the reaction. Describe both the substance observed and the measurement taken. [2 marks]

3.
    1.  On the energy profile diagram below, label lines **A** and **B**. Then, state if the reaction is endothermic or exothermic and justify your response. [4 marks]

    ![CH0742-MSc](images/CH0742-MSc.png)

    2.  If the energy profile diagram above represents the reaction with a catalyst, sketch how the curve would change if the reaction proceeded without a catalyst. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Curve drawn that begins at a pH of 1.0 and ends at a pH of approximately 2.5, but the initial slope is less than the original curve. $\checkmark$
    ![CH0742-MSa](images/CH0742-MSa.png)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    One solid piece of zinc would have less surface area than zinc pellets. With less surface area, fewer Zn particles can interact or collide with the nitric acid particles. Therefore, the reaction proceeds more slowly. However, if an equal mass of Zn is added to the same volume and concentration of nitric acid, the final pH will remain the same.

2.
    1.  $(5.53\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1})\left(\dfrac{1\,\text{mol}\,\ce{Zn^2+}}{2\,\text{mol}\,\ce{HNO3}}\right) = 2.77\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Student should recognize the stoichiometric relationships between products and reactants in a chemical reaction when a rate of one substance is given.

        $\ce{a A + b B -> c C + d D}$

        $\dfrac{-\Delta [\ce{A}]}{a\Delta t} = \dfrac{-\Delta [\ce{B}]}{b\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta [\ce{C}]}{c\Delta t} = \dfrac{\Delta [\ce{D}]}{d\Delta t}$

    2.  The volume of hydrogen gas could be observed and measured using water displacement / If the reaction is run in an open container, the mass of hydrogen lost could be measured with a sufficient analytical scale. $\checkmark$ $\checkmark$

        *A statement of measuring any substance's concentration without mentioning how the concentration is to be measured is not accepted;\
    Another product or reactant other than nitric acid must be measured.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Conductivity would be unpredictable as the strong acid, $\ce{HNO3}$, and the ionic salt, $\ce{Zn(NO3)2}$ would be good conductors. This reaction involves no color change, so spectrophotometric measurements are also not possible.

        **B** is $\Delta H$ of the reaction, or enthalpy of the reaction; $\checkmark$

        The reaction is exothermic; $\checkmark$

        The energy of the products is at a lower state than that of the reactants/ $\Delta H_{rxn} < 0$ / $\checkmark$

    2.  Any curve drawn that increases the activation energy and lies above the existing curve. $\checkmark$

        ![CH0742c](images/CH0742c.png)

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        A catalyst lowers the activation energy, and, therefore, allows the reaction to proceed at a faster rate. If the existing curve represents the reaction with a catalyst, without a catalyst, the curve would be higher, creating greater activation energy.
