# CH0189

For a reaction that is first order with respect to $\ce{[M]},$ the rate of the reaction is $0.5 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \text{s}^{-1}$ when the concentration of M is $1.0\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$. What would be the most likely rate when $\ce{[M]} = 1.5\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}?$

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ $1.0\ \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \text{s}^{-1}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ $2.0\ \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \text{s}^{-1}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ $0.25\ \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \text{s}^{-1}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ $0.75\ \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \text{s}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

For a first order reaction, the rate law is 

rate$\  = k[\ce{M}]$

so the rate is directly proportional to the concentration of M.  As the concentration of M has increased by a factor of 1.5, the rate must also be multiplied by 1.5.

Alternatively, both reactions must obey the rate law so,

$\dfrac{\textrm{rate}_2}{\textrm{rate}_1}  = \dfrac{k[\ce{M}]_2}{k[\ce{M}]_1}$

Substituting in the values,

$\dfrac{\textrm{rate}_2}{0.5 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}\,\textrm{s}^{-1}}  = \dfrac{\cancel{k} \times 1.5 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}}{\cancel{k} \times 1.0 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}}\ ;\ \ \ \textrm{rate}_2 = 0.75\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}\,\textrm{s}^{-1} $
