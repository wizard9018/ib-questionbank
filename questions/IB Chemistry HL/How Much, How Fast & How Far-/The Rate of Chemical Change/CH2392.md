# CH2392

The chlorination of methane proceeds by a free radical mechanism. Three steps from this mechanism are shown.

-   I.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH_4}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Cl}$•$\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow$ •$ \ce{CH_3}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{HCl}\textrm{(g)}$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}2\ce{\,Cl}$•$\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cl_2}\textrm{(g)}$
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$•$\ce{CH_3}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Cl_2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CH_3Cl}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{Cl}$•$\textrm{(g)}$

Which step has the highest probability of a successful collision between the reactant species?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A successful collision requires both sufficient energy to overcome the activation energy barrier and the correct orientation between the colliding species. These two factors determine the probability that any given collision leads to a reaction.

Reaction II is the recombination of two chlorine radicals. Each radical has an unpaired electron and is highly reactive. When two such simple species collide, there is very little orientation requirement and effectively no activation energy barrier, since a new bond is simply formed without any bond breaking. This gives reaction II the highest probability of a successful collision.

Reactions I and III are both propagation steps. In reaction I, a chlorine radical must abstract a hydrogen atom from methane, which requires breaking a $\ce{C-H}$ bond while forming an $\ce{H-Cl}$ bond. The radical must approach a specific hydrogen atom at the correct angle, and the collision must provide enough energy to begin breaking the $\ce{C-H}$ bond. In reaction III, a methyl radical attacks a chlorine molecule, breaking the $\ce{Cl-Cl}$ bond while forming a $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond. Both propagation steps involve simultaneous bond breaking and bond making, giving them higher activation energies and stricter orientation requirements than simple radical recombination.
