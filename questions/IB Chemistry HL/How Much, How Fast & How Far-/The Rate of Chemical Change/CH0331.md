# CH0331

Two experiments were conducted, and the rates of reactions were measured. In the first experiment, 50 cm$^3$ of 0.010 mol dm$^{-3}$ of sulfuric acid was added to 0.200 g of zinc metal at 25 $^{\circ}$C. In the second experiment, 50 cm$^3$ of 0.010 mol dm$^{-3}$ of sulfuric acid was added to 0.200 g of zinc metal at 25 $^{\circ}$C, followed immediately by 100 cm$^3$ of distilled water.

What happened to reaction rate in the second experiment, and why?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$Rate of reaction is increased because water lowers the activation energy

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$Rate of reaction decreased since the frequency of effective collisions between reactant molecules lowered

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$Rate of reaction increased because the proportion of reactant molecules possessing the activation energy lowered

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$Rate of reaction increased because there are more particles in the solution, and the frequency of effective collisions increased

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Adding water will reduce the concentration (dilution), and, therefore the frequency of effective collisions will decrease.
