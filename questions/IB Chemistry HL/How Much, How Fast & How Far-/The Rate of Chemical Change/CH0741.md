## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  $P = \dfrac{n}{V}RT$ $\checkmark$

        $P = (0.250\,\text{mol dm}^{-3})(8.31\,\text{J mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1})(298\,\text{K}) = 619\,\text{kPa}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        $PV = nRT$ and $\dfrac{n}{V}$ = $\ce{[(CH3)2O]}$ in mol dm$^{-3}$.

    2.  The pressure will increase. $\checkmark$

        One mole of gas on the reactant side is decomposing to three moles of gas on the product side. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Because there are three moles of gas produced on the product side as one mole of reactant gas decomposes, there will be an increase in the number of gas particles on the product side, thereby increasing the pressure.

2.
    1.  The rate of reaction will be the fastest at $t = 0$ $\checkmark$,

        because according to collision theory, there is the highest concentration of reaction molecules available to collide.  $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        The aspects of collision theory are as follows:
        -   Particles must collide;
        -   Particles must have sufficient energy to allow the reaction to occur;
        -   Particles must collide with the correct orientation or the orientation that will allow the reaction to occur.

    3.  Activation energy is the minimum amount of energy required by reactant particles to achieve an effective collision, thereby allowing the reactants to proceed to products. $\checkmark$

    4.  The addition of a catalyst; $\checkmark$

        An increase in temperature. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        The aspects of collision theory are as follows:
        -   Particles must collide;
        -   Particles must have sufficient energy to allow the reaction to occur;
        -   Particles must collide with the correct orientation or the orientation that will allow the reaction to occur.

        The addition of a catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway that lowers the activation energy, thereby allowing the reaction to proceed with less energetic collisions. Increasing the temperature increases the particle speed, which will increase particle energy, creating more effective collisions.
