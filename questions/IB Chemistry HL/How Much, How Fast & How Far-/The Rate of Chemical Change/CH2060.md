        The products of the electrophilic addition reaction between an asymmetric alkene and HBr can be understood using Markovnikov's rule shich states that the hydrogen will add to the carbon with more hydrogens in the major adduct.  Here, to form the minor adduct, the H is placed on carbon 2 and the Br is placed on carbon 1.

        The general reaction mechanism has the nucleophilic $\pi\text{--}$bond attack the partially positive H to form a $\ce{C-H}$ bond.  The hydrogen releases the bonding electrons to Br, forming bromide. In the second step, the bromide anion attacks the positve carbon to form the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond.

    2.  secondary carbocation is more stable due to positive inductive effect $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        Carbocation stability is influenced by electron$\text{--}$donating effects from nearby alkyl groups. One key factor is the positive inductive effect, where alkyl groups push electron density toward the positively charged carbon, helping to stabilize the charge.  In a primary carbocation $\ce{R-CH2+},$ the positively charged carbon is bonded to only one alkyl group. This means there is limited electron donation to stabilize the charge, making the carbocation less stable. In a secondary carbocation $\ce{R2-CH+},$ the positively charged carbon is bonded to two alkyl groups which help distribute the positive charge more effectively, stabilizing the carbocation more than a primary one.

        ***An alternate explanation:*** *Carbocation stability is influenced by hyperconjugation, a phenomenon where adjacent $\ce{C-H}$ $\sigma\text{--}$bonds can donate electron density to a nearby empty p-orbital of a positively charged carbon. This delocalizes the positive charge, making the carbocation more stable.*

6.  mechanism consistent with rate law $\checkmark$

    rate determining step is the first step with collision of HBr and but$\text{--1--}$ene $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    While rate laws are determined experimentally, they also be derived from proposed reaction mechanisms. For this electrophilic addition, the mechanism involves a slow (rate$\text{--}$determining) step where the double bond attacks the electrophile to form a carbocation intermediate.  This is followed by a fast step where the carbocation reacts with bromide to form the final product.

    $\begin{aligned}
    \ce{but\text{--}1\text{--}ene + HBr &->[slow] C4H9+ + Br-} \\
    \ce{C4H9+ + Br- &->[fast] C4H9Br}
    \end{aligned}$

    The rate law for any mechanistic step is written as the product of the rate constant and each reactant in the rate determining step. Here, the proposed rate law is $\text{rate} = k[\text{but--1--ene}][\ce{HBr}].$ This is consistent with the experimental rate law determined by the method of initial rates above.
