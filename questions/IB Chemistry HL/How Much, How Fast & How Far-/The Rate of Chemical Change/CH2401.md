# CH2401

Which properties can be monitored to reliably measure the rate of the following reaction?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$\ce{Na_2CO_3}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,NaCl}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H_2O}\textrm{(l)} + \ce{CO_2}\textrm{(g)}$

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$Change in volume of gas
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Change in temperature
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Change in mass

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Carbon dioxide gas is produced in this reaction, and two physical properties change continuously as it forms: the total volume of gas increases (measurable with a gas syringe or by collection over water), and the total mass of the open reaction vessel decreases as $\ce{CO_2}$ escapes. With a molar mass of $44\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$, the mass loss is large enough to be tracked reliably on a balance. Both measurements are directly proportional to the amount of $\ce{CO_2}$ produced, giving a clear rate curve when plotted against time.

Statement II is incorrect. Although the reaction is exothermic and the mixture does warm up, temperature is not a reliable way to monitor rate. In a well$-$designed rate experiment, temperature is a controlled variable.  It is held constant using a water bath so that changes in rate reflect only the changing concentration of reactants. If temperature is instead allowed to rise as the reaction proceeds, it becomes both a consequence of the reaction and a factor that accelerates it. The rising temperature increases the proportion of particles with $E \geq E_a$, which speeds up the reaction, which releases more heat. This feedback means a temperature *vs.* time curve does not cleanly reflect the extent of reaction in the way that gas volume or mass loss does.
