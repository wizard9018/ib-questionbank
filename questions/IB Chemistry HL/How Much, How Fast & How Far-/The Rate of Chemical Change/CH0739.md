# CH0739

Experimental data was collected for the following reaction.

$\ce{NO(g) + NO2(g) + O2(g) -> N2O5(g)}$

-   | Experiment | [NO]$_\mathbf{0}$ $\newline$mol  dm$\mathbf{^{-3}}$| [NO$\mathbf{_2}$]$_\mathbf{0}$ $\newline$mol  dm$\mathbf{^{-3}}$   |[O$\mathbf{_2}$]$_\mathbf{0}$ $\newline$mol  dm$\mathbf{^{-3}}$ | Rate   $\newline$mol  dm$\mathbf{^{-3}}$  s$\mathbf{^{-1}}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | 1 | 0.050 | 0.050 | 0.050 | $4.80 \times 10^{-3}$ |
    | 2 | 0.10 | 0.050 | 0.050 | $9.60 \times 10^{-3}$ |
    | 3 | 0.10 | 0.15 |0.10| $2.88 \times 10^{-2}$ |
    | 4 | 0.050 | 0.050 |0.10| $4.80 \times 10^{-3}$ |

Using this data, determine the rate law.

-   A. $\hspace{1em}rate = k\ce{[NO][NO2][O2]}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}rate = k\ce{[NO][NO2]}^3$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}rate = k\ce{[NO][NO2]}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}rate = k\ce{[NO][O2]}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

When [NO] doubles, but [NO$_2$] and [O$_2$] remain the same (Experiments 1 and 2), the rate doubles, therefore the order with respect to NO is 1.

When [O$_2$] doubles and [NO] and [NO$_2$] remain the same (Experiments 1 and 4), the rate does not change, and therefore the order with respect to O$_2$ is 0 and the concentration of O$_2$ does not affect the rate.

Because O$_2$ does not affect the rate, it can be ignored. [NO$_2$] triples while [NO] remains the same (Experiments 2 and 3), the rate triples and therefore the order with respect to $\ce{NO2}$ is also 1.

The rate law is

$\begin{aligned}
rate &= k[\ce{NO}]^1[\ce{O2}]^0[\ce{NO_2}]^1 \\
rate &= k[\ce{NO}][\ce{NO_2}]
\end{aligned}$

$\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

Another approach requires using the general form of the rate equation and solving for the unknown orders as follows.

The rate law for a chemical reaction can be written as the the rate constant $k$ multiplied by the concentrations of the reactants raised to an unknown power:

$rate = k[\ce{NO}]^a[\ce{O2}]^b[\ce{NO_2}]^c$

To evaluate the order of [NO], use Experiments 1 and 2:

$\dfrac{\textrm{Exp} \ 2}{\textrm{Exp} \ 1} \Longrightarrow \dfrac{9.60 \times 10^{-3}}{4.80 \times 10^{-3}} = \dfrac{k(0.10)^a(0.050)^b(0.050)^c}{k(0.050)^a(0.050)^b(0.050)^c}$

Canceling out factors that appear in the denominator and the numerator, we find

$2 = \dfrac{(0.10)^a}{(0.050)^a}$

$2 = 2^a; \hspace{1em} a = 1$

To evaluate the order of $[\ce{O2}]$, use Experiments 1 and 4:

$\dfrac{\textrm{Exp} \ 4}{\textrm{Exp} \ 1} \Longrightarrow \dfrac{4.80 \times 10^{-3}}{4.80 \times 10^{-3}} = \dfrac{k(0.050)^a(0.050)^b(0.10)^c}{k(0.050)^a(0.050)^b(0.050)^c}$

Canceling out factors that appear in the denominator and the numerator, we find

$1 = \dfrac{(0.10)^c}{(0.050)^c}$

$1 = 2^c; \hspace{1em} c = 0$

To evaluate the order with respect of $[\ce{NO2}]$, use Experiements 2 and 3.  Since the reaction is 0th order with respect to oxygen, the fact that the concentration changes will not impact our ability to determine the order.

$\dfrac{\textrm{Exp} \ 3}{\textrm{Exp} \ 2} \Rightarrow \dfrac{2.88 \times 10^{-2}}{9.60 \times 10^{-3}} = \dfrac{k(0.10)^a(0.15)^b(0.10)^c}{k(0.10)^a(0.050)^b(0.050)^c}$

Canceling out factors that appear in the denominator and the numerator, we find

$3 = \dfrac{(0.15)^b(0.10)^c}{(0.050)^b(0.050)^c}$

Recall that $c=0$ and anything raised to the power of 0 is equal to 1.  This expression further simplifies To

$3 = \dfrac{(0.15)^b}{(0.050)^b}$

$3 = 3^b; \hspace{1em} b = 1$

Inserting the values for $a$, $b$, and $c$ into the rate expression we fine that the rate law is

$rate = k[\ce{NO}]^1[\ce{O2}]^0[\ce{NO_2}]^1$

$rate = k[\ce{NO}][\ce{NO_2}]$
