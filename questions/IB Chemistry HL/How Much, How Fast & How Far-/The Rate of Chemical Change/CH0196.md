# CH0196

**Maximum mark: 10**

A student used an online simulation to collect the following data on kinetics of a clock reaction, timing the reaction until a dark blue colour appeared suddenly. They kept all other factors constant, including the concentrations of the other reactants ($\ce{[H2SO4]} = 0.10\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$, $\ce{[Na2S2O3]} = 0.030\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$, $[\text{starch}] = 0.001\,\text{mol dm}^{-3})$. 

-   :::tableoptions{}

    | Trial | $\ce{[H2O2]}\,(\text{mol dm}^{-3})$ | $\ce{[KI]}\,(\text{mol dm}^{-3})$ | Reaction Time (s) | Rate of Reaction |

    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |

    | 1 | 0.045 | 0.100 | 3.59 |

    | 2 | 0.045 | 0.050 | 7.20 |

    | 3 | 0.045 | 0.025 | 14.00 |

    | 4 | 0.0225 | 0.100 | 7.05 |

1.  Fill in the table with the respective rate of reaction for each trial. [2 marks]

2.  Sketch the graph for rate vs [KI] and state the order of reaction with respect to KI. [3 marks]

3.  Determine the order of reaction with respect to hydrogen peroxide using the data table. [2 marks]

4.  Suggest two further experiments that could be done to determine the rate expression. [2 marks]

5.  Suggest an advantage or a disadvantage about using a simulation to collect data. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Rate can be calculated using $\dfrac{1}{\text{total time}}$;

    For trial 1: rate = $\dfrac{1}{3.59} = 0.279\,\text{s}^{-1}$

    

    For trial 2: rate = $0.139\,\text{s}^{-1}$

    For trial 3: rate = $0.0714\,\text{s}^{-1}$

    For trial 4: rate= $0.142\,\text{s}^{-1}$ 

    *One mark for using rate= $\dfrac{1}{\text{total time}}$*; $\checkmark$

    *One mark for correct final answers.*$\checkmark$

2.  The sketch of graph should show:

    1 mark for axes; $\checkmark$

    1 mark for shape of graph linear up, (points not needed). $\checkmark$

    **First order with respect to KI** as double concentration doubles rate. $\checkmark$

    ![CH0196](images/CH0196.png)

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    The data shows a directly proportional increase of rate with concentration. Concentration is the independent variable and so should be written on the x axis.

3.  First order with respect to $\ce{H2O2}$; $\checkmark$

    Trials 3-4 show half conc of $\ce{H2O2 ->}$ half rate. $\checkmark$

    **OR** Calculations to show same:

    $rate = k[\ce{H2O2}]^m[KI]^1\,\checkmark$

    Trials 1 and 4:

    $\begin{aligned}

    rate_1 &= k(0.045)^m(0.100)^1 \\

    rate_4 &= k(0.0225)^m0.100)^1 \\

    \dfrac{rate_1}{rate_4} &= \dfrac{0.279}{0.142} = \dfrac{(0.045)^m}{(0.0225)^m} \\

    2 &= (2)^m\quad \Longrightarrow \text{ therefore, } m = 1\,\checkmark

    \end{aligned}$

4.  Need to vary concentration of other species in reaction eg. $\ce{[H2SO4], [Na2S2O3]}$;

    While keeping other concentrations the same / controlling temperature / other variables; $\checkmark$

    And recording time to colour change to calculate rate. $\checkmark$

5.  Advantage:

    No environmental cost to materials or disposal;\

    Accessible to all without lab equipment;\

    Reliable results as long as programmed correctly (no instrument uncertainty, etc).

    Disadvantage:

    Results rely on correct programming not chemistry; \

    No way to determine uncertainty or reliability of results; \

    (Sometimes) no variation in results gives false/unrealistic impression of reaction;  \

    No experience with materials and setting up equipment for fair experiment.

    *Any one of above. Other sensible responses possible.* $\checkmark$
