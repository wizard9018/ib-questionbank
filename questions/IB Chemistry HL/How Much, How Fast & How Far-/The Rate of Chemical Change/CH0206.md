# CH0206

Which of the following phenomena could be explained using the Arrhenius equation?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$Alkane cracking can occur at a lower temperature if a zeolite catalyst is added. 

-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Fireflies flash more rapidly during warmer nights. 

-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$The dissolution of ammonium nitrate in water causes its temperature to decrease.

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

The Arrhenius equation is used to analyze the effect of temperature on the rate of a chemical process.

The flashing of fireflies is catalyzed by the luciferase enzyme, and has a measurable activation energy; this example is arguably obscure and possibly misleading (flashing rate may also be controlled by biological stimuli, for example) but is mentioned in the syllabus so should be known or deduced through the relationship between rate and temperature.

Zeolites lower the activation energy for cracking, which can be measured using Arrhenius plots.

The endothermic nature of a dissolution depends on the enthalpy change rather than the rate, so cannot be explained using the Arrhenius equation.

This question is hard as it forces students to apply their knowledge to real$-$world situations without explicit reference to the rate constant or activation energy.
