# CH2399

Which statements correctly describe the effect of doubling the concentration of hydrochloric acid in the reaction between hydrochloric acid and excess calcium carbonate?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$2\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{CaCO_3}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{CO_2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{CaCl_2}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H_2O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$The frequency of collisions between $\ce{HCl}$ particles and the $\ce{CaCO_3}$ surface increases.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$The proportion of particles with $E \geq E_\textrm{a}$ increases.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$The total volume of $\ce{CO_2}$ produced increases.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Doubling the concentration of hydrochloric acid doubles the number of acid particles per unit volume. With more $\ce{HCl}$ particles in the same space, collisions with the surface of the solid calcium carbonate occur more frequently. Statement I is correct.

The proportion of particles with kinetic energy equal to or greater than the activation energy depends on temperature, not concentration. Doubling the concentration increases the total number of particles but does not change how their energies are distributed. The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution is unchanged, so the fraction of particles with $E \geq E_\textrm{a}$ remains the same. Statement II is incorrect.

Since calcium carbonate is in excess, hydrochloric acid is the limiting reactant. Doubling the concentration of $\ce{HCl}$ (at the same volume) doubles the number of moles of acid available to react. From the stoichiometry, $2\textrm{\ mol}$ of $\ce{HCl}$ produces $1\textrm{\ mol}$ of $\ce{CO_2},$ so twice the moles of acid yields twice the moles of carbon dioxide, and therefore a greater total volume of gas. Statement III is correct.
