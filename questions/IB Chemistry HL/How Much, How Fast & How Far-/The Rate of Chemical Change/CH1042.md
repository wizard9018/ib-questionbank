# CH1042

The data below shows the effect of the variation of the reactant concentrations on the rate of the following reaction at $298\,\text{K}.$

$\ce{A2 + 2 B2 -> 2 AB2}$

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| Initial [$\mathbf{A_2}$] $\newline$ ($\mathbf{mol\ dm^{-3}}$) | Initial [$\mathbf{B_2}$] $\newline$ ($\mathbf{mol\ dm^{-3}}$) | Initial rate  $\newline$  ($\mathbf{mol\ dm^{-3}\ s^{-1}}$) |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 0.200 | 0.050 | $2.2\times 10^{-2}$ |
| 0.400 | 0.050 | $4.4\times 10^{-2}$ |
| 0.200 | 0.150 | $6.6\times 10^{-2}$ |

What are the correct units of the rate constant, $k$, in the rate equation?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\text{s}^{-1}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\text{dm}^3\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\text{mol}\,\text{dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\text{dm}^6\,\text{mol}^{-2}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The rate law can be deduced from the given data. First, compare the first and second trials where the initial concentration of $\ce{B2},$ $[\ce{B_2}]_0,$ is kept constant.  When the initial concentration of $\ce{A2},$ $[\ce{A2}]_0,$ doubles, the rate doubles so the reaction is first order with respect to $\ce{A2}$. In the first and third trials, where $[\ce{A_2}]_0$ is kept constant, when $[\ce{B2}]_0$ triples, the rate triples.  The reaction is  first order with respect to $\ce{B2}$. The overall rate law is

$\textrm{rate} = k\ce{[A2][B2]}$

We can use the rate law to deduce the units of the  rate constant, $k.$ The rate has units of $\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ and the concentrations of $\ce{A2}$ and $\ce{B2}$ have units of $\text{mol dm}^{-3}.$

$\textrm{rate} = k\ce{[A2][B2]}$ \
$\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1} = k\left(\text{mol dm}^{-3}\right)\left(\text{mol dm}^{-3}\right)$ \

This question is categorized as hard. Students must deduce the rate law from the given data and evaluate the rate constant units from this previous result.
