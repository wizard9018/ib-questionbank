# CH0199

Reaction 1 has a smaller frequency factor, $A$, than reaction 2 but the same activation energy, $E_a$.

 Which property of reaction 1 could be the cause of this difference?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Use of a catalyst

- B.$\hspace{1em}$More complex reactant molecules

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Higher temperature

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Higher reactant concentration

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

The Arrhenius equation, $k = A e^{-E_a/RT}$, describes how the rate constant $k$ depends on the activation energy $E_a,$ the temperature $T,$ and the frequency factor $A.$ 

The term $e^{-E_a/RT}$ accounts for the proportion of molecules that have sufficient energy to overcome the activation barrier. The frequency factor reflects both the collision frequency and the fraction of collisions that occur with the correct orientation. When reactant molecules are more structurally complex, it becomes less likely that their collisions align in a way that leads to reaction, which lowers $A$.

Changing concentration affects the number of collisions in the bulk reaction but does not alter the intrinsic orientation factor within the Arrhenius expression. Therefore, the correct explanation is that the greater molecular complexity of reaction 1 reduces the probability of successful collisions, giving it a smaller value of $A$.

This is a mid$-$range question in difficulty because it requires students not just to recall the Arrhenius expression but also to connect the meaning of $A$ with molecular orientation and complexity.
