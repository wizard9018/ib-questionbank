# CH0838

0.24 g of magnesium reacts with 200 cm$^3$ of 0.50 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{H2SO4}$. The volume of gas produced over time is recorded and presented on the graph below with a solid line.

Which line represents the same reaction using 200 cm$^3$ of 1.0 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{H2SO4}$?

![CH0838](images/CH0838.png)

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Increasing the concentration of the reactant $\ce{H2SO4}$ increases the frequency of collisions and hence the number of successful collisions. Therefore, the rate of the reaction will increase, leading to a steeper slope.

$\ce{Mg + H2SO4 -> MgSO4 + H2}$

*Case 1*:

-   $\ce{n(Mg)} = \dfrac{0.24\,\text{g}}{24\,\text{g mol}^{-1}} = 0.01\,\text{mol}$
-   **AND**
-   $\ce{n(H2SO4)} = 200\times 10^{-3}\,\text{dm}^3\times 0.5\,\text{mol dm}^{-3} = 0.1\,\text{mol}$

With a ratio of 1 : 1, the acid is in excess, and $\ce{Mg}$ is the limiting reactant.

$n\ce{(H2)} = 0.01\,\text{mol}$

*Case 2*:

-   $n\ce{(Mg)} = \dfrac{0.24\,\text{g}}{24\,\text{g mol}^{-1}} = 0.01\,\text{mol}$
-   **AND**
-   $n\ce{(H2SO4)} = 200\times 10^{-3}\,\text{dm}^3\times 1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3} = 0.2\,\text{mol}$

With a ratio of 1 : 1, the acid is still in excess, and Mg is the limiting reactant.

$n\ce{(H2)} = 0.01\,\text{mol}$

An increased concentration of $\ce{H2SO4}$ will **not** result in an increased yield of $\ce{H2}$.
