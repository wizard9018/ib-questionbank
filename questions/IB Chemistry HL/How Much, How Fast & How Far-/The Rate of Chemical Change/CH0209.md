# CH0209

**Maximum mark: 8**

Concentrated aqueous hydrogen peroxide $\ce{(H2O2)}$ decomposes when heated in the presence of iodide ions.

$\ce{2H2O2(aq) -> 2H2O(l) + O2(g)}$

1.  Suggest how the rate of the reaction could be measured. [2 marks]

2.  The proposed mechanism for the reaction involves two steps.

    -   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide" col3="hide"}
        | | | |
        | -: | -: | :- |
        | Step 1: | $\ce{H2O2(aq) + I-(aq)}$ | $\ce{-> H2O(l) + IO-(aq)}$ |
        | Step 2: | $\ce{H2O2(aq) + IO-(aq)}$ | $\ce{-> H2O(l) + I-(aq) + O2(g)}$ |

    The energy level diagram for this mechanism is shown.

    ![16.2-209](images/16.2-209.png)

    1.  Explaining your reasoning, use the energy level diagram to deduce the rate-determining step (RDS). [1 mark]

    2.  Estimate the activation energy for the reaction, $E_a$, in kJ mol$^{-1}$. [1 mark]

        $E_a:\rule{20mm}{0.1mm}\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$

    3.  The reaction rate is measured at different reactant concentrations. Explain how this experiment could be used to prove or disprove the proposed reaction mechanism. [2 marks]

3.  The reaction can also be catalyzed by the enzyme catalase. The activation energy for this reaction, $E_a$, is 21.3 kJ mol$^{-1}$ and the frequency factor, A, is $4.1\times 10^{10}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\text{dm}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

    Calculate the value of the rate constant at $20\,{}^\circ \mathrm{C}$, in units of mol$^{-1}$ dm$^3$ s$^{-1}$. Use sections 1 and 2 of the data booklet. [2 marks]

    $k =\rule{20mm}{0.1mm}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\text{dm}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}$

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

at several time intervals. $\checkmark$

    *Do not accept titration methods, e.g. titration of iodide with thiosulfate.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Rate can be measured as change in mass/concentration/volume of a reactant or product per unit time.

2. 1. Step 1 is the RDS *AND* this step has the largest activation energy / energy barrier. $\checkmark$

    2. From graph, $E_a = 55 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}\, \checkmark$

        *Accept answers in range 52$-$58 kJ mol$^\mathit{\,-1}$;*\
    *Accept $E_a$ = 10$-$15 kJ mol$^\mathit{\,-1}$ if step 2 identified as RDS in (i) (follow$-$through error).*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    The activation energy here is the difference in energy between the reactants and the first peak.

    3. Expected rate equation is Rate $= k\ce{[H2O2][I-]}$ **OR** rate should be directly proportional to both $\ce{H2O2}$ and $\ce{I-}$ **OR** equivalent. $\checkmark$\
    Doubling $\ce{[H2O2]}$ or $\ce{[I-]}$ should cause the rate to double if other concentrations remain constant **OR** similar. $\checkmark$

3.  $21.3 \textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1} = 21300\textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}$ **OR** $R = 0.00831\textrm{\ kJ K}^{-1}\textrm{mol}^{-1}$ **OR** conversion factor of 1000  included in equation. $\checkmark$

    *Accept $\mathit{6.50 \times 10^6}\, mol^{-1}\, dm^3\, s^{-1}$ (incorrect rounding);*\
*Award [2] for correct answer with no workings.*
