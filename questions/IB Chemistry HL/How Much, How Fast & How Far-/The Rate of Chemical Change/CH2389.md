# CH2389

A mixture of $3$ moles of nitrogen and $1.5$ moles of hydrogen is kept at room temperature in a sealed container.  Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$No reaction occurs because the molecules do not collide with the correct orientation.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$No reaction occurs because the activation energy is not reached during collisions.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The mean kinetic energy of nitrogen molecules is greater than that of hydrogen molecules.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The mean speed of nitrogen molecules is greater than that of hydrogen molecules.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
For a reaction to occur, colliding particles must have kinetic energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, $E_\textrm{a}.$ The reaction between nitrogen and hydrogen has a very high activation energy because the $\ce{N\bond{#}N}$ triple bond $(945\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1})$ must be broken. At room temperature, virtually no molecular collisions possess enough energy to overcome this barrier, so no reaction is observed. This is why the industrial Haber process requires both high temperature $({\sim}450\,{}^\circ\textrm{C})$ and an iron catalyst to achieve a measurable rate.

Orientation is a factor in collision theory: not every collision has the geometry needed for bond rearrangement. However, at room temperature, even collisions with perfect orientation lack sufficient energy. Energy, not orientation, is the limiting factor here.

At the same temperature, all ideal gases have the same mean kinetic energy regardless of molar mass. Mean translational kinetic energy depends only on temperature. Nitrogen and hydrogen molecules at room temperature share the same mean kinetic energy.

While mean kinetic energy is the same for both gases at the same temperature, mean speed is not. Since $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2,$ lighter molecules must move faster to have the same kinetic energy. Hydrogen $(M = 2.02)$ moves considerably faster than nitrogen $(M = 28.02),$ not slower.
