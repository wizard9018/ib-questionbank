$\ce{Na2CO3(s) + 2 HNO3(aq) -> H2O(l) + CO2(g) + 2 NaNO3(aq)}$

    2.  $\ce{CO2}$ is produced in the reaction; hence, the volume of $\ce{CO2}$ increases over time.$\checkmark$

        Initially, the slope is the steepest, and the rate of reaction is the highest. This is because there is a greater concentration/number of particles, and hence, the frequency of successful collisions is higher in the beginning. $\checkmark$

        As the reaction progresses, the concentration/number of reactant particles decreases; hence, the frequency of successful collisions decreases. Thus, the curve becomes gentler over time. The graph flattens as all the sodium carbonate is used up and reacted with nitric acid.$\checkmark$

4.  The size of sodium carbonate./surface area.$\checkmark$

    The smaller the size, or the more powdered the sodium carbonate, the larger the surface area of the particles, increasing the frequency of effective collisions. $\checkmark$\

    $\textbf{OR}$\

    Adding a catalyst.$\checkmark$

    Catalysts provide an alternative pathway for the reaction with lower activation energy/ more particles have sufficient energy.$\checkmark$\

    $\textbf{OR}$\

    Temperature. $\checkmark$

    Increasing temperature increases the number of molecules with energy equal to or more than activation energy / and the frequency of effective collisions. $\checkmark$ *(or vice versa).*
