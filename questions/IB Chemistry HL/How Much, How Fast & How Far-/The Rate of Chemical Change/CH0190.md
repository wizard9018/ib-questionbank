# CH0190

If the units of the rate constant, $k$, for a reaction is $\textrm{mol}^{-2}\,\text{dm}^6\,\text{s}^{-1},$ what is the overall order of the reaction?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$4

- B.$\hspace{1em}$3

- C.$\hspace{1em}$2

- D.$\hspace{1em}$1

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

Units for $k$ are determined by dividing the units of rate by the units of the concentrations raised to their respective powers.

$\textrm{rate} = k[\textrm{reactants}]^n$

The rate of a chemical reaction is the change in concentration with respect to time and is expressed in units of $\textrm{mol dm}^{-3}\textrm{\,s}^{-1}.$ The units of concentration are $\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$.  Substituting these into the rate law: 

$\textrm{mol dm}^{-3}\textrm{\,s}^{-1} =  \textrm{\ mol}^{-2}\,\text{dm}^6\,\text{s}^{-1} [\textrm{mol dm}^{-3}]^n$

Rearranging this expression to solve for $n$, the overall order, 

$\textrm{mol}^3\, \textrm{dm}^{-9} =  [\textrm{mol dm}^{-3}]^n$

So $n=3.$
