# CH2576

Propane undergoes complete combustion according to the equation below.

$\ce{C3H8}\textrm{(g)} + 5\ce{\,O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 3\ce{\,CO2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

What are the volumes of oxygen and carbon dioxide when $4.0\textrm{ dm}^3$ of propane is burned at constant temperature and pressure?

| $\hspace{1.1em}$  | $\hspace{0.em}$ | | $\mathbf{V({O_2})\textbf{ / dm}^3}$ | $\mathbf{V({CO_2})\textbf{ / dm}^3}$ |
| :-- |:-:| :-- | :-: | :-:|
|| A. || $20.0$  | $16.0$ |
|| B. || $5.0$  | $3.0$ |
|| C. || $20.0$  | $12.0$ |
|| D. || $10.0$  | $6.0$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
At constant temperature and pressure, equal volumes of gas contain equal amounts of substance (Avogadro's law). Volume ratios of gases are therefore equal to mole ratios given by the stoichiometric coefficients.

The equation shows a $1:5:3$ mole ratio of $\ce{C3H8} : \ce{O2} : \ce{CO2}.$ Starting from $4.0\textrm{ dm}^3$ of propane:

$V(\ce{O2}) = 4.0\textrm{ dm}^3 \times \dfrac{5}{1} = 20.0\textrm{ dm}^3$

$V(\ce{CO2}) = 4.0\textrm{ dm}^3 \times \dfrac{3}{1} = 12.0\textrm{ dm}^3$

Water is produced as a liquid at this temperature, so it does not contribute a gas volume and is not included in the volume calculation.
