# CH0844

The rate of formation of $\ce{CH3OH}$ over the first 3 minutes at standard temperature and pressure is 4.0 g min$^{-1}$. What volume of $\ce{H2}$ in cm$^3$ has reacted over the same 3 minutes?

$\ce{CO(g) + 2 H2(g) -> CH3OH(l)}$

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\dfrac{12.0\times 22.7\times 10^3}{2\times (12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00)}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\dfrac{12.0\times 2\times 22.7\times 10^3}{(12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00)}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\dfrac{12.0\times 22.7\times 10^3}{(12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00)}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\dfrac{12.0\times 22.7\times 2}{(12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
In the first three minutes, the mass of methanol formed is

$m(\ce{CH3OH}) = 4.0 \textrm{\ g min}^{-1} \times 3 \textrm{\ min} = 12.0 \textrm{\ g}$

To determine the amount of hydrogen gas that reacted we will need to use the molar ratio.  First, find the moles of methanol formed:

$n\ce{(CH3OH)} = \dfrac{m}{M_r} = \dfrac{12.0\,\text{g}}{(12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00) \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}}$

The balanced chemical reaction shows the ratio of $\ce{CH3OH}$ to $\ce{H2}$ is 1 : 2, so

$n\ce{(H2)} = 2 \times  n\ce{(CH3OH)}$

The molar volume of hydrogen is $22.7\times 10^3\,\text{cm}^3\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.  To find the volume of hydrogen used over the three minutes, use the relationship $V = nV_m$:

$V\ce{(H2)} = \dfrac{12.0\,\text{g} \times 2 }{(12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00) \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} \times 22.7 \times 10^3 \textrm{\ cm}^3 \textrm{\ mol}^{-1}$

Simplifying reveals the answer in $\textrm{\ cm}^3$:

$V\ce{(H2)} = \dfrac{12.0 \times 2 \times 22.7 \times 10^3} {(12.01 + 4.04 + 16.00) }$
