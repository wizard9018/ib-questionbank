# CH2712

**Maximum mark: 11**

A student determined the percentage by mass of calcium carbonate, $\ce{CaCO3},$ in crushed, dried eggshell. A $0.4250 \textrm{\ g}$ sample of eggshell was added to $50.00 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $0.2000 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ hydrochloric acid, an excess. When the reaction was complete, the unreacted acid was titrated with $0.1980 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ standardized sodium hydroxide, $\ce{NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}.$

| $\mathbf{Trial}$ | $\mathbf{1}$ | $\mathbf{2}$ | $\mathbf{3}$ | $\mathbf{4}$ |
:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:
| **Final burette reading $\pm$ 0.05 / cm$^3$** | 11.20 | 9.70 | 19.45 | 29.25 |
| **Initial burette reading $\pm$ 0.05 / cm$^3$** | $\ $0.00 | $\ $0.00 | $\ $9.70 | 19.45 |
| **Volume of NaOH added / cm$^3$** | 11.20 | $\ $9.70 | $\ $9.75 | $\ $9.80 |

**Table 1:** Burette readings for the titration of the unreacted hydrochloric acid with $0.1980 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \ce{\,NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}.$ Trial 1 was a rough titration.

1. Write a balanced equation, including state symbols, for the reaction of calcium carbonate with hydrochloric acid. [1 mark]

2. Outline two reasons why a back$-$titration is used, rather than titrating the eggshell directly with the hydrochloric acid. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the amount, in mol, of $\ce{HCl}$ initially added to the eggshell. [1 mark]

4. Determine the amount, in mol, of unreacted $\ce{HCl},$ using the concordant titres from Table 1. [2 marks]

5. Determine the percentage by mass of $\ce{CaCO3}$ in the eggshell sample. $M_\textrm{r}(\ce{CaCO3}) = 100.09.$

    If you did not obtain an answer to part (d), use $2.00 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol},$ although this is not the correct answer. [2 marks]

6. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the mean titre. [1 mark]

7. Chicken eggshell is typically 94$-$97\% calcium carbonate by mass; the remainder is mostly an organic protein membrane that does not react with hydrochloric acid.

    Comment on whether the result is consistent with this composition, and explain why the result would be expected to be less than 100\% even for an error$-$free experiment. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\ce{CaCO3}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{CaCl2}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} + \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The 1 : 2 ratio between $\ce{CaCO3}$ and $\ce{HCl}$ carries through every later calculation; a 1 : 1 equation propagates a factor$-$of$-$two error into each of them. Counting atoms on both sides confirms the coefficients.

2. $\ce{CaCO3}$ is insoluble and reacts slowly  $\checkmark$ \
    endpoint is not sharp while undissolved solid remains $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    A direct titration requires the reaction at the point of addition to be rapid and the end point to be sharp. Neither holds for a suspension of solid carbonate: the acid reacts over minutes rather than instantly, and an indicator cannot signal a meaningful end point while undissolved solid remains. The back$-$titration converts the analysis into two homogeneous acid$-$base reactions in solution, each fast and each with a clean end point.

3. $1.000 \times 10^{-2} \textrm{\ mol}$ $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The moles of hydrochloric acid initially is $n=CV$ where the volume is expressed in $\textrm{dm}^3.$

    $n(\ce{HCl}) = 0.05000 \textrm{\ dm}^3 \times 0.2000 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} = 1.000 \times 10^{-2} \textrm{\ mol}$

4. mean titre $= 9.75 \textrm{\ cm}^3$  $\checkmark$ \
    $n(\ce{HCl})_{\textrm{unreacted}} = 1.93 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$ $\checkmark$

    *Award [2] for correct final answer.*\
    *Do not award M1 if Trial 1 is included in the mean.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Trials 2$-$4 agree within $0.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3,$ so all three are averaged; Trial 1 is the rough titration and is set aside:

    $\bar{V} = \dfrac{9.70 + 9.75 + 9.80}{3} \textrm{\ cm}^3 = 9.75 \textrm{\ cm}^3$

    Sodium hydroxide reacts with hydrochloric acid in a 1 : 1 ratio, so

    $n(\ce{HCl})_{\textrm{unreacted}} = n(\ce{NaOH}) = 9.75 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ dm}^3 \times 0.1980 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} = 1.93 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

5. $n(\ce{CaCO3}) = 4.04 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$ $\checkmark$ \
    $95.0\%$ $\checkmark$

    *Award [2] for correct final answer.*\
    *Accept 94.2\% from the value 2.00 $\times$ 10$^\mathit{-3}$ mol.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The acid consumed by the eggshell is the difference between what was added and what was left over:

    $n(\ce{HCl})_{\textrm{reacted}} = 1.000 \times 10^{-2} \textrm{\ mol} - 1.93 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol} = 8.07 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

    The 1 : 2 ratio then converts acid to carbonate:

    $n(\ce{CaCO3}) = \dfrac{8.07 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}}{2} = 4.04 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

    $m(\ce{CaCO3}) = 4.04 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol} \times 100.09 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = 0.404 \textrm{\ g}$

    $\%\ \ce{CaCO3} = \dfrac{0.404 \textrm{\ g}}{0.4250 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% = 95.0\%$

6. $1.0\%$ $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Each titre is the difference of two burette readings, so its absolute uncertainty is $2 \times 0.05 = 0.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3:$

    $\dfrac{0.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3}{9.75 \textrm{\ cm}^3} \times 100\% = 1.0\%$

7. consistent, as $95.0\%$ lies within 94$-$97% $\checkmark$ \
    membrane is included in the mass of the sample but does not react with the acid $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The measured 95.0% is inside the 94$-$97% literature window, so the analysis is consistent with real eggshell composition. The shortfall from 100% is not an error at all: the protein membrane is weighed in the denominator but never reacts, so the percent calcium carbonate is expected to be less than 100%.
