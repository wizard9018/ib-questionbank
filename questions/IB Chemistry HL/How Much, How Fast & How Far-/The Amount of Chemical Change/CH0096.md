# CH0096

**Maximum mark: 11**

Separate 20.00 $\text{cm}^3$ samples of a strong monoprotic acid in solution of unknown concentration were titrated with 0.500 $\text{mol}\,\text{dm}^{-3}$ NaOH.

1.  State a suitable indicator for this titration. Give a reason for your choice. [2 marks]

2.  A student carried out the titration 5 times with the following results:

    -
        | Trial                  | 1     | 2     | 3     | 4     | 5     | Mean  |
        | :-:                    | :-:   | :-:   | :-:   | :-:   | :-:   | :-:                   |
        | Volume NaOH used $(\pm 0.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3$)  | 14.30 | 13.55 | 13.55 | 13.45 | 13.50 | 13.67                 |

    $\mbox{}\\[-20pts]$

    1.  Using the mean volume, determine the concentration of the acid.[3 marks]

    2.  Suggest with a reason whether it is correct to use the mean volume to determine the concentration of the acid. [2 marks]

3.  Later, 0.50 moles of the strong monoprotic acid reacted with an excess NaOH. The products were gently heated to dryness. The resulting solid was weighed and found to be 29.4 g.

    Suggest the identity of the solid and the acid, giving reasons. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  phenol red (pH $6.8 - 8.4$)  or  bromothymol blue  (pH $6 - 7.6) \: \checkmark$\
    match equivalence point in strong acid$-$base titration at $\textrm{pH} = 7\: \checkmark$

2.  1.  $n  = 0.500\times 0.01367= 6.84\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol} \: \checkmark$\
        $1 : 1$ mole ratio $\checkmark$\
        $C = \dfrac{n}{V} = \dfrac{6.84\times 10^{-3}}{0.020} = 0.342\,\text{mol}\,\text{dm}^{-3} \: \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The amount of NaOH can be determined from the relationship
        $n = C V$, with the volume expressed in $\textrm{dm}^3.$

        $n = 0.500\,\text{mol}\,\text{dm}^{-3} \times 0.01367\ \text{dm}^3 = 6.84\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol}$

        The reaction

        $\ce{HA + NaOH -> NaA + H2O}$

        shows a $1:1$ ratio between the base and the acid.  The moles of acid must be equal to the moles of base.  The concentration of the acid can then be deduced:

        $C = \dfrac{n}{V} = \dfrac{6.84\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol}}{20\times 10^{-3}\,\text{dm}^{3}} = 0.342\,\text{mol}\,\text{dm}^{-3}$

    3.  no $\checkmark$\
        first trial is not concordant / rough titre / overshot endpoint $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        It would not be correct to use the mean volume, because the first titration is inconsistent with the others, and likely a rough titre to estimate the endpoint. The four concordant titres differ by only $0.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3,$ which is within the typical uncertainty of $\pm 0.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ for a burette reading. The first value, however, is  0.8 cm³ greater and should be treated as an outlier.

        Using the overall mean of $13.67 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ would introduce systematic error and give an inaccurate concentration for the acid.

3.  $M_{\ce{A}} =  \dfrac{29.4}{0.50} - 22.99 = 35.8\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\checkmark$\
    NaCl and HCl $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The reaction:

    $\ce{HA + NaOH -> NaA + H2O}$

    shows a $1 : 1$ mole ratio between the acid and the salt product. Therefore, 0.50 moles of salt is formed. The molar mass of the salt can be determined

    $M_{\ce{NaA}} = \dfrac{m}{n} = \dfrac{29.4\,\text{g}}{0.50\,\text{mol}} = 58.8\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$

    The molar mass of the conjugate base, $\ce{A-}$, forming the salt is $58.8 - 22.99 = 35.81\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ . This is very close to the molar mass of Cl. The other strong acids common in the syllabus $\ce{H2SO4}$, $\ce{HNO3}$, and HBr have very different masses.
