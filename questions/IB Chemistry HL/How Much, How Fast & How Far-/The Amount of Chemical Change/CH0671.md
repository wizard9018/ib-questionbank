# CH0671

**Maximum mark: 15**

A solution of an unknown hydrate of calcium chlorate ($\ce{Ca(ClO3)2}\cdot x\ce{H_2O}$) is made by dissolving  $4.164 \textrm{\ g}$ in $150.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of water. The resulting solution of calcium chlorate has a concentration of $0.1142 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$

1.
    1.  Determine the number of moles and the mass of calcium chlorate present in the solution. [2 marks]

    2.  Determine the mass of water and then the number of moles of water that was present in the hydrate used to make the original solution. [2 marks]

    3.  Determine the value of $x$ in the hydrate formula, $\ce{Ca(ClO3)2}\cdot x\ce{H_2O}.$ [1 mark]

2.  The calcium chlorate solution made above is then mixed with 200.0 cm$^3$ of $0.07500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ sodium phosphate  $\ce{(Na3PO4)}$, producing a precipitate of calcium phosphate, $\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}$.

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

    1.  Write the net ionic equation for this reaction, including state symbols. [2 marks]

    2.  Determine the limiting reactant in this reaction, showing your work. [2 marks]

    3.  If 1.535 g of calcium phosphate is recovered, determine the percent yield in this reaction. [3 marks]

    4.  Determine the concentration of phosphate ions remaining in the solution after removing the precipitate, assuming no loss of material. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. $n = 0.1142 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.1500 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2} \: \checkmark$\
        $m =  0.01713 \textrm{\ mol} \times 206.98 \textrm{\ g  mol}^{-1} = 3.546 \textrm{\ g\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2} \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        First, determine the number of moles of the $\ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$ using the relationship $n = CV.$

        $n = 0.1142 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.1500 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = $:answer[$0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$]

        To determine the mass of $\ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2},$ use the relationship $m = n M.$ The molar mass is

        $M\ce{(Ca(ClO_3)_2)} = 40.08 + 35.45 \times 2 + 16.00 \times 6 = 206.98 \textrm{\ g  \ mol}^{-1}$

        The mass is

        $m =  0.01713 \textrm{\ mol} \times 206.98 \textrm{\ g  mol}^{-1} = $ :answer[3.546 $\textrm{\ g\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$]

    2. $m_\ce{H2O} = 4.164 \textrm{\ g} - 3.546 \textrm{\ g} = 0.6180 \textrm{\ g} \: \checkmark$\
        $n_\ce{H2O} = \dfrac{0.6180 \textrm{\ g}}{18.02 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.0343 \textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        From part (a), we have determined the mass of the anhydrous compound, $3.546 \textrm{\ g } \ce{Ca(ClO3)_2}.$ In the experiment, $4.164 \textrm{\ g}$ of the hydrate was dissolved in the solution.  The difference in mass is the mass of water in the hydrate.

        $m_\ce{H_2O}$ = $m_\ce{hydrate} - m_\ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$ \
        $m_\ce{H2O} = 4.164 \textrm{\ g} - 3.546 \textrm{\ g} = $:answer[$0.6180 \textrm{\ g}$]

        The moles of water can be deduced from the relationshin $m = nM:$

        $n = \dfrac{0.6180 \textrm{\ g}}{18.02 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = $ :answer[$0.0343 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{H_2O}$]

    3. $x = \dfrac{n_\ce{H_2O}}{n_\ce{Ca(ClO3)2}} = \dfrac{0.0343 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{H_2O}}{0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}} = 2 \:\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$: \
        The formula for the hydrate, $\ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}\cdot x \ce{H_2O},$ shows that there are $x$ water molecules for every 1 $\ce{Ca(ClO3)2}$.  The ratio of the moles calculated in parts (a) and (b) is equal to the ratio $x:1.$

    $\dfrac{x}{1} = \dfrac{n_\ce{H_2O}}{n_\ce{Ca(ClO3)2}} = \dfrac{0.0343 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{H_2O}}{0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}} ; x = 2$

    -   The formula for the hydrate is $\ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2} \cdotp 2\ce{H_2O}.$

2.
    1. $3 \ce{\ Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ PO_4^{3-}}\textrm{(aq)}\longrightarrow \ce{Ca_3(PO_4)_{2}}\textrm{(s)} \: \checkmark \checkmark$

        *Award M1 for correct ion formulas and balancing.*\
        *Award M2 for correct states of matter.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The products of the double replacement reactions are $\ce{Ca_3(PO_4)_{2}}\textrm{(s)}$ and  $\ce{NaClO3}\textrm{(aq)}$. $\ce{Na^+}$ and $\ce{ClO3^-}$ are spectator ions and are eliminated from the net ionic equation.

        $3 \ce{\ Ca(ClO3)2}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ Na3PO_4}\textrm{(aq)}\longrightarrow \ce{Ca_3(PO_4)_{2}}\textrm{(s)} + 6 \ce{\ NaClO3}\textrm{(aq)}$

        $3 \ce{\ Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 6\ce{\ ClO3-}\textrm{(aq)} + 6 \ce{\ Na+}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ PO_4^{3-}}\textrm{(aq)}\longrightarrow \ce{Ca_3(PO_4)_{2}}\textrm{(s)} + 6 \ce{\ Na+}\textrm{(aq)} + 6 \ce{\ ClO3-}\textrm{(aq)}$

    2. $n_\ce{Na_3PO_4}$ = $0.07500 \textrm{\ mol \ dm}^{-3} \times 0.2000 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.01500 \textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$

        $\dfrac{0.01713}{3} < \dfrac{0.01500}{2}, \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$ is the limiting reagent $\checkmark$

        *Award full marks for any work which mathematically supports the determination of the limiting reagent.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The limiting reactant can be found by determining the moles of each reactant using the relationsip $n = CV,$ and dividing by the coefficient in the balanced equation.

        Alternatively, the limiting reagent can be determined by using the known number of moles of one reactant and determining how much of the other reactant would be required to react with it completely:

        $n_\ce{Na3PO4} = 0.07500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.2000 \textrm{\ dm}^3  = 0.01500 \textrm{\ mol \ } \ce{Na_3PO_4}$

        $n_\ce{Ca(ClO3)2, needed} = \dfrac{3 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}}{2 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{Na_3PO_4}} \times n_\ce{Na3PO4}$

        $n_\ce{Ca(ClO3)2, needed} = \dfrac{3}{2}\times  0.01500 \ = 0.02250 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$

        There is only $0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}$. Therefore Ca(ClO$_3$)$_2$ is the limiting reagent.

    3. Molar mass of calcium phosphate is $310.18 \textrm{\ g \ mol}^{-1} \: \checkmark$\
        Theoretical yield: $(0.01713 \textrm{\ mol})\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)(310.18 \textrm{\ g \ mol}^{-1}) = 1.771 \textrm{\ g} \: \checkmark$\
        $\textrm{Percent yield} = \dfrac{1.535 \textrm{\ g}}{1.771 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% = 86.67\% \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The expected or theoretical yield is calculated using the moles of the limiting reactant, $0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2}.$ Using the mole ratio from the balanced chemical equation, find the moles of precipitate, $\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}.$

        $n_\ce{Ca3(PO4)2} = 0.01713 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca(ClO_3)_2} \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}}{3 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{Ca(ClO3)_2}}$

        $n_\ce{Ca3(PO4)2} = 0.005710 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca3(PO4)2}$

        Multiply the moles of precipitate by the molar mass to determine the expected/theoretical mass of the precipitate.

        $M(\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}) = 3\times 40.07 + 30.97 + 4\times16.00 = $:answer[310.18 $\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$]

        $m_\ce{Ca3(PO4)2} = 0.005710 \textrm{\ mol\ }  \times 310.18 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = $:answer[1.771 $\textrm{\ g\ }\ce{Ca3(PO4)2}$]

         The actual or experimental yield is the collected mass given in the problem, $m = 1.535 \textrm{\ g}.$  The percent yield is calculated as the ratio

         $\textrm{Percent yield} = \dfrac{\textrm{actual}}{\textrm{theoretical}} \times 100\% = \dfrac{1.535 \textrm{\ g}}{1.771 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% = $:answer[86.67\%]

    4. Initial $n_\ce{PO_4^{3-}} = 0.07500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.2000 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.01500 \textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$\
        Removed $n_\ce{PO_4^{3-}} =  0.005710 \textrm{\ mol} \times \dfrac{2}{1} = 0.01142 \textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$\
        $C_\ce{PO_4^{3-}} = \dfrac{(0.01500-0.01142) \textrm{\ mol}}{0.350 \textrm{\ dm}^3} = 0.01023 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Each unit of  Na$_3$PO$_4$  contains one phosphate ion.  Initially, we found in part (i) that there were

        $n_\ce{Na3PO4} = 0.07500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.2000 \textrm{\ dm}^3  = 0.01500 \textrm{\ mol \ } \ce{Na_3PO_4}$

        moles of sodium phosphate, so there are :answer[$0.01500 \textrm{\ mol \ } \ce{PO_4^{3-}}$] at the start.

        Some of the phosphate ion precipitates as $\ce{Ca_3(PO_4)_2}$.  In part (iii), we found that there were

        $n_\ce{Ca3(PO4)2} = 0.005710 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Ca3(PO4)2}$

        that precipitated.  There is a $2:1$ ratio between calcium phosphate and phosphate, the number of moles of phosphate that precipate out is:

        $n_\ce{(PO4)^{3-}, ppt} = 0.005710 \textrm{\ mol\ } \times \dfrac{2}{1} = \ $:answer[$0.01142 \textrm{\ mol \ } \ce{PO_4^{3-}}$]

        The concentration of phosphate is the remaining moles of phosphate dividide by the total volume, $200.0 + 150.0 = 350.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$, reported in $\textrm{dm}^3.$

        $ C = \dfrac{n}{V} = \dfrac{(0.01500 - 0.01142) \textrm{\ mol}}{0.3500 \textrm{\ dm}^3 } = $:answer[$0.01023 \textrm{\ mol dm}^3$]
