# CH1505

Complete combustion of $50\text{\ cm}^3$ of a hydrocarbon vapour produces $350\text{\ cm}^3$ of carbon dioxide at the same temperature and pressure. Which is a possible molecular formula of the hydrocarbon?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{C8H18}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{C7H16}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{C6H14}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{C5H12}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

To determine the molecular formula of the alkane, we start by understanding the general reaction for the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon:

$\ce{C_xH_y + \left(\dfrac{x+y}{4}\right) O2 -> x CO2 + \left(\dfrac{y}{2}\right) H2O}$

-   Volume of hydrocarbon vapour $= 50\text{\ cm}^3$ \
    Volume of carbon dioxide produced $= 350\text{\ cm}^3$

The volumes of gases are proportional to the number of moles (at constant temperature and pressure). Therefore, we can infer the number of moles directly from the volumes given.

From the reaction stoichiometry, we see that 1 mole of hydrocarbon $(\ce{C_xH_y})$ produces $x$ moles of $\ce{CO2}.$ Since we start with $50\text{\ cm}^3$ of hydrocarbon and produce $350\text{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{CO2}:$

$\dfrac{x}{1} = \dfrac{350\text{\ cm}^3}{50\text{\ cm}^3};\quad  x = 7$

As an alkane, the general formula is $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}}.$ The alkane here is $\ce{C7H16}.$
