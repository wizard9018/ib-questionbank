# CH2382

In the reaction below, $16.0\textrm{\ g}$ of $\ce{Mg_3N_2}$ was obtained from $14.4\textrm{\ g}$ of $\ce{Mg}.$ What is the percentage yield? \
$(A_r: \ce{Mg} = 24 ; M_r:\textrm{\ } \ce{Mg3N2} = 100)$

$3\ce{\,Mg}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{N_2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{Mg_3N_2}\textrm{(s)}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}80\%$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}90\%$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}70\%$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}75\%$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Percentage yield compares the actual amount of product obtained to the theoretical maximum:

$\% \textrm{\ yield} = \dfrac{\textrm{actual yield}}{\textrm{theoretical yield}} \times 100$

First find the theoretical yield by working from the mass of $\ce{Mg}$:

$n(\ce{Mg}) = \dfrac{14.4\textrm{\ g}}{24\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.600\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ Mg}$

From the balanced equation, $3\textrm{\ mol } \ce{Mg}$ produces $1\textrm{\ mol } \ce{Mg_3N_2},$ so:

$n(\ce{Mg_3N_2}) = 0.600\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ Mg} \times \dfrac{1\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ Mg_3N_2}}{3\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ Mg}} = 0.200\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ Mg_3N_2}$

The theoretical yield of $\ce{Mg_3N_2}$ is:

$m(\ce{Mg_3N_2}) = 0.200\textrm{\ mol} \times 100\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = 20.0\textrm{\ g}$

Therefore:

$\% \textrm{\ yield} = \dfrac{16.0 \textrm{\ g}}{20.0 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100 = 80\%$
