# CH2381

Which reaction has the lowest atom economy?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{N_2} + 3\ce{\,H_2} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,NH_3}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C_6H_{12}O_6} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,C_2H_5OH} + 2\ce{\,CO_2}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}2\ce{\,Na} + \ce{Cl_2} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,NaCl}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C} + \ce{O_2} \longrightarrow \ce{CO_2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Atom economy measures how much of the total mass of reactants ends up in the desired product:

$\textrm{atom economy} = \dfrac{M \textrm{\ of desired product(s)}}{M \textrm{\ of all products}} \times 100$

A reaction with only one product always has $100\%$ atom economy because there is no waste. The formations of ammonia, sodium chloride and carbon dioxide each have have $100\%$ atom economy.

The fermentation of glucose, which produces both ethanol (the desired product) and carbon dioxide (a byproduct):

$\textrm{atom economy} = \dfrac{2 \times 46.08}{(2 \times 46.08) + (2 \times 44.01)} = \dfrac{92.16}{180.18} \times 100 = 51.1\%$

Any reaction that produces a byproduct will have an atom economy below $100\%.$
