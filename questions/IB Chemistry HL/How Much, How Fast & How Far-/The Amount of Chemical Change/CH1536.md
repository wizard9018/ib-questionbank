# CH1536

Hydrogen peroxide undergoes a catalytic decomposition, as shown below.

$ 2 \textrm{\ H}_2\textrm{O}_2\textrm{(l)} \xrightarrow{{\ce{MnO2}}} 2 \ce{\ H2O}\textrm{(l)}+ \ce{O2}$

Three experiments were conducted, and the volume of oxygen produced was measured as a function of time. (The gas was collected at a constant temperature of 298 K.) The processed data are shown in the graph below.

![CH1536](images/CH1536.png)

- Which statement is correct?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Experiments 1 and 2 had the same volume and concentration of hydrogen peroxide solution, but Experiment 1 was performed at a lower temperature.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$Experiments 2 and 3 were performed at the same temperature and had the same volume of hydrogen peroxide solution, but a more concentrated solution was used in Experiment 2.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Experiments 1 and 3 were performed at the same temperature and used the same volume of hydrogen peroxide solution, but Experiment 1 used a more concentrated solution.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Variations in the experiments could correspond to different masses of $\ce{MnO2}.$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

The student must understand the factors affecting the rate of a chemical reaction. 

- Temperature affects the rate; the higher the temperature, the higher the rate. Temperature does not affect the final amount of product formed. 

    - Option A cannot be correct as Experiment 1 is the one with the largest gradient (greatest initial rate), so it does not correspond to a lower temperature than Experiment 2 under the same conditions. 

- Concentration affects the rate of a chemical reaction. The higher the concentration, the higher the rate of the reaction (as demonstrated by a steeper curve) as the collision between reactants will be more frequent. 

    - An increase in the concentration of hydrogen peroxide, with a constant volume, will increase the amount of oxygen produced and increase the rate of the reaction. Option B is false, the initial gradient for Experiments 2 and 3 is the same. This is not consistent with Experiment 2 having a higher concentration of hydrogen peroxide. 

    - Option C is true as an increase in the concentration for Experiment 1 shows a steeper gradient and a larger volume of oxygen produced. 

- The use of a catalyst does not affect the final amount of product formed.

    - Option D is incorrect. all of the curves should asymptote to the same final volume of oxygen produced.

This question is categorized as hard as the student must use the understanding of different factors affecting the rate of a chemical reaction (such as temperature, concentration and presence of catalyst) to analyze the given trends.
