# CH1544

A titration of $100 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of HCl(aq) required $36.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $0.100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ NaOH(aq) to reach the endpoint. What would be the volume of $0.100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$(aq) solution, in $\textrm{\ cm}^3$, required to reach the endpoint if the titration was repeated?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$36.0

- B.$\hspace{1em}$18.0

- C.$\hspace{1em}$72.0

- D.$\hspace{1em}$9.0

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:  

The student must be able to compare the neutralization reactions.

$\textrm{HCl(aq)} + \textrm{NaOH(aq)} \longrightarrow \textrm{NaCl(aq)} + \textrm{H}_2\textrm{O(l)}$

$ 2 \textrm{\ HCl(aq)} + \ce{Ca(OH)_2}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{CaCl_2}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

In the second neutralization, two HCl molecules react every $\ce{Ca(OH)_2}$. 

$\ n_{\ce{Ca(OH)_2}}= \frac{1}{2} \times n_{\ce{HCl}}$

The concentration of $\ce{Ca(OH)_2}$ is equal to that of NaOH. In order to neutralize the HCl, we need half the volume of NaOH:

$V = \frac{1}{2} \times {36.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3}=18.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$

This question is categorized as easy as the student must work out the neutralization reactions and deduce the volume needed.
