# CH0030

Nitrogen gas reacts with hydrogen gas to produce ammonia as per the following chemical equation:

$\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) -> 2NH3(g)}$

If $0.86\,\text{moles}$ of $\ce{N2}$ are mixed with $0.50\,\text{moles}$ of $\ce{H2}$, which reactant is in excess?

- A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{N_{2}}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{H_{2}}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{NH_{3}}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Both reactants are in excess

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To identify the limiting reactant, compare the mole ratio of the reactants to the stoichiometric ratio required by the balanced equation.

The balanced equation requires $\ce{N2}$ and $\ce{H2}$ in a $1:3$ ratio. Calculate the amount of $\ce{N2}$ needed to react with all of the $\ce{H2}:$

$n(\ce{N2}) \textrm{\ required} = 0.50 \times \dfrac{1}{3} = 0.17 \textrm{\ mol}$

Since $0.86 \textrm{\ mol}$ of $\ce{N2}$ is available but only $0.17 \textrm{\ mol}$ is needed, $\ce{N2}$ is in excess and $\ce{H2}$ is the limiting reactant.

**Alternatively:**

The excess can also be confirmed from the other direction. The amount of $\ce{H2}$ needed to react with all of the $\ce{N2}$ is:

$n(\ce{H2}) \textrm{\ required} = 0.86 \times 3 = 2.58 \textrm{\ mol}$

Only $0.50 \textrm{\ mol}$ of $\ce{H2}$ is available, confirming that $\ce{H2}$ is insufficient and is therefore the limiting reactant.
