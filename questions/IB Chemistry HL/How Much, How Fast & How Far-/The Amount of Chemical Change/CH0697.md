# CH0697

**Maximum mark: 7**

Phosphorus is an element in group 15 of the periodic table.

1.  State the total number of occupied p orbitals at all levels in an atom of phosphorus. [1 mark]

2.  Phosphorus, $\ce{P4}$, reacts with oxygen to form phosphorus oxide, $\ce{P4O10}$, by the following equation, $\ce{P4 + 5 O2 -> P4O10}$.

    1.  Calculate the minimum volume of oxygen gas required to fully react with 16.7 g phosphorus at 273 K and 100 kPa. [2 marks]

    

    2.  20.4 g of phosphorus oxide was formed during this reaction. Calculate the percentage yield. [2 marks]

3.  Describe the acid-base nature of phosphorus oxide and use an equation to demonstrate this behaviour. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  6.$\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Electron configuration $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^3$. Using Hund's rule, the 3$p$ orbitals must each be singly occupied.

2.  1.  $n(\ce{P4}) = \dfrac{16.7}{123.88} = 0.135\,\text{mol}$ *AND* $n(\ce{O2}) = 0.135\times 5 = 0.674\text{mol}$.  $\checkmark$

        $V(\ce{O2}) = 0.674\times 22.7\,\text{dm}^3\,\text{mol}^{-1} = 15.3\text{dm}^3\ \text{OR}\ 15300\,\text{cm}^3\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        $moles = \dfrac{mass}{Mr}$;

        Ratio $\ce{P4}:\ce{O2}$ is $1:5$;

        $volume = moles\times molar\ gas\ volume$.

    2.  Actual $n(\ce{P4O10}) = \dfrac{20.4}{283.88} = 0.0719\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

        Percentage yield $= \dfrac{0.0719}{0.135}\times 100 = 53.3\%\,\checkmark$

        **OR**

        Theoretical mass $(\ce{P4O10}) = 0.135\times 283.88 = 38.3\,\text{g}.\,\checkmark$

        Percentage yield $ = \dfrac{20.4}{38.3}\times 100 = 53.3\%\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        $Percentage\ yield = \dfrac{Actual\ yield}{Theoretical\ yield}\times 100$. This can be calculated using the moles (method 1) or the mass (method 2).

3.  Phosphorus oxide is acidic; $\checkmark$

    $\ce{P4O10(s) + 6 H2O(l) -> 4 H3PO4(aq)}\,\checkmark$

    *State symbols NOT required.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Non-metal oxides are acidic. This can be shown through their reactions with water.
