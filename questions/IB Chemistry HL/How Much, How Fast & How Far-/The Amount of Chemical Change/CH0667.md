# CH0667

Barium hydroxide and phosphoric acid react according to the following equation:

$\ce{3 Ba(OH)2(aq) + 2 H3PO4(aq) -> 6 H2O(l) + Ba3(PO4)2(s)}$

What volume of 0.100 mol dm$^{-3}$ phosphoric acid is required to completely neutralize 50.0 cm$^3$ of 0.030 mol dm$^{-3}$ barium hydroxide solution?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}10\,\text{cm}^3$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}22.5\,\text{cm}^3$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}15\,\text{cm}^3$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}5.0\,\text{cm}^3$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
For neutralization, the moles of the acid must be $\textit{stoichiometrically equivalent}$ to the moles of base, that is they must appear in the $3:2$ ratio given by the balanced chemical equation.

First, find the moles of barium hydroxide using the relationship $n = CV$ where $n$ is the amount in moles, $C$ is the concentration in mol dm$^{-3}$ and $V$ is the volume in dm$^3$.

$n_\ce{Ba(OH)2} = 0.030\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\times \dfrac{50.0\,\text{cm}^3}{1000\,\text{cm}^3\,\text{dm}^{-3}} = 0.0015\,\text{mol}$

The moles of phosphoric acid can be deduced from the mole ratio:

$n_\ce{H3PO4} = 0.0015\,\text{mol }\ce{Ba(OH)2}\times \dfrac{2\,\text{mol }\ce{H3PO4}}{3\,\text{mol }\ce{Ba(OH)2}} = 0.0010\,\text{mol}$

The volume of phosphoric acid can be found with the relathionship $V = \dfrac{n}{C}$, where the volume is converted back the cm$^3$ to match the answer choices.

$V = \dfrac{0.0010\,\text{mol }\ce{H3PO4}}{0.100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}}\times 1000\,\text{cm}^3\,\text{dm}^{-3} = 10\,\text{cm}^3$

The total volume required is $10\,\text{cm}^3$.
