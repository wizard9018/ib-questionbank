# CH0672

**Maximum mark: 11**

Siderite is a mineral containing iron in the form of iron (II) carbonate, $\ce{FeCO3}$.

A 15.00 g sample of impure siderite was ground into a fine powder and dissolved in 250.0 cm$^3$ of hot water, and then filtered to remove the insoluble components. After filtration, a 35.00 cm$^3$ sample of the iron solution was analyzed via redox titration using a 0.120 mol dm$^{-3}$ solution of potassium dichromate. The balanced redox equation is given below:

$\ce{Cr2O7^2-(aq) + 14 H+(aq) + 6 Fe^2+(aq) -> 2 Cr^3+(aq) + 6 Fe^3+(aq) + 7 H2O(l)}$

1.
    1.  Calculate the theoretical percent composition of iron in a sample of pure siderite or iron (II) carbonate. [1 mark]

    2.  If 18.1 cm$^3$ of dichromate solution is used to titrate the actual iron sample to the equivalence point, calculate the number of moles of iron in the titrated sample. [2 marks]

    3.  Using your value from a(ii), determine the mass of iron present in the original sample of siderite. [2 marks]

    4.  Calculate the mass of iron carbonate ($\ce{FeCO3}$) in the impure sample and then calculate the percent of impurities found in this sample. [2 marks]

2.  Another iron$-$containing mineral is a hydrate of iron sulfate, $\ce{Fe_x(SO4)_y}\cdot n \ce{H2O}$. A pure sample is first heated to drive off all of the water. After heating to a consistent mass, a sample with a mass of 5.250 g is found to have 3.561 g of anhydrous material remaining. The remaining anhydrous material was analyzed and found to have 36.76$\%$ iron, 21.11$\%$ sulfur, and 42.13$\%$ oxygen.

    1.  Determine the empirical formula of the anhydrate. [1 mark]

    2.  If the molar mass of the anhydrate is determined to be 151.92 g mol$^{-1}$, what is the actual formula of the anhydrate? [1 mark]

    3.  Using your answer to b(ii), determine the number of moles of water present and, thus, the formula of the hydrate. [2 marks]

    $\ $

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. $\% \textrm{\ Fe} = \dfrac{55.85}{55.85 + 12.01 + 3 \times 16.00} \times 100\%$ =
    :answer[$48.20\%$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: To determine the percent composition of an element in a compound, start by considering one mole of the compound. The mass of the element in this mole is equal to its molar mass, and the total mass of the compound is its molar mass. The percent composition of the element is then calculated by dividing the element’s molar mass by the molar mass of the compound and multiplying by 100.

         $\% \textrm{\ composition of Fe} = \dfrac{M_\ce{Fe}}{M_\ce{FeCO_3}} \times 100\%$

    2. $n_\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}} = 0.120 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.0181 \textrm{\ dm}^3 \ \checkmark$
        $n_\ce{Fe^{2+}} = n_\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}} \times \dfrac{ 6 \textrm{\ mol \ }\ce{Fe^{2+}}}{1 \textrm{\ mol \ }\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}}$ = :answer[0.0130 mol] $\checkmark$  \
        *Award M1 and M2 for the correct answer.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:  First, determine the moles of dichromate used in the titration:

        $n_\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}} = C \times V $

        $n_\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}} = 0.120 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 18.10 \textrm{\ cm}^3 \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ dm}^3}{1000 \textrm{\ cm}^3} $

        $n_\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}} = 0.0002172 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Cr2O7^{2-}} $

        From the balanced chemical equation, find  the ratio of dichromate ion to iron (II) is $1:6$, so  determine the moles of iron using the molar ratio.

        $n_\ce{Fe^{2+}} = n_\ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}} \times \dfrac{6 \textrm{\ mol \ }\ce{Fe^{2+}}}{1 \textrm{ \ mol \ } \ce{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}}$

        $n_\ce{Fe^{2+}} = 0.0002172 \textrm{\ mol} \times 6 = 0.0130 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{Fe^{2+}}$

    3. $n_\textrm{total} = 0.0130 \textrm{\ mol} \times \dfrac{0.250 \textrm{\ dm}^3}{0.035 \textrm{\ dm}^3} = 0.0929 \textrm{\ mol}$ $\checkmark$ \
        $m_\textrm{total} = 0.0929 \textrm{\ mol\ } \ce{Fe^{2+}} \times 55.85 \textrm{\ g  mol}^{-1} = \ $:answer[5.19 g] $\checkmark$\
        *Award M1 and M2 for the correct answer.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
        The moles of Fe$^{2+}$ determined in (ii) is in the $35.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ sample. The entire sample of siderite was added to $250.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of water.  The total number of moles can be determined using ratios:

        $\dfrac{n_\ce{35 cm^3}}{35 \textrm{\ cm}^3} = \dfrac{n_\textrm{total}}{250 \textrm{\ cm}^3}$

         $\dfrac{0.0130 \textrm{\ mol}}{35 \textrm{\ cm}^3} = \dfrac{n_\textrm{total}}{250 \textrm{\ cm}^3}$

         $n_\textrm{total} = 0.0130 \textrm{\ mol} \times \dfrac{250 \textrm{\ cm}^3}{35 \textrm{\ cm}^3} = 0.0929 \textrm{\ mol}$

         The total mass of iron is given by

        $m = n M$

        $m= 0.0929 \textrm{\ mol} \times 55.85 \textrm{\ g  mol}^{-1} = 5.19 \textrm{\ g}$

    4. $m_\ce{FeCO3} = 0.0929 \textrm{\ mol\  }\ce{Fe^{2+}} \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ mol \ }\ce{FeCO_3}}{1 \textrm{\  mol \ }\ce{Fe^{2+}}} \times 115.86 \textrm{\ g \ mol}^{-1}$ = :answer[10.8 g] $\checkmark$\
        $\% \textrm{\ impurity} = \dfrac{15.00 \textrm{\ g} - 10.8 \textrm{\ g}}{15.00 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100$ = :answer[$28.0\%$] $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

        **Method 1:** Using the total number of moles determine in part a(iii), find the mass of iron (II) carbonate as follows:

        $m_\ce{FeCO_3} = n_\textrm{total} \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{ \ mol \ }\ce{FeCO_3}}{1 \textrm{\  mol \ }\ce{Fe^{2+}}} \times \dfrac{115.86 \textrm{\ g\ }\ce{FeCO3}}{1 \textrm{\ mol \ }\ce{FeCO3}} = 10.8 \textrm{\ g \ }\ce{FeCO3}$

       because there is a $1:1$ mole ratio between iron (II) ions and the iron (II) carbonate.  To convert from moles to grams, multiply by the molar mass.

        **Method 2:** Using the total mass of iron found in part a(iii), use the percent composition to determine the mass of the iron (II) carbonate as follows:

        $\dfrac{m_\ce{Fe}}{m_\ce{FeCO3}} \times 100\% = 48.20$

        $ \dfrac{5.19 \textrm{\ g}}{m_\ce{FeCO3}} = 0.4820; \hspace{1em} m_\ce{FeCO3} = 10.8 \textrm{\ g}$

        To determine the percent impurity, subtract the mass of the FeCO$_3$ from the mass of the sample. What remains represents the impurities. Divide by the mass of the original sample to find the percentage.

        \% impurity = $\dfrac{m_\ce{sample} - m_\ce{FeCO_3}}{m_\ce{sample}} \times 100\%$

        $\% \textrm{\ impurity} = \dfrac{15.00 \textrm{\ g} - 10.8 \textrm{\ g}}{15.00 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% =28.0\%  $

    3. $n_\ce{FeSO_4} = \dfrac{3.561 \textrm{\ g}}{151.92\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.02344 \textrm{\ mol}$ \
    **AND**\
        $n_\ce{H_2O} =  \dfrac{5.250 \textrm{\  g\ } \ce{FeSO4\cdot nH2O} - 3.561 \textrm{\  g\ }\ce{FeSO4}}{18.02 \textrm{\ g  mol}^{-1}} = 0.09373 \textrm{\ mol}$ $\checkmark$

       $n = \dfrac{n_{\ce{H_2O}}}{n_\ce{FeSO4}} = \dfrac{0.09373 \textrm{\ mol}}{0.02344 \textrm{\ mol}} \approx 4$\
        **AND**\
         $\ce{FeSO_4\cdot 4 H_2O}$ $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:  Determine the mass of water in the hydrate, $\ce{FeSO4\cdot nH2O}$:

        $m_\ce{H_2O} =  5.250 \textrm{\  g\ } \ce{FeSO4\cdot nH2O} - 3.561 \textrm{\  g\ }\ce{FeSO4}$

        $m_\ce{H2O} = 1.689 \textrm{\ g\ }\ce{H2O}$

        Using the mass of the water and the anhydrate, $\ce{FeSO4}$, find the moles of each and their ratio.

        $n_\ce{H2O} = \dfrac{1.689 \textrm{\ g\ }\ce{H2O}}{18.02 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.09373 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{H2O}$

         $n_\ce{FeSO_4} = \dfrac{3.561 \textrm{\ g}}{151.92\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.02344 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{FeSO4}$

        There ratio will reveal $n$, the numbers of waters of hydration in the compound:

        $n = \dfrac{n_{\ce{H_2O}}}{n_\ce{FeSO4}} = \dfrac{0.09373 \textrm{\ mol}}{0.02344 \textrm{\ mol}} \approx 4$

        The compound is $\ce{FeSO4\cdot 4 H2O}$
