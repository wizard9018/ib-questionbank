# CH2380

A reaction between $4.04\textrm{\ g}$ of hydrogen gas $(\ce{H_2})$ and $16.00\textrm{\ g}$ of oxygen gas $(\ce{O_2})$ forms water. What is the limiting reactant?

$2\ce{\,H_2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O_2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,H_2O}\textrm{(l)}$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Hydrogen gas $(\ce{H_2})$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Oxygen gas $(\ce{O_2})$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Water $(\ce{H_2O})$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$There is no limiting reactant

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The limiting reactant is the reactant that is completely consumed first, determining the maximum amount of product that can form. To identify it, convert both masses to moles and compare them using the stoichiometric ratio.

$n(\ce{H_2}) = \dfrac{4.04 \textrm{\ g} \ce{\ H2}}{2.02 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 2.00\textrm{\ mol} \ce{\ H2}$

$n(\ce{O_2}) = \dfrac{16.00 \textrm{\ g}\ce{\ O2}}{32.00 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.500\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ O2}$

From the balanced equation, $2\textrm{\ mol } \ce{H_2}$ reacts with $1\textrm{\ mol } \ce{O_2}.$ The amount of $\ce{O_2}$ required to react with $2.00\textrm{\ mol } \ce{H_2}$ is:

$n(\ce{O_2}\textrm{ required}) = 2.00 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ H2} \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ O2}}{2\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ H2}} = 1.00\textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ O2}$

Only $0.500\textrm{\ mol } \ce{O_2}$ is available, which is less than the $1.00\textrm{\ mol}$ required. Oxygen is therefore the limiting reactant and hydrogen is in excess.
