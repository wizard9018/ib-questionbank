# CH2048

What is the limiting reactant and how many moles of water would be expected from the reaction of 2.0 moles of oxygen gas and 5.0 moles of hydrogen gas?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Oxygen gas is limiting, 5.0 mol of water is formed

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Hydrogen gas is limiting, 5.0 mol of water is formed

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Oxygen gas is limiting, 4.0 mol of water is formed

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Hydrogen gas is limiting, 4.0 mol of water is formed

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

To answer this question, the balanced chemical equation must first be determined:

$\ce{O2(g) + 2 H2(g) -> 2 H2O(g)}$

From this balanced equation, the mole ratio between the oxygen gas and hydrogen gas is $1:2.$
Therefore, twice as much hydrogen gas is required. There are two possible scenarios for which reactant is limiting:

***Oxygen gas is limiting:***\
This would require

$2.0\text{ mol }\ce{O2}\times \dfrac{2\text{ mol }\ce{H2}}{1\text{ mol }\ce{O2}} = 4.0\text{ mol }\ce{H2}$

$4.0\text{ mol }\ce{H2} < 5.0\text{ mol }\ce{H2};\quad \ce{H2}\text{ in excess}$

***Hydrogen gas is limiting:***\
This would require

$5.0\text{ mol }\ce{ H2}\times \dfrac{1\text{ mol }\ce{O2}}{2\text{ mol }\ce{H2}} = 2.5\text{ mol }\ce{O2}$

$2.5\text{ mol }\ce{O2} > 2.0\text{ mol }\ce{O2};\quad \ce{O2}\text{ limits}$

The yield of water can now be deduced:

$2.0\text{ mol }\ce{O2}\times \dfrac{2\text{ mol }\ce{H2O}}{1\text{ mol }\ce{O2}} = 4.0\text{ mol }\ce{H2O}$
