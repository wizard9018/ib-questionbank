# CH2388

[Maximim marks: 12]

Sodium azide $(\ce{NaN_3})$ is widely used in automobile airbags, where it decomposes on impact to rapidly produce nitrogen gas.

1.  One of the most common methods to prepare sodium azide is the reaction between sodium nitrite $(\ce{NaNO_2})$ and hydrazine $(\ce{N_2H_4}).$

    $\ce{NaNO_2}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{N_2H_4}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{NaN_3}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{H_2O}\textrm{(l)}$

    Calculate the mass of sodium nitrite needed to obtain $330.0\textrm{\ g}$ of sodium azide, assuming a percentage yield of $95.0\%.$ [3 marks]

2. Formulate a balanced equation, including state symbols, for the decomposition of sodium azide in an airbag. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the volume, in $\textrm{m}^3,$ of nitrogen produced when $330.0\textrm{\ g}$ of sodium azide decomposes at a pressure of $150\textrm{\ kPa}$ and a temperature of $30\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ [2 marks]

4. Sodium azide is toxic and can be converted to less harmful products by reaction with freshly prepared nitrous acid $(\ce{HNO_2})$ as shown.

    $2\ce{\,NaN_3}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,HNO_2}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 3\ce{\,N_2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,NO}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}$

    A safety engineer claims that the minimum volume of $12.0\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ nitrous acid solution needed to fully convert $330.0\textrm{\ g}$ of sodium azide is $500\textrm{\ cm}^3.$ Evaluate this claim, showing your working. [3 marks]

5. Sodium azide can also be synthesized by the reaction of sodium metal with nitrous oxide $(\ce{N_2O})$ and ammonia under anhydrous conditions.

    $2\ce{\,Na}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{N_2O}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{NH_3}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{NaN_3}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{NaOH}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{H_2}\textrm{(g)}$

    Compare the atom economy of the two methods for the manufacture of sodium azide, showing your calculations. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $m(\ce{NaN_3})_{\textrm{theoretical}} = 347.4\textrm{\ g NaN}_3 \: \checkmark$\
    $n(\ce{NaNO_2}) = 5.342\textrm{\ mol NaNO}_2 \: \checkmark$\
    $m(\ce{NaNO_2}) = 368.6\textrm{\ g} \: \checkmark$\
    *Award [3] marks for correct mass.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    330.0 g of sodium azide was made with a 95.0% yield. Determine the theoretical yield if all of the sodium nitrite was converted:

    $\textrm{\% yield} = \dfrac{\textrm{actual yield}}{\textrm{theoretical yield}} \times 100\% \Rightarrow \textrm{theoretical yield} = \dfrac{\textrm{actual yield}}{\% \textrm{yield}} \times 100\%$

    $m(\ce{NaN_3}) = \dfrac{330.0 \textrm{\ g}}{0.950} = 347.4\textrm{\ g} \ce{\ NaN_3}$

    Find the moles of $\ce{NaN_3}$:

    $n(\ce{NaN_3}) = \dfrac{347.4\textrm{\ g}}{65.02\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 5.342\textrm{\ mol NaN}_3$

    There is a $1:1$ molar ratio between sodium azide and sodium nitrite in the balanced chemical equation:

    $n(\ce{NaNO_2}) = 5.342\textrm{\ mol NaN}_3 \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ NaNO_2}}{1 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ NaN_3}} = 5.342\textrm{\ mol NaNO}_2$

    Convert to mass using $M(\ce{NaNO_2}) = 69.00\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$:

    $m(\ce{NaNO_2}) = 5.342\textrm{\ mol} \times 69.00\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = 368.6\textrm{\ g}$

    $\ $

2.  $2\ce{\,NaN_3}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Na}\textrm{(s)} + 3\ce{\,N_2}\textrm{(g)} \: \checkmark \checkmark$\
    *Award M1 for balanced equation.*\
    *Award M2 for correct state symbols.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The decomposition of sodium azide produces sodium metal and nitrogen gas. Balancing requires $2$ moles of $\ce{NaN_3}$ to give $2$ moles of $\ce{Na}$ and $3$ moles of $\ce{N_2}$:

    $2\ce{\,NaN_3}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Na}\textrm{(s)} + 3\ce{\,N_2}\textrm{(g)}$

    State symbols: $\ce{NaN_3}$ is a solid, $\ce{Na}$ is a solid, and $\ce{N_2}$ is a gas.

3.  $n(\ce{N_2}) = 7.614\textrm{\ mol N}_2 \: \checkmark$\
    $V = 0.128\textrm{\ m}^3 \: \checkmark$\
    *Award [2] for correct volume.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The amount of sodium azide is

    $n(\ce{NaN_3}) = \dfrac{330.0 \textrm{\ g}}{65.02 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 5.076 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ NaN_3}$

    From the reaction in (b), every $2\textrm{\ mol } \ce{NaN_3}$ produces $3\textrm{\ mol } \ce{N_2}$:

    $n(\ce{N_2}) = 5.076\textrm{\ mol NaN}_3 \times \dfrac{3\textrm{\ mol } \ce{N_2}}{2\textrm{\ mol NaN}_3} = 7.614\textrm{\ mol N}_2$

    Apply the ideal gas law with $T = 30\,{}^\circ\textrm{C} = 303.15\textrm{\ K}$ and $P = 150\textrm{\ kPa} = 150 \times 10^3\textrm{\ Pa}$:

    $V = \dfrac{nRT}{P} = \dfrac{7.614\textrm{\ mol} \times 8.314\textrm{\ J K}^{-1}\textrm{mol}^{-1} \times 303.15\textrm{\ K}}{150 \times 10^3\textrm{\ Pa}} = 0.128\textrm{\ m}^3$

4.  $n(\ce{HNO_2}) = 5.076\textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$\
    $V = 423\textrm{\ cm}^3 \: \checkmark$\
    claim incorrect; minimum volume $423\textrm{\ cm}^3 < 500\textrm{\ cm}^3 \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    From the equation, $2\textrm{\ mol } \ce{NaN_3}$ reacts with $2\textrm{\ mol } \ce{HNO_2},$ so the mole ratio is $1:1.$ The moles of $\ce{HNO_2}$ required equals $n(\ce{NaN_3}) = 5.076\textrm{\ mol}.$

    $V(\ce{HNO_2}) = \dfrac{n}{C} = \dfrac{5.076\textrm{\ mol}}{12.0\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}} = 0.423\textrm{\ dm}^3 = 423\textrm{\ cm}^3$

    Since $423\textrm{\ cm}^3 < 500\textrm{\ cm}^3,$ the engineer's claim overestimates the minimum volume. The claim is incorrect, only $423\textrm{\ cm}^3$ is needed.
    $\ $

5.  Method 1 $(\ce{NaNO_2} + \ce{N_2H_4}): 64.4\% \: \checkmark$\
    Method 2 $(\ce{Na} + \ce{N_2O} + \ce{NH_3}): 60.7\% \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Atom economy considers only the molar masses of reactants and the desired product:

    $\textrm{atom economy} = \dfrac{M_{\textrm{desired product}}}{\sum M_{\textrm{reactants}}} \times 100$

    For method 1 $(\ce{NaNO_2} + \ce{N_2H_4} \longrightarrow \ce{NaN_3} + 2\ce{\,H_2O})$:

    $\textrm{atom economy} = \dfrac{65.02}{69.00 + 32.05} \times 100 = \dfrac{65.02}{101.05} \times 100 = 64.4\%$

    For method 2 $(2\ce{\,Na} + \ce{N_2O} + \ce{NH_3} \longrightarrow \ce{NaN_3} + \ce{NaOH} + \ce{H_2})$:

    $\textrm{atom economy} = \dfrac{65.02}{2(22.99) + 44.01 + 17.04} \times 100 = \dfrac{65.02}{107.03} \times 100 = 60.7\%$

    Method 1 has the higher atom economy at $64.4\%$ compared to $60.7\%$ for method 2.
    $\ $
