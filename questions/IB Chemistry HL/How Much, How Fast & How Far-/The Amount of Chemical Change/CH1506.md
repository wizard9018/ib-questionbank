# CH1506

0.10 mol of $\ce{O2(g)}$ reacted completely with excess sulfur. What volume, in $\text{cm}^3,$ of sulfur trioxide would form at STP?

$\ce{2 S(s) + 3 O2(g) -> 2 SO3(g)}$

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\dfrac{0.10\times 3\times 22.7\times 1000}{2}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\dfrac{0.10\times 3\times 1000}{2\times 22.7}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\dfrac{0.10\times 2\times 22.7\times 1000}{3}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\dfrac{0.10\times 2\times 22.7}{3\times 1000}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

From the balanced equation, we see that 3 moles of $\ce{O2}$ produce 2 moles of $\ce{SO3}.$ We can use this stoichiometric relationship to find the moles of $\ce{SO3}$ produced from 0.10 mol of $\ce{O2}.$

First, calculate the moles of $\ce{SO3}$ produced from 0.10 mol of $\ce{O2}$

$n_\ce{SO3} = (0.01\text{\ mol }\ce{O2})\left(\dfrac{2\text{\ mol }\ce{SO3}}{3\text{\ mol }\text{O2}}\right)$

Converting the molar gas volume from $\text{dm}^3$ to $\text{cm}^3$ will lead to the expression below for the molar gas volume at STP in $\text{cm}^3$

$V_m = (22.7\text{\ dm}^3\text{\ mol}^{-1})\left(\dfrac{1000\text{\ cm}^3}{1\text{\ dm}^3}\right)$

To calculate the volume of $\ce{SO3}$ produced at STP, the moles of $\ce{SO3}$ must be multiplied by the molar volume of the gas at STP in $\text{cm}^3.$ Combining both the moles and volume expressions above will lead to

$V = n_\ce{SO3}V_m$

$V = \dfrac{(0.10)(2)(22.7)(1000)}{3}$
