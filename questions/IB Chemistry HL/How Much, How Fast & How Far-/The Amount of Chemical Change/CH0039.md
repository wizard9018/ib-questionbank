# CH0039

**Maximum mark: 4**

Methanol, $\ce{CH3OH}$, is a good fuel formed by the hydrogenation of carbon monoxide according to the following chemical equation:

$\ce{CO}\textrm{(g)} + 2 \ce{H_{2}}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CH3OH}\textrm{(l)}$

1. If $600 \textrm{\ g}$ of CO reacts with an excess of $\ce{H2}$, calculate the theoretical yield for methanol. [3 marks]

2. Determine the percentage yield if the reaction has formed $485 \textrm{\ g}$ of $\ce{CH3OH}$. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $n = \dfrac{600 \textrm{\ g CO}}{28.01 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} \textrm{\ }}  \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ mol }\ce{CH3OH}}{1 \textrm{\ mol CO}}= 21.4 \textrm{\ «mol }\ce{CH3OH}» \: \checkmark$\
$m = 21.4 \textrm{\ mol} \times 32.05 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = 686 \textrm{\ «g»} \: \checkmark$\
*Award both marks for correct final answer.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    To determine the theoretical yield, take the mass of the limiting reactant, CO, and find the amount in moles using the relationship $n = \dfrac{m}{M}$ where $n$ is the amount in moles, $m$ is the mass, and $M$ is the molar mass of CO.

    $n_\ce{CO} = \dfrac{m}{M} = \dfrac{600 \textrm{\ g CO}}{28.01 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 21.4 \textrm{\ mol CO}$

    In the balanced chemical equation,

    $\ce{CO}\textrm{(g)} + 2 \ce{\ H_{2}}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow \ce{CH3OH}\textrm{(l)}$

    the ratio of CO to $\ce{CH3OH}$ is 1:1, so the moles of methanol that will be formed is

    $n_\ce{CH3OH} = 21.4 \textrm{\ mol CO} \times \dfrac{1 \textrm{\ mol }\ce{CH3OH}}{1 \textrm{\ mol CO}}= 21.4 \textrm{\ mol }\ce{CH3OH} $

    The mass of methanol can be found by rearranging the relaionship used in the first step, $m = nM.$

    $m = n M = 21.4 \textrm{\ mol} \times 32.05 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = $ :answer[$686 \textrm{\ g\ }\ce{CH3OH}$]

2. $\dfrac{485 \textrm{\ g}}{686 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% = 70.7\% \: \checkmark$\
*Allow error carried forward from incorrect theoretical yield in (a)*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    To determine the percent yield, use the relationship

     \% yield = $\dfrac{\text{experimental yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \times 100 \%$

     where the experimental yield is the mass that was actually collected and the theoretical yield was calculated in part (a).

     \% yield $ = \dfrac{485 \textrm{\ g}}{686 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\%$ = :answer[70.7 %]
