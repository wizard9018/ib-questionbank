2.  The yield of compound **C** may be improved by performing the reaction using Dean-Stark apparatus, as shown in the diagram.

    ![CH1277A](images/CH1277A.png)

    In the Dean$-$Stark process, the reaction mixture is continuously distilled and collected in a burette.

    The condensed reactants and products separate into two layers.

    The lower layer consists mostly of water and can be removed by opening the burette tap.

    Compounds **A, B** and **C** form the upper layer, which overflows the top of the burette and returns to the reaction mixture.

    1.  State why the layer containing **A, B** and **C** lies above the layer containing water. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain why compound **C** does not mix with water based on the intermolecular interactions formed by the two liquids. [3 marks]

    3.  Explain why the removal of water from the reaction mixture increases the yield of compound **C**. [2 marks]

    4.  The physical properties of two acid catalysts are given in the table.

        | Acid catalyst | Boiling point / $\mathbf{^\circ C}$ | $\mathbf{pK_a}$ |

        Based on the information in the table and the details of the Dean$-$Stark process, suggest one reason why sulfuric acid is more useful than nitric acid as a catalyst for this reaction. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  **A:** ethanoic acid $\checkmark$\
    **B:** butan$-1-$ol $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    Compound **A** has a carboxylic acid group $(\ce{-COOH})$ and two carbon atoms, so it is ethanoic acid. Compound **B** has a hydroxyl group on the first carbon of a four$-$carbon chain, so it is butan$-1-$ol.

2.
    1.  the upper / organic layer is less dense **OR** the lower / water layer is more dense $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The two liquids are immiscible and settle by density, with the less dense liquid on top. Compounds **A, B** and **C** are less dense than water, so they form the upper layer.

    2.  water molecules attract one another by hydrogen bonds $\checkmark$\
        compound **C** offers only weaker dipole$-$dipole and dispersion forces $\checkmark$\
        mixing would replace strong water$-$water hydrogen bonds with weaker attractions, which is energetically unfavourable $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Water molecules hydrogen bond strongly, because the $\ce{O-H}$ bond is very polar and the small $\delta^+$ hydrogen has a high charge density. Ester **C** has polar $\ce{C=O}$ and $\ce{C-O}$ bonds but no $\ce{O-H},$ so it can only form the weaker dipole$-$dipole and dispersion forces, and much of the molecule is nonpolar. To mix, many strong water$-$water hydrogen bonds would have to break and be replaced by much weaker water$-$ester attractions; this is energetically unfavourable, so the liquids stay separate.

    3.  removing water shifts the equilibrium to the right / favours the forward reaction $\checkmark$\
        so the equilibrium amount of **C** increases $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The esterification is reversible, so **C** and water can react back to **A** and **B**. Removing water continuously means the equilibrium shifts to the right to replace it (Le Châtelier), converting more **A** and **B** and raising the yield of **C**. Equivalently, a lower $\ce{[H2O]}$ slows the reverse reaction, so the forward reaction runs further before the rates become equal.

    4.  nitric acid boils below the distillation temperature **AND** would be distilled out of the mixture with the water $\checkmark$ \
        **OR** \
        nitric acid has a higher $\textrm{p}K_\textrm{a}$ / is the weaker acid **AND** provides $\ce{H+}$ less readily $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Removing water requires distilling above $100\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$ Sulfuric acid boils at $337\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ and stays in the flask, whereas nitric acid boils at $83\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ and would distil out with the water, removing the catalyst. Sulfuric acid is also the stronger acid, with the more negative $\textrm{p}K_\textrm{a},$ so it supplies $\ce{H+}$ more readily.

3.
    1.  $K_c = \dfrac{[\ce{C}][\ce{H2O}]}{[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]} \: \checkmark$\
        $K_c = 27.4 \: \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        $K_c$ is the product of the product concentrations divided by the product of the reactant concentrations at equilibrium. The catalyst does not appear, because it changes the rate but not the position of equilibrium.

        $K_c = \dfrac{[\ce{C}][\ce{H2O}]}{[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]}$

        Plugging in the equilibrium concentration gives

        $K_c = \dfrac{7.24 \times 0.25}{0.33 \times 0.20} = 27.4 $

    2.  the forward reaction is exothermic / has a negative enthalpy change $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        Lowering the temperature favours the exothermic direction, which releases heat and opposes the cooling. Since $K_c$ increases on cooling, the favoured forward reaction must be exothermic.
