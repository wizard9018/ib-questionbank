# CH0622

The diameter of a cylindrical sample was measured using a vernier caliper at three different points along the sample. The diameters measured were: 13.50 mm, 13.51 mm, 13.50 mm. The true diameter of the sample is 12.14 mm. What is the likely type of error, and how can the error be reduced?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Random error; repeating the experiment 3 more times

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Random error; calibrating the vernier caliper

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Systematic error; repeating the experiment 3 more times

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Systematic error; calibrating the vernier caliper

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The readings are close to each other but are all consistently far from the true value, indicating that the error is likely to be systematic.

Systematic errors cannot be corrected by repeating the experiment, and instead, the vernier caliper must be calibrated to correct the zero error.
