# CH1086

The literature value of an enthalpy $\Delta H_x$ is $-73.8\pm 0.3\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}.$ After conducting an experiment, Anna got $-71.0\pm 0.2\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ as the experimental enthalpy value.

Which statements are correct about Anna's result?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | Anna's result shows that there were systematic errors throughout the experiment |
  | II. | Anna's result is within the range of the accepted literature value |
  | III. | Anna could improve the result by increasing the insulation in the experimental procedure |

- A. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

- D. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

We must compare Anna's result with the literature value.

Anna's result lies within $-71.2\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ and $-70.8\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. This is not included in the expected range of the literature value, within $-74.1\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ and $-73.5\,\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ (statement II is incorrect).

Anna's result is inaccurate. Inaccuracies provide evidence of systematic errors (statement I is correct).

Finally, determining the enthalpy of an exothermic reaction involves measuring the heat evolved by the system. Insulation is a must to avoid heat loss. It is a significant systematic error in most exothermic enthalpy determinations (so statement III is correct). The enthalpy determination will be less negative than expected if energy is released and heat is lost.

This question is categorized as hard as students must interpret and compare the result with scientific literature values. In addition, they are expected to assess the error present (systematic) and analyse the potential impact of a limitation (such as bad insulation).
