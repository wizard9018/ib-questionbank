# CH1088

A student determined the mass of a reactant to perform a chemical reaction. He measured the mass of the solid three times and calculated the average mass for the data processing. However, after the experiment, he realized that the reactant used was impure and the balance used was uncalibrated.

Which hypothesis is correct about the student's experiment?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
  | | |
  | :-- | :-- |
  | I. | The use of an impure reactant introduces a systematic error |
  | II. | The calculation of the average mass from the three trials reduces the precision in the mass determination |
  | III. | The error due to the uncalibrated balance is compensated by the repetitions in the mass measurement |

- A. $\hspace{1em}$I only

- B. $\hspace{1em}$II only

- C. $\hspace{1em}$III only

- D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Sources of error and their impact:

The use of impure reactants reduces the actual mass of reactant available. This has an impact on the amount of product produced. Not considering the presence of impurities is regarded as a systematic error that reduces the accuracy of the results. Statement I is correct.

Repeated trials allow the average calculation, reducing the random errors. This process improves the precision of the mass informed. Statement II is incorrect.

The uncalibrated balance would increase the source of error. This could cause an increase in the systematic error. However, systematic errors are not reduced by repeated trials. Statement III is incorrect.

This question is categorized as hard as different sources of error must be analysed and interpreted in detail.
