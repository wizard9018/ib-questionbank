# CH1090

A student ran a paper chromatography for black ink, as shown below.

![CH1090_Image1](images/CH1090_Image1.png)

A piece of $8.0\,\text{cm}\times 2.0\,\text{cm}$ paper chromatography was used. A glass jar with water as solvent was prepared. A lid was placed till the end once the paper chromatography was placed inside the jar. The student evaluated the result.

Which hypothesis would be valid?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The black ink is a mixture of only six pure pigments

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The separation of the components of black ink could be enhanced by adding more solvent to the glass jar

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The separation of the components of black ink could be enhanced by the use of a $15.0\,\text{cm}\times 2.0\,\text{cm}$ paper chromatography

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The separation of the components of black ink could be enhanced by removing the lid of the glass jar during the experiment

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The separation allows us to conclude that the initial ink was impure. However, it does not confirm the number of components of the mixture. Different spots can be observed after the solvent moves up the paper. However, we do not know if these spots are pure components or unseparated mixtures. Option A is not valid.

Adding more solvent would not modify the chromatography process. Adding too much solvent could affect the process if the solvent is above the starting line, as the black ink might get into the solvent rather than being carried up with the solvent throughout the paper chromatography. Option B is not valid.

Using a larger paper chromatography would increase the separation of the spots detected. Chromatography in a larger piece of paper continues, and different spots moving along with different rates are more separated at the end of the process. Option C is valid.

Finally, removing the lid can allow solvent evaporation if the process takes too long. However, this evaporation does not affect the performance of the separating technique. Option D is not valid.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must understand paper chromatography and evaluate the effect of changes over the experimental procedure.
