# CH0618

A student conducted a titration experiment. However, instead of rinsing his apparatus with distilled water, he rinses them with tap water only. What is likely the type of error?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$Systematic error

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$Random error

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$Both random and systematic errors

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$Neither random nor systematic error

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Rinsing titration apparatus ($\textit{e.g.},$ burette, pipette, conical flask) with tap water instead of distilled water introduces impurities including ions and dissolved gases that may react with or dilute the solutions used. This creates a consistent bias in the measured volumes or concentrations, the hallmark of a systematic error. Unlike random errors, which vary unpredictably, systematic errors affect results in a consistent direction (too high or too low).
