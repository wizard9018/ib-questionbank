# CH0809

The potential of a cell under non$-$standard conditions can be calculated using the Nernst equation:

$E = E^\ominus - \dfrac{RT}{nF}\ln Q$

For the graph below, which components of the equation represent the gradient and the $y-$intercept?

![CH0809](images/CH0809.png)

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
  | | $\qquad$ Gradient $\qquad$ | $\qquad$ y$-$intercept $\qquad$|
  | :-: | :-: | :-: |
  |A.$\hspace{1em}$| $\dfrac{RT}{nF}$  | $\ \newline E^\ominus\newline\  $ |
  |B.$\hspace{1em}$ | $-\dfrac{RT}{nF}$| $\ \newline  E^\ominus \newline\ $ |
  |C.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ \newline E^\ominus \newline\ $ | $- \dfrac{RT}{nF} $  |
  |D.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ \newline - E^\ominus\newline\ $ | $\dfrac{RT}{nF}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Rewriting the equation in the form of $y = mx + c$ gives:

$E = -\dfrac{RT}{nF}\ln Q + E^\ominus$

where $y = E$,

$x = \ln Q$,

$m = gradient =  -\dfrac{RT}{nF}$,

$c = y-intercept = E^\ominus$
