# CH2436

The accepted activation energy for a reaction is $43.1\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}.$ Three students determined this value experimentally.

| Student | $\mathbf{E_a \textbf{ / kJ mol}^{-1}}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| Sky | $42 \pm 1$ |
| Yasmine | $38 \pm 5$ |
| Sundeep | $45.00 \pm 0.05$ |

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Sky's result is the most precise.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Yasmine's result has the largest percentage error and the largest percentage uncertainty.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Sundeep's result is the least precise.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Sundeep's result is both the most precise and the most accurate.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Precision is reflected in the size of the percentage uncertainty, and accuracy is reflected in the percentage error (how close the measured value is to the accepted value).

| Student | $\mathbf{\% \textbf{ uncertainty}}$ | $\mathbf{\% \textbf{ error}}$ | Range |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Sky | $\dfrac{1}{42} \times 100 = 2.4\%$ | $\dfrac{\vert43.1 - 42\vert}{43.1} \times 100 = 2.6\%$ | $41 - 43$ |
| Yasmine | $\dfrac{5}{38} \times 100 = 13\%$ | $\dfrac{\vert43.1 - 38\vert}{43.1} \times 100 = 12\%$ | $33 - 43$ |
| Sundeep | $\dfrac{0.05}{45.00} \times 100 = 0.11\%$ | $\dfrac{\vert43.1 - 45.00\vert}{43.1} \times 100 = 4.4\%$ | $44.95 - 45.05$ |

Option A: Sky has a percentage uncertainty of 2.4%, which is larger than Sundeep's 0.11%. Sundeep's result is the most precise, not Sky's.

Option B: Yasmine has the largest percentage uncertainty (13%) and the largest percentage error (12%). This is the correct statement.

Option C: Sundeep has the smallest percentage uncertainty (0.11%), making Sundeep's result the most precise, not the least.

Option D: Sundeep is the most precise (smallest percentage uncertainty), but the accepted value (43.1) falls well outside the range 44.95 to 45.05. Sky has a smaller percentage error (2.6%) than Sundeep (4.4%), so Sky is the most accurate. Sundeep's result illustrates that high precision does not guarantee accuracy, which is a hallmark of systematic error.
