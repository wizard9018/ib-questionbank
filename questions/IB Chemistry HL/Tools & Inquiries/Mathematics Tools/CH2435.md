# CH2435

Standard solutions of a dye were used to construct a Beer$-$Lambert calibration curve. The line of best fit passes through the origin with the equation:

$\textrm{Absorbance} = 0.0400 \times c$

where $c$ is the concentration in $\textrm{mg dm}^{-3}.$

An unknown solution of the dye has absorbance $0.800 \pm 0.020.$ What is the concentration reported with its propagated uncertainty?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}20.00 \pm 0.01\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}20.00 \pm 0.02\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}20.0 \pm 0.05\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}20.0 \pm 0.5\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Rearranging the calibration equation gives $c = \dfrac{\textrm{Absorbance}}{0.0400}.$

$c = \dfrac{0.800}{0.0400} = 20.0\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}$

The concentration is reported to three significant figures, both the absorbance and proportionality constant have three significant figures.

Only the absorbance carries an uncertainty (the calibration slope is treated as exact), so the fractional uncertainty in the concentration equals the fractional uncertainty in the absorbance:

$\%\textrm{ unc.} = \dfrac{0.020}{0.800} \times 100 = 2.5\%$

$\Delta c = \dfrac{2.5}{100} \times 20.0\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3} = 0.50\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}$

The result is reported as $20.0 \pm 0.5\textrm{ mg dm}^{-3}.$ The absolute uncertainty is reported to the tenths place to match the precision of the concentration.
