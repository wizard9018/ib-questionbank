# CH2434

Consider the measuring cylinders containing water and mercury shown below.

![CH2434_cylinders](images/CH2434_cylinders.png)

What are the correct volume readings and uncertainties?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}V_\textrm{mercury} = 33.0 \pm 0.5\textrm{ cm}^3$ and $V_\textrm{water} = 25.4 \pm 0.5\textrm{ cm}^3$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}V_\textrm{mercury} = 32.4 \pm 0.5\textrm{ cm}^3$ and $V_\textrm{water} = 26.0 \pm 0.5\textrm{ cm}^3$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}V_\textrm{mercury} = 33 \pm 1\textrm{ cm}^3$ and $V_\textrm{water} = 25 \pm 1\textrm{ cm}^3$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}V_\textrm{mercury} = 32 \pm 1\textrm{ cm}^3$ and $V_\textrm{water} = 26 \pm 1\textrm{ cm}^3$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Mercury does not wet glass, so it forms a convex meniscus (curving upward at the centre). The volume is read at the top of the meniscus. Water wets glass and forms a concave meniscus (curving downward at the centre). The volume is read at the bottom of the meniscus.

For the mercury cylinder, the top of the convex meniscus sits at the 33 cm$^3$ mark, giving $V_\textrm{mercury} = 33.0\textrm{ cm}^3.$ For the water cylinder, the bottom of the concave meniscus sits between the 25 and 26 cm$^3$ marks, giving $V_\textrm{water} = 25.4\textrm{ cm}^3.$

The smallest division on each cylinder is 1 cm$^3.$ For an analogue scale, the uncertainty is estimated as half the smallest division, so the uncertainty is $\pm 0.5\textrm{ cm}^3.$
