# CH2427

The initial burette reading in a titration is $5.00 \pm 0.05\textrm{\ cm}^3$ and the final burette reading is $25.00 \pm 0.05\textrm{\ cm}^3.$ What is the percentage uncertainty of the delivered volume?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$0.25

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$0.10

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$0.50

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$1.0

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The delivered volume is the difference between the final and initial burette readings:

$V = 25.00\textrm{\ cm}^3 - 5.00\textrm{\ cm}^3 = 20.00\textrm{\ cm}^3$

When subtracting two measurements, the absolute uncertainties are added. Each burette reading carries an uncertainty of $\pm 0.05\textrm{\ cm}^3,$ so the uncertainty in the delivered volume is:

$\Delta V = 0.05\textrm{\ cm}^3 + 0.05\textrm{\ cm}^3 = 0.10\textrm{\ cm}^3$

The percentage uncertainty is then:

$\%\textrm{\ uncertainty} = \dfrac{0.10\textrm{\ cm}^3}{20.00\textrm{\ cm}^3} \times 100 = 0.50\%$

A common error is to use only one reading's uncertainty, which gives $\dfrac{0.05}{20.00} \times 100 = 0.25\%.$ A burette delivery always involves two readings (initial and final), so both uncertainties must be included.

It is a convention in IB Chemistry to report the percent uncertainty to two significant figures if it is less than 2%, and to one significant figure otherwise.
