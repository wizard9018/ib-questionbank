# CH2415

Three samples of urea were prepared in the laboratory. The mass and melting point of each sample were recorded.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\mathbf{Sample}$ | $\mathbf{Mass\,/\,g\;(\pm 0.001)}$ | $\mathbf{Melting\;point\,/\,{}^\circ C\;(\pm 1)}$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| A | $1.212$ | $120$–$125$ |
| B | $1.090$ | $131$–$132$ |
| C | $1.050$ | $127$–$130$ |

The literature melting point of urea is $133\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Sample A has the greatest mass and is the purest sample.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Sample A is the least pure sample.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The data show that a greater sample mass always corresponds to lower purity.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The melting point data allow the percentage purity of each sample to be calculated.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A pure substance melts sharply at a temperature that matches its literature value. Impurities lower the melting point and broaden the melting range. The closer and sharper the melting point is to the literature value, the purer the sample.

Sample B melts over a narrow $1\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ range just below the literature value of $133\,{}^\circ\textrm{C},$ so it is the purest. Sample C melts over $3\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ and is further from the literature value, making it moderately impure. Sample A melts over $5\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ at temperatures well below the literature value, making it the least pure. Statement B is correct.

Although Sample A has the greatest mass, mass alone says nothing about purity. Statement A is incorrect because it conflates mass with purity.

The data do show that A (greatest mass) is the least pure and B is more pure with a smaller mass, but C has the smallest mass yet is less pure than B. There is no consistent trend between mass and purity in these data. Statement C is incorrect.

Melting point analysis is qualitative: it reveals whether a sample is pure or impure but cannot determine the percentage of impurity. A quantitative technique, such as titration or spectroscopy with a calibration curve, would be needed for that. Statement D is incorrect.
