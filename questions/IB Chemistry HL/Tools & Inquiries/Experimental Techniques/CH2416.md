# CH2416

The absorbance spectrum of a solution of compound X is shown.

![CH2416_spec-1](images/CH2416_spec-1.png)

A calibration curve was constructed at $\lambda = 600\textrm{\ nm}$ using standard solutions of $\ce{X}\textrm{(aq)}.$ An unknown sample of $\ce{X}\textrm{(aq)}$ gave an absorbance of $0.150$ at $600\textrm{\ nm}.$

![CH2416_cal-1](images/CH2416_cal-1.png)

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$The concentration of the unknown sample is approximately $0.30\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The concentration of the unknown sample is approximately $0.50\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$A more accurate result would be obtained by constructing the calibration curve at $460\textrm{\ nm}.$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The solution of X appears orange.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Absorbance is proportional to concentration (Beer$-$Lambert relationship). A calibration curve plots the absorbance of standard solutions of known concentration against concentration at a fixed wavelength. The best$-$fit line then allows the concentration of an unknown sample to be read from its measured absorbance.

![CH2416_calms-1](images/CH2416_calms-1.png)

From the calibration curve, an absorbance of $0.150$ corresponds to a concentration of approximately $0.30\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$ Using the equation of the best$-$fit line:

$c = \dfrac{0.150 - 0.060}{0.300} = 0.30\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$

The calibration curve should be constructed at $\lambda_{\max},$ the wavelength of maximum absorbance, because this is where small changes in concentration produce the largest changes in absorbance. From the spectrum, $\lambda_{\max} \approx 600\textrm{\ nm},$ which is the wavelength that was used. At $460\textrm{\ nm}$ the absorbance is much lower, giving a less sensitive calibration.

![CH2416colorwheel](images/CH2416colorwheel.png)

The solution absorbs light most strongly at $600\textrm{\ nm},$ which is in the orange region of the visible spectrum. A solution that absorbs orange light transmits the complementary colour and appears blue.
