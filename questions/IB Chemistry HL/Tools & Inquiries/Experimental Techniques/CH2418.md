# CH2418

A student heats a sample of hydrated sodium tetraborate, $\ce{Na2B4O7}\cdot x\ce{H2O},$ in a crucible to determine the value of $x.$ The results are shown below.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

|  | Mass / g $\pm$ 0.0001 |
|:--|:-:|
| Crucible + lid | 15.091 |
| Crucible + lid + salt (before heating) | 18.905 |
| Crucible + lid + salt (after 1st heating) | 17.401 |
| Crucible + lid + salt (after 2nd heating) | 17.104 |
| Crucible + lid + salt (after 3rd heating) | 17.104 |

$M_r(\ce{H2O}) = 18.02 \\
M_r(\ce{Na2B4O7}) = 201.22$

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$The third heating was unnecessary to guarantee an accurate result.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The water of hydration, $x,$ can be deduced accurately without a second and a third heating.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The heating must be done with the lid covering the crucible completely.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}x = \dfrac{\hspace{0.5em}\dfrac{18.905 - 17.104}{18.02}\hspace{0.5em}}{\dfrac{17.104 - 15.091}{201.22}}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The water of hydration is found by comparing the moles of water lost on heating to the moles of anhydrous salt remaining. Using the constant mass (17.104 g) reached after the second and third heatings, find the mass of water released and the mass of the anhydrous salt:

$m(\ce{H2O}) = 18.905\textrm{\ g} - 17.104\textrm{\ g} = 1.801\textrm{\ g} \ce{\ H2O}$

$m(\ce{Na2B4O7}) = 17.104\textrm{\ g} - 15.091\textrm{\ g} = 2.013\textrm{\ g} \ce{\ Na2B4O7}$

The moles of each compound can be found using the relationship $n = \dfrac{m}{M_r}:$

$x = \dfrac{n(\ce{H2O})}{n(\ce{Na2B4O7})} = \dfrac{\hspace{0.5em}\dfrac{1.801 \textrm{\ g} \ce{\ H2O}}{18.02 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}}\hspace{0.5em}}{\dfrac{2.013 \textrm{\ g} \ce{\ Na2B4O7}}{201.22 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}}} $

$x =  \dfrac{0.09994 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ H2O}}{0.01000 \textrm{\ mol}\ce{\ Na2B4O7}} = 10$

The salt is borax, $\ce{Na2B4O7}\cdot 10\ce{H2O}.$

Heating to constant mass means repeating the heat$-$cool$-$weigh cycle until two consecutive masses agree. The first heating gave 17.401 g and the second gave 17.104 g, so water still remained after the first cycle. The third heating confirmed the mass at 17.104 g, establishing constant mass. Without the third heating there is no verification that all the crystallisation water has been removed.

The crucible lid must be held slightly ajar during heating so that water vapour can escape. A completely sealed lid would trap the steam and prevent dehydration.
