# CH0853

A dye is contaminated with another substance. The chromatograms below show the dye before recrystallization (1) and after recrystallization (2).

Which chromatogram provides evidence that recrystallization has successfully removed the contaminant?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
In chromatography, the presence of more than one spot indicates that the sample contains more than one component. A successful recrystallization should therefore result in only a single spot remaining, at the same position as the pure dye. One chromatogram shows this clearly: before recrystallization two spots are present, but afterwards only one spot remains. This is consistent with the removal of the contaminant and the retention of the dye.

By contrast, chromatograms that still display multiple spots after recrystallization show that impurities remain in the sample. A result with no visible spots suggests that the dye itself has been lost, while a chromatogram that continues to display both dye and contaminant indicates that separation was unsuccessful.

This question is of medium difficulty because students must connect the theoretical purpose of recrystallization with the experimental evidence provided by chromatography.
