## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

To set up a functioning electrolytic cell to electroplate silver on a copper spoon, a beaker is filled with enough silver salt solution, like silver nitrate solution, to cover the copper spoon. The silver electrode is connected to the battery's positive terminal, and the copper spoon is connected to the battery's negative terminal.

At the anode (silver electrode), the silver metal gives up an electron to the circuit, becoming an $\ce{Ag+}$ ion in solution; $\ce{Ag(s) -> Ag+(aq) + e-}$

At the cathode (copper spoon): The $\ce{Ag+}$ ion in solution gains an electron, becoming silver metal. This silver metal then plates onto the copper spoon; $\ce{Ag+(aq) + e- -> Ag(s)}$

Overall, silver metal is transferred from the anode to the cathode, where it plates onto the copper spoon. The electrolyte, a silver nitrate $\ce{(AgNO3)}$ solution, provides the $\ce{Ag+}$ ions needed for the plating reaction to occur.
