# CH2417

A student investigates the enthalpy change when a salt dissolves in water, using a coffee$-$cup calorimeter. The salt is added at $t = 20 \textrm{\ s}$ and the temperature of the solution is recorded. The results are plotted below to determine the temperature change, minimising the effect of heat exchange with the surroundings.

![CH2417questions](images/CH2417questions.png)

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$The dissolution is exothermic, and $\Delta T \approx 5.1\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The dissolution is endothermic, and $\Delta T \approx 5.1\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The dissolution is exothermic, and $\Delta T \approx 6.2\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The dissolution is endothermic, and $\Delta T \approx 6.2\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}.$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
After the salt is added at $t = 20\textrm{\ s}$, the temperature of the solution drops. The dissolution absorbs energy from the water, confirming that the process is endothermic.

The lowest temperature recorded on the graph is approximately $12.9\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$ (at $t \approx 90\textrm{\ s}$), which gives

$\Delta T = 18.0 - 12.9 = 5.1\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

However, this underestimates the true temperature change because heat flows into the solution from the surroundings while the salt is still dissolving.

The extrapolation technique corrects for this: a best$-$fit line is drawn through the post-minimum data (the gradual warming region) and extended back to $t = 20\textrm{\ s}$, the moment of mixing. This gives an extrapolated temperature of approximately $11.8\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$, so:

$\Delta T = 18.0 - 11.8 = 6.2\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}$

![CH2417_ms-1](images/CH2417_ms-1.png)

Mistaking the change in temperature is the most common error: correctly identifying the process as endothermic but reading $\Delta T$ from the graph minimum rather than extrapolating. The extrapolation is essential in calorimetry because heat exchange with the surroundings begins immediately, and the minimum on the graph does not represent the temperature that would have been reached if the dissolution were instantaneous.
