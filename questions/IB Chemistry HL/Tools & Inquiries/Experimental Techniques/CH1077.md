# CH1077

**Maximum mark: 3**

Two setups, **A** and **B**, were used to determine the molar enthalpy of $20.0\,\text{mL}$ ethanol (in the burner) using $50.0\,\text{mL}$ of water (in the iron can).

![CH1077_Image1](images/CH1077_Image1.png)

1.  Identify the technique used. [1 mark]

2.  When all conditions are controlled, which setup is expected to yield values closer to accuracy and why? [1 mark]

3.  Suggest one other piece of equipment required to conduct this experiment. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Calorimetry $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    Calorimetry is a technique that enables the measurement of temperature change for a diverse range of chemical reactions. This information makes it possible to calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction, which measures the amount of heat that is either released or absorbed during the reaction. The heat released from a combustion reaction can warm up a substance such as water, which has a specific heat capacity that is known.

2.  Setup B **AND** because it keeps the volume of water constant/does not allow water evaporation/reduces heat loss. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    A covered iron can is a closed system, i.e., it allows for the movement of thermal energy but limits the movement of mass (water). When the volume of water is controlled in the experiment (i.e., the water is not lost due to evaporation), the systematic error is reduced, and setup is expected to yield values closer to accuracy.

3.  Thermometer/temperature probe $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    In calorimetry, a change in the temperature of water is equated to the amount of energy absorbed by it by the equation;

    $Q = m\ c\ \Delta T$

    where:
    -   $Q =$ energy absorbed
    -   $m =$ mass of water
    -   $c = $ specific heat capacity of water
    -   $\Delta T =$ cchange in the water temperature

    Thus, a thermometer is essential for this experiment.
