# CH2419

A mixture of ethanol, ethanal and ethanoic acid is separated by fractional distillation. The mixture is heated, the vapour passes through a fractionating column, and the condensed liquid is collected in a receiving flask.

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Ethanoic acid has the highest boiling point, is the most volatile, and is the first liquid collected.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Ethanal has the highest boiling point, is the most volatile, and is the first liquid collected.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Ethanal has the lowest boiling point, is the most volatile, and is the first liquid collected.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Ethanal has the highest boiling point, is the least volatile, and is the last liquid collected.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Fractional distillation separates miscible liquids by exploiting differences in boiling point. The fractionating column provides a temperature gradient where repeated vaporisation and condensation cycles enrich the vapour in the most volatile component. The liquid with the weakest intermolecular forces has the lowest boiling point, vaporises most easily, and is therefore the most volatile. It is the first component to reach the top of the column and be collected.

Ethanal $(\ce{CH3CHO})$ is an aldehyde. The carbonyl group is polar, so ethanal experiences dipole$-$dipole forces, but there is no $-$OH group, so it cannot act as a hydrogen$-$bond donor. Ethanol $(\ce{C2H5OH})$ and ethanoic acid $(\ce{CH3COOH})$ both contain an $-$OH group and form hydrogen bonds, giving them significantly higher boiling points. Ethanoic acid can also dimerise through two simultaneous hydrogen bonds, making it the least volatile of the three.

The boiling points reflect this reasoning: ethanal $(20\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}),$ ethanol $(78\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}),$ ethanoic acid $(118\,{}^\circ\textrm{C}).$ Ethanal vaporises first, is the most volatile component, and is the first liquid collected in the receiving flask.

A common error is to confuse boiling point with volatility. A high boiling point means strong intermolecular forces and low volatility, not high. The substance that distils first always has the lowest boiling point.
