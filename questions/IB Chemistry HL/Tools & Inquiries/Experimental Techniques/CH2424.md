# CH2424

A conductimetric titration is used to determine the unknown concentration of $100\textrm{\ cm}^3$ of an aqueous acid. The titrant is $0.500\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ $\ce{NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}.$ The graph below shows the conductivity of the solution as a function of the volume of $\ce{NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}$ added.

![CH2424_conductivity-1](images/CH2424_conductivity-1.png)

Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$The high initial conductivity is only due to the high concentration of mobile hydrogen ions.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The volume at the equivalence point is $17\textrm{\ cm}^3,$ and the analyte is a weak acid.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The volume at the equivalence point is $40\textrm{\ cm}^3,$ and the analyte is a strong acid.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The conductivity is proportional to the concentration of mobile ions in the solution, and the analyte is a strong acid.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Conductivity measures the ability of a solution to carry an electric current, which depends on the total concentration and mobility of the ions present. In a conductimetric titration, the conductivity is monitored as the titrant is added, and the equivalence point is identified as the minimum of the curve.

The steep initial decrease is characteristic of a strong acid. A strong acid is fully dissociated, so the solution begins with a high concentration of $\ce{H+}$ ions, which have an exceptionally high molar conductivity. The conjugate base anion also contributes to the initial high conductivity.

As $\ce{NaOH}$ is added, the neutralisation reaction replaces highly mobile $\ce{H+}$ ions with less mobile $\ce{Na+}$ ions, causing the conductivity to fall. At the equivalence point (approximately $17\textrm{\ cm}^3$), the conductivity reaches a minimum because virtually all the $\ce{H+}$ has been consumed. Beyond the equivalence point, excess $\ce{NaOH}$ introduces additional $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{OH-}$ ions, and the conductivity rises again.

A weak acid would produce a very different curve. Because a weak acid is only partially dissociated, its initial conductivity is much lower and the pre$-$equivalence decrease is far less steep.
