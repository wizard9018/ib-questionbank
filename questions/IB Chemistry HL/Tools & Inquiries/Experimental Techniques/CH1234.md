# CH1234

A bomb calorimeter is described below.

![CH1234](images/CH1234.png)

Dieticians can determine the energy values of food in $\text{J g}^{-1},$ with this instrument.

$120\,\text{g}$ of sample X were burnt so that $200\,\text{cm}^3$ of water increased its temperature by $2.00\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$ Which statement below is correct?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$Water keeps the bomb calorimeter at a constant temperature, and the energy value of X is $13.9\,\text{J g}^{-1}$

- B. $\hspace{1em}$Water keeps the bomb calorimeter at a constant temperature, and the energy value of X is $5.02\,\text{J g}^{-1}$

- C. $\hspace{1em}$The air jacket acts as an insulator, and the energy value of X is $13.9\,\text{J g}^{-1}$

- D. $\hspace{1em}$The air jacket acts as an insulator, and the energy value of X is $5.02\,\text{J g}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Diagram given for a bomb calorimeter: as the sample is burnt, heat is evolved and absorbed by water. The water absorbs heat only from the burnt sample as an air jacket acts an insulator, preventing the heat exchange with the surroundings (it acts as an insulator).

The energy value for X can be determined by the heat absorbed by water divided by the mass of X burnt, as shown below. We can assume that water has a density of $1\,\text{g cm}^{-3},$ so $200\,\text{cm}^3$ is equivalent to $200\,\text{g}.$

$\text{Energy value of X} = \dfrac{q}{mass\ burnt} = \dfrac{(200\,\text{g})(4.18\,\text{J g}^{-1}\,{}^\circ\text{C}^{-1})(2.00\,{}^\circ\text{C})}{120\,\text{g}} = 13.9\\text{J g}^{-1}$

This question is categorized as hard as students must interpret the experimental device described in the image and the purpose of its components.
