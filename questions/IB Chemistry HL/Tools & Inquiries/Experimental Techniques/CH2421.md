# CH2421

A titration is performed to determine the unknown concentration of a $10.0\textrm{\ cm}^3$ sample of $\ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)}$ using a $0.100\textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ standard $\ce{KOH}\textrm{(aq)}$ solution. Phenolphthalein is used as the indicator.

Which combination is correct for the volume$-$measuring equipment required?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\hspace{1.em}$||| KOH(aq) | HCl(aq) | Phenolphthalein |
|:-: |:-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
|| A. | | Burette | Burette | Pipette |
|| B. | | Burette | Pipette | Dropper |
|| C. | | Pipette | Burette | Dropper |
|| D. | | Pipette | Pipette | Pipette |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
In a titration, the analyte (the solution of unknown concentration) is measured precisely with a volumetric pipette and transferred to a conical flask. The titrant (the standard solution of known concentration) is placed in a burette so that it can be added gradually, in small increments, until the equivalence point is reached. The indicator is added as a few drops from a dropper, since its purpose is simply to signal the endpoint and its exact volume does not need to be controlled.

Here, $\ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)}$ is the analyte, so $10.0\textrm{\ cm}^3$ is measured with a volumetric pipette. $\ce{KOH}\textrm{(aq)}$ is the standard solution (titrant) and is dispensed from the burette. Phenolphthalein is added with a dropper.

Swapping the burette and pipette would mean the analyte is added gradually and the titrant is fixed, which reverses the logic of the titration. The titrant must be the solution added incrementally so that the exact volume at the equivalence point can be read from the burette scale.
