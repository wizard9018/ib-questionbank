# CH2091

A chemist wants to purify a solid organic compound contaminated with small amounts of a soluble impurity. Which technique is most appropriate?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Evaporation

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Distillation
-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Recrystallization
-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Filtration

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\
Recrystallization is the most effective technique for purifying a solid organic compound when small amounts of a soluble impurity are present. In this process, the impure solid is dissolved in a hot solvent, and as the solution cools, the desired compound crystallizes out, leaving impurities behind in the solution. The purified crystals are then collected by filtration. This method works best when the desired compound is highly soluble in the solvent at high temperatures but only sparingly soluble at low temperatures, while the impurity remains dissolved in the solvent even at lower temperatures.

Other techniques are less suitable in this case. Filtration would only be effective if the impurity were insoluble, which is not the case here. Evaporation would remove the solvent but leave behind both the solid and its impurities. Distillation is used to separate liquids based on boiling points and is not suitable for purifying a solid. Since recrystallization allows selective separation based on solubility differences, it is the best choice for purifying a solid contaminated with small amounts of a soluble impurity.
