# CH1275

**Maximum mark: 13**

A student measures the quantity of $\ce{FeSO4}$ in a tablet by titration with a $0.01\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ acidic solution of $\ce{KMnO4},$ which is purple in color.

The tablet is dissolved in $20\,\text{cm}^3$ of water, and the titration is performed immediately to minimize the error in the result.

The equation for the reaction between $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ and $\ce{MnO4-}$ ions is shown.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

$\ce{5 Fe^2+(aq) + MnO4-(aq) + 8 H+(aq) -> 5 Fe^3+(aq) + Mn^2+(aq) + 4 H2O(l)}$

1.  State the oxidation state of manganese before and after the reaction takes place. [1 mark]

2.  The $\ce{Fe^3+}$ ion is produced by many other oxidation reactions, including the corrosion of iron and steel.  Based on this information, explain why the result of the titration changes if the solution of $\ce{FeSO4}$ is not prepared immediately before the titration experiment. [2 marks]

3.  The following diagram shows the experimental setup **near the endpoint** of the titration experiment.

    ![CH1275C](images/CH1275C.png)

    1.  State how the endpoint of the titration would be detected. [1 mark]

    2.  Based on the information in the diagram, state why the precision of the titer is likely to be poor. [1 mark]

    3.  In a repeat experiment, the student doubles the volume of the analyte solution but keeps the concentration of $\ce{FeSO4}$ the same. State how the change to the analyte solution affects the volume of the titer, giving a reason for your answer. [2 marks]

    4.  State one source of random error and one source of systematic error in the experiment. [2 marks]

4.  The student analyzes four tablets by titration and calculates their iron contents. The results of the experiments are given in the table.

    | Tablet | Iron content / $\mathbf{\pm 0.2\, mg}$ |
    | :-: | :-: |
    | 1 | 56.0 |
    | 2 | 51.7 |
    | 3 | 50.5 |
    | 4 | 50.9 |

    The packaging of the $\ce{FeSO4}$ tablets states that the mean iron content of 100 tablets is $50.0\,\text{mg}.$

    1.  Calculate the percentage error between the mean iron content of the tablets and the expected value. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  +7 in $\ce{MnO4-}$ becomes +2 in $\ce{Mn^2+}\,\checkmark$

    *Do not accept 7+ and 2+*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
     In $\ce{MnO4-},$ the oxidation state of O is $-2,$ and the total charge of the $\ce{MnO4-}$ ion is $1-.$ Thus, the oxidation state of Mn must be $-1 - 4(-2) = +7.$ The $\ce{Mn^2+}$ ion contains only Mn, so the oxidation state of Mn in this ion is equal to the charge: +2. The oxidation state of Mn decreases by 5 during the reaction, so it is acting as an oxidizing agent.

2.  $\ce{Fe^2+}$ would react with oxygen to form $\ce{Fe^3+}$ $\checkmark$\
    (measured) concentration of $\ce{FeSO4}$ would be lower $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
    In aqueous solution, $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions may be oxidized to $\ce{Fe^3+}$ ions.

    The oxidizing agent is molecular oxygen $(\ce{O2})$ in the air. This reaction is similar to the corrosion (rusting) of metallic iron by water and oxygen.

    If some of the $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions are converted to $\ce{Fe^3+}$ ions before the titration, there will be fewer $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions remaining to react with $\ce{KMnO4},$ and a smaller titer will therefore be measured. This error will cause the iron content of the analyte to be underestimated.

3.
    1.  solution becomes purple $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The solution of $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions would be a very pale green initially, but would change color during the titration due to the formation of yellow $\ce{Fe^3+}$ and pale pink $\ce{Mn^2+}$ ions.

        While any $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions remained, the purple color of the $\ce{MnO4-}$ ions would not be observed, as these ions would react to form $\ce{Mn^2+}$ ions.

        At the endpoint of the reaction, however, the $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions would have fully reacted, so any additional $\ce{MnO4-}$ ions would persist in solution. The endpoint would therefore be indicated by the persistent purple color of the excess titrant.

    2.  measured volume is small **AND**  measurement errors represents a large percentage error $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        In this experiment, only a small volume of the titrant solution has been added to the analyte, as the level of the solution remaining in the burette is close to the highest marking. The titer cannot be measured with a precision greater than half the spacing of the burette markings.

        Although the absolute error in the measurement is constant, the percentage error depends on the size of the measurement. For example, a titer of $10.00\pm 0.05\,\text{cm}^3$ has a percentage error

        $\left(\dfrac{0.05}{10}\right)\times 100\% = 0.5\%$

        while a titre of $1.00\pm 0.05\,\text{cm}^3$ has a percentage error of

        $\left(\dfrac{0.05}{1}\right)\times 100\% = 5 \%.$

    3.  analyte contains more / twice as many moles of $\ce{FeSO4}\,\checkmark$\
        volume of $\ce{KMnO4}$ reacted is doubled $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The concentration of a solution is given by the number of moles divided by the volume. If the volume of the $\ce{FeSO4}$ solution is doubled but the concentration remains the same, the solution must contain twice as many moles of $\ce{FeSO4}.$

        If the number of moles of $\ce{FeSO4}$ in the solution is doubled, twice as many moles of $\ce{KMnO4}$ must react to reach the endpoint of the titration. The concentration of the titrant solution is not changed, so the volume of the titer must also double.

    4.   *random error:* any **one** of:
            $\mbox{}\\[-10pt]$

            -   uncertainty in reading the burette scale / meniscus $\checkmark$
            -   judging the first permanent colour change at the end point $\checkmark$
            -   the size of the final drop added $\checkmark$

            *systematic error:* any **one** of:
            $\mbox{}\\[-10pt]$

            -   overshooting the end point, so the titre is consistently too high $\checkmark$
            -   the burette not rinsed with $\ce{KMnO4},$ so residual water dilutes it and the titre is too high $\checkmark$
            -   the $\ce{KMnO4}$ not exactly $0.01 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ / not freshly standardised $\checkmark$
            -   $\ce{Fe^2+}$ oxidised by air before the titration, so the titre is too low $\checkmark$

            $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
            A random error scatters repeated titres about the mean and is reduced by averaging: the burette reading and the judgement of the exact end$-$point shade both vary from trial to trial. A systematic error shifts every titre in the same direction and is not reduced by repeating: overshooting the end point, a titrant that is diluted or not exactly standardised, and pre$-$titration oxidation of $\ce{Fe^2+}$ each bias the result one way.

4.
    1.  $\text{mean} = 52.3\,\text{mg}\,\checkmark$ \
        $\text{percent error} =  4.6\%\,\checkmark$

        *Accept mean of 51.0 mg and 2.0% error if outlier is explicitly excluded.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        The mean iron content is calculated by adding the values and dividing by the number of measurements.

        $\text{mean} = \dfrac{56.0 + 51.7 + 50.5 + 50.9}{4} = 52.3\,\text{mg}$

        The measured iron content differs from the expected value. Calculating percent errors allows the effects of different errors to be compared, even if they affect quantities with different units.

        $\text{percent error} = \dfrac{|50.0 - 52.3|}{50.0}\times 100\% = 4.6\%$

    2.  any **two** of the following:
        $\mbox{}\\[-10pt]$

        -   $56.0\,\text{mg}$ measurement is an outlier **AND** increases the calculated error / mean $\checkmark$
        -    percent difference (between the mean and expected value) is similar to / less than the largest difference between measurements (may not be significant) $\checkmark$
        -   more repeat measurements must be taken to determine whether the percentage difference is significant $\checkmark$
        -   sample size is small **AND**  mean may not be representative of the true mean value $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\
        An average value is not reliable or meaningful if it is calculated from a sample that is unrepresentative of the whole population. In this question, the number of analyzed tablets is much smaller than the number of tablets in the packet. Although all of the tablets have iron contents higher than 50.0 mg, it is possible that other tablets in the packet have lower iron contents and that the average is close to the expected value. Four tablets with relatively high iron contents could easily have been selected by chance.

        Furthermore, one of the tablets in the sample has a much higher iron content than the others. This tablet can be considered an outlier. If this value is excluded, the average is much closer to the expected value.

        The maximum difference in iron contents, $(56.0 - 50.5) = 5.5\,\text{mg},$ is larger than the difference between the calculated mean and the expected value. Since such large variations are possible, there is a reasonable probability that the average iron content of a larger sample would be closer to the expected value. A larger sample would need to be measured to ensure a reliable result.
