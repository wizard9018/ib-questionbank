# CH0927

Sodium hydroxide reacts with both 1$-$chloropentane and 1$-$bromopentane in ethanol.

Which halogenoalkane reacts at the greatest rate?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}1-$Chloropentane, because the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is weaker than the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond

-   B.$\hspace{1em}1-$Bromopentane, because the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is stronger than the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond

-   C.$\hspace{1em}1-$Chloropentane, because the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is more polar than the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond

-   D.$\hspace{1em}1-$Bromopentane, because the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is less polar than the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The $\ce{C-Cl}$ group reacts more slowly than the $\ce{C-Br}$ group.

The $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond is more polar than the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond, as the Cl atom is more electronegative. However, this effect causes the C atom of the $\ce{C-Cl}$ group to have a larger $\delta^+$ charge, improving its ability to act as an electrophile.

The $\ce{C-Br}$ bond reacts more rapidly because the Br atom is larger, making the bond longer, weaker and easier to break. This effect outweighs the polarity effect, making the bromide ion the better leaving group.
