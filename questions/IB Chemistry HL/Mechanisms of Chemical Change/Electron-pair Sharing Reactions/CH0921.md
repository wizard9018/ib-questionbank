# CH0921

A three-step synthetic route is shown.

![CH0921_Image1](images/CH0921_Image1.png)

Which of the following best describes the **major** product of this synthetic route?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
  | | Number of double bonds | Number of chiral centres |
  | :-: | :-: | :-: |
  | A. | 1 | 0 |
  | B. | 2 | 0 |
  | C. | 1 | 1 |
  | D. | 2 | 1 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\
The first step is an electrophilic addition:

![CH0921step1](images/CH0921step1.png)

The $\ce{C=C}$ bond is broken and a $\ce{C-H}$ and $\ce{C-Br}$ bond are formed. The most stable intermediate for this reaction is a tertiary carbocation, formed when the $\ce{C-H}$ bond forms on the secondary carbon atom.  This follows Markovnikov's rule, the H atom adds to the carbon with more hydrogens.

In the second step, the bromo group is replaced by a hydroxy group in a nucleophilic substitution $(S_\ce{N}1)$.

![CH0921step2](images/CH0921step2.png)

In the third step, the secondary hydroxyl group is oxidized to a $\ce{C=O}$ (ketone) group.

![CH0921step3](images/CH0921step3.png)

The alcohol in the reactant is chiral, but the ketone group is trigonal planar so cannot be chiral. The tertiary hydroxyl group cannot be oxidized.

The final product contains no chiral atoms and only one double bond.
