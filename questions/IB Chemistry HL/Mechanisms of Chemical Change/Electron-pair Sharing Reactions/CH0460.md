# CH0460

**Maximum mark: 6**

$\ce{H2O}$ is an example of an amphiprotic species meaning it can act as either an acid or base.

1.  

    1.  Draw the Lewis structure of $\ce{H2O}$. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain why $\ce{H2O}$ can act as both a Brønsted$-$Lowry acid and Brønsted$-$Lowry base. [1 mark]

    3.  Explain why $\ce{H2O}$ can act as a Lewis base. [1 mark]

2.  

    1.  State an example of an acid-base reaction where $\ce{H2O}$ acts as a Brønsted$-$Lowry acid and another where $\ce{H2O}$ acts as a Brønsted$-$Lowry base. [2 marks]

    2.  State an example of a Lewis acid$-$base reaction that is not a Brønsted$-$Lowry acid$-$base reaction where $\ce{H2O}$ acts as a Lewis base. [1 mark]

    2.  $\ce{H2O}$ can both accept and donate a proton, hence can act as both a Brønsted-Lowry acid or a Brønsted-Lowry base. $\checkmark$

    3.  $\ce{H2O}$ can donate lone pairs, making it a Lewis base. $\checkmark$

    2.  $\ce{Al^3+ (aq) + 6H2O (l) <=> [Al(H2O)6]^3+ (aq)}$ $\checkmark$ *Other examples are possible*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        Ligands forming dative covalent bonds with a central metal ion is an example of a Lewis acid-base reaction that is not a Brønsted-Lowry acid-base reaction.
