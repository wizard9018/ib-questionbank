$\ce{C3H6 + IBr -> C3H6IBr}$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        

        Propene is an unsymmetrical alkene, so its electrophilic addition reactions with $\ce{I-Br}$ can lead to the formation of two different products. When the double bond in propene reacts with the $\ce{I-Br}$ molecule, it first attacks the partially positive iodine, leading to the formation of a carbocation intermediate. This carbocation can form on either carbon 1 or carbon 2 of the original double bond.

        If the carbocation forms on carbon 2, a secondary carbocation is created. This secondary carbocation is more stable due to the positive inductive effect, where the adjacent alkyl groups donate electron density, stabilizing the charge. As a result, the major product of the reaction is 2$\text{--}$bromo$\text{--}$1$\text{--}$iodopropane, where the bromine attaches to carbon 1, and the iodine attaches to carbon 2.

        Alternatively, if the carbocation forms on carbon 1, a less stable primary carbocation is formed. This carbocation is less stable because it has only one alkyl group providing a stabilizing inductive effect. This intermediate leads to the formation of the minor product, 1$\text{--}$bromo$\text{--}$2$\text{--}$iodopropane, where the bromine attaches to carbon 2, and the iodine attaches to carbon 1.

    2.  1$\textit{--}$bromo$\textit{--}$2$\textit{--}$iodopropane $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        

        To name the compound using IUPAC rules, begin by identifying the longest carbon chain, which in this case consists of three carbon atoms, giving the stem *prop-*. Next, identify the substituents attached to this carbon chain: a *bromo* group (Br) and an *iodo* group (I). The carbon chain should be numbered so that the substituents receive the lowest possible numbers. Here, the bromine is attached to the first carbon, and the iodine is attached to the second carbon. According to IUPAC conventions, substituents are listed in alphabetical order, so *bromo-* comes before *iodo-*. Thus, the correct IUPAC name is 1$\textit{--}$bromo$\textit{--}$2$\textit{--}$iodopropane.

    4.  Rotate plane$\text{--}$polarized light in equal magnitude and opposite direction $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        

        Enantiomers have the same physical and chemical properties except for their optical activity and their reactivity with other chiral molecules. The two enantiomers of a chiral compound rotate plane$\text{--}$polarized light in equal amounts and opposite directions.

    5.  Racemic mixture $\checkmark$

        Rotations of plane$-$polarized light of the enantiomers are cancelled as they are in equal amounts $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        

        A mixture containing an equal amount of the two enantiomers is known as a racemic mixture. This mixture is optically inactive as the rotation of plane$\text{--}$polarized light is compensated by these two equal amounts.
