# CH2059

In the reaction of 2$\text{--}$bromobutane with cyanide ion $(\ce{CN-}),$ what are the correct identities of the nucleophile, electrophile, and leaving group?

![CH2059](images/CH2059.png)

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Nucleophile | Electrophile|Leaving Group |
    | :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{CN-}$ | $\ce{Br-}$ |2$\text{--}$bromobutane |
    | B. | $\ce{CN-}$ | 2$\text{--}$bromobutane | $\ce{Br-}$ |
    | C. | 2$\text{--}$bromobutane | $\ce{CN-}$ | $\ce{Br-}$ |
    | D. | $\ce{Br-}$ | 2$\text{--}$bromobutane | $\ce{CN-}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

In a nucleophilic substitution reaction, the nucleophile attacks the electrophile, driving the leaving group off of the molecule resulting in the nucleophile taking its place. In the reaction of 2$\text{--}$bromobutane with cyanide, the cyanide is a nucleophile and the bromide ion is the leaving group (so these two ions switch places). The electrophile is the starting organic molecule (specifically the partially positive carbon atom that is bonded to the bromine).
