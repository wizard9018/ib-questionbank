# CH0449

Which species cannot act as a Lewis base?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{NH3}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{NH4+}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{H2O}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{F^-}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\

A Lewis base must have a lone pair of electrons available to donate. In $\ce{NH4+}$ the lone pair of ammonia has already been used to form the fourth $\ce{N-H}$ bond, so no lone pair remains and the ion cannot donate an electron pair. $\ce{NH3},$ $\ce{H2O}$ and $\ce{F^-}$ all carry lone pairs and can each form a coordination bond with a Lewis acid.
