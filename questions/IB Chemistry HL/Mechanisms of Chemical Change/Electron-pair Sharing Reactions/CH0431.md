# CH0431

**Maximum mark: 8**

When pent$-$1$-$ene reacts with $\ce{HBr}$, there are two possible products, $X$ and $Y$.

![CH0431](images/CH0431.png)

1.  Identify the major product of the reaction. [1 mark]

2.  Explain why the product identified in (a) is favoured. [2 marks]

3.  Draw a mechanism for the formation of one of the products. [4 marks]

4.  Some physical properties of the reactants and products are shown.

    | $\enskip$Compound$\enskip$ | $\enskip$Melting point / $\mathbf{^\circ \,C} \enskip$|$\enskip$Boiling point / $\mathbf{^\circ \,C} \enskip$ |$\enskip$Soluble in water?$\enskip$ |
    | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
    | Pent$-$1$-$ene | $-165$ |30 | No|
    | $X$ | $-95$ | 130|No |
    | $Y$ | $-96$ | 117 |No |

    Based on the physical properties shown, name a technique that could be used to separate the products of the reaction from unreacted pent$-$1$-$ene. [1 mark]

2.  reaction involves a carbocation intermediate $\checkmark$\
    secondary / more substituted carbocation is more stable $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Product $Y$ is the formed via a secondary carbocation intermediate which is more stable than the primary carbocation that would produce $X$.

3.  arrow from $\ce{C=C}$ bond to $\ce{H} \text{ of } \ce{H-Br} \,\checkmark$\
    arrow from $\ce{H-Br}$ bond to $\ce{Br}\, \checkmark$\
    arrow from $\ce{Br-}$ to positive charge on carbocation **AND** negative charge on $\ce{Br}\,; \checkmark$\
    correct structures of carbocation. $\checkmark$

    ![CH0434-1](images/CH0434-1.png)
    [Source: own work, based on data from materials.springer.com/isp/crystallographic/docs/sd_1925872; image prepared using Olex2]

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Curly arrows indicate the movement of a pair of electrons from an electron rich to an electron poor area.

4.  distillation **OR** fractional distillation $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    They are all insoluble in water, and $X$ and $Y$ have similar melting points. Fractional distillation separates substances based on varying boiling points.
