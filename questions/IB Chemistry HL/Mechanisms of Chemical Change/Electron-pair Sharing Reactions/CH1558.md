# CH1558

Which combination is correct for the mechanism of nitration of benzene?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"} 

    | | Electrophile | Reaction Temperature / $^\circ$C |

    | --: |  :-: | :-: |

    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{NO2^\textcolor{white}{+}}$ | 25 |

    | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{NO2+}$ | 25 |

    | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{NO2^\textcolor{white}{+}}$ | 60 |

    | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{NO2+}$ | 60 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The student must recall the nitration mechanism.

Concentrated sulfuric and nitric acid are used to produce the electrophile. In this reaction, nitric acid acts as a proton acceptor (Brønsted$\text{--}$Lowry base) and sulfuric acid acts as a proton donor (Brønsted$\text{--}$Lowry acid).

$\ce{HNO3 + H2SO4 -> H2NO3+ + HSO4-}$

Then, the conjugate acid of nitric acid decomposes and nitronium ion (the electrophile) is formed.

$\ce{H2NO3+ -> H2O + NO2+}$

Nitration occurs at $60^\circ\text{C},$ as shown below.

$\ce{C6H6 + NO2+ ->[H2SO4][60^\circ\text{C}] C6H5NO2 + H+}$

Temperatures above $60^\circ\text{C}$ encourage the formation of di$\text{--}$ or tri$\text{--}$substituted benzene molecules. Lower temperatures make the reaction too slow.

This question is categorized as medium as the student must recall details of the mechanism of the electrophilic substitution (nitration) of benzene.
