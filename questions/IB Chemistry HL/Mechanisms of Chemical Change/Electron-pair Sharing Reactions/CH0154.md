# CH0154

**Maximum mark: 4**

1. Differentiate between electrophile and nucleophile. [1 mark]

2. The reaction of benzene with elemental bromine is an example of electrophilic substitution.

    1. Suggest a catalyst for this reaction. [1 mark]

    2. Write a balanced chemical equation for this reaction. [1 mark]

3. Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction between chloroethane and aqueous sodium hydroxide. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  A nucleophile is an electron-rich species;

    An electrophile is an electron-deficient species. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    A nucleophile contains a lone pair that it donates to an electron-deficient species, the electrophile.

2.
    1.  $\ce{FeBr3 / AlBr3}\,\checkmark$

    2.  $\ce{C6H6 + Br2 ->[FeBr3] C6H5Br + HBr}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    This is a substitution reaction, one of the H atoms on benzene is replaced by a Br atom.

3.  $\ce{CH3CH2Cl (g) + OH- (aq) -> CH3CH2OH (aq) + Cl- (aq)}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Halogenoalkanes react with hydroxide ions through nucleophilic substitution reactions.
