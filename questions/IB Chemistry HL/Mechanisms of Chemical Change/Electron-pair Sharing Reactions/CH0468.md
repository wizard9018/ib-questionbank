# CH0468

Which salt produces a solution with the lowest $\ce{pH}$ when dissolved in water?

- A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{KCl}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{FeCl3}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{NaBr}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{KNO3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
$\ce{KCl},$ $\ce{NaBr},$ and $\ce{KNO3}$ are salts formed from strong acids and strong bases. Their ions do not undergo hydrolysis, so their solutions are neutral with pH 7.

$\ce{FeCl3}$ contains the small, highly charged $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ ion, which acts as a Lewis acid in solution. It polarises the O$-$H bonds in coordinated water molecules, releasing $\ce{H^{+}}$ ions:

$\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \ce{[Fe(H2O)5(OH)]^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H^{+}}\textrm{(aq)}$

This hydrolysis makes the solution acidic, giving the lowest pH of the four options.
