        Do **not** award M3 if negative charge of chloride ion omitted; \

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation}}$:

        The mechanism of the electrophilic addition begins with the C=C bond breaking as the bonding electron pair is donated to the electrophile. The movement of the electron pair is represented by a curly arrow. The nucleophile attacks the I atom of ICl, which has a $\delta^+$ charge due to the higher electronegativity of Cl. As the $\ce{C-I}$ bond is formed, the $\ce{I-Cl}$ bond breaks. The products of this step are a chloride ion and a carbocation. The chloride ion acts as a nucleophile, donating a lone pair to the positively charged carbon atom to produce a $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond.

    2.  The $\ce{I-Cl}$ bond is polar **OR** the I atom is $\delta^+$ (and the Cl atom $\delta^-$) $\checkmark$

        The $\delta^+$ (I atom) is attracted (electrostatically) to the bonding electrons of the C=C bond $\checkmark$

        *Do **not** award M1 for a description of electronegativity if the effect on bond polarity is not explicitly stated.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation}}$:

        The Cl is a period 3 halogen, while I is a period 5 halogen. Since the Cl atom has fewer electron shells, its valence electrons are closer to the nucleus and more strongly attracted to it. The Cl atom therefore has a higher electronegativity, and more strongly attracts the bonding electrons in the $\ce{I-Cl}$ bond. This makes the bond polar, reducing the electron density around the I atom and giving it a partial positive $(\delta^+)$ charge. As ICl approaches the electron-rich C=C bond, the $\delta^+$ I atom is electrostatically attracted to the negatively charged bonding electrons so is the favoured site for nucleophilic attack.

2.  $\e{C-I}$ group is replaced by $\ce{C-OH}$ group $\checkmark$

    Stereochemistries of $\ce{C-OH}$ group and substituted halo group are different *AND* stereochemistry of unreacted halo group is unaffected $\checkmark$

    ![ CH0929_Image5](<images/ CH0929_Image5.png>)

    *Award M2 if incorrect functional group is substituted but inversion of stereochemistry is shown;\
    Accept skeletal formula; \
    Do **not** award M2 if stereochemistry is ambiguous.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation}}$:

    In an $S_N2$ reaction, the nucleophile forms a bond with the electrophile and a bond in the electrophile breaks in a concerted fashion. The transition state for the reaction is a five-coordinate structure with the breaking bond opposite the bond that is formed. Thus, there is an inversion of stereochemistry: if the original functional group was positioned above the ring, the new functional group is below it. The product stereochemistry is always the reverse of the reactant stereochemistry, so the reaction is stereospecific.

    In this reaction, the $\ce{OH-}$ ion acts as the nucleophile.

    The $\ce{C-I}$ bond is weaker than the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond, as the I atom is larger than the Cl atom and the bonding electrons are further from its nucleus. Thus, the $\ce{C-I}$ bond is easier to break and is the favoured site for the $S_N2$ reaction. The byproduct of the reaction, the $\ce{I-}$ ion, is described as a better leaving group than the $\ce{Cl-}$ ion.
