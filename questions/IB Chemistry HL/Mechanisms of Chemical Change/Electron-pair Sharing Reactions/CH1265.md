# CH1265

Consider the chemical equation below.

$\ce{(CH3)3CCl + NaOH -> (CH3)3COH + NaCl}$

The reaction was performed so that the rate at $298\,\text{K}$ is $2.98\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ when $0.100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ of $\ce{(CH3)3CCl}$ and $0.100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ of NaOH were mixed.

Which is the correct value for the rate constant at $298\,\text{K}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1em}2.98\times 10^{-2}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}0.00298\ \text{s}^{-1}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}0.00298\ \text{dm}^3\ \text{mol}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}2.98\times 10^{-2}\ \text{dm}^3\,\text{mol}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Students must identify $\ce{(CH3)3CCl}$ as a tertiary halogenoalkane and determine that the nucleophilic substitution occurs via $S_N1.$

The rate of the $S_N1$ mechanism depends on the RDS (rate-determining step) that involves the breaking of the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond, and the rate equation is $rate = k[\ce{(CH3)3CCl}].$

From the given information, we can deduce $k$ as shown below.

$k = \dfrac{\textrm{rate}}{[\ce{(CH3)3CCl}]} = \dfrac{2.98\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\text{s}^{-1}}{0.100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}} = 2.98\times 10^{-2}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

This question is categorized as hard as students must figure out the rate equation after identifying the mechanism involved in deducing the $k$ magnitude with its corresponding units.
