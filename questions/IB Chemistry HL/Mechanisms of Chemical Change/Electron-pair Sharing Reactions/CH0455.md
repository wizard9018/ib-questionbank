# CH0455

Which species can act as a Lewis acid but **not** as a Brønsted$-$Lowry acid?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{BF3}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{HCl}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH3COOH}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{NH3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\

A Lewis acid accepts an electron pair; a Bronsted$-$Lowry acid donates a proton. The two definitions overlap wherever a proton is transferred, because the $\ce{H^+}$ itself accepts an electron pair from the base (water in the reaction below), so every Brønsted$-$Lowry acid behaves as a Lewis acid. 

$\underset{\textrm{\normalsize \textcolor{red}{acid}}}{\ce{HA}\textrm{(aq)}}

+

\underset{\textrm{\normalsize \textcolor{blue}{base}}}{\ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}}

\longrightarrow

\underset{\textrm{\normalsize \textcolor{red}{c.\ base}}}{\ce{A-}\textrm{(aq)}}

+

\underset{\textrm{\normalsize \textcolor{blue}{c.\ acid}}}{\ce{H3O+}\textrm{(aq)}}$

![lewisacid-1](images/lewisacid-1.png)

The reverse is not true. $\ce{BF3}$ has only six electrons around boron and readily accepts an electron pair, forming a coordination bond, as in $\ce{F3B-NH3}$. With no hydrogen to donate, it can never act as a Bronsted$-$Lowry acid. 

$\ce{HCl}$ and $\ce{CH3COOH}$ are proton donors. $\ce{NH3},$ with its lone pair, is both a Lewis and Bronsted$-$Lowry base.
