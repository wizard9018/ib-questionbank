# CH1027

Acyl halides have the general formula RCOX and can undergo nucleophilic substitution reactions in which a nucleophile substitutes the halogen.

This reaction involves a different substitution mechanism from that of halogenoalkanes. The mechanism is described below for ethanoyl chloride and ammonia.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The nucleophile is an electron-rich species attracted to groups that can accept electron pairs. In this case, the ammonia is the nucleophile and the first red arrow shows its attraction to the carbon in the $\ce{C=O}$ group of the ethanoyl chloride.

In addition, students must distinguish an amine ($\ce{-N=}$) from an amide ($\ce{-NH-CO -}$).

Finally, the leaving groups in a substitution must be identified as the group replaced in the substitution. In this case, the chloride is the leaving group.

This question is categorized as hard as students must understand the given mechanism (not requested within the IB syllabus) and interpret it as a regular nucleophilic substitution, recalling their knowledge of nucleophiles, leaving groups and functional groups.
