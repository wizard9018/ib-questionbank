# CH1015

Which statements about the chemical reaction between $\ce{C6H14}$ and bromine water are correct?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | The reaction only takes place in the presence of UV light. |
    | II. | Bromine water is decolorized as the reaction occurs. |
    | III. | This is an addition reaction. |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Students must recognize the substitution reaction of an alkane because the compound is of the general form $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}}$. This reaction requires UV light to initiate the formation of free radicals through homolytic fission, as shown below.

$\ce{Br2 ->[UV light] 2 Br.}$

Later on, bromine as a free radical reacts with hexane. Propagation takes place. Some chemical equations are provided below to illustrate how the reaction proceeds.

$\begin{aligned}
\ce{Br. + C6H14 -> .C6H13 + HBr} \\
\ce{.C6H13 + Br2 -> C6H13Br + Br.}
\end{aligned}$

Substituted bromoalkanes are formed. As bromine water has a brownish characteristic color, decolorization is observed.

This reaction is not classified as an addition, it is a radical substitution reaction.

This question is categorized as hard as the student must understand the difference between addition over alkenes and substitutions in alkanes. Students should recall the conditions for these two chemical reactions and remember color changes with bromine water.
