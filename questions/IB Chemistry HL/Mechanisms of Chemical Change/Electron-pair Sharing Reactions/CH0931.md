## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Pentan-2-ol $\checkmark$

    *Accept pentane-2-ol.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    The compound is an alcohol, so the name ends with -ol. The carbon chain contains five carbon atoms, so the name begins pentan-.

    The position of the hydroxyl group is indicated by numbering the carbon atoms in order from the end nearest the hydroxyl group. Thus, the hydroxyl group is in position 2.

2.  Formula drawn for a structural isomer of **X** with a hydroxyl or ether group; $\checkmark$

    Structural formula corresponds to 2-methoxybutane. $\checkmark$

    ![CH0931_Image3](images/CH0931_Image3.png) **OR** ![CH0931_Image4](images/CH0931_Image4.png)

    *Accept condensed structural formula if arrangement of atoms is unambiguous.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Structural isomers of a compound contain the same atoms bonded in a different arrangement. A structural isomer of **X** must therefore have the same molecular formula, $\ce{C5H12O}$. This molecular formula matches the general formula $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}O}$, which corresponds to an ether or an alcohol with no double bonds or rings.

    Compound **X** is chiral because the C atom attached to the OH group is attached to three other different groups, meaning that the molecule cannot be superimposed with its mirror image.

    The only isomer of **X** with a C atom bonded to four different groups is 2-methoxybutane.

3.  The reaction rate is not affected by $\ce{[NaOH]}$ **OR** the reaction is zeroth order (with respect to $\ce{NaOH}$) **OR** the reaction rate (in the graph) is constant $\checkmark$

    The rate-determining / rate-limiting / slowest step (in the $S_N1$ reaction) is the breaking of the C-Br bond / the loss of the leaving group / the formation of the carbocation intermediate $\checkmark$

    This step does not involve NaOH / $\ce{OH-}$ $\checkmark$

    *Award M2 for an equation or mechanism for the first step of the $S_N1$ reaction; \
    Do **not** award M1 unless the data points in the graph are described; \
    Do **not** award M2 if the link between the rate of the first step and the total reaction rate is not clearly stated; \
    Do **not** award M2 unless the details of the first step of the reaction are described.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    The reaction rate only depends on the concentration of a reactant if that reactant is involved in the slowest step of the reaction, the rate-determining step.

    In this $S_N1$ reaction, the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond is broken to form a carbocation, and the positively charged carbon atom then reacts with a hydroxide ion to form a new $\ce{C-O}$ bond. The first step is the slowest, rate-determining step because a large activation energy is needed to break the $\ce{C-Br}$ bond, whereas the second step involves no bond breaking.

    Hydroxide ions are not involved in the first step, so the rate equation is $rate = k[bromoalkane]$, i.e. the rate is constant with respect to [NaOH].
