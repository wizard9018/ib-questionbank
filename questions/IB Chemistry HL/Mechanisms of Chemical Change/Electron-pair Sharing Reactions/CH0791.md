# CH0791

Propane and propene both react with elemental bromine.

By which type of mechanism does each reaction occur?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
  | | Propane + bromine | Propene + bromine |
  | :-: | :-: | :-: |
  |A.$\hspace{1em}$| Free radical substitution | Electrophilic addition |
  |B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Free radical addition | Electrophilic addition |
  |C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Free radical substitution | Electrophilic substitution |
  |D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Free radical addition | Electrophilic substitution |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Alkanes such as propane react with bromine to form bromoalkanes. In this reaction, the $\ce{Br2}$ is converted to two bromine radicals, which react with the alkane to produce an alkyl radical and HBr. When the alkyl radical reacts with a further Br radical, a $\ce{C-Br}$ bond is formed. The reaction is a substitution because there are two reactants and two products.

Conversely, alkenes such as propene react with $\ce{Br2}$ to form dibromoalkanes. There are two reactants and only one product, so the reaction is an addition. The $\ce{Br2}$ molecule initially acts as an electrophile, accepting an electron pair from the $\ce{C=C}$ double bond, so the addition is classed as electrophilic.
