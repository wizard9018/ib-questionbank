# CH2028

One enantiomer of 2$\text{--}$chlorobutane undergoes nucleophilic substitution with warm, aqueous sodium hydroxide, producing a racemic mixture of products. Which statement about this reaction is correct?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The rate equation is $\text{rate} = k [\ce{C4H9Cl}][\ce{OH-}],$ and the hydroxide ion acts as a nucleophile.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The rate equation is $\text{rate} = k [\ce{C4H9Cl}][\ce{OH-}],$ and a secondary carbocation intermediate is formed.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The rate equation is $\text{rate} = k [\ce{C4H9Cl}],$ and the hydroxide ion acts as an electrophile.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The rate equation is $\text{rate} = k [\ce{C4H9Cl}],$ and the relative rate of substitution of 2$\text{--}$iodobutane is greater.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

The student must understand that a nucleophilic substitution reaction that produces a racemic mixture follows an $S_N1$ mechanism. In contrast, $S_N2$ reactions are stereochemically selective.

Unimolecular nucleophilic substitutions take place in two steps. In the first step, the $\ce{C-Cl}$ bond in the halogenoalkane undergoes heterolytic fission, producing a a secondary carbocation intermediate and the chloride ion.

![CH2028a](images/CH2028a.png)

In the second step, the carbocation reacts with the $\ce{OH-}$ nucleophile to create a new $\ce{C-O}$ bond. The alcohol can be formed with the nucleophile attacking the trigonal planar carbocation from either side. It will produce both products with equal probability: a racemic mixture.  To visualize this, the carbocation intermediate has been rotated so that the trigonal plane is perpendicular to the screen.

![CH2028b](images/CH2028b.png)

![CH2028c](images/CH2028c.png)

Once the student figured out that the mechanisms is unimolecular, they should identify the first step as the rate$\text{--}$determining step (RDS). This step depends on breaking the $\ce{C-X}$ bond in the halogenoalkane only. The rate equation is $\text{rate} = k[\ce{C4H9Cl}].$

The hydroxide ion acts as an excellent nucleophile as it is an electron$\text{--}$rich species. It bonds to the carbocation (which acts as the electrophile).

This question is categorized as hard as the student must identify the unimolecular nucleophilic substitution and its characteristics.
