# CH2700

Which reaction involves homolytic fission?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C2H4 + Cl2 -> C2H4Cl2}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH3Cl + NaOH -> CH3OH + NaCl}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H8 + Cl2 -> C4H8Cl2}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C2H6 + Cl2 -> C2H5Cl + HCl}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Homolytic fission occurs in the initiation step of a free$-$radical substitution mechanism. In this step, a covalent bond breaks so that each atom retains one electron from the bonding pair, producing two radicals.

The reaction of an alkane with a halogen proceeds through a free$-$radical mechanism. The $\ce{C-H}$ and $\ce{C-C}$ bonds in alkanes are strong and nonpolar, so alkanes do not react with halogens through ionic or polar pathways. Instead, UV light supplies the energy for homolytic fission of the $\ce{Cl-Cl}$ bond, generating chlorine radicals that initiate the chain reaction. The overall equation $\ce{C2H6 + Cl2 -> C2H5Cl + HCl}$ represents this free$-$radical substitution.

The addition of $\ce{Cl2}$ to $\ce{C2H4}$ is an electrophilic addition. The $\pi$ bond in ethene acts as a region of high electron density that attracts the electrophile. No homolytic fission is involved. The same reasoning applies to the addition of $\ce{Cl2}$ to $\ce{C4H8}.$

The reaction of $\ce{CH3Cl}$ with $\ce{NaOH}$ is a nucleophilic substitution. The hydroxide ion acts as a nucleophile, donating an electron pair to the electrophilic carbon. This proceeds through heterolytic bond breaking, not homolytic fission.
