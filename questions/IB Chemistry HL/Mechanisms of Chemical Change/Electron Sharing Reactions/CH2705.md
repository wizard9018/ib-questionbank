# CH2705

Which species is a radical?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{NO}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{OH^-}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{N2O3}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH3^+}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A radical is a species that contains one or more unpaired electrons. Radicals can be atoms, molecules, or ions.

Nitrogen monoxide has a total of $11$ valence electrons: $5$ from nitrogen and $6$ from oxygen. An odd number of electrons means that at least one electron must be unpaired. The nitrogen atom in $\ce{NO}$ carries this unpaired electron, making it a radical. This is one reason why $\ce{NO}$ is highly reactive in atmospheric chemistry.

The hydroxide ion $\ce{OH^-}$ has $8$ electrons in total $(6$ from oxygen, $1$ from hydrogen, and $1$ from the negative charge$).$ All electrons are paired: two in the $\ce{O-H}$ bond and three lone pairs on oxygen. It is not a radical.

$\ce{N2O3}$ has $24$ valence electrons (an even number), and all are paired in bonds and lone pairs. It is not a radical.

The methyl cation $\ce{CH3^+}$ has $6$ valence electrons $(4$ from carbon, $3$ from hydrogen, minus $1$ for the positive charge$).$ These six electrons form three $\ce{C-H}$ bonds, with no unpaired electrons. It is not a radical.
