# CH2702

What is formed in a propagation step of the reaction between bromine and methane?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Br^-}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Br^+}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Br}$ꞏ

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{H}$ꞏ

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The free$-$radical substitution of methane with bromine proceeds through three stages. In the initiation step, UV light causes homolytic fission of the $\ce{Br-Br}$ bond, producing two bromine radicals. The propagation steps are:

$\ce{CH4 + Br}$ꞏ$\ \ce{-> \ } \ $ꞏ$\ce{CH3 + HBr}$

ꞏ$\ce{CH3 + Br2 -> CH3Br + Br}$ꞏ

In the second propagation step, the methyl radical reacts with a bromine molecule to form bromomethane and regenerate a bromine radical. The bromine radical is therefore formed as a product of propagation and goes on to sustain the chain reaction.

$\ce{Br^-}$ and $\ce{Br^+}$ are ions produced by heterolytic fission, where both bonding electrons transfer to the same atom. Heterolytic fission does not occur in a free$-$radical mechanism.

The hydrogen radical $\ce{H}\text{ꞏ}$ is not formed in this mechanism. When a bromine radical reacts with methane, it abstracts a hydrogen atom to form $\ce{HBr},$ not $\ce{H}\text{ꞏ}.$ The hydrogen remains bonded throughout the process.
