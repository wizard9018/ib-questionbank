## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Homolytic fission is the breaking of a covalent bond so that each atom retains one electron from the bonding pair. This produces two radicals, each with an unpaired electron. The movement of a single electron is shown with a single$-$headed curly arrow (a fishhook arrow). Two fishhook arrows are needed, one pointing toward each atom, to show that each atom receives one electron.

Heterolytic fission is the breaking of a covalent bond so that both bonding electrons transfer to the same atom, producing one cation and one anion. This is shown with a double$-$headed curly arrow pointing toward the atom that gains both electrons.

The correct representation of homolytic fission uses two fishhook arrows curving in opposite directions from the bond, one toward each atom. This shows the equal distribution of the bonding pair.
