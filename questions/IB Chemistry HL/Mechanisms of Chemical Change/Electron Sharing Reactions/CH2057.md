# CH2057

Which of the following correctly represents a propagation step in the free$\text{--}$radical substitution reaction between methane  and chlorine gas when exposed to UV light?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH3Cl + Cl}$• $\longrightarrow$ •$\ce{CH3 + Cl2}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$•$\ce{CH3 + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + Cl}$•

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{Cl2 -> 2 Cl}$•

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH4 + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + HCl}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The propagation step of a free radical substitution reaction is characterized by one radical on both the reactant side and product side. This ensures that a radical is formed that can go on to react with another non$\text{--}$radical reactant, thus allowing the reaction to continue.

While the reaction

$\ce{CH3Cl + Cl}$• $\ce{-> CH3}$• $\ce{ + Cl2}$

has radicals on both sides of the reaction, this reaction is written backwards. In the free radical substitution reaction of methane with chlorine, the $\ce{Cl2}$ molecule is broken apart by UV light and it only reforms if two chlorine radicals recombine in a termination step. Therefore, the correct answer is

$\ce{CH3}$• $\ce{+ Cl2 -> CH3Cl + Cl}$•.
