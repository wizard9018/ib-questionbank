# CH2701

Which equation does **not** represent a propagation step in the free$-$radical substitution of butane with chlorine?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H10 + Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{\  -> C4H9}$ꞏ$\ce{ + HCl}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H9}$ꞏ$\ce{ + Cl2 -> C4H9Cl + Cl}$ꞏ

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H9Cl + Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{\  -> C4H8Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{+ HCl}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H10 + Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{\ -> H}$ꞏ $\ce{+ C4H9Cl}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A propagation step involves the reaction of one radical with a molecule to produce a new radical. The total number of radicals remains the same: one radical is consumed and one is generated.

In the first propagation step, a chlorine radical abstracts a hydrogen atom from butane. The chlorine radical pairs with the hydrogen atom to form $\ce{HCl},$ and the butyl radical $\ce{C4H9}\text{ꞏ}$ is produced. In the second propagation step, the butyl radical reacts with a chlorine molecule to form chlorobutane and regenerate a chlorine radical. Further substitution is also possible: a chlorine radical can abstract a hydrogen atom from chlorobutane to produce $\ce{C4H8Cl\text{ꞏ}}$ and $\ce{HCl}.$ All three of these steps follow the pattern of one radical in, one radical out.

The equation $\hspace{1.em}\ce{C4H10 + Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{\ -> H}$ꞏ $\ce{+ C4H9Cl}$ does not represent a step that occurs in this mechanism. When a chlorine radical encounters butane, it abstracts a hydrogen atom by pairing the hydrogen with its own unpaired electron. The product is $\ce{HCl},$ not a free hydrogen radical. The formation of $\ce{H\text{ꞏ}}$ would require homolytic fission of the $\ce{C-H}$ bond while the chlorine radical simultaneously forms a bond to carbon, which is not how this reaction proceeds.
