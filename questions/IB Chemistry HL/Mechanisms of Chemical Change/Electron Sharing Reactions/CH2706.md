# CH2706

Which step in a free$-$radical mechanism cannot be classified as a termination step?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH3}$ꞏ$\ce{ + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + Cl}$ꞏ

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH3}$ꞏ$\ce{ + CH3}$ꞏ$\ce{\  -> C2H6}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{ + Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{\  -> Cl2}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CH3}$ꞏ$\ce{ + Cl}$ꞏ$\ce{\ -> CH3Cl}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A termination step occurs when two radicals combine to form a stable molecule. Both unpaired electrons pair up, and no new radicals are generated. This removes radicals from the reaction mixture and slows the chain reaction.

The reaction $\ce{CH3}$ꞏ$\ce{\  + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + Cl}\text{ꞏ}$ is a propagation step, not a termination step. A methyl radical reacts with a chlorine molecule (not a radical) to form chloromethane and regenerate a chlorine radical. One radical is consumed and one is produced, so the chain continues.

The combination of two methyl radicals to form ethane is a termination step. Two radicals are consumed and no new radicals are produced. The same applies to two chlorine radicals combining to regenerate $\ce{Cl2},$ and to a methyl radical combining with a chlorine radical to form $\ce{CH3Cl}.$ In each case, the unpaired electrons pair up and the chain reaction loses radicals.
