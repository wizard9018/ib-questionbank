# CH0095

In a reaction between an excess of potassium carbonate and 2.0 mol of hydrochloric acid, how much water is produced in moles?     

- A.$\hspace{1em}$0.5

- B.$\hspace{1em}$1.0

- C.$\hspace{1em}$2.0

- D.$\hspace{1em}$4.0

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\

Write the balanced equation for the reaction between potassium carbonate and hydrochloric acid:

$\ce{K2CO3}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,KCl}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{CO2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

Since potassium carbonate is in excess, $\ce{HCl}$ is the limiting reactant. The stoichiometric ratio of $\ce{HCl}$ to $\ce{H2O}$ is $2:1,$ so:

$n(\ce{H2O}) = 2.0 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 1.0 \textrm{\ mol}$
