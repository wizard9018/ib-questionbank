# CH0910

An aqueous indicator changes colour at 25 $^\circ$C when $\ce{[OH-]} = 2.51\times 10^{-6}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}.$

What are the $pK_a$ and $\textrm{p}K_b$ values of this indicator at 25 $^\circ$C?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | $\mathbf{pK_a}$ | $\mathbf{pK_b}$ |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A. | $-\log_{10} (2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ | $14 - \log_{10}(2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ |
    | B. | $-\log_{10}(2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ | $14 + \log_{10}(2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ |
    | C. | $14 - \log_{10} (2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ | $-\log_{10} (2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ |
    | D. | $14 + \log_{10} (2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ | $-\log_{10} (2.51 \times 10^{-6})$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

An indicator changes colour over a pH range of $pK_a \pm 1$.

The colour change occurs because the acid and base forms of the indicator have different colours, and the $pK_a$ is the pH at which the concentrations of the two forms are equal.

The $pK_a$ value is equal to the pH when the colour change is observed, given by $\text{pH} = 14 - \text{pOH}$, where $\text{pOH} = -\log_{10}\ce{[OH-]}.$

Thus, $pK_a = 14 -\left(-\log_{10}\ce{[OH]}^-\right) = 14 + \log_{10}\ce{[OH-]}$.

Likewise, the $pK_b$ value is equal to the pOH when the colour change is observed, so $pK_b = -\log_{10}\ce{[OH-]}.$
