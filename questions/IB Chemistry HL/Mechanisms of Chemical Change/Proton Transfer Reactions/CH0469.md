# CH0469

Which of the following combinations of $\ce{NH3}\textrm{(aq)}$ and $\ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)}$ forms a buffer solution?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}25\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{NH3(aq)}\text{ and }25\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{HCl(aq)}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}50\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{NH3(aq)}\text{ and } 25\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{HCl(aq)}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}12.5\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{NH3(aq)}\text{ and } 50\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{HCl(aq)}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}25\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{NH3(aq)}\text{ and }50\,\text{cm}^3 \text{ of } 0.1\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\ce{HCl(aq)}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A buffer solution contains a weak base and its conjugate acid in significant quantities. When $\ce{NH3}$ is partially neutralised by $\ce{HCl},$ some of the $\ce{NH3}$ is converted to $\ce{NH4^{+}}\textrm{:}$

$\ce{NH3}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \rightarrow \ce{NH4Cl}\textrm{(aq)}$

Since all solutions share the same concentration ($0.1 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$), the mole ratio depends only on volume. For a buffer to form, $\ce{NH3}$ must be in excess so that unreacted $\ce{NH3}$ and the newly formed $\ce{NH4^{+}}$ both remain in solution.

Mixing $50 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{NH3}$ with $25 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{HCl}$ provides twice as many moles of $\ce{NH3}$ as $\ce{HCl}.$ Half the $\ce{NH3}$ is neutralised to form $\ce{NH4^{+}},$ leaving equal amounts of $\ce{NH3}$ and $\ce{NH4^{+}}.$ This is a buffer.

When equal volumes are mixed ($25 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ each), the moles are equal and all $\ce{NH3}$ is neutralised. Only $\ce{NH4^{+}}$ remains, with no free $\ce{NH3}.$ This is not a buffer.

When $\ce{HCl}$ is in excess ($50 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{HCl}$ with $25 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{NH3},$ or $50 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{HCl}$ with $12.5 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $\ce{NH3}$), all the $\ce{NH3}$ is consumed and unreacted strong acid remains. This is not a buffer.
