# CH2207

Two $0.10\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ aqueous solutions are prepared, one of hydrochloric acid (HCl) and one of ethanoic acid $\ce{(CH3COOH)}.$

Which of the following statements about these solutions is/are correct?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | The pH of the hydrochloric acid solution is higher than that of the ethanoic acid solution. |
    | II. | $\ce{CH3COO-}$ is a stronger base than $\ce{Cl-}.$ |
    | III. | Magnesium reacts more rapidly with the hydrochloric acid solution than with the ethanoic acid solution. |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$II only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The student must identify HCl as a strong acid which dissociates completely

$\ce{HCl -> H+ + Cl-}$

and $\ce{CH3COOH}$ is a weak acid which only partially dissociates.

$\ce{CH3COOH <=> H+ + CH3COO-}$

Since the concentration of both acids is the same, the resulting hydrogen ion concentration in ethanoic acid is smaller due to its partial dissociation. The smaller hydrogen ion concentration results in a higher pH as $\text{pH} = -\log\ce{[H+]}.$ So ethanoic acid has a higher pH. Statement I is incorrect.

As hydrochloric acid dissociates completely, the chloride ion has no tendency to take a proton from water. A solution of NaCl is neutral. Ethanoic acid, on the other hand, is a weak acid, so the ethanoate ion can take a proton from water and act as a base.

$\ce{CH3COO- + H2O <=> CH3COOH + OH-}$

Statement II is correct.

Finally, reactive metals react with acids to produce salt and hydrogen gas.

$\ce{Mg(s) + 2 H+(aq) -> H2(g) + Mg^2+(aq)}$

However, the reaction rate depends on the nature of the acid. Strong acids react faster because they have a higher concentration of H+ ions. Statement III is correct.

This question is categorized as a medium as the student must distinguish the characteristics of strong and weak acids regarding their pH, the strength of their conjugate bases, and their reactions with metals.
