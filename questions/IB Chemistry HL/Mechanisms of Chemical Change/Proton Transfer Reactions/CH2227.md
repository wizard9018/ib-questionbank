# CH2227

A weak base has a $\text{p}K_b$ value of 9.9. At what pH does a buffer solution of this base and its conjugate acid have its maximum buffering capacity?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}4.1$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}5.0$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}9.0$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}9.9$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

A buffer solution resists changes in pH most effectively when the concentrations of the weak base and its conjugate acid are equal. Under these conditions, the Henderson$-$Hasselbalch equation reduces to $\text{pOH} = \text{p}K_b.$

For this base, $\text{p}K_b = 9.9,$ so the pOH at maximum buffering efficiency is 9.9. Using the relationship $\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14,$ the pH is calculated as $14 - 9.9 = 4.1.$ Thus, the buffer will have its greatest ability to resist changes in pH at 4.1.

This question is medium in difficulty, as students must connect the concept of maximum buffer capacity with the equality of conjugate species and then correctly manipulate pH and pOH.
