# CH0867

1000 cm$^3$  of 1 mol dm$^{-3}$ solutions of each of the reactants were used in the reactions between the species below:
-   $\ce{HCOOH}$ and $\ce{Na2CO3}$
-   $\ce{HCOOH}$ and $\ce{NaHCO3}$

How could you distinguish between the two reactions?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | The amount of gas evolved |
    | II. | The pH of the final solution |
    | III. | The amount of $\ce{HCOONa}$ produced |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

$\ce{2 HCOOH(aq) + Na2CO3(aq) -> 2 HCOONa(aq) + CO2(g) + H2O(l)}$

$\ce{HCOOH(aq) + NaHCO3(aq) -> HCOONa(aq) + CO2(g) + H2O(l)}$

For the first reaction, the limiting reagent is methanoic acid. Only half of the sodium carbonate is required to neutralize it.

This means the final solution will have 0.5 mol of the original sodium carbonate and 1 mol of sodium methanoate with 0.5 mol of carbon dioxide released. The pH will be basic due to the presence of the original sodium carbonate.

For the second reaction, there are stoichiometrically equal amounts of methanoic acid and sodium hydrogen carbonate. The final solution will have 1 mol of sodium methanoate with 1 mol of carbon dioxide released. The pH will be close to neutral as all the acid and base has been neutralized.

Therefore, statements I and II are valid.
