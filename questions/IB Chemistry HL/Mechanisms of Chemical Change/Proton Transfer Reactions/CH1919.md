# CH1919

Consider the equilibrium reaction:

$\ce{HPO_4^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \rightleftharpoons \ce{H_2PO_4^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)}$

Which statement is correct regarding the Brønsted$-$Lowry nature of the species involved?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{HPO_4^{2-}}$ acts as a Bronsted$-$Lowry acid because it donates a proton.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{H_2O}$ acts as a Bronsted$-$Lowry base because it accepts a proton.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{H_2PO_4^-}$ and $\ce{HPO_4^{2-}}$ differ by one proton, making them a conjugate acid$-$base pair.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{OH^-}$ is the conjugate acid of $\ce{H_2O}.$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
In this equilibrium, the hydrogen phosphate ion, $\ce{HPO4^{2-}},$ accepts a proton from water to form $\ce{H2PO4-}.$ This makes $\ce{HPO4^{2-}}$ the Bronsted$-$Lowry base and $\ce{H2PO4-}$ its conjugate acid. Conjugate acid$-$base pairs always differ by exactly one proton, and here the difference between $\ce{HPO4^{2-}}$ and $\ce{H2PO4-}$ is a single $\ce{H+}.$ At the same time, water donates the proton and acts as the acid, forming hydroxide as its conjugate base. The important point is that conjugate species always come in linked pairs across the equilibrium, and identifying the one$-$proton difference is the most reliable way to recognize them.
