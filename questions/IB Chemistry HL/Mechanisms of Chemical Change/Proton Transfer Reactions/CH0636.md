# CH0636

The reversible reaction of NaClO with $\ce{CH3COOH}$ is shown below:

$\ce{NaClO + CH3COOH <=> HClO + CH3COONa}$

Why does the addition of CH$_3$COONa affect the position of equilibrium?                 

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH3COONa}$ is the conjugate acid of NaClO

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH3COONa}$ is the conjugate base of NaClO

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH3COONa}$ is the conjugate acid of $\ce{CH3COOH}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\ce{CH3COONa}$ is the conjugate base of $\ce{CH3COOH}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

If $\ce{CH3COONa}$, a product of the forward reaction, is added to the system, the position of equilibrium shifts to the left. The $\ce{CH3COO^-}$ ions bond to hydrogen ions from HClO to produce $\ce{CH3COOH}$.

$\ce{CH3COONa}$ is therefore the conjugate base of $\ce{CH3COOH}$, while HClO is the conjugate acid of NaClO.

This question is moderately difficult as the student must identify and correctly categorise a conjugate base in the context of an unfamiliar reaction equation.
