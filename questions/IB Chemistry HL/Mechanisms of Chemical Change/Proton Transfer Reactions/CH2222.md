# CH2222

The table shows three acids, HA, HB and HC.

| Acid | $\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{a}}$ | p$\mathbf{K_a}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| HA | $5.0\times 10^{-5}$ | $-$ |
| HB | $-$ | 2.0 |
| HC | $-$ | 6.9 |

Which is the correct order of increasing acid strength?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HC}<\ce{HA}<\ce{HB}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HA}<\ce{HC}<\ce{HB}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HB}<\ce{HC}<\ce{HA}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HB}<\ce{HA}<\ce{HC}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

To determine the relative strength of these acids, students must recall the fundamental relationship between $K_a,$ $\text{p}K_a,$ and acid strength.

A larger $K_a$ value indicates a stronger acid because it dissociates to a greater extent. Conversely, a smaller $\text{p}K_a$ value corresponds to a larger $K_a$ and therefore greater acidity.

From the data given, HB with a $\text{p}K_a$ of 2.0 has a $K_a$ of approximately $10^{-2},$ making it the strongest acid of the three. HC, with a $\text{p}K_a$ of 6.9, corresponds to a $K_a$ between $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-7},$ identifying it as the weakest. HA, with a $K_a$ of $5.0\times 10^{-5},$ falls in between. Thus, the correct order of increasing acid strength is HC < HA < HB.

This question is considered medium in difficulty, as it requires students to interpret both $K_a$ and $\text{p}K_a$ values and apply them to rank acid strengths.
