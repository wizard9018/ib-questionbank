# CH0912

The $pK_a$ of aqueous benzoic acid is $4.2$.

When benzoic acid is dissolved in water, only a small amount of the acid dissociates. At 20 $^\circ$C, a saturated aqueous solution of benzoic acid has a concentration of approximately 0.024 mol dm$^{-3}$.

What is the value of $\ce{[H+]}$ in this saturated aqueous solution?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\sqrt{10^{-4.2}\times 0.024}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\sqrt{10^{4.2}\times 0.024}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\sqrt{\dfrac{10^{-4.2}}{0.024}}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\sqrt{\dfrac{10^{4.2}}{0.024}}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The $K_a$ of an acid is given by $K_a =\dfrac{\ce{[H+][A-]}}{\ce{[HA]}}$, where HA is the undissociated acid and $\ce{A-}$ the conjugate base.

When HA dissociates, an equal number of $\ce{H+}$ and $\ce{A-}$ ions are formed, so $K_a =\dfrac{[\ce{H+}]^2}{[\ce{HA}]}$.

Rearranging this equation gives $\ce{[H+]} = \sqrt{(K_a\times [\ce{HA}])}$.

The $pK_a$ and $K_a$ of HA are related by the equation $pK_a = -\log_{10} K_a$, so $K_a = 10^{-pKa}$.

Thus, $\ce{[H+]} = \sqrt{(10^{-pKa}\times [\ce{HA}])}$.

For a weak acid such as benzoic acid, only a small amount of the acid dissociates, so [HA] is approximately equal to the concentration of HA before dissociation.
