# CH0248

Which ions are present in solution when carbonic acid dissociates in water?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{H+}$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}\ce{CO3^2-}$
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}\ce{HCO3^-}$

- A.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

- C.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Carbonic acid is $\ce{H2CO3}$ and is diprotic and dissociates according to the equations below:

| | | |
| -: | -: | :- |
| Step 1: | $\ce{H2CO3(aq)}$ | $\ce{<=> H+(aq) + HCO^-3(aq)}$ |
| Step 2: | $\ce{HCO3-(aq)}$ | $\ce{<=> H+(aq) + CO3^2-(aq)}$ |

Step 1 produces $\ce{H^{+}}$ and $\ce{HCO3^{-}},$ and step 2 produces additional $\ce{H^{+}}$ and $\ce{CO3^{2-}}.$ All three ions (I, II, and III) are therefore present in solution.

Carbonic acid is a weak acid ($K_{a1} \approx 4.3 \times 10^{-7}$), and the second dissociation is much weaker still ($K_{a2} \approx 4.7 \times 10^{-11}$). This means $\ce{CO3^{2-}}$ is present only in trace amounts, but the question asks which ions are present, not which are present in significant concentration. Since all three ions are produced by the dissociation equilibria, the correct answer includes all three.
