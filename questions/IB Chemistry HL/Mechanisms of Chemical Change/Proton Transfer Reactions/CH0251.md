# CH0251

**Maximum mark: 5**

 Acids and bases can be classified as strong or weak.

1.  Explain why ammonia, $\ce{NH3}$ in an aqueous solution, acts as a weak base,  using an equation. [2 marks]

2.  An experiment was carried out to determine the energy change during the following reaction:

    $\ce{2NH3 (aq) + H2SO4 (aq) -> (NH4)2SO4 (aq)}$

    $\begin{aligned}
    \text{Initial temperature of each solution} &= 21.4^\circ \mathrm{C} \\
    \text{Final temperature of the mixture} &= 23.8^\circ \mathrm{C} \\
    \end{aligned}$

    1.  State with a reason whether the sign of the enthalpy change for this reaction is positive or negative. [1 mark]

    2.  Suggest with a reason whether the magnitude of the enthalpy change will be greater or smaller if aqueous ammonia is replaced with potassium hydroxide. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Ammonia is a poor proton ($\ce{H+}$ ions) acceptor and it dissociates/ionises partially in aqueous solution $\checkmark$

    $\ce{NH3 (aq) + H2O (l) <=> NH4+ (aq) + OH- (aq)}\, \checkmark$

2.  1.  negative $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        The temperature increases during the reaction so it is exothermic.

    2.  greater $\checkmark$

        KOH is a strong base, the formation of $\ce{OH-}$ requires less energy $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        Potassium hydroxide KOH is a strong base which dissociates/ionises completely and less energy will be required in dissociating the $\ce{KOH (aq)}$ compared to $\ce{NH3 (aq)}.$

        The reaction with sulphuric acid will be more exothermic with  potassium hydroxide, and the enthalpy change will be more negative, so the magnitude of the enthalpy change will be greater.
