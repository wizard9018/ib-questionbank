# CH0459

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the following reaction:

$\ce{H2SO4 (aq) + NH3 (aq) <=> HSO^-4 (aq) + NH^+4 (aq)}$

1.  

    1.  Define a Brønsted-Lowry acid and a Lewis acid. [2 marks]

    2.  Deduce the two species acting as acids and their conjugate bases in the equation above. [2 marks]

2.  Give an example of a species that is a Lewis acid but not a Brønsted-Lowry acid and explain why this is the case. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  

    1.  A Brønsted-Lowry acid is a species that donates a proton/H+. $\checkmark$

        A Lewis acid is a species that accepts an electron/ lone pair. $\checkmark$

    2.  $\ce{H2SO4}$, conjugate base $\ce{HSO^-4};$ $\checkmark$

        $\ce{NH^+4}$ conjugate base $\ce{NH3}$ .$\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        A conjugate base is the species formed when an acid has donated a proton ($\ce{H+}$).

2.  $\ce{BF3}$ has an incomplete octet, so can accept a lone pair of electrons, and is a Lewis acid but it does not contain any H atoms so cannot donate a proton and is not a Brønsted-Lowry acid.

    Other examples are possible ($e.g.\,\ce{AlCl3},\,\ce{Fe^{3+}}$).
