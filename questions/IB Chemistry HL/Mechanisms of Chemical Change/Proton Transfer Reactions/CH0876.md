# CH0876

**Maximum mark: 9**

$\ce{HCl}$ is a dangerous substance at high concentrations and is typically diluted to under 1 mol dm$^{-3}$ for use in school laboratories.

1.
    1.  State and explain whether $\ce{HCl}$ is a strong or weak acid. [1 mark]

    2.  Calculate the pH of a $0.5 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ solution of $\ce{HCl}$. [1 mark]

2.  Calculate the volume of **water** required to dilute a solution of $\ce{HCl}$ with an initial volume of 500 cm$^3$ and a concentration of 12.0 mol dm$^{-3}$ to a final concentration of 0.500 mol dm$^{-3}$. [3 marks]

$\ce{CH3COOH}$ is a weak, organic acid.

3.
    1.  State the equations for the reaction of $\ce{HCl}$ with water and $\ce{CH3COOH}$ with water. [2 marks]

    2.  State and explain one difference and one similarity between the reactions of both acids with magnesium. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  strong **AND** completely dissociates in water $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
        A strong acid completely dissociates in water whereas a weak acid only partially dissociates in water. $\ce{HCl}$, $\ce{HNO3}$ and $\ce{H2SO4}$ are examples of strong acids common in this course.

    2.  $\textrm{pH} = -\log_{10}(0.5) = {0.30}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The pH is dependent on the concentration of $\ce{H+}$ ions in the solution according to the relationship

        $\textrm{pH} = -\log_{10}[\ce{H3O+}]$

        As $\ce{HCl}$ is a strong monoprotic acid, the concentration of $\ce{H3O+}$ is equal to the concentration of $\ce{HCl}$ which is given in the question. We can substitute this concentration into the equation to find the pH of the solution.

        $\textrm{pH} = -\log_{10}(0.5) = 0.30$

2.  $n = C_{conc} V_{conc} = 12\times 0.500 = 6.0\,\text{mol}\: \checkmark$ \
    $V_{dilute} = \dfrac{n}{C_{dilute}} = \dfrac{6.0}{0.50} = 12\,\text{dm}^{3} = 12000 \text{\ cm}^{3}\: \checkmark$\
    $V_{\ce{H2O}} = V_{dilute} - V_{conc} = 12000 - 500 = 11500\,\text{cm}^{3}\:\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
    To answer this question, first, we need to find the number of moles of $\ce{HCl}$ in the concentrated solution remembering to convert cm$^3$ to dm$^3$.

    $n = C_{conc} V_{conc} = 12.0 \textrm{\ mol dm}^3 \times 0.500 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 6.00\ \text{mol}$

    When the acid is diluted, the moles of HCl will remain constant.  We can use the same expression to determine the volume of the dilute solution:

    $V_{dilute} = \dfrac{n}{C_{dilute}} = \dfrac{6.00 \textrm{\ mol}}{0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}} = 12.0\,\text{dm}^{3}$

    As the original solution had a volume of 500 cm$^3$, or $0.500 \textrm{\ dm}^3,$ we need to subtract this to find the volume of water that needs to be added.

    $V_{\ce{H2O}} = V_{dilute} - V_{conc} = 12.0 \textrm{\ dm}^3 - 0.50\textrm{ dm}^3 = 11.5\ \text{dm}^{3}$

    As the original solution had a volume of 500 cm$^3$, we need to subtract this to find the volume of water that needs to be added.

3.
    1.  $\ce{HCl(aq) + H2O(l) -> H3O+(aq) + Cl-(aq)}\,\checkmark$ \
        $\ce{CH3COOH(aq) + H2O(l) <=> H3O+(aq) + CH3COO-(aq)}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
        When acids react with water, the acid loses an $\ce{H+}$ and the water gains an $\ce{H+}$ to become $\ce{H3O+}$.

    2.  ***difference:***  faster reaction with $\ce{HCl} \: \checkmark$ \
        ***similarity:***  salt and hydrogen gas are produced $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The reaction between a metal and an acid produces a salt and hydrogen gas.

        $\ce{2 HA(aq) + Mg(s) -> MgA2 + H2}$

        A strong acid fully dissociates in solution, so the reaction occurs faster and more vigorously with a strong acid than with a weak acid.
