# CH1267

Which combination applies for the titration of an $\ce{NH3(aq)}$ solution with $0.0100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ HCl(aq) solution?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
 | | Initial pOH | pH at equivalence | Indicator |
 | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
 | A. | Above 7 | Above 7 | phenolphthalein |
 | B. | Above 7 | Above 7 | methyl red |
 | C. | Below 7 | Below 7 | phenolphthalein |
 | D. | Below 7 | Below 7 | methyl red |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Ammonia is a weak base. Its initial concentration will determine the pOH or pH. Its concentration is unknown. However, before titration, we can expect a pH above 7, or pOH below 7.

Moreover, the initial pH will slowly decrease as the acid is added, the curve flattens out as the ammonia is partially neutralized ,and the buffer region forms.

Then, a sudden decrease occurs, and the pH decreases till it approaches $\text{pH} = 2,$ the pH of the HCl solution being added.

The equivalence point lies in the middle of the sharp change in the pH. This sharp change will go from somewhere near $\text{pH} = 9$ to $\text{pH} = 2.$ We can estimate the equivalence point to be near $\text{pH} = 5.5.$ As the titration occurs with a strong acid, the pH at the equivalence point will be lower than 7. Finally, the indicator should have a pH range below 7, so methyl red (pH range $4.4-6.2)$ is appropriate, whereas phenolphthalein (pH range $8.3-10.0)$ is not for this titration. The pH ranges of the indicators can be checked in Section 18 in the IB Chemistry data booklet.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must be able to identify general trends in the titration curve of a weak base with a strong acid.
