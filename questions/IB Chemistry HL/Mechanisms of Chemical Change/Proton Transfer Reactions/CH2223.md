# CH2223

Which of the following, when added to a solution of ethanoic acid, would not produce a buffer solution?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$sodium ethanoate

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$sodium hydroxide

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$hydrochloric acid

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$potassium hydroxide

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

A buffer solution requires the presence of a weak acid and its conjugate base in appreciable amounts. Ethanoic acid can form such a system in two ways. The most direct method is to add its salt, sodium ethanoate, which immediately introduces the conjugate base into solution. Alternatively, the addition of a strong base such as sodium hydroxide or potassium hydroxide in limited quantities will neutralize part of the ethanoic acid, generating ethanoate ions in the process, while leaving unreacted acid present. Both conditions give the necessary weak acid$\text{--}$conjugate base pair. However, adding a strong acid like hydrochloric acid simply increases the concentration of hydrogen ions, suppressing ionization of the weak acid and leaving no significant concentration of conjugate base. For this reason, hydrochloric acid does not produce a buffer solution.

This question is categorized as a medium, as the student must understand the necessary components of a buffer solution.
