# CH2209

A $50.0\text{ cm}^3$ sample of hydrochloric acid is titrated with $0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ sodium hydroxide. During the titration the temperature of the reaction mixture is recorded after each small addition of NaOH and plotted against the cumulative volume of titrant, as shown below.

![CH2209](images/CH2209.png)

Which of the following statements about this experiment is/are correct?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | The volume of NaOH(aq) at the equivalence point is $11\text{ cm}^3.$ |
    | II. | The hydrochloric acid concentration is $0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}.$ |
    | III. | If the procedure were carried out with ethanoic acid of the same concentration and volume, the maximum temperature reached would be lower than $34^\circ\text{C}.$ |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$II only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

In a thermometric titration the thermometer replaces the pH probe: temperature is monitored as the dependent variable.

The neutralisation of hydrochloric acid by sodium hydroxide is strongly exothermic. While any HCl remains in excess, each increment of NaOH reacts completely, so progressively more moles are neutralised and progressively more heat is released, causing the temperature to rise. At the equivalence point every proton has been consumed; further additions of NaOH produce no additional heat because no acid is left to react. Instead, the incoming titrant only increases the mass of the solution. With the same amount of heat now distributed through a larger mass, the observed temperature begins to fall after the equivalence point, giving the characteristic peak that pinpoints the titrant volume required for complete neutralisation.

Plotting temperature against cumulative volume of titrant thus produces two roughly linear trends: an upward line while neutralisation continues and a downward line after the acid becomes the limiting reagent. Extrapolating these straight portions to their point of intersection pinpoints the equivalence point of the titration, without the need for pH measurements.

![CH2209ms](images/CH2209ms.png)

In this case, the volume of $0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ NaOH(aq) volume required at the equivalence point is $10\text{ cm}^3.$ Statement I is incorrect.

From here we can deduce the concentration of the HCl(aq). At the equivalence point, the amount of acid is equal to the amount of base, $n_\ce{HCl} = n_\ce{NaOH}.$ Since the number of moles, concentration and volume are related by the equation $n = C\times V,$ the concentration of the HCl can be found as follows.

$C_\ce{HCl}\times V_\ce{HCl} = C_\ce{NaOH}\times V_\ce{NaOH}$

$C_\ce{HCl}\times 50.0\text{ cm}^3 = 0.500\text{ mol dm}^{-3}\times 10.0\text{ cm}^3$

$C_\ce{HCl} = 0.100\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$

Statement II is correct.

Finally, ethanoic acid is a weak acid that only partially dissociates in water. As neutralization occurs, part of the energy must be used to ionize the undissociated ethanoic acid. The neutralization of a weak acid does not release as much energy as the neutralization of a strong acid.  As a result, the maximum temperature reached is expected to be lower. Statement III is correct.

This question is categorized as hard as the student must identify differences between strong and weak acids and interpret the thermometric titration in detail.
