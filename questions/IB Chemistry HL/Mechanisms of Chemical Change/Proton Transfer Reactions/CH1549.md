# CH1549

The autoionization constant for water depends on temperature. 

Which of the statements below is correct for a system at 50$^\circ$C?

$\textrm{p}K_w^{50^\circ \textrm{C}} = 13.27$

- I.$\hspace{1em}\textrm{pH} = 6.80$ corresponds to a basic solution.

- II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Pure water has $\textrm{pH} =  7.00.$

- III.$\hspace{0.4em}\textrm{pH} = 7.80$ corresponds to a basic solution.

- A.$\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

- B.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

 The student must understand that $\textrm{p}K_w$ and the $\textrm{pH}$ scale vary with temperature. Neutrality corresponds to 

$\textrm{pH} = \dfrac{\textrm{p}K_w}{2} = \dfrac{13.27}{2}$ = 6.635 

so statement II is incorrect as $\textrm{pH} = 7.00$ corresponds to neutrality at 25 $^\circ$C only.

At 50  $^\circ$C, any $\textrm{pH}$ value below 6.635 will be acidic, and any above it is basic. Statements I and III are correct.

This question is categorized as a medium as the student must understand that $\textrm{p}K_w$ depends on temperature, and so does the $\textrm{pH}$ scale.

$\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\

The autoionization of water is described by the equilibrium

$\ce{H2O(l)} + \ce{H2O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \ce{H3O+(aq)} + \ce{OH-(aq)}$

The equilibrium constant $K_w$ can be expressed as the product of the hydroxide and hydronium concentrations:

$\ K_w = [\ce{H3O+}][\ce{OH-}]$ 

In pure water, the amount of hydroxide is equal to the amount of hydronium: for every water molecule that donates a proton (forming hydroxide ion), another must accept that proton (forming hydronium).  Simplifying the expression for $K_w$, we get

$K_w = [\ce{H3O+}]^2$

To solve, we can take the negative logarithm of both sides, recall that $-\log K_w = \textrm{p}K_w$ and $-\log[\ce{H3O+}] = \textrm{pH}$, we get

$- \log K_w = -\log  [\ce{H3O+}]^2$

$- \log K_w = - 2 \log  [\ce{H3O+}]$

$\textrm{p}K_w = 2 \textrm{\ pH}$

In pure water the $\textrm{pH}$ will be half of the $\textrm{p}K_w$, or 6.635.
