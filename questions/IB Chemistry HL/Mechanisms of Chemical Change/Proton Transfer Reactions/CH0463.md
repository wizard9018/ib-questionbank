# CH0463

Which of the following solutions, when mixed with equal volumes at equal concentrations, form a buffer solution?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HCN} \text{ and } \ce{NaCN}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{HCN} \text{ and } \ce{NH3}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{H3PO4} \text{ and } \ce{NH3}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{H3PO4} \text{ and } \ce{NaCN}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

An acidic buffer solution contains a weak acid and the salt of its conjugate base in approximately equal amounts.

$\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

Option B has equal amount of a weak acid and a weak conjugate base. Traditional buffer solutions have a conjugate acid/base pair and this solution does not.  For an acidic buffer, the pH of the buffer system should be less than 7. This system might have some buffering capacity but ammonia is a stronger base than HCN is an acid so it would be the most effective around a pH of 9.

Option C is incorrect because when equal amounts of phosphoric acid and ammonia are mixed, all of the ammonia will be converted into ammonium ion. An acid buffer requires approximately equal amounts of an acid and its conjugate base and that is not present here.

Option D is incorrect because all of the cyanide will be converted to HCN.

Buffers are usually made by adding equal parts of an acid and its conjugate base or by adding a 2 : 1 ratio of a weak acid : strong (monobasic) base or a 2 : 1 ratio of a weak base : strong (monoprotic) acid.
