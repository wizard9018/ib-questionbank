# CH1200

What is the \[H$^+$\], in $\textrm{mol\ dm}^{-3}$, of a 0.0100 $\textrm{mol\ dm}^{-3}$ solution of calcium hydroxide at 25 $^\circ \textrm{C}$?

- A.$\hspace{1em}1.00 \times  10^{-12}$

- B.$\hspace{1em}1.00 \times  10^{-2}$

- C.$\hspace{1em}2.00 \times  10^{-2}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}5.00 \times  10^{-13}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The calcium hydroxide formulae and its dissociation in water are shown below:

$\ce{Ca(OH)2} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ OH^-}\textrm{(aq)}$

$\ce{Ca(OH)2}$ is a strong base that dissociates completely in water. The hydroxide ion concentration is twice the concentration of the base.

$[\ce{OH^-}] = 2 \times 0.0100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} = 0.0200 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$

The autoionization of water is governed by the equation

$K_w$= \[H$^{+}$\] \[OH$^-$\]

which relates the concentration of hydroxide ions to the concentration of $\ce{H+}$ ions. At 298 K,
rearranging the equation and plugigng in values gives

$[\ce{H^{+}}] = \dfrac{K_w}{[\ce{OH^-}]} = \dfrac{1.00 \times 10^{-14}} {0.0200} = 5.00 \times 10^{-13} \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$
