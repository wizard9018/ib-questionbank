# CH1305

What is the correct order of increasing strength for these $0.100\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ solutions?

  | | | |
  | :-: | :-- |:-:
  | Acid X(aq) |$\qquad$ |$K_a=4.30 \times 10^{-4}$ |
  | Acid Y(aq) || $\textrm{p}K_a=2.30$ |
  | Acid Z(aq) || $K_a=9.30 \times 10^{-6}$ |

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Z < X < Y

- B.$\hspace{1em}$Y < X < Z

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Z < Y < X

- D.$\hspace{1em}$X < Y < Z

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The strength of an acid is related to its ability to dissociate into its ions in water.

The greater the acidic constant, the stronger the acid. Greater $K_a$ means more product of dissociation against acid left.

$\ce{HA(aq) <=> H+(aq) + A-(aq)}$

$K_a = \dfrac{\ce{[H+][A-]}}{\ce{[HA]}}$

$\textrm{p}K_a = -\log\,K_a.$ Therefore, $K_a = 10^{-\textrm{p}K_a}.$

For acid Y, as the $\textrm{p}K_a = 2.30$ and lies between 2 and 3, we can estimate that its $K_a$ is between $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}.$

This $K_a$ is the greatest out of all, meaning that acid Y is the strongest acid. The weakest acid is Z, as its $K_a$ is the smallest. The increasing order is Z < X < Y.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must understand the relationship between $K_a$ and $\textrm{p}K_a$ to compare and arrange in increasing strength order.
