## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Hydrobromic acid is a strong acid with a weak conjugate base, and ammonia a weak base with a strong conjugate acid. At the equivalence point, therefore, the pH is below 7.

Sodium hydroxide is a strong base, so has a weak conjugate acid. When it reacts with hydrobromic acid, a strong acid, the pH at the equivalence point is approximately 7.

The two reactions involve the same acid solution, so the initial pH is the same. When the base is in excess, however, the strong base produces a higher hydroxide concentration and a higher pH than the weak base.
