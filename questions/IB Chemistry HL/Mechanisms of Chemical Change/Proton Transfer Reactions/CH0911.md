# CH0911

When an acid **P** is titrated against aqueous $\ce{NaOH}$, the following pH curve is obtained.

![18-911](images/18-911.png)

Which of the following statements are true?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}

    | | |

    | :- | :- |

    | I. | The $pK_a$ of **P** is higher than the $pK_a$ of $\ce{H2SO4}$ |

    | II. | The buffer region occurs when the pH is between 4.0 and 6.0 |

    | III. | The $pK_a$ of **P** is equal to the pH when the titrant volume is 40 cm$^3$ |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The sharp rise in a pH curve occurs around the equivalence point, where the concentrations of the acid and base species are equal. At the equivalence point, the strong base $\ce{NaOH}$ has been converted to the weak conjugate acid $\ce{H2O}$.

The pH at the equivalence point is higher than $7$, so the conjugate base of **P** must be relatively strong and **P** must be a weak acid.

Weaker acids have higher $pK_a$ values, so it is true that the $pK_a$ of **P** is higher than that of the strong acid $\ce{H2SO4}$ (sulfuric acid).

The buffer region of the pH curve is the flat region over which added $\ce{NaOH}$ produces only a small change in pH. For this curve, a buffer region is observed over the pH range $4.0 - 6.0$, as stated.

The $pK_a$ of **P** is equal to the pH at the half-equivalence point, or the point at which the acid is half-dissociated. The titrant volume of 40 cm$^3$ corresponds to the point at which **P** has fully reacted with $\ce{NaOH}$, so the half-equivalence point must occur when the titrant volume is smaller.
