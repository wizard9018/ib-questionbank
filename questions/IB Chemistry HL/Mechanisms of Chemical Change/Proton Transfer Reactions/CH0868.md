# CH0868

How could you distinguish between equal concentrations of $\ce{HCl}$ and $\ce{HNO2}$ solutions?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | React with calcium oxide and observe the rate of formation of gas bubbles |
    | II. | Connect a lamp and battery in a circuit using the solutions and observe the brightness of the lamp |
    | III. | React with sodium carbonate and monitor the mass loss |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

$\ce{HCl}$ is a strong acid, whereas $\ce{HNO2}$ is a weak acid.

Calcium oxide reacts with acids to form salt and water. No gas is evolved, so the rate of formation of bubbles can't be used to distinguish between the solutions. Therefore, statement I is not valid.

A strong acid fully dissociates in water, whereas a weak acid only partially dissociates in water. This means a solution of $\ce{HCl}$ would be more conductive than a solution of $\ce{HNO2}$, and the lamp would glow brighter. Therefore, statement II is valid.

Unlike calcium oxide, sodium carbonate reacts with acids to form a gas - carbon dioxide, along with a salt and water. This means that we would monitor the loss of mass from gas being released from the reaction vessel. $\ce{HCl}$ would have a faster rate of reaction than $\ce{HNO2}$. Therefore, statement III is valid.
