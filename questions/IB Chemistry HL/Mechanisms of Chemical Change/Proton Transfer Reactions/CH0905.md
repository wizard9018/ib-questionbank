# CH0905

The variation in pH during an acid-base titration is shown in the graph below:

![18-905](images/18-905.png)

Which of the following indicators would be most suitable for this titration?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Methyl violet $(pK_a = 0.8)$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Methyl orange $(pK_a = 3.7)$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Bromothymol blue $(pK_a = 7.0)$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Bromophenol blue $(pK_a = 11.2)$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

An indicator is a weak acid that changes colour when it dissociates. The colour change is most visible at its half-equivalence point, where the acid and its conjugate base are equal in concentration.

The colour change occurs in the range pH $= pK_a \pm 1$.

An indicator for titration should be chosen to change colour at the equivalence point, where the acid in the sample is fully consumed, and the pH of the solution increases sharply. The equivalence point occurs near pH $7.0$ in this titration, so bromothymol blue is the most suitable indicator.
