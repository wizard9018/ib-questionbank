# CH1595

Consider the following acid$-$base equilibrium

$\ce{H2BO3- + H2O <=> HBO3^2- + H3O+}\qquad K_c = 5 \times 10^{-12}$

Which option best describes the species in the equation above.

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}

    | | Conjugate base | Acid strength |  

    | :-- | :-: | :-:|  

    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{HBO3^2-}$ |$\ce{H3O+}$ is weaker than $\ce{H2BO3-}$ |

    | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{HBO3^2-}$ |$\ce{H2BO3-}$ is weaker than $\ce{H3O+}$ | 

    | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{H2O}$ |$\ce{H2BO3-}$ is weaker than $\ce{H3O+}$ |

    | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | $\ce{H2O}$ |$\ce{H3O+}$ is weaker than $\ce{H2BO3-}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

According to the Bronsted$\textrm{--}$Lowry theory of acids and bases, an acid is a donor of protons while a base is an acceptor of protons. Applying this to the equation given in the question:

$\ce{H2BO3- + H2O <=> HBO3^2- + H3O+}$

-   $\ce{H2BO3-}$ loses a proton to form $\ce{HBO3^2-}.$ Hence, it is an acid. The conjugate base of an acid is formed when it loses a $\ce{H+}.$ Therefore, $\ce{HBO3^2-}$ is the conjugate base of $\ce{H2BO3-}.$

-   The equilibrium constant $K_c$ gives information about the extent of dissociation of the acid. A small value of $K_c = 5 \times  10^{-12}$ indicates that dissociation of $\ce{H2BO3-}$ is very limited, as the reaction is strongly reactant favored, and thus it is a very weak acid.

-   The reverse reaction, where hydronium acts as the acid would have an equilibrium constant of $1/K_c = 1/(5 \times 10^{-12}) = 2  \times 10^{11}.$ This large value indicates that hydronium is a much stronger acid, as it is very effective at donating a proton.
