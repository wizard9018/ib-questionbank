# CH0474

**Maximum mark: 6**

A student titrates a 10.0 cm$^3$ aliquot of balsamic vinegar against 0.500 mol dm$^{-3}$ sodium hydroxide, $\ce{NaOH}$, producing the following titration curve.

![18.3-CH0474](images/CH0474.png)

1.  Label points A and B on the titration curve above. [1 mark]

2.  The acidity of vinegars is measured by determining the mass of ethanoic acid in 100 cm$^3$ of vinegar solution $(\%w/v)$.

    The volume of $\ce{NaOH}$ at point B is 21.3 cm$^3$.

    $\ce{CH3COOH(aq) + NaOH(aq) -> CH3COONa(aq) + H2O(l)}$

    1.  Calculate the number of moles of ethanoic acid in the 10.0 cm$^3$ aliquot. [1 mark]

    2.  Calculate the acidity in $\%w/v$ of the balsamic vinegar. [2 marks]

3.  Predict the $\ce{pH}$ at point A and explain your reasoning, for ethanoic acid the p$K_a = 4.76.$ [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  A: half$-$equivalence point **AND** B: equivalence point $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    The equivalence point occurs when the stoichiometrically correct ratio of moles of acid and base have reacted. The half$-$equivalence point occurs when half of the required number of moles of acid and base have reacted.

2.  1.  $n(\ce{NaOH}) = C \times V = 0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \times 0.0213 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.0107 \,\text{mol}$\
        $n(\ce{CH3COOH}) = n(\ce{NaOH}) = 0.0107\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        The volume of $\ce{NaOH}$ at the equivalence point represents when the ratio of $\ce{NaOH}$ to $\ce{CH3COOH}$ is $1:1$.

    2.  in 100 cm$^3$, $n(\ce{CH3COOH})= 0.0107 \textrm{\ mol} \times 10 = 0.107\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$\
    $\%w/v = 0.107 \times 60.06 = 6.43\, \%w/v\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
        $\%w/v =$ mass of ethanoic acid in 100 cm$^3$\
        $\ce{M(CH3COOH)} = (12.01)(2) + (1.01)(4) + (16.00)(2) = 60.06 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}$

3.  at half$-$equivalence,  p$K_a = \textrm{pH} \: \checkmark$\
    pH is approximately $4.76$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    At the half$-$equivalence point, half of the ethanoic acid has been neutralised so there is an equal concentration of ethanoic acid and the ethanoate ion, i.e. $K_a=[\ce{H+}]$.

    $K_a=\dfrac{\cancel{\ce{[CH3COO-]}}[H+]}{\cancel{\ce{[CH3COOH]}}}$.
