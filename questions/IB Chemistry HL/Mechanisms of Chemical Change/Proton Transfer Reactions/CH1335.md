# CH1335

**Maximum mark: 14**

Vinegar is a dilute aqueous solution of ethanoic acid, $\ce{CH_3COOH}.$ The composition of a commercial vinegar is regularly tested to ensure that it is being manufactured consistently.

The ethanoic acid concentration is determined by titrating a sample of the vinegar with aqueous sodium hydroxide. Some of the variables in this experiment are listed in the table.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| Variable | Symbol | Units |

| --- | :-: | :-: |

| Volume of base added at endpoint | $V_B$ | $\text{cm}^3$        |

| Concentration of base solution    | $C_B$ | $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ |

| Moles of acid in sample           | $n_A$ | $\text{mol}$         |

| Concentration of acid in sample   | $C_A$ | $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ |

1.  State which variable in the table is a **dependent** variable, giving a reason for your answer. [2 marks]

2.  Three samples of vinegar were prepared using the apparatus listed in the table.

    | Sample | Measuring Apparatus | Precision | Sample Quantity |

    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |

    | 1 | Balance | $\pm 0.01\,\text{g}$ | $1\,\text{g}$ |

    | 2 | Volumetric pipette | $\pm 0.002\,\text{cm}^3$ | $0.1\,\text{cm}^3$ |

    | 3 | Measuring cylinder | $\pm 0.1\,\text{cm}^3$ | $20\,\text{cm}^3$ |

    $\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

    1.  State why it is **not** possible to determine, from the information in the table, which sample of vinegar was prepared with the greatest **accuracy**. [1 mark]

    2.  Determine, by means of a calculation, which sample of vinegar was prepared with the smallest **percentage uncertainty**. [2 marks]

    3.  The samples are analyzed by acid-base titration to determine the value of $n_A.$

Explain why it is **not** possible to calculate the value of $C_A$ for **all** samples listed in the table. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $V_B$ «volume of base at the end point» is the dependent variable $\checkmark$ \

    it is the quantity measured during the titration / its value depends on the acid and base used $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

    $V_B$ is read off at the end point and follows from the other variables, $V_B = n_A / C_B.$ The acid concentration $C_A$ is a fixed property of the vinegar, and $n_A$ is set by the sample size chosen, so both are independent.

    

2.   1.  systematic error of each apparatus is not given $\checkmark$

            $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

        The table gives only the precision, the random error, of each instrument. Accuracy also depends on systematic error, how far the scale reads from the true value, which is not stated, so the most accurate sample cannot be identified.

        2.  $\%\ \textrm{uncertainty} = \dfrac{\textrm{precision}}{\textrm{quantity}} \times 100\%$ $\checkmark$ \

        Sample 1 $= 1\%,$ Sample 2 $= 2\%,$ Sample 3 $= 0.5\%;$ Sample 3 is the smallest $\checkmark$

            $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

            Percentage uncertainty divides the apparatus precision by the amount measured. 

            $\textrm{Sample 1:}\ \dfrac{0.01  \textrm{\ g}}{1  \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% = 1\%$

            $\textrm{Sample 2:}\ \dfrac{0.002  \textrm{\ cm}^3}{0.1  \textrm{\ cm}^3} \times 100\% = 2\%$

            $\textrm{Sample 3:}\ \dfrac{0.1  \textrm{\ cm}^3}{20  \textrm{\ cm}^3} \times 100\% = 0.5\%$

            Although the measuring cylinder is the least precise instrument, the large $20 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ sample makes its percentage uncertainty the smallest.

        3.  the concentration of Sample 1 cannot be calculated $\checkmark$ \

        its volume is unknown because the density is not given $\checkmark$

            $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

        Concentration is $C_A = n_A / V_{\textrm{sample}}.$ Samples 2 and 3 were measured by volume, but Sample 1 was measured by mass; converting that mass to a volume needs the density, which is not given, so its concentration in $\textrm{mol dm}^{-3}$ cannot be found.

3.  

    1.  any **one** of the following:

        $\mbox{}\\[-10pts]$

        -   swirling / step 2 ensures acid and base are well mixed $\checkmark$

        - concentration of (acid / base in the analyte) is uniform $\checkmark$

        -   all of the base reacts while acid is still available $\checkmark$

        -   color change (of the indicator) is observed only when all of the acid has reacted $\checkmark$

         $\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

        rinsing returns solution splashed onto the walls to the analyte, so it reacts / prevents $V_B$ being underestimated $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

        Swirling keeps the mixture homogeneous, so the indicator colour reflects the whole solution and changes only when all the acid has reacted. Rinsing washes any acid on the walls back into the solution, so it reacts with the base and the titre is not underestimated.

    2.  adding water does not change $n_A$ / endpoint depends on the amount of acid, not its concentration $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

        Water dilutes the analyte but leaves the amount of acid, $n_A,$ unchanged, so the same volume of titrant is still required to reach the end point.

4.

    1.  $V_B = 18.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ $\checkmark$ \

        $n_A = 4.5 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$ $\checkmark$

        *Award M1 for a volume in the range 17.8$-$18.2 $\textrm{cm}^3.$*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

        At the equivalence point the pH rises steeply; reading the middle of the jump gives $V_B = 18.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3.$ Since $\ce{NaOH}$ and $\ce{CH3COOH}$ react in a $1 : 1$ ratio, 

        $n_A = n_B = \dfrac{18.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3}{1000 \textrm{\ cm}^3\textrm{\ dm}^{-3}} \times 0.250\textrm{\ mol} = 4.5 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}.$

    2.  $C_B$ should be lowered $\checkmark$ \

        this increases $V_B,$ so the fixed reading uncertainty is a smaller percentage of the titre $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\

        The uncertainty in reading $V_B$ is fixed «about $\pm 0.1 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ off the graph». Since $V_B = n_A / C_B,$ a more dilute titrant gives a larger $V_B,$ so the same absolute uncertainty is a smaller fraction of it.
