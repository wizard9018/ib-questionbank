# CH0419

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the ionization of water:

 $\ce{H2O (l) <=> H+ (aq) + OH-(aq)}$

|T / K | K$_\mathbf{w}$ |

|:-:|:-:|

298  | $1.00 \times 10^{-14}$

318 | $4.02 \times 10^{-14}$

1.  

    1.  State whether the forward reaction is endothermic or exothermic. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain what happens to the concentration of $\ce{H+}$ ions when the temperature increases. [2 marks]

2.  

    1.  Calculate the pH of pure water at $45\,{}^\circ$C. [2 marks]

    

    2.  Justify why pure water is still considered neutral at $45\,{}^\circ$C. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  

    1.  endothermic $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\

        Bond breaking requires energy and so is an endothermic process.  Also, $K$ increases as temperature increases so the reaction must be endothermic.

    2.  $[\ce{H+}]$ increases $\checkmark$\

        forward reaction favored / $K$ increases so [products] increases $\checkmark$ 

       

2.  

    1.  $[\ce{H+}] = \sqrt{K_w} = \sqrt{4.02 \times 10^{-14}} = 2.00 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}\,\checkmark$\

        $\textrm{pH} =- \log[\ce{H+}] =-  \log(2.00 \times 10^{-7}) = {6.70}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\

        For pure water, $[\ce{H+}] = [\ce{OH-}]$ so $[\ce{H+}] = \sqrt{K_w}$.

    2.  $[\ce{H+}] = [\ce{OH-}],$ neutral $\checkmark$
