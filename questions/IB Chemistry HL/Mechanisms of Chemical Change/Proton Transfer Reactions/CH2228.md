# CH2228

Which of these acids has the strongest conjugate base?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Ethanoic acid, $\text{p}K_a = 4.76$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Propanoic acid, $\text{p}K_a = 4.88$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Benzoic acid, $\text{p}K_a = 4.20$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Chloroethanoic acid, $\text{p}K_a = 2.87$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

When comparing conjugate bases, it is essential to recall that the strength of a base is inversely related to the strength of its conjugate acid. This can be expressed using the relationship $K_a K_b = K_w$ which models the inverse relationship between the acid and its conjugate base.

The quantitative measure of acid strength here is $\text{p}K_a,$ which is the negative logarithm of the acid dissociation constant, $K_a.$ A lower $\text{p}K_a,$ corresponds to a stronger acid, while a higher $\text{p}K_a,$ corresponds to a weaker acid.

Looking at the options, chloroethanoic acid with a $\text{p}K_a,$ of 2.87 is the strongest acid, and therefore its conjugate base is the weakest. The high $\text{p}K_a,$ value of propanoic acid indicates it is the weakest acid. Therefore, its conjugate base is the strongest base among the choices.

This is a medium difficulty question because it requires students not only to remember the definition of $\text{p}K_a,$ and its relationship to $K_a,$ but also to apply the principle that the weaker the acid, the stronger its conjugate base.
