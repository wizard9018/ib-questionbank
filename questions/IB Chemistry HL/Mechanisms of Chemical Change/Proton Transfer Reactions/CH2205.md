# CH2205

Which substance is a base but not an alkali?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{MgO}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{NaOH}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{KOH}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{Ba(OH)2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

A base is any species that can accept a proton or neutralise an acid. An alkali is a base that is sufficiently soluble in water to generate a significant concentration of hydroxide ions in solution. Sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide and barium hydroxide all dissolve readily, producing aqueous $\ce{OH-};$ they are therefore alkalis as well as bases.

Magnesium oxide is a basic oxide: it neutralises acids by accepting protons, for example

$\ce{MgO(s) + 2 HCl(aq) -> MgCl2(aq) + H2O(l)}$

When it comes into contact with water it forms magnesium hydroxide, but the hydroxide is so sparingly soluble that the resulting concentration of $\ce{OH-}$ is negligible:

$\ce{MgO(s) + H2O(l) -> \underset{\large insoluble}{Mg(OH)2(s)}}$

Because it does not appreciably increase the concentration of the hydroxide ion, MgO is regarded as a base but not an alkali.
