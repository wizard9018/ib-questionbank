# CH0870

Which of the following statements about amphoteric species is **incorrect**?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$The conjugate base of a diprotic acid is an amphiprotic species.

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$All amphiprotic species are amphoteric.

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$Amphiprotic species require a negative charge and a proton which can be donated.

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$The conjugate base of a triprotic acid is an amphiprotic species.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\
A diprotic acid can undergo two dissociations in water. This means that the conjugate base of the first dissociation can lose a proton in the second dissociation and act as a Bronsted-Lowry acid or gain a proton to reform the diprotic acid and act as a Bronsted-Lowry base classifying it as amphiprotic, so option A is true.

An amphoteric species can react with either an acid or a base, whereas an amphiprotic species can act as a Bronsted-Lowry acid or Bronsted-Lowry base. This makes amphiprotic species a subset of amphoteric species; therefore, option B is true.

While many amphiprotic species contain a negative charge and a proton that can be donated, water is an example of a neutral amphiprotic species, so option C is incorrect.

A triprotic acid can undergo three dissociations in water. Just like diprotic acids, this means that the conjugate base of the first dissociation can lose a proton in the second dissociation and act as a Bronsted-Lowry acid or gain a proton to reform the triprotic acid and act as a Bronsted-Lowry base classifying it as amphiprotic, so option D is true.
