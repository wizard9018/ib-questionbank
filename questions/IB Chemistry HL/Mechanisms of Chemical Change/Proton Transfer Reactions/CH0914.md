# CH0914

**Maximum mark: 8**

Boric acid, $\ce{B(OH)3}$, reacts with water. Two possible products of this reaction are compounds **A** and **B**.

The structural formulas of these compounds are shown.

![CH0914_Image1](images/CH0914_Image1.png)

1.  Explain why boric acid acts as a Lewis acid but **not** a Brønsted-Lowry in the formation of compound **A**. [2 marks]

2.  Compound **B** can dissociate to regenerate boric acid, as shown.

    $\ce{B(OH)4^-(aq) <=> B(OH)3(aq) + OH-(aq)}$

    The $K_b$ of compound **B** is $1.412\times 10^{-5}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$.

    1.  Write an expression to show how the $K_b$ value is calculated from the concentrations of the species in solution. [1 mark]

    2.  The solubility product of water, $K_w$, is $1.0\times 10^{-14}\,\text{mol}^2\,\text{dm}^{-6}$ at 20 $^\circ$C. Using this value of $K_w$ and the $K_b$ of compound **B**, calculate the $pK_a$ of boric acid. [3 marks]

3.  A third product, compound **C**, is formed when boric acid acts as a Brønsted-Lowry acid. In this reaction, each molecule of boric acid reacts with **one** molecule of water.

    Draw the structural formula of compound **C**. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  (Boric acid acts as a Lewis acid in this reaction because) it forms a dative / coordination bond (with water) OR it accepts a lone pair of electrons (from water) $\checkmark$

    (Boric acid does not act as a Brønsted-Lowry acid in this reaction because) it does not donate a hydrogen ion / $\ce{H+}$ ion / proton $\checkmark$

    *Do not award M2 if a definition is given but the behaviour of boric acid is not described.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    A Lewis acid is a molecule that accepts a lone pair of electrons from another species, a Lewis base.

    The resulting covalent bond is termed a dative bond and is sometimes labelled as an arrow in the structural formula, to indicate which atom is donating the bonding electron pair.

    Boric acid is a Lewis acid because the B atom forms a dative bond with the O atom of water.

    A Brønsted-Lowry acid is a molecule that donates a hydrogen ion to another species, via the breaking of a polar covalent bond in the molecule such as $\ce{O-H}$, $\ce{N-H}$ or $\ce{Cl-H}$. Boric acid contains $\ce{O-H}$ bonds, but these do not break in the formation of **A**.

2.
    1.  $K_b = \dfrac{\ce{[OH-][B(OH)3]}}{\ce{[B(OH)4-]}}\,\checkmark$

        *Accept [boric acid] in place of $\ce{[B(OH)3]}$; \
        Accept [**B**] or similar in place of $\ce{[B(OH)4^-]}.$*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

        $K_b$, the base dissociation constant, indicates how much compound **B** dissociates into boric acid and hydroxide ions. The higher the value of $K_b$, the further the position of equilibrium is to the right and the more dissociation takes place.

        $K_b$ is calculated by multiplying the concentrations of the products, hydroxide and boric acid, and dividing by the concentration of compound **B** remaining.

    2.  $K_w = K_a\times K_b$ **OR** $pK_w = pK_a + pK_b$ $\checkmark$

        $K_a = \dfrac{10^{-14}}{(1.412\times 10^{-5})} = 7.08(2)\times 10^{-10}\,\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ **OR** $pK_b = -\log_{10}(1.412\times 10^{-5}) = 4.85(0)\,\checkmark$

        $pK_a = -\log_{10}(7.08\times 10^{-10}) = 9.1(5)$ **OR** $pK_a = 14.00 - 4.85 = 9.1(5)\,\checkmark$

        *Accept Ka values stated without units; \
        Accept correct rearranged versions of expressions for $K_w$ or $pK_w$; \
        Award M2 if workings are shown without final value of $K_{a}$ or $pK_b$; \
        Award M3 for correct $pK_a$ with no working shown; \
        Award M3 if correct method is shown but $pK_a$ is incorrect due to an explicit follow-through error in $pK_b$ or $K_a$; \
        Award [2] for correct answer if it is not produced by the working given, e.g. if $pK_b$ is used but not explicitly calculated; \
        Do **not** award M2 for correct $K_a$ or $pK_b$ with no working shown.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

        $K_w$ indicates how much water dissociates into $\ce{H+}$ (or $\ce{H3O+}$) and $\ce{OH-}$ ions.

        If $\ce{[H+]}$ increases, $\ce{[OH-]}$ must decrease to keep the product $K_w = \ce{[H+]\times [OH-]}$ the same, and vice versa.

        Since $K_a = \dfrac{\ce{[A-][H+]}}{\ce{[HA]}}$ and $K_b = \dfrac{\ce{[HA][OH-]}}{\ce{[A-]}}$ for an acid HA, $K_a\times K_b = \ce{[H+]\times [OH-]} = K_w$.

        Rearranging this equation allows $K_a$ to be calculated, and $pK_a$ is then given by $-log^{10}\ K_a$. The steps of this calculation can be performed in a different order.

        Taking logarithms of the equation for $K_w$ gives $pK_w = -\log_{10} K_w = -\log_{10}(K_a\times K_b) = -\log_{10} K_a - \log_{10} K_b = pK_a + pK_b$, as the logarithm of a product equals the sum of logarithms of the individual terms. Thus, $pK_a = pK_w - pK_b$.

3.  B bonded to two OH groups and one O atom only $\checkmark$

    Negative charge drawn on an O atom lacking an $\ce{O-H}$ bond $\checkmark$

    ![CH0914_Image2](images/CH0914_Image2.png)

    *Accept OH groups drawn without explicit $\ce{O-H}$ bonds, provided bond from B to O is clear; \
    Ignore explicit bonding and lone electron pairs; \
    Ignore molecular geometry; \
    Do **not** award M1 if B-O bonds are omitted or unclear; \
    Do **not** award M2 if position of negative charge is ambiguous.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    Boric acid reacts as a Brønsted-Lowry acid by donating a hydrogen ion. For this to occur, an $\ce{O-H}$ bond must undergo heterolysis. This bond breaking process produces a positive ion, $\ce{H+}$, and a negative ion, $\ce{(OH)2 B - O^-}$, with the negative charge positioned on the O atom of the broken $\ce{O-H}$ bond.

    Boric acid contains three $\ce{O-H}$ bonds that could break, but removing a second hydrogen ion is more difficult as it is strongly attracted to the negatively charged ion. At low concentration, most molecules react with only one equivalent of water and donate one hydrogen ion, as stated in the question.
