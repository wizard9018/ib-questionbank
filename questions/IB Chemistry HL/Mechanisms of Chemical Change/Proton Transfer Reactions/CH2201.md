# CH2201

A weak base has a $\textrm{p}K_b$ value of 9.9. A buffer solution is prepared by mixing $100 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $1.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ of this weak base with $25.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3$ of $1.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ hydrochloric acid.
What is the pH of the resulting buffer solution?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}3.6$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}4.6$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}9.4$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}10.4$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Begin by calculating the amounts of acid and base present after the solutions have mixed.

$n_\ce{acid} = CV = 1.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^3 \times 0.025 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.025 \textrm{\ mol}$

$n_\ce{base} = CV = 1.00 \textrm{\ mol dm}^3 \times 0.100 \textrm{\ dm}^3 = 0.100 \textrm{\ mol}$

The weak base provides 0.100 mol, while the hydrochloric acid contributes 0.0250 mol. The strong acid neutralizes part of the base, producing an equal number of moles of the conjugate acid.

Reaction: | $\ce{B}$ | $+$ |  $\ce{H3O+} $ |$\longrightarrow$ | $\ce{HB+}$ |  $+$ |  $\ce{H2O}$
:--|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
$\textbf{Initial:}$ | 0.100||0.025
$\textbf{Final:}$ | 0.075 ||||0.025

This leaves 0.0750 mol of weak base and 0.0250 mol of conjugate acid in the mixture. The solutions will be on the basic side of the half$-$equivalence point where $\textrm{pH} = \textrm{p}K_a.$

For a buffer solution, the Henderson$-$Hasselbalch relationship is used in the form:

$\textrm{pOH} = \textrm{p}K_b + \log \dfrac{[\textrm{conjugate acid}]}{[\textrm{base}]}$

Substituting values gives:

$\textrm{pOH} = 9.9 + \log \dfrac{0.025}{0.075}$

Estimating the values, the pOH will be less than 9.9 so the pH will be more than 4.1.  $\textrm{pH} = 4.6$ is the best answer. Using a calculator, we find $\textrm{pOH} ≈ 9.4.$ Converting to pH using $\textrm{pH} + \textrm{pOH} = 14$ gives $\textrm{pH} = 14 - 9.4 = 4.6.$
