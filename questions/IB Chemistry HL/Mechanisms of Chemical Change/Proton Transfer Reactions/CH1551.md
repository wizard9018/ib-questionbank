# CH1551

Which combination is correct for the titration of 100.0 cm$^3$ propanoic acid $(\textrm{p}K_a = 4.87)$ with 0.100 mol dm$^{-3}$ of aqueous sodium hydroxide if 56.0 cm$^3$ were needed to reach the endpoint at 25$^\circ$C?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\

 The student must understand the titration of a weak acid with a strong base in detail.

At the equivalence point, the neutralization of propanoic acid is complete.

$\ce{CH_3CH_2COOH}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{NaOH}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{CH_3CH_2COONa}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H_2O}\textrm{(l)}$

At this point, the amount of propanoic acid and strong base are the same as the neutralization reaction has a $1:1$ stoichiometry ratio.

$\ n_{acid} = n_{base}$

$\ C_{acid}V_{acid} = C_{base}V_{base}$

The initial concentration of propanoic acid can be deduced by rearranging the above:

$\ C_{acid} = \dfrac {C_{base}V_{base}}{V_{acid}} = \dfrac {56.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3 \times 0.100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}}{100.0 \textrm{\ cm}^3}$

In a weak acid$\text{--}$strong base titration, the $\textrm{pH} = \textrm{p}K_a$ at the half$\text{--}$equivalence point, or when half of the weak acid has been neutralized to from its conjugate base. This point occurs when the volume of strong base added is half the volume needed to reach the equivalence point (in this case, 28.0 cm$^3$).

Finally, neutralization produces a basic salt. As $\ce{CH_3CH_2COONa}$ is formed, the anion can undergo a chemical reaction in water, producing $\ce{OH^-(aq)}.$

$\ce{CH_3CH_2COONa}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H_2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{CH_3CH_2COOH}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{Na^+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{OH^-}\textrm{(aq)}$

As a result, the pH at the equivalence point is basic (meaning above 7.00 at 25 $^\circ$C).

This question is categorized as hard as the student must recognize several features of the titration of a weak acid with a strong base: the definition of equivalence point,  the fact that $\textrm{pH} = \textrm{p}K_a$ at the half$\text{--}$equivalence point, the location of the half equivalence point, and the pH at equivalence due to the formation of a weak conjugate base.
