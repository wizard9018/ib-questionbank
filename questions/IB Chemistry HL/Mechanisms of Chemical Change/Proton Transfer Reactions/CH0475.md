# CH0475

**Maximum mark: 6**

One example of an important buffer system is the $\ce{H2CO3} / \ce{HCO3-}$ buffer in our blood.

1.
    1.  Define buffer solution. [2 marks]
    2.  Using relevant equations, describe how the $\ce{H2CO3} / \ce{HCO3-}$ buffer works. [2 marks]

A student has solutions of 0.5 mol dm$^{-3}$ $\ce{NaOH, HCl, H2CO3}$ and $\ce{NaHCO3}$.

2.  State two ways the $\ce{H2CO3} / \ce{HCO3-}$ buffer can be made from these solutions. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  A solution that is able to resist changes in $\ce{pH}; \,\checkmark$

        when small amounts of acid or base are added. $\checkmark$

    2.  When acid is added, the additional hydronium ions react with the $\ce{HCO3-}$ ions and are used up:

        $\ce{HCO3-}(aq)+\ce{H3O+}(aq)\ce{->H2CO3}(aq)+\ce{H2O}(l) \\
        \textbf{OR}\\
        \ce{HCO3-}(aq)+\ce{H+}(aq)\ce{->H2CO3}(aq) \,\checkmark$

        When base is added, the additional hydroxide ions react with the $\ce{H2CO3}$ and are used up:

        $\ce{H2CO3}(aq)+\ce{OH-}(aq)\ce{->HCO3-}(aq)+\ce{H2O}(l)\,\checkmark$

        *Alternative approach is to use Le Chatelier's principle with* $\ce{H2CO3(aq) + H2O(l) <=> HCO3-(aq) + H3O+(aq)} ;$

        Addition of acid shifts equilibrium to the left to restore equilibrium; $\checkmark$

        Addition of base shifts equilibrium to the right to restore equilibrium. $\checkmark$

2.  React a certain volume of $\ce{H2CO3}$ with half the volume of $\ce{NaOH};\, \checkmark$

    React/Mix an equal volume of $\ce{NaHCO3}$ and $\ce{H2CO3}.\, \checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    An acidic buffer is made up of a weak acid and the salt of its conjugate base. This can be achieved by either mixing the acid and salt in equal proportions or half neutralising the weak acid with a strong base.
