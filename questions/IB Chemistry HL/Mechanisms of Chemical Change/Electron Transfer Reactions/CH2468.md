# CH2468

Consider the following equations for metals X, Y, Z, and W.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\mathbf{Equation}$ | $\mathbf{E^{\minuso}\,/\,\textbf{V}}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| $\ce{X}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,Y+}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{X^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,Y}\textrm{(s)}$ | $+2.00$ |
| $\ce{Z}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{W^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Z^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{W}\textrm{(s)}$ | $+1.15$ |
| $\ce{W}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,Y+}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{W^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,Y}\textrm{(s)}$ | $+0.38$ |

Which statement is correct?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}E^{\minuso} = 0.47\textrm{ V}$ for $\ce{X}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{Z^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{X^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{Z}\textrm{(s)}$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Y is the least reactive metal.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$X is the strongest oxidising agent.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and III only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The target reaction $\ce{X + Z^{2+} \longrightarrow X^{2+} + Z}$ can be built by combining the three given equations, much like a Hess's law cycle. Take equation 1 as written, reverse equation 3, and reverse equation 2:

$\ce{X}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,Y+}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{X^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,Y}\textrm{(s)}\qquad E^{\minuso} = +2.00\textrm{ V}$

$2\ce{\,Y}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{W^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,Y+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{W}\textrm{(s)}\qquad E^{\minuso} = -0.38\textrm{ V}$

$\ce{W}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{Z^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{W^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{Z}\textrm{(s)}\qquad E^{\minuso} = -1.15\textrm{ V}$

The $\ce{Y}$ and $\ce{W}$ species cancel, leaving $\ce{X + Z^{2+} \longrightarrow X^{2+} + Z}.$ All three reactions transfer two electrons, so the cell potentials add directly:

$E^{\minuso} = (+2.00) + (-0.38) + (-1.15) = +0.47\textrm{ V}$

Statement I is correct.

In each reaction, the metal on the left (the one being oxidised) is more reactive. From the three equations: X displaces Y, Z displaces W, and W displaces Y. Combining these gives the reactivity order X > Z > W > Y (most to least reactive). Y is the least reactive metal. Statement II is correct.

The strongest oxidising agent is the species most easily reduced. More reactive metals are stronger reducing agents, not oxidising agents. X is the most reactive metal and therefore the strongest reducing agent. The strongest oxidising agent is $\ce{Y+},$ which has the greatest tendency to be reduced. Statement III is incorrect.
