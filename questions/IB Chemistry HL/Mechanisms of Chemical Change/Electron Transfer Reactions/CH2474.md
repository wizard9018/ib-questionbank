# CH2474

The iron$-$nickel (Edison) battery is a rechargeable cell used in some industrial applications. During discharge, the overall reaction is:

$\ce{Fe}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,NiO(OH)}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{Fe(OH)2}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,Ni(OH)2}\textrm{(s)}$

1. Identify the species that is oxidised during discharge. [1 mark]

2.  Deduce the half$-$equation for the reaction occurring at the anode during discharge. [1 mark]

3. Deduce the half$-$equation for the reaction occurring at the cathode during discharge. [1 mark]

4. State the half$-$equation occurring at the cathode during charging. [1 mark]

5. Explain why a rechargeable battery must involve a reversible reaction, while a primary cell does not require this. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\ce{Fe}$ / iron $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Iron is oxidised from 0 to $+2$ during discharge. In the overall equation, $\ce{Fe}\textrm{(s)}$ appears as a reactant and $\ce{Fe(OH)2}\textrm{(s)}$ as a product, confirming that iron loses electrons. This makes the iron electrode the anode (negative terminal).

2.  $\ce{Fe}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Fe(OH)2}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}}\:\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The electrolyte is alkaline ($\ce{KOH}$), so $\ce{OH^{-}}$ ions are available to react with the iron. The oxidation state of iron changes from $0$ to $+2$, releasing two electrons. The equation balances for mass (1 Fe, 2 O, 2 H on each side) and charge ($2{-}$ on the left from the two $\ce{OH^{-}}$, $2{-}$ on the right from the two $\ce{e^{-}}$).

3.  $\ce{NiO(OH)}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} + \ce{e^{-}} \longrightarrow \ce{Ni(OH)2}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)}\: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Nickel is reduced from $+3$ in $\ce{NiO(OH)}$ to $+2$ in $\ce{Ni(OH)2}$, gaining one electron per nickel atom. The overall equation shows two moles of $\ce{NiO(OH)}$ reacting, which accounts for the two electrons released by iron in part (b). The nickel electrode is the cathode (positive terminal) during discharge.

4. $\ce{Ni(OH)2}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{NiO(OH)}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} + \ce{e^{-}} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    During charging, an external voltage forces the reverse of the discharge reactions. The discharge cathode reaction is reversed: $\ce{Ni(OH)2}$ is oxidised back to $\ce{NiO(OH)}$. Notice that during charging the electrode labels swap. The nickel electrode becomes the anode (oxidation) and the iron electrode becomes the cathode, where the reverse reaction $\ce{Fe(OH)2}\textrm{(s)} + 2\,e^{-} \longrightarrow \ce{Fe}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)}$ regenerates iron metal.

5.  external voltage reverses the cell reaction, regenerating the original reactants $\checkmark$\
    irreversible reaction cannot be restored; applied voltage would not regenerate reactants $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    In a primary cell such as a zinc$-$carbon battery, the structural changes during discharge (electrode dissolution, gas formation, irreversible crystal growth) mean the products cannot be reconverted to reactants by reversing the current. A secondary cell is engineered so that the electrode reactions proceed cleanly in both directions without significant structural degradation. The Edison battery is particularly robust in this regard, which is why it remains in use for heavy$-$duty industrial applications despite its lower energy density compared to lithium$-$ion cells.
