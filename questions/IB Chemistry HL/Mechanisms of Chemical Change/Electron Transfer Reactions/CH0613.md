2.  1.  Hydrogen gas and oxygen gas. $\checkmark$

    2.  Hydrogen gas and chlorine gas. $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        With aqueous electrolytes, $\ce{H2O}$ is also present and can be oxidized at the anode or reduced at the cathode preferentially to $\ce{Na+}$ or $\ce{Cl-}$.

        At the cathode:

        $\ce{Na+(aq) + e- -> Na(s)} \,E^\ominus = -2.71\,\text{V}$\
        ***OR***\
        $\ce{H2O(l) + e- -> \dfrac{1}{2} H2(g) + OH-(aq)}\,E^\ominus = -0.83\,\text{V}$

        The $\ce{H2O}$ is more likely to be reduced due to a more positive electrode potential resulting in $\ce{H2}$ being the major product.

        At the anode:

        $\ce{Cl-(aq) -> \dfrac{1}{2} Cl2(g) + e-}\,E^\ominus = -1.36\,\text{V}$\
        ***OR***\
        $\ce{H2O(l) -> \dfrac{1}{2} O2(g) + 4 H+(aq) + 2 e-}\,E^\ominus = -1.23\,\text{V}$

        The $\ce{H2O}$ is more likely to be oxidized due to a less negative electrode potential resulting in $\ce{O2}$ as the major product.

        When concentrated $\ce{NaCl}(aq)$ is used, $\ce{Cl-}$ is oxidized preferentially to $\ce{H2O}$ resulting in $\ce{Cl2}$ being the major product instead of $\ce{O2}$.
