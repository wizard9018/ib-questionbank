# CH1598

Which option best describes the voltaic cell in the diagram below where silver is less reactive than chromium?

![CH1598](images/CH1598.JPEG)

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Anode | Cathode | Movement of positive ions in salt bridge |
    | :-- | :-: | :-:| :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Cr(s) | Ag(s) | Towards $\ce{Cr^3+(aq)}$ |
    | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Ag(s) | Cr(s) | Towards $\ce{Cr^3+(aq)}$ |
    | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Cr(s) | Ag(s) | Towards $\ce{Ag+(aq)}$ |
    | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Ag(s) | Cr(s) | Towards $\ce{Ag+(aq)}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

In a voltaic cell, oxidation occurs at the anode, and reduction occurs at the cathode. The more reactive metal, chromium, undergoes oxidation, becoming the anode, while the less reactive metal, silver, undergoes reduction, becoming the cathode.

-   Anode (oxidation): $\ce{Cr(s) -> Cr^3+(aq) + 3 e-}$

- Cathode (reduction): $\ce{Ag+(aq) + e- -> Ag(s)}$

The salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality by allowing ions to flow between the half$\text{--}$cells:

-   Cations move toward the cathode $(\ce{Ag+})$ to balance the buildup of negative charge.

-   Anions move toward the anode $(\ce{Cr^3+})$ to balance the positive charge buildup.

Thus, in this cell, chromium is the anode, silver is the cathode, and positive ions in the salt bridge move towards the silver half$\text{--}$cell (the cathode).
