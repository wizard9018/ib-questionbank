1. *Any one of the following.*

 - Decorative purposes / improving appearance $\checkmark$

 - Corrosion control $\checkmark$

 - Enhanced properties of materials $\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\

 Electroplating consists of plating an object with a metal by electrodeposition. The object to be plated is placed at the cathode, and electrolysis is performed. The electrolyte must contain the cations of the metal to be plated. A layer of metal covers the cathode as the process occurs. This can enhance the properties of the plated object, preventing its corrosion, improving wear resistance or aesthetic enhancement. Common examples include galvanized steel (zinc covered steel) which prevents rusting and gold$\textit{--}$plated jewelry.

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\

 Electrodeposition occurs in an electrolytic cell. The metal to be plated must be in the electrolyte, and a source of it must be at the anode (in this case, copper). The object to be coated must be at the cathode (in this case, tin). An external source of energy must be present to allow electron flow, as it must drive the non-spontaneous redox reaction, allowing electrons to flow from the anode to the cathode.

 2. Anode: $\ce{Cu(s) -> Cu^2+(aq) + 2 e-}\ \checkmark$\

 Cathode: $\ce{Cu^2+(aq) + 2 e- -> Cu(s)}\ \checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\

 At the anode, copper gets oxidized, and $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions are formed, whereas at the cathode, $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions are reduced onto the object (and produce a layer on it). The concentration of $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions in the solution is kept constant as the rate of consumption of these ions is the same as their rate of formation. The blue colour of the electrolyte is kept constant as electrodeposition occurs.

 3. $n_\ce{e-} = 0.0280\text{\ mol}\ \checkmark$\

 $n_\ce{Cu} = 0.0140\text{\ mol}\ \checkmark$ \

 $m_\ce{Cu} = 0.890\text{\ g}\ \checkmark$\

 Award [3] for final answer.

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\

 The amount of copper reduced at the cathode can be deduced by determining the charge, as shown below.

 $Q = It = (1.50\text{\ A})\left(\dfrac{1\text{\ C s}^{-1}}{1\text{\ A}}\right)(30\text{\ min})\left(\dfrac{60\text{\ s}}{1\text{\ min}}\right) = 2700\text{\ C}$

 The total charge can be used to calculate the number of electrons that passed through the cell using Faraday's constant which relates $1\text{\ mol e}^-$ to 96500 C. The amount of electrons is

 $n_\ce{e-} = \dfrac{2700\text{\ C}}{96500\text{\ C mol}^{-1}} = 0.0280\text{\ mol}$

 The reduction of copper is

 $\ce{Cu^2+(aq) + 2e- -> Cu(s)}$

 so two electrons are needed for every copper.

 $n_\ce{Cu} = \dfrac{1}{2} n_\ce{e-} = \dfrac{0.0280\text{\ mol}}{2} = 0.0140\text{\ mol}$

 Using the molar mass, the mass of copper formed can be determined:

 $m_\ce{Cu} = (0.0140\text{\ mol})(63.55\text{\ g mol}^{-1}) = 0.890\text{\ g}$

 4. copper anode is consumed/size of copper rod reduced $\checkmark$\

 colour of the cathode changes (from gray to reddish) $\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\

 The copper anode is consumed and oxidized as the electroplating proceeds. As a result, its size will be decreasing. At the cathode, deposition occurs as copper is reduced. The result is that copper covers the tin cathode. Tin is gray, whereas copper colour is reddish. As a result, a colour change at the cathode is expected (from gray to metallic red). The solution will not change colour as the copper metal is oxidized at the same rate that it is plated onto the tin electrode.

 2. Similarity: *Any one of the following.*

 - Oxidation occurs at the anode/Reduction occurs at the cathode $\checkmark$

 - Electron flow from anode to cathode $\checkmark$

 Difference: *Any one of the following.*

 - The anode is negative in a voltaic cell and positive in an electrolytic cell $\checkmark$

 - The cathode is positive in a voltaic cell and negative in an electrolytic cell $\checkmark$

 - Electrolytic cells convert electrical energy to chemical energy, and voltaic cell converts chemical energy to electrical energy $\checkmark$

 - Voltaic cells have two separate solutions connected through a salt bridge, and electrolytic cells have one solution/electrolyte without a salt bridge $\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$\

 Voltaic cells occur in different half$\text{--}$cells connected through a salt bridge, and a spontaneous reaction occurs. In electrolytic cells, an external source of energy drives the nonspontaneous reaction.
