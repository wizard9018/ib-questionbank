# CH2467

Consider the electrolysis of concentrated aqueous potassium bromide using inert electrodes. Which statement is correct?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Potassium metal is deposited at the cathode.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Water is oxidised at the anode to produce oxygen gas.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Water is reduced at the cathode to produce hydrogen gas.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Bromide ions are oxidised at the anode to produce oxygen gas.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Concentrated aqueous potassium bromide contains $\ce{K+}$ ions, $\ce{Br-}$ ions, and water. At each electrode, the question is whether a solute ion or water is discharged.

At the cathode, water is reduced in preference to $\ce{K+}$ because the reduction potential for water $(E^{\minuso} = -0.83\textrm{ V})$ is far higher than that of potassium $(-2.93\textrm{ V}).$ Hydrogen gas is produced:

$2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \longrightarrow \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,OH-}\textrm{(aq)}$

Potassium metal cannot be produced from aqueous solution. $\ce{K+}$ ions remain in solution as spectators at the cathode.

At the anode, the $E^{\minuso}$ for $\ce{Br2/Br-}$ $(+1.07\textrm{ V})$ is lower than that for $\ce{O2/H2O}$ $(+1.23\textrm{ V}),$ so bromide is more easily oxidised than water:

$2\ce{\,Br-}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Br2}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-}$
