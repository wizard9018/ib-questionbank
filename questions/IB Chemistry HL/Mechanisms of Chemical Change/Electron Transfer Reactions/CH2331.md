# CH2331

In the electrolysis apparatus shown, two cells are connected in series. $0.64 \textrm{\ g}$ of copper is deposited on the cathode of the first cell.

![CH2331](images/CH2331.png)

What is the mass of silver deposited on the cathode of the second cell?

-    A.$\hspace{1.em}0.54 \textrm{\ g}$

-    B.$\hspace{1.em}0.64 \textrm{\ g}$

-    C.$\hspace{1.em}1.08 \textrm{\ g}$

-    D.$\hspace{1.em}2.16 \textrm{\ g}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
When electrolytic cells are connected in series, the same current passes through both cells for the same duration. This means the same total charge, $Q,$ is delivered to each cell.

First, determine the amount of copper deposited. The molar mass of copper is $63.55 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}:$

$n_\ce{Cu} = \dfrac{0.64}{63.55} \approx  0.01 \textrm{\ mol}$

Copper is deposited by the reduction of $\ce{Cu^{2+}},$ which requires two electrons per ion:

$\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$

The total moles of electrons transferred is therefore:

$n_\ce{e^{-}} = 2 \times 0.01 = 0.02 \textrm{\ mol}$

In the second cell, silver is deposited by the reduction of $\ce{Ag^{+}},$ which requires only one electron per ion:

$\ce{Ag^{+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e^{-}} \longrightarrow \ce{Ag}\textrm{(s)}$

Since the same charge passes through both cells, $0.02 \textrm{\ mol}$ of electrons deposits $0.02 \textrm{\ mol}$ of silver. The molar mass of silver is $107.87 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}:$

$m(\ce{Ag}) = 0.02 \times 107.87  \approx 2.16 \textrm{\ g}$

The key insight is the stoichiometric difference: copper requires two electrons per atom deposited, while silver requires only one. For the same total charge, twice as many moles of silver are deposited compared to copper, and because silver also has a higher molar mass, the mass of silver deposited is significantly greater.

This question is categorised as hard. The student must recognise that cells in series share the same current, determine the moles of electrons from the copper half$-$equation, and apply the different stoichiometry of the silver half$-$equation.
