# CH2473

**Maximum mark: 6**

An alkaline hydrogen$-$oxygen fuel cell is used to provide electrical power on a spacecraft.

1. Write the half$-$equation for the reaction occurring at the anode. [1 mark]

2. Write the half$-$equation for the reaction occurring at the cathode. [1 mark]

3. Deduce the overall equation for the fuel cell reaction. [1 mark]

4. Explain why a hydrogen fuel cell is more efficient than a combustion engine at converting the chemical energy stored in hydrogen into useful work. [2 marks]

5. State one way in which a fuel cell differs from a rechargeable (secondary) battery. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $2\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow 4\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} + 4\ce{\,e^{-}} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    Hydrogen is the fuel and is oxidised at the anode. In alkaline conditions, $\ce{OH^{-}}$ ions from the electrolyte act as the reactant rather than water or $\ce{H^{+}}$. Each $\ce{H2}$ molecule loses two electrons, giving four electrons for two molecules. The $\ce{OH^{-}}$ consumed here is regenerated at the cathode, so the electrolyte is not depleted.

2.  $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} + 4\ce{\,e^{-}} \longrightarrow 4\ce{\,OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    Oxygen is reduced at the cathode, gaining the four electrons released at the anode. In alkaline conditions the reduction product is $\ce{OH^{-}}$ rather than $\ce{H2O}$. The half$-$equation must balance for both mass and charge: four $\ce{OH^{-}}$ ions carry a total charge of $4{-}$, matching the four electrons on the left.

3.  $2\ce{\,H2}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    Adding the two half$-$equations cancels the $\ce{OH^{-}}$, $\ce{H2O}$ on the left, and the electrons. The overall equation is identical to hydrogen combustion, but the fuel cell achieves this without a flame by physically separating the two half$-$reactions.

4.  *combustion engine:* converts chemical $\rightarrow$  thermal  $\rightarrow$ mechanical energy, losing energy at each stage $\checkmark$ \
    *fuel cell:* converts chemical $\rightarrow$ electrical energy, less loss so more energy captured for work $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    In a combustion engine, chemical energy is first converted to heat, and then heat is converted to mechanical work. Energy is lost at each conversion step, particularly as waste heat to the surroundings. A fuel cell skips the thermal stage entirely: the redox reaction drives electron flow directly through an external circuit. With fewer conversion steps, less energy is dissipated and a greater fraction reaches the intended output.

5. fuel cell requires continuous external supply of fuel and oxidant, whereas rechargeable battery contains its reactants internally $\checkmark$\
    **OR**\
    fuel cell cannot be recharged by reversing the current, whereas a secondary battery can $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    A fuel cell is neither primary nor secondary. It operates indefinitely as long as fuel flows. A primary cell is discarded after discharge; a secondary cell is recharged by applying an external potential to reverse the electrode reactions. A fuel cell does neither.
