# CH2252

The table below gives the standard reduction potentials of three half$-$cells involving iron:

|Reduction Reaction| $\mathbf{E^\mathbf{\minuso}}$/V
:-:|:-:
$\ce{Fe(phen)_3^{3+}} + \ce{e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Fe(phen)_3^{2+}}$ | $+1.15$
$\ce{Fe^{3+}} \ce{e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Fe^{2+}}$ | +0.77
$\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{3-}} + \ce{e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}$ | +0.36

where phen represents the organic compound 1,10$-$phenanothroline, $\ce{C_{12}H_8N_2}.$ From the data, under standard conditions, which of the following statements is correct?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ is a stronger reducing agent than $\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}.$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}\ce{Fe(phen)_3^{3+}}$ ions are stronger oxidizing agents than $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ ions.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}$ ions can reduce $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ ions.

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To determine whether a species is a stronger reducing agent, we consider how easily it is oxidized, which corresponds to the reverse of its reduction potential. A lower (less positive) standard reduction potential indicates a greater tendency to lose electrons and be oxidized, and therefore a stronger reducing agent. Since $\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{3-}/Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}$ has a standard potential of $+0.36 \textrm{\ V}$ and $\ce{Fe^{3+}/Fe^{2+}}$ is $+0.77 \textrm{\ V},$ $\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}$ is more easily oxidized than $\ce{Fe^{2+}}$ and thus is the stronger reducing agent. Statement I is incorrect.

Oxidizing agents are evaluated based on their ability to gain electrons, meaning species with higher (more positive) standard reduction potentials are stronger oxidizing agents. With a potential of $+1.15 \textrm{\ V},$ $\ce{Fe(phen)_3^{3+}}$ is more easily reduced than $\ce{Fe^{3+}},$ which has a potential of $+0.77 \textrm{\ V}.$ This makes $\ce{Fe(phen)_3^{3+}}$ the stronger oxidizing agent. Statement II is true.

To determine if this reaction can occur spontaneously, we need to calculate the cell potential. $\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}$ has a lower reduction potential than $\ce{Fe^{3+}},$ so it can act as the reducing agent (anode) while $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ acts as the oxidizing agent (cathode). The net cell potential for the reaction

$\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}} + \ce{Fe^{3+}} \longrightarrow  \ce{Fe(CN)_6^{3-}} + \ce{Fe^{2+}}$

is calculated as

$E^{\minuso}_\ce{cell} = E^{\minuso}_\ce{red}\ce{(cathode)}-E^{\minuso}_\ce{red}\ce{(anode)} = +0.77 - (+0.36) = +0.41 \textrm{\ V}$

Since this value is positive, the reaction is thermodynamically favorable under standard conditions, confirming that $\ce{Fe(CN)_6^{4-}}$ can indeed reduce $\ce{Fe^{3+}}$ ions. Statement III is true.

The key principles are that stronger oxidizing agents have higher reduction potentials, stronger reducing agents have lower reduction potentials, and spontaneous reactions have positive cell potentials.
