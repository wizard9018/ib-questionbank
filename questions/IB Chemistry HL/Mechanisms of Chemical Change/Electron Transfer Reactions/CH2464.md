# CH2464

What is the mass of copper, in g, produced by electrodeposition when a charge of $5760\textrm{ C}$ is passed through a solution of aqueous copper(II) sulfate?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$0.0149

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$0.0298

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$1.90

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$3.80

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The reduction half$-$reaction at the cathode is:

$\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$

The number of moles of electrons transferred is found from the charge and Faraday's constant:

$n({e^-}) = \dfrac{Q}{F} = \dfrac{5760\textrm{ C}}{96500\textrm{ C mol}^{-1}} = 0.0597\textrm{ mol}$

Each mole of copper requires two moles of electrons, so:

$n({\ce{Cu}}) = \dfrac{n({e^-})}{2} = \dfrac{0.0597 \textrm{\ mol}}{2} = 0.0298\textrm{ mol}$

The mass of copper deposited is:

$m = n \times M = 0.0298\textrm{ mol} \times 63.55\textrm{ g mol}^{-1} = 1.90\textrm{ g}$
