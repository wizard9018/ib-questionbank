# CH2713

**Maximum mark: 11**

1. A student built a concentration cell from two copper half$-$cells connected by a salt bridge (Figure 1). The right$-$hand half$-$cell contained $1.000 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3} \ce{\,CuSO4}\textrm{(aq)};$ the concentration in the left$-$hand half$-$cell, $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}},$ was varied between trials. The cell potential was measured at $298 \textrm{\ K}$ with a high$-$impedance voltmeter.

    ![CH2915](images/CH2915.png)
    **Figure 1:** Concentration cell.

    For a concentration cell, the cell potential is given by:

    $E_{\textrm{cell}} = \dfrac{2.303\,RT}{nF} \log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{high}}}{[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}}}\right)$

    where $n$ is the number of electrons transferred per copper ion, $R$ is the ideal gas constant, $T$ is the temperature in Kelvin, and $F$ is Faraday's constant.

    | $\mathbf{Trial}$ | $\mathbf{[Cu^{2+}]_{low}\textbf{ / mol dm}^{-3}}$ | $\mathbf{log_{10}([Cu^{2+}]_{high}/[Cu^{2+}]_{low})}$ | $\mathbf{E_{cell}\textbf{ / V} \pm 0.005}$ |
    :-:|:-:|:-:|:-:
    | 1 | 1.000 | 0.000 | 0.001 |
    | 2 | 0.500 |  | 0.009 |
    | 3 | 0.100 | 1.000 | 0.030 |
    | 4 | 0.0100 | 2.000 | 0.059 |
    | 5 | 0.00100 | 3.000 | 0.084 |

    **Table 1:** Measured cell potential as a function of the concentration ratio at $298 \textrm{\ K}.$

    $\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

    1. Deduce, with a reason, which electrode is the anode, and state the direction of electron flow in the external circuit in Figure 1. [2 marks]

    2. Write the half$-$equation, including state symbols, for the reaction occurring at the anode. [1 mark]

    3. State the function of the salt bridge in Figure 1. [1 mark]

    4. Calculate the missing value in Table 1. [1 mark]

2. The results were plotted, with a line of best fit drawn through Trials 1$-$4.

    ![CH2713a](images/CH2713a.png)

    **Graph 1:** Cell potential *vs.* $\log_{10}([\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{high}}/[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}}),$ with a line of best fit through Trials 1$-$4.

    $\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

    1. Calculate the theoretical gradient of the line predicted by the equation at $298 \textrm{\ K}.$ Use section 2 of the IB Chemistry data booklet. [2 marks]

    2. Determine the experimental gradient of the line of best fit in Graph 1. [2 marks]

    3. Comment on whether Trials 1$-$4 support the equation. [1 mark]

    4. The point for Trial 5 lies below the line of best fit. Suggest one reason why the actual concentration of $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ in the most dilute solution may have been higher than its nominal value. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1. anode is left/ dilute half$-$cell \
        **AND** \
        copper is oxidized where $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]$ is lower /  cell acts to equalize the concentrations / low $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]$ shifts $\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$ to the left $\checkmark$\
        from the dilute half$-$cell (left) to the concentrated half$-$cell (right) $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Both half$-$cells contain the same equilibrium, $\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}.$ In the dilute solution the lower concentration of $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ shifts this equilibrium to the left, so copper atoms are oxidized and release electrons; in the concentrated solution $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions are reduced and take up electrons. The cell therefore runs in the direction that makes the two concentrations more equal: copper dissolves on the left, raising $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}},$ and copper is deposited on the right, lowering $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{high}}.$ Oxidation occurs at the anode, so the left$-$hand electrode is the anode. Electrons released there travel through the wire and voltmeter to the right$-$hand electrode, where they are used in reduction.

    2. $\ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^-} \: \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        At the anode, copper metal is oxidized. Each copper atom loses two electrons and enters the solution as a $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ion, so the anode loses mass as the cell operates.

    3. maintains electrical neutrality in the half$-$cells / completes the circuit by allowing ions to flow $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        As the anode solution gains $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ and the cathode solution loses it, each half$-$cell would build up net charge and the cell would stop within moments. Ions from the salt bridge migrate into each half$-$cell to cancel this charge build$-$up, allowing sustained current without mixing the two solutions.

    4. $0.301$ $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        In Trial 2 the right$-$hand half$-$cell contains $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{high}} = 1.000 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}$ and the left$-$hand half$-$cell contains $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}} = 0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$ The concentration ratio is

        $\dfrac{[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{high}}}{[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}}} = \dfrac{1.000 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}}{0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}} = 2.00$

        The ratio has no units, because the units cancel. Taking the logarithm to base 10 gives

        $\log_{10}(2.00) = 0.301$

2.
    1. $n = 2$ $\checkmark$ \
        gradient $= 0.0295 \textrm{\ V}$ $\checkmark$

        *Award [2] for correct final answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The equation for the cell potential has the form of a straight line through the origin, $y = mx:$

        $\underbrace{E_{\textrm{cell}}}_{y} = \underbrace{\dfrac{2.303\,RT}{nF}}_{m} \times \underbrace{\log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{high}}}{[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}}}\right)}_{x}$

        so the gradient of the graph is $\dfrac{2.303\,RT}{nF}.$ Each copper atom is oxidized to $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ by losing two electrons, $\ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^-},$ so $n = 2.$ Substituting $R$ and $F$ from section 2 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, with $T = 298 \textrm{\ K}:$

        $\textrm{gradient} = \dfrac{2.303 \times 8.31 \textrm{\ J K}^{-1}\textrm{ mol}^{-1} \times 298 \textrm{\ K}}{2 \times 96\,500 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}} = \dfrac{5703 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1}}{193\,000 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}} = 0.0295 \textrm{\ V}$

        The units simplify because $1 \textrm{\ J C}^{-1} = 1 \textrm{\ V}.$ The gradient is the change in cell potential for each tenfold increase in the concentration ratio.

    2. two points far apart on the line of best fit $\checkmark$ \
        gradient $= 0.029 \textrm{\ V}$ $\checkmark$

        *Accept values from 0.028 to 0.030 V.*

        ![CH2713ms_v2](images/CH2713ms_v2.png)

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The gradient is found from two points on the line of best fit, chosen far apart, such as $(0.50,\ 0.015)$ and $(2.70,\ 0.079):$

        $\textrm{gradient} = \dfrac{\Delta E_{\textrm{cell}}}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{0.079 \textrm{\ V} - 0.015 \textrm{\ V}}{2.70 - 0.50} = 0.029 \textrm{\ V}$

        The points must be read from the line, not taken from the table. The line is drawn through all four trials, so it averages out the random error in each measurement, while a single data point carries its full error. Points far apart give a large rise and run, so the small error in reading each value from the graph has less effect on the gradient.

    3. equation is supported $\checkmark$\
        points are linear **AND**  gradient agrees with the theoretical value within experimental uncertainty  $\checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The equation predicts a straight line through the origin with gradient $\dfrac{2.303\,RT}{nF}.$

        -   the points for Trials 1$-$4 lie on a straight line against $\log_{10}(\textrm{ratio}),$ so $E_{\textrm{cell}}$ is proportional to $\log_{10}(\textrm{ratio})$
        -   the experimental gradient agrees with the theoretical gradient:

        $\mbox{}\\[-25pt]$

        $\dfrac{0.0295 \textrm{\ V} - 0.029 \textrm{\ V}}{0.0295 \textrm{\ V}} \times 100\% = 1.7\%$

        Both features are needed to support the equation. A straight line shows that the form of the equation is correct, and the matching gradient shows that the value of $\dfrac{2.303\,RT}{nF}$ is correct.

    4. any **one** of the following:\
        $\mbox{}\\[-22pts]$

        -   the copper electrode dissolves/corrodes, adding $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions $\checkmark$
        -   $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions diffuse through the salt bridge from the concentrated half$-$cell $\checkmark$
        -   the glassware or water was contaminated with $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$  $\checkmark$
        -   water evaporated from the dilute solution $\checkmark$

        $\mbox{}\\[-20pts]$
        *The reason must increase the concentration of copper(II) ions.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        At $0.00100 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3},$ even a tiny stray amount of $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ is a significant fraction of the nominal concentration; the same contamination would be negligible at $0.500 \textrm{\ mol dm}^{-3}.$ A higher effective $[\ce{Cu^{2+}}]_{\textrm{low}}$ shrinks the concentration ratio, so the measured potential falls below the prediction, as Graph 1 shows.
