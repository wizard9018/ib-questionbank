# CH2461

Consider the standard electrode potentials below.

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\mathbf{Half{\text{--}}reaction}$ | $\mathbf{E^{\minuso}\,/\,\textbf{V}}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| $\ce{Li+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Li}\textrm{(s)}$ | $-3.04$ |
| $\ce{Na+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Na}\textrm{(s)}$ | $-2.71$ |
| $\ce{Ni^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Ni}\textrm{(s)}$ | $-0.26$ |
| $\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$ | $+0.34$ |

Which metal does not react with dilute aqueous hydrochloric acid?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Cu

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Ni

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Na

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Li

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
A metal reacts with dilute acid when the overall cell potential for the reaction is positive. The relevant half$-$reactions are:

$\ce{M}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{M^{n+}}\textrm{(aq)} + n\ce{\,e-}\quad$ (oxidation of the metal)

$2\ce{\,H+}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \longrightarrow \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)}\quad E^{\minuso} = 0.00\textrm{ V}$

For the reaction to be spontaneous, $E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}} = E^{\minuso}_{\text{cathode}} - E^{\minuso}_{\text{anode}}$ must be positive. Since the cathode half$-$cell is the SHE at $0.00\textrm{ V},$ any metal with a negative $E^{\minuso}$ will give a positive $E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}}$ and react with the acid.

Copper has $E^{\minuso} = +0.34\textrm{ V}.$ As the anode: $E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}} = 0.00 - (+0.34) = -0.34\textrm{ V}.$ The negative cell potential means the reaction is not spontaneous, so copper does not dissolve in dilute hydrochloric acid.

Li, Na, and Ni all have negative $E^{\minuso}$ values, so each gives a positive $E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}}$ when paired with $\ce{H+/H2}$ and reacts with the acid to produce hydrogen gas.
