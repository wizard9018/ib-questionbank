# CH1554

Consider the electrolysis of sodium chloride. Which combination states the correct product formed at the cathode in each electrolysis experiment?:br 

$\\[10pts]$

|$\hspace{0.8em}$| |Molten NaCl(l)| Diluted NaCl(aq)|Concentrated NaCl(aq)|

|:--| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-:| 

||A. | Sodium | Hydrogen | Sodium|

||B. | Chlorine | Oxygen |Chlorine |

||C. | Sodium | Hydrogen |Hydrogen |

||D.|Chlorine|Hydrogen |Chlorine|

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

The student must understand the different types of electrolysis.

In the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride, sodium ions ($\ce{Na+}$) are reduced at the cathode, forming solid sodium metal, while chloride ions ($\ce{Cl^-}$) are oxidized at the anode, producing chlorine gas ($\ce{Cl2}$). 

However, the situation changes when dealing with an aqueous solution of sodium chloride. In this case, water is present and can participate in the reactions. At the cathode, both sodium ions and water molecules compete to be reduced. Because water has a higher reduction potential, hydrogen ions ($\ce{H^+}$) from water are reduced to form hydrogen gas ($\ce{H2}$), rather than sodium being reduced. This occurs in both dilute and concentrated NaCl solutions due to the significant difference in reduction potentials between hydrogen ions and sodium ions. At the anode, the species oxidized depends on the concentration of the NaCl solution. In a concentrated solution, chloride ions are oxidized to form chlorine gas. In contrast, in a dilute solution, water is more likely to be oxidized, producing oxygen gas ($\ce{O2}$). 

This question is categorized as a medium, as the student must be able to contrast the different types of electrolysis and the products formed in each.
