# CH0105

What happens to nitrogen when nitrate forms nitrite, $\ce{NO2^-}$? 

- A.$\hspace{1em}$It undergoes oxidation and its oxidation state changes from +4 to +5

- B.$\hspace{1em}$It undergoes oxidation and its oxidation state changes from +2 to +4

- C.$\hspace{1em}$It undergoes reduction and its oxidation state changes from +5 to +3

- D.$\hspace{1em}$It undergoes reduction and its oxidation state changes from +4 to +2

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

Let $x$ be the oxidation state of N in each ion.  We know that the sum of the oxidation states must equal the overall charge on the ion and we know that oxygen typically has an oxidation state of $-2$ when it is combined in a compound or ion:

- In nitrate, $\ce{NO3-}$:

 $x +3 (-2) = -1$

 

  Therefore, $x = +5$

- In nitrite, $\ce{NO2-}$ , 

$x +2 (-2) = -1$

 Therefore, $x = +3$

 Since the oxidation state of nitrogen is decreasing, nitrogen is being reduced.
