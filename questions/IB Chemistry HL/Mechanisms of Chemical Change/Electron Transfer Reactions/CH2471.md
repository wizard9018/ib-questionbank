# CH2471

Which statement correctly compares electrochemical cells?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$In both voltaic cells and fuel cells, the anode is the positive terminal.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$In an electrolytic cell, the cathode is the site of reduction and is connected to the negative terminal of the power supply.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$A fuel cell differs from a voltaic cell because the fuel cell requires an external voltage to drive a non$-$spontaneous reaction.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$In a voltaic cell, the electrolyte must be replaced after each use because the ions are consumed at the electrodes.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Reduction always occurs at the cathode, regardless of cell type. In an electrolytic cell, the cathode is connected to the negative terminal of the external power supply, which forces electrons onto the electrode and drives the reduction half$-$eaction.

In voltaic cells and fuel cells, the anode is the negative terminal. The anode is positive only in electrolytic cells.

Fuel cells, like voltaic cells, use a spontaneous reaction to generate electricity. The key difference is that reactants are continuously supplied from an external source rather than stored inside the cell.

In a voltaic cell,  at the anode, the metal is oxidised and enters the solution as cations, making the anode compartment increasingly positive. Anions from the salt bridge migrate toward the anode compartment to offset this buildup. At the cathode, cations are reduced and removed from solution, so cations from the salt bridge migrate into the cathode compartment. The anion flow is toward the anode, not the cathode.
