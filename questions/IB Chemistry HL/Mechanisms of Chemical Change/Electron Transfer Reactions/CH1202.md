# CH1202

Consider the chemical reaction below

$\ce{Mn}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\ AgNO3}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Mn(NO3)2}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ Ag}\textrm{(s)} $

and the following tabulated values:

$E^{\minuso}({\text{Ag}}^+/\text{Ag})=+0.80 \textrm{\ V} \\
E^{\minuso}({\text{Mn}}^{2+}/\text{Mn})= -1.18 \textrm{\ V}$

$F = 9.65 \times 10^4 \textrm{\ C  mol}^{-1}$

Which combination is correct at 25 $^\circ$C?

| | $\mathbf{E_{cell}^{\minuso}}$ / V | $\mathbf{\Delta G^{\minuso}}$ /  kJ mol$^\mathbf{-1}$ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\hspace{1em}$A.$\hspace{1em}$| 1.98 | $-$ 382 |
|$\hspace{1em}$B.$\hspace{1em}$ | 1.98 | 382 |
| $\hspace{1em}$C.$\hspace{1em}$ | 2.78 | $-$ 537 |
| $\hspace{1em}$D.$\hspace{1em}$ | 2.78 | 537 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The single replacement reaction can be broken down into half equations,  ignoring the nitrate ion as a spectator.\
Oxidation half$-$equation:

$\ce{Mn}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ e^-}$

Reduction half$-$equation:

$\left[\ce{Ag^+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{Ag}\textrm{(s)}\right]    \times 2$

Overall:

$\ce{Mn}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\ Ag+}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Mn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \ce{\ Ag}\textrm{(s)} $

The measured cell potential can be determined as the difference in the reduction potential at the cathode and the anode:

$E_{cell}^{\minuso}=\ E^{\minuso}_{cathode}-E^{\minuso}_{anode}$

$E_{cell}^{\minuso} = +0.80 \textrm{\ V} - (-1.18 \textrm{\ V}) = +1.98 \textrm{\ V}$

The change in the Gibbs free energy can be deduced from the standard cell potential using the relation:

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = -nFE^{\minuso}_{cell} $

where $n = 2$ is the number of electrons transferred in the redox process and $F$ is Faraday's constant.  Most importantly, for a spontaneous process the cell potential is positive and the Gibbs free energy is negative; no values are required to select the best option.

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = -2 \times 9.65 \times 10^4 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1} \times 1.98 \textrm{\ V} $

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = -3.82 \times 10^5 \textrm{\ J mol}^{-1} = - 382\textrm{\ kJ mol}^{-1}$

This question is categorized as medium difficulty. Students must calculate the standard cell potential and then apply the Gibbs free energy determination equation.
