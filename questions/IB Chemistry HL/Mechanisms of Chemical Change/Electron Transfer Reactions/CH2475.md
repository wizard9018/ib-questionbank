# CH2475

Aqueous nickel(II) sulfate, $\ce{NiSO4}\textrm{(aq)}$, is electrolysed using inert platinum electrodes. The following standard electrode potentials are relevant:

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\mathbf{Half{\text{--}}reaction}$ | $\mathbf{E^{\minuso}\;\textbf{/ V}}$ |
|:-:|:-:|
| $\ce{Ni^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Ni}\textrm{(s)}$ | $-0.26$ |
| $2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,OH^{-}}\textrm{(aq)}$ | $-0.83$ |
| $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H^{+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 4\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$ | $+1.23$ |
| $\ce{S2O8^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,SO4^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)}$ | $+2.01$ |

1.  Predict the product formed at the cathode. Justify your answer using the electrode potential data. [2 marks]

2.  Predict the product formed at the anode. Explain your answer. [2 marks]

3.  State and explain the effect on the pH of the solution as electrolysis proceeds. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  nickel / $\ce{Ni}$ $\checkmark$\
    $\ce{Ni^{2+}}$ has a more positive reduction potential  than water  $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    At the cathode, two reductions compete:

    $\ce{Ni^{2+}} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \longrightarrow \ce{Ni} \hspace{1.em}E^{\minuso} = -0.26 \textrm{\ V}$ \
    $2\ce{\,H2O} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \longrightarrow \ce{H2} + 2\ce{\,OH^{-}} \hspace{1.em}E^{\minuso} = -0.83 \textrm{\ V}$

    The half$-$reaction with the more positive $E^{\minuso}$ is thermodynamically favoured for reduction.  Consider the (minimum) voltage required to electrolyse the system

    $E_{min} = -E_{cell}$

    $E_{cell} = E^{\minuso}_{red}(\textrm{cathode}) - E^{\minuso}_{red}(\textrm{anode})$

    To minimise this driving voltage, the system selects the cathode reaction with the most positive $E^{\minuso}$ (easiest to reduce) and the anode reaction with the least positive $E^{\minuso}$ (easiest to oxidise).  Since $-0.26\;\textrm{V} > -0.83\;\textrm{V}$, nickel deposits on the cathode surface as a solid metal coating.

2. oxygen / $\ce{O2} \: \checkmark$\
    oxidation of water requires less energy than the oxidation of $\ce{SO4^{2-}}$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    For oxidation, the half$-$reaction with the less positive $E^{\minuso}$ is preferred because it requires less driving force to reverse.

    The two (reduction) reactions are:

    $\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H^{+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 4\ce{\,e^{-}} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{1.em}E^{\minuso} = 1.23 \textrm{\ V}$

    $\ce{S2O8^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,SO4^{2-}}\textrm{(aq)} \hspace{1.em}E^{\minuso} = 2.01 \textrm{\ V}$

    At the anode, the system selects the half$-$reaction with the least positive $E^{\minuso}$ because that species has the weakest tendency to be reduced, making it the easiest to oxidise. Comparing the two candidates: water $(+1.23 \textrm{\ V})$ *vs.* $\ce{SO4^{2-}} (+2.01 \textrm{\ V}),$ water is much more easily oxidised. The very high $E^{\minuso}$ of the peroxodisulfate half$-$reaction means sulfate ions are effectively inert spectators.

3.  pH decreases $\checkmark$\
    oxidation of water at the anode produces $\ce{H^{+}}$ ions $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    Both electrode processes contribute to the pH drop. At the anode, each mole of $\ce{O2}$ produced releases 4 moles of $\ce{H^{+}}$. At the cathode, because $\ce{Ni^{2+}}$ is reduced instead of water, no $\ce{OH^{-}}$ is generated to neutralise the $\ce{H^{+}}$. If water were reduced at the cathode instead, the $\ce{OH^{-}}$ produced would  offset the acid, but that does not happen here. A pH meter or universal indicator turning red/orange near the anode would confirm this experimentally.
