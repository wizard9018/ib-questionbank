# CH2295

The standard electrode potentials for two metals are shown.

$\ce{A^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons \ce{A}\textrm{(s)} \hspace{2em} E^{\minuso} = -0.44 \textrm{\ V}$

$\ce{B^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 3\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons \ce{B}\textrm{(s)} \hspace{2em} E^{\minuso} = +0.77 \textrm{\ V}$

Which species is the strongest oxidising agent?

-    A.$\hspace{1.em}$A

-    B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{A^{2+}}$
-    C.$\hspace{1.em}$B
-    D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{B^{3+}}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
 An oxidising agent is a species that gains electrons, causing another species to be oxidised. In a standard electrode potential table, the species on the left$-$hand side of each half$-$equation is the oxidised form, and it acts as the oxidising agent when the half$-$equation proceeds in the forward (reduction) direction. The more positive the standard electrode potential, the greater the tendency of that species to be reduced, and therefore the stronger it is as an oxidising agent.

Comparing the two half$-$equations:

$\ce{A^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons \ce{A}\textrm{(s)} \hspace{2em} E^{\minuso} = -0.44 \textrm{\ V}$

$\ce{B^{3+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 3\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons \ce{B}\textrm{(s)} \hspace{2em} E^{\minuso} = +0.77 \textrm{\ V}$

$\ce{B^{3+}}$ has the more positive $E^{\minuso}$ value, so it has the greatest tendency to accept electrons. $\ce{B^{3+}}$ is the strongest oxidising agent.

This question is categorised as medium. The student must recall that oxidising agents are species that accept electrons, identify which species appear on the reactant side of reduction half$-$equations, and use $E^{\minuso}$ values to compare their relative strengths.
