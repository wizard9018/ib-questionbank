# CH2330

What are the products of electrolysis when concentrated calcium bromide solution is electrolysed using graphite electrodes?

$\\[10pts]$

| $\hspace{1.em}$||| Anode $(+)$ | Cathode $(-)$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|| A. || Bromine | Hydrogen |
|| B. || Oxygen | Calcium |
|| C. || Bromine | Calcium |
|| D. || Oxygen | Hydrogen |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Calcium bromide dissociates completely in aqueous solution:

$\ce{CaBr2}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,Br^{-}}\textrm{(aq)}$

At the anode, oxidation occurs. Both bromide ions and water molecules are present and could be oxidised. The relevant standard electrode potentials from the data booklet are:

$\ce{Br2}\textrm{(l)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,Br^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} \hspace{2em} E^\circ = +1.09 \textrm{\ V}$

$\ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H^{+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 4\ce{\,e^{-}} \rightleftharpoons 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \hspace{2em} E^\circ = +1.23 \textrm{\ V}$

Since oxidation occurs at the anode, the species with the less positive standard electrode potential is more easily oxidised. Bromide $(+1.09 \textrm{\ V})$ is more easily oxidised than water $(+1.23 \textrm{\ V}).$ Furthermore, because the solution is concentrated, bromide ions are present in high concentration, further favouring their oxidation. Bromine is produced at the anode:

$2\ce{\,Br^{-}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Br2}\textrm{(l)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}}$

At the cathode, reduction occurs. Both $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$ ions and $\ce{H^{+}}$ ions (from water) are present. Calcium is an extremely reactive metal with a very negative standard electrode potential $(E^\circ = -2.87 \textrm{\ V}),$ making its reduction highly unfavourable. Hydrogen ions are preferentially reduced:

$2\ce{\,H^{+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}} \longrightarrow \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)}$

Hydrogen gas is produced at the cathode.

In general, metals more reactive than zinc (those above zinc in the reactivity series) cannot be deposited from aqueous solution by electrolysis, because water is reduced instead. Calcium is far above zinc in the reactivity series, so metallic calcium cannot be produced at the cathode in aqueous solution.

This question is categorised as medium. The student must recognise that the concentrated solution favours bromide oxidation at the anode, and that highly reactive metals cannot be deposited from aqueous solution at the cathode.
