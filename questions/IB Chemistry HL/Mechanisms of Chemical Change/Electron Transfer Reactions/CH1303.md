# CH1303

Which combination is correct for the electrolytic cell described below?

![CH1303](images/CH1303.png)

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
 | | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
 | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
 | A. | Battery | Reduction of cations | Salt bridge | Lead is formed |
 | B. | Battery | Oxidation of anions | Electrolyte | Lead is formed |
 | C. | Voltmeter | Reduction of cations | Electrolyte | Bromine is formed |
 | D. | Voltmeter | Oxidation of anions | Salt bridge | Bromine is formed |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
In molten salts, cations and anions are free to move as they are in the liquid state. The molten salt consists of the electrolyte in which the electrodes are placed.

3 is the electrolyte.

Both electrodes are placed and connected to an external energy source (a battery) to provide the energy needed for the chemical reaction.

1 is a battery.

The energy allows the flow of electrons from the anode, where oxidation occurs, to the cathode, where reduction occurs. The anode is the positive electrode on the left, and the cathode is the negative electrode on the right.

Bromide ions are oxidized at the anode to form bromine.

2 is where anions are oxidized.

$\ce{2 Br-(l) -> Br2(g) + 2 e-}$

At the cathode, cations are reduced by gaining electrons. 4 is where lead is formed.

$\ce{Pb^2+(l) + 2 e- -> Pb(l)}$

This question is categorized as medium difficulty. Students must understand the electrolysis of molten salts and distinguish the components of the device and the functions of each in a given diagram.
