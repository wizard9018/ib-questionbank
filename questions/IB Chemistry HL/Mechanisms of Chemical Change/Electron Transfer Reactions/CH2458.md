# CH2458

A steel fork is to be plated with silver by electrolysis. Which row correctly identifies the anode, cathode, and electrolyte?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\hspace{1.em}$||| $\mathbf{Anode}$ | $\mathbf{Cathode}$ | $\mathbf{Electrolyte}$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|| A. || silver metal | steel fork | $\ce{AgNO3}\textrm{(aq)}$ |
|| B. || steel fork | silver metal | $\ce{AgNO3}\textrm{(aq)}$ |
|| C. || silver metal | steel fork | $\ce{NaNO3}\textrm{(aq)}$ |
|| D. || steel fork | silver metal | $\ce{NaCl}\textrm{(aq)}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
In electroplating, the object to be coated is connected as the cathode because metal ions from the electrolyte are reduced and deposited onto its surface:

$\ce{Ag+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e-} \longrightarrow \ce{Ag}\textrm{(s)}$

The anode is made of the plating metal (silver), which oxidises to replace the silver ions consumed from solution:

$\ce{Ag}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ag+}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{e-}$

This keeps the concentration of $\ce{Ag+}$ in the electrolyte approximately constant, ensuring a uniform coating. The electrolyte must contain the ions of the plating metal, so $\ce{AgNO3}\textrm{(aq)}$ is suitable.

Placing the fork at the anode would oxidise the steel rather than coat it. Using an electrolyte without silver ions, such as $\ce{NaNO3}\textrm{(aq)}$ or $\ce{NaCl}\textrm{(aq)},$ would deposit no silver regardless of the electrode arrangement, because there are no $\ce{Ag+}$ ions available for reduction.
