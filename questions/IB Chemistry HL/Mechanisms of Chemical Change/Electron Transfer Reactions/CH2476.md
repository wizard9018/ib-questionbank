# CH2476

Two electrolytic cells are connected in series so that the same electric current passes through both cells.

||
|:--|
Cell A contains aqueous copper(II) sulfate, $\ce{CuSO4} \textrm{(aq)},$ with **copper electrodes**.
Cell B contains aqueous silver nitrate, $\ce{AgNO3}\textrm{(aq)},$ with **inert platinum electrodes**.

After the current is switched off, the copper anode in Cell A has lost $1.27\;\textrm{g}$ in mass.

1. Write the half$-$equation for the reaction at the anode in Cell A. [1 mark]

2. Calculate the amount, in mol, of electrons transferred through the circuit. [2 marks]

3. Deduce the mass of silver deposited at the cathode in Cell B. [2 marks]

4. State the product formed at the anode in Cell B. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $\ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^{-}}$ $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Cell A uses copper electrodes, which are active. The copper anode dissolves during electrolysis as each atom loses two electrons and enters solution as $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$. Meanwhile, $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions from the electrolyte deposit at the cathode, so the net effect is copper transferring from anode to cathode with no change in electrolyte concentration.

2. $n(\ce{Cu}) = 0.0200\textrm{\ mol}\: \checkmark$\
    $n(\ce{e^{-}}) =  0.04000\;\textrm{mol}\:\checkmark$

    *Award [2] for the moles of electrons.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Cells in series share the same current, so the total number of electrons that flowed is identical in both cells. The moles of electrons transferred are therefore the same in each cell. The stoichiometry of the copper half$-$equation, $2 \ce{\,e^{-}}$ per Cu, is the link between the measured mass loss and the electron count.

    $n(\ce{Cu}) = \dfrac{1.27 \textrm{\ g}}{63.55 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.0200 \textrm{\ mol Cu}$

    $n(\ce{e^{-}}) = 0.0200 \textrm{\ mol Cu} \times \dfrac{ 2 \textrm{\ mol e}^-}{1 \textrm{\ mol Cu}} = 0.0400\:\textrm{mol\ } \ce{e-}$

    This electron count then carries over to Cell B.

3.  $n(\ce{Ag}) = 0.04000\;\textrm{mol} \: \checkmark$\
    $m(\ce{Ag})  = 4.31\;\textrm{g} \: \checkmark$

    *Award [2] for the mass of silver.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further\;Explanation:}}$\
    Silver requires only $1$ electron per ion, while copper requires $2$. With the same number of electrons passing through both cells, twice as many moles of silver deposit compared to copper dissolved.

    $n(\ce{Ag}) = 0.0400 \textrm{\ mol e}^- \times \dfrac{ \textrm{1 mol Ag}}{\textrm{1 mol e}^-} = 0.0400\;\textrm{mol Ag}$

    Using the molar mass, determine the mass of silver deposited:

    $m(\ce{Ag}) = 0.0400\;\textrm{mol Ag} \times 107.87 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = 4.31 \textrm{\ g Ag}$

4. oxygen / $\ce{O2} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Cell B uses inert platinum electrodes. At the anode, water is oxidised:

    $2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H^{+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 4\,e^{-}$

     Water is oxidised in preference to $\ce{NO3^{-}}$ because nitrogen in nitrate is already at its maximum oxidation state ($+5$) and cannot be further oxidised. The $E^{\minuso}$ for $\ce{O2}/\ce{H2O}$ ($+1.23\;\textrm{V}$) is also much less positive than any feasible nitrate oxidation, confirming that water is the preferred species.
