# CH2055

Which combination of substances formed at the electrodes is true for the electrolysis of  molten aluminum oxide?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | anode | cathode |
    | :-- | :-: | :-: |
    | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Oxygen gas $\ce{(O2)}$ | Aluminum (Al) |
    | B. | Oxygen ion $\ce{(O^2-)}$ | Aluminum ion $\ce{(Al^3+)}$ |
    | C. | Aluminum (Al) | Oxygen gas $\ce{(O2)}$ |
    | D. | Aluminum (Al) | Oxygen ion $\ce{(O^2-)}$ |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$

In the electrolysis of molten binary salts, the salt is separated into its component elements.  The anion flows toward the positive anode where it is oxidized and the cation flows toward the negative cathode where is is reduced.

| $\text{oxidation (anode):}$ | $\ce{6 O^2-(l)}$ | $\ce{->}$ | $\ce{3 O2(g) + 6 e-}$ |
|--:|--:|:-:|:--
| reduction (cathode): | $\ce{2 Al^3+(l) + 6 e-}$ | $\ce{->}$ | $\ce{2 Al(l)}$ |
| overall reaction: | $\ce{6 O^2-(l) + 2 Al^3+(l)}$ | $\ce{->}$ | $\ce{3 O2(g) + 2 Al(l)}$ |

In this reaction, $\ce{O2(g)}$ is formed at the anode and Al(l) is formed at the cathode.
