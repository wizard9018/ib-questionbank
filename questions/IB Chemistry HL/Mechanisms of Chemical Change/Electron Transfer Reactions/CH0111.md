# CH0111

**Maximum mark: 8**

Titration technique can be used to determine the percentage of copper present in a sample of copper (I) oxide. The sample is first dissolved in acidic solution and $\ce{Cu^{+}}$ ions are formed. The resulting solution is then titrated against a standard solution of potassium manganate (VII), $\ce{KMnO_{4}}$. In acidic solution, $\ce{MnO4}^{-}$ reacts with $\ce{Cu^{+}}$ to form $\ce{Mn^{2+}}$ and $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions.

-   :::tableoptions{}
    | Titre                            | 1     | 2     | 3     |
    | :-                               | :-:   | :-:   | :-:   |
    | Initial burette reading/cm$^{3}$ | 2.00  | 24.70 | 5.00  |
    | Final burette reading/cm$^{3}$   | 25.80 | 48.30 | 30.60 |

-   :::tableoptions{row0="hide"}
    | $\textrm{Mass of copper oxide (I) sample / g}$             | $0.7751$           |
    | :-                                            | :--               |
    | Concentration of KMnO$_{4}$ solution / mol dm$^{-3}$ | 2.375 $\times$ 10$^{-2}$ |

1. Using Section 24 (*Section 19- 2025 Syllabus*) of the Data Booklet, write the equation of the redox reaction. [2 marks]

2. Define reducing agent and specify the reducing agent in the reaction. [2 marks]

3. Calculate the number of moles of $\ce{MnO4}^{-}$ that reacted with Cu$^{+}$. [2 marks]

4. Deduce the percentage by mass of Cu present in the sample. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{5 Cu^+(aq) + MnO4^-(aq) + 8 H^+(aq) -> 5 Cu^{2+}(aq) + Mn^{2+}(aq) + 4H2O(l)}\,\checkmark\,\checkmark$

    *Award 2 marks if correctly balanced and correct state symbols;\
    Award 1 mark for correctly placing H$^{+}$ and H$_{2}$O;\
    Award 1 mark for correct balanced equation but with electrons shown.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Equations selected are:

    $\ce{Cu^{2+} + e- -> Cu+}$ reversed to $\ce{Cu+ -> Cu^{2+} + e-}$

    &

    $\ce{MnO4- + 8 H+ + 5e- -> Mn^{2+} + 4 H2O}$

    $\ce{MnO4-}$ gains 5 electrons whereas Cu$^+$ only loses one. To balance the electrons, the Cu$^+/^{2+}$ equation must be multiplied by 5 before combining to the overall redox equation.

2.  Reducing agent is the substance that reduces another substance and itself undergoes oxidation. $\checkmark$

    The reducing agent is Cu$^{+}$. $\checkmark$

    *Do not accept copper.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Cu$^+$ loses one e- and becomes Cu$^{2+}$ $\textbf{OR}$  its oxidation state increases from +1 to +2.

3.  Average volume = $\dfrac{(25.80-2.00)+(48.30-24.70)+(30.60-5.00)}{3} = 24.33\,\text{cm}^{3}\,\checkmark$

    $n = C\times V = (2.375\times 10^{-2}\,\text{mol}\,\text{dm}^{-3})(24.33\times 10^{-3}\,\text{dm}^{3}) = 5.78\times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol}$

    *Award 1 mark for correct volume;\
    Award 1 mark for correct calculation.*

4.  Based on the reaction mole ratio: 1 mol of MnO$^{-4}$ reacts with 5 mol of Cu$^{+}$

    $n (\ce{Cu^{+}}) = 5\times 5.78\times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol} = 2.89\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol}$

    Same number of moles of Cu in copper oxide sample;

    $m (\ce{Cu}) = n\times M = (2.89\times 10^{-3}\,\text{mol})(63.55\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}) = 0.184\,\text{g}\,\checkmark$

    $\text{\% by mass} = \dfrac{m(\ce{Cu})}{m(\text{sample})}\times 100 = \dfrac{0.184\,\text{g}}{0.7751\,\text{g}}\times 100 = 23.7\%\, \checkmark$

    *Allow ECF from part (a) and (c) provided some mention of mole ratio is stated;\
    Award 2 marks if all 3 points are correct;\
    Award 1 mark if 2 points are correct;\
    No mark if 1 point is correct.*
