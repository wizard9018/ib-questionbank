# CH2457

The standard electrode potential for the half$-$reaction $\ce{Zn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Zn}\textrm{(s)}$ is $-0.76\textrm{ V}.$

What does this value indicate?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Zinc is oxidised more readily than hydrogen under standard conditions.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Zinc ions are reduced more readily than hydrogen ions under standard conditions.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$The reaction produces a potential difference of $0.76\textrm{ V}$ in any electrochemical cell containing zinc.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Zinc metal spontaneously dissolves in all aqueous solutions.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Standard electrode potentials are measured relative to the standard hydrogen electrode, which is assigned a value of $0\textrm{ V}$ by convention. A negative $E^{\minuso}$ means the metal has a greater tendency to be oxidised than hydrogen. In a cell paired with the SHE, zinc would act as the anode, losing electrons while hydrogen ions are reduced at the cathode. This is equivalent to saying zinc is oxidised more readily than hydrogen under standard conditions.

The value $-0.76\textrm{ V}$ does not mean zinc ions are easily reduced. The negative sign indicates that the reduction of $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ is less favourable than the reduction of $\ce{H+}.$ A more negative $E^{\minuso}$ means less tendency to be reduced, not more.

The claim that the potential difference is $0.76\textrm{ V}$ in any cell ignores the fact that $E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}}$ depends on both half$-$cells. The value $-0.76\textrm{ V}$ is the potential of the $\ce{Zn^{2+}/Zn}$ half$-$cell relative to the SHE, not a fixed cell voltage.

The suggestion that zinc dissolves in all aqueous solutions overgeneralises. Zinc reacts with acidic solutions containing $\ce{H+}$ because $E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}}$ is positive for that combination, but it would not spontaneously reduce ions with a more negative $E^{\minuso}$ than its own.
