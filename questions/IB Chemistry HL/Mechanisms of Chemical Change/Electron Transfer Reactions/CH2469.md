# CH2469

A voltaic cell is set up under standard conditions.

$\ce{Zn}\textrm{(s)}\,|\,\ce{Zn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}\,||\,\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}\,|\,\ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$

Which change increases the cell potential?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Adding water to the $\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$ half$-$cell

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Dissolving $\ce{ZnSO4}\textrm{(s)}$ in the $\ce{Zn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$ half$-$cell

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Adding water to the $\ce{Zn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}$ half$-$cell

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Increasing the mass of the copper electrode

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Each half$-$cell contains an equilibrium. Le $\textrm{Ch\^{a}telier's}$ principle can be applied to predict how changing concentrations shifts the position of equilibrium and affects the cell potential.

The two half$-$cell equilibria are:

$\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}\qquad$ (cathode)

$\ce{Zn^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \rightleftharpoons \ce{Zn}\textrm{(s)}\qquad$ (anode)

Adding water to the $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ half$-$cell dilutes $\ce{Cu^{2+}},$ shifting the cathode equilibrium to the left. The tendency for $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ to be reduced decreases, so the cathode potential decreases and $E_{\text{cell}}$ decreases.

Dissolving $\ce{ZnSO4}$ in the $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ half$-$cell increases $[\ce{Zn^{2+}}],$ shifting the anode equilibrium to the right (toward $\ce{Zn}).$ This increases the tendency for $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ to be reduced, raising the anode potential. Since $E_{\text{cell}} = E_{\text{cathode}} - E_{\text{anode}},$ a higher anode potential decreases $E_{\text{cell}}.$

Adding water to the $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ half$-$cell dilutes $[\ce{Zn^{2+}}],$ shifting the anode equilibrium to the left. The tendency for $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ to be reduced decreases, so the anode potential becomes more negative. The difference $E_{\text{cathode}} - E_{\text{anode}}$ increases, and $E_{\text{cell}}$ increases.

Increasing the mass of the copper electrode has no effect. Copper is a solid, and changing the amount of a pure solid does not shift the position of equilibrium. The cell potential depends on ion concentrations, not on the size of the electrodes.
