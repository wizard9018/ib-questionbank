# CH2462

Consider the following voltaic cell.

$\ce{Mg}\textrm{(s)}\,|\,\ce{Mg^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}\,||\,\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)}\,|\,\ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$

$E^{\minuso}_{\ce{Cu^{2+}/Cu}} = +0.34\textrm{ V}\qquad E^{\minuso}_{\ce{Mg^{2+}/Mg}} = -2.37\textrm{ V}$

What is the correct expression for the standard Gibbs free energy change of the cell in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}-\,2 \times 96500 \times 2.71$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}2 \times 96500 \times 2.71$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{2 \times 96500 \times 2.71}{1000}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{-\,2 \times 96500 \times 2.71}{1000}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The standard cell potential is calculated from the two half$-$cell potentials:

$E^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}} = E^{\minuso}_{\text{cathode}} - E^{\minuso}_{\text{anode}} = (+0.34) - (-2.37) = +2.71\textrm{ V}$

In cell notation the left$-$hand electrode is the anode (Mg is oxidised) and the right$-$hand electrode is the cathode ($\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ is reduced). The balanced equation involves the transfer of two electrons $(n = 2):$

$\ce{Mg}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Mg^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)}$

The relationship between Gibbs energy and cell potential is:

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = -nFE^{\minuso}_{\text{cell}}$

Substituting: $\Delta G^{\minuso} = -\,2 \times 96500 \times 2.71 = -523030\textrm{ J mol}^{-1}.$

The question asks for the answer in $\textrm{kJ mol}^{-1},$ so division by $1000$ is required:

$\Delta G^{\minuso} = \dfrac{-\,2 \times 96500 \times 2.71}{1000} = -523\textrm{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
