## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{Zn(s) + Co^2+(aq) -> Zn^2+(aq) + Co(s)}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Conventionally, the anode reaction is shown on the left, and the cathode reaction is shown on the right of the double line.

    A double line represents the salt bridge, a single vertical line represents a phase boundary, and a comma separates substances within the same phase. Specifics about the cell's reduction and oxidation processes are presented in the two sections preceding and following the double line.

2.
    1.  Half-reactions:

        -   Reaction at X: $\ce{Zn(s) -> Zn^2+(aq) + 2 e-}\,\checkmark$
        -   Reaction at Y: $\ce{Co^2+(aq) + 2 e- -> Co(s)}\,\checkmark$

    2.  Electrodes: $\checkmark$

        -   X: Anode
        -   Y: Cathode

    3.  Signs: $\checkmark$

        -   X: Negative/Minus/-
        -   Y: Positive/Plus/+

3.  Electrons flow from the anode to the cathode $\checkmark$

    via the external circuit. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Zinc is oxidized, and the electrons released flow from the anode to the wire i.e., the external circuit. They finally reach the cathode.

4.  Any one of the below functions: $\checkmark$

    - The salt bridge connects the two half-cells and completes the circuit.
    - It lets ions move in or out of the half-cells so that the charges in the half-cells are balanced.

5.  Any one of the below differences: $\checkmark$
    - In an electrochemical cell, spontaneous redox reactions occur, whereas non-spontaneous redox reactions occur in an electrolytic cell.
    - An electrochemical cell transforms chemical energy into electrical energy, whereas an electrolytic cell does the reverse.
    - In an electrochemical cell, the anode is the negative electrode, and the cathode is the positive electrode. For an electrolytic cell, the reverse is applicable.
