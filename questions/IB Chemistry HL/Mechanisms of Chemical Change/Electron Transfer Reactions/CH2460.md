# CH2460

In the electrolysis of aqueous sodium chloride, using inert electrodes, a colourless gas is produced. Which combination is correct?

$\mbox{}\\[-15pts]$

| $\hspace{1.em}$||| $\mathbf{Product\;at\;the\;cathode}$ | $\mathbf{Product\;at\;the\;anode}$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|| A. || Hydrogen | Chlorine |
|| B. || Hydrogen | Oxygen |
|| C. || Sodium | Chlorine |
|| D. || Sodium | Oxygen |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Aqueous sodium chloride contains $\ce{Na+}$ ions, $\ce{Cl-}$ ions, and water. At each electrode, the question is whether the solute ion or water is discharged.

At the cathode, water is reduced in preference to $\ce{Na+}$ because the reduction potential for water $(E^{\minuso} = -0.83\textrm{ V})$ is far higher than that of sodium $(-2.71\textrm{ V}).$ Hydrogen gas is produced:

$2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} + 2\ce{\,e-} \longrightarrow \ce{H2}\textrm{(g)} + 2\ce{\,OH-}\textrm{(aq)}$

At the anode, the two competing oxidations are water $(E^{\minuso} = +1.23\textrm{ V}$ for $\ce{O2/H2O})$ and chloride ions $(E^{\minuso} = +1.36\textrm{ V}$ for $\ce{Cl2/Cl-}).$ These values are close, so the product depends on concentration and conditions. The question states a colourless gas is produced. Oxygen is colourless, while chlorine is pale green, so the observation confirms that water is oxidised to produce oxygen:

$2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{O2}\textrm{(g)} + 4\ce{\,H+}\textrm{(aq)} + 4\ce{\,e-}$
