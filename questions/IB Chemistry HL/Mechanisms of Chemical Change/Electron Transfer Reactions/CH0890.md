# CH0890

**Maximum mark: 5**

An electrolytic cell with inert electrodes was set up to electrolyze molten aluminum oxide.

![9-890](images/9-890.png)

1.  Deduce the reaction at Y. [1 mark]

2.  Explain why this reaction cannot be done with solid aluminum oxide. [2 marks]

3.  State the electron configuration of the negative ion in this salt. [1 mark]

4.  Calculate the number of moles of the element formed at Y if 6 moles of current are passed through the circuit. [1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{Al^3+ + 3 e- -> Al}\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Positive ions are attracted to the negative electrode. They gain electrons from the external battery and are reduced.

2.  In the solid salt, the ions are confined within the lattice. $\checkmark$

    It cannot conduct electricity. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    In a lattice, the ions are held together by strong ionic bonds and are not free to move. In a molten state, the ionic compound dissociates into its ions, allowing them to move and function as charge carriers.

3.  $\ce{O^2- }: 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    Oxide $\ce{O^{2-}}$ is the negative ion in aluminum oxide. The electron configuration of O is $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4$.

4.  2 moles of $\ce{Al}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

    $\ce{Al^3+ + 3 e- -> Al}$,

    $\ce{e-}\ 3 : 1\ \ce{Al}$

    $\ce{e-}\ 1 : \frac{1}{3}\ \ce{Al}$

    $\ce{6 e-}\ :\ \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) (\ce{6 Al})$
