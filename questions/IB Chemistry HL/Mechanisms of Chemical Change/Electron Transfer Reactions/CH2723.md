# CH2723

**Maximum mark: 11**

The Faraday constant, $F,$ is the charge carried by one mole of electrons. A class determined $F$ by electrolysing aqueous copper(II) sulfate between two copper electrodes for $35.0$ minutes. In every run, charge flowed through the cell at a constant rate of $0.250 \pm 0.001 \textrm{\ C s}^{-1}.$

Six groups performed the experiment, each using a freshly cleaned copper cathode of the same size. Each cathode was rinsed with propanone, dried and weighed before and after the run.

| $\mathbf{Group}$ | $\mathbf{1}$ | $\mathbf{2}$ | $\mathbf{3}$ | $\mathbf{4}$ | $\mathbf{5}$ | $\mathbf{6}$ |
:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:
| **Initial mass $\pm$ 0.001 / g** | 41.203 | 40.885 | 41.647 | 40.112 | 41.958 | 40.534 |
| **Final mass $\pm$ 0.001 / g** | 41.367 | 41.044 | 41.814 | 40.273 | 42.123 | 40.696 |

$\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

1. Write the half$-$equation, including state symbols, for the reaction at each electrode. [2 marks]

2. Calculate the total charge, $Q,$ in C, passed during the electrolysis. [1 mark]

3. Calculate the mean and the range of the six mass increases. [2 marks]

4. Determine the value of $F,$ in $\textrm{C mol}^{-1},$ using your mean change in mass from part (c).

    If you did not determine a mean in part (c), use 0.170 $\textrm{\ g},$ although this is not the correct answer. [2 marks]

5. Calculate the percentage error in the value from part (d). The accepted value is $F = 96\,500 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}.$

    If you did not determine a value in part (d), use $9.80 \times 10^4 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1},$ although this is not the correct answer. [1 mark]

6. Using the range from part (c), comment on the precision and the accuracy of the results. [2 marks]

7. Suggest one source of systematic error that would account for the experimental value of $F$ being greater than the accepted value. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. cathode: $\ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^-} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)} \: \checkmark$ \
    anode: $\ce{Cu}\textrm{(s)} \longrightarrow \ce{Cu^{2+}}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,e^-} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    With copper electrodes in a copper(II) solution, the same half$-$reaction runs in opposite directions at the two electrodes: copper dissolves at the anode and deposits at the cathode, so the electrolyte concentration stays constant throughout.

2. $Q = 525 \textrm{\ C} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Charge is the rate of charge flow multiplied by time, with time converted to seconds:

    $t = 35.0 \textrm{\ min} \times \dfrac{60 \textrm{\ s}}{1 \textrm{\ min}} = 2100 \textrm{\ s}$

    $Q = 0.250 \textrm{\ C s}^{-1} \times 2100 \textrm{\ s} = 525 \textrm{\ C}$

3. mean $= 0.163 \textrm{\ g} \: \checkmark$ \
    range $= 0.008 \textrm{\ g} \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Each group's mass increase is the final mass minus the initial mass. The mean is their sum divided by six,

    $\bar{m} = \dfrac{0.164 + 0.159 + 0.167 + 0.161 + 0.165 + 0.162}{6} = 0.163 \textrm{\ g}$

    and the range is the largest increase minus the smallest,

    $0.167 \textrm{\ g} - 0.159 \textrm{\ g} = 0.008 \textrm{\ g}$

4. $n(\ce{e^-}) = 5.13 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol} \: \checkmark$ \
    $F = 1.02 \times 10^{5} \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1} \: \checkmark$

    *Accept 9.81 $\times$ 10$^\mathit{\,4}$ C mol$^\mathit{\,-1}$ if 0.170  g used.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    Each copper ion deposited gains two electrons, so the amount of electrons is twice the amount of copper.

    $n(\ce{Cu}) = \dfrac{0.163 \textrm{\ g}}{63.55 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}} = 2.56 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

    $n(\ce{e^-}) = 2 \times n(\ce{Cu}) = 5.13 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}$

    The Faraday constant is the measured charge divided by the amount of electrons:

    $F = \dfrac{525 \textrm{\ C}}{5.13 \times 10^{-3} \textrm{\ mol}} = 1.02 \times 10^{5} \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}$

5. $\% \textrm{\ error} = 5.7\% \: \checkmark$

    *Accept 1.6\% if 9.80 $\times$ 10$^\mathit{\,4}$ C mol$^\mathit{\,-1}$ is used.*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The percentage error is

    $\% \textrm{\ error} = \left| \dfrac{\textrm{experimental\ } - \textrm{\ literature}}{\textrm{literature}} \right| \times 100\%$

    For the Faraday constant, using the unrounded value of $F,$

    $\dfrac{1.02 \times 10^{5} \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1} - 0.965 \times 10^{5} \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}}{0.965 \times 10^{5} \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}} \times 100\% = 5.7\%$

6. precise **AND** variation among the trials is small / range is only $0.008 \textrm{\ g} \: \checkmark$ \
    not accurate **AND** percentage error $(\sim 6\%)$ is larger than total percentage uncertainty ($\sim 3\%) \: \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The six mass increases span only $0.008 \textrm{\ g}.$ Taking half the range as the uncertainty in the mean gives $\pm 0.004 \textrm{\ g},$ so the results are repeatable and the precision is high. The total percentage uncertainty in $F$ combines the mass and the current:

    $\dfrac{0.004 \textrm{\ g}}{0.163 \textrm{\ g}} \times 100\% + \dfrac{0.001 \textrm{\ C s}^{-1}}{0.250 \textrm{\ C s}^{-1}} \times 100\% = 2.5\% + 0.4\% = 2.9\%$

    The percentage error of $5.7\%$ is about twice this uncertainty, so random error cannot account for it. The results agree closely with one another but not with the accepted value, so the method contains a systematic error.

7. some deposited copper is lost from the cathode, mass increase is too small $\checkmark$

    *Accept a small fraction of the charge reducing H$^\textit{\,+}$ or water instead of Cu$^\textit{\,2+}.$*

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    $F$ is the charge divided by the amount of electrons found from the mass of copper deposited, so a value of $F$ that is too high means less copper was deposited than expected. The charge passed in each run predicts a mass increase of

    $m = \dfrac{525 \textrm{\ C}}{2 \times 96\,500 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1}} \times 63.55 \textrm{\ g mol}^{-1} = 0.173 \textrm{\ g}$

    All six mass increases are below this value. A shortfall in every run points to a systematic cause: either copper is lost from the cathode, or part of the charge does not deposit copper. A deposit that grows quickly is powdery, and loose crystals can be washed off during rinsing before the cathode is weighed. Reduction of water to hydrogen at the cathode has the same effect, because charge used to form hydrogen deposits no copper.
