# CH1553

A voltaic cell is made by connecting two half$\text{--}$cells represented by the half$\text{--}$equations below.

$\textrm{Zn}^{2+}\textrm{(aq)} + 2 \textrm{\ e}^- \rightarrow \textrm{Zn(s)} \hspace{2em}    E^{⦵} = -0.76 \textrm{\ V}$

$\ \textrm{Ag}^+\textrm{(aq)} + \textrm{e}^- \rightarrow \textrm{Ag(s)}    \hspace{2em}E^{⦵} = + 0.80 \textrm{\ V}$

What is the correct calculation to work out the corresponding $\Delta G ^{⦵}$ in kJ?

- A.$\hspace{1em}-2 \times 96500 \times 2.36$

- B.$\hspace{1em}-2 \times 96500 \times 1.56$

- C.$\hspace{1em}-\dfrac{2 \times 96500 \times 2.36}{1000}$

- D.$\hspace{1em}-\dfrac{2 \times 96500 \times 1.56}{1000}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

The student must recall the Gibbs free energy change calculation.

$\Delta G ^{⦵} = -nFE^{⦵}$

The student should also determine the chemical reaction taking place. As the standard electrode potential of silver is greater, this will be the species undergoing reduction.

The cell potential is $\ E^{⦵}_{cell} = E^{⦵}_{cathode} - E^{⦵}_{anode} = +0.80 \textrm{\ V} -(-0.76 \textrm{\ V}) = 1.56 \textrm{\ V}$. Notice that the cell potential is not affected by the coefficients of the overall balanced chemical reaction.

The overall equation can be deduced by balancing the electrons gained and lost in the oxidation$\text{--}$reduction reaction.  Since two electrons are lost by the zinc atoms, two silver atoms must accept electrons to make two moles of elemental silver.

||||

|--:| :-: | :-- |

Oxidation:| $\hspace{1em}$|    $\textrm{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \textrm{Zn}^{2+}\textrm{(aq)}+ 2 \textrm{\ e}^-$    |

Reduction:|    |$\big[\textrm{Ag}^+\textrm{(aq)}+ \textrm{e}^- \rightarrow \textrm{Ag(s)} \big] \times 2$|

Overall: ||$ \textrm{Zn(s)} + 2 \textrm{\ Ag}^+\textrm{(aq)} \rightarrow  2 \textrm{\ Ag(s)} + \textrm{Zn}^{2+}\textrm{(aq)}$

To determine the Gibbs free energy change,

$\Delta G ^{⦵} = -nFE^{⦵}$

$\Delta G ^{⦵} = -2 \textrm{\ mol} \times 96500 \textrm{\ C mol}^{-1} \times 1.56 \textrm{\ V}$

$\Delta G ^{⦵}= -2 \times 96500 \times 1.56 \textrm{\ J} = - \dfrac{2 \times 96500 \times 1.56}{1000} \textrm{\ kJ}$

where $n$ is the number of moles of electrons transferred in the overall balanced chemical reaction, $F$ is Faraday's constant which describes the charge of 1 mole of electrons in Coulombs, and $E^{⦵}_{cell}$ is the standard cell potential in Volts.  The relationship $1 \textrm{\ J} = 1 \textrm{\ C} \times 1 \textrm{\ V}$ is used to get the value in J and then divided by 1000 to get the value in kJ.

This question is categorized as a medium as the student must relate the cell potential with the Gibbs free energy and should figure out the corresponding units to deduce the correct magnitude.
