# CH0525

The structural formulae of three compounds are shown.

![CH0525](images/CH0525.png)

A student measures the optical activities of the three compounds using a polarimeter. Which of the following results are they most likely to obtain?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}

    | | $\hspace{1em}$ Activity of 1 / degrees $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ Activity of 2 / degrees $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ Activity of 3 / degrees $\hspace{1em}$ |

    | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |

    | $\hspace{1em}$ A. $\hspace{1em}$ | 3.0 | 5.0 | 3.2 |

    | $\hspace{1em}$ B. $\hspace{1em}$ | 3.0 | -5.0 | 0.0 |

    | $\hspace{1em}$ C. $\hspace{1em}$ | 5.0 | 5.0 | 3.2 |

    | $\hspace{1em}$ D. $\hspace{1em}$ | 5.0 | -5.0 | 0.0 |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Molecules 1 and 2 are chiral.  The carbon on the left is attached to four different groups: an H, a Cl, a methyl group, and a propyl group.  Chiral molecules rotate plane polarized light.  The two molecules are identical except for the position of the H and Cl atoms (notice the dash and wedge have been swapped).  When only two substituents are moved, the two molecules are enantiomers $-$ nonsuperimposible mirror images.

Since Molecules 1 and 2 are enantiomers they exhibit opposite optical activities (they rotate plane polarised light in opposite directions by the same amount).  While we cannot predict the angle, we do know that the answer choices need to be $+x$ and $-x$ degrees.

Compound 3 is achiral, because the central atom is not attached to four different groups.  The central atom has an H, a Cl, but two identical ethyl groups. Since it is not chiral, it does not rotate plane polarized light: the rotation angle is $0^\circ$.

This question is in hard, as the student must be able to determine a molecule's chirality and recall how polarimetry can be used to distinguish between stereoisomers.
