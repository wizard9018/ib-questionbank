**Maximum mark: 8**

Compounds $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ have the following structural formulas.

![CH0376](images/CH0376.png)

1.  The IR spectrum of compound Y features a signal that is not present in the IR spectrum of compound $\mathbf{X}$. Using section 26 (*section 20- 2025 Syllabus*) of the data booklet, state the range of wavenumbers in which this IR signal occurs. [1 mark]

2.  The $\ce{^1H}$ NMR spectrum of compound $\mathbf{X}$ was recorded using electromagnetic radiation with a wavelength of 1 m.

    The spectrum contains two signals with chemical shifts of $3.47$ and 1.21 ppm. The integral of the signal at 1.21 ppm is set to a value of 1.

    1.  State the type of electromagnetic radiation used in this experiment, using section 3 (*section 5- 2025 Syllabus*) of the data booklet. [1 mark]

    2.  Calculate the integral of the signal at 3.47 ppm. [1 mark]

    3.  Suggest **two** features that would be present in the $\ce{^1H}$ NMR spectrum of $\mathbf{Y}$ but **not** in the spectrum of $\mathbf{X}$, using section 27 (*section 21 NS*) of the data booklet. [2 marks]

3.  The mass spectrum of one of the compounds is shown.

    ![CH0376-2](images/CH0376-2.png)

    1.  State the *m/z* value of the molecular ion peak. [1 mark]

    2.  Explain how the identity of the compound can be determined from the peak at *m/z* = 43, using section 28 (*section 22- 2025 Syllabus*) of the data booklet. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $1700-1750$ cm$^{-1}$. $\checkmark$

    *Accept a single value within this range;*\
    *Accept values without units*.

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Compound $\mathbf{Y}$ contains a carbonyl group ($\ce{C=O}$ bond) which produces a characteristic signal at $1700 - 1750\,\text{cm}^{-1}$.

2.  1.  Radio (waves) $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        Radio waves have the longest wavelengths in the electromagnetic spectrum ($>10^8$ nm).

    2.  $\dfrac{2}{3}$ **OR** $\dfrac{4}{6}$ **OR** $0.67$ **OR** $0.6$ recurring $\checkmark$

        *Accept $0.66$, $0.666$ or similar;*\
        *Accept $67\%$, $66.7\%$ or similar*;\
        *Accept fractions equivalent to 2/3*; \
        *Do **not** accept $0.7$ or $70\%$*.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        The integral represents the number of $\ce{H}$ atoms in a particular environment. There are two proton environments in $\mathbf{X}$ with a ratio of $2:3$. The signal at $1.21$ ppm is for the $\ce{CH3}$ protons and the signal at $3.47$ ppm is for the $\ce{CH2}$ protons (expected chemical shift for that environment $3.3-3.7$ ppm in data booklet). You would expect the latter peak to have an integral 2/3 of the former.

    3.  *At least two from:*\
        (Two) signals with $1:1$ integral ratio; $\checkmark$

        One signal in the range $2.0-2.5\,\text{ppm}\,;\checkmark$

        One signal in the range $3.7-4.8\,\text{ppm}\,;\checkmark$

        Only singlets (in the spectrum of $\mathbf{Y}$) **OR** does not contain multiplets/triplets/quartets (which are present in the spectrum of $\mathbf{X}$). $\checkmark$

        *Accept signal in the range $2.2-2.7$ ppm*;\
        *Do **not** accept answers describing the absence of signals in the spectrum of $\mathbf{Y}$, e.g. at $3.47\, ppm$ / $1.21\, ppm$ / in the range $3.3-3.7\, ppm$ / in the range $0.9-1.0\, ppm$*.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        Both $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ have two environments, however, in $\mathbf{Y}$ both environments have $3$ protons and so the integrals would have a $1:1$ ratio.

        Using the data booklet, $\mathbf{Y}$ has signals characteristic of the following environments ($\ce{ROCOCH2}$ and $\ce{RCOOCH2}$).

        The final marking point is HL only where the protons in $\mathbf{Y}$ have no adjacent $\ce{H}$s and therefore only singlets.

3.  1.  74.$\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        The molecular ion peak can be found the furthest to the right.

    2.  (This peak corresponds to) the ion $\ce{CH3CO+}$ *OR* $\ce{(M - CH3O)+}$ *OR* $\ce{(\mathbf{Y} - CH3O)+}$ *OR* the fragment produced by the loss of a $\ce{CH3O}$ fragment. $\checkmark$

        The spectrum was produced from compound $\mathbf{Y}\,.\checkmark$

        *Award M1 for a structural or molecular formula of the $\ce{CH3CO+}$ fragment ion*;\
        *Award M1 for an illustration of the fragmentation process, e.g. a structural formula with the broken bond labelled;*\
        *Award M2 for a full or abbreviated structural formula of compound $\mathbf{Y}$*.

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        $\ce{CH3CO}$ has a molecular mass of $43$. Positive ions are detected by the spectrometer and so the identity of the fragment is $\ce{CH3CO+}$. Only $\mathbf{Y}$ could have lost this fragment.
