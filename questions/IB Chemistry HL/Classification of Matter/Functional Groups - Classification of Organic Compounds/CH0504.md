## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  ether $\checkmark$\
    no $\ce{O-H}$ signal in the range $3200 - 3600$ cm$^{-1}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    The IR spectrum shows no broad absorption between 3200 and $3600 \textrm{\ cm}^{-1},$ so there is no $\ce{O-H}$ stretch that would indicate the presence of an alcohol. However, a strong band appears in the region 1050$-$1150 $\textrm{\ cm}^{-1},$ typical of $\ce{C-O}$ single$-$bond stretching. This pattern is characteristic of an ether, which has the general formula $\ce{R-O-R'}.$

    $\newline$

    -   The $^1$H NMR spectrum shows four distinct signals, indicating four chemical environments. The signal at 1.05 ppm integrates to three protons and appears as a doublet, showing coupling to one neighboring hydrogen. This low chemical shift corresponds to a $\ce{-CH3}$ group attached to a single $\ce{-CH-}$ center. The singlet at 3.21 ppm integrates to three protons and has no neighboring hydrogens, identifying it as a $\ce{R-O-CH3}$ group (a methoxy substituent). The signal at 3.53 ppm integrates to two protons and shows coupling to one neighbor, consistent with a $\ce{R-O-CH2}$ group bonded to the same $\ce{-CH-}$ center. Finally, the resonance at 4.07 ppm corresponds to one proton that experiences coupling to five neighboring protons (three from the methyl group and two from the $\ce{R-O-CH2}$ group), giving rise to a sextet. This large chemical shift and complex splitting identify it as the $\ce{-CH-Hal},$ the hydrogen on the same carbon as the chlorine atom.
