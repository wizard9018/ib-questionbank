## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $n\ce{(CO2)} = \dfrac{107.8}{44.01} = 2.45\,\text{mol}$

    **AND**

    $n\ce{(CO2)} = n\ce{(C)} = 2.45\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

    $n\ce{(H2O)} = \dfrac{44.1}{18.02} = 2.45\,\text{mol}$

    **AND**

    $n\ce{(H)} = 2\times n\ce{(H2O)} = 4.89\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

    (Mass C in **X** = 2.45 $\times$ 12.01 = :answer[29.4 g]

    **AND**

    Mass H in **X** = 4.89 $\times$ 1.01 = :answer[4.95 g]

    Mass O in **X** = $50.0 - (29.4 + 4.95)$ = :answer[15.66 g]

    **AND**

    $n\ce{(O)} = \dfrac{15.66}{16.00} = 0.98\,\text{mol}\,\checkmark$

    (Divide by the smallest number of moles for ratio C =  $\dfrac{2.45}{0.98} = 2.5$; H = $\dfrac{4.89}{0.98} = 5$;  O = $\dfrac{0.98}{0.98} = 1$)

    Empirical formula = $\ce{C5H_{10}O2}$ $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    General equation for the combustion of a compound with C, H, and O:

    $\ce{C_xH_yO_z + excess O2 -> x CO2 + \frac{y}{2} H2O}$

    Therefore the number of moles of $\ce{CO2}$ is equal to the number of moles of C atoms in the original compound, and the number of moles of $\ce{H2O}$ is equal to half the number of moles H atoms in the compound.

    The number of moles of O atoms in the original compound can be calculated by determining the mass of C and H (moles $\times$ relative atomic mass) and subtracting this from the original mass of the compound burned (50.0 g).

    Once moles of all 3 elements have been calculated, divide by the smallest number to determine the whole number ratio. As the carbon gives 2.5, the numbers for all three must be doubled to give the simplest whole number ratio.

2.  Molecular ion peak at $m/z = 102$; $\checkmark$

    Mass of empirical formula = $(5 \times 12.01) + (10 \times 1.01) + (2 \times 16.00) =$ :answer[102.15]

    **AND**

    molecular formula = $\ce{C5H_{10}O2}$  $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    The peak farthest to the right is the molecular ion peak which gives the molecular mass. As the molecular mass is the same as the mass of the empirical formula, the molecular formula and the empirical formula must be the same.

3.  $\ce{C4H9+}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:

    This peak corresponds to the molecular ion ($\ce{M+}$) after losing the COOH fragment (45).

    $102 - 45 = 57$
