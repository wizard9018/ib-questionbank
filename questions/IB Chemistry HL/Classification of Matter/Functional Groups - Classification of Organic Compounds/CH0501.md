# CH0501

**Maximum mark: 10**

Compound **A** is a drug historically used for the treatment of insomnia. It consists only of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms.

1.  The $\ce{IR}$ spectrum of compound **A** contains a weak signal at 2116 cm$^{-1}$ and a strong broad signal at 3450 cm$^{-1}$. There are **no** strong signals between 1500 and 2000 cm$^{-1}$.

    State the names of the **two** functional groups present in compound A, using section 26 (*Section 20- 2025 Syllabus*) of the data booklet.[2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Alkyne **OR** ethyne; $\checkmark$

    Alcohol **OR** hydroxyl. $\checkmark$

    *Do **not** accept formulas, e.g. $\ce{C≡C}$.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Alkynes produce peaks of variable intensity in the range $2100-2260\, cm^{-1}$. Alcohols produce strong peaks in the range 3200$-$3600 cm$^{-1}$.

2.  1.  98 g mol$^{-1}\,\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        The furthest peak to the right is the molecular ion peak.

    2.  Peak at $m/z = 69$ is produced by loss of $\ce{CH2CH3} / \ce{C2H5} / \ce{Et}$ / ethyl group; $\checkmark$

        Peak at $m/z = 83$ is produced by loss of $\ce{CH3} / \ce{Me}$ / methyl group. $\checkmark$

        *Award [1] if both fragments are identified but neither is assigned to a specific peak;*\
        *Award [2] if both fragments are identified and only one is assigned to a specific peak.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        The peak at $m/z = 69$ is produced by a loss of $98 - 69 = 29$ which corresponds to $\ce{CH3CH2}$. It could not be $\ce{CHO}$ due to lack of a carbonyl group identified by $\ce{NMR}$. The peak at 83 is produced by a loss of $98-83 = 15$ which corresponds to $\ce{CH3}$.

3.  1.  Signal at 0.96 ppm corresponds to a $\ce{CH3} / \ce{Me}$ / methyl group (with 3 protons); $\checkmark$

        Signal at 2.86 ppm corresponds to a $\ce{C≡C-H}$ / alkyne proton / terminal alkyne group (with 1 proton). $\checkmark$

        *Award [1] if both groups are identified but neither is assigned to a specific signal;*\
        *Award [2] if both groups are identified and only one is assigned to a specific signal.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        Integral sizes represent the number of hydrogen atoms represented by a particular signal.

    2.  Signal at 0.96 ppm is a triplet / has three peaks due to coupling (of $\ce{CH3}$) with $\ce{CH2};\, \checkmark$

        Signal at 1.64 ppm is a quartet / has four peaks due to coupling (of $\ce{CH2}$) with $\ce{CH3}.\, \checkmark$

        *Award [1] if both multiplets are named and explained but neither is assigned to a specific signal;*\
        *Award [2] if both multiplets are named and explained but only one is assigned to a specific signal;*\
        *Award [1] if coupling between $\ce{CH2}$ and $\ce{CH3}$ is identified but one or more multiplets are not named;*\
        *Award [1] if both multiplets are named but the coupling group is not stated for one or more of the signals;*\
        *Award [0] if **both** multiplets are named but coupling between $\ce{CH2}$ and $\ce{CH3}$ is not identified.*

        $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

        Splitting patterns follow the $n+1$ rule where $n$ is the number of adjacent protons. Chemical shift data from the data booklet identify these as the methyl (0.9$-$1.0 ppm) and alkenyl ($1.8-3.1$ ppm) protons.
