# CH1053

The high resolution $\ce{^1H}$ NMR spectrum of an organic compound with molecular formula $\ce{C4H9Cl}$ has four signals. One of these signals is a doublet.

What is the name of this compound?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$1$-$chlorobutane

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$2$-$chlorobutane

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$1$-$chloro$-$2$-$methylpropane

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$2$-$chloro$-$2$-$methylpropane

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
In high$-$resolution $^1$H NMR, three main features are important: the number of signals, their overall intensity, and the splitting pattern. The number of signals tells us how many different chemical environments the hydrogens occupy. Hydrogens in the same environment are equivalent and give one signal.  The overall intensity reveals the number of hydrogens in that environment. The splitting of each signal follows the $n+1$ rule: if a set of protons has $n$ neighboring protons on an adjacent carbon, the signal will be split into $n+1$ peaks. For example, one neighboring proton produces a doublet, two neighbors a triplet, and so on.

For the formula $\ce{C4H9Cl},$ there are four possible structural isomers. Their predicted spectra are summarized in the table below:

|Molecule|Predicted NMR features
:-:|:--

![CH1053msb](images/CH1053msb.png)$\newline$ 2$-$chlorobutane | ***Chemical environments:*** 4 $\newline$ ***Intensity:*** 3:1:2:3$\newline$ ***Splitting:*** doublet, multiplet, multiplet, triplet
![CH1053msc](images/CH1053msc.png) $\newline$ 1$-$chloro$-$2$-$methylpropane|***Chemical environments:*** 3 $\newline$ ***Intensity:*** 2:1:6 $\newline$ ***Splitting:*** double, multiplet, doublet
![CH1053msd](images/CH1053msd.png)$\newline$ 2$-$chloro$-$2$-$methylpropane| ***Chemical environments:*** 1 $\newline$ ***Intensity:*** 9 $\newline$ ***Splitting:***  singlet

$^\star$ *Multiplet is used when a signal will be split by two or more different chemical environments.*

Only 2$-$chlorobutane gives four distinct signals and includes a doublet.

This question is categorized as hard as students must combine the interpretation of these structural isomers with the splitting pattern in the $\ce{^1H}$ NMR spectrum.
