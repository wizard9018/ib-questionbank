# CH0498

Eugenol, the main fragrance compound in cloves, has the following structural formula.

![21.1-CH0498](images/CH0498.png)

The mass spectrum of eugenol is shown. The molecular ion produces a peak at an $m/z$ value of 164.

![21.1-CH04981](images/CH04981.png)
[Source: webbook.nist.gov]

Which fragments are lost from eugenol to produce peaks with $m/z$ values of 137 and 149?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
  | | $\enskip$Peak at $\mathbf{m/z= 137}\enskip$ | $\enskip$Peak at $\mathbf{m/z = 149}\enskip$ |
  | :-: | :-: | :-: |
  | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Loss of $\ce{C2H3}$ | Loss of $\ce{OH}$ |
  | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Loss of $\ce{OCH3}$| Loss of $\ce{OH}$ |
  | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Loss of $\ce{C2H3}$ | Loss of $\ce{CH3}$  |
  | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Loss of $\ce{OCH3}$ | Loss of $\ce{CH3}$  |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

The fragment ions have $m/z$ values $(164 - 137) = 27$ and $(164 - 149) = 15$ less than the molecular ion.

These differences correspond to the masses of the $\ce{C2H3}$ and $\ce{CH3}$ groups. $\ce{[M - OCH3]+}$ and $\ce{[M - OH]+}$ would produce peaks at $m/z = 133$ and 147, respectively.

Although determining the ion masses is a relatively simple process, the question is categorized as hard since the student must perform several such calculations without a calculator to identify the correct fragment ions.
