## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

This question tests the ability of the student to interpret multiplet NMR signals using the $n+1$ rule, without the assistance of integrals. The use of the $n+1$ rule for non-equivalent neighbouring environments is approximately valid as the protons in the alkane display similar chemical shifts.

In option A, two methyl groups are equivalent and adjacent to one proton, producing a doublet; the next proton in the chain is adjacent to two $\ce{CH3}$ groups and one $\ce{CH2}$ group, producing a nonet; and the first, second and third proton environments in the $\ce{CH2CH2CH3}$ chain produce a quartet, sextet and triplet, respectively.

Option B produces a doublet, a triplet, a quartet, a hextet and an octet; option C produces a doublet, two triplets, a quintet and an octet; and option D produces a doublet, a quartet, a quintet and a nonet (only four signals).

This question is very hard as the student must deduce the splitting for several proton environments with many neighbouring groups and distinguish between compounds with very similar splitting patterns (especially options A and B).
