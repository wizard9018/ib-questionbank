# CH2067

[Maximum marks: 8]

A hydrocarbon $\ce{C_xH_y}$ can be used as a gasoline additive.

1.  It is 14.4\% hydrogen. Deduce the empirical formula of the hydrocarbon. [2 marks]

2.  The mass spectrum of the hydrocarbon is given below.

    ![CH2067massspec](images/CH2067massspec.png)

    1.  Determine the relative molar mass of the compound and deduce the formula of the molecular ion.    [2 marks]

    2.  Deduce the formula of the fragment responsible for the peak at 55. [1 mark]

3.  The IR spectrum shows a strong peak at $1643\text{ cm}^{-1}.$ Using Section 20 of the IB Chemistry data booklet, suggest the bond responsible. [1 mark]

4.  The $\ce{^1H NMR}$ of the compound has five signals tabulated here:

    | Chemical shift /ppm | Integration trace | Splitting pattern |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | 0.91 | 3 | triplet |
    | 1.43 | 2 | multiplet |
    | 2.02 | 2 | multiplet |
    | 4.95 | 2 | doublet |
    | 5.81 | 1 | multiplet |

    Suggest the skeletal structure and IUPAC name of the hydrocarbon. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $n_\ce{H} = \dfrac{14.4}{1.01} = 14.26\text{ mol H}$ ***AND*** $n_C = \dfrac{85.6}{12.01} = 7.127\text{ mol C}\ \checkmark$

    $\ce{CH2}\ \checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The hydrocarbon is $14.4\%$ hydrogen. The remaining $85.6\%$ corresponds to carbon. The empirical formula can be deduced from here.  Assume 100 g of the compound and find the moles of carbon and hydrogen, and their ratio.

    $\begin{aligned}
    n_\ce{H} &= \dfrac{14.4\text{ g H}}{1.01\text{ g mol}^{-1}} &= 14.26\text{ mol H} & \quad & \dfrac{14.26}{7.127} \approx 2 \\
    n_\ce{C} &= \dfrac{85.6\text{ g C}}{12.01\text{ f mol}^{-1}} &= 7.127\text{ mol C} & \quad & \dfrac{7.127}{7.127} = 1
    \end{aligned}$

    The empirical formula is $\ce{CH2},$ as there is a $1:2$ ratio.

2.
    1.  Molecular ion peak at $m/z=70\ \checkmark$

        Molecular formula: $\ce{C5H10}\ \checkmark$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        The mass spectrum shows a molecular ion peak as the peak with the largest $m/z$ value, $70,$ corresponding to the molecule's molecular mass. The mass of the empirical formula is $12.01 + 2\times 1.01 = 14.03\text{ amu}.$ To find the number of units in the molecule, use the ratio.

        $\dfrac{70}{14.03} \approx 5$

        The molecular formula is five times the empirical formula $\ce{C5H10}.$

    2.  $\ce{C4H7+}$

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

        This fragment has lost $70 - 55 = 15$ which is associated with a methyl group. The fragment can be determing by subtracting a methyl group from the molecular formula $\ce{C5H_{10}}.$ All fragments that are observed in the mass spectrum have a positive charge so the fragment is $\ce{C4H7+}.$

3.  $\ce{C=C}$ bond

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The peak at $1643\text{ cm}^{-1}$ corresponds to a $\ce{C=C}$ stretch. The molecule is an alkene, consistent with its molecular formula of $\ce{C5H10}$.

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$

    The peaks with chemical shift 4.95 and 5.81 correspond to H atoms attached to alkenyl carbons wich are found between 4.5 and 6.0 ppm. The alkene must be in position 1 to have 3 hydrogen atoms (2 of one kind) attached.   Reasonable structures could be pent$\text{--}1\text{--}$ene or 3$\text{--}$methylbut$\text{--}1\text{--}$ene.

    The triplet with intensity 3 and a multiplet with intensity 2 could indicate an ethyl group, supported by the presence of a peak at 29 in the mass spectrum. Further support comes from the 5 distinct hydrogen environments. Pent$\text{--}1\text{--}$ene seems the most likely candidate.
