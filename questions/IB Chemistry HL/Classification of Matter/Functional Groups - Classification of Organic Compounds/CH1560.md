# CH1560

Consider the isomers given below.

![CH1560_Image1](images/CH1560_Image1.png)

Which pair are *cis/trans* geometric isomers?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Structures 1 and 2

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Structures 2 and 3

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Structures 2 and 4

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Structures 1 and 3

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

*Cis/trans* isomers, also known as geometric isomers, differ in the spatial arrangement of their atoms around a double bond or within a ring structure, where there is a barrier to rotation. In cyclic molecules like cyclobutane, the carbon$\text{--}$carbon bonds in the ring prevent free rotation, so the positions of substituents like bromo groups can vary relative to the plane of the ring. If both bromo groups are positioned on the same side of the ring's plane, the isomer is classified as *cis*. If the bromo groups are on opposite sides of the plane, the isomer is classified as *trans*.

The four given structures of dibromocyclobutane are:

||| 

| --: | --: | 

Structure 1:|     *trans*$\text{--}$1,3$\text{--}$dibromocyclobutane

Structure 2:|         *cis*$\text{--}$1,2$\text{--}$dibromocyclobutane

Structure 3:|          *cis*$\text{--}$1,3$\text{--}$dibromocyclobutane

Structure 4: |        1,1$\text{--}$dibromocyclobutane

To identify the *cis/trans* isomers among these structures, focus on the position of the bromine atoms in relation to the plane of the cyclobutane ring. Structures 1 and 3 are geometric isomers because they both have bromine atoms attached to the same carbons (1 and 3), but they differ in whether these bromine atoms are on the same side (*cis*) or opposite sides (*trans*) of the ring's plane. Specifically, Structure 1 is the *trans* isomer, and Structure 3 is the *cis* isomer.

This question is categorized as medium. Understanding this concept involves recognizing that in cyclic compounds, restricted rotation leads to different spatial arrangements, or stereoisomers, which must be analyzed by comparing the positions of substituents relative to the ring's plane.
