# CH0563

A transition metal complex ion appears yellow due to the absorption of visible light, which has wavelengths in the range $400-700$ nm.

The optical absorption spectrum of the complex ion is shown.

![CH0563](images/CH0563.png)
[Source: own work, based on data from https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.28835-0]

Why does the complex ion appear yellow in colour?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$The most strongly absorbed visible light is yellow in colour

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$The absorbed visible light includes all colours other than yellow

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$The most strongly absorbed visible light is opposite yellow in the colour wheel

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$The most strongly absorbed visible light is adjacent to yellow in the colour wheel

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Colour is produced because the absorbed colour (purple/violet) is complementary to the observed colour, i.e. opposite this colour in the colour wheel.

It is possible for colour to be observed because all other colours are absorbed (cf. the green colour of leaves) but the spectrum illustrates that this is not the case: the absorbed wavelengths are at one end of the visible range.

Although the concept of complementary colours is covered explicitly in the syllabus, this question is moderately difficult as the student must interpret a UV-vis spectrum and identify the correct definition of a complementary colour.
