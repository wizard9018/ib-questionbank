    1.  $52\ \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

        

        The number of neutrons is calculated as the difference between the mass and atomic numbers.

        We can deduce the number of electrons in niobium and the number of protons (atomic number) from the electronic configuration. Krypton has 36 electrons. We should add 5 electrons as the electronic configuration is $\ce{[Kr]}\ 4d^4\ 5s^1,$ meaning we have 41 electrons and 41 protons. The atomic number is 41.

        Number of neutrons = $93 - 41 = 52$

    2.  it has incomplete $d$ subshells $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

        

        Transition metals are elements that have an incomplete $d$ subshell as atoms or  as cations. Niobium has incomplete $d$ subshells as its electronic configuration is $\ce{[Kr]}\ 4d^4\ 5s^1.$

2.  

    1.  $+5\ \checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

        

        For transition metals with variable oxidation states, determining the oxidation state requires more than finding the element's position in the periodic table.  Instead, we rely on the rule that the sum of the oxidation states of all atoms in a neutral compound must equal  zero.  In $\ce{NbCl5},$ chlorine is much more electronegative than niobium (3.0 *vs.* 1.6) so chlorine will have an oxidation state of $-1.$

        

        We can set up an algebraic equation to determine the unknown oxidation state of niobium:

        $x + 5(-1) = 0$

        Solving for $x,$ we find

        $x = +5 $

        Thus the oxidation state of niobium is +5.

    2.  $\ce{Nb/Nb^5+}$ is a Lewis acid, $\ce{Cl/Cl-}$ is a Lewis base $\checkmark$

        Cl donates a lone pair/forms coordinate covalent bond to Nb of the other molecule $\checkmark$

        $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

        

        In the interaction between two $\ce{NbCl5}$ molecules, each niobium atom acts as a Lewis acid by accepting an electron pair from a chlorine atom in the other $\ce{NbCl5}$ molecule, where that chlorine acts as a Lewis base. Specifically, the lone pair on one chlorine atom in each $\ce{NbCl5}$ molecule is donated to the niobium atom of the other molecule, forming a coordinate covalent bond. This results in the formation of two bridging chlorine atoms, each bonded to one niobium via a simple covalent bond and to the other niobium via a coordinate covalent bond, creating the compound in the question.

4.  $E = \dfrac{652000\text{\ J mol}^{-1}}{6.02\times 10^{23}\text{\ mol}^{-1}}\ \checkmark$

    $f = 1.63\times 10^{15}\text{\ s}^{-1}\ \checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

    

    The convergence limit can be deduced from the first ionization energy given.

    The first ionization energy of Nb is $652\text{\ kJ mol}^{-1},$ which corresponds to the energy needed to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of atoms in the gaseous state. Thus, the energy needed to remove one electron is calculated by dividing by Avogadro's number.

    $E = \dfrac{IE}{N_A} = \dfrac{652000\text{\ J mol}^{-1}}{6.02\times 10^{23}\text{\ mol}^{-1}} = 1.08\times 10^{-18}\text{\ J}$

    The frequency of the convergence limit can be deduced from the energy relationship with Planck's constant.

    $E = hf$ 

    $f = \dfrac{E}{h} = \dfrac{1.08\times 10^{-18}\text{\ J}}{6.63\times 10^{-34}\text{\ J s}} = 1.63\times 10^{15}\text{\ s}^{-1}$
