# CH0572

**Maximum mark: 9**

The crystalline solid $\ce{CsScCl3}$ is an ionic lattice of caesium ions and scandium complexes. A three$-$dimensional model of the bonding in $\ce{CsScCl3}$ is shown.

![13.2-CH0572](images/CH0572.png)
[Source: own work, based on data from materials.springer.com/isp/crystallographic/docs/sd_1925872; image prepared using Olex2]

Each scandium complex contains one scandium ion and six chloride ligands, and each chloride ligand bonds to two scandium ions. The sharing of ligands between complexes does $\textbf{not}$ affect the oxidation state of the chloride ligand or the arrangement of $d$ orbitals in the scandium ion.

1.  State the molecular geometry of the scandium complex in $\ce{CsScCl3}$. [1 mark]

2.  Suggest why it is possible for each chloride ligand to bond to more than one scandium ion. [2 marks]

3.  Explain how bonding to the chloride ligands affects the relative energies of the $d$ orbitals in the scandium ion. [2 marks]

4.  Determine the oxidation state of scandium in $\ce{CsScCl3}.$ [1 mark]

5.  Scandium typically has a $+3$ oxidation state.  Based on your answers to (c) and (d), state and explain  the difference in appearance of $\ce{CsScCl3}$ and most other scandium complexes. [3 marks]

1.  octahedral $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    Six electron domains around the central scandium ion result in an octahedral shape according to VSEPR theory.

2.  chloride has another lone / nonbonding pair  $\checkmark$\
    second coordinate bond can be formed with a scandium ion  $\checkmark$

3.  $d-$orbitals split into two groups $\checkmark$\
    lower enery group contains 3 orbitals and the higher group contains 2 $\checkmark$\
    *Correct drawing earns both marks.*

4.  $+2$ / II $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The oxidation state of $\ce{Sc}$ in $\ce{CsScCl3}$ is +2 since Cs has oxidation state $+1$, Cl has oxidation state $-1$, and the material has zero net charge.

5.  Sc(II) / $\ce{CsScCl3}$ is colored **AND**   Sc(III) is colorless $\checkmark$\
    $d\text{--}d$ electronic transitions absorb visible light and make transition metal complexes appear colored $\checkmark$
    Sc(II) has one $d-$ electron while Sc(III) has none $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$\
    $\ce{Sc}$(III) complexes have an empty $d$-subshell and so do not appear coloured as there is no possibility of a $d-d$ electron transition.
