# CH0399

Compounds of which of the following transition metal ions have unpaired electrons?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Sc^2+}$
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}\ce{Ti^4+}$
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}\ce{V^3+}$

- A.$\hspace{1em}$I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

- D.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
To find unpaired electrons, remove electrons from the $4s$ subshell first, then from $3d\textrm{:}$

-   $\ce{Sc}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^1\,4s^2$ $\rightarrow$ $\ce{Sc^{2+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^1$ — one unpaired electron.

-   $\ce{Ti}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^2\,4s^2$ $\rightarrow$ $\ce{Ti^{4+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^0$ — empty $d$ subshell, no unpaired electrons.

-   $\ce{V}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^3\,4s^2$ $\rightarrow$ $\ce{V^{3+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^2$ — two unpaired electrons.

Only $\ce{Sc^{2+}}$ and $\ce{V^{3+}}$ have unpaired $d$ electrons. Note that $\ce{Ti^{4+}}$ has the same electron configuration as argon and is not considered a transition metal ion, since a transition metal is defined as having a partially filled $d$ subshell in at least one stable oxidation state.
