# CH2179

Which factor explains why boron has a lower ionization energy than beryllium?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$Beryllium has a stable electronic configuration.

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$Beryllium has a greater atomic radius.

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$Boron has a greater effective nuclear charge.

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$Boron loses an electron from a different sublevel.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

The student must understand that this difference is a discontinuity in the trend for the ionization energies across period 2. Ionization energies tend to increase across periods. To understand the discontinuity, the electronic configurations should be analyzed.

**Be** ***first ionization energy:***

$1s^2\ 2s^2 \longrightarrow 1s^2\ 2s^1$

**B** ***first ionization energy***

$1s^2\ 2s^2\ 2p^1 \longrightarrow 1s^2\ 2s^2$

Even though boron has a greater nuclear charge, it is easier to remove its outer electron as it is in a higher$\text{--}$energy sublevel $(2s$ and $2p$ orbitals do not have the same energy). This discontinuity is evidence of the existence of sublevels in the atomic structure.

This question is categorized as hard as the student must identify this trend as a discontinuity and should be able to give reasons behind the discontinuity.
