# CH1256

The absorption spectrum of a transition metal complex ion with the formula $\ce{[M(H2O)6]^{3+}}$ is shown below.

![CH1256](images/CH1256.png)

Which combination about $\ce{[M(H2O)6]^{3+}}$ is correct?

$\mbox{}\\[-25pts]$

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
    | | Colour | Effect of the ligand |
    | :-: | :-: | :-: |
    | A. |  red, absorbs green light | $d$ orbitals in the metal split |
    | B. | red, absorbs green light | $p$ orbitals in the metal split |
    | C. |  green, absorbs red light | $d$ orbitals in the metal split |
    | D. |  green, absorbs red light | $p$ orbitals in the metal split |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The absorption spectrum shows strong absorption between 650 nm and 700 nm, which lies in the red region of the visible spectrum. Because the observed colour of a complex corresponds to the complementary colour of the light absorbed, the complex ion appears green. In transition metal complexes, the colour arises from electronic transitions between split $d$ orbitals within the metal ion. When the lone pairs of the ligands approach the central metal ion, they interact electrostatically with the $d$ electrons, causing the originally degenerate $d$ orbitals to split into two energy levels.

Visible light of a particular wavelength is absorbed to promote an electron from the lower to the higher energy $d$ orbital. The remaining transmitted or reflected light is of the complementary colour, which is the colour observed. Therefore, $\ce{[M(H2O)6]^{3+}}$ appears green because it absorbs red light, and the splitting occurs among the $d$ orbitals, not the $p$ orbitals.

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must interpret the absorption spectrum and understand the origin of the color in transition metal complex ions.
