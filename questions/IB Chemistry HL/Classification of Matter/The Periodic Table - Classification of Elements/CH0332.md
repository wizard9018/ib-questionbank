# CH0332

Which oxide can be classified as amphoteric?

-    A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Cl2O}$

-    B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CaO}$

-    C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{GeO2}$

-    D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{Na2O}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The student must distinguish between basic, acidic, and amphoteric oxides by reasoning from the position of each element in the periodic table.

$\ce{Na2O}$ is a basic oxide. Sodium is a reactive metal in Group 1, and its oxide reacts with water to form a strong base:

$\ce{Na2O}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,NaOH}\textrm{(aq)}$

$\ce{CaO}$ is also a basic oxide. Calcium is a metal in Group 2, and its oxide reacts with water to form a strong base:

$\ce{CaO}\textrm{(s)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow \ce{Ca(OH)2}\textrm{(aq)}$

$\ce{Cl2O}$ is an acidic oxide. Chlorine is a nonmetal in Group 17, and its oxide reacts with water to form hypochlorous acid:

$\ce{Cl2O}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)} \longrightarrow 2\ce{\,HOCl}\textrm{(aq)}$

$\ce{GeO2}$ is amphoteric. Germanium is a metalloid in Group 14, and unlike the clearly metallic or nonmetallic elements above, its oxide can react with both acids and bases:

Acidic behaviour: $\ce{GeO2}\textrm{(s)} + 2\ce{\,NaOH}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{Na2GeO3}\textrm{(aq)} + \ce{H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

Basic behaviour: $\ce{GeO2}\textrm{(s)} + 4\ce{\,HCl}\textrm{(aq)} \longrightarrow \ce{GeCl4}\textrm{(aq)} + 2\ce{\,H2O}\textrm{(l)}$

The student should recognise that metal oxides are basic and nonmetal oxides are acidic. After classifying $\ce{Na2O}$ and $\ce{CaO}$ as basic and $\ce{Cl2O}$ as acidic, $\ce{GeO2}$ remains as the only candidate. Germanium sits on the metal$-$nonmetal boundary, and its oxide exhibits intermediate (amphoteric) behaviour.

This question is categorised as medium, as the student must classify four oxides using periodic trends rather than recall a memorised fact.
