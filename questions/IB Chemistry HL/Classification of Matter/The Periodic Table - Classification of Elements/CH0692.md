# CH0692

Which of the below statements about the halogens in group 17 is/are correct?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}$The electron affinity for bromine is the energy required to add one mole of electrons to gaseous bromine molecules to form bromide ions.

-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$The electron affinity for bromine is smaller in magnitude than that of chlorine.

-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Potassium reacts more vigorously with chlorine than with bromine.

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$II only

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Electron affinity is the energy change when one mole of electrons is added to one mole of gaseous *atoms* to form one mole of gaseous ions. Statement I is incorrect because it refers to bromine *molecules* rather than atoms. The electron affinity of bromine describes this process:

$\ce{Br}\textrm{(g)} + \ce{e-} \longrightarrow \ce{Br-}\textrm{(g)}$

In bromine $([\ce{Ar}]\, 3d^{10}\, 4s^2\, 4p^5),$ the electron is added to a $4p$ orbital, which is farther from the nucleus than the $3p$ orbital in chlorine $([\ce{Ne}]\, 3s^2\, 3p^5).$ The additional inner-shell electrons in bromine, including the complete $3d^{10}$ subshell, provide greater shielding of the nuclear charge. The incoming electron therefore experiences a weaker effective nuclear attraction, and less energy is released when it is gained. Chlorine's $3p$ orbital is closer to the nucleus with less shielding, so the electron is held more tightly and the electron affinity is more exothermic. Statement II is correct.

This same reasoning explains Statement III. Because chlorine attracts electrons more strongly, it is a more powerful oxidizing agent than bromine. The energy released in forming the $\ce{Cl^-}$ ion is greater than that released in forming the $\ce{Br^-}$ ion, so the reaction of potassium with chlorine is more exothermic and proceeds more vigorously. Statement III is correct.
