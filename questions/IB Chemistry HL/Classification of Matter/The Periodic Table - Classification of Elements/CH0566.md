# CH0566

The $d-$electron configuration of a transition metal complex is shown.

![CH0566](images/CH0566.png)

A reaction causes the $d-$orbital splitting energy $\Delta$ to increase but does **not** affect the shape of the complex or the ligands bonding to the metal ion.

Which reaction equation is correct?

-   A. $\hspace{1em}\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]^2+ -> [Cr(H2O)6]^3+}$

-   B. $\hspace{1em}\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]^3+ -> [Cr(H2O)6]^2+}$

-   C. $\hspace{1em}\ce{[Mn(H2O)6]^2+ -> [Mn(H2O)6]^3+}$

-   D. $\hspace{1em}\ce{[Mn(H2O)6]^3+ -> [Mn(H2O)6]^2+}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The diagram shows four $d$ electrons arranged one per orbital across the lower $t_{2g}$ set and one in the upper $e_g$ set of an octahedral complex. The task is to identify which complex has four $d$ electrons.

For chromium complexes: $\ce{Cr}$ is $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^5\,4s^1.$ Removing two electrons gives $\ce{Cr^{2+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^4$ — four $d$ electrons. Removing three electrons gives $\ce{Cr^{3+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^3$ — three $d$ electrons.

For manganese complexes: $\ce{Mn}$ is $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^5\,4s^2.$ Removing two electrons gives $\ce{Mn^{2+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^5$ — five $d$ electrons. Removing three electrons gives $\ce{Mn^{3+}}\textrm{:}$ $\textrm{[Ar]}\,3d^4$ — four $d$ electrons.

The diagram therefore matches either $\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]^{2+}}$ or $\ce{[Mn(H2O)6]^{3+}}.$

The reaction must increase $\Delta$ without changing the ligands or geometry. A higher oxidation state means a greater positive charge on the metal centre, which increases the electrostatic attraction between the metal ion and the lone pairs on the ligands. This stronger interaction pushes the $d$ orbitals further apart, increasing the crystal field splitting energy $\Delta.$ The reaction must therefore be an oxidation. $\ce{Cr^{2+}} \rightarrow \ce{Cr^{3+}}$ is an oxidation that increases the charge, increasing $\Delta.$ $\ce{Mn^{3+}} \rightarrow \ce{Mn^{2+}}$ would be a reduction, decreasing the crystal field splitting.
