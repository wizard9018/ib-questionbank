# CH0567

Transition metal complex $\textbf{P}$ appears $\textbf{blue}$ in solution.

Complex $\textbf{Q}$ is produced when the metal ion in $\textbf{P}$ is oxidized, increasing the crystal field splitting energy.

Complex $\textbf{R}$ is produced when the ligands in $\textbf{P}$ are replaced by weaker$-$field ligands.

The optical absorption spectra of the three complexes are shown.

![13.2-CH0567](images/CH0567.png)

Which colour of light is most strongly absorbed by each of the three complexes?

- :::tableoptions{col1="hide"}
  | | $\enskip$ Colour absorbed by P $\enskip$ | $\enskip$Colour absorbed by Q$\enskip$ |$\enskip$Colour absorbed by R$\enskip$ |
  | :-: | :-: | :-: |:-: |
  | A.$\hspace{1em}$ | blue | orange  |red  |
  | B.$\hspace{1em}$ | blue | red  |orange  |
  | C.$\hspace{1em}$ | orange | blue |red  |
  | D.$\hspace{1em}$ | orange | red |blue  |

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Complexes appear coloured because they absorb light of the complementary colour. Complex **P** appears blue so must absorb light nearer the red end of the spectrum. Complex **Q** is produced by oxidation, which increases the separation of the $d$ orbitals as oxidation number increases. The wavelength of the absorbed light for **Q** is therefore shorter than **P**. Finally, complex **R** contains ligands that are weaker$-$field which decreases the splitting energy, so must absorb longer wavelength light than complex **P**.

This question is medium as the student must understand the link between the energy and wavelength of absorbed light, and deduce the relative $d$ orbital splitting energies of unknown complexes.
