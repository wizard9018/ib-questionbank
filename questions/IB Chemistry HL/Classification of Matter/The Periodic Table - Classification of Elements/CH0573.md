# CH0573

**Maximum mark: 9**

Brass, an alloy of copper and zinc, reacts with acids to produce bubbles. When the acid is hydrochloric acid, the solution remains colourless. When the acid is nitric acid, the solution turns blue.

![13.2-573](images/13.2-573.png)

1.  Identify the gas produced when brass reacts with hydrochloric acid. [1 mark]

2.  State the $\textbf{range}$ of wavelengths of light, in nm, that are most strongly absorbed by the product solution in the beaker where nitric acid is used, using Section 15 of the data booklet. [1 mark]

3.  When brass reacts with nitric acid, $\textbf{two}$ octahedral complexes are formed with water acting as the $\textbf{only}$ ligand. The metal atoms in the two complexes each have a +2 oxidation state.

    1.  Explain how water forms a bond with a metal ion to form a complex. [2 marks]

    2.  Draw the structural formula of the complex responsible for the colour of the product solution in the beaker with nitric acid, using dash$-$and$-$wedge notation to indicate the geometry of the metal$-$ligand bonds. [3 marks]

    3.  Explain why the second complex does not affect the colour of the solution. [2 marks]
