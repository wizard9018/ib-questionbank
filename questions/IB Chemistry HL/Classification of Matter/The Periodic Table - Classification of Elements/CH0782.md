# CH0782

Which statement(s) regarding the first ionization energies of the period 2 elements correctly support the existence of sublevels in atoms?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}$There is an increase in ionization energies across the table from left to right.

-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$Boron has a lower first ionization energy than beryllium because boron has an electron in the $2p$ sublevel.

-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$Oxygen has a lower first ionization energy than nitrogen due to oxygen's greater effective nuclear charge.

- A.$\hspace{1em}$I only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$II only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$I and II only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$II and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

The general trend for ionization energy across a period is to increase, but small decreases are observed between $\ce{Be}$ and $\ce{B}$ and again between $\ce{N}$ and $\ce{O}$, making statement I incorrect.

These small decreases are often used as evidence for subshells in atoms.

Boron has the electron configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^1.$ Its lower ionization energy is associated with the $2p$ electron being farther away from the nucleus *and* more shielded than the $2s$ electrons.  Boron has a lower ionization energy than beryllium. Statement II is correct.

Oxygen has the electron configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^4.$  The fourth electron in the $2p$ sublevel shares an orbital and experiences more electron$-$electron repulsion making its ionization energy lower.  Statement III is incorrect.
