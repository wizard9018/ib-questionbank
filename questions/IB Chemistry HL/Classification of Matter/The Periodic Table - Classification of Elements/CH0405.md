# CH0405

**Maximum mark: 7**

Copper is a transition metal that reacts with chlorine to produce both $\ce{CuCl}$ and $\ce{CuCl2}$.

1.  State the charge and electron configurations of copper ions in each of the above compounds. [2 marks]

2.  Use your answer to part (a) to explain why $\ce{CuCl}$ is a colourless compound whereas $\ce{CuCl2}$ is green-blue. [2 marks]

3.  In the presence of excess $\ce{HCl}$, copper ions form complexes with chloride ligands to produce $\ce{H2[CuCl4]}$.

    1.  Explain why chloride ions are able to act as ligands. [2 marks]

    2.  Deduce the charge of the complex ion. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $\ce{Cu+}$ **AND** $1s^2\,2s^2\,3s^2\,3p^6\,3d^{10}\, \checkmark$

    $\ce{Cu^2+}$ **AND** $1s^2\,2s^2\,3s^2\,3p^6\,3d^9\, \checkmark$

    *ALLOW condensed configurations $\ce{[Ar]}\,3d^{10}$ and $\ce{[Ar]}\,3d^9$.*

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    Chloride ions have a $1-$ charge, therefore for neutral ionic compounds, the charges of the copper ions must be $1+$ and $2+$ respectively.

    The electron configuration of $\ce{Cu}$ is $1s^2\,2s^2\,3s^2\,3p^6\,4s^1\,3d^{10}$ (full d subshell is more stable).

    One $4s$ electron is lost to form $\ce{Cu+}$, one $4s$ and one $3d$ electron are lost to form $\ce{Cu^2+}$.

2.  The copper ion in $\ce{CuCl/Cu+}$ has a filled $d$ subshell/$d$ orbitals $\checkmark$

    $\textit{ALLOW has all 10 electrons in d subshell}.$

    The copper ion in $\ce{CuCl2/Cu^2+}$ has a partially filled $d$ subshell/$d$ orbitals. $\checkmark$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    See part (a) for electron configurations.
