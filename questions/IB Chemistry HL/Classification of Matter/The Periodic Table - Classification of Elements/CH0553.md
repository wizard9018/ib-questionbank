# CH0553

The atomic numbers in two periods of the $d$ block of the periodic table are shown below:

 ![CH0533](images/CH0533.png)

Why does the atomic number increase by a larger amount across period 6 of the $d-$block than across period 5?   

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$In the period 6 lanthanoids, electrons are added to 7 $f$ orbitals rather than the $d$ subshell.

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$In the period 6 lanthanoids, electrons are added to 14 $f$ orbitals rather than the $d$ subshell.

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$In the period 6 actinoids, electrons are added to 7 $f$ orbitals rather than the $d$ subshell.

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$In the period 6 actinoids, electrons are added to 14 $f$ orbitals rather than the $d$ subshell.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\

In period 5, the atomic number increases by $1$ across each element in the $d$ block (from $39$ to $48).$ In period 6, the atomic number jumps from $57$ to $72,$ an increase of $15$ across the same number of $d-$block positions. The extra $14$ elements are the lanthanoids, which sit between $\ce{La}$ $(Z = 57)$ and $\ce{Hf}$ $(Z = 72).$

The lanthanoids arise because the $4f$ subshell is filled before the $5d$ subshell resumes filling. The $f$ subshell contains $7$ orbitals, each holding $2$ electrons, accommodating $14$ elements in total.  

The actinoids belong to period 7, not period 6, so any option referring to actinoids is incorrect.
