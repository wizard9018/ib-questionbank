# CH0565

A cobalt(II) complex absorbs light near the red end of the visible spectrum.

Which of the following statements is true?

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$The complex contains one unpaired electron and appears orange in colour

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$The complex contains three unpaired electrons and appears orange in colour

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$The complex contains one unpaired electron and appears blue in colour

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$The complex contains three unpaired electrons and appears blue in colour

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
Cobalt has the electron configuration $\ce{[Ar]}\,3d^74s^2$ so cobalt(II) complexes contain seven $d$ electrons. The $d$ orbitals are occupied by single electrons where possible, so three electrons are unpaired.

The complex absorbs light at the red end of the visible spectrum so could not appear orange, since complementary colours are not adjacent in the visible spectrum.

Of the options given, blue is the only possible colour.

This question is hard as the student must recall or deduce the complementary colour of the absorbed light and determine the electron configuration of the complex without the aid of diagrams.
