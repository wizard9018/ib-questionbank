# CH0569

The colours of many transition metal complexes can be explained using the following colour wheel model.

![13.2-CH0569](images/CH0569.png)

A metal ion $\ce{M^2+}$ bonds with ligands $\textbf{X}$, $\textbf{Y}$, and $\textbf{Z}$ to produce the green complex $\ce{[M\textbf{X}_6]^2+}$, orange complex $\ce{[M\textbf{Y}_6]^2+}$, and blue complex $\ce{[M\textbf{Z}_6]^2+}$. The relative strength of the ligands is determined by placing the complexes in order of increasing $d$ orbital splitting energy.

What are the relative strengths of the ligands $\textbf{X}$, $\textbf{Y}$, and $\textbf{Z}?$

-   A.$\hspace{1em}\textbf{Y}<\textbf{Z}<\textbf{X}$

-   B.$\hspace{1em}\textbf{X}<\textbf{Z}<\textbf{Y}$

-   C.$\hspace{1em}\textbf{Y}<\textbf{X}<\textbf{Z}$

-   D.$\hspace{1em}\textbf{Z}<\textbf{X}<\textbf{Y}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
The colour of a complex is complementary to the colour of the light absorbed and appears opposite this colour on the colour wheel. Ligands producing smaller $d$ orbital splitting energies are characterised by longer absorbed wavelengths and are considered weak$-$field ligands.

$\textbf{X}$ absorbs red light, the smallest energy (longest wavelength); $\textbf{Z}$ absorbs orange light, the second smallest energy; and $\textbf{Y}$ absorbs blue light, the highest energy (shortest wavelength).

This question is categorized as medium since the student must know how to interpret a colour wheel in the context of complex colours, understand the relationship between energy and wavelength, and correctly deduce the absorbed colours of multiple unknown complexes.
