# CH2042

Which set of statements best compares the $s\text{--}$block elements to $p\text{--}$block elements?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | $s\text{--}$block elements are generally more reactive metals than $p\text{--}$block metals. |
    | II. | $s\text{--}$block metals typically form cations with higher charges than $p\text{--}$block metals. |
    | III. | $s\text{--}$block elements tend to have lower first ionization energies than $p\text{--}$block metals in the same period. |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and III only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$II and III only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$I, II and III

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$

Metals in the $s\text{--}$block (Groups 1 and 2) are typically more reactive than $p\text{--}$block metals because they lose their valence electrons more readily due to their larger size and lower effective nuclear charge. Statement I is correct.

Metals in the $s-$block form $1+$ or $2+$ cations (*e.g.*, $\ce{Na+, Mg^2+}),$ not higher charges. Some $p\text{--}$block metals (*e.g.*, lead in oxidation state $+4)$ can form higher$\text{--}$charge cations. Statement II is incorrect.

Within the same period, $s\text{--}$block elements generally have lower first ionization energies than $p\text{--}$block elements. For example, Na has a much lower first ionization energy than Al (both in Period 3). Statement III is correct.
