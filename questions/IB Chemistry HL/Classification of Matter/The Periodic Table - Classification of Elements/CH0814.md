# CH0814

Which of the following statements are true regarding the transition metals?

-   :::tableoptions{col1="hide" col2="hide"}
    | | |
    | :- | :- |
    | I. | Only the electrons found in the $d$ orbitals are involved in bonding |
    | II. |    Transition metals typically have higher electrical conductivity due to the metallic bonding of the $d$ orbital electrons |
    | III. | All of the $3d$ block metals will form colored compounds |

-   A. $\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

-   B. $\hspace{1em}$I and II only

-   C. $\hspace{1em}$I only

-   D. $\hspace{1em}$II only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

I. The $3d$ and $4s$ orbitals are close in energy and all are involved in bonding. The $4s$ and $3d$ electrons make up the delocalized electrons that create the strong metallic bonding found in transition metals resulting in many of the desirable physical characteristics such as strength, high melting point, high thermal and electrical conductivity.

II. Transition metals generally have higher electrical conductivity than other metals due to their partially filled $d$-orbitals. In transition metals, the partially filled $d$-orbitals are more diffuse than the $s$-orbitals, which means they can more easily overlap with neighboring atoms to form metallic bonds. As a result, transition metals tend to have a higher density of conduction electrons, leading to higher electrical conductivity.

III. Zn will not form colored compounds due to the complete $3d$ sublevel. Generally, Zn is not considered a transition metal. Additionally, the most common ion of Sc ($\ce{Sc^3+}$) does not form colored compounds because both the single $3d$ electron and the two $4s$ electrons are lost in this common form of the ion.
