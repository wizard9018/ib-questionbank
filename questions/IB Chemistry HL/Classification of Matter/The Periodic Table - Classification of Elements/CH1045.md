# CH1045

Consider the diagram below.

![CH1045](images/CH1045.png)

Which statement(s) is(are) correct about the $\ce{Cu^2+}$ ion?

-   I.$\hspace{1.em}$It can act as an electron pair acceptor.
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$It is Brønsted$-$Lowry acid.
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$It forms coordinate bonds with water molecules.

-   A.$\hspace{1em}$I, II, and III

-   B.$\hspace{1em}$II only

-   C.$\hspace{1em}$III only

-   D.$\hspace{1em}$I and III only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:\
A Brønsted$-$Lowry acid is a proton donor, and a Lewis acid is an electron pair acceptor. $\ce{Cu^2+}$ can not donate a proton so it is not a Brønsted$-$Lowry acid. However, $\ce{Cu^2+}$ is electron deficient, so it can accept electron pairs and act as Lewis acid.  In transition metal chemistry, the metal cation accepts lone pairs from the ligands in a Lewis acid / Lewis base reaction to form coordinate covalent bonds in the transition metal complex ion.

$\ce{Cu^2+ + 6 H2O \rightarrow [Cu(H2O)6]^2+}$

![CH1045ms](images/CH1045ms.png)

This question is categorized as medium difficulty as students must distinguish the Lewis and Brønsted-Lowry theories of acids and bases and apply their knowledge on $\ce{Cu^2+}.$
