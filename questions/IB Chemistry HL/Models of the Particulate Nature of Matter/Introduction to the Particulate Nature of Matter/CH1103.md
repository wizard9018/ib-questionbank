# CH1103

**Maximum mark: 11**

The melting point of benzene is $5.5\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, and its boiling point is $80.1\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$

1. Draw the heating curve for benzene from $-10\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ to $120\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$ [3 marks]

2. State the state of matter of benzene at $-6.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$ [1 mark]

3. Describe the arrangement and movement of benzene molecules at $50.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}.$ [2 marks]

4. $5.00\,\text{g}$ of $\ce{NaCl}$ is added to $100\,\text{cm}^3$ of benzene. The salt is insoluble in the solvent.

 1. Determine the type of mixture formed. [1 mark]

 2. Suggest a technique that can be applied to separate and recover benzene and sodium chloride. Sketch a labeled diagram to show how the separation will take place. [4 marks]

3. molecules close to one another in an irregular arrangement $\checkmark$\

 molecules can slide over each other / flow $\checkmark$

 $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation}}$:\

 At $50.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, benzene is above the melting point ($5.5\,{}^\circ\text{C}$) and below the boiling point ($80.1\,{}^\circ\text{C}).$ Benzene is in the liquid state.

 In a liquid, the particles are very close together, making the liquid nearly incompressible. They have enough kinetic energy to overcome some of the attractive forces that hold them in fixed positions, so the particles are not arranged in a regular lattice and can slide past one another. Because the particles remain close together, a liquid has a definite volume, but since they can move freely, it takes the shape of its container.
