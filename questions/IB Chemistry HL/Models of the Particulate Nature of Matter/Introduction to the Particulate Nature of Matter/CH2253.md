# CH2253

A research team needs to separate a mixture containing:

-   ::indent
    -   * Vegetable oil (density $0.9 \textrm{\ g/cm}^3,$ immiscible with water)
    -   * Ethanol (density $0.8 \textrm{\ g/cm}^3,$ miscible with water, boiling point $78^\circ\textrm{C})$
    -   * Water (density $1.0 \textrm{\ g/cm}^3,$ boiling point $100^\circ\textrm{C})$

Which separation technique would be most appropriate to isolate pure water from this mixture?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$Decanting followed by crystallization

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$Filtration followed by evaporation

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$Simple distillation after removing the oil layer

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$Fractional distillation after removing the oil layer

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The mixture contains two separation problems: an immiscible liquid (oil) and two miscible liquids (ethanol and water). Each requires a different technique.

Oil floats on the aqueous layer because it is less dense and immiscible with water. It can be removed by separating funnel or decanting, leaving behind a homogeneous ethanol$-$water mixture.

Ethanol and water are miscible, so no physical boundary exists between them. The separation now depends on their boiling points $(78^\circ\textrm{C}$ and $100^\circ\textrm{C}).$ When two miscible liquids both have appreciable vapour pressures, fractional distillation is needed. The fractionating column provides repeated condensation and re$-$vaporisation cycles that progressively enrich the vapour in the more volatile component, giving a cleaner separation than a single vaporisation step.

Simple distillation is suited to separating a liquid from a dissolved solid, or from a second liquid whose boiling point is very much higher. For two miscible liquids with boiling points only $22^\circ\textrm{C}$ apart, the vapour produced at any temperature contains both components, and a single$-$stage distillation does not produce a pure distillate or a pure residue.

Filtration separates an insoluble solid from a liquid and is not applicable to a mixture of three liquids. Crystallization recovers a dissolved solid from solution, so it has no role here either.
