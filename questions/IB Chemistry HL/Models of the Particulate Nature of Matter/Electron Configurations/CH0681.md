## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation:}}$\

To answer this, we recall that in the ground state, first$-$row transition metals fill their $3d$ orbitals after the $4s$ orbital, following the general pattern $4s^2 3d^x$ where $x$ varies from 1 to 10. However, there are two notable exceptions: chromium $(4s^13d^5)$ and copper $(4s^1 3d^{10}).$ These occur because a half$-$filled  or completely filled $d$ subshell offers extra stability.

Thus, $4s^1 3d^5$ (chromium) and $4s^1 3d^{10}$ (copper) are both valid ground$-$state configurations. The $4s^2 3d^7$ configuration represents cobalt, which is also correct. The remaining diagram, $4s^0 3d^{10},$ cannot represent the ground state of any neutral transition metal atom in the first row. This configuration would occur only for a copper(II) ion, where both the $4s$ electron and one $3d$ electron are lost.
