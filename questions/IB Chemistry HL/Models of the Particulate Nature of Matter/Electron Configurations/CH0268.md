# CH0268

**Maximum mark: 4**

Hydrogen exhibits two emission lines, $D1$ and $D2$. $D1$ has a wavelength of $656 \textrm{\ nm}$ and $D2$ has a frequency of $6.17\times 10^{14}\textrm{\ s}^{-1}$.

1. Calculate the frequency of the $D1$ emission line of hydrogen. [1 mark]

2. Calculate the wavelength of the $D2$ emission line of hydrogen. [1 mark]

3. Compare the two emission lines, and using the IB Chemistry data booklet, suggest which of the emission lines is most likely to be red. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The frequency of $D1$ emission line of hydrogen:

    $\begin{aligned}
    f &= \dfrac{3.00 \times 10^8 \textrm{\ m s}^{-1}}{656 \textrm{\ nm} \times \frac{1\textrm{\ m}}{1 \times 10^9 \textrm{\ nm}}} \\
    f &= 4.57 \times 10^{14} \textrm{ s}^{-1}\,\checkmark
    \end{aligned}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    $\begin{aligned}
    \textrm{Rearranging the formula:}\ c &= \lambda f, \\
    \textrm{We obtain:}\  f &= \dfrac{c}{\lambda} \\
    \end{aligned}$

2.  The wavelength of the $D2$ emission line of hydrogen:

    $\begin{aligned}
    \lambda &=\dfrac{3.00 \times 10^8 \textrm{\ m s}^{-1}}{6.17 \times 10^{14} \textrm{\ s}^{-1}}  \\
    \lambda &= 4.86\times 10^{-7} \textrm{\ m} \times \dfrac{1\textrm{\ m}}{1 \times 10^9 \textrm{\ nm}} = 486 \textrm{\ nm}\,\checkmark
    \end{aligned}$

    $\underline{\textrm{Further explanation:}}$

    $\begin{aligned}
    \textrm{Rearranging the formula:}\ c &= \lambda f, \\
    \textrm{We\ obtain:}\ \lambda &=\dfrac{c}{f}  \\
    \end{aligned}$

3. The frequency $D2 > D1$ and the wavelength $D1 > D2$; $\checkmark$

    $D1$ with the larger wavelength and smaller frequency is more likely to be on the red end of the visible spectrum. $\checkmark$
