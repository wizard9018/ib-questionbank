# CH2572

Which compound has the highest percentage of oxygen by mass?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{SO2}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CO}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{CO2}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\ce{SO3}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The percentage of oxygen by mass in each compound is calculated from the molar mass:

$\%\textrm{O in }\ce{CO2} = \dfrac{2 \times 16.00}{12.01 + 2 \times 16.00} \times 100 = \dfrac{32.00}{44.01} \times 100 = 72.7\%$

$\%\textrm{O in }\ce{SO3} = \dfrac{3 \times 16.00}{32.07 + 3 \times 16.00} \times 100 = \dfrac{48.00}{80.07} \times 100 = 59.9\%$

$\%\textrm{O in }\ce{CO} = \dfrac{16.00}{12.01 + 16.00} \times 100 = \dfrac{16.00}{28.01} \times 100 = 57.1\%$

$\%\textrm{O in }\ce{SO2} = \dfrac{2 \times 16.00}{32.07 + 2 \times 16.00} \times 100 = \dfrac{32.00}{64.07} \times 100 = 49.9\%$

$\ce{CO2}$ has the highest percentage at $72.7\%.$

For oxides of the same element, the formula with more oxygen atoms
per central atom has the higher percentage of oxygen by mass. This
reduces the comparison to $\ce{CO2}$ and $\ce{SO3}.$
