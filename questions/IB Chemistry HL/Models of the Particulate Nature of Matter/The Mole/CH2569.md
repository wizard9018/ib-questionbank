# CH2569

What is the amount, in mol, of ammonia that contains $9.03 \times 10^{23}$ hydrogen atoms?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}0.17$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}0.50$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}1.5$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}3.0$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
The amount of hydrogen atoms is:

$n(\ce{H}) = \dfrac{9.03 \times 10^{23}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} = 1.50\textrm{ mol}$

Ammonia has the molecular formula $\ce{NH3},$ so each molecule contains three hydrogen atoms. The amount of ammonia molecules is:

$n(\ce{NH3}) = \dfrac{1.50\textrm{ mol}}{3} = 0.50\textrm{ mol}$

The subscript in $\ce{NH3}$ gives the ratio: three moles of hydrogen atoms correspond to one mole of ammonia.
