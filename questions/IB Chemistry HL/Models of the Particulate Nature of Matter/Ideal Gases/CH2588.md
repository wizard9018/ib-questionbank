# CH2588

Which sample has the same number of oxygen atoms as in $45.4\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(g)}$ at STP?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}22.7\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{O3}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}2.27\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{N2O5}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}22.7\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{O2}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}11.4\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{H2O2}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
At STP, the molar volume of any ideal gas is $22.7\textrm{ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}.$ The amount of $\ce{H2O}\textrm{(g)}$ in the original sample is:

$n(\ce{H2O}) = \dfrac{45.4\textrm{ dm}^3}{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} = 2.00\textrm{ mol}$

Each $\ce{H2O}$ molecule contains one oxygen atom, so the sample contains $2.00\textrm{ mol}$ of oxygen atoms.

Checking each option:

-   $22.7\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{O3}$

    $n(\ce{O}) =  \dfrac{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3 \ce{\ O3}}{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} \times \dfrac{3 \textrm{\ mol O}}{1 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{O3}} = 3.00 \textrm{\ mol O}$

-   $2.27\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{N2O5}$

    $n(\ce{O}) =  \dfrac{2.27\textrm{ dm}^3 \ce{\ N2O5}}{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} \times \dfrac{5 \textrm{\ mol O}}{1 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{N2O5}} = 0.500 \textrm{\ mol O}$

-   $22.7\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{O2}$

    $n(\ce{O}) =  \dfrac{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3 \ce{\ O2}}{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} \times \dfrac{2 \textrm{\ mol O}}{1 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{O2}} = 2.00 \textrm{\ mol O}$

-   $11.4\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{H2O2}$

    $n(\ce{O}) =  \dfrac{11.4\textrm{ dm}^3 \ce{\ H2O2}}{22.7\textrm{ dm}^3\textrm{ mol}^{-1}} \times \dfrac{2 \textrm{\ mol O}}{1 \textrm{\ mol\ }\ce{O2}} = 1.00 \textrm{\ mol O}$

Only $22.7\textrm{ dm}^3$ of $\ce{O2}$ gives $2.00\textrm{ mol}$ of oxygen atoms.
