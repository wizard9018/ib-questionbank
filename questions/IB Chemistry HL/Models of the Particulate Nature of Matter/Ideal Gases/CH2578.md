# CH2578

The ideal and real gas models help us to predict the behaviour of gases. Which principles apply to both?

-   I.$\hspace{1em}$Molecules are in constant motion
-   II.$\hspace{0.7em}$The volume occupied by the gas molecules is negligible
-   III.$\hspace{0.4em}$There are no intermolecular forces between gas particles

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$I, II and III

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$I and II only

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$II only

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$I only

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：D**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Both the ideal and real gas models assume that gas molecules are in constant random motion (Statement I). This is a fundamental assumption of the kinetic molecular theory shared by both models.

The ideal gas model assumes that the volume occupied by the molecules themselves is negligible compared to the volume of the container. The real gas model accounts for the fact that at high pressures the molecular volume becomes significant. Statement II applies only to the ideal model.

The ideal gas model assumes no intermolecular forces exist between gas particles. The real gas model recognises that at low temperatures molecules move more slowly, and when they collide or pass close to one another, attractive forces can take effect. Statement III applies only to the ideal model.

Only Statement I is common to both models.
