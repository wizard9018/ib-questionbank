# CH2586

What is the correct expression for the density, in $\textrm{g m}^{-3},$ of an ideal gas at STP?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{10^5 \times {M _r}}{8.31 \times 273}$

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{10^3 \times M_r}{8.31 \times 273}$

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{8.31 \times 273}{10^5 \times M_r}$

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}\dfrac{8.31 \times 273}{10^3 \times M_r}$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：A**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
Starting from the ideal gas law, $pV = nRT,$ the amount of gas can be written as $n = \dfrac{m}{M}$ where $m$ is the mass and $M$ is the molar mass. Substituting:

$pV = \dfrac{m}{M} \times RT$

Rearranging for $\dfrac{m}{V},$ which is the density $d$:

$d = \dfrac{m}{V} = \dfrac{pM}{RT}$

Since $1\textrm{ J} = 1\textrm{ Pa m}^3,$ the gas constant $R = 8.31\textrm{ J K}^{-1}\textrm{ mol}^{-1}$ can be written as $8.31\textrm{ Pa m}^3\textrm{ K}^{-1}\textrm{ mol}^{-1}.$ With pressure in Pa and molar mass in $\textrm{g mol}^{-1},$ the units of the expression are:

$d = \dfrac{\textrm{Pa} \times \textrm{g mol}^{-1}}{\textrm{Pa m}^3\textrm{ K}^{-1}\textrm{ mol}^{-1} \times \textrm{K}} = \textrm{g m}^{-3}$

At STP, $p = 100\textrm{ kPa} = 1.00 \times 10^5\textrm{ Pa}$ and $T = 273\textrm{ K}.$ The relative molecular mass $M_r$ is numerically equal to the molar mass in $\textrm{g mol}^{-1}$ but is itself dimensionless. Substituting:

$d = \dfrac{10^5 \times M_r}{8.31 \times 273}$
