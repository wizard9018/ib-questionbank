# CH2592

**Maximum mark: 5**

A $5.00\textrm{ dm}^3$ tank at temperature $T$ and pressure $p$ contains $7.99\textrm{ g}$ of argon.

1. Calculate the number of atoms of argon in the tank. [1 mark]

2. Another $5.00\textrm{ dm}^3$ tank at the same temperature $T$ and pressure $p$ is used to store xenon.

    (i) Determine the mass of xenon in the tank. [2 marks]

    (ii) Calculate the density of the xenon in the tank, in $\textrm{g cm}^{-3}.$ [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $N = 1.20 \times 10^{23}\textrm{ atoms}$ $\checkmark$

    $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
    The amount of argon is:

    $n(\ce{Ar}) = \dfrac{7.99\textrm{ g}}{39.95\textrm{ g mol}^{-1}} = 0.200\textrm{ mol}$

    Argon is monoatomic, so the number of atoms equals the number of particles:

    $N = 0.200\textrm{ mol} \times 6.02 \times 10^{23}\textrm{ mol}^{-1} = 1.20 \times 10^{23}\textrm{ atoms}$

2.  1.  $n(\ce{Xe}) = 0.200 \textrm{\ mol}$ / same amount of gas as argon $\checkmark$\
        $m(\ce{Xe}) =  26.3\textrm{ g} \: \checkmark$\
        *Award [2] for correct answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        Both tanks have the same volume, temperature, and pressure. By Avogadro's law, equal volumes of gas at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of particles. The xenon tank therefore contains the same amount of gas as the argon tank: $0.200\textrm{ mol}.$

        $m(\ce{Xe}) = n \times M = 0.200\textrm{ mol} \times 131.29\textrm{ g mol}^{-1} = 26.3\textrm{ g}$

    2.  $V = 5000 \textrm{\ cm}^3 \: \checkmark$\
        $d = 5.26 \times 10^{-3}\textrm{ g cm}^{-3}$ $\checkmark$\
        *Award [1] for answer in  g dm$^\mathit{-3}.$*\
        *Award [2] for correct answer.*

        $\textrm{\underline{Further Explanation:}}$\
        The volume must be converted to $\textrm{cm}^3$: $5.00\textrm{ dm}^3 = 5000\textrm{ cm}^3.$

        $d = \dfrac{m}{V} = \dfrac{26.3\textrm{ g}}{5000\textrm{ cm}^3} = 5.26 \times 10^{-3}\textrm{ g cm}^{-3}$
