# CH0041

**Maximum mark: 8**

The ideal gas equation is a formula that combines the relationship between pressure, volume, temperature, and the number of moles.

1. Complete the table below by calculating the missing variables of the ideal gas equation. [4 marks]

| $\mathbf{P} $         | $\mathbf{V}$            | $\mathbf{n}$         | $\mathbf{T}$        |
| :-:        | :-:           | :-:       | :-:      |
| (1)        | $\quad 0.015\,\textrm{m}^{3}\quad$ | $0.400\,\textrm{mol}$ | $37\,{}^\circ \mathrm{C}$    |
| $\quad 152,000\,\textrm{Pa}\quad$ | (2)           | $2.00\,\textrm{mol}$  | $\quad 298\,\textrm{K}\quad$ |
| $31,000\,\textrm{Pa}$  | $0.25\,\textrm{m}^{3}$  | (3)       | $271\,\textrm{K}$ |
| $250\,\textrm{kPa}$     | $0.75\,\textrm{dm}^{3}$ | $\quad 0.580\,\textrm{mol}\quad$ | (4)      |

2. A $256\,\textrm{cm}^{3}$ flask contains $0.800\,\textrm{g}$ of a gaseous compound with an empirical formula of CHCl. The pressure and the temperature of the gas are respectively $1.00\times 10^{5}\,\textrm{Pa}$ and $373\,\textrm{K}$. Determine the molecular formula of the compound. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. For (1): Use the ideal gas equation to calculate the pressure of the gas.

   $\begin{aligned}
   PV &= nRT \\[3pt]
   P &= \dfrac{nRT}{V} \\
   P &= \dfrac{(0.400\,\text{mol})(8.31\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1})(310\,\text{K})}{0.015\,\text{m}^{3}} = 6.9\times 10^{4}\,\text{Pa}\,  \checkmark
   \end{aligned}$

   $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

   Temperature must be given in Kelvin.

   $T(\text{K}) = 37 + 273 = 310\,\text{K}$

   For (2): Use the ideal gas equation to calculate for the volume of the gas:

   $\begin{aligned}
   PV &= nRT \\
   V &= \dfrac{nRT}{P} \\
   V &= \dfrac{(2.00\,\text{mol})(8.31\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1})(298\,\text{K})}{152000\,\text{Pa}} = 0.0326\,\textrm{m}^{3}\, \checkmark
   \end{aligned}$

   For (3): Use the ideal gas equation to calculate for the number of moles of the gas.

   $\begin{aligned}
   PV &= nRT \\
   n &= \dfrac{PV}{RT} \\
   n &= \dfrac{(31000\,\text{Pa})(0.25\,\text{m}^{3})}{(8.31\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1})(271\,\text{K})} = 3.4\,\textrm{mol}\, \checkmark
   \end{aligned}$

   For (4): Use the ideal gas equation to calculate the temperature of the gas. Make sure that pressure is in Pascals and volume is in m$^{3}$.

   $\begin{aligned}
   PV &= nRT \\
   T &= \dfrac{PV}{nR} \\
   T &= \dfrac{(250000\,\text{Pa})(0.00075\,\text{m}^{3})}{(0.580\,\text{mol})(8.31\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1})} = 39\,\text{K}\, \checkmark
   \end{aligned}$

   $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

   Volume must be given in m$^3$.

   $V = (0.75\,\text{dm}^3)\left(\dfrac{1\,\text{m}^3}{1000\,\text{dm}^3}\right) = 0.00075\,\text{m}^3$

2. $M_{gas} = \dfrac{(0.800\,\text{g})(8.31\,\text{J K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1})(373\,\text{K})}{(1.00\times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa})(256\times 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^{3})} = 96.9\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\, \checkmark$

   $M_{\ce{CHCl}} = 12.01 + 1.01 + 35.45 = 48.47\,\text{g}\,\text{mol}^{-1}\, \checkmark$

   $\dfrac{96.9\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}}{48.47\textrm{\ g mol}^{-1}}$ ≈ :answer[2] $\checkmark$

   The molecular formula is :answer[$\ce{C2H2Cl2}$] $\checkmark$

   $\underline{\textrm{Further Explanation:}}$

   Step 1: Use the ideal gas equation to calculate the molar mass of the gas: $PV = nRT = \left(\dfrac{m}{M}\right)RT$

   Therefore, $M = \dfrac{mRT}{PV}$

   Step 2: Calculate the empirical formula molar mass.

   Step 3: Determine the molecular formula.
