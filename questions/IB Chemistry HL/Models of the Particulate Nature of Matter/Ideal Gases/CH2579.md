# CH2579

What is meant by perfectly elastic collisions between particles in an ideal gas?

-   A.$\hspace{1.em}$No energy is transferred between particles during collisions.

-   B.$\hspace{1.em}$The particles have negligible volume compared to the container.

-   C.$\hspace{1.em}$No kinetic energy is converted to other forms of energy.

-   D.$\hspace{1.em}$The particles all move at the same speed.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：C**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
In a perfectly elastic collision, the total kinetic energy of the colliding particles before and after the collision is the same. No kinetic energy is converted to heat, sound, or any other form of energy. Individual particles may speed up or slow down as energy is transferred between them, but the total kinetic energy of the system remains constant.

The negligible volume of particles is a separate assumption of the kinetic molecular theory, unrelated to the elasticity of collisions.

The particles in an ideal gas do not all move at the same speed. The Maxwell$-$Boltzmann distribution describes the range of particle speeds at a given temperature.
