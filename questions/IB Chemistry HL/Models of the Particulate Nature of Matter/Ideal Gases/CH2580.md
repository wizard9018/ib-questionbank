# CH2580

Which curve shows the relationship for a fixed amount of a real gas at constant temperature?

 ![CH2580](images/CH2580.png)

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**答案：B**

$\textrm{\underline{Explanation:}}$\
For an ideal gas, $pV = nRT.$ At constant temperature with a fixed amount of gas, $pV$ is constant regardless of pressure. This gives a horizontal line on a $pV$ versus $p$ graph.

A real gas deviates from this behaviour. At moderate pressures, attractive forces between molecules reduce the effective pressure, causing $pV$ to dip below the ideal value. At high pressures, the finite volume of the molecules becomes significant and $pV$ rises above the ideal value.

The characteristic shape for a real gas is therefore a curve that first dips below the horizontal ideal line and then rises steeply at high pressure.
