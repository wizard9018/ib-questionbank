# BI0081

This table shows the estimated number of genes in *E. coli* bacteria, fruit flies, dogs, humans, and rice.

| Species | Estimated number of genes |
| :-- | :-- |
| *E.coli* (bacteria species) | 4,000 |
| *Drosophila melanogaster* (fruit fly) | 13,000 |
| *Canis lupis familiaris* (dog) | 20,000 |
| *Homo sapiens* (humans) | 30,000 |
| *Oryza sativa* (rice) | 40,000 |

What can you conclude from the above information?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ Species in the same kingdom of life have a similar number of genes

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ Different species have different numbers of genes

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ More complex species have more genes

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ The number of genes is not a characteristic of a species

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
**Choice B** is the correct answer.  This is a small sample of species, but the process of elimination means **Choice B** is the only possible correct answer. Fruit flies, humans and dogs are all in the animal kingdom but have different numbers of genes (**Choice A**).  This information shows that the number of genes does not determine the complexity, as rice has more genes than humans (**Choice C**). It is not possible to conclude that the number of genes is not a characteristic of a species from this small sample of data (**Choice D**).
