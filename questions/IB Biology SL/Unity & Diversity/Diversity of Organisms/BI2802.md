The table shows genome sizes for several different species.

|Organism | Genome Size (base pairs) |
| :-: | :-: |

| *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (Yeast) | 12 million |
| *Drosophila melanogaster* (Fruit Fly) | 140 million |
| *Xenopus laevis* (Frog) | ???? |
| *Homo sapiens* | 3 billion |

1. Describe the challenges of predicting the size of the genome of *Xenopus laevis* based on the information provided in the table.     [2 marks]

Single-cell genome sequencing allows researchers to analyse the genetic material of individual cells. Cancer is characterised by significant genomic instability, leading to massive variations in genome size when compared to noncancerous cells.

2. Explain how single-cell genome sequencing can be more useful than traditional genome sequencing for developing effective cancer treatments.    [3 marks]

The diagram below depicts nanopore DNA sequencing technology.  This portable technology involves passing a single piece of DNA through a tiny pore in a membrane in an electrical field in a manner such that the current can reveal the DNA sequence.  This allows for the analysis of environmental DNA (eDNA), which consists of DNA fragments shed by organisms into their surroundings (e.g., skin cells, waste).  It can also give almost immediate results for shorter fragments, and sequence entire genomes in as little as 3 days.

2. any **three** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ traditional whole-genome sequencing provides an average genomic profile of the whole organism **OR** not just the tumour     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ single-cell sequencing can show differences between the typical genome of that organism and the tumor     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ single-cell sequencing can show differences within the tumour itself     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ heterogeneity within the tumour can provide clinicians **OR** researchers with clues as to the best treatment options      [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ comparison of single-cell genome sequencing of tumour with originating tissue can help clinicians predict which treatment option will best allow healthy tissue to survive     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ single-cell sequencing facilitates the development of more personalised and effective therapies     [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    **Traditional whole-genome** sequencing provides an overall genomic view of a **whole organism** ($\checkmark$), giving us an average of the genetic information across all cells. **Single-cell sequencing** offers a more **detailed** perspective by revealing **differences** ($\checkmark$) between the typical genome of that organism and the tumour cells.  Heterogeneity within the tumour can provide clinicians and researchers with crucial clues for determining the **most effective treatment options** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **two** of the following *(must include **one** benefit and **one** risk)*:

    ***benefits***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ faster response time for public health interventions     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tracking of endangered **OR** invasive **OR** elusive species by eDNA without the need for lab facilities     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ identification of species present in an ecosystem based on eDNA without the need for lab facilities  [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    One potential benefit of portable DNA sequencing is that it can provide **faster results** ($\checkmark$) because the sequencing can be done on-site rather than sent to a lab, which can take days.  One possible risk is that DNA samples can be collected from the environment **without the consent of the people**, raising important **ethical concerns** ($\checkmark$).

Whole-genome sequencing can contribute to personalised medicine through the **identification of genetic diseases** ($\checkmark$) and predispositions to disease, as well as probable individual physiological **responses to treatment** options ($\checkmark$).
