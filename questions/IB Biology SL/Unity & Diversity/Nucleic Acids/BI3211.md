# BI3211

The table compares characteristics of bonding in the sugar–phosphate backbone and between complementary bases in DNA.

| Property | Sugar–phosphate backbone | Cross-strand base pairing |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Type of bond | **P** | **Q** |
| Relative strength of individual bonds | Strong | **R** |
| Role | **S** | Links complementary bases |

What are P, Q, R and S?

| | P | Q | R | S |
| --- | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| A.$\hspace{1em}$ | Covalent | Hydrogen | Weak | Forms the structural framework of each strand |
| B.$\hspace{1em}$ | Hydrogen | Covalent | Strong | Forms the structural framework of each strand |
| C.$\hspace{1em}$ | Covalent | Hydrogen | Strong | Encodes genetic information |
| D.$\hspace{1em}$ | Hydrogen | Covalent | Weak | Encodes genetic information|

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Covalent bonds link the sugars and phosphate groups of neighbouring nucleotides, forming a strong, continuous sugar-phosphate backbone. Therefore, P is covalent, and S is “forms a continuous structural backbone.”

Complementary bases on opposite strands are linked by hydrogen bonds. Individual hydrogen bonds are relatively weak compared with covalent bonds, although many hydrogen bonds collectively help to stabilise the DNA molecule. Therefore, Q is hydrogen and R is weak. **Choice A** is correct.

**Choice B** is incorrect because it reverses the bond types: the backbone contains covalent bonds, while complementary bases are linked by hydrogen bonds.

**Choice C** is incorrect because individual hydrogen bonds are relatively weak, and genetic information is encoded by the base sequence rather than by the sugar–phosphate backbone.

**Choice D** is incorrect because it reverses the bond types and incorrectly states that the backbone encodes genetic information.
