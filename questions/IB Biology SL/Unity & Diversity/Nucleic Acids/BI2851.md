# BI2851

The table shows the base composition of three DNA samples from different organisms:

| Sample | %A | %T | %G | %C |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| X | 24 | 24 | 26 | 26 |
| Y | 21 | 21 | 49 | 49 |
| Z | 20 | 30 | 20 | 30 |

Which sample(s) most likely represent double-stranded DNA molecules?

- A.$\hspace{1em}$ X only

- B.$\hspace{1em}$ X and Y only

- C.$\hspace{1em}$ Y and Z only

- D.$\hspace{1em}$ X, Y and Z

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
To identify double-stranded DNA, two rules must be satisfied:

-   1\. Base pairing rule: %A = %T and %G = %C

-   2\. Total base composition must add up to 100%

Sample **X** has equal proportions of adenine and thymine (24% each) and equal proportions of guanine and cytosine (26% each). The total adds up to 100%, which satisfies all criteria for double-stranded DNA. This sample is therefore most likely double-stranded.

Sample **Y** also has equal proportions of A and T (21%) and G and C (49%), but the total adds up to 140%, which is biologically impossible. This does not follow the base composition rule for double-stranded DNA.

Sample **Z** has mismatched base pairs: A $\not=$ T and G $\not=$ C. Although the total adds up to 100%, the base-pairing rules are not followed. This suggests that Z is likely single-stranded DNA.

**Choice A** is therefore the correct answer because only sample **X** likely represents a double-stranded DNA molecule.\
**Choices B, C** and **D** are all incorrect as they include one or more samples that are not double-stranded.
