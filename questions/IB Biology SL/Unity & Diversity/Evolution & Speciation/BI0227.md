# BI0227

The forelimbs of a human, bird, and whale have the same basic bone pattern but different functions.

![BI0227ii](images/BI0227ii.png)

What does this provide evidence for?

- A. $\hspace{1em}$ The differences are caused by acquired characteristics that are passed on

- B. $\hspace{1em}$ The limbs formed due to selective breeding by humans
- C. $\hspace{1em}$ The limbs are analogous, showing convergent evolution from unrelated ancestors

- D. $\hspace{1em}$ The limbs are homologous, suggesting a common ancestor and divergent evolution

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \
A shared underlying structure with different functions is evidence of homologous structures. Homology supports common ancestry, and the differences in function are explained by divergent evolution (adaptation to different environments). **Choice D** is correct.

**Choice A** is incorrect because this is a Lamarck-style idea, which says that acquired characteristics are passed on. Evolutionary change occurs through inheritance of alleles and changes in allele frequencies, not traits acquired during an individual’s lifetime being inherited.

**Choice B** is incorrect because selective breeding requires humans intentionally choosing parents to reproduce. The limb similarities across humans, bats, and whales are not explained by human-directed breeding; they are found in natural lineages over long timescales.

**Choice C** is incorrect because analogous structures have similar function but different underlying structure and do not come from a common ancestor (e.g., bird wing vs insect wing). In this case, the underlying bone plan is very similar, so they are not analogous.
