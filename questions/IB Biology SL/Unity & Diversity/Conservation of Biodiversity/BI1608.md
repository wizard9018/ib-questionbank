# BI1608

**Maximum mark: 16**

Organisms that are better adapted to their environment are more likely to survive and thrive.

1.  Describe how the skin and mucous membranes provide both physical and chemical defence against pathogens.    [4 marks]

2.  Explain the importance of concentration gradients and the mechanisms that regulate them in the human ventilation system.    [5 marks]

3.  Discuss to what extent the fossil record provides evidence for evolution.    [7 marks]

    ***Physical defence***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ skin provides a physical barrier **OR** is composed of multiple tissue layers preventing pathogens from entering the body    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ skin continuously sheds dead cells which removes any pathogens on the surface    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cells in skin are held together by tight junctions reinforcing the barrier function **OR** preventing pathogens from passing between cells    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mucus produced by mucous membranes can trap pathogens     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mucus containing trapped pathogens is either expelled by the body or swallowed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ cilia on mucous membrane surfaces move pathogens **OR** foreign particles out of the body    [1 mark]

    ***Chemical defence***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ sebaceous glands in the skin secrete sebum, which lowers the pH of the skin and inhibits the growth of bacteria and fungi    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mucus contains lysozyme **OR** antimicrobial enzymes which break down bacterial cell walls    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sweat contains antimicrobials **OR** acids which inhibit growth of pathogens     [1 mark]

    [4 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    The **skin** provides **physical defence** by forming a **barrier** that **prevents pathogens** from **entering** the **body** ($\checkmark$). It also provides **chemical defence** against pathogens by having **sebaceous glands** that **secrete sebum**, which **lowers** the **pH** of the skin and so **inhibits** the **growth** of **bacteria** and **fungi** on the skin ($\checkmark$). Mucous membranes line the internal surfaces of some organs and body cavities and secrete mucus, which can **trap pathogens** ($\checkmark$). The mucus is then **expelled** by the body or **swallowed** ($\checkmark$).

2.  any **five** of the following:\

    $\ce{ -> }$ steeper the concentration gradient, the greater the rate of diffusion, allowing for faster movement of molecules from areas of high concentration to areas of low concentration    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ inhaled air in alveoli has a higher concentration of oxygen than oxygen-depleted blood in the capillaries surrounding the alveoli **OR** blood has a higher concentration of carbon dioxide than the air in the alveoli. [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ concentration gradients facilitate diffusion of oxygen from alveoli into blood **OR** carbon dioxide from blood into alveoli, which provides cells with oxygen for respiration and removes waste carbon dioxide    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ large network of capillaries surrounding alveoli rapidly transport oxygen away from the lungs **OR** rapidly transport carbon dioxide from the body to the lungs    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the processes of inhalation and exhalation ensure that there is a constant flow of air in and out of lungs    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemoreceptors in carotid artery **OR** aorta **OR** medulla monitor blood pH levels **OR** blood carbon dioxide concentrations [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ chemoreceptors send signals to respiratory centre in medulla oblongata OR brain stem     [1 mark]

    respiratory centre **OR** medulla oblongata controls breathing rate **OR** depth of breathing to maintain concentration gradient of oxygen    [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    Efficient gas exchange in the lungs relies on maintaining concentration gradients, where **inhaled air** in the **alveoli** has a **higher concentration** of **oxygen** than **oxygen-depleted blood** and a **lower concentration** of **carbon dioxide than that in** the surrounding **capillaries** ($\checkmark$). This gradient allows **oxygen** to **diffuse from** the **alveoli** into the **blood** and **carbon dioxide from** the **blood into** the **alveoli** ($\checkmark$). An extensive **network of capillaries** surrounding the alveoli rapidly **transports oxygen away** from the **lungs** and **carbon dioxide to** the lungs ($\checkmark$). The processes of **inhalation** and **exhalation** ensure a **constant flow** of **air** in and out of the lungs, **maintaining** these **gradients** ($\checkmark$). **Chemoreceptors** in the **carotid artery** and **aorta** monitor **blood pH** ($\checkmark$), sending **signals to** the **respiratory centre** to **adjust breathing rate** and **depth** ($\checkmark$).

3.  any **seven** of the following (*answer must include at least **one** answer from ‘provides evidence’ and **one** answer from ‘doesn’t provide’ evidence to gain full marks*):

    

    ***Provides evidence***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ some organisms have very good fossil records, which allow their evolution to be tracked easily, e.g. horses        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the sequence of fossils that appear in layers of rock matches the sequence in which they would be expected to evolve     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ older organisms appear in deeper rock layers **OR** strata, and more recently evolved organisms appear in the upper layers    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there are fossils of extinct organisms with transitional forms **OR** fossils of extinct organisms that have features that link different stages of evolution, e.g. Archaeopteryx, bird-like dinosaurs that support the hypothesis that birds evolved from reptiles        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the fossil record shows evidence of mass extinction events **OR** there are many more extinct species than living ones        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fossils show evidence of adaptive radiation **OR** divergence of different organisms from a common ancestor **OR** speciation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ the fossil record shows evidence of vestigial structures that are no longer present in current organisms    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is fossil evidence for the existence of the last universal common ancestor (LUCA) near hydrothermal vents        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fossils provide chronological evidence that supports the theory of gradualism in evolution, where small changes accumulate over long periods    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fossilised remains of early hominids show a clear progression in brain size **OR** bipedalism, supporting human evolution    [1 mark]

    ***Doesn’t provide evidence***\

    $\ce{ -> }$ fossil record is incomplete, with many gaps **OR** some fossil species appear abruptly in the fossil record without any precursors    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ some species in the fossil record appear not to have changed much over long periods of time        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ convergent evolution **OR** fossils of unrelated organisms with similar features makes it difficult to track evolution accurately    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ soft-bodied organisms are rarely fossilised, leading to an incomplete record that skews our understanding of evolutionary history    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ process of fossilisation is rare and biased towards certain environments, meaning that the fossil record may not accurately represent all ecosystems and organisms    [1 mark]

    [7 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$:\

    The fossil record supports evolution because some organisms have **well-documented fossil records** that allow their evolutionary changes to be **tracked easily** ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the **sequence** in which **fossils appear in rock layers** matches the sequence in which they would be **expected to evolve** ($\checkmark$). For example, **older** organisms are found in **deeper layers** of rocks, while more **recently evolved organisms** are found in the **upper layers** ($\checkmark$). There are also fossils of extinct organisms with **transitional features**, such as Archaeopteryx, a bird-like dinosaur, which supports the hypothesis that birds evolved from reptiles and provides **evolutionary links** between present and extinct species ($\checkmark$). However, there are **gaps** in the fossil record ($\checkmark$), some species appear not to have **changed over long periods of time** ($\checkmark$), and **convergent evolution** can make it difficult to track evolution accurately ($\checkmark$).
