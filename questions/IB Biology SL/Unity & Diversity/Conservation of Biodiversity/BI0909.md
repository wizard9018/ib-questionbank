# BI0909

**Maximum mark: 10**

The image shows the changes to mixed dipterocarp forests in Southeast Asia over 100 years. Researchers have concluded that the changes are mainly due to anthropogenic factors.

![BI0904bi](images/BI0904bi.png)
[Source: Adapted from San Jose. (2006, February 8). File: map of east asia.png - wikimedia commons. Wikimedia commons. Retrieved July 10, 2023 from URL https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File: Map_of_East_Asia.png. Copyright under CC BY 3.0]

1. Distinguish between the forest coverage in 1900 and 2000  [1 mark]

2. State the definition for ‘anthropogenic’.    [1 mark]

3. Explain two possible anthropogenic reasons for differences in forest coverage between 1900 and 2000.    [2 marks]

4. Suggest what evidence scientists would need to collect from the forests to provide evidence for a biodiversity crisis in South East Asia.    [1 mark]

5. Suggest why it might be important to increase biodiversity in the area.    [1 mark]

6. Conservationists in the area have started a reforestation project in the area, in an attempt to restore the forest to 1900 coverage levels. Discuss what effects this would have on biodiversity.    [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is less forest coverage in the year 2000 **COMPARED TO** the year 1900, **OR** there is more forest coverage in the year 1900 to the year 2000     [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
There is **less forest** coverage in the year 2000 compared to the year 1900 ($\checkmark$).

2. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ environmental **OR** ecological change caused by human activity     [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
The term anthropogenic refers to an **environmental change** caused, directly or indirectly, by **human activity** ($\checkmark$).

3. Any **two** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ deforestation for agriculture OR urbanisation     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pollution from factories OR vehicles weakening trees     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ human population growth resulting in more trees being cut to build infrastructure **OR** provide fuels **OR** make resources such as paper     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ spread of pests **OR** diseases **OR** introduction of invasive species causes damage to trees     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ forest fires caused by human activities     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ mining **OR** oil drilling **OR** other resource extraction     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ specific examples related to climate change such as changing rainfall patterns **OR** increased frequency of extreme weather *(do **NOT** allow climate change without a specific example)*     [1 mark]

    [2 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Two anthropogenic reasons for the loss of forests between 1900 and 2000 are **deforestation** to grow **crops**  ($\checkmark$) and **human population growth** resulting in more trees being cut down for the **building of infrastructure** ($\checkmark$).

4. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ surveys of plant and animal species in forested **AND** deforested areas to compare species richness **OR** abundance **OR** diversity over time     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ analysis of species population trends     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ amassing data from citizen scientists on species richness **OR** abundance, **OR** diversity     [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
To provide evidence for the biodiversity crisis in South East Asia, scientists would need to conduct surveys on **species diversity in the area over time**. ($\checkmark$).

5. Any **one** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ maintain food chains **OR** webs     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ trees carry out carbon capture **OR** release oxygen into the atmosphere **OR** climate change mitigation     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prevent extinction of organisms     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ promote tourism in the area **OR** aesthetic reasons     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ slow down the spread of zoonotic diseases (as loss of habitat means that animals will live closer together and closer to humans)     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ potential medical resources **OR** webs     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ preservation of cultural heritage of indigenous communities     [1 mark]
    $\ce{ -> }$ ethical responsibility to preserve biodiversity     [1 mark]

    [1 mark]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
It is important to increase biodiversity in the area, as **trees** carry out **carbon capture** ($\checkmark$) which contributes to mitigating climate change and promotes **tourism** in the area, which would **boost the economy** of South East Asia ($\checkmark$).

6. Any **three** of the following (**must** state what effect it has on biodiversity for mark):

    ***Increase biodiversity because***...

    $\ce{ -> }$ provides more habitats     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ provides places for organisms to hide from predators **OR** hunters         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prevents floods **OR** landslides due to increased transpiration     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ provides more food for organisms     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ encourages the establishment of complex ecological interactions **OR** an example such as an increase in number of pollinators for plant reproduction     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increases species richness     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ increases genetic diversity **OR** gene flow     [1 mark]

    ***Decrease biodiversity because***...

    $\ce{ -> }$ invasive species are introduced     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ native species are displaced during deforestation         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ there is competition between newly planted trees and existing vegetation         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ species competition is influenced by edge effects     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ soil disturbance during planting impacts other species negatively     [1 mark]

    ***Have little effect on biodiversity because***...

    $\ce{ -> }$ more than one approach is needed to change biodiversity in the area     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ limited species of trees **OR** monoculture are planted         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ tree species are not selected for their suitability to the area     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reforested areas are isolated **OR** lack connections to the existing forest     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ they are not monitored **OR** managed properly     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ reforested areas are too close to human settlements, potentially leading to conflict     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ trees are planted but animals are not reintroduced    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \
Reforesting may increase biodiversity in the area as it will provide more habitats for organisms ($\checkmark$) and provide organisms with a place to hide from hunters or predators ($\checkmark$). However, it may decrease biodiversity if only limited species of trees are replanted in the area ($\checkmark$).
