# BI2798

**Maximum mark: 16**

1. Electron microscopy provides images of cells with significantly higher resolution when compared to light microscopy. Evaluate the implications and limitations of relying primarily on electron micrographs to understand cell function.            [3 marks]

2. Explain how advances in microscopy have contributed to our understanding of cell structure and function.                                [5 marks]

3. Discuss the evidence for and against the idea that prokaryotic cells are “simple” compared to eukaryotic cells.                             [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. any **three** of the following, *must include at least **one** implication and **one** limitation*

    ***Implications***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ fine detail resolution has led to a more detailed understanding of structure-function relationships    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ discovery of new organelles and structures    [1 mark]

    ***Limitations***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ requires complex sample preparation    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ sample preparation can leave artefacts    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ samples must be fixed, dead, meaning no dynamic cellular processes can be observed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ pigmentation or colour is not detected    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ maximum understanding comes from combining information from both light and electron microscopy    [1 mark]

    [3 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Electron microscopy gives us very **detailed images**, which let us see how the tiny parts of a cell are **structured to fulfil** their **functions** ($\checkmark$). It has also led to the discovery of **new things inside** cells ($\checkmark$). Unfortunately, to **prepare specimens** for electron microscopy, they must be treated in such a way that they are **killed**, and so we **cannot observe processes** happening in real time ($\checkmark$).

2. any **five** of the following:

    $\ce{ -> }$ light microscopy allowed for the initial visualisation of cells and basic structures    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ staining techniques have enhanced the visibility of cell components    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ fluorescence microscopy enables visualisation and localisation of specific molecules     [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ live-imaging techniques allow dynamic processes to be observed    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ electron microscopy provides significantly higher resolution than light microscopy    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ transmission electron microscopy reveals details of the internal structure of organelles    [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ scanning electron microscopy provides 3-D images of cell surfaces **OR** cell shape    [1 mark]

    [5 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Light microscopy first allowed us to see **cells** and their **basic** parts ($\checkmark$). Later, **staining** made it easier to see these parts more **clearly** ($\checkmark$). **Fluorescence** microscopy has allowed scientists to visualise and pinpoint the **locations** of specific molecules within cells ($\checkmark$).  **Electron** microscopy provides **far greater resolution** than light microscopy ($\checkmark$), making it possible to see **internal structures** like mitochondria and membranes in **fine detail** ($\checkmark$).

3. any **eight** of the following:

    ***In favour***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells lack membrane-bound organelles **OR** have less compartmentalisation        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells have a single, circular chromosome; eukaryotic cells have multiple linear chromosomes        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells are generally smaller        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells reproduce by binary fission, which is less complex than mitosis/meiosis        [1 mark]

    ***Against***\
    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells efficiently carry out all life processes within a single cell, demonstrating functional sophistication        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells exhibit diverse and complex metabolic pathways        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells exhibit unique metabolic pathways, ie chemosynthesis        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells can engage in cell signaling        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells have highly specialised adaptations (ie flagellae)        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells have a high surface area to volume ratio, increasing the exchange rate with the surroundings         [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ prokaryotic cells are structurally simpler but functionally complex        [1 mark]

    $\ce{ -> }$ evaluation of the application of the word “simple”        [1 mark]

    [8 marks]

    $\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
    Prokaryotes are generally **smaller** ($\checkmark$) than most eukaryotic cells, and they reproduce by **binary fission** ($\checkmark$) instead of mitosis or meiosis. Prokaryotic cells are often called more simple than eukaryotic cells, but it is important to **distinguish between structure and function** ($\checkmark$). Prokaryotic cells may appear simple, but they still carry out all of the **functions of life** ($\checkmark$) and have some that are unique to prokaryotes. Although prokaryotes **do not have membrane-bound organelles** ($\checkmark$), they still have **complex metabolic** pathways like cellular respiration and even pathways eukaryotes do not have, like **chemosynthesis** ($\checkmark$). Prokaryotes can communicate with each other via **cell signalling** pathways like quorum sensing ($\checkmark$) and also have highly **specialised structures** such as flagellae for motility ($\checkmark$). The term “simple” is an oversimplification of a superficial observation.
