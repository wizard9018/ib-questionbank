## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 1, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Dans quelques semaines, je vais passer une semaine $\textrm{\`a}$ Marrakech avec deux amis pendant les vacances de printemps." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$In a few weeks, I am going to spend a week in Marrakech with two friends during the spring holidays.") The expression *dans quelques semaines* (*in a few weeks*) and the near future tense *je vais passer* (*I am going to spend*) confirm that the trip is planned but has not yet taken place, which matches *Projet de voyage* (*Travel project*).

- *Option incorrecte :* B

- **B**: *Souvenirs de Marrakech* (*Memories of Marrakech*) is not correct because it refers to past memories. In paragraph 1, the text clearly focuses on a future trip using the future tense, not on recollections of a past visit.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 2, the text states: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Nous avons $\textrm{d\'ej\`a}$ $\textrm{r\'eserv\'e}$ un riad qui semblait $\textrm{tr\`es}$ accueillant." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$We have already booked a riad that seemed very welcoming.") The adverb *$\textit{d\'ej\`a}$* (*already*) and the $\textrm{pass\'e}$ $\textrm{compos\'e}$ *avons $\textit{r\'eserv\'e}$* (*have booked*) confirm that the accommodation has been arranged, which matches *$\textit{H\'ebergement}$ et $\textit{pr\'eparation}$* (*Accommodation and preparation*).

- *Option incorrecte :* D

- **D**: *Une $\textit{r\'eservation}$ $\textit{\`a}$ venir* (*A reservation to come*) is not correct because it implies the reservation has not yet been made. In paragraph 2, the text states $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Nous avons $\textrm{d\'ej\`a}$ $\textrm{r\'eserv\'e}$" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$We have already booked"), confirming the reservation is complete.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 3, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nous visiterons $\textrm{diff\'erents}$ quartiers et nous nous $\textrm{prom\`enerons}$ dans les $\textrm{march\'es}$ $\textrm{anim\'es}$... nous irons au Jardin Majorelle... nous passerons du temps $\textrm{\`a}$ Jemaa el-Fna." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$we will visit different neighbourhoods and walk around the lively markets... we will go to the Majorelle Garden... we will spend time at Jemaa el-Fna.") The future tense throughout confirms these are planned activities, and the variety of locations described matches *$\textit{Activit\'es}$ $\textit{pr\'evues}$ en ville* (*Planned activities in the city*).

- *Option incorrecte :* E

- **E**: *Les $\textit{march\'es}$ de la ville* (*The city markets*) is not correct because, although paragraph 3 mentions *les $\textit{march\'es}$ $\textit{anim\'es}$* (*the lively markets*), the paragraph describes a wide range of planned activities across several locations, not only the markets.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- The word *tranquille* is correct because in paragraph 3, the text describes the *Jardin Majorelle* as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$connu pour $\textrm{\^etre}$ calme." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$known for being calm.") The word *calme* describes a place that is peaceful and restful, and *tranquille* (*quiet/peaceful*) expresses the same idea.

- *Option incorrecte :* C

- **C**: *Silencieux* (*silent*) is not correct because it suggests a complete absence of noise. In the text, *calme* describes a peaceful atmosphere, which does not necessarily mean total silence.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

- The word *$\textit{m\'emorable}$* is correct because in paragraph 3, the narrator expresses the hope that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Ce voyage sera... une $\textrm{exp\'erience}$ inoubliable." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$This trip will be... an unforgettable experience.") The word *inoubliable* (*unforgettable*) describes something that cannot be forgotten and leaves a lasting impression, and *$\textit{m\'emorable}$* (*memorable*) expresses the same idea.

- *Option incorrecte :* A

- **A**: *Important* (*important*) is not correct because something can be important without being unforgettable. In the text, *inoubliable* specifically describes an experience that stays in the memory, which is not the same as simply being important.
