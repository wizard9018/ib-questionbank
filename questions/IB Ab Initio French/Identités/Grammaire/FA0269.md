## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

* *Sont* is correct because *ils* is the subject and we use *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* to express nationality.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, D, E, F

* **A**: *Ai* is the *je* form of *avoir*.

* **B**: *Sommes* is the *nous* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, but the subject is *ils*.

* **D**: *Es* is the *tu* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, but the subject is *ils*.

* **E**: *Ont* is the *ils* form of *avoir*.

* **F**: *As* is the *tu* form of *avoir*.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

- *As* is correct because *tu* is the subject and we use *avoir* to express age.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, E

* **A**: *Ai* is the *je* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *tu*.

* **B**: *Sommes* is the *nous* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **C**: *Sont* is the *ils/elles* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **D**: *Es* is the *tu* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **E**: *Ont* is the *ils/elles* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *tu*.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

* *Mes sœurs* corresponds to *elles*, so the correct form is *ont*. We use *avoir* to describe physical features such as eye colour.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, F

* **A**: *Ai* is the *je* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *elles*.

* **B**: *Sommes* is the *nous* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **C**: *Sont* is the *ils/elles* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **D**: *Es* is the *tu* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **F**: *As* is the *tu* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *elles*.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Sommes* is correct because *nous* is the subject. We use *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* with *$\textit{fatigu\'{e}s}$* to describe a state.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **A**: *Avons* agrees with *nous*, but we use *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* with *$\textit{fatigu\'{e}s}$*.

* **C**: *Avez* is the *vous* form of *avoir*.

* **D**: *Sont* is the *ils/elles* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, but the subject is *nous*.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

- *Avez* is correct because *vous* is the subject. *Avoir froid* is a fixed expression in French.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C

* **A**: *$\textit{\^{E}tes}$* agrees with *vous*, but French uses *avoir* in the expression *avoir froid*.

* **B**: *Ont* is the *ils/elles* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *vous*.

* **C**: *Sommes* is the *nous* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

**Grammar reminder**

*$\textit{\^{E}tre}$* means *to be* and *avoir* means *to have*. Both are very common irregular verbs in French.

When choosing between them, think first about $\underline{\textrm{what you want to express}}$, then choose the correct form of the verb for the subject.

**Use *$\textbf{\textit{\^{e}tre}}$* for:**

* **Identity**: *Je suis Linda.*

* **Nationality**: *Nous sommes $\textit{fran\c{c}ais}$.*

* **Profession**: *Elle est $\textit{m\'{e}decin}$.*

* **Location**: *Vous $\textit{\^{e}tes}$ $\textit{\`{a}}$ $\textit{Montr\'{e}al}$.*

* **Personal qualities / characteristics**: *Ils sont sympathiques.*

* **Feelings / states**: *Je suis content. / Nous sommes $\textit{fatigu\'{e}s}$.*

**Use *avoir* for:**

* **Age**: *J'ai quinze ans.*

* **Family**: *Elle a deux $\textit{fr\`{e}res}$.*

* **Possession**: *Nous avons un $\textit{v\'{e}lo}$.*

* **Physical features**: *Il a les yeux bleus. / Elle a les cheveux bruns.* (see list below)

* **Certain physical states**: *J'ai faim. / Vous avez froid.*

* **Personality with the word $\textbf{caract\`{e}re}$**: *Il a un $\textit{caract\`{e}re}$ calme.*

$\underline{\textbf{Expressions to know}}$

Some common expressions with avoir need to be learned with the verb they use :

* *Avoir faim* : to be hungry

* *Avoir soif* : to be thirsty

* *Avoir froid* : to be cold

* *Avoir chaud* : to be hot

* *Avoir peur* : to be afraid

Remember that the verb must also agree with the subject:

| $\hspace{1em}$Subject pronoun $\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}\text{\^{E}tre}$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$Avoir$\hspace{1.5em}$ |

| :- | :- | :- |

| $\hspace{1em}$Je | $\hspace{1em}$suis | $\hspace{1em}$ai |

| $\hspace{1em}$Tu | $\hspace{1em}$es | $\hspace{1em}$as |

| $\hspace{1em}$Il/elle/on | $\hspace{1em}$est | $\hspace{1em}$a |

| $\hspace{1em}$Nous | $\hspace{1em}$sommes | $\hspace{1em}$avons |

| $\hspace{1em}$Vous | $\hspace{1em}\text{\^{e}tes}$ | $\hspace{1em}$avez |

| $\hspace{1em}$Ils/elles$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$sont | $\hspace{1em}$ont |

**Common mistake:** Do not choose the verb by translating directly from English. Some ideas are expressed differently in French.

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$French uses *avoir* for age (*J'ai quinze ans*).\

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$French uses *avoir* in specific expressions such as *avoir faim* and *avoir froid*, but *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in expressions such as *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$ $\textit{fatigu\'{e}}$* and *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$ content*.
