# FA0265

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

* *Ai* is the correct form because the subject is *je*. We use *avoir* to express age.

* *Options incorrectes :* B, C, D

* **B** : *As* is used with *tu*.

* **C** : *A* is used with *il/elle/on*.

* **D** : *Ont* is used with *ils/elles*.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Avons* is the correct form because the subject is *nous*. We use *avoir* here to talk about family.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **A** : *Avez* is used with *vous*.

* **C** : *Ont* is used with *ils/elles*.

* **D** : *Ai* is used with *je*.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

* *As* is the correct form because the subject is *tu*.

* *Options incorrectes :* B, C, D, E, F

* **B** : *Ont* is the *ils/elles form*, but the subject is *tu*.

* **C** : *Avons* is the *nous* form, but the subject is *tu*.

* **D** : *Ai* is the *je* form, but the subject is *tu*.

* **E** : *Avez* is the *vous* form, but the subject is *tu*.

* **F** : *A* is the *il/elle/on* form, but the subject is *tu*.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

* *A* is the correct form because the subject is *elle*. The correct form for *il/elle/on* is *a*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, E

* **A** : *As* is the *tu* form, but the subject is *elle*.

* **B** : *Ont* is the *ils/elles* form, but the subject is *elle*.

* **C** : *Avons* is the *nous* form, but the subject is *elle*.

* **D** : *Ai* is the je *form*, but the subject is *elle*.

* **E** : *Avez* is the *vous* form, but the subject is *elle*.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Ont* is the correct form because the subject is *ils*. The correct form for *ils/elles* is *ont*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, E, F

* **A** : *As* is the *tu* form, but the subject is *ils*.

* **C** : *Avons* is the *nous* form, but the subject is *ils*.

* **D** : *Ai* is the *je* form, but the subject is *ils*.

* **E** : *Avez* is the *vous* form, but the subject is *ils*.

* **F** : *A* is the *il/elle/on* form, but the subject is *ils*.

**Grammar reminder**

Avoir means to have. It is one of the most common verbs in French.

Use avoir for:

* Age: *J'ai quinze ans.*

* Family: *Nous avons deux enfants.*

* Possession: *Tu as un $\textit{v\'{e}lo}$.*

* Physical features: *Elle a les yeux bleus.*

* Some physical states: *J'ai faim. Nous avons froid.*

* Personality with the word *$\textit{caract\`{e}re}$* : *Il a un $\textit{caract\`{e}re}$ calme.*

*Avoir* is an irregular verb, which means that its forms do not follow a regular conjugation pattern. Below is its present-tense conjugation:

| $\hspace{1em}$Subject pronoun | $\hspace{1em}$Avoir$\hspace{1.5em}$ |

| :- | :- |

| $\hspace{1em}$J' | $\hspace{1em}$ai |

| $\hspace{1em}$Tu | $\hspace{1em}$as |

| $\hspace{1em}$Il/elle/on | $\hspace{1em}$a |

| $\hspace{1em}$Nous | $\hspace{1em}$avons |

| $\hspace{1em}$Vous | $\hspace{1em}$avez |

| $\hspace{1em}$Ils/elles$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$ont |

**Common mistake** : When the subject is a name or a noun phrase, first identify which subject pronoun it corresponds to. For example, *$\textit{L\'{e}a}$ et moi = nous*, so we say *$\textit{L\'{e}a}$ et moi avons les yeux verts*. Similarly, *mes cousins = ils*, so we say *mes cousins ont un chien*.
