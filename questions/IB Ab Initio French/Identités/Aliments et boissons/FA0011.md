# FA0011

[Note maximale : 5\] 

**Mon restaurant $\textbf{pr\'ef\'er\'e}$**

Le samedi, je vais au restaurant « Chez Marc » avec ma famille. Nous aimons aller $\textrm{l\`a-bas}$. Le restaurant est petit, mais il est populaire dans notre quartier, loin du centre-ville.

Je mange toujours une quiche aux $\textrm{l\'egumes}$ et je bois une limonade.

Ma $\textrm{s{\oe}ur}$ prend des spaghettis et mes parents prennent une salade avec du poulet.

Ensuite, nous mangeons un dessert au chocolat. Nous partageons le dessert. $\underline{\textrm{C'est}}$ $\textrm{d\'elicieux}$ !

Mohamed

**Choisissez la bonne $\textbf{r\'eponse}$.** 

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$Qui accompagne Mohamed au restaurant « Chez Marc »?    [1 mark] 

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Les membres de sa famille.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$Les personnes de son quartier.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Sa $\textrm{s{\oe}ur}$ et son $\textrm{p\`ere}$.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Les voisins de la famille.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$Comment est le restaurant « Chez Marc » ?    [1 mark] 

- A.$\hspace{1em}$C'est un restaurant familial. 

- B.$\hspace{1em}$Il est au centre-ville.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Il est peu populaire.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Il est $\textrm{tr\`es}$ $\textrm{fr\'equent\'e}$ dans le quartier.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$Quel plat la $\textrm{s{\oe}ur}$ de Mohamed choisit-elle au restaurant ?    [1 mark] 

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Une salade $\textrm{accompagn\'ee}$ de poulet.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$Une quiche avec $\textrm{diff\'erents}$ $\textrm{l\'egumes}$.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Un plat de $\textrm{p\^ates}$.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Un dessert au chocolat.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$Que fait la famille une fois le plat principal $\textrm{termin\'e}$ ?     [1 mark] 

- A.$\hspace{1em}$Elle commande une limonade.

- B.$\hspace{1em}$Elle partage un dessert.

- C.$\hspace{1em}$Elle choisit un autre plat principal.

- D.$\hspace{1em}$Elle demande l'addition $\textrm{imm\'ediatement}$.

**$\textbf{\`A}$ qui ou $\textbf{\`a}$ quoi le mot $\textbf{soulign\'e}$ fait-il $\textbf{r\'ef\'erence}$ ? $\textbf{R\'epondez}$ en utilisant les mots tels qu'ils apparaissent dans le texte.**

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}\underline{\textrm{C'est}}$ $\textrm{d\'elicieux}$ !    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 1, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Le samedi, je vais au restaurant 'Chez Marc' avec ma famille." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$On Saturdays, I go to the restaurant 'Chez Marc' with my family.") The phrase *avec ma famille* (*with my family*) directly answers who accompanies Mohamed to the restaurant.

- *Options incorrectes :* B, C, D

- **B**: *Les personnes de son quartier* (*The people from his neighbourhood*) is not correct because in paragraph 1, the text says: *populaire dans notre quartier*. (*popular in our neighbourhood*.) Here, *quartier* (*neighbourhood*) refers only to the location of the restaurant, not to the people Mohamed goes with.

- **C**: *Sa $\textit{s{\oe}ur}$ et son $\textit{p\`ere}$* (*His sister and his father*) is not correct because In paragraph 1, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$avec ma famille." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$with my family.") The word *famille* (*family*) includes both parents, not just the father.

- **D**: *Les voisins de la famille* (*The family's neighbours*) is not correct because in paragraph 1, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$avec ma famille." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$with my family.") Neighbours are never mentioned as accompanying Mohamed.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 1, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$il est populaire dans notre quartier." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$it is popular in our neighbourhood.") The word *populaire* (*popular*) shows the restaurant is well visited by many people in the neighbourhood, which matches *$\textit{tr\`es}$ $\textit{fr\'equent\'e}$ dans le quartier* (*very popular in the neighbourhood*). The adjective *$\textit{fr\'equent\'e}$* means *frequently visited by many customers*.

- *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C

- **A**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$C'est un restaurant familial" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$It is a family restaurant") is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$je vais au restaurant... avec ma famille." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$I go to the restaurant... with my family.") This only describes who goes there, not the type of restaurant.

- **B**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Il est au centre-ville" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$It is in the city centre") is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$loin du centre-ville." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$far from the city centre.") The word *loin* (*far*) directly contradicts this option.

- **C**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Il est peu populaire" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$It is not very popular") is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$il est populaire." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$it is popular.") The word *populaire* (*popular*) directly contradicts *peu populaire* (*not very popular*).

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 2, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Ma $\textrm{s{\oe}ur}$ prend des spaghettis." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$My sister has spaghetti.") Spaghetti is a type of pasta, so *un plat de $\textit{p\^ates}$* (*a pasta dish*) is the correct general description of what the sister orders.

- *Options incorrectes :* A, B, D

- **A**: *Une salade $\textit{accompagn\'ee}$ de poulet* (*A salad with chicken*) is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mes parents prennent une salade avec du poulet." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$my parents have a salad with chicken.") This is what the parents eat, not the sister.

- **B**: *Une quiche avec $\textit{diff\'erents}$ $\textit{l\'egumes}$* (*A vegetable quiche)* is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$je mange toujours une quiche aux $\textrm{l\'egumes}$." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$I always eat a vegetable quiche.") This refers to Mohamed himself, not his sister.

- **D**: *Un dessert au chocolat* (*A chocolate dessert*) is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nous mangeons un dessert au chocolat. Nous partageons le dessert." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$we eat a chocolate dessert. We share the dessert.") This is a shared dessert eaten after the main course, not the sister's main dish.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 3, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nous mangeons un dessert au chocolat. Nous partageons le dessert." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$we eat a chocolate dessert. We share the dessert.") The verb *partageons* (*we share*) confirms that the whole family shares a dessert together after the main course.

- *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

- **A**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Elle commande une limonade" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$She orders a lemonade") is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$je bois une limonade." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$I drink a lemonade.") The lemonade is ordered earlier with the meal by Mohamed, not after the main course.

- **C**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Elle choisit un autre plat principal" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$She chooses another main course") is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nous mangeons un dessert au chocolat." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$we eat a chocolate dessert.") The family moves on to dessert, not another main course.

- **D**: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Elle demande l'addition $\textrm{imm\'ediatement}$" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$She asks for the bill immediately") is not correct because the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nous mangeons un dessert au chocolat." ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$we eat a chocolate dessert.") The family eats dessert after the main course, asking for the bill is not mentioned.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{(un) dessert au chocolat}$    [1 mark]

- In paragraph 3, the text says: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nous mangeons un dessert au chocolat. Nous partageons le dessert. C'est $\textrm{d\'elicieux}$ !" ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$we eat a chocolate dessert. We share the dessert. It is delicious!") The demonstrative expression *c'est* (*it is*) refers back to the noun mentioned immediately before. Here, it refers to *un dessert au chocolat* (*a chocolate dessert*), the item the family has just shared.
