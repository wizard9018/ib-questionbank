# FA0313

**Choose the correct form of the verb in the perfect tense.**

$\hspace{1em}$Hier, Sonia **\[-1-]** $\text{\`{a}}$ Paris avec sa famille.    [1 mark]

* A.$\hspace{1em}$sommes $\text{all\'{e}s}$

* B.$\hspace{1em}$est $\text{all\'{e}}$

* C.$\hspace{1em}$est $\text{all\'{e}e}$

* D.$\hspace{1em}$sont $\text{all\'{e}es}$

$\hspace{1em}$Samedi dernier, mes $\text{fr\`{e}res}$ **\[-2-]** $\text{tr\`{e}s}$ $\text{t\^{o}t}$ le matin.    [1 mark]

* A.$\hspace{1em}$est parti

* B.$\hspace{1em}$sont parties

* C.$\hspace{1em}\text{\^{e}tes}$ partis

* D.$\hspace{1em}$sont partis

**Complete the sentences. Choose the correct verb in the perfect tense : *sont sorties*, *sommes $\textbf{\textit{arriv\'{e}s}}$* or *est venue*.**

$\hspace{1em}$Hier soir, mon ami et moi, nous **\[-3-]** $\text{\`{a}}$ l'$\text{h\^{o}tel}$ vers huit heures.    [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}$Ma cousine **\[-4-]** chez nous pendant les vacances.    [1 mark]

$\hspace{1em}\text{Apr\`{e}s}$ le $\text{d\^{\i}ner}$, mes soeurs **\[-5-]** avec leurs amis.    [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

* *Est $\textit{all\'{e}e}$* is correct because *Sonia* refers to *elle*. The form est is used with *elle*, and the past participle *$\textit{all\'{e}e}$* agrees with the feminine singular subject.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, D

* **A** : *Sommes $\textit{all\'{e}s}$* is used with *nous*, not with the singular subject *Sonia*.

* **B** : *Est $\textit{all\'{e}}$* does not agree with the feminine singular subject *Sonia*.

* **D** : *Sont $\textit{all\'{e}es}$* is used with a feminine plural subject, not with the singular subject *Sonia*.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

* *Mes $\textit{fr\`{e}res}$* refers to *ils*. The correct form is *sont partis*: sont is used with a plural subject, and *partis* agrees with the masculine plural subject.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C

* **A** : *Est parti* is singular, but *mes $\textit{fr\`{e}res}$* is plural.

* **B** : *Sont parties* is feminine plural, but *mes $\textit{fr\`{e}res}$* is masculine plural.

* **C** : *$\textit{\^{E}tes}$ partis* is used with *vous*, not with the subject *mes $\textit{fr\`{e}res}$*.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{sommes arriv\'{e}s}$    [1 mark]

* *Mon ami et moi* refers to *nous*, so the correct form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* is *sommes*. The past participle *$\textit{arriv\'{e}s}$* agrees with the masculine plural subject.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{est venue}$    [1 mark]

* *Ma cousine* refers to *elle*, so the correct form of *$\textit{\^{e}tr}$*&#x65; is *est*. The past participle of *venir* is *venu*, and it becomes *venue* to agree with the feminine singular subject.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{sont sorties}$    [1 mark]

* *Mes soeurs* refers to *elles*, so the correct form of $\text{\^{e}tre}$ is *sont*. The past participle *sorties* agrees with the feminine plural subject.

**Grammar Reminder**

The perfect tense (*$\textit{pass\'{e}}$ $\textit{compos\'{e}}$*) is used for completed actions in the past.

Some French verbs form the perfect tense with *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

With *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, the structure is: subject + *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in the present + past participle

| $\hspace{1em}$Subject pronoun$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1em}\textbf{\^{E}tre}$ $\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$Example: Rester$\hspace{1.5em}$ |

| :- | :- | :- |

| $\hspace{1em}$Je | $\hspace{1em}$suis | $\hspace{1em}$je suis $\text{rest\'{e}}$(e) |

| $\hspace{1em}$Tu | $\hspace{1em}$es | $\hspace{1em}$tu es $\text{rest\'{e}}$(e) |

| $\hspace{1em}$Il/elle/on | $\hspace{1em}$est | $\hspace{1em}$elle est $\text{rest\'{e}e}$ |

| $\hspace{1em}$Nous | $\hspace{1em}$sommes$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$nous sommes $\text{rest\'{e}}$(e)s |

| $\hspace{1em}$Vous | $\hspace{1em}\text{\^{e}tes}$ | $\hspace{1em}$vous $\text{\^{e}tes}$ $\text{rest\'{e}}$(e)(s) |

| $\hspace{1em}$Ils/elles$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$sont | $\hspace{1em}$ils sont $\text{rest\'{e}s}$ |

For many regular verbs, the past participle follows a regular pattern:

* verbs ending in *-er* → *$\textit{-\'{e}}$*

* verbs ending in *-ir* → often *-i*

* verbs ending in *-re* → often *-u*

Some verbs have irregular past participles:

* *venir* → *venu*

* *$\textit{na\^{\i}tre}$* → *$\textit{n\'{e}}$*

* *mourir* → *mort*

With *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, the past participle agrees with the subject:

* masculine singular → *$\textit{rest\'{e}}$*

* feminine singular → *$\textit{rest\'{e}e}$*

* masculine or mixed plural → *$\textit{rest\'{e}s}$*

* feminine plural → *$\textit{rest\'{e}es}$*

Here is a list of common verbs that take *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in the perfect tense:

**Movement and travel**

* aller: to go

* venir: to come

* arriver: to arrive

* partir: to leave

* entrer: to enter

* sortir: to go out

* monter: to go up

* descendre: to go down

* retourner: to return / go back

* rentrer: to return / go home

* revenir: to come back

* repartir: to leave again

* passer: to pass / go past

**Other common verbs**

* tomber: to fall

* rester: to stay

* $\text{na\^{\i}tre}$: to be born

* mourir: to die

**Reflexive verbs**

* se $\text{r\'{e}veiller}$: to wake up

* se lever: to get up

* se laver: to wash oneself

* se doucher: to shower

* se brosser les dents: to brush one’s teeth

* se coiffer/se brosser les cheveux

* se raser: to shave

* s’habiller: to get dressed

* se $\text{pr\'{e}parer}$: to get ready

* se coucher: to go to bed

**Common mistake**: when the perfect tense is formed with *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, remember to make the past participle agree with the subject.
