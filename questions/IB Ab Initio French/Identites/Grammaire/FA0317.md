# FA0317

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Aller* uses *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*. *Lucas* refers to *il*, so the correct form is *est*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **A** : *A* is an *avoir* form, but *aller* requires *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* here.

* **C** : *As* is the *tu* form of *avoir*; both the verb and the person are wrong for *Lucas*.

* **D** : *Es* is the *tu* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, but *Lucas* requires the *il* form.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

* *Visiter* takes *avoir*, and *Mes parents* refers to *ils*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, D

* **A** : *Sont* agrees with a plural subject, but it is an *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* form. *Visiter* requires *avoir*.

* **B** : *A* is a singular *avoir* form, but *mes parents* is plural.

* **D** : *Est* is a singular *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* form, but *visiter* requires *avoir* and *mes parents* is plural.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

* *Mon $\textit{fr\`{e}re}$ et moi* includes the speaker and refers to *nous*. *Rentrer* is a movement verb, so it takes *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*. *$\textit{Rentr\'{e}s}$* agrees with this masculine or mixed plural group.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, E, F

* **A** : *Avons* is the *nous* form of *avoir*, but *rentrer* requires *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in this sentence.

* **B**: *Sont* is the *ils/elles* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, but the subject is *nous*.

* **C** : *Avez* is the *vous* form of *avoir*;  but *rentrer* requires *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* and the subject is *nous*.

* **E** : *As* is the *tu* form of *avoir*; but *rentrer* requires *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* and the subject is *nous*.

* **F** : *Ont* is the *ils/elles* form of *avoir*; but *rentrer* requires *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* and the subject is *nous*.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

* *Prendre* takes *avoir*. The subject *tu* requires *as*. The past participle of *prendre* is *pris*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, F

* **A** : *Avons* is the *nous* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *tu*.

* **B** : *Sont* is the *ils/elles* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **C** : *Avez* is the *vous* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *tu*.

* **D** : *Sommes* is the *nous* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

* **F** : *Ont* is the *ils/elles* form of *avoir*, but the subject is *tu*.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Partir* takes *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in the perfect tense. *Mes cousines* refers to *elles*, so it requires *sont*. Parties agrees with this feminine plural subject.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D, E, F

* **A** : *Avons* is the *nous* form of *avoir*.

* **C** : *Avez* is the *vous* form of *avoir*.

* **D** : *Sommes* is the *nous* form of *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, but *mes cousines* requires the *elles* form.

* **E** : *As* is the *tu* form of *avoir*.

* **F** : *Ont* agrees with *elles*, but it is an *avoir* form. *Partir* requires *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*.

**Grammar Reminder**

The perfect tense (*$\textit{pass\'{e}}$ $\textit{compos\'{e}}$*) describes completed actions in the past. Its structure is: *avoir* or *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in the present + past participle.

**Choosing between *$\textbf{\textit{\^{e}tre}}$* and *avoir***

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$Use *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* in two cases:

* Use *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* with the movement list: *aller, venir, revenir, arriver, partir, entrer, rentrer, sortir, retourner, tomber, rester* and *devenir*. Example: *Elle est partie*.

* Also use *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$* with reflexive verbs (verbs with *se*): *se lever → Elle s'est $\textit{lev\'{e}e}$*.

With *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*, the past participle agrees with the subject: *Il est parti. Elle est partie. Elles sont parties*.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$Use *avoir* the rest of the time:

* Use *avoir* with all the other verbs, for example: *visiter, acheter, prendre, voir, regarder, marcher, nager* and *voyager*. Example: *Elle a $\textit{voyag\'{e}}$*.

With *avoir*, the past participle does not agree with the subject: *Il a $\textit{voyag\'{e}}$. Elle a $\textit{voyag\'{e}}$. Elles ont $\textit{voyag\'{e}}$*.

**Common mistake**

* Movement alone does not mean *$\textit{\^{e}tre}$*: *Elle a $\textit{march\'{e}}$*.

* With *sortir*, use *avoir* when someone takes something out: *Elle a sorti une carte*.
