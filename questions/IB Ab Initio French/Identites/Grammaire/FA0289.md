# FA0289

**Choose the correct form of the  verb in the present tense.**

$\hspace{1em}$Ma soeur  et moi **\[-1-]** $\text{fran\c{c}ais}$ $\text{\`{a}}$ la maison.    [1 mark]

* A.$\hspace{1em}$parle

* B.$\hspace{1em}$parlons

* C.$\hspace{1em}$parlent

* D.$\hspace{1em}$parles

$\hspace{1em}$Nous **\[-2-]** un $\text{g\^{a}teau}$ pour l'anniversaire de Jasmine.    [1 mark]

* A.$\hspace{1em}$mangeons

* B.$\hspace{1em}$mangons

* C.$\hspace{1em}$mangent

* D.$\hspace{1em}$mange

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Parlons* is correct because *Ma soeur  et moi* refers to *nous*. With nous, regular *-er* verbs end in *-ons*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D

* **A** : *Parle* is the *je/il/elle* form and does not agree with *nous*.

* **C** : *Parlent* is the *ils/elles* form and does not agree with nous.

* **D** : *Parles* is the *tu* form and does not agree with *nous*.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

* *Mangeons* is correct because the subject is *nous*. *Manger* is a *-ger* verb, so before *-ons* it takes an extra *e* to keep the soft $\text{\textquotedblleft}$j" sound: mangeons.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **B** : *Mangons* is missing the *e* needed to keep the soft $\text{\textquotedblleft}$j" sound before -ons.

* **C** : *Mangent* is the *ils/elles* form and does not agree with *nous*.

* **D** : *Mange* is the *je/il/elle* form and does not agree with *nous*.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

* *$\textit{T\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$* is correct because the subject is *je*. With *je*, regular *-er* verbs end in *-e*. The expression is *$\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phoner}$* $\text{\`{a}}$ quelqu'un.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D

* **A** : *Aides* is the *tu* form and does not agree with *je*.

* **B** : *$\textit{T\'{e}l\'{e}phonent}$* is the *ils/elles* form and does not agree with *je*.

* **C** : *Visite* agrees with *je*, but cannot be followed by *$\textit{\`{a}}$*.

* **E** : *Visitent* is the *ils/elles* form and does not agree with *je*.

* **F** : *Aide* cannot be followed by *$\textit{\`{a}}$*.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

* *Aides* is correct because the subject is *tu*. With *tu*, regular *-er* verbs end in *-es*. The expression is *aider quelqu'un avec ses devoirs*.

* *Options incorrectes :* B, C, D, E, F

* **B** : *$\textit{T\'{e}l\'{e}phonent}$* is the *ils/elles* form and does not agree with *tu*.

* **C** : *Visite* is the *je/il/elle* form and does not agree with *tu*.

* **D** : *$\textit{T\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$* is the *je/il/elle* form and does not agree with *tu*.

* **E** : *Visitent* is the *ils/elles* form and does not agree with *tu*.

* **F** : *Aide* uses the correct verb, but it is the *il/elle* form.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{E}$    [1 mark]

* *Visitent* is correct because the subject is *ils*. With *ils*, regular *-er* verbs end in *-ent*. The expression is *visiter des endroits*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, F

* **A** : Aides is the tu form and does not agree with ils.

* **B** : $\text{T\'{e}l\'{e}phonent}$ agrees with ils, but $\text{t\'{e}l\'{e}phoner}$ beaucoup d'endroits does not make sense.

* **C** : Visite uses the correct verb, but it is the je/il/elle form.

* **D** : $\text{T\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$ is the je/il/elle form and does not agree with ils.

* **F** : Aide is the il/elle form and does not agree with ils.

**Grammar reminder**

Regular verbs ending in *-er* change their ending to agree with the subject.\

First, remove *-er*. Then add the correct ending:

| $\hspace{1em}$Subject pronoun$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$-ER ending$\hspace{1.5em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$Example: Parler$\hspace{1.5em}$ |

| :- | :- | :- |

| $\hspace{1em}$Je | $\hspace{1em}$-e | $\hspace{1em}$parle |

| $\hspace{1em}$Tu | $\hspace{1em}$-es | $\hspace{1em}$parles |

| $\hspace{1em}$Il/elle/on | $\hspace{1em}$-e | $\hspace{1em}$parle |

| $\hspace{1em}$Nous | $\hspace{1em}$-ons | $\hspace{1em}$parlons |

| $\hspace{1em}$Vous | $\hspace{1em}$-ez | $\hspace{1em}$parlez |

| $\hspace{1em}$Ils/elles$\hspace{1em}$ | $\hspace{1em}$-ent | $\hspace{1em}$parlent |

Examples:\

Je *$\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$* $\text{\`{a}}$ ma $\text{grand-m\`{e}re}$.\

Tu *aides* ton $\text{fr\`{e}re}$.\

Ma soeur et moi *parlons* $\text{fran\c{c}ais}$.

**Spelling changes**

* Verbs ending in *-cer* and *-ger*, like *commencer* (to start) and *manger* (to eat), need a small spelling adjustment in the *nous* form to keep their pronunciation consistent: *-ger* verbs add an *e* before *-ons* (*nous mangeons*, to keep the soft $\text{\textquotedblleft}$j" sound of the g), and *-cer* verbs take a cedilla, *$\textit{\c{c}}$*, before *-ons* (*nous $\textit{commen\c{c}ons}$*, to keep the soft $\text{\textquotedblleft}$s" sound of the c).

* Verbs ending in *-oyer* or *-uyer*, like *employer*, change the *y* to an *i* in every form except *nous* and *vous*: *j'emploie, tu emploies, elle emploie, ils emploient*, but *nous employons, vous employez*.

* Verbs with *$\textit{\'{e}}$* + consonant + *er*, like *$\textit{pr\'{e}f\'{e}rer}$*, change the *$\textit{\'{e}}$* to *$\textit{\`{e}}$* in every form except *nous* and *vous*: *je $\textit{pr\'{e}f\`{e}re}$, tu $\textit{pr\'{e}f\`{e}res}$, elle $\textit{pr\'{e}f\`{e}re}$, ils $\textit{pr\'{e}f\`{e}rent}$*, but *nous $\textit{pr\'{e}f\'{e}rons}$, vous $\textit{pr\'{e}f\'{e}rez}$*.

* Verbs with *e* + consonant + *er*, like *acheter*, follow the same pattern: the *e* becomes *$\textit{\`{e}}$* everywhere except *nous* and *vous*: *j'$\textit{ach\`{e}te}$, tu $\textit{ach\`{e}tes}$, elle $\textit{ach\`{e}te}$, ils $\textit{ach\`{e}tent}$*, but *nous achetons, vous achetez*.

* Verbs ending in *-eler* or *-eter*, like *appeler*, usually double the *l* or *t* instead of taking an accent: *j'appelle, tu appelles, elle appelle, ils appellent*, but *nous appelons, vous appelez*.
