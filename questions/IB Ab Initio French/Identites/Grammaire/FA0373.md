## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Lait* is masculine singular and begins with a consonant. Use *du* for an unspecified amount of milk: *Adam boit du lait.*

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **A** : *De la* is used with a feminine singular noun, but *lait* is masculine.

* **C** : *Des* is plural, but *lait* is singular.

* **D** : *De l'* is used before a vowel or silent *h*, but *lait* begins with a consonant.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

* *Soupe* is feminine singular and begins with a consonant. Use *de la* for an unspecified amount: *Lina mange de la soupe.*

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, D

* **A** : *Du* is masculine singular, but *soupe* is feminine.

* **B** : *De l'* is used before a vowel or silent *h*, but soupe begins with a consonant.

* **D** : *Des* is plural, but *soupe* is singular.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

* *Pain* is masculine singular and begins with a consonant. The affirmative sentence refers to an unspecified amount of bread.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, E, F

* **A** : *De* alone does not introduce this unspecified amount in an affirmative sentence.

* **B** : *Des* is plural, but *pain* is singular.

* **C** : *De la* is feminine singular, but *pain* is masculine.

* **E** : *D'* is the short form of *de*, and *pain* begins with a consonant.

* **F** : *De l'* is used before a vowel or silent *h*, but *pain* begins with a consonant.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{F}$    [1 mark]

* *Huile* is feminine singular and begins with a silent *h* so the correct form is de *l'*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C, D, E

* **A** : *De* alone does not express this unspecified amount.

* **B** : *Des* is plural, but *huile* is singular.

* **C** : *De la* must become *de l'* before the silent *h* in *huile*.

* **D** : *Du* does not fit the feminine noun *huile*; before its silent *h*, use de *l'*.

* **E** : *D'* is used after a quantity expression.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *$\textit{P\^{a}tes}$* is a plural food noun. Use *des* for an unspecified amount.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D, E, F

* **A** : *De* alone would be used after a quantity expression or an ordinary negative verb; neither occurs here.

* **C** : *De la* is singular, but *$\textit{p\^{a}tes}$* is plural.

* **D** : *Du* is singular, but *$\textit{p\^{a}tes}$* is plural.

* **E** : *D'* is the short form of de, and *$\textit{p\^{a}tes}$* begins with a consonant.

* **F** : *De l'* is singular and is used before a vowel or silent *h*; *$\textit{p\^{a}tes}$* is plural and begins with a consonant.

**Grammar Reminder**

Partitive articles express an $\underline{\textrm{unspecified amount}}$ of food or drink. They often mean $\text{\textquotedblleft}$some”, although English can $\text{Salom\'{e}ve}$ this word out: *Je mange du fromage* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$I eat (some) cheese”.

| Noun | Partitive article | Example |

| :-: | :-: | :-: |

| Masculine singular, before a consonant | *du* | *du fromage* / some cheese |

| Feminine singular, before a consonant | *de la* | *de la limonade* / some lemonade |

| Masculine or feminine singular, before a vowel or silent *h* | *de l'* | *de l'omelette* / some omelette; *de l'huile* / some oil |

| Plural food nouns used for an unspecified amount | *des* | *des $\textit{p\^{a}tes}$* / some pasta |

* In an ordinary negative sentence meaning $\text{\textquotedblleft}$not any”, the partitive article becomes *de*. Use *d'* before a vowel or silent *h*: *Je ne mange pas de fromage* (I do not eat cheese); *Je n'ajoute pas d'huile* (I do not add any oil).

* After a quantity expression such as *beaucoup de, un peu de* or a measure such as *un kilo de*, use *de* or *d'*: *beaucoup de fromage* (a lot of cheese), *un kilo de farine* (a kilogram of flour), *un peu d'huile* (a little oil). The quantity expression replaces the partitive article. In these constructions, *de / d'* does not change.

**Common mistake**: To say that you do not eat any cheese, write *Je ne mange pas de fromage*, not *Je ne mange pas du fromage*. The ordinary negative construction requires *de*.
