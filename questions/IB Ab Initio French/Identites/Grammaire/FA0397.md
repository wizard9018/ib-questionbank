## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$1.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{A}$    [1 mark]

* *Ne* goes before *parle* and *pas* after it.

* *Options incorrectes :* B, C, D

* **B** : *Ne pas parle* puts *pas* before the verb; it must be after *parle*.

* **C** : *Parle ne pas* puts *ne* after the verb; it must be before *parle*.

* **D** : *Ne parle* leaves out *pas*, which is needed for the negative expression requested.

$2.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

* *Ne... jamais* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$never”. Put *ne* before the verb *$\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$* and *jamais* after it.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C

* **A** : *Ne jamais $\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$* puts *jamais* before the verb; it must be after *$\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$*.

* **B** : *$\textit{T\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$ ne jamais* puts *ne* after the verb; it must be before *$\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$*.

* **C** : *Ne $\textit{t\'{e}l\'{e}phone}$ pas jamais* adds *pas*. This is not the requested pattern *ne... jamais* meaning $\text{\textquotedblleft}$never”.

$3.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Ne... plus* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$no longer”. It surrounds *jouons*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **A** : *Ne plus jouons* puts *plus* before the verb; it must be after *jouons*.

* **C** : *Jouons ne plus* puts *ne* after the verb; it must be before *jouons*.

* **D** : *Ne jouons pas plus* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$do not play more”. It does not express the requested meaning $\text{\textquotedblleft}$no longer play”.

$4.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{C}$    [1 mark]

* *Ne... rien* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nothing” or $\text{\textquotedblleft}$not anything”. Put *ne* before *racontes* and *rien* after it.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, D

* **A** : *Ne rien racontes* puts *rien* before the verb; it must be after *racontes*.

* **B** : *Ne racontes pas rien* adds *pas* to *rien*. For the requested meaning $\text{\textquotedblleft}$tell nothing”, use *ne... rien* without *pas*.

* **D** : *Racontes ne rien* puts *ne* after the verb; it must be before *racontes*.

$5.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{B}$    [1 mark]

* *Ne... pas* makes the verb *vais* negative. The speaker does not go to school today because *c'est dimanche*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, C, D

* **A** : *Jamais* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$never”, but the speaker does not go to school only today because it's Sunday.

* **C** : *Plus* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$no longer”, but the speaker has not stopped going to school. They go back on Monday.

* **D** : *Rien* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nothing” and replaces a thing. *Aller $\textit{\`{a}}$ l'$\textit{\'{e}cole}$* needs *ne... pas* to make the verb negative.

$6.$ $\hspace{1em}$ $\textrm{D}$    [1 mark]

* *Ne... rien* means $\text{\textquotedblleft}$nothing”. The fridge is empty (*le frigo est vide*), so there is nothing to eat. *Rien* comes directly before *$\textit{\`{a}}$ manger*.

* *Options incorrectes :* A, B, C

* **A** : *Jamais* needs an object after *il n'y a*, for example *il n'y a jamais de pain*.

* **B** : *Pas* needs *de* and a noun after *il n'y a*, for example *il n'y a pas de pain*.

* **C** : *Plus* needs *de* and a noun after *il n'y a*, for example *il n'y a plus de pain*.

**Grammar Reminder**

These four negative expressions change what a sentence says. In written French, keep both parts of the expression.

| $\hspace{1em}$Expression | $\hspace{1em}$Meaning | $\hspace{1em}$Example |

| :- | :- | :- |

| $\hspace{1em}$*ne... pa*s | $\hspace{1em}$not | $\hspace{1em}$*Il ne sourit pas.* $\newline\hspace{1em}$(He is not smiling). |

| $\hspace{1em}$*ne... jamais* | $\hspace{1em}$never | $\hspace{1em}$*Elle ne ment jamais.* $\newline\hspace{1em}$(She never lies.) |

| $\hspace{1em}$*ne... plus* | $\hspace{1em}$no longer or not any more $\newline\hspace{1em}$The action happened before and has stopped. | $\hspace{1em}$*Nous ne vivons plus ensemble.*$\newline\hspace{1em}$(We no longer live together.) |

| $\hspace{1em}$*ne... rien* | $\hspace{1em}$nothing or not anything | $\hspace{1em}$*Je ne remarque rien.*$\newline\hspace{1em}$(I do not notice anything.) |

* *Ne* becomes *n'* immediately before a vowel or a silent *h*.

**Where to place the negation:**

* $1.$ **General rule**: \

  Put *ne* before the verb and *pas, jamais, plus* or *rien* after it. \

  Example: *Mon $\textit{fr\`{e}re}$ ne danse pas.* (My brother does not dance.)

* $2.$ **Some past forms have two words**, such as *a $\textit{demand\'{e}}$*: \

  Put *ne* or *n'* before the first word, and *pas*, *jamais*, *plus* or *rien* between the two words.\

  Examples: *Il n'a rien $\textit{demand\'{e}}$.* (He has not asked for anything.) / *Nous n'avons jamais $\textit{cri\'{e}}$.* (We have never shouted.)

* $3.$ **Object and reflexive pronouns.**\

  When you conjugate a reflexive verb (a verb used with se), put *ne* or *n'* before the reflexive pronoun. Keep any object or reflexive pronoun between *ne* and the verb.\

  Examples: *Elle ne me comprend pas.* (She does not understand me.) / *Ils ne se critiquent jamais.* (They never criticise each other.)

**Common mistake**: Do not forget ne in formal writing. Write *je ne mange pas de viande* and not *je mange pas de viande*.
