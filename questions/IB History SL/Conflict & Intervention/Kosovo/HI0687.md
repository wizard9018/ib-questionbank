## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |
| :-- | :-- |
|Includes clear points of comparison| ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) |
|Includes clear points of contrast| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |
|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence** from the texts.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both sources identify NATO as a primary international actor in the crisis.
- Both acknowledge NATO's humanitarian justification for its actions.
- Both mention the harm to civilians caused by NATO's actions.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Source Q is more critical of NATO's actions, suggesting ethical and legal flaws, while Source S is more balanced. It acknowledges harm but suggests that the end goal of pressuring Milosevic was achieved.
- Source Q focuses more on a legal critique of NATO's actions, while Source S addresses primarily political and military consequences.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both Source Q and Source S agree that NATO's intervention in Kosovo primarily involved an extensive air campaign aimed at stopping the Serbian campaign of ethnic cleansing and protecting Kosovo Albanians ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).

Source Q states that NATO engaged in a “massive air campaign involving nearly 1,000 aircraft" to avoid friendly casualties, while Source S notes that NATO “launched an air campaign that attacked Yugoslavian military targets" ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Additionally, both sources recognize the political outcome of this intervention: the capitulation of Yugoslavian President Milosevic ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). According to Source Q, “Yugoslavia submitted to most of NATO's demands,” and Source S confirms that “on June 10, 1999, the Yugoslavian president finally capitulated,” leading to the withdrawal of Serbian forces and the return of Kosovo Albanians ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Despite these similarities, the sources differ significantly in their assessment of NATO's intervention regarding legality and effectiveness ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source Q emphasizes the lack of a clear legal basis, explaining that “NATO air strikes violated well-established international laws” and that the operation was “neither based on a Security Council decision...nor pursued in collective self-defence,” highlighting serious ethical concerns, including “high civilian casualties.” In contrast, Source S does not focus on legality but frames the intervention as effective because it influenced political change in Serbia, noting that NATO bombings “made Milosevic's war in Kosovo unpopular” and sparked opposition rallies calling for his resignation ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Furthermore, while Source Q critiques NATO's failure to prevent a humanitarian disaster or ensure long-term stability ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$), Source S stresses that “NATO bombs brought an end to the killing in Kosovo” and facilitated the return of displaced Kosovars, portraying the intervention as a successful resolution to the conflict ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
