# HI0523

Explain **two** consequences of the NATO bombing campaign against Serbia during the Kosovo conflict.    [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting explanations:

- Serbian Military Withdrawal from Kosovo:

    - NATO's bombing campaign forced Serbia to withdraw its military and police forces from Kosovo, ending the violent repression of Kosovar Albanians. This paved the way for NATO's Kosovo Force (KFOR) and UNMIK to take over peacekeeping and governance in the region.

- Strained Relations Between NATO and Russia

    - Russia strongly opposed NATO's intervention, viewing it as a violation of international law and its own influence in the Balkans. This created lasting tensions between Russia and the West, contributing to a deterioration in post-Cold War relations.

- Destruction of Serbia's Infrastructure and Economy

    - NATO airstrikes caused extensive damage to Serbia's infrastructure, including bridges, factories, and power plants. This worsened Serbia's economic situation, increased domestic discontent, and weakened Milošević's regime.

- Return of Kosovar Albanian Refugees

    - After Serbia's forces withdrew, many Kosovar Albanians returned to Kosovo, reversing the mass displacement caused by the conflict. However, this also led to ethnic tensions and attacks on the remaining Serb minority, contributing to instability in the region.

- Political Downfall of Slobodan Milošević

    - The NATO bombing campaign undermined Milošević's authority at home. His failure to maintain control over Kosovo and Serbia's economic collapse contributed to his eventual overthrow in 2000 and his subsequent trial for war crimes at the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY).

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One consequence of NATO's bombing campaign was that Serbia was forced to withdraw its troops from Kosovo ($\checkmark$), bringing an end to the ethnic cleansing of Kosovar Albanians and allowing NATO and UN forces to establish peacekeeping and civil administration ($\checkmark$).

Another consequence was the worsening of NATO's relations with Russia, which opposed the intervention and viewed it as a breach of international law ($\checkmark$). This contributed to increased geopolitical tensions and damaged cooperation between Russia and the West in the years that followed ($\checkmark$).
