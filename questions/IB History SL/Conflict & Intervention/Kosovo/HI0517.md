# HI0517

Explain **two** ways in which the Račak massacre influenced the course of the Kosovo conflict.  [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:

- It intensified diplomatic condemnation of Serbia by the international community

    - The massacre sparked outrage among Western governments and human rights organizations, leading to increased pressure on Milošević's regime and strengthening calls for intervention to protect the Kosovar Albanian population.

- It increased recruitment and support for the Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA).

    - Anger over the killings encouraged more Kosovar Albanians to join the KLA, boosting its capacity to resist Serbian forces and escalating the armed conflict within Kosovo.

- It served as evidence in war crimes indictments against Serbian leaders.

    - The massacre was cited by the International Criminal Tribunal for the Former Yugoslavia (ICTY) in its prosecution of Slobodan Milošević and others, helping establish international legal accountability for crimes committed during the conflict.

- The massacre prompted NATO to launch an aerial bombing campaign against Serbia.

    - The killings at Račak were widely seen as proof of systematic Serbian human rights abuses, providing the key justification for NATO's 1999 intervention. This escalated the conflict from a regional crisis to an international military operation aimed at ending the violence.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One way the Račak massacre influenced the Kosovo conflict was by prompting NATO to launch an aerial bombing campaign against Serbia 

($\checkmark$). The massacre was seen as clear evidence of systematic atrocities, which convinced NATO that military intervention was necessary to prevent further human rights abuses and protect Kosovar Albanians 

($\checkmark$).

Another way it influenced the conflict was by intensifying diplomatic condemnation of Serbia from the international community 

($\checkmark$). Widespread outrage following the massacre increased pressure on Milošević's government and strengthened calls for intervention, isolating Serbia diplomatically and paving the way for international action 

($\checkmark$).
