$\\$

For comparison, a minimum of **two** of the following with supporting evidence:
- Both authorize international intervention in Kosovo.
    - Source A gives a broad mandate for international civil and security presence.
    - Source B details the operational terms under which KFOR will act.
- Both emphasize the role of KFOR.
    - Source A gives KFOR a peacekeeping mandate.
    - Source B allows KFOR to use “necessary force” to enforce the agreement and secure the area.
- Both aim to restore security and support post-conflict transition.
    - Source A outlines provisional governance via UNMIK.
    - Source B describes establishing a safe environment for the civil presence to function.

For contrast, a minimum of **two** of the following with supporting evidence:
- Source A frames intervention as a collective, legal response, while Source B is a military capitulation.
    - Source A establishes international authority through the UN
    - Source B reflects a surrender of authority by Yugoslav forces.
- Source A includes language reaffirming Yugoslav sovereignty, while Source B focuses on military withdrawal.
    - Source A tries to balance autonomy and territorial integrity
    - Source B prioritizes immediate demilitarization and enforcement.
- Source B is more specific and tactical, while Source A is broader in scope.
    - Source A discusses governance, autonomy, and long-term political goals
    - Source B is operational-focused on troop movement, enforcement, and field logistics.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Sources A and B both show that the international community intervened in Kosovo to restore peace and security in 1999 ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A authorizes an international civil and security presence through the UN, while Source B outlines the conditions under which KFOR will take control of security and ensure compliance on the ground ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Both emphasize KFOR's central role ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A gives it peacekeeping authority, while Source B allows KFOR to use “necessary force” to enforce the agreement and protect personnel ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Additionally, both sources show that a secure environment was needed to support post-conflict recovery ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A links this to UNMIK and provisional institutions, and Source B describes KFOR creating conditions for a civil implementation presence ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, Source A establishes international authority through a multilateral UN resolution, while Source B reflects a military surrender by the Yugoslav government, agreeing to terms dictated by NATO ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source A represents diplomatic consensus; Source B is a binding military concession ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Also, Source A reaffirms Yugoslavia's sovereignty while granting autonomy to Kosovo, whereas Source B contains no reference to sovereignty and focuses entirely on the withdrawal and restrictions placed on Serbian forces ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$ $\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
