# HI0514

Describe **two** constitutional or political reforms introduced in Serbia between 1989 and 1994 and their impact on Kosovar Albanians.  [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following for the description of the Reform:

- Serbia amended the constitution to remove Kosovo's status as an autonomous province.

- Kosovo's legislature and government were abolished, and all governance decisions were centralized in Belgrade.

- Kosovo's judicial system was merged into Serbia's, eliminating local Kosovar Albanian judges and courts.

- The Serbian government shut down Albanian-language schools and universities.

- Large numbers of Kosovar Albanian teachers, doctors, and civil servants were removed from their jobs.

- Serbia banned Kosovo's independent political parties and arrested key activists.

Any **two** of the following for the impact of Kosovar Albanians:

- Kosovar Albanians lost political self-governance, with Serbia assuming full control over Kosovo's administration.

- Kosovar Albanians had no official representation or political influence in decision-making.

- Kosovar Albanians were subjected to a legal system that discriminated against them, making it difficult to seek justice

- Kosovar Albanian students were forced to either learn in Serbian or attend underground schools.

- Economic hardship increased among Kosovar Albanians, and essential services in Albanian-speaking communities suffered.

- Kosovar Albanians were deprived of legal political avenues to advocate for their rights, leading to underground resistance movements.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One reform introduced by Serbia was the revocation of Kosovo's autonomy in 1989, which ended Kosovo's special status within Yugoslavia ($\checkmark$). This meant that all major political decisions were now controlled by the Serbian government, and Kosovar Albanians lost all self-governance ($\checkmark$).

Another reform was the closure of Albanian-language schools in 1991, which prevented Kosovar Albanian students from being educated in their native language ($\checkmark$). As a result, many Albanians were forced to study in Serbian or attend underground schools, making education less accessible ($\checkmark$).
