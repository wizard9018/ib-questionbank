# HI0986

Discuss the role of social and economic factors that contributed to the outbreak of genocide in Rwanda.
[7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
**A full-mark response should:**
1. Include at least **two** of the following with supporting details.
2. Address both social **and** economic factors.
3. Include a concluding statement that aligns with the discussion.

- Poverty and land pressure
    - Rwanda's high population density and reliance on subsistence agriculture created land scarcity
    - As the economy worsened in the early 1990s, resentment toward the Tutsi minority—who were perceived by some as economically privileged or “foreign”—intensified.
- Impact of economic crisis and structural adjustment
    - Rwanda's economy suffered from a drop in coffee prices (its main export), leading to widespread unemployment and rising cost of living
    - IMF-imposed structural adjustment policies reduced public sector spending, intensifying hardship and fuelling ethnic scapegoating
- Refugee return fears
    - The planned return of hundreds of thousands of Tutsi exiles under the Arusha Accords generated fear among Hutu farmers and elites who were concerned about losing land or political influence.
- Historical social divisions
    - Long-standing discrimination against the Tutsi minority, dating back to colonial rule and reinforced by post-independence Hutu regimes, had led to ethnic stereotyping and polarisation.
    - These divisions were exploited by Hutu Power ideology in the early 1990s.
- Illiteracy and reliance on media
    - High illiteracy rates meant many Rwandans were vulnerable to hate propaganda spread by radio stations like RTLM and magazines like *Kangura*, which blamed Tutsis for the country's suffering.
\
[6 marks]

Concluding statement:
- Should include an overall analysis of the role of the factors discussed in the outbreak of the genocide.
\
[1 marks]
\
[7 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
A key economic factor was the severe downturn in Rwanda's economy in the early 1990s. Falling global coffee prices led to widespread poverty, especially in rural areas ($\checkmark$). This created frustration and fear, which extremist politicians exploited by blaming Tutsis for the country's problems ($\checkmark$). Along with the struggling economy, many Hutu farmers feared that if Tutsi exiles returned to Rwanda under the Arusha Accords, they would lose access to land and political influence ($\checkmark$). This fear of continued economic struggles helped justify violence in the minds of some ordinary citizens ($\checkmark$). However, social divisions also played a role. For decades, Tutsis were portrayed as a separate, untrustworthy group ($\checkmark$). These long-standing ethnic stereotypes, reinforced by colonial policies and Hutu-led governments, laid the groundwork for genocidal thinking ($\checkmark$).

The combination of economic desperation with long-standing ethnic tensions created a volatile environment that enabled extremist ideologies to grow, sparking mass violence ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
