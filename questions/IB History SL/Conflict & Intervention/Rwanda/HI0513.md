# HI0513

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying Hutu ideology in Rwanda before the 1994 genocide.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  An excerpt from the “Hutu Ten Commandments,” published in 1990 in the Rwandan extremist magazine *Kangura*. |

[Source: Wikipedia (n.d). *Hutu Ten Commandments.* Retrieved April 30, 2025 from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu_Ten_Commandments. CC BY-SA 4.0.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- The source was published in 1990 in Kangura, a Hutu Power magazine, making it a primary source that reflects the extremist ideology circulating in Rwanda shortly before the 1994 genocide.
- It provides detailed insight into the social and political goals of Hutu extremists, including ethnic exclusion, propaganda, and identity formation.
- The source outlines specific expectations placed on Hutu men and women, revealing how hate ideology was promoted in both public and private life.
- It reflects efforts to mobilise support and unify Hutu people around an anti-Tutsi agenda, offering valuable context for understanding the causes of the genocide.

For limitations, any of the following:
- As a piece of extremist propaganda, the source is highly biased and not a balanced or objective account of events.
- It does not reveal how widely these ideas were accepted, resisted, or implemented in practice across Rwandan society.
- The source represents only the viewpoint of one group (Hutu extremists) and does not include the perspectives of moderate Hutu, Tutsi, or international actors.
- While it reflects intent and ideology, it does not provide direct evidence of the actions that followed or how these ideas translated into violence.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The origin of the source is Kangura, a Hutu Power magazine published in 1990 by Hutu extremists. It is valuable because it serves as a primary source from the period leading up to the Rwandan genocide, offering direct insight into the ideology being promoted at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). However, its limitation lies in the fact that it reflects only the views of extremist groups and does not reveal how these ideas were received or challenged by the broader Rwandan population ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).

The purpose of the source was to promote Hutu supremacist ideology and to mobilise support for the exclusion and marginalisation of Tutsi people. This is valuable as it illustrates how propaganda was strategically used to encourage ethnic division, define loyalty, and prepare the population for violence ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).

The content of the source is valuable because it contains explicit instructions to exclude Tutsi individuals from social, political, and economic life, including marriage, employment, and education. It also calls on Hutu women to help enforce ideological loyalty within families. This provides historians with a clear example of how hatred and division were encouraged in everyday interactions ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).
