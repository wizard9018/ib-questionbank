# HI0501

Describe the role played by media and propaganda in enabling the genocide.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **four** of the following:

 - The media contributed to the spread of propaganda by promoting the Hutu Power ideology, spreading hate speech, and dehumanising the Tutsi population. This created an atmosphere of hostility and reinforced ethnic divisions.

 - The media helped with the organisation of violence by mobilising listeners and giving specific instructions on actions, such as what to do at roadblocks. This made the killings more systematic and widespread.

 - Constant repetition of hate-filled messaging led to the amplification of hate speech, influencing large portions of the population to support or participate in the genocide.

 - Propaganda played a key role in fuelling conflict by dehumanising the Tutsi. For example, the Kangura magazine published the Hutu Ten Commandments, which labelled Tutsi as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$enemies" and promoted hostility towards them.

 - The government also spread messages through media outlets, such as the radio station Radio Télévision Libre des Mille Collines (RTLM), which referred to Tutsi as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$cockroaches" and called for their extermination. This continuous promotion of hate normalised violence and primed the population for genocide.

 - Political and military elites used propaganda to frame the genocide as self-defence

The RPF was accused of shooting down President Habyarimana's plane, an incident that was used as a pretext for stating the genocide

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The media was key in facilitating the Rwandan genocide by spreading divisive messages and encouraging violent actions. Radio Télévision Libre des Mille Collines (RTLM) incited violence by labelling Tutsi as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$cockroaches” and calling for their extermination continuously both before and during the genocide ($\checkmark$). This radio station was also nicknamed $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Hutu Power Radio” and was used to spread Hutu Power, an ethnic supremacist ideology ($\checkmark$).

Similarly, Kangura magazine reinforced hostility by publishing the Hutu Ten Commandments, which depicted Tutsi as enemies, liars, and traitors, promoting discrimination ($\checkmark$). The media also facilitated violence by providing instructions, such as directing attackers to roadblocks and identifying targets, making killings more systematic ($\checkmark$).
