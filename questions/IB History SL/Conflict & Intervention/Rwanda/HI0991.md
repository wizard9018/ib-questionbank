# HI0991

Using Source A and your own knowledge, discuss the extent to which the failure of UNAMIR was due to the limitations imposed by the Security Council.
[6 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An extract from a Master's thesis by Michael L. Artbauer which examines the failure of UNAMIR in Rwanda. Written in 2001, the extract outlines the constraints placed on the mission by the UN Security Council, especially the United States. |

:box[The size of the UNAMIR force contingent was an immediate source of debate. A UN military expert recommended a force size of 8,000 men, General Dallaire asked for 4,500, and the United States recommended a force of 500. It was not until October 5, that a force of approximately 2,500 was approved for the mission. The UNAMIR force next suffered from a delay in funding. They arrived ill equipped to serve in their role and were undersupplied once the genocide commenced [...] The Security Council decided to place clear limits on the UNAMIR mandate. As a result, the mandate plainly deviated from the role as envisioned in the actual Arusha Accords. Several key considerations had been weakened, including notably the premise that UNAMIR would guarantee overall security in Rwanda. The mandate restricted UNAMIR from assisting in the search and recovery of arms caches and the neutralisation of armed gangs throughout the country as called for in the Arusha Accords. UNAMIR as established, notes Des Forges, could only $\text{\textquotedblleft}$contribute to security, and not throughout the country, but only in the city of Kigali."]

[Source: Artbauer, M. L. (2001). *Rwanda Reconsidered* (thesis). Calhoun: The NPS Institutional Archive. Retrieved July 24, 2025, from http://hdl.handle.net/10945/10937. Public domain.]

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A full-mark response should:
- Include evidence from Source A, either directly quoted or paraphrased
- Provide evidence from outside of Source A (own knowledge)

From Source A:
- UNAMIR was given a restricted mandate, authorising it only to act in self-defence, which prevented it from taking proactive measures during the genocide.
- The Security Council failed to respond adequately, even after warnings of mass killings.
- Reinforcement requests were ignored, and the Council avoided invoking the term “genocide” to circumvent international legal obligations under the Genocide Convention.
- The actions of dominant powers (e.g. the United States and France) reflected strategic political interests rather than humanitarian concern.

Own knowledge:
- Belgian troop withdrawal following the murder of ten peacekeepers early in the conflict, reducing UNAMIR's operational capacity.
- Lack of political will among influential states, particularly the US, which was deeply affected by the failure in Somalia in 1993.
- Limited international media coverage and insufficient public pressure meant Rwanda did not become a policy priority for most governments.
- Structural and logistical challenges, including UN bureaucracy, further delayed an effective response.
- The genocide unfolded rapidly, making intervention even more difficult.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Security Council assigned UNAMIR a highly restrictive mandate, permitting the use of force solely in self-defence, which left peacekeepers unable to act decisively in the face of mass violence ($\checkmark$). Despite receiving reports of atrocities, the Council refused to deploy additional troops, leaving the mission under-resourced and overwhelmed ($\checkmark$). It also deliberately avoided characterising the crisis as “genocide”, thereby evading legal obligations under the 1948 Genocide Convention ($\checkmark$).

The mission's failure, however, was not solely the result of Security Council decisions. The withdrawal of Belgian forces following the murder of ten of their peacekeepers further undermined the mission's capacity ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the United States, deeply affected by its failed intervention in Somalia, opposed any substantial engagement in Rwanda, reflecting a wider absence of political will among key powers ($\checkmark$).

While the Security Council's constraints critically undermined UNAMIR's effectiveness, the mission ultimately collapsed due to the intersection of political apathy, troop withdrawals, and the broader unwillingness of the international community to intervene ($\checkmark$).
