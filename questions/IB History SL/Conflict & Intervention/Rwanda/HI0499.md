# HI0499

Explain how ethnic divisions contributed to the outbreak of the 1994 conflict in Rwanda.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

The following:

 - The ethnic divisions between the Hutu and Tutsi were solidified during Belgian colonial rule, where the Tutsi were favoured for administrative roles. This led to resentment among the Hutu majority, who felt excluded.

 - After independence, the Hutu majority reversed this dynamic, cementing discrimination against the Tutsi through policies that excluded them from leadership and public life. This long-standing inequality deepened divisions and made large-scale violence more likely.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Belgian colonial rule in Rwanda deepened ethnic divisions by favouring the Tutsi minority for administrative roles while marginalising the Hutu majority ($\checkmark$). This created resentment among the Hutu, who felt excluded from political and economic opportunities ($\checkmark$). After Rwanda gained independence in 1962, the Hutu majority reversed this dynamic by implementing policies that discriminated against the Tutsi, excluding them from leadership positions and public life ($\checkmark$). These long-standing inequalities fuelled ethnic tensions, ultimately contributing to large-scale violence, including the 1994 genocide ($\checkmark$).
