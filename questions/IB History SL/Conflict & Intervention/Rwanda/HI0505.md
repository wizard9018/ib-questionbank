# HI0505

Describe the role of the Catholic Church in shaping Rwandan society under Belgian rule.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following:
- The Catholic Church collaborated with colonial authorities, working closely with the Belgian administration to reinforce social and political structures.
- The Catholic Church favoured the Tutsi elite, as missionaries viewed Tutsis as more “civilised” and supported their dominance in leadership roles.
- It promoted Christianity, encouraging large-scale conversion, including compulsory conversion for the Tutsi elite, with Christianity becoming a key part of colonial rule.
- It influenced education, as the Catholic Church helped establish Western-style schools, which mainly benefited Tutsis and deepened ethnic divisions.
- The Catholic Church played a role in the 1959 Hutu Revolution by supporting the shift in Belgian policy from favouring the Tutsi elite to backing the Hutu majority. Many clergy members aligned with Hutu political movements, helping to legitimise their rise to power.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Catholic Church played a key role during Belgian rule by collaborating with colonial authorities to maintain and reinforce the social hierarchy. Missionaries viewed Tutsis as more advanced than Hutus and supported their continued dominance in leadership positions ($\checkmark$). Christianity was widely promoted, with mass conversions encouraged and made mandatory for the Tutsi elite, embedding religion into colonial governance ($\checkmark$). The Church also played a major role in education by establishing Western-style schools, but these primarily served Tutsis, further deepening divisions between ethnic groups ($\checkmark$). Additionally, in the late 1950s, the Church played a role in the shift of Belgian support from the Tutsi elite to the Hutu majority, backing the 1959 Hutu Revolution by aligning with Hutu political movements and legitimising their rise to power ($\checkmark$).
