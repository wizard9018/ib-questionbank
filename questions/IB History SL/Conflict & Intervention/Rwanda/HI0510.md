# HI0510

Using the information in Source A and your own knowledge, discuss the extent to which French military intervention in the Rwandan genocide caused more harm than good.
[6 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  An extract from *South African History Online,* which outlines the nature and impact of the French military intervention in Rwanda during the 1994 genocide. |

[Source: South African History Online (1994, 21 August). *The last French troops pull out of Rwanda.* Retrieved March 3, 2025 from: https://www.sahistory.org.za/dated-event/last-french-troops-pull-out-rwanda. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
From Source A:
- Good:
    - The French claimed to have saved thousands of lives by establishing a “safe zone” in south-west Rwanda.
- Harm:
    - French troops were accused of supporting the genocidal regime by training militias and supplying them with weapons.
    - French soldiers committed human rights abuses including raping survivors.

From own knowledge:
- Good:
    - Although geographically limited, Operation Turquoise did provide some short-term protection for Tutsi and moderate Hutu civilians by setting up protected camps and evacuating thousands of people who would otherwise have been killed in the genocide
    - The intervention helped deliver humanitarian aid and stabilise parts of the country during the final stages of the genocide.
- Harm:
    - Operation Turquoise allowed many perpetrators of the genocide to flee into neighbouring countries, especially Zaire, helping them escape justice.
    - France had strong political and military ties with the Hutu-led government before and during the genocide, raising questions about bias and motives.
    - The intervention began late, after much of the genocide had already occurred, limiting its effectiveness and making it appear symbolic or politically driven.
    - The mission is described as “highly controversial,” suggesting widespread international and local criticism.
\
[5 marks]

Concluding sentence:
- Should provide a clear summary judgment that reflects the overall argument, linking the evidence clearly to the good/harm caused by French troops.
\
[1 marks]

\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
To a large extent, French military intervention in Rwanda caused more harm than good. Source A states that French troops were accused of helping the genocidal regime by training militias and supplying them with weapons ($\checkmark$).  It also mentions that French soldiers committed serious human rights abuses, including the rape of survivors ($\checkmark$). Additionally, while it provided some temporary protection for civilians who were potential targets of the genocide and delivered humanitarian aid, Operation Turquoise allowed many perpetrators of the genocide to escape into neighbouring Zaire, helping them avoid justice ($\checkmark$). France's long-standing ties with the Hutu-led government further raised doubts about the neutrality of their intervention ($\checkmark$). Although Source A notes that France claimed to have saved thousands of lives by establishing a “safe zone,” the overall impact was limited and came too late to stop the genocide ($\checkmark$). In conclusion, both the source and historical context show that despite some humanitarian efforts, the French intervention did more harm than good ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$).
