# HI0504

Explain **two** factors that increased tensions between Hutus and Tutsis as a result of Belgian rule.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- Belgian policies introduced a system of forced labour, which disproportionately affected the Hutu majority. This economic inequality deepened ethnic tensions.
- European colonial powers propagated racial theories that described the Tutsis as inherently superior due to their physical features and supposed intelligence, further institutionalising divisions.
- Belgians collaborated with the Church to promote Christianity, which also served to reinforce ethnic stratification, as Tutsis were favoured for education and leadership roles while Hutus were marginalised. The colonial emphasis on cash crops like coffee led to economic policies that favoured Tutsi landowners, sidelining Hutu farmers and creating resource-related conflicts.
- The shift from indirect rule under Germans to direct rule by Belgians centralised power, which they allocated to the Tutsi elite, further isolating the Hutu majority from political participation.
- Ethnic classification was formalised in 1933 with identity cards that labelled individuals as Hutu, Tutsi, or Twa, preventing social mobility and institutionalising ethnic divisions.
- In the 1950s, Belgium reversed its support for the Tutsi elite and began favouring the Hutu majority, responding to growing anti-colonial sentiment and aiming to maintain influence after Rwanda's independence. This shift inflamed ethnic tensions, leading to violent uprisings and setting the stage for future conflicts.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One factor that increased tensions between Hutus and Tutsis during Belgian rule was the system of forced labour introduced by the Belgians. The Hutu majority were often forced to work on cash crop plantations, such as coffee farms, under harsh conditions ($\checkmark$). Meanwhile, Tutsis benefited economically as they were favoured by the colonial administration, exacerbating resentment among the Hutu population ($\checkmark$). Another significant factor was Belgium's reliance on the Tutsi elite to administer the colony. The Tutsis were given preferential treatment, including access to education and political power while the Hutus were excluded from these opportunities ($\checkmark$). This inequality created feelings of marginalisation and fuelled ethnic hostilities that persisted even after independence ($\checkmark$).
