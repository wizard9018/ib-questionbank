# HI0652

Summarize the reasons for United States' involvement in World War I.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following:
- The German practice of unrestricted submarine warfare targeted any ships that entered the war zone. This resulted in the sinking of American merchant ships and the Lusitania, a British cruise liner, resulting in the deaths of 128 Americans. Though the Germans briefly stopped this practice for a short time after the Lusitania, they started up again, angering the American public.
- The Zimmerman Telegram was sent by the German Foreign Secretary  to the German minister in Mexico and proposed an alliance between Germany and Mexico against the United States. The telegram offered to help Mexico reclaim Texas, Arizona, and New Mexico from the United States. The telegram was intercepted by the British who shared their intel with the United States. Resentment towards the Mexicans from earlier wars combined with growing resentment for the Germans.
- United States President Woodrow Wilson hesitated to declare war on Germany despite the Zimmerman Telegram and unrestricted submarine warfare. However, public opinion shifted greatly against the Germans because of these slights, allowing Wilson to abandon the US policy of neutrality.
- The American government gave several loans to the Allied powers, giving them a reason to be vested in their victory. Even American bankers and investors like J.P. Morgan helped secure funding for the Allied powers.
- In his declaration of war, President Woodrow Wilson claimed that democratic ideals had to be protected, appealing to the United States' belief systems.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The United States entered the war in 1917 after holding onto its policy of neutrality for several years. Germany's policy of unrestricted submarine warfare was the German practice of using submarines to sink any boats in the warzone without warning. This led to several civilian ships being destroyed, including the Lusitania, a British passenger liner with over 100 Americans on it. While the Germans briefly stopped this practice to maintain a diplomatic relationship with the United States, they started again in 1917, angering the Americans ($\checkmark$). In 1917, a German Foreign secretary sent a telegram to their ambassador to Mexico. This note suggested an alliance between Mexico and Germany if the United States joined the war. In return, Germany promised Mexico that they would help reclaim land from the United States. This telegram was intercepted by the British and shared with President Woodrow Wilson, heightening tensions with Germany ($\checkmark$). Together, these two events shifted public opinion away from neutrality and toward war. This shift in public opinion was an important part of Wilson's declaration of war, as he was unlikely to have gotten involved if the public were overwhelmingly against it ($\checkmark$). Finally, the United States had a vested financial interest in an Allied victory. They had given several large loans to the Allied countries, and American financiers such as J.P. Morgan also gave many private loans, so the US involvement would also protect their investments ($\checkmark$).
