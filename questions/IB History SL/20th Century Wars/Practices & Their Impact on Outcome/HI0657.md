# HI0657

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for an historian studying the Allied response to the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | A New Year greeting from the United States Ambassador to Russia, David R. Francis. This was published on January 13, 1918 and sent to the Secretary of State, Robert Lansing.|

[Source: *Foreign Relations of the United States: 1918 The Conclusion of the Peace of Brest Litovsk.* (1918, January 13). The Avalon Project. Retrieved March 25, 2025 from https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/bl15.asp. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- A primary source written by an American diplomat living in Russia during this time is a good indicator of the Allied response to Russia's separate peace.
- The source's purpose is to convey that the U.S. and their allies in the war do not believe that Russia leaving the war would be beneficial to them in the long run.
- The content is valuable as it gives some explicit concerns that the Allies may have about Russia's separate peace, such as their exclusion from a longer more permanent peace solution and the possibility of Germany dominating their new government.

For limitations, any of the following:
- The primary source is written by an American who would want to keep Russian soldiers in the war to keep the soldiers divided between two fronts in order to better the Allies' odds.
- The content is limited, however, because it only considers the American perspective. The historian would likely want to explore the feelings of multiple Allied countries.
- The purpose is limited as it is only reaffirming statements that had already been made by President Woodrow Wilson.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is valuable to a historian looking at the allied response to the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk because it is a primary source written by an American diplomat living in Russia at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). However, the content of the source is limited because it only considers the American perspective and none of the other allies ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$). The content of this source is valuable as it considers specific reasons why the treaty would not benefit Russia in the long-term. Specifically, the document states that the Russians would be left out of a more permanent peace settlement ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). Still, the purpose of the document is limited as it is just a reiteration of statements that had previously been made by President Wilson and does not add any new thoughts or ideas ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).
