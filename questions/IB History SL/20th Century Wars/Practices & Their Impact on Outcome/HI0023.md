# HI0023

Define *Blitzkrieg* and explain how its tactics contributed to Germany's early successes in the Second World War. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Any of the following (**up to one**) for definition: 

- A military tactic meaning “lightning war” involving fast, coordinated attacks using infantry, tanks, and air power.

A strategy designed to quickly overwhelm the enemy through rapid, concentrated strikes.

Any of the following (**up to two**) for explanation:

- *Blitzkrieg* allowed Germany to catch enemies by surprise, leading to swift victories and minimal German casualties.

The tactic overwhelmed unprepared and slower-moving armies, such as Poland in 1939 and France in 1940.

- *Blitzkrieg*'s coordination between airstrikes and ground forces disorganised enemy defences, making counter attacks difficult.

It helped Germany capture key territories quickly, destabilising opposing nations' war efforts in the early stages of the war.

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:\

*Blitzkrieg* is a military tactic that involves fast, sudden attacks combining air strikes, tanks, and ground troops to overwhelm the enemy before they can effectively respond ($\checkmark$). This tactic was crucial to Germany's early victories because it took their opponents by surprise, such as in the invasions of Poland and France ($\checkmark$). The speed and coordination between air and ground forces disrupted enemy defences, preventing them from organising counterattacks ($\checkmark$).
