# HI0257

What, according to the source, were the reasons for the use of atomic weapons on Japan?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpt from the article $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Final Months of the War with Japan" by MacEachin, D. J., former Deputy Director for Intelligence at the US Central Intelligence Agency, published 1998. |

:box[…for all practical purposes the decision on whether to use the nuclear weapon against Japan had already been reached by the time the President arrived in Potsdam. On this point virtually all scholars who have studied the issue seem to concur, however much they may disagree on the motives for its use and whether its use was justified. …
\
\
Nor should the evidence be construed as indicating that concern over casualties was the only factor exerting critical force on the A-bomb decision. What the evidence does indicate is that the view of the bomb as a potential way to end the war quickly--in the hope of (1) avoiding the need for an invasion with resulting casualties that by any standard would be of intimidating proportions, (2) minimizing the USSR's postwar leverage, and (3) not having to confront debate over concessions on the terms of unconditional surrender--was the driving force in the minds of the US leadership team before Potsdam, and before the acquisition of intelligence showing much-larger-than-expected Japanese forces on Kyushu [the southernmost Japanese home island]. Nonetheless, it is certainly plausible that the buildup disclosed by early August reinforced the belief that the decision to use the bomb was the path of least resistance. …
\
\
For those favoring a bomb-and-blockade approach or even just a postponement of any invasion, Soviet entry could have been cited as an additional reason why surrender could be obtained without invading the main Japanese islands. On the other hand, if Japan's surrender did not take place until after the Soviets had been in the Pacific war for some length of time, and if there were no US forces on Japanese territory (because no US invasion had occurred), how could the United States and its Allies acquire the control over occupation that they were seeking? Unless the mere entry by the USSR somehow caused an immediate surrender on the unconditional terms being demanded by Washington, the Potsdam experience was likely to reinforce the tendency among at least some US officials to see any gain resulting from Soviet entry as also carrying a serious potential cost--the possible emergence of a Far Eastern version of the Soviet hegemony that was beginning to be imposed on Eastern Europe.
 \
]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- To avoid casualties
- To end the war quickly
- To minimize the USSR's postwar leverage: they wanted to avoid the possible emergence of a Far Eastern version of the Soviet hegemony that was beginning to be imposed on Eastern Europe.
- To not have to confront debate over concessions on the terms of unconditional surrender
- Knowledge of military buildup in Kyushu disclosed by early August reinforced the belief that the decision to use the bomb was the path of least resistance.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
From the source, we can learn that the atomic weapons were used on Japan because the United States wanted to end the war as quickly as possible ($\checkmark$). Also, they wanted to avoid American casualties ($\checkmark$). Moreover, there was the worry that if the USSR participated in the defeat of Japan, it would impose its hegemony over the Far East just like it had done in Eastern Europe ($\checkmark$).
