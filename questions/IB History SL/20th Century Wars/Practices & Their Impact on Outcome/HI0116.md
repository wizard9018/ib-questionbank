# HI0116

Explain **two** limitations of the support provided to the Republicans by the Soviet Union during the Spanish Civil War. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

- Soviet support was politically motivated and caused internal divisions

    - The USSR gave aid mainly to strengthen communist control within the Republican side, not to support broader unity.

    - Soviet advisors influenced the Spanish Communist Party, leading to conflicts with other Republican factions such as anarchists and socialists.

    - This ideological interference weakened Republican unity and military coordination.

- Soviet aid was inconsistent and declined over time

    - Although the USSR sent weapons, tanks, and military advisors, the support lacked long-term consistency.

    - By 1938, Stalin reduced and eventually withdrew Soviet aid.

    - This left the Republicans under-equipped during the final stages of the war, while the Nationalists continued to receive steady support from Germany and Italy.

- Reliance on Soviet aid limited Republican autonomy

    - The Republicans became dependent on Soviet supplies and political direction.

    - This undermined their domestic and international credibility, particularly among Western democracies who feared communist influence.

    - It also led to purges of non-communist left-wing figures within the Republican camp, further weakening their effectiveness.

- Weapons and equipment were outdated or limited in quantity

    - Much of the equipment sent by the USSR was second-hand or outdated.

    - In contrast, Germany and Italy provided Franco with more modern aircraft, tanks, and artillery.

    - This technological gap made it difficult for Republican forces to compete on the battlefield.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One major limitation of Soviet support was that it was politically motivated and caused internal division within the Republican side. Stalin used his aid to increase the influence of the Spanish Communist Party, which led to tensions and infighting with other left-wing factions such as anarchists and socialists ($\checkmark$). This disunity weakened the Republicans' ability to coordinate militarily and politically ($\checkmark$).

Another limitation was that Soviet aid was inconsistent and declined over time ($\checkmark$). Although the USSR initially sent weapons and advisors, by 1938 Stalin had reduced support significantly and eventually withdrew aid completely. This left the Republicans poorly equipped in the later stages of the war, while Franco continued receiving strong and consistent support from Germany and Italy ($\checkmark$).
