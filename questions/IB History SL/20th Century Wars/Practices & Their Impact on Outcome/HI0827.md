# HI0827

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyze the value and limitations of Source A for a historian studying U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War.
[4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

Origin
- Value:
    - Official U.S. government document (Joint Resolution by the 88th Congress, 1964), providing an authoritative primary source on U.S. legislative and executive justification for escalation in Vietnam.
    - Reflects the formal legal basis granted to President Johnson to expand military action, showing how U.S. policy was legitimized domestically.
- Limitations:
    - Reflects only the U.S. government's official stance, so it may omit internal debates, dissent, or alternate perspectives within the U.S. political system.
    - Produced shortly after the Gulf of Tonkin incidents, it may contain biased or incomplete information about the actual events, limiting its reliability on factual accuracy.
Purpose
- Value:
    - Intended to justify U.S. military escalation by portraying North Vietnam as the aggressor threatening international peace, showing how the U.S. framed its intervention as defensive and lawful.
    - Demonstrates U.S. commitment to Cold War alliances (Southeast Asia Treaty Organisation) and the wider goal of containing communism.
    - Helps historians understand the rhetoric and reasoning used to gain congressional and public support for the war.

- Limitations:
    - Purpose is inherently political and persuasive; designed to rally support and legitimize U.S. military action, so it may exaggerate or oversimplify North Vietnamese aggression.
    - Omits discussion of U.S. strategic interests beyond stated ideals (e.g., economic, geopolitical), limiting insight into the full scope of motivations.
Content
- Value:
    - Provides direct evidence of the U.S. claim that North Vietnam attacked American naval vessels in international waters, a key justification for military action.
    - Specifies the extent of presidential authority granted to use "all necessary measures," illustrating the legal framework for escalating U.S. involvement.
    - Emphasizes U.S. self-portrayal as a defender of freedom and international peace, useful for analyzing Cold War ideological narratives.

- Limitations:
    - The claim of repeated attacks on U.S. vessels has been contested or re-evaluated by later historians, raising questions about factual accuracy.
    - Does not acknowledge the Vietnamese perspective or broader regional complexities, limiting its scope for comprehensive understanding.
    - The language is vague about what "all necessary measures" entails, leaving room for varied interpretation and use of power.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
As an official U.S. government document, specifically a Joint Resolution passed by  Congress in 1964, it serves as an authoritative source that reflects the formal legislative and executive rationale for escalating American military involvement in Vietnam. It offers direct insight into the political mindset of U.S. leaders at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$).

Secondly, the content of the resolution is valuable in that it presents the United States as a defender of freedom and international peace. This self-portrayal helps historians understand the ideological framing of the Cold War, particularly how the U.S. justified its intervention in Southeast Asia in terms of protecting democratic values and resisting communism ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

However, the purpose of the document introduces limitations. Since it was intended to rally public and congressional support for military action, it is inherently political and persuasive in nature. As such, it may exaggerate or simplify the threat posed by North Vietnam, portraying the U.S. as purely defensive and omitting the complexities of the conflict ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).

Finally, its origin also limits its reliability. Written shortly after the Gulf of Tonkin incident, the resolution may reflect incomplete or biased accounts of those events, which were later shown to be more ambiguous than initially reported. This reduces the source's usefulness for establishing an accurate account of what happened, but highlights how the events were used to justify escalation
($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).
