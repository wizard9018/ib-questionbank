# HI0081

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyze the value and limitations of the source for a historian studying the Long March.
[4 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source** $\\$ $\\$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  *Excerpt from Report by Mao Tse-tung at a Communist Party Conference*, December 27th, 1935.  |

:box[The Long March is a manifesto. It has proclaimed to the world that the Red Army is an army of heroes, while the imperialists and their running dogs, Chiang Kai-shek [Jiang Jieshi] and his like, are impotent. It has proclaimed their utter failure to encircle, pursue, obstruct and intercept us. The Long March is also a propaganda force. It has announced to some 200 million people in eleven provinces that the road of the Red Army is their only road to liberation. Without the Long March, how could the broad masses have learned so quickly about the existence of the great truth which the Red Army embodies? The Long March is also a seeding-machine. In the eleven provinces it has sown many seeds which will sprout, leaf, blossom, and bear fruit, and will yield a harvest in the future. In a word, the Long March has ended with victory for us and defeat for the enemy. Who brought the Long March to victory? The Communist Party. Without the Communist Party, a long march of this kind would have been inconceivable. (...) A new situation arose as soon as the Long March was over. In the battle of Chihlochen the Central Red Army and the Northwestern Red Army, fighting in fraternal solidarity, shattered the traitor Chiang Kai-shek's campaign of "encirclement and suppression" against the Shensi-Kansu border area and thus laid the cornerstone for the task undertaken by the Central Committee of the Party, the task of setting up the national headquarters of the revolution in northwestern China. \
]

[Source: Mao, Tse-tung (1935, December 27th). *On Tactics Against Japanese Imperialism*. Marxists Internet Archive. Retrieved September 26th, 2024 from https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_11.htm. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
If only values or limitations are discussed, the response cannot earn more than **two** marks. There must be at least one reference to origin, purpose and content in either value or limitations to reach **four** marks.

$\\$

 |  | Value |Limitations|
 | --- | --- | --- |
| **Origin** | Mao has participated in the Long March and therefore has insight. | Mao was responsible for taking the decision to undergo the Long March and therefore is biased in its favor.|
| **Purpose** | To inform the Communist Party Conference about the impacts of the Long March. Since the purpose is to inform, Mao is giving reliable information.  |To persuade the Communist Party Conference about how positive for the Revolution the Long March was. Since the purpose is to persuade, he is exaggerating certain points and leaving out others as can be seen in the content.  |
| **Content** | It lets us know about the immediate impacts of the Long March, like the fact that it served as a way of spreading communist ideas. It also lets us know that the Communists are not planning to set up their headquarters in northwestern China. |It does not mention any of the negative impacts of the Long March. For example, it does not mention the number of casualties. For a historian studying the Long March, the source leaves out the events of the March itself. |

[4 marks]
$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
This source is valuable in its origin since it`s made by one of the leaders of the Communist forces during the Long March itself so he has insight into the situation ($\checkmark$). However, since Mao was the one that decided to undertake the Long March, he is inclined in favor of it ($\checkmark$). Since he is speaking to the Communist Party Conference the purpose is limited as he is trying to convince the Conference that the Long March was a good idea ($\checkmark$). However, since he is speaking at a Conference of people that belong to his own party, and are on his side during the ongoing civil war, he needs to tell them what actually happened so the Party can make informed decisions ($\checkmark$) so this element of the purpose does provide some value to an historian studying the Long March. When talking about content, while the source does mention many of the positive consequences of the Long March ($\checkmark$), like it being a great propaganda tool, it leaves out the negative consequences of the March and so a historian studying the Long March will not get the full picture of its impacts ($\checkmark$).
