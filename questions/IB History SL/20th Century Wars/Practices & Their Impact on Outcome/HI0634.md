# HI0634

Define guerrilla warfare and describe **two** of its features.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
For definition, the following or similar:
- Guerrilla Warfare refers to a form of irregular warfare where smaller groups of combatants or irregular troops, use unconventional tactics to fight a larger, more traditional military force.
\
[1 marks]

For key features, any **two** of the following:
- Typically involves a significant imbalance of power, where the guerrilla forces are much smaller and less equipped compared to the regular army or occupying force. This imbalance forces guerrillas to use unconventional methods and tactics to counter the superior strength of their opponents.
- Guerrilla forces avoid direct confrontation with larger, well-equipped armies. Instead, they use ambushes and surprise attacks to harass enemy forces, strike vulnerable targets (such as supply lines or command posts), and quickly vanish before retaliation can occur.
- Guerrilla fighters often seek to exploit difficult or inaccessible terrain, such as forests, mountains, or urban areas, to hide, launch attacks, and retreat. This allows them to sustain resources.
- Guerrillas frequently seek to gain the support of local communities, who provide them with resources, intelligence, shelter, and recruits. Without such support, guerrilla fighters would have limited success in their operations.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Guerrilla warfare is a type of unconventional combat in which small, irregular units engage a larger, conventional military force using nontraditional strategies ($\checkmark$). One of its key features is the imbalance of power: guerrilla fighters are typically outnumbered and outgunned by professional armies. This imbalance of power encourages them to adopt creative and irregular tactics to overcome their disadvantage ($\checkmark$). Another key feature is the avoidance of direct, large-scale battles. Guerrilla fighters rely on ambushes and surprise strikes to disrupt enemy operations, targeting weaknesses like supply routes then retreating swiftly to avoid counterattacks ($\checkmark$).
