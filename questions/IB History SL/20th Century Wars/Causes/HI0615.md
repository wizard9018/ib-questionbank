# HI0615

What does Source B suggest about the troubles in the Balkans?   [2 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | A political cartoon titled “The Boiling Point” published in the British magazine *Punch* in 1912 which shows the great powers of Europe, Germany, Great Britain, Austria-Hungary, Russia, and France, sitting on a boiling pot labeled “Balkan Troubles”.|

:box[![hi0614](images/hi0614.png)]

[Source: Raven-Hill, L. (1912). *The Boiling Point*. Wikimedia Commons. Retrieved March 11, 2025  from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Balkan_troubles1.jpg  Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

- The boiling pot symbolizes that troubles in the Balkans may be ready to “boil over” or explode.

- The men sitting on the top of the pot indicate that they are trying to stop the pot from boiling over or to control trouble in the area.

- The worried faces on the men show that they are not sure how much longer they can control the situation.

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The pot is labeled Balkan Troubles. It is shown to be close to boiling over which indicates that the problems in the Balkans may be getting worse. ($\checkmark$) The men sitting on the lid represent some of the powerful nations in Europe, such as Germany and Great Britain. By sitting on the lid, the cartoon indicates that they are trying to stop the pot from boiling over, or keep control of the problems in the Balkans. ($\checkmark$)
