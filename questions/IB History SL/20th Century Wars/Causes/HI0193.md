# HI0193

According to Source B, what were the objectives of the United States in Indochina during the French occupation?

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - The U.S. sought to limit Communist influence in Southeast Asia, particularly in Indochina, where Ho Chi Minh’s Communist-led movement threatened the stability of the region and U.S. interests.

 - A U.S. goal was to assist France, a key ally, in maintaining control over Indochina to prevent further economic strain on France, which was vital to European recovery after World War II.

 - The U.S. aimed to achieve stability in Southeast Asia by encouraging France to address the nationalist aspirations of the Vietnamese people, as ending the conflict was seen as crucial to reducing anti-Western sentiment.

 - The U.S. sought to avoid becoming directly involved in the conflict by pressing France to resolve the situation through reforms and negotiations rather than relying solely on military solutions.

 - The U.S. recognized the importance of maintaining French influence in Indochina, both to sustain a Western-aligned presence in the region and to counter Communist expansion.

 - The U.S. aimed to foster non-Communist nationalist movements in Indochina as an alternative to Ho Chi Minh's Communist-dominated leadership, though this goal was hampered by a lack of viable alternatives.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

According to Source B, the United States had several objectives in Asia in 1949. One key aim was to curb the spread of Communism, especially in Indochina, where Ho Chi Minh's leadership posed a direct threat to stability and U.S. strategic interests ($\checkmark$). Another goal was to bolster France’s position in the region, as maintaining French control was seen as critical to preventing further economic strain on a vital European ally recovering from World War II ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, the U.S. prioritized fostering stability in Southeast Asia by urging France to meet the nationalist demands of the Vietnamese, which would help reduce anti-Western sentiment and ease tensions in the region ($\checkmark$).
