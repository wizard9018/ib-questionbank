# HI0431

Identify **two** key events in Spain between 1931 and 1936 and explain how they increased tensions leading to the war.  [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:

- Proclamation of the Second Republic (April 1931) - King Alfonso XIII abdicated, and Spain became a republic. The new progressive government introduced reforms that angered conservatives, including land reform, military restructuring, and anti-clerical policies. The shift from monarchy to a republic immediately polarised Spanish society.

- Anti-Clerical Reforms and Church Burnings (1931-1932) - The government restricted the Church's power by banning religious education and nationalising Church property. Leftist groups attacked churches and monasteries, burning them down in 1931, which led to conservative outrage and deepened divisions between secular and religious factions.

- Failed Military Coup by General Sanjurjo (1932) - Right-wing general José Sanjurjo attempted a coup to overthrow the Republic, but it failed. This event demonstrated that conservative factions and the military were willing to use force to oppose the government, increasing political instability.

- Land Reforms and Peasant Uprisings (1932-1933) - The government attempted to redistribute land from wealthy landowners to poor peasants, but landowners resisted, and reforms were slow. Peasants began staging uprisings, and the failure to effectively implement reforms led to further radicalisation of both landowners, who supported the right, and peasants, who turned to more extreme leftist movements.

- Right-Wing Victory in the 1933 Elections (CEDA Government) - The right-wing Confederación Española de Derechas Autónomas (CEDA) won the elections and began rolling back progressive reforms. This angered left-wing workers, anarchists, and socialists, who viewed it as a betrayal of the Republic's values and responded with strikes and revolts.

- Asturias Miners' Revolt (October 1934) - Left-wing workers in Asturias staged an armed revolt against the right-wing government in response to CEDA's policies. The uprising was brutally suppressed by government forces under Francisco Franco, resulting in thousands of deaths and imprisonments. This deepened class divisions and helped elevate Franco as a key figure for right-wing forces.

- Popular Front Victory in the 1936 Elections - In February 1936, the left-wing Popular Front coalition won the elections, prompting mass strikes, land occupations, and violent street clashes between left- and right-wing militias. Right-wing groups, including Franco and other military officers, saw this as a threat and began actively planning a coup.

- Assassination of José Calvo Sotelo (July 1936) - Right-wing politician José Calvo Sotelo was assassinated by leftist police officers in retaliation for the killing of a Republican officer. His murder was seen as a major provocation by the right, and shortly after, Franco and the military launched a coup on July 17, 1936, triggering the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Asturias Miners' Revolt of October 1934 was an armed uprising by left-wing workers in response to the right-wing CEDA government's reversal of progressive reforms ($\checkmark$). The miners seized key areas in Asturias, but the government, led by Francisco Franco, brutally suppressed the rebellion using the army and Moroccan troops ($\checkmark$). Thousands were killed, and mass imprisonments followed, deepening class divisions and increasing hostility between left-wing and right-wing factions in Spain ($\checkmark$). 

Another key event was the assassination of José Calvo Sotelo in July 1936, a prominent right-wing monarchist politician ($\checkmark$). He was killed by leftist police officers in retaliation for the murder of a Republican officer, and his death was perceived by many on the right as a symbol of the Republic's inability to maintain law and order ($\checkmark$). The assassination radicalised conservative factions and was seen as the final spark that justified military intervention ($\checkmark$).
