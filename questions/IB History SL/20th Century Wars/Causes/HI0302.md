# HI0302

Compare and contrast the role of nationalism as a cause for the First and Second World Wars

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **four** of the following with at least **one** comparison (similarity) and one difference (contrast). Answers which only address similarities OR differences can achieve a maximum of **three** marks.

For comparison, any **two** of the following for similarities:

 - In both wars, nationalism fueled tensions and rivalries. In WWI, nationalism created rivalries among European powers (e.g., France seeking revenge against Germany for the loss of Alsace-Lorraine). In WWII, nationalism drove aggressive expansion (e.g., Nazi Germany's vision of a Greater Germany)

 - Nationalism motivated territorial ambitions in both wars. In WWI, Balkan nationalism fueled disputes, such as Serbia's desire to unite South Slavic peoples. In WWII, territorial ambitions were central to expansionist nationalism, such as Japan's $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Asia for Asians” policy and Germany's pursuit of Lebensraum.

 - Nationalism shaped alliance systems in both wars. In WWI, alliances like the Austro-Hungarian partnership with Germany were motivated by nationalist goals. In WWII, nationalism was key to forming the Axis powers, including the alliance of Germany, Italy, and Japan.

 - Nationalism played a role in mobilizing public opinion in both wars. In WWI, propaganda emphasized patriotic duty and loyalty to the nation. In WWII, nationalist rhetoric was used to justify aggressive policies and maintain public support for the war effort.

For contrast, any **two** of the following for differences:

 - Nationalism was often defensive in WWI but aggressive in WWII. In WWI, Serbian nationalism sought to protect sovereignty and oppose Austro-Hungarian dominance. In WWII, nationalism was more aggressive, as seen in Nazi Germany's Lebensraum policy and Japan's imperial ambitions in Asia.

 - Nationalism in WWI was not as ideologically driven as in WWII. In WWI, it focused on independence and territorial disputes, such as Balkan nationalism. In WWII, nationalism was deeply tied to ideologies like fascism and racial superiority, exemplified by Nazi Germany's belief in Aryan supremacy and Japan's cultural superiority.

 - Nationalism in WWI was largely confined to Europe, while in WWII it had a global reach. In WWI, nationalism was concentrated in the Balkans and among European powers. In WWII, it influenced conflicts worldwide, including Japan's Pan-Asianism and anti-colonial nationalism in Asia and Africa.

 - Colonies were less significant in the nationalism of WWI compared to WWII. In WWI, colonial nationalism played a minor role in the causes of the war. In WWII, Axis powers exploited colonial nationalist movements to weaken the Allies (e.g., Japan fostering anti-colonial sentiments in Southeast Asia).

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Nationalism was a driving force in both the First and Second World Wars, creating rivalries and fueling territorial ambitions. For example, in WWI, nationalism intensified tensions between France and Germany, as France sought to reclaim Alsace-Lorraine. Similarly, in WWII, nationalism inspired Nazi Germany's expansionist policies, such as its aim to establish a Greater Germany ($\checkmark$ similarity ). Additionally, nationalism influenced the formation of alliances in both wars. In WWI, nationalist goals aligned countries like Austria-Hungary and Germany, while in WWII, the Axis powers were formed through shared nationalist and expansionist ideologies ($\checkmark$ simiarlity).

However, nationalism in WWI was more focused on defense and sovereignty, such as Serbian nationalism resisting Austro-Hungarian control. In contrast, WWII nationalism was more aggressive, with nations like Japan pursuing imperial domination in Asia ($\checkmark$ difference). Furthermore, nationalism in WWI lacked a strong ideological foundation, focusing instead on independence and territorial disputes. In WWII, it was deeply linked to ideologies like fascism and racial superiority, as seen in Nazi Germany's policies and Japan's belief in cultural dominance ($\checkmark$ difference).
