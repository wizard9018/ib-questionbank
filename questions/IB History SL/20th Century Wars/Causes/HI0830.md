# HI0830

According to Source C, what was the ultimate goal of U.S. policy in Afghanistan?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  An excerpt from the “National Security Decision Directive 166: U.S. Policy Toward Afghanistan” given by US President Ronald Reagan, 1985, and declassified in 2008.|

[Source: Reagan, R. (1985, March 27). *National Security Decision Directive 166: U.S. Policy Toward Afghanistan.* Ronald Reagan Presidential Library. Retrieved June 14, 2025 from https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/archives/reference/scanned-nsdds/nsdd166.pdf Public Domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following
- Removal of Soviet forces: The ultimate goal was “the removal of Soviet force from Afghanistan.”
- Restoration of Afghan independence: The U.S. aimed to restore Afghanistan's “independent status,” free from Soviet control or influence.
- Undermine the Soviet long-term strategy: The U.S. aimed to demonstrate that the Soviet Union's long-term strategy of subjugating Afghanistan would ultimately fail.
- Create pressure for Soviet policy change: By worsening the situation for Soviet forces, the U.S. aimed to “build pressure on the Soviet Union to adjust its policies” in a manner favourable to U.S. interests and the Afghan people.
- Prevent Soviet consolidation of power in the region: The U.S. aimed to stop the Soviets from using Afghanistan as a “secure base” to project power into the surrounding region, especially Iran and Pakistan.
- Support an Indigenous resistance movement: Preventing the defeat of Afghan resistance fighters was key to U.S. goals to avoid signalling that Soviet military intervention could succeed.
- Promote Soviet political isolation: A goal was to “promote Soviet isolation in the Third and Islamic worlds” by ensuring continued condemnation of Soviet actions in Afghanistan.
- Deny strategic regional advantages to the USSR: The U.S. sought to prevent the Soviet Union from using Afghanistan as a launching point for influence over post-Khomeini Iran or as a means of destabilising Pakistan.
- Demonstrate U.S. resolve in Cold War conflicts: Implicitly, this goal also served to show American commitment to containing Soviet expansion and supporting anti-communist movements globally.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, according to the source, the ultimate goal of U.S. policy in Afghanistan was “the removal of Soviet force from Afghanistan” ($\checkmark$). Second was the restoration of the country's “independent status”($\checkmark$). Finally, the United States sought to undermine the Soviet Union's broader ambitions by demonstrating that its long-term strategy for conquering Afghanistan would not succeed ($\checkmark$).
