# HI0304

Compare and contrast the role of foreign intervention in the causes of the Spanish Civil War and the Korean War.

[4 marks]

---

At least **two** arguments, **one** comparison and one contrast, both with accompanying evidence

For comparison, any of the following similarities:

 - In both wars, foreign powers played a crucial role in escalating tensions before the conflict began. In the Spanish Civil War, international support for different factions increased polarization, while in the Korean War, Cold War superpowers influenced the division of Korea and its growing hostilities.

 - Both conflicts were shaped by the ideological rivalry between communism and anti-communism. In Spain, the USSR supported the Republicans, while fascist powers aided Franco's Nationalists. In Korea, the Soviet Union and China backed North Korea, while the United States supported South Korea.

 - In both cases, foreign intervention contributed to transforming internal struggles into larger conflicts with international dimensions. Spain became a battleground for European fascist and communist forces, while Korea became a proxy war between the U.S. and the USSR.

For contrast, any of the following differences:

 - In Korea, both sides received direct military intervention, with the U.S. and UN forces actively fighting for South Korea and China directly sending troops to support North Korea. In contrast, in Spain, only the Nationalists received direct military support in the form of German and Italian troops, while Soviet aid to the Republicans was limited to weapons, advisors, and volunteers.

 - The Spanish Civil War remained a civil conflict within one country, whereas the Korean War became an international war, involving multiple nations under the UN command and full-scale military operations.

 - The timing of foreign involvement differed. In the Spanish Civil War, foreign powers intervened after the war had already begun, supporting one side or the other as the conflict developed. In contrast, in Korea, foreign involvement was a direct cause of the war, as the division of Korea was largely driven by U.S. and Soviet policies after World War II, long before fighting started.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Foreign intervention played a significant role in the lead-up to both the Spanish Civil War and the Korean War. In both conflicts, foreign involvement intensified pre-existing divisions within the country, making reconciliation between opposing factions nearly impossible ($\checkmark$ compare). Similarly, both wars became battlegrounds for broader ideological struggles, with the Spanish Civil War featuring support from the USSR for the Republicans and assistance from fascist Germany and Italy for Franco, while in Korea, the Soviet Union and China backed North Korea and the U.S. supported South Korea ($\checkmark$ evidence ).

However, the nature of direct involvement differed. In the Korean War, both sides received full military support, with the U.S. and its UN allies directly fighting for South Korea and China sending troops to assist North Korea. In contrast, in the Spanish Civil War, only Franco's Nationalists received direct foreign military assistance, with German and Italian forces actively participating, while Soviet aid to the Republicans was limited to arms, advisors, and volunteers ($\checkmark$ contrast). Another key difference was the timing of foreign involvement. In Spain, external support was provided only after the war had begun, as foreign powers chose sides once the conflict was underway. Meanwhile, in Korea, foreign influence was a direct cause of the war, as the division of the peninsula between North and South was largely driven by U.S. and Soviet policies in the post-World War II period, long before fighting officially started ($\checkmark$ evidence).
