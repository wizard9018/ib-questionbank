# HI0593

What, according to Source C, was the impact of internment on Japanese Americans?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An explanation of Executive Order 9066 published by the National Archives (2022).

[Source: Executive Order 9066: Resulting in Japanese-American Incarceration (2022, January 24). *Milestone Documents. National Archives*. Retrieved February 10, 2025 from https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/berlin-airlift //archives.gov/milestone-documents/executive-order-9066. Public domain. ]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

- They lost personal liberties. 

- Due to the speed at which the orders were implemented, many were unable to sell their property quickly. 

- They were treated as criminals as the camps were built in isolated places and surrounded by guards. 

- They had fewer rights than criminals since they could not even appeal their incarceration.

- Even though they were treated as enemies of the country, some were still drafted into the army. 

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

From Source C, we can learn that internment for Japanese Americans was a traumatic experience. Being confined, they lost many of their personal liberties ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, due to the rapid implementation of the order, many were forced to sell their property as quickly as possible, often unable to get a reasonable price ($\checkmark$). Perhaps the worst consequence was that they were treated as criminals, put in camps built in the middle of nowhere, and surrounded by guards ($\checkmark$).
