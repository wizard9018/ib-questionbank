# HI0295

Evaluate the impact of WWII on the role of women in the United States.

[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Since the command term is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$evaluate”, the response must contain both positive and negative results to reach more than four marks. To reach full marks, there must be a conclusion. 

Positive results: 

 - Creation of Women's Army Corps (WACs), US Naval Women's Reserve (WAVES), Women's Air Force Service Pilots (WASPs) and Women's Coast Guard Reserve. In total, 200,000 serve in war,

 - 6 million enter the workforce, 57$\%$ more than before WWII

 - The absence of husbands made women heads of the family.

Negative results: 

 - Female stereotypes are reinforced in the Armed Forces despite women's new roles. For example, in 1944 a contest was organized under the title:  $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Who is the prettiest in the Armed Forces?”

 - They have no way to take care of their children if there are no grandparents/aunts: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$8-hour orphans” the media calls them, criticizing the mothers

 - With demobilisation and the smaller demand for goods that characterised the post war years, most women lost the jobs they had done during the war: 2.5 million women stopped working outside the home. 

 - Women still earned an average of 60$\%$ less than men.

\

[5 marks]

Concluding remark:

 - Responses are likely to conclude that the overall results of WWII were short lived for the role of women in opening up new possibilities in the economic sphere.  

 - The strongest responses will mention the overall positive results while acknowledging the negative.

[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

WWII had a considerable impact on the role of women in the United States. On the positive side, 200,000 women were able to join the Armed Forces participating in almost all tasks except combat ($\checkmark$). Women were even part of the Air Force though the Women's Air Force Service Pilots (WASPs)  that had the job of transporting material and men. The war also made it possible for many women to join the workforce now that more than 10 million men were overseas fighting ($\checkmark$). In 1945 there were  57$\%$ more women working outside the home than before WWII.

However, most of these advances were lost once the war ended. Female sections of the Armed Forces were no longer needed and in fact, even during the war itself female stereotypes were reinforced despite the new positions allowed to women ($\checkmark$). For example, in 1944 a beauty contest was organized under the title  $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Who is the prettiest in the Armed Forces?” showing that women were still valued for their looks and not for their contribution to the war effort. The same can be said of their role in the job market. After 1945 with demobilisation and the smaller demand for goods that characterised the post war years, most women lost the jobs they had done during the war: 2.5 million women stopped working outside the home ($\checkmark$). 

In conclusion, WWII allowed more women to enter the job market and to participate in the Armed Forces than ever before. However, once the war ended many doors were shut to women. The Armed Forces became again a place for men and many women lost the jobs they had since it was necessary to accommodate the returning soldiers. The changes to the role of women were therefore short lived.  What cannot be denied is that millions of women were not limited to the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$home life” for at least some years and the experiences they had changed the lives of many ($\checkmark$ conclusion).
