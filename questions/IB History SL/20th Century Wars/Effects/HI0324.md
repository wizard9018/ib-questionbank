# HI0324

Explain the reasons for the differing treatment of Japanese Americans and Japanese Argentinians.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with its corresponding evidence:

 - The Japanese threat was much bigger to the US due to its geographical location and the amount of people of Japanese origin. 

     - The Japanese-born population of Argentina in 1940 was only 5000 people. In the US, around 120,000 Japanese and Japanese Americans were interned.  

 - The US had a long history of racism against its Asian population, while Argentina did not

     - Since passage of the Immigration Act of 1924 (the Johnson-Reed Act), Japanese were not allowed to migrate to the US. In Argentina, there were no laws that excluded migrants from any country. 

 - Argentina remained neutral throughout most of the war, declaring war on Japan much later than the US. 

     - Argentina declared war on Japan in March 1945, 5 months after the Japanese surrender, and only when it was already clear the war was almost over. There was not that much time to implement measures against the Japanese community.

 - Japan attacked the US, while Japan and Argentina had friendly relations. 

     - The Japanese attacked the US naval base at Pearl Harbor, while in 1940, Japan elevated its diplomatic representation in Argentina to the embassy level. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

While the United States followed a harsh policy of internment of people of Japanese origin who were living on its West coast, Argentina only confiscated the goods of businesses that were subsidiaries of Japanese companies. There are many reasons for the different ways in which each country dealt with what it considered the domestic Japanese threat during WWII. 

In the case of the US, the threat of Japanese aggression was much greater for the United States than for Argentina due to geographical proximity and demographic factors ($\checkmark$). In 1940, Argentina had only 5,000 Japanese-born residents, whereas in the U.S., approximately 120,000 Japanese and Japanese Americans who were concentrated along its western coast ($\checkmark$). The U.S. also had a long history of racism against its Asian population while this was not the case in Argentina ($\checkmark$). Since 1924, Japanese migration to the U.S. had been legally prohibited whereas in Argentina, there were no laws that excluded migrants from any country ($\checkmark$).
