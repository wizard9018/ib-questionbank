# HI0750

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for an historian studying changes to the role and status of women as a result of World War One.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Resolution to parliament by Herbert Samuel (former Home Secretary), motion to allow women to be eligible as Members of Parliament, 23 October 1918. |

[Source: Samuel, H. (1918, 23 October). *Women As Members Of Parliament.* HC Deb 03 July 1919 vol 110 cc814-5. Hansard. Retrieved April 31, 2025 from https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1918-10-23/debates/c5284686-3207-4f38-9119-906afe488f00/WomenAsMembersOfParliament. Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:
- The origin is valuable as:
    - It is delivered by Herbert Samuel, a senior British politician (former Home Secretary) and key policymaker during WWI.
    - The speech is dated October 1918, during the final weeks of World War I, providing a contemporary view of attitudes as the war concluded.
    - It is an official parliamentary debate, the source offers insight into how government leaders assessed the changing status of women due to the war.
- The purpose is valuable as:
    - It is intended to justify women's right to stand as MPs, so it openly discusses the transformation of women's roles during the war.
    - It offers a clear political acknowledgement that the war changed societal perceptions of women's capabilities and contributions.
    - It demonstrates that wartime service influenced legislative reform, making it valuable for tracing connections between the war and shifts in women's status.
- The content is valuable as:
    - It acknowledges pre-war gender inequality (“woman was not a noun but an adjective”), showing how far perceptions had shifted.
    - It describes how war drew women into public life and new responsibilities, “industrial employment, prices, international politics,” highlighting expanded roles.
    - It reflects the growing political agency of women due to wartime contributions, connecting social roles to political rights.
    - It could be useful for showing how elite male politicians interpreted and responded to women's wartime involvement.

For limitations, any combination of the following:
- The origin is limited as:
    - It focuses on political eligibility post-war rather than on the full range of women's wartime roles (e.g. nursing, munitions work).
    - It represents an elite political opinion, not the views or lived experiences of ordinary women or working-class voices.
    - It provides a British context only; does not provide a wider European perspective on women in WWI.
- The purpose is limited as:
    - It is persuasive speech, it may overstate progress to win support for a political motion.
    - It focuses more on political enfranchisement than on the daily realities or hardships faced by women during the war.
    - It may exclude counter arguments or dissenting views, leading to a one-sided portrayal.
- The content is limited as
    - It lacks specific examples of roles women undertook (e.g. nursing, factory work, voluntary services).
    - It does not mention women's struggles, unequal pay, dangerous working conditions, or opposition to their involvement.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The origin of the source is valuable because it comes from a speech delivered in October 1918, near the end of World War I. This timing gives historians a useful contemporary perspective on how attitudes toward women were shifting as the war drew to a close ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The purpose also adds value, as the speech supports legal reform, directly linking women's wartime contributions to political change, and helps illustrate how their role in society was being reconsidered ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).

However, the source also has limitations. The origin offers insight only into the British experience, without reflecting the broader European context or differences in women's roles across other nations during the war ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). In terms of content, the speech remains general and does not provide concrete examples of the specific jobs or responsibilities women took on, such as work in munitions factories, military nursing, or volunteer organizations, limiting its usefulness for understanding the full scope of their wartime roles ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
