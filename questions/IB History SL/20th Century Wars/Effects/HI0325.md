# HI0325

Describe **two** impacts of World War II on the United States labor movement.

[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - Roosevelt supported trade unions during the Great Depression and, in turn, trade unions did not organize strikes that might hamper the war effort. 

 - In 1943, Congress passed the Smith-Connally Act (or War Labor Disputes Act) to ensure continued wartime production. It allowed the president to seize productions of wartime industries. Roosevelt would use it to intervene in labor disputes and ensure the production of goods to support the war effort

 - With the end of the war and the return of 10 million demobilised soldiers, the national government passed the Taft-Hartley Act. This measure prohibited strikes unless they were first authorized by the government and reserves, clearly limiting the power of trade unions. 

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Roosevelt supported the work of trade unions through most of the Great Depression and unions responded in support by pledging not to strike during the war to assist in the final victory ($\checkmark$). However, in light of a few exceptions, Congress passed the Smith-Connally Act (War Labor Disputes Act) in 1943, granting the president the authority to seize control of industries critical to the war ($\checkmark$). Roosevelt utilized this power to intervene in labor disputes and maintain the steady production of goods necessary to support the war.
