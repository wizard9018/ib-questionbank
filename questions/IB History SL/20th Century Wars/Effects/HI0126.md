# HI0126

What does the Source suggest about peace negotiations after the Russo-Japanese War?
[3 marks]

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source** $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Cartoon by Clifford Berryman, which appeared in the Washington Post on August 6, 1905. The Cartoon shows President Theodore Roosevelt offering peace pipes to Japan and Russia at mediation talks after Japan had defeated Russia in the Russo - Japanese War. |

![HI0126](images/HI0126.jpg)

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Russia was left badly hit by the war and thus was in a weaker position to negotiate.
- Japan still had the power to do more damage if it required indemnities, as seen in the club next to the Japanese man.
- Roosevelt was happy and calm during the peace negotiations which indicates the war was beneficial for the US.
- Japan looks sure of itself while Russia looks worried at the results of the peace negotiations.
- Neither of them seem to want to make peace as none is taking the pipe and they both have their arms crossed.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source suggests that Russia was left badly hit by the war and thus was in a weaker position to negotiate ($\checkmark$). We can see this in the injured face of the Russian character and the smug expression of the Japanese character implying that Japan expects negotiations to favor it ($\checkmark$). In fact, Japan has the power to do more damage to Russia since it can require compensation for the war, as seen in the club labeled “indemnities” next to the Japanese character ($\checkmark$).
