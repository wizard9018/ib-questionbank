# HI0123

What, according to the source, were the Allies' plans for Germany at the end of WWII?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Excerpt from the "Speech of Hope" by James F. Byrnes, United States Secretary of State (September 6, 1946). This speech was given in Stuttgart, Germany.|

:box[When the ruthless Nazi dictatorship was forced to surrender unconditionally, there was no German government with which the Allies could deal. The Allies had temporarily to take over the responsibilities of the shattered German state, which the Nazi dictatorship had cut off from any genuine accountability to the German people. The Allies could not leave the leaders or minions of Nazism in key positions, ready to reassert their evil influence at first opportunity. They had to go.![Screeshot 2-01-14 t 12.04.37](<images/Screeshot 2-01-14 t 12.04.37.png>)But it never was the intention of the American Government to deny to the German people the right to manage their own internal affairs as soon as they were able to do so in a democratic way, with genuine respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms.(...) The Potsdam Agreement wisely provided that administration of the affairs of Germany should be directed toward decentralization of the political structure and the development of local responsibility. This was not intended to prevent progress toward a central government with the powers necessary to deal with matters which would be dealt with on a nation-wide basis. But it was intended to prevent the establishment of a strong central government dominating the German people instead of being responsible to their democratic will. ]

[Source: Byrnes, J. F.  (1946, September 6). *Speech of Hope*. German History in Documents and Images. Retrieved October 17, 2024 from https://ghdi.ghi-dc.org/docpage.cfm?docpage_id=2992.  Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- To temporarily take over the responsibilities of the  German state.
- To remove the Nazis from key positions
- To decentralize the political structure and allow for the development of local responsibility.
- To prevent the establishment of a strong central government that ignores the will of the people
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
After the defeat of Germany, the Allies agreed to fix the lack of a state organization that the Nazis had left behind ($\checkmark$). The Allies also promised to remove Nazis from important positions in the administration ($\checkmark$). In the longer run, they planned for Germany to have a national government that would respect the will of the people ($\checkmark$).
