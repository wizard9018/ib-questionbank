$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source B and C agree on several issues regarding the Paris Peace Conference. Both recognize that there was a disagreement between the aims of Wilson and the aims of the other members of the conference ($\checkmark$ similarity). Source B shows this when it says that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$delegates at the conference had deep misgivings about the American president's plans” and Source C speaks of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two rival schemes for the future polity of the world took the field,—the Fourteen Points of the President, and the Carthaginian Peace of M. Clemenceau”  ($\checkmark$ evidence). For Wilson, they both identify that his only objective was peace and not to demand anything from the ones who lost the war ($\checkmark$ similarity). We can see this in Source B when it states Wilson wanted  $\text{\textquotedblleft}$peace without victory” and in Source C which speaks of Wilson's desire $\text{\textquotedblleft}$For a Peace of magnanimity or of fair and equal treatment” ($\checkmark$ evidence). The sources recognize that this was not the case for the other delegates at the Conference. Source B says, $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Britain and France in particular sought substantial monetary reparations, as well as territorial gains, at Germany's expense”and Source C mentions that the winners wanted to place $\text{\textquotedblleft}$their unbearable financial burdens on to the shoulders of the defeated” ($\checkmark$ similarity and evidence).

On the other hand, the sources identify different perceived future problems for the nations of Europe. While Source B recognizes that the negotiators saw $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the threat posed by a Bolshevik Russia under Vladimir Lenin”, Source C adds the possibility of a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$union of forces between Russia and Germany” as something that worried many of the negotiators ($\checkmark$ contrast and evidence). Source C is also much more extreme regarding the consequences of the Conference by predicting the future destruction of France and Italy if Germany is destroyed due to their $\text{\textquotedblleft}$being so deeply and inextricably intertwined with their victims by hidden psychic and economic bonds.” Source B, on the other hand, mentions that the winners were able to use the treaties to secure “their strategic interests” ($\checkmark$ contrast and evidence).
