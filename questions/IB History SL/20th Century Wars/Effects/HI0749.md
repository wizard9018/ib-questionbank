# HI0749

What is the message of Source B?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Resolution to parliament by Herbert Samuel (former Home Secretary), motion to allow women to be eligible as Members of Parliament, 23 October 1918. |

[Source: Samuel, H. (1918, 23 October). *Women As Members Of Parliament.* HC Deb 03 July 1919 vol 110 cc814-5. Hansard. Retrieved April 31, 2025 from https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1918-10-23/debates/c5284686-3207-4f38-9119-906afe488f00/WomenAsMembersOfParliament. Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The idea that women are inferior or secondary to men is being challenged: society has “given up the old theory that woman's interests were not on a level with man's.”
- Women's political engagement has evolved: “The War, however, has brought great changes.”
- As a result of the war, women have gained public and political awareness: “it has made women familiar with many aspects of public life.”
- Women have become engaged with “industrial employment, prices, international politics,” suggesting they are equipped to participate in governance.
- The war has shifted public opinion in women's favour: “many of us have been ready during the War to modify our previous view,” showing that the war catalyzed support for women's political rights.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, the source argues that traditional views of women as inferior to men are being rejected, by saying that many had “given up the old theory that woman's interests were not on a level with man's" ($\checkmark$). Secondly, it emphasizes that World War I brought “great changes,” helping women become more engaged in politics and public life ($\checkmark$). Finally, the source argues that, as a result of their wartime experiences, women gained greater awareness of national issues and proved their capability beyond the domestic sphere, saying “it has made women familiar with many aspects of public life”($\checkmark$).
