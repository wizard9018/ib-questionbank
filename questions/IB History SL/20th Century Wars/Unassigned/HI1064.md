# HI1064

With reference to its origin, purpose, and content, assess the value and limitations of Source B for a historian studying German foreign policy in early 1939.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from a secret speech given by Adolf Hitler to his military commanders on February 10, 1939. |

[Source: Hitler, A. (10 February 1939). *Hitler's Secret Speech to Military Commanders.* Published in German History in Documents and Images. Retrieved 29 November 2025 from https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/hitlers-secret-speech-to-military-commanders-february-10-1939 Public Domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:

- Origin:
    - Delivered by Adolf Hitler, the head of state, on 10 February 1939 — providing a direct insight into Nazi leadership decision-making.
    - A private speech to military commanders, making it less likely to be shaped by public propaganda motives.
- Purpose:
    - Intended to inform and prepare top military leaders, suggesting a more honest or strategic tone than public speeches.
    - Useful for understanding how Hitler justified future military action to those in command.
- Content:
    - Declares that Germany's foreign policy since 1933 was part of a broader, long-term strategy of expansion and rearmament.
    - Contrasts public claims of peace with secret preparations for war — offering a clearer picture of Nazi intent.

For limitations, any combination of the following:

- Origin:
    - As a speech by Hitler, it reflects the views and agenda of one individual, potentially distorting the broader decision-making process.
    - Offers only the Nazi leadership perspective; excludes military dissent, civilian opinions, or international views.
- Purpose:
    - Though private, Hitler may have aimed to persuade or unify military leaders, possibly overstating confidence or inevitability.
    - May have simplified or distorted the complexity of Germany's position to maintain control or loyalty.
- Content:
    - Focuses on intentions, not actions — it doesn't confirm that all planned steps were carried out.
    - Lacks details on how other countries were reacting or what specific policies would follow — limiting broader historical understanding.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source B is useful because it was spoken directly by Hitler just months before World War II, offering a rare glimpse into his mindset and priorities at the time ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). Since the speech was delivered behind closed doors to high-ranking military officials, it was not meant to shape public opinion, making it more likely to reflect real intentions and strategies ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).  In terms of content, it shows that Hitler viewed past actions — like rearmament and territorial expansion — as steps in a broader plan, helping historians understand that Germany's aggressive policies were calculated and long-standing ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

Still, the source has its limits. Being a speech from Hitler, it only presents his point of view and doesn't include the perspectives of other Nazi leaders or military figures who may have disagreed ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$).  Even in a private setting, he may have aimed to rally support among his generals by presenting a confident and simplified version of events ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).  The speech also doesn't give practical details or include any reactions from foreign governments, so it doesn't fully explain how this policy fit into the larger international context ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
