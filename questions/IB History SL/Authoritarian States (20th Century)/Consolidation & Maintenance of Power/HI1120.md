# HI1120

Compare and contrast the role and significance of Fidel Castro and Ernesto $\lq$Che' Guevara in the consolidation and maintenance of power in Cuba.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |
| :-- | :-- |
|Includes clear points of comparison| ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) |
|Includes clear points of contrast| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |
|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **three** similarities and differences, all supported by clear **evidence**.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both Castro and Guevara spearheaded nationwide campaigns promoting nationalism and anti-imperialism such as the nationalisation of US-owned companies and rejection of US interventions.
- Both Castro and Guevara were committed to strengthening the social fabric of Cuba through tailor-made programs for vulnerable groups.
- Both Castro and Guevara positioned Cuba in the international arena through pro-revolution and anti-imperialist public speeches and active participation in revolutionary attempts in countries in the Americas and Africa.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Castro was the main political and military leader of Cuba after rising to power as commander in chief of the armed forces, whereas Guevara was responsible for taking on key ministerial posts and carrying out a wide range of measures in land and literacy programs.
- While Castro was aligned with the USSR early on, Guevara viewed the Cuban-Soviet relations as detrimental to Cuba’s opposition to foreign influence and consolidation of the revolution.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both Fidel Castro and Ernesto $\lq$Che' Guevara contributed to the consolidation and maintenance of power in Cuba as main leaders of the revolution. This entailed the promotion of nationalism and anti-imperialism ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). For example, the nationalisation of US-owned companies allowed for major independence in the national production system, particularly in the 1960s. In terms of international relations, Castro and Guevara capitalised on the failed Bay of Pigs invasion attempt to further anti-US sentiments and gain support of Cuban fellow nationals ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Moreover, Castro and Guevara were able to strengthen the Cuban state's position domestically through their active participation if foreign affairs ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). For instance, Castro's anti-imperialist speeches in international media outlets and at the United Nations were significant in legitimising his leadership and revolutionary discourse in Cuba. Similarly, Guevara's role in supporting anti-US revolutions in Central America and Africa were paramount for positioning Cuba's foreign policy in the international arena and maintaining a coherent stance with and reinforcing domestic policies ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, both leaders played different roles and had different impacts on the consolidation and maintenance of power in Cuba. While Castro centralised the institutional power through his roles as Prime Minister and chief in commander of the armed forces, Guevara's role in ministerial posts was paramount to the implementation of social measures ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Castro spearheaded the control of Cuba's political developments through the elimination of opposition sectors and centralisation of power as elections and political parties were banned. By contrast, Guevara was essential in strengthening the state's power amongst vulnerable groups through the implementation of land and literacy programs ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
