# HI0316

Explain **two** impacts of the Bay of Pigs Invasion on Castro's consolidation of power.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following impacts, each with supporting explanations:
- Strengthened Castro's Image
    - The victory against the U.S. made Castro appear as a strong leader, boosting his popularity and legitimacy. He used the failed invasion as propaganda to showcase the strength of the revolution and his leadership in defending Cuba against imperialism.
- Created a Unifying Enemy
    - The invasion gave Castro a clear external threat, helping him unite the Cuban people under his leadership. By portraying the U.S. as an aggressor seeking to overthrow the revolution, he fostered a sense of national unity and justified continued revolutionary measures.
- Pushed Cuba Closer to the USSR
    - The attack led Cuba to seek Soviet support, increasing military and economic aid that strengthened Castro's position. In response to the invasion, the USSR expanded its assistance, providing weapons, economic resources, and eventually nuclear protection, which bolstered Castro's regime.
- Justified Further Political Repression and Elimination of Opposition Within Cuba
    - Castro used the invasion as justification for cracking down on internal dissent, arguing that opposition groups could be working with the U.S. The government increased surveillance, imprisoned suspected counter-revolutionaries, and suppressed political freedoms under the pretext of national security.
- Weakened U.S. Influence and Advanced Nationalism in Cuba
    - The failure of the invasion discredited U.S. efforts to control Cuba, allowing Castro to position himself as the leader of an independent, anti-imperialist state. The event fueled nationalist sentiment, reinforcing Cuban sovereignty and diminishing support for U.S.-backed opposition groups.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Bay of Pigs Invasion strengthened Castro's image as a powerful and victorious leader. When the U.S.-backed exiles failed to overthrow him in April 1961, Castro used the moment to showcase his ability to defend Cuba against a foreign threat ($\checkmark$). This victory allowed him to present the revolution as successful and resilient, increasing his popularity among Cubans. As a result, he consolidated his power by reinforcing national unity and silencing opposition under the justification of defending the revolution against imperialist aggression ($\checkmark$).

A different impact of the invasion was that it pushed Cuba into a closer alliance with the Soviet Union ($\checkmark$). The failure of the U.S. attack convinced Castro that he needed stronger international support to protect his government from future interventions. In response, the USSR increased its military and economic aid to Cuba, providing weapons, advisors, and eventually nuclear protection. This alliance gave Castro the external backing he needed to strengthen his regime and secure his position both domestically and internationally, reinforcing his control over the island ($\checkmark$).
