# HI1123

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for an historian studying the significance of legal methods in the consolidation and maintenance of power of the Cuban authoritarian state.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** | $\hspace{3em}$ |  CIA's report on the status of media control by the Castro regime by the early 1980s. |

[Source: Adapted from Central Intelligence Agency. (1983, December). *Cuba: Castro's Propaganda Apparatus and Foreign Policy.* Directorate of Intelligence, p. 3. Retrieved October 15, 2025 from https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP84S00897R000200050008-4.pdf. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, a maximum of **two** marks is awarded.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- The source is valuable in terms of origin because it is a government agency's internal document, which suggests a candid account of the political developments in Cuba.
- The value of the source's purpose is that it informs on the relationships between Castro's regime and the control of the media apparatus in Cuba, drawing upon information gathered by US intelligence services.
- The content is valuable because it outlines different perspectives on how the CIA characterises the influence of Castro within pro-government and independent media outlets.

For limitations, any of the following:
- It is limited in terms of origin because it offers the CIA's one-sided perspective on the extent of the Cuban state's control of the media.
- The source's purpose is limited to outlining the extent of Castro's control over the media with respect to Cuba's anti-US campaign, thereby overlooking the consolidation of power domestically.
- The content is limited to aspects related to how Castro used the media to influence the dissemination of anti-US propaganda and does not account for the wider effects of the Cuban state's power in society.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is valuable in terms of origin because the tone of the report is candid as it is meant for the government's internal use, which allows for using it as one perspective on the extent of Cuban state's consolidation of power ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). With reference to purpose, the value of the source is supported by the informative nature of the internal report meant for guiding the governmental course of action regarding Castro's control over media outlets ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$).

Moreover, the content is valuable because the CIA accounts for different proofs that speak to a wide range of perspectives on the relationships between Castro and both pro-regime and independent outlets ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$). However, the source's content is also limited because it mainly addresses how Castro controls the media as a platform for attacking the USA through propaganda. This focus does not allow to identify the Cuban state's power in wider sectors of society ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
