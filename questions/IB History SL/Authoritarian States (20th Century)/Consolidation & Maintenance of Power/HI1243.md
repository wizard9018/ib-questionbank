# HI1243

“Propaganda was the most significant factor in the maintenance of power in authoritarian states.” Discuss with reference to **two** states, each chosen from a different region.
[15 marks]

Possible suitable examples include:
- **Germany** - Hitler (1933-1945): There were Nazi posters and flags all over Germany, as well as photographs of Hitler. In factories, people had to listen to Nazi propaganda through radios and loudspeakers. Children were indoctrinated in school through the curriculum, which supported Nazi ideas. People would hear this propaganda so often that many people would end up accepting it as the truth. Hitler was portrayed as the infallible *Führer*, while propaganda promoted nationalism, anti-Semitism, and the idea of a racially unified Volksgemeinschaft. Mass rallies, films like “Triumph of the Will,” and cheap radios (*Volksempfänger*) ensured widespread dissemination.
    - Other factors to consider: general terror and repression, SS powers to remove political opponents, Gestapo powers to hunt out enemies of Nazism and Nazi control of the courts and the judges. Responses may also mention economic recovery via rearmament and public works programs, which bolstered public support for Hitler.
- **USSR** - Stalin (1924-1953): Stalin developed a cult of personality around himself. Images of Stalin were everywhere - photographs, statues, posters and pictures. Stakhanovites, Soviet workers of the 1930s, used posters, slogans and radio broadcasts to celebrate communist successes in the Five-Year Plans. It was all this propaganda that made people loyal to Stalin, allowing him to control the Soviet Union. Education, posters, newspapers like *Pravda*, and socialist realism reinforced ideological conformity.
    - Other factors to consider: the Great Terror, NKVD violence and executions, use of the Purges to get rid of rivals and use of gulags for slave labour.
- **China** - Mao (1949-1976): Mao used mass media to spread Maoist ideas and promote the Cult of Personality. Mao's Five-Year Plan propaganda used posters to motivate the workforce. The commune system enabled Mao to establish schools, exposing children to Maoist propaganda and ideology. All this brainwashed people into worshipping Mao, and this helped keep Mao in power.
    - Other factors to consider: mass mobilisation combined with the removal and persecution of landlords and persecution of political enemies during the Hundred Flowers Campaign, Cultural Revolution, and mobilisation of young Chinese in the Red Guards.
- **Cuba** - Castro (1959-2008): The *Granma* newspaper disseminated anti-imperialist and pro-socialist propaganda. Castro's government used a “Golden Age” of poster art to promote revolutionary figures and public health campaigns. The Cuban Institute of Friendship with the Peoples educated the world about the Cuban Revolution. Fidel Castro used speeches, education, state media, and symbolism to promote nationalism, anti-imperialism, and social equality. Propaganda framed economic hardship as the cost of resisting U.S. aggression, especially after the Bay of Pigs invasion and during the Cold War.
    - Other factors to consider: Castro's charismatic leadership, Revolutionary Tribunals for former Batista officials and Committees for the Defense of the Revolution to monitor neighbourhoods. Other social achievements in healthcare and education reinforced propaganda and enhanced popular support in the early part of Castro's rule.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
On the one hand, Hitler's Germany and Mao's China both used propaganda to maintain power ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). In Germany, the Nazi regime organised mass rallies, controlled radio broadcasting and indoctrinated young people to make people loyal to Hitler and the Nazis ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). In China, the Communist Party used posters, the commune system and the Little Red Book to spread Maoist ideas and promote the cult of Mao. On the other hand, both regimes used other methods to maintain power, so to suggest propaganda was the most significant factor would be an overstatement ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). Propaganda, force, terror, and economic policies played a critical role in maintaining power in both regimes ($\checkmark\, \text{organisation}$).

Propaganda was an important tool for the Nazis to spread their ideology quickly and effectively to all parts of German society ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). Firstly, Goebbels, Minister of Propaganda, brought all German broadcasting under Nazi control by creating the Reich Radio Company ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). There was a great extension of the audience, helped by the mass production of very cheap radios called $\lq$The People's Receiver' ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). By 1939, 70% of German homes had a radio. There were also loudspeakers installed in the streets. By controlling radio stations, the Nazis could transmit Hitler's speeches and ideas about German expansion into Eastern Europe and the inferiority of the Jews until these ideas came to be accepted as normal by the German people ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). Secondly, Goebbels organised mass rallies to commemorate historic days in the Nazi calendar, such as Hitler's birthday, Party Foundation Day and Heroes' Remembrance Day ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). These large rallies were very popular and helped strengthen loyalty to the Nazi state ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Nazi propaganda was also embedded in education where curricula reinforced racial ideology, nationalism and loyalty to Hitler ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$), ensuring long-term ideological conformity among German youth ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). The German people did not hear any dissenting voices, making it difficult to challenge what they were told ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

However, propaganda cannot completely account for the Nazi's maintenance of power, as fear, coercion, and economic recovery under the regime also helped them dominate and gain support ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). Firstly, the Nazis used the state police, the Gestapo, and the SS to make people afraid of them ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). Gestapo agents had sweeping powers to spy on Germans by tapping telephones, intercepting mail and using information from a network of informers. They could arrest people and send them to concentration camps without trial ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). This meant that most Germans were frightened to speak out against the regime, so there was little opposition to Nazi policies ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Secondly, Nazi economic policy from 1933 reduced unemployment ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). The Law to Reduce Unemployment was renewed and expanded, and the Reich Labour Service was expanded to employ 19-to 25-year-olds. As a result, state investment was directed towards job-creation schemes such as reforestation, land reclamation, the construction of autobahns, housing and public facilities. As a result of these policies, there was dramatic job growth. From the registered peak of 5.6 million unemployed in 1932, the official figure showed that it had declined to 1.6 million ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). Hitler promised and delivered lower unemployment, which helped to ensure popularity among industrial workers and increase loyalty to the regime ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

Propaganda was also important in maintaining the Communist Party's rule in China ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). Mao used mass media, most notably posters, to help workers reach production targets in the first Five-Year Plan, a programme of industrial development launched in 1953 ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). Posters promoted collective effort, selflessness and faith in Mao's vision. Posters were simple and effective at communicating with China's millions of peasants, who were largely illiterate. Secondly, Mao used the commune system to propagate Communist ideas and his own cult of personality ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). After 1958, Mao set up schools and Party headquarters in 70,000 communes and collective farms across rural China. Communist Party personnel controlled educational policies, exposing children to Maoist propaganda and teaching them to worship Mao and fear Western ideas ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). During the Cultural Revolution, Mao promoted an extensive cult of personality through posters, newspapers, and the Little Red Book ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$), portraying himself as the infallible leader and source of ideological truth ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). Propaganda projected the image of Mao as the saviour of the nation and the great benefactor of the people; as a result, Mao became little less than a god to the great mass of the Chinese people ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

However, similar to Hitler and Germany, propaganda alone cannot explain Mao's maintenance of power in Communist China ($\checkmark\, \text{focus}$). Firstly, the Chinese Communist Party persecuted landlords to reduce their power and redistribute land to peasants, ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$) thus gaining their support ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$). The Agrarian Reform Law of 1950 created collective farms. The property of landlords was confiscated and redistributed among former tenants, further increasing peasant support of the regime. At the same time, the People's Courts persecuted landlords by putting them on public trial and denouncing them as enemies of the people, resulting in the deaths of one million landlords ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). Secondly, Mao purged Communist Party members during the 1957 Hundred Flowers Campaign ($\checkmark\, \text{knowledge}$). He invited Communist Party members to criticise government and party policies, which led to bitter denunciations of Mao. In response, Mao implemented policies against rival politicians, intellectuals, students, artists, and professors. Those who spoke out were condemned as $\lq$rightists' and thousands were arrested and imprisoned, ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$) allowing stronger Communist Party control ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).Faced with Mao's determination to remove anyone regarded as a threat to his authority, Communist Party members dropped all thought of serious opposition ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).

In conclusion, the use of force rather than propaganda was the most important factor in maintaining power for Hitler in Germany and for Mao in China ($\checkmark\, \text{conclusion}$). In Germany, the SS and the Gestapo dealt with any sign of opposition from the Left, made up of communists and socialists. In China under Mao, the People's Liberation Army removed anyone regarded as a threat to Mao's authority, landlords during the anti-landlord campaign and Communist Party members condemned as $\lq$anti-socialists' during the anti-rightist campaign ($\checkmark\, \text{examples}$). While propaganda was important to the maintenance of power of both, it was secondary to the power of fear and coercion to suppress opposition ($\checkmark\, \text{analysis}$).
