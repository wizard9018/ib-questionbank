# HI0375

Describe **three** characteristics of the Cuban state's control of the media mentioned in Source C.
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  CIA's report on the status of media control by the Castro regime by the early 1980s. |

[Source: Adapted from Central Intelligence Agency. (1983, December). *Cuba: Castro's Propaganda Apparatus and Foreign Policy.* Directorate of Intelligence, p. 3. Retrieved October 15, 2025 from https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP84S00897R000200050008-4.pdf. Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The media was controlled by a main administrative body linked to the Cuban regime (e.g., the Communist Party's Political Bureau).
- The control of the media was overarching, including professional associations, publishing houses, newspapers, and radio stations.
- The Cuban state limited control over independent media outlets.
- The Cuban state's control of the media had support from foreign organisations and allies.
- The central government, led by Castro, used the media outlets as platforms for spreading and leveraging nationalist anti-US propaganda internationally.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
According to Source C, the Cuban state was able to control the media through its central institutional apparatus. The Communist Party's Political Bureau was essential in establishing top-down directives for media outlets under the state's control ($\checkmark$). Additionally, Castro's regime was able to strengthen the reach of the media because it was supported by foreign networks aligned with the Cuban central government ($\checkmark$). Nonetheless, the Cuban state's control of the media was partly limited with respect to independent outlets ($\checkmark$).
