# HI1118

Compare and contrast the treatment of opposition in the Chinese and Cuban authoritarian states.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |
| :-- | :-- |
|Includes clear points of comparison| ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) |
|Includes clear points of contrast| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |
|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop a total of **three** similarities and differences, all supported by clear **evidence**.

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both Chinese and Cuban authoritarian states established a one-single party system and banned political parties.
- Both states suppressed anti-regime groups through violence (e.g. Chinese nationalists and former GMD members in the early 1950s and Cuban anti-communist and pro-USA individuals in the early 1960s).
- Governments in both states expressed openness to listening to the disagreements of the opposition, but dissent was met with censorship and persecution (Hundred Flowers Campaign in 1956 and the Anti-Rights Movement in 1957 in China and the relative freedom of the press and expression in Cuba).

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- While the Chinese authoritarian state used deliberate and systematic violence to hinder any opposition (e.g. the Great Leap Forward 1958-1961), the Cuban authoritarian state engaged in specific targeted attacks against and imprisonment of opposition groups more gradually in the early 1960s.
- The Chinese state created the Red Guards as a parallel force to violently attack individuals from the opposition, whereas the Cuban state usually used locally-based organisations such as the Committees for the Defense of the Revolution to both provide social services in the communities and inform of anti-revolutionary actions.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both Chinese and Cuban authoritarian states hindered any political opposition in formal spaces through the ban of elections and political parties ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). The Chinese government established the Chinese Communist Party as the leading partisan institution across the country in the early 1950s by creating distinct regional units in cities and the countryside. Similarly, the Cuban state formalised a single-party system under the Cuban Communist Party in the Constitution of 1976 ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Moreover, both states used violence against opposition groups that challenged the Chinese and Cuban regimes' authorities, particularly shortly after Mao and Castro rose to power ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). For instance, nationalists and former GMD members were executed or sent to labour camps in the early 1950s, and pro-Batista and anti-communist Cubans were executed and exiled in the early 1960s ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, the treatment of opposition also differed in the Chinese and Cuban authoritarian states regarding the extent of the use of force ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). While the Chinese state created the Red Guards as a parallel force mainly comprised of students to systematically attack right-aligned businesspeople and intellectuals, the Cuban state established the Committees for the Defense of the Revolution as a hybrid organisation responsible for engaging in counterintelligence activities and providing social services in communities ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
