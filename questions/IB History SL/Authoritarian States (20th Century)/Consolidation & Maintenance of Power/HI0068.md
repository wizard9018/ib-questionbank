# HI0068

Compare and contrast how propaganda was used by Hitler and Mao Zedong to maintain their authority.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Award up to **four** marks for a response that includes comparisons and contrasts between the use of propaganda by Hitler and Mao Zedong. Students should include at least one similarity and one difference to achieve full marks. **One** mark for each accurate similarity/difference.

For similarities:

- Both Hitler and Mao utilised propaganda to create a cult of personality, portraying themselves as infallible leaders. Hitler was depicted as the savior of Germany through films, speeches, and posters, while Mao was presented as the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Great Helmsman” whose thoughts, such as those in the Little Red Book, were treated as guiding principles.

- Both regimes exerted strict control over the media to spread their messages. Hitler used state-controlled media like radio broadcasts and the press to propagate Nazi ideology, while Mao's government tightly controlled newspapers and broadcast media to ensure that only Communist messages were heard.

- Both leaders focused their propaganda efforts on youth to ensure long-term loyalty. Hitler targeted young people through the Hitler Youth organisation, using it to instill Nazi ideology from a young age. Similarly, Mao used the Red Guards, made up largely of students, to spread his messages during the Cultural Revolution and reinforce his ideology among the youth.

For Differences

- Hitler's propaganda often emphasised themes of Aryan superiority, anti-Semitism, and the need for living space (Lebensraum), targeting specific external and internal enemies like Jews and Communists. In contrast, Mao's propaganda focused more on class struggle, socialist ideals, and mobilising the masses for internal campaigns like the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution.

- Hitler's propaganda relied heavily on modern mass media, such as cinema and large-scale rallies (e.g., Nuremberg rallies), which helped create a highly orchestrated image of Nazi power. Mao, while also using mass rallies, heavily utilised grassroots methods like distributing the Little Red Book to spread his ideology among peasants and students, ensuring that his thoughts reached all levels of society.

- Hitler's propaganda often centred on grand public spectacles like mass rallies at Nuremberg, designed to showcase the power and unity of the Nazi regime. These events were highly choreographed and meant to project strength. Mao, on the other hand, emphasised mass participation, encouraging the public to engage directly with his propaganda through campaigns like $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Speak Bitterness” sessions and distributing the Little Red Book, which aimed to engage people at a grassroots level.

- Hitler's propaganda largely remained consistent throughout his time in power, focusing on the central themes of racial purity, nationalism, and anti-Semitism. Mao's propaganda, however, adapted to different political needs, such as shifting from promoting the success of the Communist Party after 1949 to attacking counter-revolutionaries during the Cultural Revolution, which required a shift in messaging to suit changing campaigns.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Both Hitler and Mao used propaganda as a means to foster a cult of personality, portraying themselves as indispensable and almost divine figures ($\checkmark$ similarity). Each leader maintained tight control over the media landscape in their respective countries—Hitler manipulated radio broadcasts and the press to spread Nazi ideology, while Mao controlled newspapers and radio to ensure a consistent Communist message ($\checkmark$ similarity). Despite these similarities, the focus of their propaganda efforts differed: Hitler's messaging concentrated on racial superiority and the demonization of Jews, while Mao prioritised themes of class struggle and the promotion of socialist ideals ($\checkmark$ difference). Moreover, while Hitler often used dramatic, large-scale public events like the Nuremberg rallies to display the unity and power of the Nazi state, Mao relied on more direct engagement with the population, such as the distribution of the Little Red Book and encouraging public participation in revolutionary activities ($\checkmark$ difference).
