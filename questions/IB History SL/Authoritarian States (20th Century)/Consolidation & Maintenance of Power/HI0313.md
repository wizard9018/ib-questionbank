# HI0313

Identify **two** major reforms implemented by Fidel Castro between 1959 and 1962 and explain how they contributed to the consolidation of his power.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following reforms each with its supporting explanation/connection to Castro's consolidation of power:
- Agrarian Reform
    - The 1959 Agrarian Reform Law redistributed land from large landowners and foreign companies to peasants and state-run cooperatives.
    - The Second Agrarian Reform Law (1963) further limited private land ownership to a maximum of 67 hectares and nationalized the remaining large estates.
- Rent and Wage Reforms
    - Implemented wage increases for urban and rural workers, improving their purchasing power.
    - Rent reductions significantly lowered housing costs, making homes more affordable for the lower classes.
    - State control over wages and prices aimed to prevent inflation and ensure equitable access to basic goods.
    - Gained massive support from the working class, weakened landlords, and the bourgeoisie, who lost income from rent and economic control
- Social Reforms
    - The 1961 Literacy Campaign reduced illiteracy from 23% to 4%. Schools were established in rural areas, and free education was expanded at all levels.
    - A universal healthcare system was introduced, providing free medical care to all citizens.
- Other economic reforms
    - The Cuban government nationalized banks, foreign companies (including U.S.-owned firms), oil refineries, and electricity companies. Eliminated U.S. economic influence and weakened the Cuban bourgeoisie, dismantling opposition
    - The market economy was replaced with a state-controlled, centrally planned economy. Reinforced Cuban independence and strengthened Castro's anti-imperialist rhetoric, boosting public support
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One major reform implemented by Fidel Castro between 1959 and 1962 was Agrarian Reform. The 1959 Agrarian Reform Law redistributed land from wealthy landowners and foreign companies to peasants and cooperatives, while the Second Agrarian Reform Law (1963) further restricted private land ownership to 67 hectares, nationalizing larger estates ($\checkmark$). This reform weakened the landowning elite and U.S. economic influence while securing the support of rural workers who benefited from land redistribution. Additionally, by centralizing agricultural production under state control, Castro strengthened his authority over the economy, reducing opposition from private landowners ($\checkmark$).

Another significant reform was education and healthcare reform. The 1961 Literacy Campaign dramatically reduced illiteracy rates, expanding access to education, especially in rural areas. At the same time, universal healthcare was introduced, ensuring free medical services for all Cubans ($\checkmark$). These reforms fostered widespread support from marginalized sectors, as they improved living conditions and reinforced Castro's image as a leader committed to social equity. Moreover, education became a tool for ideological indoctrination, promoting revolutionary ideals and discouraging dissent, which further secured his grip on power ($\checkmark$).
