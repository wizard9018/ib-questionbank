# HI1155

What does source C suggest about the US perspective on its assets in Cuba in 1960?
[2 marks]

$\\$

|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Cartoon by Jim Berryman published November 3, 1960 titled $\text{\textquotedblleft}$In English or Spanish, It Means What It Says." |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- The source shows that the US is determined to protect its strategic position in Cuba - a clear message in both the caption and the sign on the ship to keep its hands off US property.
- The cartoon highlights the significance of this area as a strategic location, marked by the label $\text{\textquotedblleft}$GUANTANAMO" directly beneath the ship in the water.
- The cartoon also highlights the military strength of the US during this period of increasing tensions. This is evident in the size of the US ship and the fact that Castro appears frustrated when confronted with the instructions on the ship.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source C is showing that the US is determined to protect its strategic interests in Cuba. This is seen in the cartoon by the clear message in both English and Spanish of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Hands off” and supported by the caption which says $\text{\textquotedblleft}$In English or Spanish, It Means What It Says" ($\checkmark$).

The cartoon also highlights the military strength of the US during this period of increasing tensions. This is shown by the size of the US ship and the fact that Castro looks frustrated when confronted with the instructions on the ship ($\checkmark$).
