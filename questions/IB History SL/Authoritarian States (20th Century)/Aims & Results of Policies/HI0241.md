# HI0241

Discuss the successes and failures of Stalin's first 5-year plan.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For **successes**, any of the following:

 - Collectivisation allowed the USSR to have more centralised control over agricultural resources and land.

 - Due to collectivisation, the USSR launched the dekulakization to eradicate the kulaks, which were considered a threat.

 - The Gosplan was able to mobilise more resources to industrialize the USSR's economy.

 - The USSR was able to transform itself from an agrarian economy to industrial economy. 

The 1st 5-year plan laid the foundation for the future 5-year plans.

For **failures**, any of the following:

 - The 1st 5-year plan had drawbacks in poor planning. 

 - Due to a strong emphasis on heavy industries, little consumer goods were produced. 

 - The rural society was disrupted since the kulaks were eliminated as a class enemy and peasants were forced to work in state collective farms in which they could not produce food for themselves. 

 - The collective farms were poorly managed and were inefficient. Peasants had little incentive to produce food since the food supplies would be immediately given to the state.

\

[4 marks]

For concluding remarks:

 - Responses should consider both successes and failures, but this need not be balanced between the two.

 - The best responses will have a conclusion that synthesises all the main arguments and present a concluding which provides a justification on whether the 5-year plans were mostly a success or failure. 

\

[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The 1st 5-year plans of the USSR were successful to a large extent in radically transforming the USSR into a major industrial power. 

During the 1st 5-year plan, the USSR greatly increased its industrial production capacity in iron and steel, most notably in Magitorosk ($\checkmark$), which became the USSR's center for steel production. The development of industrial cities also helped expand the Soviet Union's capacity in heavy industry. For example, the USSR was able to produce up to 4.8 million tonnes of steel in 1928 ($\checkmark$).

Despite the successes of the 1st 5-year plan, there were also some drawbacks. The industrialisation of the USSR was made possible by the use of forced labor that was made available from the countryside after the USSR forced rural households into collective farms ($\checkmark$). Moreover, the result of rapid transition to heavy industry had negative effects on the countryside. Around 5 million people in the countryside died as a result of the dekulakization campaigns. Agricultural production feel since the peasants would destroy their own livestock rather than having them be sent to collective farms ($\checkmark$).

Overall, though there some minor setbacks in the 1st 5-year plans, the 1st 5-year plans were mostly successful in transforming the USSR's economy ($\checkmark$).
