# HI0055

Evaluate the extent to which the Catholic Church in Italy was a collaborator in the fascist government of Mussolini.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

The response must include both points of view between collaborator and not collaborator.  One point is awarded for evaluation and a maximum of **five** points for ‘evidence’ presented.  There is to be a mix of any of the pro and contra points.

Any combination of the following as evidence:\

Collaborator:

- By signing the Lateran Treaty, the Church endorsed Mussolini’s government and gave some people more confidence in the regime.

- By signing the Lateran Treaty, the Church accepted money from the fascist regime.

- By signing the Lateran Treaty, the Church accepted the position of being the state Church in a fascist regime and thereby had a large extent on carrying out fascists policies.

- The church reinforced the ‘moral’ order that Mussolini wanted to carry out - particularly in matters where the Church and the fascist state were in agreement (Battle for Births, for example).

- The fascist government put the schools in the hands of the Church which ensured that the students’ education was aligned with both the Church’s values and the state’s values.

Not a collaborator:

- Ideological differences, Mussolini’s fascist goal of totalitarianism is not the same as ‘spiritual authority’. The Church collaborated with the government when it was in its own interests (see above) but remained independent when Mussolini’s aims did not align with Church values.

- Disagreements over Youth policies - in 1931 the Church criticised the government’s attempts to monopolise Italian youth and limit the Church’s role in education. Therefore, the Church’s willingness to stand up to Mussolini when its autonomy was threatened, was demonstrated.

- The Church opposed Mussolini’s anti-Semitic Laws (1938). 

\

[5 marks]

Any evaluation statement that aligns with the evidence provided:

- Students may argue that the Church collaborated when it suited them to do so. 

- The strongest answers will argue a combination of the Church collaborating when Mussolini’s aims aligned with the Church’s but also recognise that the Church was capable of actively opposing the regime when contradicted their aims. 

\

[1 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

To a large extent, the Catholic Church collaborated with Mussolini’s fascist regime ($\checkmark$). When the Church signed the Lateran Treaty, they showed support for the government and this impacted the religious population in Italy as they saw it as a show of support for Mussolini ($\checkmark$). This had a positive effect for him as people were more supportive of the fascist government.  Additionally, the Church also received money from the government which made them complicit in the fascist government ($\checkmark$). The Church also benefited from being put in charge of the schools which meant that the State and the Church’s values became one and the same ($\checkmark$). The Church did object to the state trying to force youth movements on the children as they were seen as competition with Church organised youth groups and many people remained in the Church groups ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, the Church was opposed to Mussolini’s anti-Semitism laws which came in 1938 ($\checkmark$). Therefore it can be argued that the Church collaborated when the fascist regime’s goals aligned with the Church's but was capable of opposing the regime when it contradicted the Church’s aims ($\checkmark$ evaluation) .
