# HI0271

Outline the main features of Mao's $`$Great Leap Forward' in 1958. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any combination of **four** of the following:

- The Great Leap Forward was the mass mobilisation of society launched in 1958 and aimed at making China a world superpower that would challenge the USA and overtake Britain in fifteen years. 

- In urban areas there was an increase in State-Owned Enterprises while in the countryside, the Agricultural Producers' Cooperatives (APCs)  were amalgamated into Communes. 

- Each Commune consisted of 40-100 villages. There were approximately 53000 communes in China. Private farming was to cease and all aspects of production were now centrally directed by the Communist Party. 

- Each commune would also contain factories to provide work for women and children. 

- Communes were self-contained units that had nurseries for young children, old people's homes, health clinics, communal canteens and unisex dormitories for the inhabitants of the commune, who were encouraged to abandon personal possessions. 

- Rural peasants were now also expected to contribute to industrial growth and the production of steel and were encouraged to build and operate brick furnaces in their backyards to smelt crude steel. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Great Leap Forward was the mass mobilisation of society launched in 1958 and aimed at making China a world superpower that would challenge the USA and overtake Britain in fifteen years ($\checkmark$). In urban areas, there was an increase in State-Owned Enterprises, while in the countryside, the Agricultural Producers' Cooperatives (APCs)  were amalgamated into Communes ($\checkmark$). As a result, private farming was to cease, and all aspects of production were now centrally directed by the Communist Party ($\checkmark$). Communes were self-contained units with nurseries for young children, old people's homes, health clinics, communal canteens and unisex dormitories for the inhabitants ($\checkmark$). One final feature of the Great Leap Forward was that rural peasants were also expected to contribute to industrial growth and steel production, including building and operating brick furnaces in their backyards to smelt crude steel ($\checkmark$).
