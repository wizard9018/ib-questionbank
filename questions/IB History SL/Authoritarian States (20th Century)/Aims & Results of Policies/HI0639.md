$\\$
For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Both Communist China and Nazi Germany saw women as crucial to their national vision.
    - Source A: Women were needed in the workforce to advance socialism and industrialization.
    - Source B: Women were expected to focus on homemaking, which was seen as the foundation of the state.
- Both regimes controlled and dictated women's roles to align with state objectives.
    - Source A: Women were expected to work and contribute equally to the economy, with policies enforcing their participation.
    - Source B: Women were encouraged to focus on motherhood and household duties through propaganda and policy.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Equal vs. Complementary.
    - Source A: Advocated for full gender equality, promoting women as equal workers and contributors.
    - Source B: Defined men and women as having separate but complementary roles, with men as workers and women as caretakers.
- Workforce vs. Domesticity.
    - Source A: Women's liberation was tied to their participation in production and economic growth.
    - Source B: Women were primarily responsible for maintaining the home and raising children to support the state.
- Progressive vs. Traditional View of Women's Role.
    - Source A: Encouraged the transformation of gender roles through socialist policies.
    - Source B: Reinforced traditional values, discouraging social change in gender roles.

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources emphasize the role of women in nation-building ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A states that it is “of the utmost importance to arouse the broad masses of women to join in productive activity,” indicating that women's active participation in economic production was essential to the development of a socialist society ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Source B similarly presents women as essential to the nation's success, claiming that “the greater world is built on the foundation of this smaller world,” suggesting that women's domestic role was vital for national stability ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). In both cases, the state defines the ideal role of women as contributing to the broader aims of the regime, whether through labor or family life ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).

However, the sources contrast significantly in how they define women's roles. Source A emphasizes equality in public life and labor, asserting that “men and women must receive equal pay for equal work in production” and that “genuine equality between the sexes can only be realized” through socialist transformation ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). This reveals Maoist China’s ideological commitment to gender equality in the public and economic spheres. In contrast, Source B reinforces traditional gender roles, asserting that “her world is her husband, her family, her children, and her home” ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Rather than advocating equality, it describes gender roles are seen as different but complementary ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$).
