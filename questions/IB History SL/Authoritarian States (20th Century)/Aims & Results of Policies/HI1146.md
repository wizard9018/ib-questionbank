# HI1146

Explain the significance of Castro's cultural policies for authoritarian control.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Cultural change had to happen alongside social and economic change to build his idea of a new society.
- The 1961 speech "Words to the Intellectuals" set the tone for cultural policy, supporting artistic freedom within revolutionary boundaries. Saw culture as integral to national identity, education and social development
- Access for all - Castro supported the arts through education and trained art teachers nationwide to promote interest and participation in the arts.
- Founded new institutions (1959) to support the arts - the Cuban Institute of Cinematographic Art and Industry (ICAIC), the National Theater, National Symphony Orchestra, and Casa de las Américas to promote cultural unity and cooperation among Latin American and Caribbean countries.
- Rejected Soviet-style Socialist Realism censorship in favour of a more open, diverse artistic environment.
- Castro also maintained control over cultural expression and did not allow it to become a platform for political dissent.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
For Castro to achieve his ideals of a new society, the role of culture was a crucial factor; he believed that cultural change had to occur alongside social and economic change for his vision to be realized ($\checkmark$). He was keen to support the arts and founded new institutions in 1959 to support them. He established the Cuban Institute of Cinematographic Art and Industry (ICAIC), the National Theater, National Symphony Orchestra, and Casa de las Américas to promote cultural unity and cooperation among Latin American and Caribbean countries ($\checkmark$). Castro was aware of how the arts could both promote and undermine his rule, and so maintained careful control over cultural expression to prevent it from becoming a platform for political dissent ($\checkmark$).
