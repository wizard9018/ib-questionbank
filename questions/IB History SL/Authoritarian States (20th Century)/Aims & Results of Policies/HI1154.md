$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Both sources A and B focus on the role that sugar production played on Cuba's economy ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source A suggests that the US, by $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the cutting of the sugar quota”, was trying to use its economic power to control Cuba. Source B shows the importance of sugar by stating that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$investments in sugar mills gave U.S. companies control of 60 per cent of Cuban sugar production by the 1920s” and were crucial to the US economic position in Cuba ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

However, the two sources differ in their perspectives regarding the impact of the relationship between the US and Cuba ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source A states that this relationship was negative and accelerated the revolution and describes the US as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the enemy of the peoples" and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$an ally of military, reactionary organizations." Source B states that the relationship is positive and modernised Cuba, saying that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$the building by a U.S. company of a railway along the length of the island was an achievement from which Cuba will always benefit" ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
