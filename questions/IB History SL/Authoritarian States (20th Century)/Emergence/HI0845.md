# HI0845

Discuss the significance of Mussolini’s March on Rome (October 1922) in his rise to power.
[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two to four** of the following for significance with supporting details and analysis:
- Demonstration of strength and intimidation tactics
    - The march showcased Mussolini's ability to mobilize large numbers of supporters, particularly among the military.
    - The march displayed the growing power and organization of the Fascist movement and created an atmosphere of fear and instability that Mussolini exploited.
- Political maneuvering and the catalyst for a legal transfer of power
    - The march led King Victor Emmanuel III to invite Mussolini to form a government, marking the transition from a democratic system to Fascist rule
    - Mussolini used the march as leverage to demand political power, and the threat of violence and potential civil war pressured the government to capitulate, helping him to position himself as the only figure capable of restoring order
- Symbolic importance
    - The march became a powerful propaganda tool for the Fascist regime, and it was mythologized as a heroic revolution in Fascist narratives
- International impact
    - The march attracted attention worldwide, raising Mussolini's profile as well as inspiring similar tactics by other far-right movements in Europe
- Turning point
    - The event marked the end of the liberal parliamentary era in Italy, and it set the stage for Mussolini's consolidation of power in the following years
\
[4 marks]

Concluding sentence:
- Should provide a clear summary judgment that reflects the overall significance of the March on Rome, linking the arguments clearly to Mussolini's rise to power.
\

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Mussolini's March on Rome was significant for several reasons.  Firstly, it showed the world and Italy how much support he had, particularly from the 30,000 militant participants in the march ($\checkmark$). The pressure this show of support created also increased Mussolini's political power to the point that it forced the King to form a government to avoid the threat of civil war ($\checkmark$).

Internationally, the march drew great attention and gave Mussolini more credibility in the eyes of Italians and the world ($\checkmark$). Despite the relatively small number of actual participants, the march would be used as a symbol of Mussolini's power and as a tool for Fascist propaganda during his rule ($\checkmark$).

Overall, the march seemed relatively small at the time, with some in Italy still thinking Mussolini could be controlled. However, in the end, it marked the end of Italy's parliamentary power and signalled a turning point towards a Fascist, authoritarian regime ($\checkmark$).
