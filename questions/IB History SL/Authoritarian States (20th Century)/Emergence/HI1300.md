# HI1300

Explain how the Long March contributed to Mao’s emergence as an authoritarian leader in China.

[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Any of the following:

- Allowed Mao to outmaneuver rival factions inside the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) after early defeats.

- Strategic proposals like the use of guerrilla tactics proved effective and allowed him to gain greater control of decision-making.

- Helped establish Mao’s cult of personality and elevated his image above other prominent figures in the CCP. 

- Propaganda, including poetry, songs, posters and later films, about the march was used extensively both before his full control in China and throughout his time as leader.

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

The Long March contributed to Mao’s emergence by enabling him to outmaneuver rival factions within the Chinese Communist Party. As other leaders were discredited by early failures, Mao’s strategic ideas, especially his emphasis on guerrilla tactics, proved more effective, allowing him to take control of key decisions ($\checkmark$). In addition, the Long March became central to the creation of Mao’s cult of personality. Party propaganda, through stories, songs, and later films, portrayed him as the inspirational figure who had guided the Communists to survival, helping solidify his authority as an unquestioned leader. ($\checkmark$)
