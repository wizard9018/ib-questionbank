# HI0285

Explain **two** ways the influence of landowners (*estancieros*) and business owners contributed to the request for Perón's resignation in 1945.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following ways with supporting explanation:

 - Opposition from Landowners (*Estancieros*)

     - Perón's Estatuto del Peón introduced better working conditions for rural laborers, which angered landowners as they viewed these reforms as expensive and a threat to their control over the workforce. This economic burden and perceived erosion of their authority led to their opposition against Perón.

 - Resistance from Business Owners and Professionals

     - Business owners and professionals opposed the Ley de Asociación de Profesionales, which allowed workers to form unions and strengthened Perón's influence over the labor force. They saw this as a move that undermined their economic interests and increased Perón's political power, prompting them to join the anti-Peronist movement.

 - Collective Protest (March of September 1945)

     - The anger of landowners, business owners, and the middle class culminated in the March of September 1945 with a large-scale demonstration against Perón's policies. This collective pressure, along with public protests, forced the government to request Perón's resignation on October 9, 1945, showcasing the united strength of his opposition.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The reactions to Perón's reforms caused strong opposition from landowners and business owners. Landowners were upset by the Estatuto del Peón, which improved rural workers' conditions, as they saw it as too costly and a loss of control over labor ($\checkmark$). At the same time, business owners and professionals opposed the Ley de Asociación de Profesionales because it allowed workers to form unions, which they believed gave Perón too much power and hurt their interests ($\checkmark$).

These frustrated groups - landowners, business owners, and the middle class - came together and organized the March of September 1945, demanding Perón's removal ($\checkmark$). The protests created so much pressure that the government forced Perón to resign on October 9, 1945 ($\checkmark$).
