# HI0843

Explain the impact of **two** weaknesses of recently unified Italy (1870) on Mussolini's rise to power.
[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following weaknesses:
- Regional economic differences, the wealthy north and the poorer south
- Weak, liberal democracy run by elites
- Lack of a national identity as $\lq$Italians'.
- The new state caused conflict between the government and the Church
- Centralization and elite rule during unification set a precedent for top-down governance
- Lack of modernisation
\
[2 marks]

Any **two** of the following explanations connected to the relevant weakness:
- The North-South divide made national unity fragile and allowed Mussolini to present Fascism as a unifying force
- The alienation of the South (due to historical neglect and repression) made parts of the population more receptive to strong leadership promising order and national pride.
- Mussolini criticized the failures of parliamentary democracy and promised a new, stronger system.
- Mussolini was able to use the resentment people felt towards the elites to gain support from the people.
- A lack of national identity allowed Mussolini the opportunity to create one based on fascism and nationalism, Roman symbolism, and militaristic pride
- The people were used to top-down governance, so Mussolini's increasing fascist control went unchallenged
- Since unification, ongoing tensions between Church and State had alienated devout Catholics from politics.
Mussolini's regime promised modernization, land reform, and employment, especially appealing to those neglected by the earlier liberal state.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Mussolini was able to exploit several of Italy's weaknesses.  Italy was unified in 1870, and there was a lack of national identity  ($\checkmark$).  As a result, Mussolini was able to help create an identity based on fascist nationalism and pride in Italy's Roman past ($\checkmark$).  Furthermore, the government was weak and run by a few elites ($\checkmark$). Mussolini exploited this by harnessing the people's distrust and dislike for the elite government, which, from their perspective, had failed them ($\checkmark$).
