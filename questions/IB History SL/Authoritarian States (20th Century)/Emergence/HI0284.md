# HI0284

Explain **two** reasons middle-class Argentines grew dissatisfied with Perón's government measures and how this contributed to rising tensions in Argentina.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following reasons with supporting explanation:

- Dissatisfaction with a military government and Perón's growing influence:

     - Many middle-class groups, including political parties, were frustrated with the military government and Perón's growing influence because they felt excluded from decision-making processes under military rule. They demanded a return to democracy.

 - Reintroduction of religious education in state schools:

     - Teachers were angered by the reintroduction of religious education in state schools, as it was seen as a step backward for secular education, creating tension within the education sector.

 - State intervention in universities:

     - Students and professionals opposed state intervention in universities, undermining academic freedom and autonomy. This intervention alienated the middle sectors and fueled their dissatisfaction with Perón's government policies.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One reason for the dissatisfaction of the middle sectors with Perón's government was the political frustration caused by the continuation of military rule ($\checkmark$). Many political parties, such as the Radical Civic Union (UCR), demanded a return to democracy after years of military control and were angered by Perón's growing influence over government decisions, especially through his roles as Minister of War and Secretary of Labor ($\checkmark$).

Another reason was the state intervention in universities, which limited academic freedom and autonomy ($\checkmark$). For example, Perón's government placed university administrators under state control, replacing independent leaders with government-aligned figures. Students and professionals opposed these actions, seeing them as a threat to intellectual independence. These factors deepened the resentment of the middle sectors and heightened tensions with Perón's government. ($\checkmark$)
