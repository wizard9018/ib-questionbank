# HI0098

Compare and contrast the factors that helped Mussolini and Hitler rise to power.

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For comparison, any **two to four** of the following similarities:

- Both dictators capitalized on the anger generated by the Paris Peace Treaties. 

- Both dictators focused their propaganda on nationalist principles that aimed to strengthen their countries. 

- Both dictators were aided by the economic unrest facing their countries- Hitler by the Great Depression and Mussolini by the economic impact of the First World War and the industrial unrest of the *biennio rosso* years. 

- Both leaders emphasized their anti-communist viewpoints in propaganda and violent actions against communist groups. 

For contrast, any **two to four** of the following differences:

- While Mussolini used the threat of a forced take over of government with his Blackshirts' use of violence and the attempted coup with the March on Rome, Hitler sought to contest elections to help the Nazi party gain power.

- Hitler used propaganda against particular races as evident in his anti-semetic rhetoric. This tactic was not resorted to by Mussolini who instead focused  on an unifying theme, emphasizing the greatness of the Roman Empire. 

- Mussolini sought the support of the Catholic Church in his rise to power by removing elements hostile to the church from his group and dropping anti-clerical aspects of the fascist programme, unlike Hitler who did not feel the need to seek  support from the church. 

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

When comparing factors that helped Mussolini and Hitler rise to power, both leaders benefited from the anger felt by the citizens of Italy and Germany against the governments that signed the Paris Peace Treaties ($\checkmark$ similarity). The Italians were disappointed at the rejection of their demand for the port of Fiume and the Dalmatian coast, while German anger was directed at the terms of the Treaty of Versailles that deprived them of land and resources together with \$33 billion reparations  imposed for war damages ($\checkmark$ evidence). Both Mussolini and Hitler capitalized on the economic suffering of their people and promised them a better economic future ($\checkmark$ similarity). Mussolini included measures to deal with Italian inflation and unemployment resulting from World War I in his fascist programme, while Hiter's promise of a strong and prosperous Germany ensured an increase in popular support for the Nazis following the economic disaster of the Great Depression ($\checkmark$ evidence). Both dictators gained support of the middle and industrial classes as a result of their strong anti-communist rhetoric ($\checkmark$ similarity). The Blackshirts and the SA targeted communists in Italy and Germany with violent attacks that served to weaken the existing democratic governments and help their rise to power ($\checkmark$ evidence).

There were also several contrasting factors that contributed to the rise to power of both dictators. Mussolini relied on the use of force with the attacks by the Blackshirts on communists and organizing the March on Rome with the aim of putting pressure on the government. He was then able to take advantage of the resulting chaos in government to position himself for the post of prime minister on the invitation of the King. However in contrast Hitler was made Chancellor not due to the threat of Nazi violence but because of the gains made by the Nazi party in the elections of July and November 1932 ($\checkmark$ difference and evidence). Another contrast in the rise to power of the two dictators was their attitude with regard to Jews ($\checkmark$ difference). Hitler's anti-semitism was a bedrock of his ideology and contributed to garnering support in his rise to power while Mussolini did not use anti-semitism to help him gather support ($\checkmark$ evidence). Lastly, both leaders differed in their approach to the Catholic Church ($\checkmark$ difference). While Mussolini dropped the anti-clerical stance he had initially adopted from his fascist programme in order to ensure support from the Catholic church to help his rise to power, Hitler did not modify his programme to gain support from the Church ($\checkmark$ evidence).
