# HI1299

Explain two factors that influenced Mao’s emergence as an authoritarian leader in China.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$: \

Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation: 

- Economic problems

    - Hyperinflation, corruption, and mismanagement under the Nationalist government destroyed savings and trust, pushing public opinion toward the Communist Party. Mao’s early land reform policies in the CCP-controlled areas helped legitimize him as a leader and earn trust.

- Conflict

    - The Second Sino-Japanese War allowed the CCP to expand its influence and gain military experience while simultaneously weakening the Nationalist army and government.

- Warlordism/political instability

    - Warlordism in the period preceding Mao’s control gave the central government limited authority and made alternative governance appealing in the eyes of the public.

- Persuasion and coercion

    - Mao’s Yan’an Rectification Movement pushed for ideological uniformity and Mao’s charismatic speeches gained him personal fame and loyalty, helping establish his cult of personality. Mao also used purges, such as the Futian Incident, to help remove dissenters and rivals.

- Mao’s leadership

    - Mao’s leadership through the Long March (1934-1935) helped cement him as a heroic and resilient leader. His use of guerrilla tactics to outmaneuver the GMT also allowed the CCP to survive and expand.

- Use of force

    - Red Army actions to defeat the GMT during the civil war, as well as his use of party purges and executions.

- Propaganda

    - Mao used propaganda to establish his culty of personality both before his complete control and after. Control of party newspapers, education and art in Yan’an helped promote his achievements.

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$: \

A critical factor behind Mao’s rise was his use of conflict and violence, both military and political, in China ($\checkmark$). Both the war against Japan (1937–45) and the subsequent Civil War created conditions that allowed the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) to expand its influence. During the Japanese invasion, the Nationalist government bore the bulk of the fighting, suffered major losses, and saw its authority erode. At the same time, the CCP utilised the war years to consolidate its base areas, recruit followers, and present itself as a patriotic and disciplined force that could protect ordinary citizens. ($\checkmark$)

A second significant factor was the weakness of the existing political system, which struggled to provide effective national governance ($\checkmark$). The Nationalist regime faced chronic corruption, factional disputes, and limited control outside major cities. Many rural regions remained under the influence of local warlords, undermining the authority of the central government. This lack of political cohesion made it easier for the CCP to offer itself as a credible alternative ($\checkmark$).
