## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For full marks, both similarities and differences must be addressed.

For comparison, any **one to three** of the following similarities:

 - Both sources recognize the significant role of workers in the events of October 17, 1945.

 - Both portray the demonstration as an important, large-scale event that marked a key moment in Argentina's history.

 - Both acknowledge Perón as the central figure directly tied to the workers' mobilization and its impact on Argentine politics.

For contrast, any **two to four** of the following differences:

 - The cartoon depicts the workers as being deceived by leaders like Borlenghi, suggesting they lacked awareness, while Perón praises their loyalty and political consciousness.

 - The cartoon describes the event as chaotic and staged to manipulate people and using  phrases like $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mislead a few honest workers" mock the legitimacy of the demonstrations, suggesting that Perón and his allies exploited inexperienced workers for their own gain. Perón's speech portrays the events as a profound and genuine moment of national pride. Words like $\text{\textquotedblleft}$rebirth," $\text{\textquotedblleft}$workers' consciousness," and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$great and immortal Nation" emphasize a vision of unity and progress led by the working class. 

 - The cartoon portrays Perón as a dictator holding a sword, linking him to authoritarianism. Perón presents himself as a humble leader serving the workers.

 - The cartoon's language, as indicated in the caption, is critical and disparaging, using terms like $\text{\textquotedblleft}$thugs,” $\text{\textquotedblleft}$criminals,” and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mislead” to portray the event as chaotic and reflects skepticism toward Perón and his policies. While Perón Perón's speech uses positive and inclusive language to celebrate the event as a demonstration of genuine worker empowerment. Using words such as $\text{\textquotedblleft}$rebirth," $\text{\textquotedblleft}$workers' consciousness," and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$great and immortal Nation" emphasize a vision of unity and progress led by the working class. The speech's tone seeks to reinforce loyalty and trust in his leadership, presenting the demonstrations as a legitimate and organic expression of popular will.

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Both the cartoon and Perón's speech agree on the central role of workers as key participants in the events at the Plaza de Mayo in October of 1945 ($\checkmark$ similarity), though their interpretation of that role differs ($\checkmark$ difference). 

In Source B, Perón refers to the event as the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$rebirth of workers' consciousness," highlighting it as a spontaneous and historic movement that can $\text{\textquotedblleft}$make the Nation great and immortal" ($\checkmark$ evidence). He uses emotive language, expressing personal pride by stating his desire to "embrace all of them deeply against my heart, as I would embrace my mother" ($\checkmark$ evidence), portraying the workers, as honorable and central to Argentina's future. In contrast, the cartoon frames the workers as manipulated by leaders like Borlenghi, describing them as a "cast of thugs and criminals" and "a few honest workers lacking experience and political insight" ($\checkmark$ evidence), emphasizing deceit and coercion ($\checkmark$ difference).  Furthermore, the cartoon describes the event as disorderly and orchestrated for political manipulation, ($\checkmark$ difference) as reflected in the imagery of a fishing rod used to reel in workers ($\checkmark$ evidence). Perón, by contrast, presents it as a unifying and spontaneous demonstration of loyalty and trust ($\checkmark$ difference), emphasizing that the people $\text{\textquotedblleft}$do not deceive those who do not betray them” ($\checkmark$ evidence).
