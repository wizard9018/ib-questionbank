# HI1472

$\text{\textquotedblleft}$The impact of war was the most important condition in the emergence of authoritarian states." Discuss with reference to two states, each chosen from a different region.[15 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

 

**General Guidance:**

- Answers should frame $\text{\textquotedblleft}$impact of war” as a condition for emergence (destruction of existing institutions, militarization of politics, social polarization, etc.) and will likely explore $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mergence” as a process rather than a single event (breakdown of previous systems and the construction of new authoritarian structures)

- The best answers will compare two states from different regions in an integrated way (how war shaped each case differently) and evaluate relative importance by contrasting war with other conditions (economic crisis, ideological factors, political culture, etc.) to reach a balanced judgment (war may be decisive in some cases, but only contributory in others).

**Possible suitable examples include:**

- Spain, Franco, and the Spanish Civil War (Europe)

    - The Spanish Civil War (1936-1939) destroyed the democratic Second Republic and created a power vacuum that enabled Franco's rise.

    - The war allowed Franco to eliminate political rivals, unify Nationalist factions, and establish himself as the sole military and political leader.

    - The conflict generated extreme polarization, legitimizing authoritarianism as a means to restore $\text{\textquotedblleft}$order” and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$national unity.”

    - Military victory provided Franco with ideological legitimacy, framing his rule as the outcome of a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$crusade” against communism and regional separatism.

    - The postwar reconstruction of the state was entirely shaped by wartime dynamics, enabling centralized control, repression, and the institutionalization of authoritarian rule. 

    - Impact: In Spain, war was the most important condition for the emergence of Franco's authoritarian state.

- China, Mao, and the Chinese Civil War (Asia)

    - Mao's emergence was closely tied to prolonged conflict, including the Second Sino-Japanese War and the Chinese Civil War.

    - The war against Japan weakened the Nationalist government, eroded its legitimacy, and allowed the CCP to expand its territorial control and popular support.

    - The CCP used wartime conditions to build parallel institutions, mobilize peasants, and present itself as a disciplined alternative to the corruption of the Nationalists.

    - The civil war (1946–1949) provided Mao with opportunities to consolidate leadership within the CCP and demonstrate military effectiveness.

    - Victory in the civil war enabled the creation of a new political order centered on Mao's authority and justified through revolutionary struggle. 

    - Impact: In China, war was a central condition for Mao's emergence, although ideology and social mobilization also played significant roles.

- Cuba, Castro, and the Cuban Revolutionary War (Americas)

    - The failure of Batista's military responses during the armed struggle weakened state authority and increased support for the revolutionary movement.

    - The experience of guerrilla warfare allowed Castro to build organizational cohesion and present the 26 July Movement as a legitimate alternative to the collapsing regime.

    - The victory in 1959 created a power vacuum that enabled the rapid centralization of authority and the construction of new authoritarian structures.

[15 marks]

 

$\\$

 

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

 

The Spanish Civil War and Chinese Civil War provide contrasting opportunities to explore the role of war in the emergence of authoritarian rule ($\checkmark$ focus). For Franco's emergence in Spain, war played a decisive role, whereas in China, the civil war was transformative, though not exclusive. The argument, therefore, demands an analysis of both the destructive and constructive effects of war, as well as the extent to which prewar conditions shaped authoritarian outcomes. This essay will compare Spain and China to assess the relative importance of war in their authoritarian trajectories and argue that war was essential to Franco's rise but only one of several significant conditions in Mao's emergence. ($\checkmark$ organization)

In Spain, the Spanish Civil War destroyed the democratic Second Republic, creating the structural vacuum that enabled Franco's ascent. The war produced extreme polarization, militarized politics, and legitimized authoritarian solutions to perceived national chaos. ($\checkmark$ knowledge) Franco's rise depended on the wartime unification of Nationalist factions, which enabled him to eliminate rivals and establish himself as the sole military and political leader. The conflict also provided ideological legitimacy, as Franco framed his victory as a crusade against communism, secularism, and regional separatism. The postwar reconstruction of the state was shaped entirely by wartime dynamics, enabling centralized control, repression, and the institutionalization of authoritarian rule. ($\checkmark$ examples) The impact of war in Spain was therefore not merely contributory but foundational. Without the Civil War, Franco would not have gained the authority, legitimacy, or institutional control required to establish an authoritarian regime. The war created the conditions for the collapse of the previous system, the consolidation of military power, and the ideological justification for authoritarianism. This makes war the most important condition in the emergence of Franco's state, outweighing other factors such as prewar political instability or economic tensions. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

China presents a contrasting case. Mao's emergence did not originate solely in war but in a combination of ideological mobilization, social structures, and political fragmentation that preceded major conflict. Before full-scale war, the Chinese Communist Party had already developed organizational discipline, a distinct ideological program, and a base of rural support. The weakness of the Nationalist government, marked by corruption, factionalism, and limited control over rural areas, created structural conditions that facilitated Mao's rise. ($\checkmark$ knowledge) The CCP's early experiences in guerrilla warfare and the Long March strengthened Mao's leadership position and allowed him to consolidate authority within the party before the decisive phases of conflict. War nevertheless played a transformative role. The Second Sino-Japanese War weakened the Nationalist government militarily and politically, eroding its legitimacy and enabling the CCP to expand its influence. The CCP used wartime conditions to build parallel institutions, mobilize peasants, and present itself as a disciplined alternative to the failing Nationalist regime. The Chinese Civil War further strengthened Mao's position, as military victories allowed him to eliminate rivals and demonstrate strategic effectiveness. ($\checkmark$ examples) Wartime mobilization also reinforced ideological cohesion, as Mao framed the struggle as both a national liberation effort and a revolutionary transformation of Chinese society. However, war was not the only significant condition in Mao's emergence. Ideology played a central role, providing a coherent framework that justified social mobilization and political restructuring. The CCP's emphasis on land reform, peasant participation, and anti-corruption campaigns created a broad base of support that was not solely dependent on wartime dynamics. Structural weaknesses in the Nationalist government, including economic crisis, inflation, and administrative collapse, also contributed to Mao's rise. These factors interacted with wartime conditions but were not created by them. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

Franco's portrayal of the Civil War as a crusade against communism can be read either as a genuine ideological conviction or as a retrospective justification for an opportunistic seizure of power. The convenient early deaths of rival Nationalist generals Sanjurjo and Mola, which cleared Franco's path to sole command, support the justification reading: ideology legitimized power already seized rather than caused its seizure. ($\checkmark$ perspectives) Similarly, official CCP narratives present the united front against Japan, and the mobilization of the peasantry as principled national liberation, yet the CCP used the same period to expand its territorial base and strengthen its position relative to the Nationalists — suggesting nationalist framing coexisted with calculated strategic self-interest. Evaluating these competing readings suggests that in both cases, war supplied not only the material conditions for authoritarian consolidation but also a rhetorical resource that leaders shaped after the fact. ($\checkmark$ perspectives)

The comparison also highlights differences in leadership dynamics. Franco's authority stemmed directly from wartime command, whereas Mao's leadership developed through a combination of ideological innovation, party consolidation, and military experience. In Spain, the war unified factions under a single leader, whereas in China, Mao had already established his position within the CCP before the decisive phases of conflict. The ideological role of war also differed: Franco used war to justify national reconstruction, whereas Mao used war to reinforce revolutionary ideology that predated the conflict. These differences underscore the need to evaluate war within broader historical contexts rather than assume its universal primacy. ($\checkmark$ analysis)

In conclusion, war was the most important condition in the emergence of Franco's authoritarian state, but only one of several significant conditions in Mao's rise. The Spanish Civil War laid the structural, ideological, and military foundations for Franco's rule, making war essential to the authoritarian outcome. In China, prewar ideological development, rural mobilization, and structural weaknesses in the Nationalist government played more significant roles, with war serving primarily to accelerate and consolidate existing tendencies. The impact of war, therefore, cannot be generalized across cases. Its relative importance depends on the specific historical context and the interplay of structural and ideological factors. ($\checkmark$ conclusion)
