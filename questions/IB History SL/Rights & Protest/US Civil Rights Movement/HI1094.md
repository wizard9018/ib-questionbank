# HI1094

Compare and contrast the responses to the Sunday School bombing presented in Sources C and D.
[6 marks]

$\\$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

|Top Markband Descriptors (5-6 marks)|Labels |
| :-- | :-- |
|Includes clear points of comparison| ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$) |
|Includes clear points of contrast| ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) |
|Provides evidence from the text (SBQ) or own knowledge (SAQ) to validate arguments| ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$) |

$\\$

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four** marks.
- Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.
- The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear **evidence** from the texts.

For comparison, the following:
- Both President Kennedy and Rev. Oliver express grief at the bombing
- Both President Kennedy and Rev. Oliver state that actions are needed now to bring peace and justice in the aftermath

For contrast, any **two** of the following:
- Source C states that restraint is needed, whilst Source D makes no mention of restraint and instead calls for urgent action
- Source D states that the feeling of safety has completely disappeared for the Negro community, whereas Source C makes no mention of security and safety disappearing
- Source D states that the bombing is part of a wider context of police brutality and violence, whereas Source C does not mention police brutality at all
- Source C states that law enforcement is there to support investigation and prosecution, whereas Source D states that law enforcement officers have inflicted further injuries since the bombing took place
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Sources C and D are similar in that they both express sadness at the bombing ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source C states that it is written out of $\lq$outrage and grief' whilst Source D states that it is written $\lq$out of deep sorrow' ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Both leaders also state that actions are necessary to restore calm and security as well as call on citizens' participation($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Source D states that it seeks $\lq$remedial justice' and to $\lq$awaken the nation' whilst Source C calls on every citizen to $\lq$join in this effort' for domestic peace and security ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

In contrast, the sources differ in their representation of the impacts of the bombing on the black community in Birmingham ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source D states that $\lq$the feeling of safety has completely disappeared from the Negro community,', whilst Source C makes no mention of this ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). The sources also diverge in their commentary about the role of law enforcement in the aftermath of the bombing ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Source C mentions that law enforcement is there to help, whilst Source D includes a reference to injuries inflicted by law enforcement officers ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Source C also notes that FBI $\lq$bomb specialists' are present to help, whilst Source D makes no mention of this and instead states that there have been $\lq$death and injuries' inflicted by policemen even after the bombing ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
