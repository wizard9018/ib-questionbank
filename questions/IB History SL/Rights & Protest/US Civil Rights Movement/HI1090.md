# HI1090

Explain the significance of the Sunday School Bombing (1963, aka 16th Street Baptist Church bombing) to the civil rights movement in the US.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with its corresponding evidence:
- Dr Martin Luther King Jr., a key figure in the Civil Rights movement used the event to bring national attention to the cause of the Civil Rights Movement
    - Dr Martin Luther King Jr. called on the President and federal government to intervene, raising the event to a tragedy with national implications
- The President involved himself in the crisis
    - President Kennedy made a national address in the aftermath of the crisis and then met with community leaders on all sides to try and bring reconciliation
- The age and gender of the victims drew national sympathy
    - As four young girls were killed in the bombing, public sympathy was heightened and awakened by the bombing in a way that had not been so pronounced for other violent acts in Birmingham.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Sunday School bombing was an event that drew national attention to Birmingham due to its victims ($\checkmark$). Four young girls were killed in the bombing which created a level of national sympathy and awareness that was particularly pronounced ($\checkmark$). Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. who was a prominent activist used the event to bring awareness of the need for progress in the civil rights cause ($\checkmark$). He emphasised the nature of the tragedy as an event with national significance and helped draw President Kennedy to take action to bring justice and peace ($\checkmark$).
