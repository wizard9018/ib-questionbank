# HI1087

Describe the Birmingham Campaign of 1963 and explain its significance.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
For **describe**, the following or similar:
- It was a campaign of non-violent protest led by the SCLC (Southern Christian Leadership Conference) designed to achieve progress in the fight for civil rights and equality. Actions included sit-ins, kneel-ins, and marches culminating in the Children's Crusade.
[1 marks]

For **explanation of significance**, any **two** of the following:
- The Children's Crusade effectively utilized children and young people to highlight the generational aspect of the fight for civil rights. It was significant for the media to see that youth, as well as better-known older campaigners, were hungry for change
- The media attention garnered by the campaign further publicised the cause of the Civil Rights Movement. This was significant as getting their cause into the public gaze was one of the goals of the movement.
- As one of the most segregated cities in the country, progress achieved by the campaign had a significant impact in reducing inequality and segregation for the people of Birmingham.
- Represented a turning point that indicated the potential effectiveness of nonviolent direct action campaigns.
\
[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Birmingham campaign of 1963 was a model of nonviolent protest to bring about greater equality that was led by the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC) and drew media attention to the civil rights cause ($\checkmark$).

It was significant because it took place in an extremely segregated city, so that any progress made was highly impactful for the people who faced daily discrimination there ($\checkmark$). It was also significant as it was widely reported in the media, which helped publicise the movement and its goals across the nation ($\checkmark$).
