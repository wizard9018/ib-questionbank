# HI0724

Compare and contrast what Sources O and P reveal about the perspectives on school desegregation.

[6 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source O** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Excerpt from *Brown vs Board of Education* ruling by the US Supreme Court, 1954.

:box[

[Black children] had been systematically denied admission to schools attended by white children under laws requiring or permitting segregation according to race. This segregation was alleged to deprive the plaintiffs of the equal protection of the laws under the Fourteenth Amendment.

\

\

…

\

\

The plaintiffs contend that segregated public schools are not $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equal" and cannot be made $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equal,” and that hence they are deprived of the equal protection of the laws. Because of the obvious importance of the question presented, the Court took jurisdiction. Argument was heard in the 1952 Term, and reargument was heard this Term on certain questions propounded by the Court.

\

\

Reargument was largely devoted to the circumstances surrounding the adoption of the Fourteenth Amendment in 1868. It covered exhaustively consideration of the Amendment in Congress, ratification by the states, then existing practices in racial segregation, and the views of proponents and opponents of the Amendment. This discussion and our own investigation convince us that, although these sources cast some light, it is not enough to resolve the problem with which we are faced. At best, they are inconclusive. The most avid proponents of the post-War Amendments undoubtedly intended them to remove all legal distinctions among $\text{\textquotedblleft}$all persons born or naturalized in the United States.” Their opponents, just as certainly, were antagonistic to both the letter and the spirit of the Amendments and wished them to have the most limited effect. …

\

\

An additional reason for the inconclusive nature of the Amendment's history, with respect to segregated schools, is the status of public education at the time. In the South, the movement toward free common schools, supported by general taxation, had not yet taken hold. Education of white children was largely in the hands of private groups. Education of Negroes was almost nonexistent, and practically all of the race were illiterate. In fact, any education of Negroes was forbidden by law in some states.

]

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both similarities and differences must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **four marks**.

 - Responses that only describe the content of the source can achieve a maximum of **two** marks.

 - The strongest answers will identify and develop at least **two** similarities and at least **two** differences, both supported by clear evidence from the texts.

For **comparison**, any of the following similarities:

 - Both sources show the importance of the Fourteenth Amendment to both the arguments of both pro- and anti-integration perspectives..

 - Both sources agree that segregated education was to be taken as a historical fact, regardless of debates surrounding legal provisions and judicial decisions.

 - Both sources suggest that the Supreme Court played a significant role in establishing provisions on racial segregation in the education system.

For **contrast**, any of the following differences:

 - While Source O calls for a revision of the scope of the Fourteenth Amendment on education matters, arguing that segregated schools are not (and cannot be made) equal. In contrast, Source P stresses that the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$separate but equal” doctrine is still legitimate according to the court's interpretation of Plessy.

 - Source O supports the nationwide decision against the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$separate but equal” doctrine in public schools due to historical racial inequalities in the South, whereas Source P maintains that states' autonomy should be guaranteed and that the court's decision was overstepping and illegitimate.

\

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Both sources agree that the 14th Amendment was central to the debate about integration in education ($\checkmark\text{ similarity}$). Source O says that the debate in the courts was $\text{\textquotedblleft}$devoted to the circumstances surrounding the adoption of the Fourteenth Amendment in 1868.” Similarly, Source P says that conversations about the 14th Amendment $\text{\textquotedblleft}$clearly show that there was no intent that it should affect the systems of education maintained by the States” ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$). Both sources also acknowledge the long tradition of segregated schools in the United States ($\checkmark\text{ similarity}$). Source O points out that schools serving black children were $\text{\textquotedblleft}$almost nonexistent” or even “forbidden by law in some states” while Source P points out that the application of the “constitutional doctrine of separate but equal schools began in the North-not in the South” ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$).

\

\

However, the sources differ with reference to the scope of the Supreme Court's jurisdiction on school desegregation matters and their precise interpretations of the Fourteenth Amendment ($\checkmark\text{ difference}$). While Source O argues that the Supreme Court should revise the application of the Fourteenth Amendment because “segregated public schools are not $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equal" and cannot be made $\text{\textquotedblleft}$equal.” Source P maintains that its provisions were clear in that there “was no intent” for it to influence the education system in the US ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$). 

\

\

Finally, Source O argued that the court is fulfilling its duties according to the constitution, while Source P suggests it is abusing its power ($\checkmark\text{ difference}$). Source O mentions the “obvious importance of the question presented” and its constitutional responsibility to rule on the Equal Protection Clause, while Source P denounces the ruling as a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$clear abuse of judicial power” ($\checkmark\text{ evidence}$).
