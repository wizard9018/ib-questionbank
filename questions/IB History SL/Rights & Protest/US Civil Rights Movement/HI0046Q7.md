# HI0046Q7

Explain the significance of the Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party (MFDP) in the context of the Freedom Summer.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any combination of **three** of the following:
- The MFDP was founded during the Freedom Summer as an integrated party that challenged the dominance of the all-white Mississippi Democratic Party.
- The MFDP was an organisational platform for significant civil rights leaders, especially Fannie Lou Hamer who was the Vice Chairwoman of the party and spoke from personal experience about the challenges African Americans faced in Mississippi, drawing national attention to the issues.
- The MFDP drew national attention to racial injustice in Mississippi when it attempted to challenge the legitimacy of the Mississippi Democratic Party during the Democratic National Convention of 1964.
- The MFDP helped create pressure on the federal government to pass reforms, especially the Voting Rights Act of 1965.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The MFDP was a racially integrated party created during Freedom Summer to challenge the authority of the traditional Mississippi Democratic Party which was all-white ($\checkmark$). Specifically, the MFDP created a 68-member delegation which attended the 1964 Democratic National Convention and attempted to be recognised as the legitimate representative delegation from Mississippi instead of the all-white party ($\checkmark$). One of its leaders, Fannie Lou Hamer, drew national attention when she spoke about the discrimination she had faced when trying to register to vote in Mississippi ($\checkmark$). However, the MFDP’s attempt to unseat the all-white delegation ultimately failed ($\checkmark$).
