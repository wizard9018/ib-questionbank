# HI0723

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source M for an historian studying desegration legislation in the US Civil Rights era.

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source M** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Public of delivery by Congressman Adam Clayton Powell Jr. before the House of Representatives in 1956. His address revolves around the reasons for his initiative to amend the conditions on government funding of schools that did not desegregate education.

:box[

With the executive branch of Government now indicating that it will $\text{\textquotedblleft}$discharge whatever obligations are imposed upon it whether by Congressional action" it is now obvious that the Powell amendments to the Federal aid to school construction bill are mandatory.

\

\

Seven objections have been raised to my amendments: 

\

\

…

\

\

Fourth. \[Presidential Candidate\] Adlai Stevenson has come out against the amendment because he believes in moderation and gradualism. You cannot apply the principle of moderation and gradualism to any legislation that is a crash legislation which will be over within 4 years. I ask this question of Mr. Stevenson and his crowd: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$How can new Jim Crow schools built in States that are defying by legislative action the Supreme Court help even gradualism?”

\

\

Fifth. The Christian Science Monitor \[newspaper\] has pointed out that my amendments are punitive. This they are not. In the first place, I am not forcing integration upon any school district. If they do not want to integrate, that is a matter to be taken up in other ways. But if they do want to integrate, then, and only then, can they receive Federal funds. My amendments are not punitive because my second amendment would hold the accumulated funds for the whole 4 years of the bill until any district now segregated does integrate during the life of the Kelley bill or the McConnell substitute.

\

\

Sixth. It has been indicated that this would even hurt Negro children. I am happy to announce that every single editor of every single Negro newspaper in this country, monthly, weekly, and daily, who has replied to my letter, has indicated 100 percent backing of the Powell amendments.

\

\

Seventh. That the sentiment of the American people is not in favor of such amendments. This is totally erroneous. I have just conducted a quick questionnaire of the Members of the House of Representatives and the answers to these questionnaires mailed to every Member of the House are now running more than 2 to 1 in favor of the Powell amendments.

]

[Source: Adapted from Powell, A. C. (1956, February 16). *Federal Aid to School Construction.* Congressional Record, p. 2731. Retrieved July 9, 2025 from https://congress.gov/bound-congressional-record/1956/02/16/house-section. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of two marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full four marks.

For value, any of the following:

 - Powell is a member of Congress and it is a direct account of congressional proceedings.

 - The purpose is to justify the desegration amendments based on legal precedents.

 - Provides an overview of the legal background of the reasons why the proposed amendments should be considered.

For limitations, any of the following:

 - The formal, congressional setting where the legislative process was taking place does not allow tracking of the desegregation provisions on the ground.

 - Offers a one-sided perspective in support of the amendments and  interpretation of legal precedents to justify them.

 - Only addresses the debate in Congress and does not indicate whether or not the amendments were passed or had any impact.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

With reference to its origin, the source is valuable because Adam Powell's role as a congressman and the setting of a congressional hearing provides a firsthand insight into the legislative processes on desegregation in education ($\checkmark\text{ VO}$). However, it should be noted that it is also limited because the accounts of the process mainly take place in the formal setting of the Congress, thereby disregarding the effects of and reactions to the legislative decisions in local places ($\checkmark\text{ LO}$).

In terms of its purpose, the source is valuable because Powell present both the legal basis for the amendments and their feasibility for implementation as desegration in education might proceed after their passage ($\checkmark\text{ VP}$). Nonetheless, the source content is limited because the Powell amendments are only proposed to one bill, which does allow for a well-rounded study of wider legislative processes on education and the source content gives no indication as to whether or not the amendments passed and were made into law ($\checkmark\text{ LC}$).
