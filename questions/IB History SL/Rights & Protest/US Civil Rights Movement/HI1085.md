# HI1085

Analyse the significance of the Little Rock Nine (1957).
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following plus its corresponding evidence:
- The Little Rock Nine put defiance of Jim Crow practices into action
    - The bravery of the students to go to school in the face of intimidation was a clear sign of the strength of resistance within the Civil Rights movement
- The youth and gender of the Little Rock Nine showed the widespread appeal of the Civil Rights movement
    - Carlotta Walls was just 14 years old at the time and the age of the group ranged from 14 to 16, including both boys and girls.
- The fierce resistance to the Little Rock Nine displayed the levels of racism that the Civil Rights movement had to face
    - The shocking images of racist insults being hurled at young children walking to school made it clear for all to see how much hatred and oppression African-Americans still faced
- The national media attention gave publicity to the Civil Rights movement
    - As the publicity around the Little Rock Nine grew and televised images were broadcast, people around the USA had the dramatic tension of the Civil Rights movement beamed into their living rooms
- The decision of the President to send in troops to escort students revealed the conflict between the Southern states and the federal government
    - When President Eisenhower was forced to send in troops to keep the peace, it became clear that federal government action and involvement in the Civil Rights movement would be necessary and could not be avoided.
- Signalled the first major test of the *Brown v Board of Education* decision.
    - Demonstrated that Supreme Court rulings alone were not adequate to change local behaviors and the enforcement for these rulings was lacking.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Little Rock Nine was a highly significant moment in the Civil Rights movement as their determination was an example of putting non-violent theory into action ($\checkmark$). The bravery of the students in resolutely attending school in the face of such racism showed the strength of the movement ($\checkmark$). The youth and gender of the students showed the wide appeal of the movement ($\checkmark$). The youngest student was just 14 at the time and the group included both boys and girls ranging from 14 to 16 years old ($\checkmark$). The national media attention given to the incident publicised the cause of the movement ($\checkmark$). As televised images of the students and their racist opponents were beamed across the nation, Americans saw the dramatic tension at the heart of the Civil Rights movement for themselves which increased support for its goals ($\checkmark$).
