# HI0952

Explain **three** key ways the federal government responded to resistance to school desegregation.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** with explanation:
- Judicial actions and court rulings
    - Brown v. Board of Education II (1955): Follow-up to the original 1954 Brown decision. Directed schools to desegregate “with all deliberate speed.” Gave federal courts ongoing oversight of desegregation, though the vague language allowed Southern states to delay compliance.
    - Court enforcement of desegregation orders: Federal courts issued injunctions and orders against school boards refusing to comply. Judges could hold local officials in contempt of court for noncompliance.
- Executive actions and federal intervention
    - Little Rock, Arkansas (1957): President Dwight D. Eisenhower deployed the 101st Airborne Division to escort the Little Rock Nine into Central High School after the Arkansas governor used the National Guard to block integration. Marked the first time since Reconstruction that federal troops were used to enforce African American civil rights.
    - Department of Justice involvement: The DOJ increasingly intervened in desegregation cases, filing lawsuits and supporting civil rights plaintiffs. Used amicus curiae ($\text{\textquotedblleft}$friend of the court") briefs to support integration.
- Legislation
    - Civil Rights Act of 1964: Title IV: Gave the federal government authority to file suits to desegregate public schools. Title VI: Allowed the government to withhold federal funds from school districts practising discrimination. Created the Office for Civil Rights (OCR) within the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare to monitor school compliance.
    - Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965): Provided federal funding to schools but tied it to compliance with desegregation mandates. Used financial incentives to encourage local school districts to implement integration.
- Monitoring and compliance mechanisms
    - Federal oversight of school districts: The federal government, often through the courts or the OCR, monitored desegregation plans and ensured districts made $\text{\textquotedblleft}$good faith" efforts to comply. Required submission of desegregation plans and regular progress reports.
- Public statements and pressure
    - Presidents (especially Kennedy and Johnson) used the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$bully pulpit” to support civil rights and encourage compliance with desegregation: JFK sent federal marshals to enforce court orders (e.g., University of Mississippi, 1962). LBJ pushed through legislation and linked civil rights to national unity and moral responsibility.
    - Brown II (1955) ruling mandated that desegregation occur $\text{\textquotedblleft}$with all deliberate speed," although the term was vague.
    - Eisenhower's intervention in Little Rock (1957): Sent federal troops to enforce integration of Central High School.
    - Civil Rights Act of 1964: Gave the federal government power to withhold funds from segregated schools.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
One major way the federal government responded was through judicial action ($\checkmark$). After the Supreme Court's decision in Brown v. Board of Education (1954), federal courts continued to oversee desegregation cases and issued orders to force school districts to comply. In Brown II (1955), the Court instructed schools to integrate with "all deliberate speed," and federal judges later used their authority to hold districts accountable :span($\checkmark$).

Secondly, the federal government used executive power to enforce desegregation when state authorities refused to comply ($\checkmark$). For example, in 1957, President Eisenhower sent federal troops to Little Rock, Arkansas, to protect nine African American students entering Central High School, directly confronting resistance from the state governor :span($\checkmark$).

Finally, federal legislation played a critical role :span($\checkmark$). The Civil Rights Act of 1964 gave the federal government the power to cut funding to segregated schools and enabled the Department of Justice to bring legal action against non compliant districts. This increased pressure on local authorities to end school segregation and comply with federal law ($\checkmark$).
