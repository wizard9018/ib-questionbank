# HI0401

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of the source for an historian studying the March on Selma of 1965.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source** $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ $\\$ $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Photograph by Stanley Wofson for the newspaper New York World Telegram & Sun, March 1965. This Photograph shows an African American man carrying a child on his shoulders with a placard telling President Johnson to go to Selma, Alabama. |

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following:
- The source provides insight as it was made in 1965 by a photographer who was present at the march on Selma (origin).
- The source seeks to show what was going on during the March on Selma since the photograph was published in the newspaper The New York World Telegram & Sun (purpose).
- We can learn from the source that the March was organized with peaceful intentions as the man portrayed is carrying a small child. We also can see that, at least this individual, was calling for Johnson's presence (content).

For limitations, any combination of the following:
- The source has a limited perspective of what the photographer was pointing at when he took the picture. The picture was probably taken at the beginning of the march before the situation turned violent (origin).
- The source seeks to convey the urgency of Johnson's actions by only focusing on this particular participant's sign (purpose).
- The source portrays a very limited angle of the march and leaves out other demands of the people who were there as well as the impact of the March itself (content).
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is valuable in its origin since it has insight into the situation at Selma as the photograph was taken in 1965 ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$). The purpose is also valuable since the photographer wanted to document what was happening at the March for the newspaper The New York World Telegram & Sun ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). The content of the source is valuable as we can learn that the march was organized with peaceful intentions, as evidenced by the man depicted carrying a small child. Additionally, the photograph shows that at least one individual was calling for President Johnson's involvement ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

However, the source also has limitations for a historian studying the March on Selma of 1965. First, its origin is limited since it only shows the perspective the photographer chose to focus on. Moreover, the photograph was obviously taken before the situation turned violent, so it has limited information on the whole development of the March ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). The purpose is limited since the photograph seeks to emphasize the need for President Johnson to act by concentrating solely on one character's sign ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$). Since the picture provides a narrow view of the march, its content is limited since the other demands of the marchers are left out, as is the overall impact of the March itself ($\checkmark\, \text{LC}$).
