# HI0957

Compare and contrast the strategies and beliefs of Martin Luther King Jr and Malcolm X in achieving civil rights for African Americans.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following. Responses may include two points for each side (2/2), or be weighted (3/1 or 1/3),

For comparison, any of the following similarities:
- Committed to Black empowerment and dignity. Both wanted to end white oppression and improve the social, political, and economic conditions of African Americans.
- Challenged systemic racism. Each condemned the systemic discrimination that African Americans faced in housing, education, and employment.
- Influential orators. Both used powerful speeches to inspire their followers and influence public opinion.
- Supported Black pride and self-worth. Especially in later years, both emphasised African American identity and self-respect.

For contrast, any of the following differences:
- Philosophy of non-violence vs. self-defence
    - King: Advocated non-violent civil disobedience, inspired by Gandhi and Christian theology.
    - Malcolm X: Originally promoted self-defence “by any means necessary”, rejecting non-violence if attacked.
- Views on integration
    - King believed in racial integration and cooperation between races.
    - Malcolm X (early): Advocated for Black separatism, believing that integration was impossible in a racist society.
- Relationship with the U.S. political system
    - King: Worked within the system, lobbying politicians and pushing for federal civil rights legislation (e.g., Civil Rights Act, Voting Rights Act).
    - Malcolm X: Distrusted the system, viewing it as fundamentally corrupt; often rejected appealing to white institutions for justice.
- Evolution over time
    - Malcolm X's views evolved after 1964; he softened his stance on working with white allies and began advocating for global human rights.
    - King also became more radical in his later years, focusing on economic justice and opposing the Vietnam War.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Firstly, both leaders utilised powerful public speeches to raise awareness and inspire their followers, thereby becoming national symbols of the civil rights movement ($\checkmark$). Secondly, each sought to empower Black communities and condemned systemic racism in American society, although they approached the issue differently ($\checkmark$).

However, there were important differences in their strategies and beliefs. King believed in nonviolent protest, using peaceful marches and civil disobedience to push for legal reforms such as the Civil Rights Act of 1964. In contrast, Malcolm X initially argued that African Americans should defend their rights “by any means necessary,” including violence if provoked ($\checkmark$). Also, King welcomed white support and believed interracial cooperation was essential for progress. He worked closely with white activists and religious leaders and emphasised the shared moral responsibility of all Americans to fight racism. However, Malcolm X rejected alliances with white liberals or civil rights groups, believing that African Americans should build their independent power without interference. He viewed white involvement as potentially condescending or untrustworthy. However, this view evolved later in his life after his 1964 pilgrimage to Mecca, where he expressed greater openness to working with people of all races who supported human rights ($\checkmark$).
