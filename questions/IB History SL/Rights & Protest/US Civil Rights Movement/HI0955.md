# HI0955

Explain **three** ways the Student Non-Violent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) contributed to the Civil Rights Movement from 1960 to 1965.
[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** with explanation:
- Grassroots activism. Focused on empowering local communities, particularly young Black activists in the South.
- Sit-ins. Helped coordinate and expand sit-ins after the Greensboro Four protest in 1960.
- Freedom Rides. Participated in the 1961 Freedom Rides alongside CORE to challenge segregation on interstate buses.
- Voter registration drives. Led campaigns in the Deep South, especially in Mississippi (e.g., 1964 Mississippi Freedom Summer).
- Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party (MFDP). Helped organise this alternative political party to challenge the exclusion of Black voters in Mississippi.
- March on Washington (1963). SNCC leader John Lewis gave a speech (moderated by other leaders for tone) representing youth activism.
- Selma Voting Campaign (1965). Organised efforts in Selma prior to SCLC's involvement, showing its early leadership.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
In 1961, SNCC members joined the Freedom Rides, risking violence and arrest to challenge the illegal segregation of interstate bus travel in the South ($\checkmark$). These actions drew national attention to ongoing racial injustice and helped push federal enforcement of desegregation laws ($\checkmark$).

The SNCC also played a leading role in voter registration efforts, particularly in areas where white resistance was particularly extreme ($\checkmark$).Its most notable campaign was the Mississippi Freedom Summer (1964), where volunteers worked to register Black voters and establish schools and community centres, despite threats and violence ($\checkmark$).

Finally, SNCC helped form the Mississippi Freedom Democratic Party (MFDP) to challenge the all-white Mississippi delegation at the 1964 Democratic National Convention ($\checkmark$). This effort highlighted the exclusion of Black citizens from political representation and pressured the national Democratic Party to confront racism in its ranks ($\checkmark$).
