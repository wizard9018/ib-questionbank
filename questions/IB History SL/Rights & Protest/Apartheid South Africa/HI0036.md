# HI0036

Define the terms “petty apartheid” and “grand apartheid” and give examples of each.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

Any combination of following to define “petty apartheid” for **one** mark:
-   Everyday, small-scale segregation and discrimination that affected the daily lives of non-white South Africans.
-   Racial separation in public spaces and services.

Any of the following examples to illustrate “petty apartheid” for **one** mark:
-   Separate facilities and amenities such as buses, public restrooms, schools, hospitals, beaches, and even park benches.

Any combination of the following to define “grand apartheid” for **one** mark:
-   Large-scale, systematic policies that structured South African society along racial lines
-   Policies that created separate political and economic systems for white and non-white South Africans.

Any of the following examples to illustrate “grand apartheid” for **one** mark:
-   The creation of the Bantustans (homelands) where black South Africans were forced to live, often in underdeveloped and remote areas.
-   Laws that restricted land ownership (rural or urban), employment opportunities, and movement/migration based on race.
-   Naming and description of specific legislation including The Population Registration Act, the Group Areas Act, and/or the Bantu Authorities Act.

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Petty apartheid refers to the day-to-day forms of segregation experienced by black South Africans ($\checkmark$) including restrictions on use of public restrooms, beaches or parks ($\checkmark$).

Grand apartheid created entirely different systems for non-white South Africans at a large scale ($\checkmark$). The South African government created legislation like the Population Registration Act which classified South Africans racially and then was used to determine what parts of society they could access including which kinds of jobs they were allowed to work in ($\checkmark$).
