# HI1108

According to Source C, what impacts did detention without trial have on Africans during apartheid?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  “The many faces of apartheid repression” is an article whose content was taken from the book A Crime Against Humanity - Analysing the Repression of the Apartheid State, edited by Max Coleman (Cape Town). |

[Source: South African History Online. (2013, July 26). *The Many Faces of Apartheid Repression.* Retrieved November 1, 2025, from https://sahistory.org.za/article/many-faces-apartheid-repression Public Domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** or a combination of the following:
- Detention for interrogation or 'preventive' reasons was common since the 1960s, often without access to courts, family, or other necessary support.
- Detainees could be held in solitary confinement for nearly indefinite periods.
- Isolation promoted abuses, and there were ongoing allegations of torture and assault.
- There was a continuous series of deaths in custody, many of them officially classified as suicides.
- Since 1960, more than 75,000 people have been detained without trial.
- These practices led to severe physical, psychological, and social consequences for African people.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
According to Source C, detention without trial allowed for detention for interrogation or $\lq$preventive' reasons, without access to courts, lawyers, family, or friends ($\checkmark$). As a result, this system kept many activists in solitary confinement for nearly indefinite periods, sometimes lasting up to 32 months, promoting abuse, torture, and assault on detainees ($\checkmark$). Source C also says that many deaths occurred in custody, with 69 reported between 1963 and 1988, many of which were suicides ($\checkmark$).
