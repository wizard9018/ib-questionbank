# HI1099

Outline the South African government's goals in implementing the Bantu Self-Government Act of 1959.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The Bantu Self-Government Act of 1959 aimed to establish $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Separate Development" by creating autonomous homelands for Black South Africans.
- The government planned to reclassify Black South Africans as citizens of these homelands, stripping them of their rights as South African citizens.
- The Act created homelands as self-governing regions, which could eventually become formally independent states.
- Precedent for The Black Homelands Citizenship Act of 1970, which granted all Black South Africans citizenship of the homelands, even if they lived in $\text{\textquotedblleft}$White South Africa."
- Then reinforced by the Bantu Homelands Constitution Act of 1971, which established a framework for developing the homelands toward complete independence.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The Bantu Self-Government Act of 1959 aimed to create distinct, self-governing regions for different racial groups by creating independent homeland territories for Black South Africans, known as Bantustans ($\checkmark$).

The government sought to reclassify Black South Africans as citizens of these Bantustans instead of South Africa itself, effectively stripping them of their remaining rights as South African citizens and increasing racial segregation ($\checkmark$).

This policy aimed to reduce the power of the Black population in South Africa and further entrench disenfranchisement. The Act established the plan called $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Separate Development," which allowed the so-called $\text{\textquotedblleft}$homeland territories” to be set up as autonomous regions and, eventually, as nominally fully independent states. Bantustans were encouraged to choose independence, which would greatly decrease the number of Black citizens in South Africa ($\checkmark$).
