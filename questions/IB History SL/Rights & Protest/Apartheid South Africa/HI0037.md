# HI0037

Compare and contrast the roles and significance of the African National Congress (ANC) and the South African Communist Party (SACP) in the anti-Apartheid movement in South Africa. [6 marks]

For comparison, any **two to four** of the following similarities:
-   Both organisations played a critical role in the actions that would lead to the eventual collapse of the apartheid regime in South Africa.
-   Both the ANC and the SACP were banned by the South African government because of the threat they presented to its all-white leadership.
-   After being banned, both parties continued to work underground and often in alliance with each other against the apartheid regime.
-   Both parties produced significant leaders in the anti-apartheid movement including Nelson Mandela (ANC), Albert Luthuli (ANC), Dr. Yusuf Dadoo (SACP) and Moses Kotane (SACP).
-   Both parties played a substantial role in the formation and actions taken by Umkhonto we Sizwe (the “MK”), the armed, militant wing of the ANC and both participated in sabotage, guerrilla warfare and other armed resistance against the South African government.
-   Both parties drew significant international support in terms of money, materials and philosophical backing.

For contrast, any **two to four** of the following differences:
-   While the ANC advocated for some socialist ideals/goals including policies to reduce inequality and nationalise certain industries, its primary political ideology was based in African nationalism and focused on the liberation of non-white South Africans. The SACP's ideology was primarily a class-based, anti-capitalist struggle for both white and non-white working class South Africans.
-   The ANC drew its membership from a variety of political and economic perspectives including black nationalists, white liberals, socialists and religious leaders while the SACPs membership was more limited as it drew from a narrower ideological group committed more to Marxist-Leninst principles.
-   While both groups drew significant international support, the ANC's support tended to come from the capitalist, democratic Western World (especially Great Britain) while the SACP's was drawn more from the Soviet Union and its sphere of influence.
-   Because the SACP was banned and forced underground much earlier than the ANC, its role in the anti-apartheid struggle tended to be more covert, acting more through other larger, less-restricted organisations including, but not limited to the ANC.

[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample answer}}$: \
Both the ANC and SACP played a significant role in the struggle against apartheid and shared a common goal of dismantling the white-dominated apartheid government in South Africa ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). As a result of the threat they posed to the South African government, both parties were also effectively banned ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). The CPSA, the predecessor to the SACP, voluntarily disbanded in 1950 in the face of the Suppression of Communism Act and reformed in 1953 as the SACP. The ANC was banned in 1960 in the aftermath of the Sharpeville massacre ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

After the banning of the ANC, both the ANC and SACP continued to work in an underground alliance to form the MK, the armed militant wing of the ANC which would carry out various forms of resistance including sabotage, bombings of civilian facilities and guerrilla tactics ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).

While both organisations worked in alliance for many years, they still maintained some significant differences. One major difference was the size and membership base ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). The ANC's membership included a variety of people from a variety of ideological perspectives appealing to African/black nationalists and white liberals and while the SACP's narrow ideological origins in communism/socialism tended to limit its membership and appeal ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).

Both groups drew significant international support ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$), but from entirely different sources ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). The ANC's international support tended to come from Western nations and particularly Great Britain while the SACP's support, both financial and material, came primarily from the Soviet Union including the training of MK members ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
