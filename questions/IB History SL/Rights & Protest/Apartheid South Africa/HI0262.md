# HI0262

Describe the Defiance Campaign and discuss why it can be seen as both a success and failure. 

[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For description, the following: 

 - The Defiance Campaign (1952) was a mass protest movement led by the African National Congress (ANC) and its allies which aimed to challenge apartheid laws through nonviolent civil disobedience by deliberately breaking discriminatory laws, such as pass laws and curfews.

\

[1 marks]

For successes and failures, a combination of five of the following that includes at least one success and one failure: 

 - Success:

     - The campaign significantly increased membership in the ANC, from approximately 7,000 to over 100,000.

     - It united people across racial and ethnic lines, creating solidarity among oppressed groups, such as Black, Indian and “Coloured” South Africans 

     - The campaign attracted international attention, highlighting the injustices of apartheid to a global audience, and partially contributing to the UN General Assembly formally recognising apartheid as a threat to international peace. 

     - It set the stage for the anti-apartheid movement's future support from global organisations like the United Nations.

     - The Defiance Campaign established nonviolent resistance as a powerful tool, inspiring future movements, such as the Freedom Charter in 1955.

 - Failure: 

    - The campaign failed to force the apartheid government to repeal any major discriminatory laws, as the government responded with repression rather than reform.

     - The government introduced harsher penalties for civil disobedience and passed stricter laws to punish protesters more severely.

     - The campaign primarily involved urban areas and had limited impact on rural South Africans, who were equally affected by apartheid policies. 

\

[5 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Defiance Campaign of 1952 was a coordinated protest effort spearheaded by the African National Congress (ANC) in collaboration with allied groups. Its primary objective was to challenge apartheid policies through peaceful nonviolent resistance, deliberately violating discriminatory regulations such as pass laws, curfews, and segregation measures ($\checkmark$).

The campaign boasted notable achievements. It successfully expanded ANC membership, surging from approximately 7,000 to over 100,000, illustrating its effectiveness in mobilising support on a national scale ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, it united diverse racial and ethnic communities, including Black, Indian, and "Coloured" South Africans,instilling a shared sense of purpose among oppressed groups ($\checkmark$). It also gained international attention, exposing apartheid's injustices to the world, and paved the way for global organisations like the United Nations to recognise apartheid as a threat to peace ($\checkmark$).

However, the campaign was not without shortcomings. It did not compel the apartheid government to repeal any significant laws; instead, the authorities intensified their oppression by enacting stricter regulations and imposing harsher punishments on protestors ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the movement primarily affected urban areas, leaving many rural South Africans, who faced similar hardships, largely untouched by its effort ($\checkmark$).
