# HI0267

What does Source B suggest about the nature of resistance to apartheid in the 1950s? 

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | The photograph below shows all the accused at the Treason Trial. 

![hi0267](images/hi0267.jpg)

[Source: Weinberg, E. (1956, December 1). *Treason Trial*. Originally found in Mayibuye Archive. Retrieved January 18, 2025 from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:TreasonTrial1956.jpg.  Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - Resistance to apartheid was collective and organised, as shown by the large group of individuals accused during the Treason Trial, united in their opposition to apartheid policies.

 - The movement was diverse and inclusive, with participants of different races and genders working together to challenge apartheid, reflecting the principle of non-racialism.

 - The scale of the opposition is evident, with a significant number of individuals involved, suggesting widespread participation in anti-apartheid activities.

 - The photo portrays a sense of unity and solidarity among those resisting apartheid, as they are pictured together, smiling and defiant in the face of prosecution.

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Firstly, the image suggests that resistance to apartheid in the 1950s was united and organised across racial and gender lines. This is evident in the photograph, which shows a large, diverse group of individuals—including men and women of different racial backgrounds—gathered together in solidarity during the Treason Trial of 1956 ($\checkmark$). Secondly, the image also suggests that there was a strong sense of solidarity amongst the accused, who are photographed smiling together in spite of the fact that they are facing prosecution ($\checkmark$).
