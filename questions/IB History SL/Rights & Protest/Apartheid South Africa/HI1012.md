# HI1012

Explain **two** ways apartheid laws affected the economic and social lives of Africans between 1948 and 1964.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Guidelines:
- Must include  a combination of **both** social and economic effects **and** mention the relevant law.
- **One** mark is awarded for each law and **one** mark for accurate explanation of  effect.

**Economic impact laws:**
- Pass Laws: Restricted African movement into cities; limited access to jobs.
- Job Colour Bar (reinforced by labour regulations): Reserved skilled, better-paid jobs for whites.
- Bantu Education Act (1953): Legally restricted Africans to inferior education, preparing them only for menial labour.
- Group Areas Act (1950): Forced removals made commuting costly and disrupted economic stability

**Social impact laws:**
- Population Registration Act (1950): Classified all South Africans by race, forming the basis of discrimination.
- Group Areas Act (1950): Segregated residential areas, breaking up communities and families.
- Bantu Education Act (1953): Also reinforced social inequality by denying equal schooling.
- Petty Apartheid Laws: Enforced segregation in public spaces (schools, hospitals, transport, shops, benches).
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Two key apartheid laws that shaped Africans' lives were the Pass Laws ($\checkmark$)  and the Group Areas Act (1950) ($\checkmark$). The Pass Laws restricted Africans from freely entering cities, and as a result they were blocked from better-paid urban jobs. This meant many families stayed trapped in poverty, showing how the law mainly benefited white employers by controlling cheap labour ($\checkmark$). The Group Areas Act of 1950 forced people into segregated townships far from their workplaces. Because of this, families and communities were broken apart, and workers had to spend more time and money travelling. Therefore, this law had both an economic and social impact, making daily life much harder ($\checkmark$).
