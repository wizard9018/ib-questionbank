# HI0269

With reference to origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source C for an historian studying the events at the Sharpeville Massacre. 

[4 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | The following extract comes from an article written by a journalist, Humphrey Tyler, who was present during the Sharpeville Massacre in 1960. He was the assistant editor of the anti-apartheid publication Drum magazine at the time of the shooting. 

[Source: Tyler, H. (March 21, 1960) *Eyewitness accounts of the Sharpeville Massacre 1960*. Retrieved January 18, 2025 from https://sahistory.org.za/article/eyewitness-accounts-sharpeville-massacre-1960. CC-BY-SA 4.0.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

A combination of the ideas below that meets the following guidelines:

 - Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.

 - Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any combination of the following: 

 - The source is a firsthand account from an observer at the Sharpeville Massacre, providing direct observations that add credibility to its claims.

 - Its purpose is to document the events and challenge the police's official narrative, making it valuable for offering a critical perspective on police actions.

 - The content includes specific details, such as the lack of warning shots, the sustained shooting, and the absence of weapons, which provide explicit evidence of the events.

For limitations, any combination of the following:: 

 - The observer's personal biases as an anti-apartheid journalist could influence their interpretation.

 - The intent to discredit the police may also lead to an unbalanced perspective, exaggerating the peacefulness of the crowd or minimizing any potential threat.

 - The source only reflects the author's viewpoint and does not account for the police's perspective or the broader context of the protest, limiting its comprehensiveness.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source C is valuable for a historian studying the Sharpeville Massacre because the origin is a firsthand account of an observer present at the scene ($\checkmark$ VO). The content provides detailed observations, such as the lack of weapons, the absence of warning shots, and the disproportionate force used, which highlight the excessive violence of the police ($\checkmark$ VC). However, the source has limitations. The purpose is to discredit the police, which could lead to selective reporting ($\checkmark$ LP), and the content only offers one perspective, omitting the police's view or events leading up to the massacre ($\checkmark$ LC).
