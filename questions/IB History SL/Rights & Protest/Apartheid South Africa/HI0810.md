# HI0810

Using evidence from Source A, analyze how the ANC justifies its planned civil disobedience campaign.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Extracts from a letter calling for the repeal of repressive legislation and threatening a “defiance” campaign from ANC leaders Dr. J. S. Moroka and W. M. Sisulu to Prime Minister D. F. Malan. Sent January 21, 1952. |

[Source: Moroka, J.S., Sisulu W. M. (1952, January 21). *EXCHANGE OF CORRESPONDENCE BETWEEN THE AFRICAN NATIONAL CONGRESS, THE SOUTH AFRICAN INDIAN CONGRESS, AND THE PRIME MINISTER OF THE UNION OF SOUTH AFRICA.* African National Congress. Retrieved May 30, 2025 from https://web.archive.org/web/20070714142402/http://www.anc.org.za/ancdocs/history/struggles/correspondence.html. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **four** of the following ways:
- The ANC emphasizes its long history of peaceful, constitutional attempts (40 years since 1912) to portray itself as patient and having exhausted all legal options before resorting to civil disobedience.
- The letter stresses the ANC's repeated offers of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$cooperation" and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$desire for peace, harmony and friendship" to present themselves as the responsible party seeking dialogue. At the same time, the government is portrayed as uncompromising.
- The ANC provides a specific deadline (February 29, 1952) and clear warning of consequences to demonstrate that it is acting reasonably by giving the government a final chance to avoid conflict.
- The letter explicitly states the struggle is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$not directed against any race or national group but against unjust laws" to present their actions as targeting discriminatory legislation rather than being motivated by racial hatred.
- The ANC references support from $\text{\textquotedblleft}$all enlightened and honest men and women, black and white, in our country and across the seas" to suggest their cause has broad moral backing and legitimacy.
- The letter blames the government for creating $\text{\textquotedblleft}$tension and crises" rather than anti-apartheid leaders, positioning the ANC as responding to government provocation rather than initiating conflict.
- The ANC presents its actions as being $\text{\textquotedblleft}$in the interest of all" sections of the population, suggesting that its campaign serves the greater good rather than narrow interests.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source A presents the ANC as reasonable and legitimate by emphasizing their extensive history of using $\text{\textquotedblleft}$constitutional methods" over $\text{\textquotedblleft}$forty years" to demonstrate they had exhausted all legal avenues before considering civil disobedience ($\checkmark$). The letter also presents their approach as legitimate by highlighting their repeated demonstrations of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$willingness and readiness to cooperate with the Government" and their $\text{\textquotedblleft}$sincere desire for peace, harmony and friendship," positioning the ANC as the responsible party seeking dialogue, while the government rejected these efforts ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the ANC presents their planned campaign as reasonable by providing the government with a specific ultimatum and deadline of $\text{\textquotedblleft}$29th day of February, 1952" to repeal discriminatory acts, showing they were giving a final opportunity for peaceful resolution rather than acting hastily ($\checkmark$). Finally, the letter presents the campaign as legitimate by explicitly stating that their struggle is $\text{\textquotedblleft}$not directed against any race or national group but against unjust laws," demonstrating that their actions target discriminatory legislation rather than being motivated by racial animosity ($\checkmark$).
