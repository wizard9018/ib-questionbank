# HI1015

Using the information in Source B, explain how apartheid laws affected the Church's ability to provide welfare and services.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** | $\hspace{3em}$ | An extract from the 1972 SPRO-CAS Church Commission Report, edited by Peter Randall, which analysed how apartheid laws restricted the work of Christian churches in South Africa. |

[Source: Randall, P. (Ed.). (1972). *Apartheid and the Church: Report of the Church Commission of the Study Project on Christianity in Apartheid Society (SPRO-CAS Publication No. 8).* SPRO-CAS / Christian Institute of Southern Africa, pp.12,13. Retrieved September 10, 2025, from https://sahistory.org.za/sites/default/files/archive-files3/rep19720000.037.052.008.pdf Public domain.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following with supporting explanation:
- Apartheid created material need (poverty, broken families, lack of accommodation), increasing demands on Churches.
- Racial separation made it difficult for white congregations to see or help African communities.
- Laws restricted Church access to resettlement camps, prisons, and detainees.
- Welfare organisations faced government pressure to stop multi-racial work, threatening subsidies.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source explains that apartheid laws increased the Church's welfare burden by creating “breakdown of African family life” through the migrant labour system and “poverty of Africans in resettlement camps” ($\checkmark$). These conditions meant there were “great material and spiritual need,” so the Church had to devote more “manpower and money” to helping people, making its welfare role much harder ($\checkmark$).

It also shows that the state directly limited the Church's work by restricting access to those who needed help. The report mentions that the Church had “difficulty in obtaining access to these resettlement camps” and that it was “denied access to detainees” held under security laws ($\checkmark$). This meant the Church could not easily reach prisoners or people in camps, severely reducing its ability to provide service where it was most needed ($\checkmark$).
