# HI0809

What, according to Source A, were the consequences of government policies on black South Africans?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Extracts from a letter calling for the repeal of repressive legislation and threatening a “defiance” campaign from ANC leaders Dr. J. S. Moroka and W. M. Sisulu to Prime Minister D. F. Malan. Sent January 21, 1952. |

[Source: Moroka, J.S., Sisulu W. M. (1952, January 21). *EXCHANGE OF CORRESPONDENCE BETWEEN THE AFRICAN NATIONAL CONGRESS, THE SOUTH AFRICAN INDIAN CONGRESS, AND THE PRIME MINISTER OF THE UNION OF SOUTH AFRICA.* African National Congress. Retrieved May 30, 2025 from https://web.archive.org/web/20070714142402/http://www.anc.org.za/ancdocs/history/struggles/correspondence.html. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Gradual worsening of the social, economic and political position of the black South Africans
- Increase in racial bitterness and tension in South African society
- Black South Africans were deprived of fundamental human rights enjoyed in democratic communities
- They were insulted and degraded through discriminatory legislation
- Opposition organizations were being suppressed
- Economic position of the people was being eroded
- Creation of a reservoir of cheap labour for farms and gold mines
- Black South Africans were being prevented from obtaining full nationhood
- Humiliation of black South Africans in various ways
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
According to Source A, government policies caused the social, economic, and political conditions for black South Africans to steadily deteriorate ($\checkmark$). The letter also speaks about increased racial tensions and hostility across the country ($\checkmark$). In addition, the source states that black South Africans faced discrimination and were denied basic rights that would typically be guaranteed in democratic societies ($\checkmark$).
