# HI0228Q2

What does Source C suggest about opposition to apartheid policies?

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source C** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Photo taken at an anti-apartheid protest in The Hague, The Netherlands. The banners read: $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Against Apartheid,” $\text{\textquotedblleft}$One Race of Men,” $\text{\textquotedblleft}$One People Humanity,” $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Equal rights for black and white,” $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Black, white, together one,” and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Luns, protest” (a reference to Joseph Luns, the Dutch Minister of Foreign Affairs at the time).

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - That the anti-apartheid struggle had an international element

 - That many people in the Netherlands/Holland were outspoken about opposition to apartheid

 - That one of the primary messages of the protest was equal rights among races

 - That the protestors wanted Joseph Luns, the Dutch Minister of Foreign Affairs at the time, to take action

\

[2 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Source C suggests that the opposition to apartheid extended outside of the country as well, as this protest is taking place in The Hague, Netherlands ($\checkmark$). The source also suggests that the protestors felt it was the responsibility of their own government to take action. This is shown by the signs that read $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Luns, protest!” in reference to Joseph Luns, the Dutch Minister of Foreign Affairs ($\checkmark$).
