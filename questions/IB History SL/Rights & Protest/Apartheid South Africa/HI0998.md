# HI0998

What, according to Source A, was the importance of the participation of Rusty Bernstein in the Congress of the People?

[3 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source A** $\\$ $\hspace{5em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Extract from the book $\textit{\textquotedblleft}$The Final Prize" by Norman Levy and published in 2013. Levy opposed Bantu Education and promoted alternative learning for black students during apartheid. He was a member of the Congress of the People, and also contributed to founding the Congress of Democrats, a white anti-apartheid organization, also integrated by Rusty Bernstein. 

:box[

(…) the idea of a Congress of the People (COP) at which the delegates would adopt a Freedom Charter which would assert the demand for civil rights for all South Africans, seemed to be the appropriate next step. (…) The leadership believed that this was an objective (...) about which $\text{\textquotedblleft}$all levels of the congress membership were passionate”. That this assessment was accurate was to some extent manifested in (…) the language of the campaign. This was essentially the genius of Rusty Bernstein who (…) reached out to the masses with a quality of prose that matched the expectations of the occasion. Accordingly, the Freedom Charter helped to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mobilize the people up and down the land and awaken an echo \[for freedom\] in their hearts”. (…)

\

\

This excitement was captured in $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Call to the Congress of the People”, a passionate document in the format of a leaflet, drafted (…) by Rusty Bernstein. (…) Rusty was obviously touched by the emotions that the idea of the COP had aroused. He was not by nature overtly passionate (…). $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Call” (…) was an exception, an indication of the extent to which Bernstein was personally inspired by the egalitarian idealism of the action. Its stirring message reached out $\text{\textquotedblleft}$to brothers without land and children without schooling”; to the farmers in the reserves; to the miners $\text{\textquotedblleft}$in the dark shafts and cold compounds, far from their families”; and to the farm workers and workers in the factories. It called on them to speak of long hours, housing and pass laws, of taxes and of cattle-culling and of famine, ending with the repetitive refrain:

$\text{\textquotedblleft}$Let us speak together, all of us together - African and European, Indian and Coloured. Voter and voteless. Privileged and rightless. The happy and the homeless. Let us speak of freedom.”

]

[Source: Levy\, N. (2015, June 18). *The Final Prize by Norman Levy. South African History Online.* Retrieved August 2, 2025, from https://sahistory.org.za/article/freedomcharter-norman-levy. CC.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following:

 - Rusty Bernstein's participation signified a commitment to inclusivity and unity across racial lines.

 - Bernstein played a key role, as a white leader, in uniting South Africans for a common cause.

 - He authored influential material that inspired support and collective action against apartheid.

 - Bernstein's leadership and rhetoric helped transform the Freedom Charter into a symbol of hope and solidarity.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

According to source A, Rusty Bernstein, as part of a racial minority group, played a key role in uniting South Africans of all races, calling to $\text{\textquotedblleft}$African and European, Indian and Coloured” and demonstrating that the movement should go beyond racial profiling to focus on the shared goal of fighting for the freedoms and rights everyone deserved equally. ($\checkmark$)

In his $\text{\textquotedblleft}$The Call to the Congress of the People," Bernstein used persuasive language that connected with all social and racial groups. His ability to emphasize the struggles of the marginalized and to call for collective action with his refrain $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Let us speak together, all of us together. Let us speak of freedom” made the Congress of the People a powerful force in the fight for racial unity against apartheid. ($\checkmark$)

Through his leadership skills and unifying rhetoric -as can be seen in source A-, Rusty Bernstein played a vital role in turning the Freedom Charter into a document that symbolized hope for all South Africans, and $\text{\textquotedblleft}$mobilized the people up and down the land”, awakening $\text{\textquotedblleft}$an echo for freedom in their hearts”. ($\checkmark$)
