# HI0259

What does Source D suggest about the ideals of the apartheid resistance movement in the 1950s? 

[2 marks]

$\\$

| | | |

| :-- | :-- | :-- |

|**Source** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | An extract from the 1956 Freedom Charter. 

[Source: South African History Online. (November 4, 2013). *Notes on meeting with Mr. Sam Nujoma*. Retrieved January 26, 2025 from https://sahistory.org.za/archive/note-meeting-mr-sam-nujoma-1983. CC-BY-SA 4.0.]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following:

 - The extract suggests the apartheid resistance movement in the 1950s was grounded in the belief that South Africa should belong equally to all its people, regardless of race.

 - The extract demonstrates a strong commitment to democratic principles, stating that legitimate governance must be based on the collective will of all citizens.

 - The extract emphasises unity between black and white South Africans, with a shared pledge to work together towards achieving democratic reforms.

 [2 marks]

 $\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The extract suggests that the apartheid resistance movement in the 1950s was founded on the principle of non-racialism, declaring that $\text{\textquotedblleft}$South Africa belongs to all who live in it, black and white" ($\checkmark$). It reflects a commitment to equality and democracy, asserting that governance must be "based on the will of all the people" ($\checkmark$).
