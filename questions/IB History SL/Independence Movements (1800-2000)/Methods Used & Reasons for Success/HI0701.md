# HI0701

Describe the methods used by Gandhi to resist British rule during the Non-Cooperation Campaign of 1920-22 and explain their impact.

[1 mark]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

For methods, the following or similar:

 - Gandhi supported nonviolent resistance based on the concept of Satyagraha, or $\text{\textquotedblleft}$soul force", which states that ordinary people can use peaceful means to fight for justice. 

 - This involved a boycott of British schools, universities, law courts, and British goods. 

\

[1 marks]

For impacts, any **four** of the following:

 - Thousands of students left schools and colleges to attend $\text{\textquotedblleft}$national schools" set up outside the government system.

 - Protestors picketed shops that sold British textiles and made bonfires of foreign clothes. 

 - The visit of the Prince of Wales to India in 1921 was met with empty streets and official receptions held for him were boycotted by Indians . 

 - The British arrested 20,000 protestors.

 - The protests turned violent in Bombay resulting in the death of demonstrators. A violent mob in the village of Chauri Chaura killed 22 Indian policemen in February 1922, by setting the police station alight. 

 - This led Gandhi to call off the Non-Cooperation Campaign as he felt the people were not ready to apply the tactics of Satyagraha as intended. 

 - Gandhi was arrested, charged with sedition and sentenced to six years in prison. He was released after two years for health reasons. He now abstained from political activity till 1929 and focused instead on fighting for greater rights for those adversely impacted by the caste system in India. 

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The Non-Cooperation Movement, launched by Gandhi in 1920 to resist British rule, was based on non-violence. This method relied on Gandhi's teaching that ordinary people could use peaceful means to bring about change, including the boycott of British schools, universities, law courts, and goods ($\checkmark$). 

The Non-Cooperation Campaign involved the boycott of shops that sold British textiles, with protestors making bonfires of British cloth and having an adverse economic impact on British businessmen in India ($\checkmark$). 

Indians conveyed their displeasure with British rule during the visit of the Prince of Wales to India in 1921. The Prince was greeted with empty streets and Indians boycotted official receptions held for him, in a bid to take their message of non-cooperation crystal clear to the British government. In addition, the boycott was seen as a humiliation for the British Empire ($\checkmark$).

The protests turned violent in Bombay and resulted in the deaths of people demonstrating. The British used force against the protestors and arrested 20,000 Indians.  Violence also broke out in the village of Chauri Chaura, where a mob set fire to the police station, killing 22 Indian policemen ($\checkmark$). 

The violence at Chauri Chaura led Gandhi to call off the Non-Cooperation movement as he felt people were not ready to apply the principles of non-violence as required for Satyagraha. Gandhi was arrested and sentenced to six years in prison on the charges of sedition ($\checkmark$).
