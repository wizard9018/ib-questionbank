# HI0553

According to Source A, what did Spain do to maintain control of Cuba?
[3 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source A** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ |  Herminio Portell-Vilá (1901-1992) was a professor at the University of Havana, where he once taught a young Fidel Castro.   He left the country for Miami shortly after the Revolution, where he worked for the Voice of America, was Director of the American Security Council, and continued to write his historical studies. |

:box[The revolutionary effort of Cubans to achieve their independence was prolonged for much more time than should have been necessary, and it required far more heroism and greater sacrifices than that of any other people of the Americas. During the course of nearly one hundred years, Cuba was in continuous political tumult, marked by both smaller, abortive conspiracies and larger-scale attempts to throw off the Spanish colonial yoke. Because Spain had lost the majority of its New World dominions during the first third of the nineteenth century, it had learned its lesson well and went on to concentrate all remaining resources in Cuba, along with an intensified hatred of republicanism and all that was American. With jealous zeal, the Spanish Crown’s most characteristic strategy was to keep Cubans out of the armed services and to render it impossible for their sons to become sailors. The imperial government dared not manufacture any guns, sabres, or munitions on the island itself; all weaponry had to be brought to Cuba from abroad through official Spanish channels. Its aim was to maintain domination over a docile and disarmed population. Cubans should be kept unfamiliar with the technical knowledge of warfare and lack access to such maritime communication as would be indispensable for any successful rebellion. This imperial monopoly over the trade in arms and explosives characterized a regime of externally-controlled defense that left the average Cuban to his own devices when personally threatened or when contemplating a rebellion. For example, the machete was a key tool for agricultural workers, but in the hands of the *mambises* [a colloquial name for Cuban revolutionaries], it could be turned into a terrible weapon.]

[Source: Portell Vilá, Herminio. (2010, March). John O'Brien: *Captain Dynamite of the Cuban War for Independence, 1868-1898.*  Irish Migration Studies in Latin America, Volume 7, No. 3. Retrieved May 7, 2025 from https://www.irlandeses.org/1003portellvila.htm.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Spain developed a strong hostility toward republican ideas and American influence, which it actively resisted in Cuba
- The Spanish Crown deliberately excluded Cubans from military service and naval opportunities to keep them inexperienced in warfare
- Spain maintained strict control over weapons manufacturing and importation—no arms were produced in Cuba, and all had to come through Spanish channels
- The Cuban population was deliberately kept unarmed and unfamiliar with military technology to prevent effective rebellion
- Spain limited Cubans' access to maritime communication, further hindering coordination and the spread of revolutionary ideas
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Spain attempted to prohibit Cuban independence by squashing ideologies and influences that were viewed as threats, such as republicanism and the influence of the US ($\checkmark$). They also excluded Cubans from any military service or experience to keep them ignorant of warfare ($\checkmark$).  Lastly, Spain imported all weapons so that Cubans would not know how to make them or use them effectively ($\checkmark$).
