# HI0549

Compare and contrast the roles and significance of Máximo Gómez and Calixto García in the Cuban Independence Movement.
[6 marks]

---

For comparison, any of the following similarities with supporting evidence:
- Both were veterans of all three Wars of Independence, which earned them considerable support and respect, as well as strategic experience and deep knowledge of guerrilla warfare in Cuban terrain.
- Both were among the most experienced military leaders. They both held high-ranking military positions: Gómez served as a General in Chief, and García was a major General and commander in the East.
- They were both deeply committed to the cause of independence; Both made personal sacrifices for independence. García was captured and attempted suicide to avoid dishonor; Gómez fought for Cuban freedom, though he was from the Dominican Republic.
- Both were seen as symbols of the resistance and respected at home in Cuba and internationally. They represented the struggle for independence from Spanish imperialism throughout Latin America.

For contrast, any of the following differences with supporting evidence:
- Different origins: Máximo Gómez was born in the Dominican Republic and fought for Cuban Independence as a foreigner. He saw Cuba's struggle as part of a larger fight against imperialism. Calixto García came from an established Creole-Cuban family with a long history of rebellion. Both his father and grandfather fought against Spanish imperialism.
Contrasting military strategies: Máximo Gómez was known for his scorched earth policy and his determination to economically weaken Spain, whereas Calixto García focused more on strategic battles and guerrilla warfare.
- Differing roles in diplomacy: Máximo Gómez was focused on military command, while Calixto Garcia was more focused on coordination with the US during the Spanish-American War.
- Differing relations/perspectives of the US: Máximo Gómez was wary of working with the US, whereas Calixto García advocated for more cooperation with the US, particularly in the future after the war.
\
[6 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Máximo Gómez and Calixto García played significant roles in the Cuban Independence Movement, marked by shared military experience, deep commitment, and widespread respect. Both men were veterans of all three wars for independence ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$), which provided them with battlefield experience and intimate knowledge of Cuba’s terrain ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Each held a significant military command ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$). Gómez became General-in-Chief of the Cuban Liberation Army, while García commanded key forces in eastern Cuba as a major general ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Beyond their tactical war experience, both figures came to represent the Cuban resistance and were admired both within Cuba and by pro-independence supporters across Latin America ($\checkmark\, \text{similarity}$).

Despite their shared goals, the two leaders differed in background, strategy, and international engagement. Máximo Gómez, born in the Dominican Republic, was an outsider by nationality ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) and viewed Cuba's independence as part of a broader anti-imperialist/anti-Spanish struggle ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). In contrast, Calixto García was Cuban-born, from a Creole family deeply rooted in rebellion ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$) as both his father and grandfather had taken part in earlier uprisings against Spain ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$). Strategically, Gómez favored a scorched-earth approach ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$), destroying sugar plantations to destabilize Spanish power economically ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$), whereas García concentrated on targeted guerrilla battles and territorial gains, particularly in eastern Cuba ($\checkmark\, \text{difference and evidence}$). Their relationships with the United States were also different ($\checkmark\, \text{difference}$). Gómez remained cautious of U.S. intentions in Cuba ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$), while García actively collaborated with American forces during the Spanish-American War, hoping for support in the island’s transition to independence ($\checkmark\, \text{evidence}$).
