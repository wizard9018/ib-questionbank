# HI0484

Explain **two** impacts of guerrilla tactics in the Cuban War of Independence.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following with supporting evidence:

 - Maximize mobility 

     - Invasion of the West - Led by Máximo Gómez and Antonio Maceo in 1895-96, this campaign moved rebel forces over 1,800 kilometers from eastern to western Cuba, covering nearly the entire island. 

     - The rebels moved in small, fast-moving columns on horseback, often with minimal supplies, enabling them to evade larger Spanish forces.

 - Strike Unpredictably

     - Rebel fighters launched surprise attacks on Spanish patrols, supply lines, and small garrisons, often melting back into the countryside before reinforcements could arrive.

    - Rebels used mountain passes, dense forests, and sugarcane fields to hide movements and strike from unexpected directions.

    - In places like the Sierra Maestra and Camagüey plains, Cuban forces had a tactical advantage through local knowledge

 - Control rural areas

    - The Cuban rebels drew support from rural peasants, former slaves, and small farmers, who provided food, shelter, and intelligence.

    - By burning sugar plantations and mills, the rebels not only damaged Spain's economic interests but also drove Spanish troops out of rural production zones

    - Established hidden trails and supply lines across the countryside, allowing them to move between provinces and coordinate regional offensives

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

Guerrilla tactics allowed rebels to strike unpredictably because they were familiar with the terrain and could use forests and mountain passes ($\checkmark$). Specifically, knowledge of the Sierra Maestra proved advantageous to rebels in their use of guerrilla tactics ($\checkmark$). Additionally, the guerrilla tactics allowed the rebels to control the rural areas ($\checkmark$). The Spanish presence was often more significant in the cities, and the rebels knew the locals who might provide support and unique knowledge that the Spanish could not ascertain ($\checkmark$).
