# HI0704.5

Describe **four** impacts of religious divisions within the Nationalist movement.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **four** of the following:

 - The Viceroy's decision to declare Indian involvement in the Second World War, resulted in the resignation of the provincial Congress governments, which the League declared a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$day of deliverance". The League also declared its  support for the British war effort. 

 - In March 1940,the League passed the Lahore resolution calling for creating a separate Muslim state. Jinnah in his speech, stated that Hindus and Muslims were two distinct and irreconcilable communities as justification for the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Nation" theory. 

 - Jinnah accepted the British offer of delayed independence in return for support during the war, put forward by the Cripps Mission in 1942. He however demanded a two-state solution after independence . 

 - At the Simla Conference arranged by the British in June 1945, to discuss the future of post-war India, the Viceroy Wavell proposed an equal representation of Hindus and Muslims on the Executive Council to function as an interim government and plan future constitutional developments. Jinnah's insistence that all Muslim members had to be from the League, resulted in the failure of the Conference.  

 - In the 1946 elections for the central and provincial assemblies, the Congress won 90$\%$ of the $\text{\textquotedblleft}$open seats" in the central legislature and a majority in 8 of the 11 provinces. The Muslim League won all 30 seats in the central legislature, reserved for Muslim voters and 442 of the 500 reserved seats in the provincial legislatures. This strengthened Jinnah's claim that the Muslim League represented all Muslims in India. 

 - The proposals put forward by the Cabinet Mission in March 1946 to facilitate the transfer of power failed after Muslim League withdrew its initial acceptance of the proposals, due to disagreement with the Congress stand which viewed the proposals as transitional rather than fixed. 

 - The League reiterated its call for the creation of Pakistan as a separate state and Jinnah called for $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Direct Action" in support of the League's demand for partition. $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Direct Action Day" on August 16, 1946 resulted in rioting and widespread communal violence that spread from Calcutta to other parts of India. The $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Great Calcutta Killing" resulted in 5000 deaths and 20,000 injured. The inclusion of members of the Muslim League in the interim government failed to stop the violence.

 - The Congress reluctantly came to accept that partition was the only viable solution. The last Viceroy of India, Lord Mountbatten, arrived in February 1947 to oversee the transfer of power by June 1948. He brought this forward to August 1947. The partition resulted in violence and a refugee crisis with more than 5,000,000 Hindus, Muslims, and Sikhs fleeing from their homes.

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

The religious divisions within the Nationalist movement was apparent when the Muslim League declared a $\text{\textquotedblleft}$day of deliverance", on the resignation of Provincial Congress governments protesting against the unilateral declaration of the Viceroy of Indian participation in the Second World War.The League also declared its  support for the British war effort ($\checkmark$).

These divisions were further reinforced when in March 1940,the League passed the Lahore resolution for the creation of a separate Muslim state. The $\text{\textquotedblleft}$Two Nation" theory was put forward by Jinnah in his speech, stating that Hindus and Muslims were two distinct and irreconcilable communities  ($\checkmark$). 

The acceptance by Jinnah and the Muslim League of the British offer of delayed independence in return for support during the war, proposed by the Cripps Mission in 1942 further highlighted the differences with the Congress rejection of these proposals. The Muslim League however demanded a two-state solution after independence in return for its support ($\checkmark$). 

The League's stand at the Simla Conference arranged by the British in June 1945, to discuss the future of post-war India, prevented any resolution of the widening divisions between the parties. Jinnah's insistence that all Muslim members on the Executive Council of the Viceroy, that was to function as an interim government and plan future constitutional developments, had to be only from the League, resulted in the failure of the Conference ($\checkmark$).
