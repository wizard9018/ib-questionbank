# HI0754

Describe the impacts of Communalism on post-independence India.
[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **five** of the following:
- The rise of communalism (politicization of religious identities) threatened this principle of secularism adopted in the Indian constitution. Post-independence, groups like the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) and the Bharatiya Jan Sangh (BJS) promoted the idea of India as a Hindu nation.
- Gandhi was assassinated in January 1948 by Godse, a young Hindu extremist and active supporter of the communalist RSS, opposed to the creation of a secular state in India. The shock of Gandhi's death strengthened the position of secularists in government and helped calm communal tension immediately after independence. In the 1952 general elections, the BJS won only 3% of the vote.
- The BJS was succeeded by the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) in 1980 as the main Hindu nationalist party and emerged as a powerful force in Indian politics. It promoted the creation of a state modelled on Hindu beliefs and culture. The movement was stridently anti-Muslim and triggered communal riots.
- In 1992, Hindu extremists demolished a mosque in Ayodhya, claiming it was built on one of the holiest Hindu sites. This resultant violence led to the deaths of 3000 people.
- The 1998 general elections saw the defeat of the Congress and the emergence of the BJP, riding on the support from increasingly communalised politics, as the largest single party.
- Rising Hindu nationalism has led to increased insecurity among religious minorities, especially Muslims and Christians. Hindu nationalism has blurred the lines between religion and state, raising questions about minority rights. Hindu nationalist influence has led to attempts to rewrite history textbooks, emphasizing ancient Hindu glories and minimizing contributions from Muslim rulers or other communities.
\
[5 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The rise of communalism (politicization of religious identities) in the post-independence years with groups like the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) and the Bharatiya Jan Sangh (BJS) who promoted the idea of India as a Hindu nation, threatened secular India ($\checkmark$). The initial impact of this rising communalism resulted in Gandhi's assasination in January 1948 by Godse, a young Hindu extremist and active supporter of the RSS who was opposed to the creation of a secular state in India ($\checkmark$).

Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) in 1980, succeeded the BJS as the main Hindu nationalist party and soon emerged as a powerful force in Indian politics ($\checkmark$). It campaigned to promote the creation of a state modelled on Hindu beliefs and culture, with a movement that was stridently anti-Muslim and which frequently triggered communal riots in different parts of India ($\checkmark$).  As a result of increasing communalism in politics, the BJP emerged as the largest single party in the 1998 general elections after defeating the Congress ($\checkmark$). Communalism promoted the rise of Hindu nationalism, blurring the lines between religion and state in India as well as raising questions about minority rights.
