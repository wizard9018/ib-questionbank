# HI0753

Describe **three** problems related to initial territorial consolidation faced by the new Indian state.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- At independence, over 560 princely states existed, each with varying degrees of autonomy. The Indian government led efforts to integrate them into the Indian Union through persuasion and, in some cases, force. Most acceded peacefully and rulers of princely states were asked to sign the Instrument of Accession, which formally recognized their state's merger with the Indian Union.
- Resistance from some large princely states created major issues.
- The states of Hyderabad and Junagadh had Muslim princes ruling over large Hindu populations. Hyderabad refused to join and were annexed to India by force while Junagadh joined after 91% of the population voted to join India in a plebescite held in 1948.
- Kashmir was strategically placed with a Hindu prince and a predominantly Muslim population. Initially hesitant to join India, an armed uprising followed by a Pakistani-backed tribal invasion, forced the ruler Hari Singh to seek help from India and sign the Instrument of Accession on October 26, 1947. India sent troops to Kashmir to counter the invasion, marking the beginning of the first Indo-Pakistan War; the start of an ongoing dispute.
- Pondicherry was handed over by France in 1954. Portugal continued to occupy Goa, which was invaded by the Indian army and united with India in 1961.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The newly independent Indian state was faced with the challenge of incorporating over 560 princely states that existed within the borders of the former British Indian state. These states had varying degrees of autonomy and the Indian government moved to successfully integrate most of them through persuasion and by offering to financially compensate them. These rulers signed the Instrument of Accession formally recognising their merger with the Indian Union ($\checkmark$).

However, there was resistance from the Muslim rulers of Hyderabad and Junagad; states with large Hindu populations, who refused to join the newly independent Indian state. The ruler of Junagad agreed to join in February 1948, following a plebiscite, where 91% of the population voted to join India. The state of Hyderabad was annexed to India by force in September 1948 when negotiations with the Nizam of Hyderabad failed ($\checkmark$).

Kashmir was to prove another challenge as the state was strategically placed and had a predominantly Muslim population. It was ruled by a Hindu prince who was initially resistant to joining India. An armed uprising within the state followed by a Pakistani-backed tribal invasion, forced the ruler Hari Singh to seek help from India and to sign the Instrument of Accession on October 26, 1947. India sent troops to Kashmir to counter the invasion, marking the beginning of the first Indo-Pakistan War; the start of an ongoing dispute that has still not been resolved ($\checkmark$).
