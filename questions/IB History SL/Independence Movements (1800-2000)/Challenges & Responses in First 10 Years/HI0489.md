# HI0489

Examine **two** impacts of the US on Cuba's economy in the first ten years of Independence.

[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** of the following impacts: 

 - Sugar industry owned by the US

 - US land ownership

 - US control over resources

 - US banking and financial control

 - Unemployment 

 - Labour exploitation

 - Lack of industrial development

\

[2 marks]

Any **four** of the following explanations connected to the relevant impact: 

 - The reliance on sugar as the main crop led to a mono-economy

 - The sugar industry was seasonal leaving many Cubans unemployed in the off season with no alternative work

 - The US controlled not only land but also the infrastructure needed for trade which further made Cuba dependent on the US

 - Profits from the infrastructure and the sale of natural resources did not remain in Cuba for further investment but rather went back to the US

 - The US made Cuba reliant on investment from the US since the US controlled the banks

 - The Cuban peso was tied to the US dollar giving stability but also allowing the US to control Cuba's monetary policies

 - Many Cuban workers faced horrible working conditions in US owned industry.

 - Many US companies preferred to hire foreigners over Cubans for higher positions in the US owned industries

 - The Reciprocity Treaty of 1903 gave Cuban sugar preferential access to U.S. markets, but in return, U.S. goods flooded Cuban markets, making it hard for local industries to develop.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One major issue was that the U.S. controlled vast amounts of Cuban land  ($\checkmark$), including sugar plantations, railroads, and trade infrastructure. This control made Cuba economically dependent on the U.S. since American companies owned the land and controlled the infrastructure needed for trade  ($\checkmark$). As a result, Cuba had little control over its own economic growth and was vulnerable to U.S. decisions regarding trade and investment ($\checkmark$). Additionally, profits from the sale of natural resources and infrastructure did not remain in Cuba for reinvestment but instead flowed back to the U.S., preventing economic development ($\checkmark$).

Another key factor was U.S. banking and financial control ($\checkmark$), which made Cuba reliant on U.S. investment. Since the U.S. controlled the banks, Cuba depended on American loans and financial policies, limiting its ability to establish independent economic strategies ($\checkmark$). Furthermore, the Cuban peso was tied to the U.S. dollar, providing stability, but also allowing the U.S. to dictate Cuba's monetary policies ($\checkmark$). This meant that Cuba's financial system was not truly independent, making it difficult to develop a strong, self-sufficient economy.
