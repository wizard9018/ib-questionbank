# HI0492

List **three** social challenges Cuba faced in the first ten years of Cuban Independence.

[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **three** of the following: 

 - Despite Afro-Cubans playing a significant role in the wars of independence, they faced widespread discrimination and were largely excluded from political and economic opportunities.

 - Many Afro-Cubans were denied government jobs and faced barriers to land ownership and education.

 - The Afro-Cuban Rebellion of 1912: the Cuban government violently suppressed demands for racial equality.

 - Cuba's economy was heavily reliant on sugar production, which was seasonal, leaving many workers unemployed during the off-season.

 - Poor wages and lack of alternative industries led to economic hardship, particularly for rural workers and former soldiers who struggled to find stable employment.

 - Many Cubans lived in poverty, while wealth remained concentrated among landowners and foreign investors.

 - Corruption and election fraud led to political instability, causing uprisings and protests among dissatisfied groups.

 - The U.S. military intervened in 1906-1909, further disrupting Cuban society and limiting the country's ability to govern itself.

 - Many Cubans felt their independence was incomplete due to continued U.S. influence and intervention, leading to growing nationalist resentment.

\

[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

One challenge to Cuba was the discrimination that Afro-Cubans faced even after having contributed greatly to the cause of independence; they were treated less than other Cubans  ($\checkmark$).  Another difference was that since the economy was based on sugar, which is a seasonal crop, for much of the year, many sugar workers were unemployed  ($\checkmark$).  Finally, there was widespread corruption among the political elites, and this caused protests and uprisings ($\checkmark$).
