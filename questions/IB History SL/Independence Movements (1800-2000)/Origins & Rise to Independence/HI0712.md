# HI0712

Describe **three** reasons for the reversal of the Partition of Bengal in 1911.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The anti-partition movement protestors organised a boycott of British goods, which became known as the Swadeshi (self-sufficiency) movement. This resulted in a 25% drop of British imports into India and helped expand Indian industries.
- Confrontation over Bengal radicalised parts of the Congress, and a revolutionary wing called the New Party emerged under the leadership of Bal Gangadhar Tilak, which urged active opposition to British rule.
- The Morley-Minto reforms were implemented in the Indian Councils Act of 1909, by which Indians could elect representatives to sit on the Viceroy's council. The provincial councils were enlarged and given more power, and two Indians were nominated to serve on the Council for India in London, which advised the Secretary of State.
- The 1910 elections for the central and provincial legislative councils gave the Muslims separate representation with separate electorates and reserved seats. Economic and educational qualifications determined the right to vote for all Indians.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The reversal of the Partition of Bengal in 1911 resulted from increasing opposition, which had economic and political repercussions that threatened to weaken British rule.

The reversal of the Partition of Bengal was a result of the adverse economic impact on Britain from the boycott of British goods in India along with the promotion of the Swadeshi or self-sufficiency movement.  The anti-partition movement organised a boycott of British goods and spread the message of self-sufficiency to encourage the use of Indian goods. The Swadeshi (self-sufficiency) movement impacted Britain's economy adversely as it resulted in a 25% drop in British imports into India and helped expand Indian industries. The movement also revealed the power of economic boycotts, which soon became a cornerstone of the Indian National Congress's policy ($\checkmark$).

Another reason for the reversal of the partition was the British fear of destabilisation of their control in India from the increasing use of more violent revolutionary tactics. The protests against the partition led to the emergence of leaders in the Indian National Congress who supported the use of such revolutionary tactics against British rule. A revolutionary wing of the Congress led them, called the New Party, which emerged under the leadership of Bal Gangadhar Tilak, who urged active opposition to British rule ($\checkmark$).

Finally, the reversal of the Partition was prompted by British fears of losing control over India's army. British attempts to crush the protests resulted in greater opposition with fears that Indian soldiers in the British army in Punjab were about to go on strike. The threat of destabilisation of British rule prompted the Secretary of State for India, John Morley, and the Viceroy Minto to implement concessions to retain British control of the subcontinent. The Morley-Minto reforms or the Indian Councils Act of 1909 permitted Indian elected representatives to sit on the Viceroy's council, the enlarged provincial councils were given more power, and two Indians were nominated to serve on the Council for India in London, who advised the Secretary of State. Finally, the partition was reversed in 1911 and the capital of British India moved from Calcutta to Delhi ($\checkmark$).
