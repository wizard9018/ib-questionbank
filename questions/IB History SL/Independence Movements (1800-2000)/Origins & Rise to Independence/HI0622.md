# HI0622

Explain **two** steps Britain took to retain control of India following the First World War.
[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **two** of the following:
- In August 1917, Edwin Montagu, the new Secretary of State, together with Lord Chelmsford, the Viceroy, announced the British government's intention to encourage the gradual development of self-governing institutions for the progressive realisation of $\lq$responsible government' in India. This was done to calm the nationalist agitation calling for increased self-government and ensure continued control of India.
- The British government passed the Rowlatt Acts of March 1919 based on the recommendations of the Rowlatt Commission that stated in its report that $\lq$an under-manned police force was bound to be overwhelmed once wartime legislation lapsed'.  The Rowlatt Acts provided for continuing wartime controls over political activities, including detention without trial, censorship, and house arrest. Normal judicial procedures, such as trial by jury, were abolished for political offences. This gave the British continued extraordinary powers and allowed them to bypass normal legal channels to quell any nationalist opposition.
- The Montagu-Chelmsford report, published in July 1918, proposed a system of $\lq$dyarchy' (dual government) where the British-appointed central government in Delhi would control key areas of government such as foreign policy, defence, and taxation. Indians only had advisory power here. The elected provincial governments would only control education, health, and economic development. The right to vote based on property and income qualifications extended only to 1.5 million of India's total population of 300 million. With the right to decision-making retained in British hands, Indians lacked any real influence on matters that influenced the entire country. In addition, the restricted right to vote strengthened British control as the upper classes were less inclined to push for drastic change.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
At the end of the First World War, Britain attempted to retain control of India with the promise of greater self-governance to calm the rising demands of Indian nationalists. This was outlined in the Montagu-Chelmsford report published in July 1918. The report proposed a system of dual government ($\checkmark$) in which the British-appointed central government based in Delhi held control of key government elements such as defence, taxation, and foreign policy. In contrast, the elected provincial governments controlled the provinces' education, health, and economic development. The provincial governments were to be elected, and the right to vote was based on property and income qualifications, restricting the vote to 1.5 million of the total population of 300 million ($\checkmark$).

Before the British Parliament passed the Montague-Chelmsford reforms as the Government of India Act in November 1919, the British passed the Rowlatt Acts in March 1919 ($\checkmark$). This was done to retain control using force, as the Rowlatt Commission highlighted the dangers of an under-manned police force in India, which it said was bound to be overwhelmed once wartime legislation lapsed. The Act allowed the British to continue wartime controls over political activities such as detention without trial, censorship, and house arrest. It also set aside normal judicial procedures, such as trial by jury for political offences  ($\checkmark$).
