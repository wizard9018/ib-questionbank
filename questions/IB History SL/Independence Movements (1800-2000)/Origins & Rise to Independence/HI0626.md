# HI0626

With reference to its origin, purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of Source B for an historian studying the impact of the Partition of Bengal on the Nationalist movement in India.
[4 marks]

$\\$

| |  | |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
|**Source B** $\hspace{3em}$ | $\hspace{3em}$ | Bipin Chandra Pal, Indian nationalist, journalist and social reformer in his book, $\lq$The Spirit of Indian Nationalism'. Published by *The Hind Nationalist Agency* (London), 1910. |

:box[On the 16th of October, 1905, the old Province of Bengal was divided into two administrative units by Lord Curzon. The old unit comprised three provinces, Bengal proper, Behar, and Orissa. It was under one Lieutenant-Governor, who was assisted in legislative work by a Legislative Council, and in revenue work by a Board of Revenue, composed of the senior members of the Civil Service. The plea for a redistribution of the administrative area was that the burden of responsibility had become too heavy for one Lieutenant-Governor.\
\
But why should a mere redistribution of administrative area be so strongly resented by the people? The reason of it is that the people of Bengal saw in it an attempt to weaken the solidarity of the Bengalee-speaking people, and, by depriving Calcutta of the support of Eastern Bengal, to curb and cripple the increasing political strength and influence of Bengal.[...]the most strong objection, was that it struck at the sense of unity of the Bengalee race. The Eastern and Western districts had always formed an integral social, literary and commercial, as well as an integral political and administrative unit. This redistribution destroyed to some extent, or at least sought to destroy, this sense of national unity. It was interpreted as a strategic move, and the interpretation came from the general  policy of Lord Curzon, whose one aim was, it seemed to the thinking and intelligent classes in the country, to kill the growing political power of the educated classes.\
\
The proposal for thus dividing Bengal at once called forth an agitation, the like of which had never been seen before in British India. The classes and the masses stood shoulder to shoulder to protest against what they all regarded as a wanton outrage on their cherished sentiments. Mass meetings were held in every part of the province, and people came to these in their thousands and tens of thousands. Petitions were sent to the Government. The Hindus and the Mahomedans both joined together to support these. The protest, especially from Eastern Bengal, was absolutely unanimous.[...]\
\
When the final decision of the Government of India on the Partition of Bengal was published, people saw that their suspicions of the scheme were fully justified. In this document, Lord Curzon gave a new reason for it; and this was to institute a special Mahomedan Province in British India. In the eastern districts of Bengal there is a very great preponderance of Mahomedan populations. But still there never had been any Hindu-Mahomedan disturbance there within living memory. The two communities had always lived together in peace and amity.
]

[Source: Pal, B.P. (1910). *The Spirit of Indian Nationalism.* The Hind Nationalist Agency (London). Retrieved March 15, 2025 from https://hdl.handle.net/2027/umn.31951p009866515. Public domain.]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
A combination of the below that meets the following guidelines:
- Both values and limitations must be addressed. If only one is addressed, award a maximum of **two** marks.
- Origin, purpose and content must all be referenced at some point in the response (either as a value, limitation or both) to receive the full **four** marks.

For value, any of the following:
- The author was a prominent Indian nationalist and journalist suggesting access to people who shaped the events of the partition. Another value is the date of publication in 1910, where the events and impact of the partition is still recent.
- The purpose of the source is to provide an in-depth focus on the factors driving the spirit of Indian nationalism.
- The content analyses the reasons for and people's reactions to the partition.

For limitations, any of the following:
- Because the source was published in 1910 it cannot provide insight into the long-term impact of the Partition of Bengal on the growth of the Nationalist movement.
- The source's purpose has limitations, as the author's nationalist leanings tend to influence the viewpoints presented in the source.
- The author was part of a triumvirate of assertive nationalists in British India in the early 20th century, from 1906 to 1918 referred to a Lal Bal Pal, who advocated the Swadeshi movement during the anti-Partition agitation in Bengal.
\
[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The source is valuable with reference to the origin as the author was a well-known Indian nationalist and also a journalist. In both these roles, he would have gained an insider view on events that shaped the nationalist movement along with the factors impacting the partition of Bengal. The date of publication of the source in 1910 also holds value as the reaction to the partition is still recent ($\checkmark\, \text{VO}$).  The purpose of the source also holds value as its focus on $\lq$the spirit of Indian nationalism', aims to provide an indepth dive into the factors shaping Indian nationalism ($\checkmark\, \text{VP}$). Finally the content of the source provides value as it provides an in-depth analysis of the reasons for partition and the reactions of people to this event ($\checkmark\, \text{VC}$).

The source also has some limitations with reference to the origin as it was published in 1910 and so fails to provide an analysis of the long-term impact of the partition of Bengal on the growth of the nationalist movement and its contribution to the factors that shaped the eventual independence of India ($\checkmark\, \text{LO}$). Further the source is also limited with reference to its purpose as the author's nationalist leanings tend to influence the viewpoints presented in the source ($\checkmark\, \text{LP}$).
