# HI0625

What does Source A suggest about the constitutional reforms passed by the British?
[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- Source A suggests the constitutional reforms passed by the British do not result in any real devolution of power to the Indians. This is apparent from the writing on howdah which states, $\lq$CONSTITUTION. Front seats strictly reserved' and the howdah itself being occupied by British individuals.
- The source also suggests that the Congress party is not inclined to support the constitutional reforms. This is indicated by the elephant which represents the Congress refusing to carry the British passengers.
- The source further suggests that opposition to the constitutional reforms in India is through strikes. This is indicated by the caption that reads $\lq$sit-down strikes in India' and by the depiction of Congress leader Nehru who is shown as a mahout unwilling to ask or persuade the elephant to move.
- The source also suggests that there are some amongst the British who are not inclined to struggle with the opposition to British rule in India and would want to exit and leave the problems behind. This is indicated by the blurb with one of the British passengers asking, $\lq$Gad sir, call a taxi'.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Source A suggests that the British did not intend to empower Indians with the right to self-government through the constitutional reforms they had passed. This is apparent from the words on the howdah in which only the British are seated, which states, $\lq$CONSTITUTION. Front seats strictly reserved' ($\checkmark$). The Source also suggests that the constitutional reforms will not be supported by the Congress Party in India. This is conveyed by the elephant representing the Congress, refusing to carry the British passengers ($\checkmark$). The source further suggests that the constitutional reforms will be opposed in India through strikes and protests. This is highlighted by the caption of the cartoon that reads, $\lq$sit-down strike in India' and by the depiction of Congress leader Nehru, who is shown as a mahout unwilling to ask or persuade the elephant to move along with Gandhi who is shown disinterested, sitting and reading in a tree ($\checkmark$).
