# HI0620

Identify the reasons for religious tension between the Hindus and Muslims as a result of the Partition of Bengal in 1905.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any **three** of the following:
- The British viceroy's 1905 partition of Bengal created an eastern province with a Muslim majority and a western province with a majority of Bihari and Oriya-speaking Hindus. Bengali-speaking Hindus saw this as an attempt by the British to weaken the strong nationalist movement in the province.
- An anti-partition movement was organised by different groups that included groups that appealed to Hindu nationalism.
- The opposition to the partition was viewed with apprehension by the Muslims of Bengal, who believed the partition would benefit them, and support for the Muslim League increased.
- In October 1906, Muslim community representatives met with Lord Minto, the Viceroy at Simla. This $\lq$Simla Deputation' received assurances from Minto that the political interests of Muslims would be safeguarded in any future constitutional reforms introduced.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
The partition of Bengal in 1905 created a split between Bengali Hindus and Bengali Muslims. The Bengali Hindus viewed the establishment of a Muslim-dominated eastern province as an attempt to weaken the strong nationalist movement that was centered in Bengal ($\checkmark$). The anti-partition agitation created apprehension amongst the Bengali Muslims due to the strong emphasis on Hindu nationalism and served to further religious tensions ($\checkmark$). Apprehension amongst the Muslims resulted in increased support for the Muslim League, and the division between the two religious groups increased when the Viceroy, Lord Minto, met with representatives of the Muslim community in 1906 and granted an assurance that the political interests of the Muslim community would be safeguarded when issuing future constitutional reforms ($\checkmark$).
