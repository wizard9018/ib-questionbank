# HI0714

Describe **three** impacts of the Second World War on India's independence.
[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:
\
Any of **three** of the following:
- The British viceroy committed India to fight on the Allied side at the start of the Second World War, without consulting the Indian Legislative Council. This emphasised India's subservience as a colony and strengthened the resolve of the nationalist movement to aim for total independence.
- In 1941, the British Prime Minister Winston Churchill after signing the Atlantic Charter which supported the rights of all people to self-determination, told the British parliament this provision did not apply to India. This further outraged Indian nationalists.
- Churchill changed his stance as a result of sweeping Japanese victories in Asia. Under pressure from the US government  and recognising the need for support from Indian leaders, Churchill sent Stafford Cripps to India in 1942 to negotiate with Indian nationalist leaders. The proposals put forward by Cripps included the commitment to grant India independence after the war was over. However,the mission failed as the Congress was unhappy at proposals that would allow states to opt out of an united India and Churchill was unwilling to make any real meaningful concessions.
- In 1943, two to four million people died in the Great Bengal famine, caused by loss of rice imports from Japanese occupied Burma and by the British administrative decision to divert food from the Bengal countryside to feed the military in Europe,despite being aware of the famine conditions. The famine was also aggravated by the $\lq$scorched earth' policy implemented by the British to stall Japanese advance into Bengal. This resulted in the destruction of 20000 boats, depriving the region of transporting food or fishing along with the destruction of bridges across rivers.
- India's contribution to the allied victory consisted of Indian soldiers fighting in North Africa, Italy and Burma. The Indian economy was also a significant contributor to the defeat of the Axis Powers. The Jamshedpur steel complex became the largest producer of steel in the British Empire. Bombay became a major center for light engineering and manufacturing of pharmaceuticals and chemicals.
- The war transformed India's economic relationship with Britain. India had been in debt to Britain prior to the war. The war forced Britain to borrow heavily from India and by 1945 Britain owed India large sums of money. Britain emerged victorious from the Second World War but with her economy on the brink of collapse. It became apparent that she was not in a position to maintain her global empire and by 1945, it was time for Britain to make good its promises of independence and negotiate with nationalist leaders.
- The election of a Labour government in 1945 hastened Britain's determination to reach a settlement with Indian nationalist leaders as they were anxious to focus on domestic reforms in Britain and the creation of a welfare state.
\
[3 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:
\
Britain's entry into the Second World War gave a boost to the Indian Independence Movement, as the war economically weakened Britain and defeats at the hands of the Japanese in South East Asia dented its aura of invincibility which catalyzed the resolve of the nationalist movement to work towards independence ($\checkmark$).

Indian commitment to fight in the war was made without consulting the Indian Legislative Council which emphasised India's subservience as a colony. This, together with the stand taken by Churchill in the British parliament that the Atlantic Charter and the right of self-determination of all people did not apply to India, angered the Indians and motivated the  nationalist movement ($\checkmark$).

The war also strengthened the Indian economy while weakening the British economy. The Jamshedpur steel complex became the largest producer of steel in the British Empire and Bombay a major center for light engineering and manufacture of pharmaceuticals and chemicals. Britain was now forced to borrow heavily from India during the war and, by 1945, Britain owed India large sums of money leaving it in no position to maintain its global empire thus ensuring the inevitability of Indian independence ($\checkmark$).
