# HI0573

Describe **two** short-term and **two** long-term results of the Revolt of the Comuneros in 1781.

[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

$\underline{\textrm{Explanation}}$:

\

Any **two** from each of the following:

 - Short term:

    - The revolt was ultimately defeated, and the Spanish maintained control over New Granada.

     - Brutal repression, as many Comuneros, including José Antonio Galán, were executed, and indigenous and mestizo rebels faced severe punishment.

     - Spanish reforms were finally established, even though Spain initially refused to meet demands, and some minor tax reductions were later implemented to appease the population.

 - Long term:

     - It exposed deep divisions between the Spanish Crown and its colonies. It simultaneously united the various classes of people, creoles, mestizos, and indigenous, against Spanish authority. 

     - The memory of the revolt fueled Creole nationalism, contributing to the general unrest. This memory of organized rebellion stayed alive, influencing the political thinking of Creole elites by proving that popular action could challenge Spanish authority.

     - It inspired future revolutionary leaders, including Simón Bolívar, by demonstrating the power of mass resistance.The Comuneros' failure also taught the new leaders that stronger unity and clearer political goals were needed, shifting the struggle from short-term demands to a full independence movement.

\

[4 marks]

$\underline{\textrm{Sample Answer}}$:

\

In the short term, Spanish authorities responded with harsh repression, executing many Comuneros, including José Antonio Galán, and severely punishing indigenous and mestizo rebels to discourage future uprisings. ($\checkmark$) However, after initially ignoring the rebels' demands, Spain later introduced small tax reductions and reforms to calm the growing unrest in the colony. ($\checkmark$) 

In the long term, the revolt inspired future leaders like Simón Bolívar, proving that people could come together to resist Spanish rule and fight for independence. ($\checkmark$) Another long-term consequence was the exposure of the divide between the Spanish authorities and their colonies, thus driving greater cohesion and collaboration among various social classes to resist Spanish control. ($\checkmark$)
