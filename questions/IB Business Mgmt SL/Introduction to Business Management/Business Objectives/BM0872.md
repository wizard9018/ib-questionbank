# BM0872

PopNorth is a craft soda company based in Manchester that floated on the stock exchange two years ago. Since becoming a PLC, shareholder pressure has pushed management to prioritise short-term profit growth, leading to decisions such as switching to cheaper ingredients, cutting the marketing budget for its award-winning premium range, and delaying a planned expansion into sustainable packaging. The company's original mission was to "create exceptional soda responsibly, supporting local suppliers and minimising environmental impact." Staff morale has fallen, with several senior staff resigning over what they describe as a "race to the bottom."

**Analyse** two conflicts between PopNorth's current decisions and its original mission. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**Sample response:**

One conflict is that PopNorth is reducing product quality by switching to cheaper ingredients, which goes against its original mission. Focusing on short-term profit maximisation creates a conflict with ethical and quality objectives, which often require higher costs. PopNorth is “switching to cheaper ingredients,” suggesting it is lowering production costs to increase profit; however, this contradicts its mission to “create exceptional soda responsibly,” as the product is no longer high-quality or ethically produced as originally intended.

Another conflict is shareholder pressure for profit is leading PopNorth to delay sustainable packaging, contradicting its environmental goals. Shareholder pressure to increase profits and pay dividends can conflict with long-term sustainability and CSR, as environmentally responsible actions often involve higher costs. PopNorth “delaying the sustainable packaging” shows it is avoiding additional costs to protect profits and satisfy shareholders, which contradicts its mission to “minimise environmental impact” by postponing environmentally responsible practices.
