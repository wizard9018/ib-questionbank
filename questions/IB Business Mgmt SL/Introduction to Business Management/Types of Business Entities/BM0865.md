# BM0865

Tariq has been running a plumbing business as a sole trader for three years, building a strong local reputation in Bristol. The business has grown steadily and now generates £120,000 in annual revenue. However, Tariq recently took on a large commercial contract that went wrong, leaving him with £40,000 in unpaid claims from a client. As a sole trader, Tariq is personally exposed to these debts.  Following advice from his accountant, Tariq is considering incorporating the business and forming a privately held company (Ltd). He hopes this will protect his personal assets — including his home — and make it easier to bring in two trusted colleagues as co-owners to help manage the growing workload.

Explain one advantage and one disadvantage for Tariq of incorporating his business. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

**Sample response:**

By incorporating, Tariq's business becomes a separate legal entity from himself. This means Tariq's personal assets, such as his home or savings, would be protected if the business were to accumulate debts, reducing his personal financial risk significantly.

As a private limited company, Tariq would be required to file accounts with Companies House, making financial information publicly accessible. This increases administrative burden and costs, and means competitors could gain insight into the business's financial performance.

**Mark allocation:**

**Mark as 2 + 2**

Award **[1]** for identifying the advantage/disadvantage and **[1]** for application to Tariq.

Accept other valid advantages e.g. easier to raise capital, greater credibility. Accept other valid disadvantages e.g. more complex and costly to set up, profit must be shared with shareholders.
