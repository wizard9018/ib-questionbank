# BM1082

Smart Hazard Management (SHM) is a private limited company based in Birmingham, UK. While SHM operates primarily in the tertiary sector by providing waste collection services to local government agencies (public sector), it has recently expanded into the secondary sector by opening a high-tech plant that processes plastic waste into construction materials.

SHM’s board of directors is currently drafting a formal business plan to pitch to a group of business angels*. The plan highlights SHM's value-added through its patented "Clean-Sort" technology, which allows for 30% more recycling than standard methods.

Despite its growth, SHM suffers from poor departmental integration. For example, the Marketing department recently promised clients a 24-hour turnaround time without consulting the Operations manager, who lacks the specialized vehicles to meet that demand. Furthermore, the Finance department has restricted the Human Resources (HR) budget, making it difficult to recruit the specialized engineers needed for the new processing plant.

*A business angel is a wealthy individual who invests their own money in a small or new business, usually a start-up with high growth potential. In return they take an equity stake, meaning part-ownership of the business.

1. **Define** the term value-added. [2 marks]

2. With reference to SHM, **distinguish** between the secondary sector and the tertiary sector. [4 marks]

3. **Explain** the roles of any two business functions within SHM. [4 marks]

4. **Explain** two reasons why a business plan is important for SHM. [4 marks]

[14 marks]

- Value-added is the difference between the cost of purchasing raw materials or “inputs" and the price that the finished goods or services are sold for.

2. **Sample response:**

- The secondary sector involves the manufacturing or processing of raw materials into finished goods.   At SHM, this is demonstrated by its “high-tech plant", where plastic waste is transformed from a raw material into finished construction materials.

- In contrast, the tertiary sector involves the provision of services to consumers, businesses and governments rather than the production of physical, tangible goods.   At SHM, this is demonstrated by its "waste collection services" provided to local government agencies in Birmingham.

3. **Sample response:**

- At SHM, the role of the Operations function is to manage the efficient production of goods and delivery of services, such as waste collection and the high-tech processing plant. For example, Operations must assess whether SHM has sufficient resources, such as specialised vehicles, to deliver the 24-hour turnaround promised by Marketing, as failing to do so could reduce customer satisfaction and damage the company’s reputation.

- At SHM, the role of the Human Resources (HR) function is to recruit, train, and manage employees needed to support the company’s operations and expansion, such as developing its new plastic processing plant.   For example, HR must recruit specialised engineers with the skills required to operate the high-tech processing plant, but restrictions on the HR budget from Finance may limit its ability to do this, preventing SHM from operating the plant effectively and achieving the benefits of expansion.

- **Mark allocation:**
- **Mark as 2 + 2**

- Award **[1]** for identifying one relevant role of one business function  and an additional **[1]** for application to the case.

4. **Sample response:**

- A business plan is important for SHM because it provides business angels with detailed information about SHM’s financial forecasts, reducing their perceived risk of investing in the business.   This increases the likelihood of SHM receiving equity finance to support expansion, such as funding its high-tech plastic processing plant, allowing SHM to increase its production capacity and grow.

- A business plan is important to SHM because it helps departments work together by setting clear objectives and explaining the strategies and resources needed to achieve them. This could prevent departmental conflicts, such as Marketing promising a 24 hour turnaround when Operations lacks the specialised vehicles to meet this demand, consequently improving operational efficiency by ensuring departments have the resources and capabilities needed to meet customer expectations.
