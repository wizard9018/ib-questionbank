# AA327

**Maximum mark: 23**

The points A, B, C and D have position vectors
$\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ and $\mathbf{d}$, relative
the $\text{origin}$ O.

It is given that $\vec{\mathrm{AD}} = \vec{\mathrm{BC}}$.

1.
    1.  Explain why ABCD is a parallelogram.

    2.  Using vector algebra, show that
        $\vec{\mathrm{AB}} = \vec{\mathrm{DC}}$. [4 marks]

The position vectors $\vec{\mathrm{OA}}$, $\vec{\mathrm{OB}}$,
$\vec{\mathrm{OC}}$ and $\vec{\mathrm{OD}}$ are given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{a} &= 5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} \\[6pt]
\mathbf{b} &= p\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k} \\[6pt]
\mathbf{c} &= 2\mathbf{i} + q\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k} \\[6pt]
\mathbf{d} &= 6\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} + r\mathbf{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $p$, $q$ and $r$ are constants.

2.  Show that $p = 1$, $q = 2$ and $r = 0$. [4 marks]

3.  Find the area of the parallelogram ABCD. [4 marks]

The point where the diagonals of ABCD intersect is denoted by M.

4.  Find the vector equation of the straight line $L$ passing
    through M and normal to the plane $\mathit{\Pi}$ containing ABCD.
    [4 marks]

5.  Find the Cartesian equation of $\mathit{\Pi}$. [3 marks]

The plane $\mathit{\Pi}$ cuts the $x$, $y$ and $z$ axes at P, Q and R
respectively.

6.
    1.  Find the coordinates of P, Q and R.

    2.  Find PR. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Given $\vec{\mathrm{AD}} = \vec{\mathrm{BC}}$, we have
        1\. \[AD\] and \[BC\] is a pair of opposite sides of
            ABCD;

        2\. \[AD\] and \[BC\] are parallel and have the same
            length.

        Hence ABCD is a parallelogram.

    2.  Using vector algebra, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\mathrm{AD}} &= \vec{\mathrm{BC}} \\[6pt]
        \vec{\mathrm{OD}} -\vec{\mathrm{OA}} &= \vec{\mathrm{OC}} -\vec{\mathrm{OB}} \\[6pt]
        \vec{\mathrm{OB}} -\vec{\mathrm{OA}} &= \vec{\mathrm{OC}} -\vec{\mathrm{OD}} \hspace{2.2em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \vec{\mathrm{AB}} = \vec{\mathrm{DC}} \hspace{2.2em}
        $$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \vec{\mathrm{AD}} &= \vec{\mathrm{BC}} \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{d} - \mathbf{a} &= \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{b} \\[6pt]
    (6\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j} + r\mathbf{k}) - (5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}) &= (2\mathbf{i} + q\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}) - (p\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j} + (r-3)\mathbf{k} &= (2-p)\mathbf{i} + (q+6)\mathbf{j} - 3\mathbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we get
    $$

    \hspace{0.525em} \begin{array}{rclcl}
    1 &=& 2 - p &\hspace{0.5em}\Rightarrow& \hspace{0.5em} \hspace{0.525em}p = 1 \\[6pt]
    8 &=& q + 6 &\hspace{0.5em}\Rightarrow& \hspace{0.5em} \hspace{0.525em}q = 2 \\[6pt]
    r - 3 &=& -3 &\hspace{0.5em}\Rightarrow& \hspace{0.5em} \hspace{0.525em}r = 0
    \end{array} \hspace{3.6em}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{5.9em} \vec{\text{AB}} &= \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} \\[6pt]
    &= (\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}) - (5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= -4\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} \vec{\text{AD}} &= \mathbf{d} - \mathbf{a} \\[6pt]
    &= (6\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j}) - (5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= \mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j} - 3\mathbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    using the vector product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7em} \vec{\text{AB}} \times \vec{\text{AD}} &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \textbf{i} & \textbf{j} & \hspace{0.75em}\textbf{k} \\ -4 & -4 & 2 \\ 1 & 8 & -3  \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[6pt]
    &= [12-16]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [12-2]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [-32-(-4)]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k} \\[8pt]
    &= -4\textbf{i} - 10\textbf{j} - 28\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    using the area of a parallelogram formula, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.8em} A_{\square\text{ABCD}} &= \big|\vec{\text{AB}} \times \vec{\text{AD}}\big| \\[6pt]
    &= \textstyle \sqrt{(-4)^2 + (-10)^2 + (-28)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{16 + 100 + 784} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{900} \\[8pt]
    &= 30
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{12em} \mathbf{d} &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\big(\vec{\text{AB}} \times \vec{\text{AD}}\big) \hspace{3em} [\text{direction vector of $L$}] \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2}(-4\textbf{i} - 10\textbf{j} - 28\textbf{k}) \\[10pt]
    &=  2\textbf{i} + 5\textbf{j} + 14\textbf{k} \\[6pt]
    &= \begin{pmatrix*} 2 \\ 5 \\ 14 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8em} \vec{\text{OM}} &= \dfrac{1}{2}[\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{d}] \hspace{3em} [\text{position vector of M}] \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{2}[(\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k}) + (6\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j})] \hspace{0.6em} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{7}{2}\mathbf{i} + \dfrac{5}{2}\mathbf{k} \\[6pt]
    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r] \frac{7}{2} \\ 0 \\ \frac{5}{2} \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the vector equation of a line formula, we find

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \mathbf{r} &= \vec{\text{OM}} + t\hspace{0.05em}\mathbf{d} \hspace{3em} \\
     \end{aligned}
     $$

    $$
    \hspace{4.6em} \mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[r] \frac{7}{2} \\ 0 \\ \frac{5}{2} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*} 2 \\ 5 \\ 14 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2em} [t \in \mathbb{R}]
    $$

5.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{11.7em} \mathbf{n} &= 2\textbf{i} + 5\textbf{j} + 14\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{a} &= 5\textbf{i} - 2\textbf{j} + \phantom{1}3\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{A lies on $\mathit{\Pi}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the scalar product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{a} &= (2)(5) + (5)(-2) + (14)(3) \\[6pt]
    &= 42
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the equation of a plane formula,
    we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.175em} \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{a} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    2x + 5y + 14z = 42 \hspace{4em}
    $$

6.
    1.  Substituting $y=0$ and $z=0$ in the equation of $\mathit{\Pi}$,
        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        2x + 5(0) + 14(0) &= 42 \hspace{2em}\\[6pt]
        2x &= 42 \\[6pt]
        x &= 21 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the coordinates of P are
         $(21,0,0)$

        Similarly, substituting $x=0$ and $z=0$ in the equation of
        $\mathit{\Pi}$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        2(0) + 5y + 14(0) &= 42 \hspace{1.75em} \\[6pt]
        5y &= 42 \\[6pt]
        y &= 8.4 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the coordinates of Q are
         $(0,8.4,0)$
        Similarly, substituting $x=0$ and $y=0$ in the equation of
        $\mathit{\Pi}$, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        2(0) + 5(0) + 14z &= 42 \hspace{2em}\\[6pt]
        14z &= 42 \\[6pt]
        z &= 3 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the coordinates of R are
         $(0,0,3)$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{PR}} &= \vec{\text{OR}} - \vec{\text{OP}} \\[6pt]
        &= 3\textbf{k} - 21\textbf{i} \\[6pt]
        &= - 21\textbf{i} + 3\textbf{k} \hspace{1em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the length of $\vec{\text{PR}}$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.9em} \mathrm{PR} &= |\hspace{-0.1em}-21\textbf{i} + 3\textbf{k}| \\[6pt]
        &= \textstyle \sqrt{(-21)^2 + 0^2 + 3^2} \\[6pt]
        &= \sqrt{441 + 0 + 9} \\[6pt]
        &= \sqrt{450} \\[6pt]
        &= 15\sqrt{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
