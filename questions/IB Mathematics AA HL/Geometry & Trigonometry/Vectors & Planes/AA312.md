# AA312

**Maximum mark: 8**

The lines $\mathit{L}_1$ and $\mathit{L}_2$ are defined as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{L}_1 &:\hspace{0.5em} \dfrac{x+2}{-3} = \dfrac{y-1}{4} = \dfrac{z+6}{2}, \\[6pt]
\mathit{L}_2 &:\hspace{0.5em} \dfrac{x+2}{6} = \dfrac{y-1}{2} = \dfrac{z+6}{-4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The plane $\mathit{\Pi}$ contains both $\mathit{L}_1$ and
$\mathit{L}_2$.

1.  Find the Cartesian equation of $\mathit{\Pi}$. [4 marks]

The line $\mathit{L}_3$ is passing through the point P$(1,-2,5)$ and
perpendicular to $\mathit{\Pi}$.

2.  Find the coordinates of the point where $\mathit{L}_3$
    intersects $\mathit{\Pi}$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathit{L}_1 &:\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{r}_1 = \mathbf{a}_0 + t\mathbf{b}_1 \\[6pt]
    \mathit{L}_2 &:\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{r}_2 = \mathbf{a}_0 + t\mathbf{b}_2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    where
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{a}_0 &= -2\textbf{i}+\phantom{1}\textbf{j}-6\textbf{k} \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{b}_1 &= -3\textbf{i}+4\textbf{j}+2\textbf{k} \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{b}_2 &= \phantom{-}6\textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}-4\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the vector product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.9em} \mathbf{n} &= \mathbf{b}_1 \hspace{-0.1em}\times \mathbf{b}_2 \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}$}] \\[8pt]
    &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \textbf{i} & \hspace{0.75em}\textbf{j} & \textbf{k} \\ -3 & 4 & 2 \\ 6 & 2 & -4 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[8pt]
    &= [-16-4]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [12-12]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [-6-24]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[8pt]
    &= -20\textbf{i}-30\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the scalar
    product formula, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.6em} \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{a}_0 &= (-20)(-2) + (0)(1) + (-30)(-6) \\[6pt]
    &= 220
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the equation of a plane formula,
    we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{a}_0 \hspace{1.2em} \\[6pt]
    -20x - 30z &= 220 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    2x + 3z = -22
    $$

2.  Using the vector equation of a line formula, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathit{L}_3 &:\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{r} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ -2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} [t \in \mathbb{R}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -2$, $z = 5 + 3t$ in the equation of
    $\mathit{\Pi}$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x + 3z &= -22 \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
    2(1+2t) + 3(5+3t) &= -22 \\[6pt]
    17 + 13t &= -22 \\[6pt]
    13t &= -39 \\[6pt]
    t &= -3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, evaluating $x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -2$,
    $z = 5 + 3t$ for $t = -3$, we obtain
    $$

    \begin{array}{rcrcl}
    x &=& 1+2(-3) &=& -5 \\[7pt]
    && y &=& -2 \\[7pt]
    z &=& 5 + 3(-3) &=& -4
    \end{array}
    $$
    Hence the coordinates of the point where
    $\mathit{L}_3$ meets $\mathit{\Pi}$ are
     $(-5, -2, -4)$
