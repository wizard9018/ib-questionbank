# AA924

**Maximum mark: 15**

Consider the planes $\Pi_1$, $\Pi_2$, $\Pi_3$ given by the following equations:

$$

\begin{aligned}

\Pi_1: &\hspace{6.27mm} 2x + y - z = -3 \\[6pt]

\Pi_2: &\hspace{4.52mm} x + 5y - 5z = -6 \\[6pt]

\Pi_3: &\enskip\hspace{1mm} 3x + 5y - 5z = -7

\end{aligned}

$$

1. Show that the three planes do not intersect. [4 marks]

It is given that the point Q$(-1,-1,0)$ lies on both $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$.

Let $\ell$ be the line of intersection of $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$.

2. Find a vector expression for $\ell$. [4 marks]

3. Show that $\ell$ is parallel to plane $\Pi_3$. [2 marks]

4. Hence or otherwise, find the distance between $\ell$ and $\Pi_3$ Express your answer in the form $\dfrac{p}{\sqrt{q}}$, where $p$, $q\in \mathbb{Z}$. [5 marks]

1. If we convert the given system of equations into an augmented matrix, we have

    Hence, using the method of Gaussian Elimination, we get

    ![AA924b](images/AA924b.PNG)

    Since $0\neq -0.1$ we conclude that the system has no solution and therefore $\text{the three planes do not intersect.}$

2. Any vector parallel to $\Pi_1$ is, by definition, perpendicular to its normal vector. Similarly, any vector parallel to $\Pi_2$ is perpendicular to its normal vector.

    The line of intersection of $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ has a direction vector that is parallel to both planes and hence is simultaneously perpendicular to both normal vectors.

    ![AA924c](images/AA924c.PNG)

    We can find a common perpendicular to the normal vectors using the vector product formula.

    The normal vectors to planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ are $n_1 =\hspace{-0.15em} \left(\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{matrix} \,\,\,2 \\[2pt] \hspace{0.6em}1 \\[2pt] -1 \end{matrix}\right)$ and $n_2\hspace{-0.15em} = \left(\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{matrix} \hspace{0.6em}1 \\[2pt] \hspace{0.6em}5 \\[2pt] -5 \end{matrix}\right)$.

    Using the vector product formula we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    v \times w &= \hspace{-0.15em}\left(\begin{matrix} v_2w_3 - v_3w_2 \\[2pt]  v_3w_1 - v_1w_3 \\[2pt] v_1w_2 - v_2w_1 \end{matrix}\right)\\[18pt]

    n_1 \times n_2 &= \hspace{-0.15em}\left(\begin{matrix} (1)(-5) - (-1)(5) \\[2pt]  (-1)(1) - (2)(-5) \\[2pt] (2)(5) - (1)(1) \end{matrix}\right)\\[18pt]

    n_1 \times n_2 &= \hspace{-0.15em}\left(\begin{matrix} 0 \\[2pt]  9 \\[2pt] 9 \end{matrix}\right)\\[18pt]

    n_1 \times n_2 &= 9\hspace{-0.15em}\left(\begin{matrix} 0 \\[2pt]  1 \\[2pt] 1 \end{matrix}\right)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore the direction vector of line $\ell$ is $\hspace{-0.15em} \left(\begin{matrix} \hspace{0em}0 \\[2pt] \hspace{0em}1 \\[2pt] 1 \end{matrix}\right)$.

    We have that point Q$(-1,-1,0)$ lies on both $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$.

    Therefore $\hspace{-0.15em} \left(\begin{matrix} \hspace{0em}-1 \\[2pt] \hspace{0em}-1 \\[2pt] \hspace{0.6em}0 \end{matrix}\right)$ is the position vector of a point on line $\ell$.

    Using the vector equation of a line, $r = a + \lambda b$, the vector equation of the line is 

    $

    \ell =

    \begin{pmatrix}

    -1 \\ -1 \\ 0

    \end{pmatrix} + \lambda 

    \begin{pmatrix}

    0 \\ 1 \\ 1

    \end{pmatrix}

    $

3. If line $\ell$ is parallel to plane $\Pi_3$, it must be perpendicular to its normal vector.

    ![AA924d](images/AA924d.PNG)

    Therefore we calculate the scalar product of $n_3$ and the direction vector of $\ell$ (vector $b$ from part (b)).

    $b$, the direction vector of $\ell$ is $\hspace{-0.15em} \left(\begin{matrix} \hspace{0em}0 \\[2pt] \hspace{0em}1 \\[2pt] 1 \end{matrix}\right)$ and $n_3 =\hspace{-0.15em} \left(\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{matrix} \hspace{0.6em}3 \\[2pt] \hspace{0.6em}5 \\[2pt] -5 \end{matrix}\right)$

    Using the scalar product formula, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    v \cdot w &= v_1w_1 + v_2w_2 + v_3w_3 \\[10pt]

    b \cdot n_3 &= (0)(3) + (1)(5) + (1)(-5) \\[10pt]

    &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Given that the scalar product is zero, line $\ell$ is perpendicular to the normal to plane $\Pi_3$.

    $$\text{Therefore, we conclude that line $\ell$ is parallel to plane $\Pi_3$.}$$

4. Since $\ell$ is parallel to the plane $\Pi_3$, it is enough to find the distance between any point on $\ell$ to the plane $\Pi_3$. We have the point Q$(-1,-1,0)$ on $\ell$. Following the normal vector will take us to point P on $\Pi_3$ as shown in the following diagram.

    ![AA924e](images/AA924e.PNG)

    The distance from point Q to point P will be our solution.

    The line passing through points P and Q has equation $r = \begin{pmatrix}

-1 \\ -1 \\ 0

\end{pmatrix} + \mu 

\begin{pmatrix}

3 \\ 5 \\ -5

\end{pmatrix}$.

    Therefore, any point on this line has coordinates $(-1+3\mu,-1+5\mu,-5\mu)$. If we substitute these coordinates into the plane equation of $\Pi_3$, we will find the coordinates of point P and hence the value of $\mu$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    3x + 5y - 5z &= -7 \\[10pt]

    3(-1+3\mu) + 5(-1+5\mu) - 5(-5\mu) &= -7 \\[10pt]

    -3 +9\mu -5 +25\mu +25\mu &= -7 \\[10pt]

    59\mu &= 1 \\[10pt]

    \mu &= \dfrac{1}{59}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

   Hence the vector from point Q to point P is the vector $\dfrac{1}{59} 

\begin{pmatrix}

3 \\ 5 \\ -5

\end{pmatrix}$.

    The magnitude of this vector is the distance from point Q to point P and therefore the distance between $\ell$ and $\Pi_3$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    |QP| &= \Bigg|\dfrac{1}{59} 

    \begin{pmatrix}

    3 \\ 5 \\ -5

    \end{pmatrix}\Bigg| \\[10pt]

    &= \sqrt{\dfrac{3^2 + 5^2 + (-5)^2}{59^2}} \\[10pt]

    &= \sqrt{\dfrac{59}{59^2}} \\[10pt]

    &= \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{59}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The distance between $\ell$ and $\Pi_3$ is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{59}} $
