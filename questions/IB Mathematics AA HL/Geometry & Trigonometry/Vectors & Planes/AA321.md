# AA321

**Maximum mark: 15**

The plane $\mathit{\Pi}$ has equation $2x - y  + z = 15$.

The line $L$ is perpendicular to the plane $\mathit{\Pi}$ and has a
vector equation

$$
\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ -p \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} +\lambda\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -4 \\ 2 \\ p \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.75em} \lambda \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

1.  Determine the value of $p$. [2 marks]

2.  Find the coordinates of the point where $L$ meets the plane
    $\mathit{\Pi}$. [3 marks]

3.  The point P$(3,-1,2)$ does not lie on the plane $\mathit{\Pi}$.
    Find:

    1.  $\vec{\text{PA}}$ if A is a general point on $L$;
    2.  $\lambda$ such that $\vec{\text{PA}}$ is perpendicular to $L$;

    3.  the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular from P to $L$;
    4.  the shortest distance from P to $L$. [7 marks]

4.  Given two planes $2x - y  +z = 15$ and $x + 3y - 2z = 8$, find the
    equation of a third plane which is perpendicular to both of these
    planes and which cuts the $x$-axis at $-5$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we write down a normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}$ and a direction
    vector of $L$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{n} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{0.65em}\text{and}\hspace{1.0em} \mathbf{d} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -4 \\ 2 \\ p \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since $L$ is perpendicular to $\mathit{\Pi}$, the vectors
    $\mathbf{n}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ are parallel.

    Hence we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.5em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} &= \alpha\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -4 \\ 2 \\ p \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2em} [\text{for some $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the vector equation $(1)$, we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -1 &= 2\alpha \\[6pt]
    1 &= p\hspace{0.05em}\alpha \hspace{1.7em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, if we solve the system of equations $(2)$-$(3)$ for $\alpha$
    and $p$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6.9em} \alpha &= -\dfrac{1}{2} & \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    p = -2 \hspace{0.85em}
    $$

2.  Substituting $x = 1 - 4\lambda$, $y = 2+2\lambda$,
    $z = 3 - 2\lambda$ in the equation of $\mathit{\Pi}$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x - y  + z &= 15 \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
    2(1 - 4\lambda) - (2+2\lambda) + (3 - 2\lambda) &= 15 \\[6pt]
    3 - 12\lambda &= 15 \\[6pt]
    -12\lambda &= 12 \\[6pt]
    \lambda &= -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the coordinates of the point
    where $L$ meets $\mathit{\Pi}$ are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.8em} (1 - 4\lambda, 2+2\lambda, 3 - 2\lambda) &= (1-4(-1), 2+2(-1), 3-2(-1)) \\[7pt]
    &= (5,0,5)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{7em} \vec{\text{OA}} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1-4\lambda \\ 2+2\lambda \\ 3-2\lambda \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2em} [\text{for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.2em} \vec{\text{PA}} &= \vec{\text{OA}} - \vec{\text{OP}} \\[6pt]
        &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1-4\lambda \\ 2+2\lambda \\ 3-2\lambda \end{pmatrix*} - \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} \\[8pt]
        &= \hspace{0.525em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2-4\lambda \\ 3+2\lambda \\ 1-2\lambda  \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the scalar product formula, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{PA}} \cdot \mathbf{d} &= 0 \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
        \begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2-4\lambda \\ 3+2\lambda \\ 1-2\lambda \end{pmatrix*} \cdot \begin{pmatrix*}[r] -4 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*} &= 0 \\[6pt]
        (-2-4\lambda)(-4) + (3+2\lambda)(2) + (1-2\lambda)(-2) &= 0 \\[15pt]
        12 + 24\lambda &= 0 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{16.8em} \lambda = -\dfrac{1}{2}
        $$

    3.  We have

        ![c3b98b2066eee6e28559368b830512468bd37c7a.svg](images/c3b98b2066eee6e28559368b830512468bd37c7a.svg)

        Evaluating the expression $(4)$ for $\lambda = -1/2$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{OA}} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3.2em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the coordinates of A are
         $(3,1,4)$

    4.  Evaluating the expression $(5)$ for $\lambda = -1/2$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{PA}} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 0 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3.05em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the shortest distance from P to $L$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.8em} \text{PA} &= |2\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}| \\[6pt]
        &= \textstyle \sqrt{0^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} \\[6pt]
        &= 2\sqrt{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathit{\Pi}_1&:\hspace{0.5em} 2x - y  + z = 15 \\[6pt]
    \mathit{\Pi}_2&:\hspace{0.5em} x + 3y - 2z = 8
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10.5em} \mathbf{n}_1 &= 2\textbf{i} - \textbf{j} + \textbf{k} \hspace{3.5em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_1$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{n}_2 &= \textbf{i} + 3\textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the vector product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.8em} \mathbf{n}_3 &= \mathbf{n}_1\times\mathbf{n}_2 \hspace{3em} [\text{$\mathbf{n}_3 \perp \mathbf{n}_1$ \& $\mathbf{n}_3 \perp \mathbf{n}_2$}] \\[6pt]
    &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \textbf{i} & \textbf{j} & \textbf{k} \\ \hspace{0.25em}2 & -1 & 1 \\ \hspace{0.25em}1 & 3 & -2 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[6pt]
    &= [2-3]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [-4-1]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [6-(-1)]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[8pt]
    &= -\textbf{i} + 5\textbf{j} + 7\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the
    scalar product formula, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} \mathbf{n}_3\cdot(-5\textbf{i}) &= (-1)(-5) + (5)(0) + (7)(0) \\[6pt]
    &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the equation of a plane formula, we
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} \mathbf{n}_3\cdot\mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{n}_3\cdot(-5\textbf{i}) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    -x + 5y + 7z = 5 \hspace{5.2em}
    $$
