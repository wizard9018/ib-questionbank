# AA994

\[Maximum mark 22\]

Consider the points $A(5,\,1,\,0)$, $B(3,\,2,\,-1)$ and $C(1,\,2,\,3)$.

1. 1. Find the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
   2. Hence, find a vector equation, $\mathbf{r_1}$, of the line, $L_1$, that is parallel to $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and passes through $C$. Use the parameter $\lambda$ in your answer. [3 marks]

A second line, $L_2$, is skew to $L_1$ and has a Cartesian equation given by
$$
\begin{equation*}
\dfrac{x+1}{3}=\dfrac{y}{2}=\dfrac{z-1}{-2}
\end{equation*}
$$
2. Show that a vector equation of line $L_2$ could be written as
    $$
    \begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{r_2} = \begin{pmatrix*}[r] -1\color{white}{1} \\ 0\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \mu\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 3\color{white}{1} \\ 2\color{white}{1} \\ -2\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em},\hspace{0.5em} \mu \in \mathbb{R}.
    \end{equation*}
    $$
    [2 marks]

The vector $\bm d = \overrightarrow{DE}$. Where $D$ is any point on $L_1$ and $E$ is any point on $L_2$.

![Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 11.45.45 AM](<images/Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 11.45.45 AM.png>)

3.  1. Show that $\bm d$, in terms of $\lambda$ and $\mu$, is
    $$
    \begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{\bm d} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2+2\lambda+3\mu \\ -2-\lambda+2\mu \\ -2+\lambda-2\mu \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}
    \end{equation*}
    $$

    2. Hence or otherwise, find the minimum distance between $L_1$ and $L_2$. [9 marks]

Plane $\Pi$ contains the points $A$, $B$ and $C$.

4. 1. Find the Cartesian equation of the plane $\Pi$.

   2. A triangle is formed from the origin and the $y$ and $z$ intercepts of plane $\Pi$. Find the area of the triangle.
    [8 marks]

    2. As it passes through $C(1, 2, 3)$
    $$

2. Use the parameter $\mu$.

    We can then write each component in terms of $\mu$.

3. 1. Parametrically write the coordinates of points $D$ and $E$.

        $D = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1-2\lambda \\ 2+\lambda \\ 3-\lambda \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}$
        &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;
        $E = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1+3\mu \\2\mu \\ 1-2\mu \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}$

        Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathbf{\bm d} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1+3\mu \\2\mu \\ 1-2\mu \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} - \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1-2\lambda \\ 2+\lambda \\ 3-\lambda \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}\\[6pt]

     As required.

    2. The vector $\bm d$ is perpendicular to both directions of the line when the distance between the lines is minimised. As shown below.

    ![Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 11.45.52 AM](<images/Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 11.45.52 AM.png>)

    We can use the dot product to form two equations which have the parameters $\lambda$ and $\mu$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2+2\lambda+3\mu \\ -2-\lambda+2\mu \\ -2+\lambda-2\mu \end{pmatrix*}\cdot\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 3\color{white}{1} \\ 2\color{white}{1} \\ -2\color{white}{1}\end{pmatrix*}=0 \text{  and  }
        &\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2+2\lambda+3\mu \\ -2-\lambda+2\mu \\ -2+\lambda-2\mu \end{pmatrix*}\cdot\begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \\ -1\color{white}{1}\end{pmatrix*}=0\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    That leads to
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &6-2\lambda-17\mu=0 &-4+6\lambda+2\mu=0\\[6pt]
        &\lambda=\dfrac{4}{7}, \mu=\dfrac{2}{7} &\text{   [Solved on G.D.C.]}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now we can find the vector $\bm d$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \bm d&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2+2\times\frac{4}{7}+3\times\frac{2}{7} \\ -2-\frac{4}{7}+2\times\frac{2}{7} \\ -2+\frac{4}{7}-2\times\frac{2}{7} \end{pmatrix*}\\[6pt]
            &=\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 0\color{white}{1}\\ -2\color{white}{1}\\ -2\color{white}{1}\end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore the length of $\bm d$ is the minimum distance.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    |\bm d| &= \sqrt{0^2+(-2)^2+(-2)^2}\\[3pt]
    &=2.8284271...\\[3pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The minimum distance between the two lines is $2.83$units.

4.  1. We can find the normal to the plane using the cross product.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bm n &= \overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}\\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \\ -1\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1-5 \\ 2-1 \\ 3-0 \end{pmatrix*}\\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \\ -1\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[r] -4\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \\ 3\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*}\\[6pt]

        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1\times3-(-1)\times1 \\ -1\times(-4)-(-2)\times(3) \\ -2\times1-(1)\times(-4) \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4\\ 10 \\2 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The normal is $\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4\\ 10 \\2 \end{pmatrix*}$ however, as we only need a direction to represent the normal we can write the normal ($\div2$) as $\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2\\ 5 \\1 \end{pmatrix*}$.

    Using the scalar product plane equation with point $A(5, 1, 0)$ which lies on the plane
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \begin{pmatrix*}[c] x\\ y \\z \end{pmatrix*}\cdot\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2\\ 5 \\1 \end{pmatrix*} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 5\\ 1 \\0 \end{pmatrix*}\cdot\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2\\ 5 \\1 \end{pmatrix*}\\[6pt]
        2x+5y+z=5\times2+1\times5+0\times1\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The Cartesian equation of the plane is $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{2x+5y+z=15}$

    2. The plane has a $y$-intercept when $x, z=0$. Therefore the $y=3$.

        The plane has a $z$-intercept when $x, y=0$. Therefore the $z=15$.

        The triangle has a base width of $3$ and a height of $15$.

        Therefore the area of the triangle is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\dfrac{3\times15}{2}\\[6pt]
            &=22.5\text{ units$^2$}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
