# AA1024

**Maximum mark: 19**

Consider a plane $\Pi_1$ that passes through the points A$(1,-5,1)$, B$(2,9,-2)$ and C$(4,-3,2)$.

1. Find the cartesian equation of plane $\Pi_1$. [5 marks]

A second and third plane have equations
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_2&: \hspace{2em} x +5y -2z = -1 \\[6pt]
\Pi_3&: \hspace{0.75em}-2x + ay +4z = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

2. 1. Determine the value of $a$ for which the system of equations formed by the three planes has multiple solutions.

    2. In this case, describe geometrically the relationship between the three planes.

    3. Find the equation of the line of intersection in parametric form. [7 marks]

3. Show that the three planes can never have a unique point of intersection. [3 marks]

4. Show that, for $a>1$, the planes will always intersect in three pairs of parallel lines. [4 marks]

1. We find $\vec{\text{AB}}$ and $\vec{\text{AC}}$, two vectors parallel to plane $\Pi_1$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \vec{\text{AB}} &= \textbf{b} - \textbf{a} \\[8pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 2\color{white}{1} \\ 9\color{white}{1} \\ -2\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} - \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1\color{white}{1} \\ -5\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} = \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1\color{white}{1} \\ 14\color{white}{1} \\ -3\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \vec{\text{AC}} &= \textbf{c} - \textbf{a} \\[8pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 4\color{white}{1} \\ -3\color{white}{1} \\ 2\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} - \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1\color{white}{1} \\ -5\color{white}{1} \\ 1\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the vector product formula, we find a common perpendicular to $\vec{\text{AB}}$ and $\vec{\text{AC}}$, which is the normal to plane $\Pi_1$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \vec{\text{AB}} \times \vec{\text{AC}} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1\color{white}{1} \\ 14\color{white}{1} \\ -3\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] (14)(1) - (-3)(2) \\ (-3)(3) - (1)(1) \\ (1)(2) - (14)(3) \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 20\color{white}{1} \\ -10\color{white}{1} \\ -40\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= 10\begin{pmatrix*}[r] 2\color{white}{1} \\ -1\color{white}{1} \\ -4\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Plane $\Pi_1$ can be expressed as $2x-y-4z=d$. Substituting A$(1,-5,1)$, we find

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2(1)-(-5)-4(1) &= d \\[8pt]

    d &= 3

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The cartesian equation of $\Pi_1$ is $\boxed{2x-y-4z=3}$

    2. When $a=-10$, the system becomes

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \Pi_1&: \hspace{2.35em} 2x - y -4z = 3 \\[6pt]

        \Pi_2&: \hspace{2.5em} x +5y -2z = -1 \\[6pt]

        \Pi_3&: \hspace{0.75em} -2x -10y +4z = 2

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The equation of $\Pi_3$ is a linear multiple of the equation of $\Pi_2$. They are therefore the same plane.

        $$\boxed{\text{$\Pi_2$ and $\Pi_3$ are coincidental, $\Pi_1$ intersects both planes in a straight line.}}$$

3. We know from part (b) (i) that the system is not unique when $a=-10$. We now consider the case where $a \neq -10$.

    Referring back to the final augmented matrix from part (b) (i), we have

    ![aa1024c](images/aa1024c.PNG)

    From row 3 we have that $(a+10)y = 0$. Given that $a \neq -10$, we must have $y=0$.

    But from row 2, $y = -\dfrac{5}{11}$. Hence we have a contradiction. If $a \neq -10$, the system is inconsistent.

4. The line of intersection of two planes is parallel to the cross product of their normal vectors. Using the vector product formula, we first find the cross product of the normal vectors to planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 2\color{white}{1} \\ -1\color{white}{1} \\ -4\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1\color{white}{1} \\ 5\color{white}{1} \\ -2\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] (-1)(-2) - (-4)(5) \\ (-4)(1) - (2)(-2) \\ (2)(5) - (-1)(1) \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 22 \\ 0 \\ 11 \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= 11\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Next, we find the cross product of the normal vectors to planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_3$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 2\color{white}{1} \\ -1\color{white}{1} \\ -4\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2\color{white}{1} \\ a\color{white}{1} \\ 4\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] (-1)(4) - (-4)(a) \\ (-4)(-2) - (2)(4) \\ (2)(a) - (-1)(-2) \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4a-4 \\ 0 \\ 2a-2 \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= (2a-2)\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Finally, we find the cross product of the normal vectors to planes $\Pi_2$ and $\Pi_3$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \begin{pmatrix*}[r] 1\color{white}{1} \\ 5\color{white}{1} \\ -2\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[r] -2\color{white}{1} \\ a\color{white}{1} \\ 4\color{white}{1} \end{pmatrix*} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] (5)(4) - (-2)(a) \\ (-2)(-2) - (1)(4) \\ (1)(a) - (5)(-2) \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2a+20 \\ 0 \\ a+10 \end{pmatrix*} \\[18pt]

    &= (a+10)\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    For $a>1$, the direction vectors of all three lines of intersection are positive multiples of the vector $2\textbf{i} + \textbf{k}$. Hence,

    $$\boxed{\text{For $a>1$, the planes always intersect in three pairs of parallel lines.}}$$
