# AA1065

**Maximum mark: 18**

Consider the following three points $A(4,-2,-2)$, $B(-3,-1,2)$ and $C(1,-3,-1)$.

1.  1.  Find the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$.

    2. Find  $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}$.

    3. Hence, show that the area of the triangle formed by $A, B$ and $C$ is $\tfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{6}$.[7 marks]

Plane $\Pi_1$ contains the points $A$, $B$ and $C$.

2. Find the Cartesian equation of plane $\Pi_1$. [2 marks]

Plane $\Pi_2$ has the Cartesian equation $-2x+2y=8+4z$.

3. Describe the geometrical relationship between $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$. [1 mark]

Point $D$ is a reflection of point $A$ in $\Pi_2$.

The pyramid $ABCD$ is such that its base is formed using the points $A, B$ and $C$ with its vertex being at point $D$.

4. Find the volume of pyramid $ABCD$. [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Consider
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \overrightarrow{AB}&=\overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} &\overrightarrow{AC}=\overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OA}\\[3pt]
            &=\begin{pmatrix}-3-(4) \\-1-(-2) \\2-(-2)\\\end{pmatrix} &\overrightarrow{AC}=\begin{pmatrix}1-(4) \\-3-(-2) \\-1-(-2)\\\end{pmatrix}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[12pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\begin{pmatrix}-7 \\1 \\4\\\end{pmatrix} &=\hspace{0.525em}\begin{pmatrix}-3 \\-1 \\1\\\end{pmatrix}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[12pt]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2. Using the formula for the vector cross product
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
            \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}&=\begin{pmatrix}1(1)-4(-1) \\4(-3)-(-7)(1) \\-7(-1)-1(-3)\\\end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}\\[12pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\begin{pmatrix}5\\-5\\10\\\end{pmatrix}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    3. The area of triangle $ABC$ is half of the parallelogram formed from $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, using the formula for the area of a parallelogram we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{\triangle ABC}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\right|\\[3pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{(5)^2+(-5)^2+(10)^2}\tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}\\[3pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{150}\\[3pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\tfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{6}\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2. The direction of the answer from (a)(ii) is the normal to the plane, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textbf{n}&=\frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix}5\\-5\\10\\\end{pmatrix}\\[3pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\\\end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the normal plane equation and choosing point $A$ as a point on the plane (note: you can choose point $B$ or $C$ and get the same result)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\\\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\\\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\\\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}4\\-2\\-2\\\end{pmatrix}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{-4.525em}x-y+2z=2\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
3. Rearrange $\Pi_2$ and then divide by $-2$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -2x+2y-4z&=8\\[3pt]
        x-y+2z&=-4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    By comparing the coefficients of $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ we can see that

    $\Pi_1 \text{ and } \Pi_2 \text{ are parallel}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
4. We know that $D$ is a reflection in $\Pi_2$ therefore $AD$ is twice the distance between the planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    To find the distance between the two planes we must find the line through $A$ which is perpendicular to $\Pi_1$, the line intersects $\Pi_2$ at $X$. This is shown below

    ![vec1](images/vec1.png)

    We can write the relationship $AD=2AX$.

    The equation of the line through $A$ and parallel to $\textbf{n}$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textbf{r}&=\begin{pmatrix}4\\-2\\-2\\\end{pmatrix}+\lambda \begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\\\end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can use the Cartesian form of $\Pi_2$ to find the value of $\lambda$ at point $X$, where the line intersects the plane.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (4+\lambda)-(-2-\lambda)+2(-2+2\lambda)&=-4\\
        \lambda&=-1 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textbf{r}&=\begin{pmatrix}4\\-2\\-2\\\end{pmatrix}+(-1) \begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\2\\\end{pmatrix}\\
        &=\begin{pmatrix}3\\-1\\-4\\\end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore we can write the coordinates $X(3, -1, -4)$.

    Next we need to find the distance between points $A$ and $X$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        D_{AX}&=\sqrt{(4-3)^2+(-2-(-1))^2+(-2-(-4))^2}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\
        &=\sqrt{6}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can find the length of $AD$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        AD&=2AX\\
        &=2\times\sqrt{6}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the formula for the volume of a pyramid
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        V_{p}&=\frac{1}{3}Ah
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Where $A$ is the base area and $h$ is the height.

    We can recognise that the height of the pyramid is the length $AD$ and the base area is the answer we found in (a)(iii), this is shown below (not to scale)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![Screenshot 2024-02-06 at 6.00.32 PM](<images/Screenshot 2024-02-06 at 6.00.32 PM.png>)

    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        V_p&=\frac{1}{3}\times 2\sqrt{6}\times \tfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{6}\\[3pt]
        &=\boxed{10 \text{ units}^3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
