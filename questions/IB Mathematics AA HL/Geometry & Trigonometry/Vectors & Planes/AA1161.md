# AA1161

Consider the points $A(1,0,-1)$, $B(-1,2,1)$ and $C(3,-1,-1)$.

The plane $\Pi$ contains the points $A$, $B$ and $C$.

1.  1.  Find the column vectors $\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{AC}$.
    2.  Hence, show that the  vector $\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}$  is normal to plane $\Pi$.
    3.  Hence, show that the Cartesian equation of  plane $\Pi$ is $x+2y-z=2$. [7 marks]

A fourth point $D(3,1,-3)$ does **not** lie in plane $\Pi$.

2.  Write down a vector equation of the line through $D$ which is perpendicular to plane $\Pi$. [1 mark]

Point $D$ is reflected in plane $\Pi$. The reflected point is labelled $E$.

3.  Find the coordinates of point $E$. [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  First find $\vec{AB}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \vec{AB} &= \vec{OB} - \vec{OA}\\[6pt]
            &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}-\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}\\[12pt]
            \vec{AB}\hspace{1em}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Next find $\vec{AC}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \vec{AC} &= \vec{OC} - \vec{OA}\\[6pt]
            &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ -1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}-\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}\\[12pt]
            \vec{AC}\hspace{1em}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  As both
        $\vec{AB}$ and $\vec{AC}$ are vectors in plane $\Pi$ then the normal vector to the plane will be their vector cross product, hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \bm{n}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\\[12pt]
            &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2(0)-2(-1) \\ 2(2)-(-2)(0) \\ -2(-1)-2(2) \end{pmatrix*} \\[12pt]
            &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2em} \text{... as required.}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        3.  To use formula for the equation of a plane we need the normal to the plane and the position vector of any point in the plane. In this answer we will use point $A$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \begin{pmatrix*}[c] x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix*}\cdot \bm{n}&=\vec{OA}\cdot \bm{n}\\[12pt]
            \begin{pmatrix*}[c] x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix*}\cdot \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}\cdot \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}\\[6pt]
            2x+4y-2z&=1(2)+0(4)+(-1)(-2)\\[6pt]
            2x+4y-2z&=4\\[6pt]
            &\hspace{-4.6em}x+2y-z=2 \hspace{2em} \text{... as required.}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  The correct answer can have any multiple of the normal vector. Here is the most likely answer
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \bm{r}=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix*}+\lambda\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  First we realise that point $E$ lies on the line found in part (b).

    ![1161_a](images/1161_a.png)

    We can now state the steps to finding the coordinates of $E$.
    - First find the point $X$ which is the intersection point of the line and plane.
    - Find the vector $\vec{DX}$.
    - Note that $\vec{DX}=\vec{XE}$.
    \item Find the coordinates of point $E$.

    Let's begin by finding the intersection point of the plane and line, $X$

    ![1161_b](images/1161_b.png)

    Recall, from part (b) we know that any point on the line can be represented using a single parameter. Therefore we can write the general coordinates of any point on the line as $(3+\lambda, 1+2\lambda, -3-\lambda)$.

    To find the intersection of the plane and line we substitute this into the Cartesian plane equation found in part (a)(iii) and solve for $\lambda$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        3+\lambda + 2(1+2\lambda) - (-3-\lambda)&=2\\[6pt]
        3+\lambda+2+4\lambda+3+\lambda&=2\\[6pt]
        8+6\lambda&=2\\[6pt]
        6\lambda&=-6\\[6pt]
        \lambda&=-1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting this into the general point we find $X$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (3+(-1), 1+2(-1), -3-(-1))\\[6pt]
        X(2, -1, -2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next we find the vector $\vec{DX}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \vec{DX}&=\vec{OX}-\vec{OD}\\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}-\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix*}\\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![1161_c](images/1161_c.png)

    As shown above we notice that $\textcolor{red}{\vec{DX}}$ is equal to $\textcolor{blue}{\vec{XE}}$ as $E$ is a reflection in the plane, hence we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \vec{DX}&=\vec{XE}\\[6pt]
        \vec{DX}&=\vec{OE}-\vec{OX}\\[6pt]
        \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}&=\vec{OE}-\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}\\[12pt]
        \vec{OE}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}+\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}\\[12pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ -3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $\hspace{0.525em}E(1,-3,-1)$
