# AA1069

**Maximum mark: 7**

Find the minimum distance between the point $A(-5, 0, 1)$ and the plane

$\textbf{\textit{r}}= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} + \lambda\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} + \mu \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -3 \\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}$

in the form $a\sqrt{b}$, where $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

The vector equation of the plane provides us with a point on the plane, $(1, 1, 2)$, and two direction vectors in the plane.

To begin with we must find the Cartesian form of the plane. This will allow us to find the coordinates of any points of intersection of the plane and a line.

If we cross the two direction vectors in the plane we will find the normal to the plane. Hence

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textbf{n}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} \times \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}\\[15pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1\times 2 - (-4) \times (3) \\ 3 \times (-3) - (-1)\times 2 \\ (-1)\times(-4) - 1\times(-3) \end{pmatrix*}\\[15pt]

    &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 14\\ -7\\ 7\end{pmatrix*}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

As we just need the normal as a direction we can divide the components by $7$, hence 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textbf{n}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}

\end{aligned}

$$

We can use the normal equation of a plane with the point $(1, 1, 2)$ given in the question.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \textbf{r} \cdot \textbf{n}&=\textbf{n} \cdot \textbf{a} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    \begin{pmatrix*}[c] x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix*}\cdot \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} \cdot \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\\[15pt]

    2x-y+z&=3 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

The point $B$ is the point on the plane closest to point $A$. The line through $A$ and $B$ is parallel to $\textbf{n}$. 

![aa1069a](images/aa1069a.PNG)

Hence we can write the vector equation of the line through $A$ and $B$

$$

\begin{aligned}

   \textbf{r}&=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -5 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} + t\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} 

\end{aligned}

$$

Where $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

Next we find the coordinates of point $B$ by substituting the vector equation of the line into the Cartesian form of the plane and solving for $t$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    2(-5+2t)-(-t)+(1+t)&=3\\[3pt]

    -10+4t+t+1+t&=3\\[3pt]

    t&=2 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Hence the position vector of $B$ is

$$

\begin{aligned}

   &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -5 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} + 2\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\\[15pt]

   &=\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1 \\ -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*}

\end{aligned}

$$

Finally we must find the distance between $A(-5, 0, 1)$ and $(-1, -2, 3)$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    D^2&=(-5-(-1))^2+(0-(-2))^2+(1-3)^2) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\

    D&=\sqrt{24}

\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore the shortest distance between the point and plane is $\boxed{ 2\sqrt{6}}$.
