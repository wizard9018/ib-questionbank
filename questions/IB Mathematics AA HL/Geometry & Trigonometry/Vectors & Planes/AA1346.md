# AA1346

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the three planes
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \Pi_1&:-x+y-2z=-1\\[6pt]
    \Pi_2&:2x-y-z=d\\[6pt]
    \Pi_3&:-x-y+cz=2
\end{aligned}
$$
where $c,d \in \mathbb{Q^+}$.
The three planes intersect at the line $L$.

1.    Show that $c=8$ and $d=0.5$. [4 marks]

2.    Determine the shortest distance from $L$ to the origin $(0,0,0)$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. There are several approaches to solve this system of equations.

    In this solution, we will use Gaussian elimination.

    We start by combining $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ to eliminate $x$.

    $2 \times \textcolor{red}{\Pi_1} + \textcolor{blue}{\Pi_2}$ gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2\textcolor{red}{(-x+y-2z)}+\textcolor{blue}{(2x-y-z)}&=2\textcolor{red}{(-1)}+\textcolor{blue}{d}\\[6pt]
    y-5z&=-2+d \hspace{2em}[1]\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next we will combine $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_3$ to eliminate $x$.

    $\textcolor{red}{\Pi_1} -\textcolor{green}{\Pi_3}$ gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textcolor{red}{-x+y-2z}-\textcolor{green}{(-x-y+cz)}&=\textcolor{red}{-1}-\textcolor{green}{2}\\[6pt]
    -x+y-2z+x+y-cz)&=-3\\[6pt]
    2y-(2+c)z&=-3 \hspace{2em}[2]\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We now have a system of two equations with two unknowns.

    Next, we combine [1] and [2] to eliminate $y$.

    $2 \times \textcolor{red}{[1]} - \textcolor{blue}{[2]}$ gives
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2 \times \textcolor{red}{(y-5z)}-\textcolor{blue}{(2y-(2+c)z)}&=2\times\textcolor{red}{(-2+d)}-\textcolor{blue}{(-3)}\\[6pt]
    -10z+(2+c)z&=-4+2d+3\\[6pt]
    cz-8z&=-1+2d\\[6pt]
    (c-8)z&=2d-1\hspace{2em}[3]\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    For all three planes to intersect at a line, there must be an infinite number of solutions for $z$ in equation $[3]$.

    Therefore $[3]$ must be of the form
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \textcolor{red}{0}z&=\textcolor{blue}{0}\tag*{\textbf{{R1}}}\\[6pt]
    \textcolor{red}{(c-8)}z&=\textcolor{blue}{2d-1}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, we can find $c$ and $d$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    c-8&=0&&&2d-1&=0\\[6pt]
    c&=8&&&d&=0.5\hspace{1em}\text{... as required.}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. Using the values of $\textcolor{red}{c=8}$ and $\textcolor{blue}{d=0.5}$ we can use a G.D.C. to solve the following system of equations.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -x+y-2z&=-1\\[6pt]
    2x-y-z&=\textcolor{blue}{0.5}\\[6pt]
    -x-y+\textcolor{red}{8}z&=2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The output from our G.D.C. will look something like this
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (3\cdot s-0.5,5 \cdot s-1.5, s )\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    This output represents a line with a parameter of $s$.

    We could also express this answer as a vector equation of a line as such
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm{r}&=\begin{pmatrix} -0.5 \\ -1.5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}+s\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we let the distance from a point on $\textcolor{blue}{L}$ to the origin be $\textcolor{red}{D}$ then we are looking to find the minimum value of $D$.

    ![AA1346-b1](images/AA1346-b1.JPEG)

    Using the distance formula to find the distance between $(3\cdot s-0.5,5 \cdot s-1.5, s )$ and the origin, we can generate a function for $D$ in terms of the parameter $s$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    D&=\sqrt{(3s-0.5)^2+(5s-1.5)^2+s^2}\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Sketching this on a G.D.C., using $x$ for $s$, we find

    ![AA1346-b2](images/AA1346-b2.JPEG)

    the minimum value of $D$ to be $0.431\,\text{units}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textcolor{black}{\textbf{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note, this minimum value of $D$ (the $y$-coordinate) occurs when $s=0.2571...$ (the $x$-coordinate).
