# AA325

**Maximum mark: 21**

Two planes have equations
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{\Pi}_1:\hspace{0.5em} 2x - 2y + z = 1 \hspace{0.75em}\text{and}\hspace{0.75em} \mathit{\Pi}_2:\hspace{0.5em} x - 4y - z = 5. \\
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Find the cosine of the angle between the two planes, giving your
    answer

    in the form $\dfrac{\sqrt{p}}{q}$ where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
     [4 marks]

Let $\mathit{L}$ be the line of intersection of the two planes.

2.
    1.  Show that $\mathit{L}$ has direction
        $2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}$.

    2.  Show that the point A$(4,1,-5)$ lies on both planes.
    3.  Write down the vector equation of $\mathit{L}$. [6 marks]

B is the point on $\mathit{\Pi}_1$ with coordinates $(a,b,3)$.

3.  Given that the vector $\vec{\text{AB}}$ is perpendicular to
    $\mathit{L}$, find the value of $a$ and the
    value of $b$. [5 marks]

4.  Show that $\text{AB} = 12$. [1 mark]

The point C lies on $\mathit{L}$ and $\text{A\^BC} = \ang{60}$.

5.  Find the coordinates of the two possible positions of C.
    [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10em} \mathbf{n}_1 &= 2\textbf{i}-2\textbf{j}+\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_1$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{n}_2 &= \phantom{4}\textbf{i}-4\textbf{j}-\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the angle between two vectors formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8em} \cos \theta &= \dfrac{\mathbf{n}_1\hspace{-0.1em}\cdot\mathbf{n}_2}{|\mathbf{n}_1||\mathbf{n}_2|} \\[7pt]
    &= \dfrac{(2)(1) + (-2)(-4) + (1)(-1)}{\big[\sqrt{2^2+(-2)^2+1^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{1^2+(-4)^2+(-1)^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{2 + 8 - 1}{\big[\sqrt{4 + 4 + 1}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{1 + 16 + 1}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{9}{\big[\sqrt{9}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{18}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[5pt]
    &= \dfrac{\cancel{9}}{\cancel{9}\sqrt{2}} \\[7pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Using the vector product formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.9em} \mathbf{n}_1\times\mathbf{n}_2 &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \hspace{0.25em} \textbf{i} & \hspace{0.8em}\textbf{j} & \textbf{k} \\ \hspace{0.25em}2 & -2 & 1 \\ \hspace{0.25em}1 & -4 & -1 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[8pt]
        &= [2-(-4)]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [-2-1]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [-8-(-2)]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[12pt]
        &= 6\textbf{i} + 3\textbf{j} - 6\textbf{k} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the direction of $L$ is

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{1}{3}(\mathbf{n}_1\times\mathbf{n}_2) &= \dfrac{1}{3}(6\textbf{i} + 3\textbf{j} - 6\textbf{k}) \hspace{5em} \\[8pt]
        &= 2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the coordinates of A$(4,1,-5)$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathit{\Pi}_1 :&\hspace{0.75em} 2(4) - 2(1) - 5 = 1 \\[6pt]
        \mathit{\Pi}_2 :&\hspace{0.75em} 4 - 4(1) - (-5) = 5 \hspace{1em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence A lies on both planes.

    3.
        $\mathbf{r} = 4\textbf{i} + \textbf{j}-5\textbf{k} + t\hspace{0.05em}(2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}),\hspace{0.25em} t \in \mathbb{R}$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{5.3em} \vec{\text{AB}} &= \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
    &= (a\textbf{i}+b\textbf{j}+3\textbf{k}) - (4\textbf{i} + \textbf{j}-5\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= (a-4)\textbf{i}+(b-1)\textbf{j}+8\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the scalar product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \vec{\text{AB}} \cdot (2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\vec{\text{AB}} \perp L$}] \\[6pt]
    \hspace{1.1em} ((a-4)\textbf{i}+(b-1)\textbf{j}+8\textbf{k}) \cdot (2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    (a-4)(2) + (b-1)(1) + (8)(-2) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    2a + b &= 25
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting the coordinates
    of B$(a,b,3)$ in the equation of $\mathit{\Pi}_1$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10em} 2(a) - 2(b) + 3 &= 1 \hspace{3em} [\text{since B lies on $\mathit{\Pi}_1$}] \\[6pt]
    2a - 2b &= -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, if we solve the
    system of linear equations $(2)$-$(3)$ for $a$ and $b$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    a = 8\hspace{0.75em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.75em}b = 9
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Substituting $a = 8$ and $b = 9$ in the expression $(1)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.15em} \vec{\text{AB}} &= (8-4)\textbf{i}+(9-1)\textbf{j}+8\textbf{k} \\[6pt]
    &= 4\textbf{i}+8\textbf{j}+8\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    evaluating the length of $\vec{\text{AB}}$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} \text{AB} &= |4\textbf{i}+8\textbf{j}+8\textbf{k}| \hspace{2.3em} \\[6pt]
    &= \textstyle \sqrt{4^2 + 8^2 + 8^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{144} \\[8pt]
    &= 12
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  We have

    ![2a10c66bfb9743b0d8821a4e9ee0fd42d4140d87.svg](images/2a10c66bfb9743b0d8821a4e9ee0fd42d4140d87.svg)

    Since C lies on $L$, the vector $\vec{\text{AC}}$ is parallel to
    $2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}$.

    Hence we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10.2em} \vec{\text{AC}} &= \lambda\hspace{0.05em}(2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}) \hspace{3em} [\text{for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Evaluating the length of $\vec{\text{AC}}$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.6em} \text{AC} &= |\lambda\hspace{0.05em}(2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k})| \\[6pt]
    &= |\lambda|\textstyle \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2 + (-2)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= |\lambda|\sqrt{9} \\[6pt]
    &= 3|\lambda| \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the triangle ABC, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan \ang{60} &= \dfrac{\text{AC}}{\text{AB}} \\[6pt]
    \sqrt{3} &= \dfrac{3|\lambda| }{12} \\[6pt]
    \sqrt{3} &= \dfrac{|\lambda|}{4} \\[10pt]
    |\lambda| &= 4\sqrt{3} \\[13pt]
    \lambda &= \pm 4\sqrt{3} \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \vec{\text{OC}} &= \vec{\text{OA}} + \vec{\text{AC}} \\[6pt]
    &= (4\textbf{i} + \textbf{j}-5\textbf{k}) \pm 4\sqrt{3}\hspace{0.05em}(2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} - 2\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= (4 \pm 8\sqrt{3})\textbf{i} + (1 \pm 4\sqrt{3})\textbf{j} + (-5 \mp 8\sqrt{3})\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the two possible positions of C have coordinates

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \hspace{0.525em}(4 + 8\sqrt{3}, 1 + 4\sqrt{3}, -5 - 8\sqrt{3})\hspace{0.75em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.75em}(4 - 8\sqrt{3}, 1 - 4\sqrt{3}, -5 + 8\sqrt{3})
     \end{aligned}
     $$
