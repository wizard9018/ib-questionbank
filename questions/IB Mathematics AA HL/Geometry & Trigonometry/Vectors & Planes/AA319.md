# AA319

**Maximum mark: 18**

A line $\mathit{L}$ has equations
$\dfrac{x-a}{2} = \dfrac{y-1}{b} = - \dfrac{z - 1}{2}$ where
$a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$.

A plane $\mathit{\Pi}$ has equation $2x + 3y - z = 6$.

1.  Show that $\mathit{L}$ is not perpendicular to $\mathit{\Pi}$.
    [3 marks]

2.  Given that $\mathit{L}$ lies in the plane $\mathit{\Pi}$, find the
    value of $a$ and the value of $b$. [4 marks]

Consider the different case where the acute angle between $\mathit{L}$
and $\mathit{\Pi}$ is $\alpha$

where $\alpha = \arcsin\hspace{-0.2em}\bigg(\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{14}}\bigg)$.

3.
    1.  Show that $b = 1$.

    2.  Given that $\mathit{L}$ intersects $\mathit{\Pi}$ at $z = -3$,
        find the value of $a$. [11 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{12em} \mathbf{n} &= 2\textbf{i}+3\textbf{j}-\textbf{k} \hspace{3.8em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{d} &= 2\textbf{i}+b\textbf{j}-2\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{direction vector of $L$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since $\mathbf{n} \neq \lambda\hspace{0.05em} \mathbf{d}$, for any
    $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, $\mathbf{n}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ are not
    proportional.

    Hence $\mathit{L}$ is not perpendicular to $\mathit{\Pi}$.

2.  Substituting $x = a + 2t$, $y = 1 + bt$, $z = 1 - 2t$ in the
    equation of $\mathit{\Pi}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x + 3y - z &= 6 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{2em} 2(a + 2t) + 3(1 + bt) - (1 - 2t) &= 6 \\[6pt]
    2a - 4 + (6 + 3b)\hspace{0.025em}t &= 0 \hspace{2.5em} [\text{for any $t \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, substituting $t = 0$ in the equation $(1)$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2a - 4 + (6 + 3b)(0) &= 0 \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
    2a - 4 &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{6.1em} a = 2 \\[3pt]
    $$
    Hence, substituting
    $a = 2$ and $t = 1$ in the equation $(1)$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2(2) - 4 + (6 + 3b)(1) &= 0 \hspace{2.8em} \\[6pt]
    6 + 3b &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{7em} b = -2
    $$

3.
    1.  Letting $\theta$ be the acute angle between $\mathbf{n}$ and
        $\mathit{L}$, we have

        ![a5d6777ef92f4b78f76e57bd13967f09f9481ca1.svg](images/a5d6777ef92f4b78f76e57bd13967f09f9481ca1.svg)

        where:

        1\. $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \alpha$;

        2\. $\sin \alpha = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{14}}$.

- Hence, using the angle between two vectors formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10.6em} \cos \theta &= \dfrac{\mathbf{n}\hspace{-0.1em}\cdot\mathbf{d}}{|\mathbf{n}||\mathbf{d}|} \\[8pt]
    \cos\hspace{-0.15em}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\Big) &= \dfrac{(2)(2) + (3)(b) + (-1)(-2)}{\big[\sqrt{2^2+3^2+(-1)^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{2^2+b^2+(-2)^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    \sin \alpha &= \dfrac{4 + 3b + 2}{\big[\sqrt{4 + 9 + 1}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{4 + b^2 + 4}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{14}} &= \dfrac{3(2 + b)}{\big[\sqrt{14}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{8+b^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[7pt]
    1 &= \dfrac{2 + b}{\sqrt{8 + b^2}} \\[14pt]
    \textstyle \sqrt{8 + b^2} &= 2 + b \\[15pt]
    8 + b^2 &= 4 + 4b + b^2 \\[16pt]
    4 &= 4b \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    b = 1 \hspace{5em}
    $$

    2.  Substituting $b = 1$ and $z = -3$ in the Cartesian
    equations of $\mathit{L}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1em} \dfrac{x-a}{2} &= \dfrac{y-1}{1} = - \dfrac{(-3) - 1}{2} \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{x-a}{2} &= \hspace{0.125em}y-1\hspace{0.125em} = 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, solving the equations above for $x$ and $y$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x &= a + 4 \\[6pt]
    y &= 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, substituting $x = a + 4$, $y = 3$,
    $z = -3$ in the equation of $\mathit{\Pi}$, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x + 3y - z &= 6 \hspace{3.2em} \\[6pt]
    2(a + 4) + 3(3) - (-3) &= 6 \\[6pt]
    2a + 20 &= 6 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{6.9em} a = -7
    $$
