# AA315

**Maximum mark: 22**

The points A, B and C have the following position vectors with respect
to an origin O.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{\text{OA}} &= \textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}+3\textbf{k} \\[6pt]
\vec{\text{OB}} &= 3\textbf{i}-2\textbf{j}+\textbf{k}  \\[6pt]
\vec{\text{OC}} &=  3\textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}-\textbf{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Find the vector equation of the line (AB). [3 marks]

2.  Determine whether the lines (AB) and (OC) are parallel, skew or
    intersecting.[6 marks]

3.  Find the Cartesian equation of the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_1$, that
    passes through the point A and is perpendicular to $\vec{\text{OC}}$.
    [3 marks]

4.  Show that the line (AB) lies in the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_1$.
    [2 marks]

The plane $\mathit{\Pi}_2$ contains the points O, A and C and the plane
$\mathit{\Pi}_3$ contains the points O, B and C.

5.  Verify that $4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}$ is
    perpendicular to the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_2$. [3 marks]

6.  Find a vector perpendicular to the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_3$.
    [1 mark]

7.  Find the acute angle between the planes $\mathit{\Pi}_2$
    and $\mathit{\Pi}_3$, giving your answer correct to the nearest
    degree. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{5.3em} \vec{\text{AB}} &= \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
    &= (3\textbf{i}-2\textbf{j}+\textbf{k}) - (\textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}+3\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= 2\textbf{i}-4\textbf{j}-2\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the vector equation of a line formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{r} &= \vec{\text{OA}} + s\hspace{0.05em}\vec{\text{AB}} \hspace{3.5em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{10.25em} \mathbf{r} = \textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}+3\textbf{k} + s\hspace{0.05em}(2\textbf{i}-4\textbf{j}-2\textbf{k}) \hspace{3em} [s \in \mathbb{R}]
    $$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{(OC)}:&\hspace{0.75em} \mathbf{r} = t\hspace{0.05em}(3\textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}-\textbf{k}) \hspace{3em} [t \in \mathbb{R}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the direction vectors of (AB) and (OC), we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.55em} 2\textbf{i}-4\textbf{j}-2\textbf{k} \neq p\hspace{0.05em}(3\textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}-\textbf{k}) \hspace{2.8em} [\text{for any $p \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence (AB) and (OC) are not parallel. They are either skew or
    intersecting.

    Using the vector equations of (AB) and (OC), we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1 + 2s &= 3t  \\[6pt]
    2 - 4s &= 2t  \\[6pt]
    3 - 2s &= -t
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving the system of linear
    equations $(2)$-$(3)$ for $s$ and $t$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.5em} s = 1 \hspace{0.5em}\&\hspace{0.5em} t = -1 \hspace{9.9em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we try to verify the equation $(1)$ for values $(4)$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1 + 2(1) &\neq 3(-1) \hspace{1.4em} \\[6pt]
    3 &\neq -3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the system of linear equations
    $(1)$-$(2)$-$(3)$ has no solution.

    Therefore (AB) and (OC) are not intersecting. They are skew.

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.5em} \mathbf{n}_1 &= 3\textbf{i}+2\textbf{j}-\textbf{k} \hspace{2em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_1$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{a} &= \textbf{i} + 2\textbf{j} + 3\textbf{k} \hspace{2em} [= \vec{\text{OA}}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the scalar product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathbf{n}_1\cdot\mathbf{a} &= (3)(1) + (2)(2) + (-1)(3) \\[6pt]
    &= 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the equation of a plane formula, we
    obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.6em} \mathbf{n}_1\cdot\mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{a} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    3x + 2y - z = 4  \hspace{3.6em}
    $$

4.  If we substitute $x = 1+2s$, $y = 2-4s$, $z = 3-2s$ in the left hand
    side of the equation $(5)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.35em} 3x + 2y - z &= 3(1+2s) + 2(2-4s) - (3-2s) \\[6pt]
    &= (3 + 4 - 3) + (6s - 8s + 2s) \\[6pt]
    &= 4 \hspace{3em} [\text{for any  $s \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence (AB) lies in the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_1$.

5.  Using the scalar product formula, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}) \cdot \vec{\text{OA}} &= (4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}) \cdot (\textbf{i} + 2\textbf{j} + 3\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= (4)(1) + (-5)(2) + (2)(3) \\[6pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the scalar product formula, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}) \cdot \vec{\text{OC}} &= (4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}) \cdot (3\textbf{i} + 2\textbf{j} - \textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= (4)(3) + (-5)(2) + (2)(-1) \\[6pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence $4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}$
    is perpendicular to the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_2$.

6.  Using the vector product formula, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.7em} \vec{\text{OB}} \times \vec{\text{OC}} &= (3\textbf{i} - 2\textbf{j} + \textbf{k})\times(3\textbf{i} + 2\textbf{j} - \textbf{k}) \\[8pt]
    &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \hspace{0.25em}\textbf{i} & \textbf{j} & \textbf{k} \\ \hspace{0.25em}3 & -2 & 1 \\ \hspace{0.25em}3 & 2 & -1 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[8pt]
    &= [2-2]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [-3-3]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [6-(-6)]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[8pt]
    &= 6\textbf{j} + 12\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
     $\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}$
    is perpendicular to the plane $\mathit{\Pi}_3$.

7.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6.25em} \mathbf{n}_2 &= 4\textbf{i} - 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_2$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{n}_3 &= \phantom{4}\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}\phantom{ - 5\textbf{j}} \hspace{3.415em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_3$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the angle between two vectors formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.8em} \cos \theta &= \dfrac{\mathbf{n}_2\hspace{-0.1em}\cdot\mathbf{n}_3}{|\mathbf{n}_2||\mathbf{n}_3|} \\[6pt]
    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{(4)(0) + (-5)(1) + (2)(2)}{\big[\sqrt{4^2+(-5)^2+2^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{0^2+1^2+2^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[10pt]
    \theta &\approx \ang{94}  \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the acute angle between $\mathit{\Pi}_2$ and $\mathit{\Pi}_3$
    is $\ang{180} - \ang{94} = \hspace{0.525em}\ang{86}$
