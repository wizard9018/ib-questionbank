# AA318

**Maximum mark: 21**

Two planes have equations
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{\Pi}_1:\hspace{0.5em} 2x + 4y + z = 9 \hspace{0.75em}\text{and}\hspace{0.75em} \mathit{\Pi}_2:\hspace{0.5em} 2x + y - z = 1. \\
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Find the cosine of the angle between the two planes, giving your
    answer

    in the form $\dfrac{\sqrt{p}}{q}$ where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
     [4 marks]

Let $\mathit{L}$ be the line of intersection of the two planes.

2.
    1.  Show that $\mathit{L}$ has direction
        $5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}$.

    2.  Show that the point P$(0,2,1)$ lies on both planes.
    3.  Write down the vector equation of $\mathit{L}$. [6 marks]

Q is the point on $\mathit{\Pi}_1$ with coordinates $(a,1,b)$.

3.  Given that the vector $\vec{\text{PQ}}$ is perpendicular to
    $\mathit{L}$, find the value of $a$ and the
    value of $b$. [5 marks]

4.  Show that $\text{PQ} = \sqrt{33}$. [1 mark]

The point R lies on $\mathit{L}$ and $\text{P\^QR} = \ang{30}$.

5.  Find the coordinates of the two possible positions of R.
    [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10em} \mathbf{n}_1 &= 2\textbf{i}+4\textbf{j}+\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_1$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{n}_2 &= 2\textbf{i}+\phantom{4}\textbf{j}-\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{normal vector to $\mathit{\Pi}_2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the angle between two vectors formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8em} \cos \theta &= \dfrac{\mathbf{n}_1\hspace{-0.1em}\cdot\mathbf{n}_2}{|\mathbf{n}_1||\mathbf{n}_2|} \\[7pt]
    &= \dfrac{(2)(2) + (4)(1) + (1)(-1)}{\big[\sqrt{2^2+4^2+1^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{2^2+1^2+(-1)^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{4 + 4 - 1}{\big[\sqrt{4 + 16 + 1}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{4 + 1 + 1}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{7}{\big[\sqrt{21}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{6}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{7}{3\sqrt{14}} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{6}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Using the vector product formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2em} \mathbf{n}_1\times\mathbf{n}_2 &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \hspace{0.25em} \textbf{i} & \hspace{0.8em}\textbf{j} & \textbf{k} \\ \hspace{0.25em}2 & 4 & 1 \\ \hspace{0.25em}2 & 1 & -1 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[8pt]
        &= [-4-1]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [-2-2]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [2-8]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[12pt]
        &= -5\textbf{i} + 4\textbf{j} - 6\textbf{k} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the direction of $L$ is

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        -(\mathbf{n}_1\times\mathbf{n}_2) &= -(-5\textbf{i} + 4\textbf{j} - 6\textbf{k}) \hspace{5.8em} \\[8pt]
        &= 5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the coordinates of P$(0,2,1)$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathit{\Pi}_1 :&\hspace{0.75em} 2(0) + 4(2) + 1 = 9 \\[6pt]
        \mathit{\Pi}_2 :&\hspace{0.75em} 2(0) + 2 - 1 = 1
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence P lies on both planes.

    3.
        $\mathbf{r} = 2\textbf{j}+\textbf{k} + t\hspace{0.05em}(5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}),\hspace{0.25em} t \in \mathbb{R}$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.7em} \vec{\text{PQ}} &= \vec{\text{OQ}} - \vec{\text{OP}} \\[6pt]
    &= (a\textbf{i}+\textbf{j}+b\textbf{k}) - (2\textbf{j}+\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= a\textbf{i}-\textbf{j}+(b-1)\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the scalar product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \vec{\text{PQ}} \cdot (5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}) &= 0 \hspace{2em} [\text{since $\vec{\text{PQ}} \perp L$}] \\[6pt]
    \hspace{0.8em} (a\textbf{i}-\textbf{j}+(b-1)\textbf{k}) \cdot (5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    (a)(5) + (-1)(-4) + (b-1)(6) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    5a + 6b &= 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting the coordinates
    of Q$(a,1,b)$ in the equation of $\mathit{\Pi}_1$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{11em} 2(a) + 4(1) + b &= 9 \hspace{2em} [\text{since Q lies on $\mathit{\Pi}_1$}] \\[6pt]
    2a + b &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, if we solve the system
    of linear equations $(2)$-$(3)$ for $a$ and $b$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    a = 4\hspace{0.75em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.75em}b = -3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Substituting $a = 4$ and $b = -3$ in the expression $(1)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.4em} \vec{\text{PQ}} &= 4\textbf{i}-\textbf{j}+(-3-1)\textbf{k} \\[6pt]
    &= 4\textbf{i}-\textbf{j}-4\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    evaluating the length of $\vec{\text{PQ}}$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{PQ} &= |4\textbf{i}-\textbf{j}-4\textbf{k}| \hspace{2.3em} \\[6pt]
    &= \textstyle \sqrt{4^2 + 1^2 + 4^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{33}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  We have

    ![6fbc0cff0e0fb28260be5afe58baf5846511d2d8.svg](images/6fbc0cff0e0fb28260be5afe58baf5846511d2d8.svg)

    Since R lies on $L$, the vector $\vec{\text{PR}}$ is parallel to
    $5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}$.

    Hence we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.2em} \vec{\text{PR}} &= \lambda\hspace{0.05em}(5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}) \hspace{2em} [\text{for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Evaluating the length of $\vec{\text{PR}}$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.6em} \text{PR} &= |\lambda\hspace{0.05em}(5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k})| \\[6pt]
    &= |\lambda|\textstyle \sqrt{5^2 + (-4)^2 + 6^2} \\[6pt]
    &= |\lambda|\textstyle \sqrt{25 + 16 + 36} \\[6pt]
    &= |\lambda|\sqrt{77} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the triangle PQR, we
    obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan \ang{30} &= \dfrac{\text{PR}}{\text{PQ}} \\[8pt]
    \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} &= \dfrac{|\lambda|\sqrt{77}}{\sqrt{33}} \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} &= \dfrac{|\lambda|\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{3}} \\[8pt]
    |\lambda| &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{7}} \\[6pt]
    \lambda &= \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{7}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \vec{\text{OR}} &= \vec{\text{OP}} + \vec{\text{PR}} \\[8pt]
    &= (2\textbf{j} + \textbf{k}) + \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{7}\hspace{0.05em}(5\textbf{i} - 4\textbf{j} + 6\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
    &= \pm\dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{7}\hspace{0.1em}\textbf{i} + \bigg(2 \mp\dfrac{4\sqrt{7}}{7}\bigg)\textbf{j} + \bigg(1 \pm \dfrac{6\sqrt{7}}{7}\bigg)\textbf{k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the two possible positions of R have coordinates

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \hspace{0.525em}\bigg(\dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{7}, 2 - \dfrac{4\sqrt{7}}{7}, 1 + \dfrac{6\sqrt{7}}{7}\bigg)\hspace{0.75em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.75em}\bigg(\hspace{-0.3em}-\dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{7}, 2 + \dfrac{4\sqrt{7}}{7}, 1 - \dfrac{6\sqrt{7}}{7}\bigg)
     \end{aligned}
     $$
