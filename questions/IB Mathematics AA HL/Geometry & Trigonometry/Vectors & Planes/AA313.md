# AA313

**Maximum mark: 12**

The points A and B have coordinates A$(1,0,4)$ and B$(2,2,3)$ relative
to an origin O.

1.
    1.  Find $\vec{\text{OA}}\times\vec{\text{OB}}$.

    2.  Determine the area of the triangle OAB, giving your answer
        correct to two decimal
        places.

    3.  Find the Cartesian equation of the plane OAB. [5 marks]

2.
    1.  Find the vector equation of the line $\mathit{L}_1$ containing
        the points A and B.

    The line $\mathit{L}_2$ has vector equation
    $\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 0 \\ 12 \\ -9 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}s\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -3 \\ 1 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix*}$,
    $s\in\mathbb{R}$.

    2.  Determine whether the lines $\mathit{L}_1$ and
        $\mathit{L}_2$ are parallel, skew or intersecting. If they
        intersect, find the coordinates of the point of intersection.
        [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Using the vector product formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3.4em} \vec{\text{OA}} \times \vec{\text{OB}} &= (\textbf{i} + 4\textbf{k})\times(2\textbf{i} + 2\textbf{j} + 3\textbf{k}) \\[8pt]
        &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[c] \hspace{0.25em}\textbf{i} & \textbf{j} & \textbf{k} \\ \hspace{0.25em}1 & 0 & 4 \\ \hspace{0.25em}2 & 2 & 3 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[8pt]
        &= [0-8]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [3-8]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [2-0]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k}  \\[12pt]
        &= -8\textbf{i} + 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the area of a triangle formula, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} A_{\triangle\text{OAB}} &= \dfrac{1}{2}\big|\vec{\text{OA}} \times \vec{\text{OB}}\big| \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}\big|\hspace{-0.15em}-8\textbf{i} + 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}\big| \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{(-8)^2 + 5^2 + 2^2} \\[10pt]
        &\approx 4.82
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{9.3em} \mathbf{n} &= -8\textbf{i} + 5\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k} \hspace{2em} [\text{normal vector to the plane}] \\[6pt]
        \mathbf{b} &= \phantom{-}2\textbf{i} + 2\textbf{j} + 3\textbf{k} \hspace{2em} [= \vec{\text{OB}}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Using the scalar product formula, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{b} &= (-8)(2) + (5)(2) + (2)(3) \hspace{0.9em}  \\[6pt]
        &= 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, using the equation of a plane
        formula, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{b} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        -8x + 5y + 2z = 0 \hspace{5em}
        $$

2.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{AB}} &= \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
        &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix*} - \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}  \hspace{0.35em} \\[6pt]
        &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, using the vector equation of a line formula, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{r} &= \vec{\text{OA}} + t\hspace{0.05em}\vec{\text{AB}} \hspace{0.5em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{7.4em} \mathbf{r} = \hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em} + \hspace{0.1em} t\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} [t \in \mathbb{R}]
        $$

    2.  Using the direction vectors of $\mathit{L}_1$ and
        $\mathit{L}_2$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{10em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -3 \\ 1 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix*} \neq \hspace{0.1em}k\hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} [\text{for any $k \in \mathbb{R}$}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence $\mathit{L}_1$ and $\mathit{L}_2$ are not parallel. They
        are either skew or intersecting.

        Using the vector equations of $\mathit{L}_1$ and $\mathit{L}_2$,
        we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        -3s &= 1 + t \hspace{1.55em} \\[6pt]
        12 + s &= 2t  \\[6pt]
        -9-4s &= 4 - t
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Solving the system of
        linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$ for $s$ and $t$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{9.9em} s = -2 \hspace{0.5em}\&\hspace{0.5em} t = 5 \hspace{2em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        If we try to verify the equation $(3)$ for values $(4)$, we
        obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        -9 - 4(-2) &= 4 - 5 \hspace{2.95em} \\[6pt]
        -1 &= -1
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence $\mathit{L}_1$ and $\mathit{L}_2$
        are indeed intersecting.

        Substituting $t = 5$ in the vector equation of $\mathit{L}_1$,
        we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{r} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 0 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*} + \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 5 \\ 10 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix*} \\[6pt]
        &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 6 \\ 10 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix*}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the point of intersection of $\mathit{L}_1$ and
        $\mathit{L}_2$ has coordinates
         $(6,10,-1)$
