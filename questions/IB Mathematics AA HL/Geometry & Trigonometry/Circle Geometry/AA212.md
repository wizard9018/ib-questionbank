# AA212

**Maximum mark: 11**

The diagram below shows two circles with centres at the points A and B
and radii $3r$ and $2r$, respectively. The point B lies on the circle
with centre A. The circles intersect at the points C and D.

![afd6e0672a0258953fa2b0338cc01b6683d850f0.svg](images/afd6e0672a0258953fa2b0338cc01b6683d850f0.svg)

Let $\alpha$ be the measure of the angle CAD and $\theta$ be the measure
of the angle CBD in radians.

1.  Find an expression for the shaded area in terms of $\alpha, \theta$
    and $r$.  [3 marks]

2.  Show that $\displaystyle\alpha = 4\arcsin\frac{1}{3}$. [2 marks]

3.  Hence find the value of $r$ given that the shaded area is equal to
    $16$ cm$^2$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The chord CD divides the shaded region into two segments:
    1\. Segment A: the segment of the circle with centre A;

    2\. Segment B: the segment of the circle with centre B.

    The following diagram shows the segment A.

    ![caa3e42d91c8eb082c4acfe1110b1d04276d5890.svg](images/caa3e42d91c8eb082c4acfe1110b1d04276d5890.svg)

    Using the area of a triangle and the area of a sector formulas, we
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.3em} A_{\text{segment\hspace{0.2em}A}} &= A_{\text{sector}} - A_{\triangle\text{ACD}} \\[10pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{2}(3r)^2\alpha - \dfrac{1}{2}(3r)(3r)\sin \alpha \\[6pt]
    &= \frac{9}{2}(\alpha - \sin\alpha)r^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Similarly, we
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.05em} A_{\text{segment\hspace{0.2em}B}} &= \dfrac{1}{2}(2r)^2\theta - \dfrac{1}{2}(2r)(2r)\sin\theta \\[10pt]
    &= 2(\theta - \sin\theta)r^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the shaded area is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.5em} A_{\text{segment\hspace{0.2em}A}} + A_{\text{segment\hspace{0.2em}B}} &=  \hspace{0.525em}\bigg[\frac{9}{2}(\alpha - \sin\alpha) + 2(\theta - \sin\theta)\bigg]r^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Let M be the midpoint of \[BD\].

    ![dda7937ee8d436b10422ba275de3a8ff4ebf9444.svg](images/dda7937ee8d436b10422ba275de3a8ff4ebf9444.svg)

    Using the right triangle ABM, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin \dfrac{\alpha}{4} &= \dfrac{\text{BM}}{\text{AB}} \\[7pt]
    \sin \dfrac{\alpha}{4} &= \dfrac{1}{3} \\[7pt]
    \dfrac{\alpha}{4} &= \arcsin \dfrac{1}{3} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{13.2em} \alpha = 4\arcsin \frac{1}{3} \hspace{3em} [\approx 1.3593 \text{ radians}]
    $$

3.  Using the right triangle ABM, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10.4em} \dfrac{\alpha}{4} + \dfrac{\theta}{2} &= \dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[9pt]
    \theta &= \pi - \frac{\alpha}{2}  \\[5pt]
    \theta &= \pi - \dfrac{1}{2}\bigg[4\arcsin \frac{1}{3}\bigg] \\[5pt]
    \theta &= \pi - 2\arcsin \frac{1}{3} \hspace{3em} [\approx 2.4619 \text{ radians}] \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, solving the equation
    $\bigg[\dfrac{9}{2}(\alpha - \sin\alpha) + 2(\theta - \sin\theta)\bigg]r^2 = 16$
    for $r$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg[\frac{9}{2}(1.3593 - \sin 1.3593) + 2(2.4619 - \sin 2.4619)\bigg]r^2 &= 16 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{9.5em} r \approx 1.72 \text{ cm} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    $$
