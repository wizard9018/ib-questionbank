# AA1334

**Maximum mark: 14**

An architect is designing a concert hall in the shape of a sector with radius, $r\,\text{metres}$, as shown in the following diagram. 

![AA1334qX](images/AA1334qX.JPEG)

The shaded region, $X$, is where the audience sits.

Sector $OAD$, with radius $a$ metres and subtending an angle of $\theta$ radians, represents the stage, where $0 < \theta < \pi$.

1.    Find an expression for the area of $X$ in terms of $r, a$ and $\theta$. [2 marks]

A fence of length $b\,$metres will enclose $X$.

The fence consists of the straight edges $AB$ and $CD$ and the arcs $\overset{\large\frown}{AD}$ and $\overset{\large\frown}{BC}$.

The architect is considering a number of different designs for the concert hall.

For the first design,

2.    $a=15\,$m and $b=200\,$m.

    1.    Find $r$ in terms of $\theta$. 

    2.    Hence find the maximum area of $X$ for the first design. [4 marks]

For the second design,

3.    $r=90\,$m and the area of $X=5000\,$m$^2$.

    1.    Show that $\theta=\dfrac{10\,000}{8\,100-a^2}$. 

    2.    Hence, find the value of $a$ that minimises $b$ for the second design. [5 marks]

For the final design it is decided that the area of sector $OAD$ and the area of $X$ must be in the ratio $1:p$ where $p \in \mathbb{R}$ and $p>65$.

Given that $a=12\,$m, $\theta=1.3$ radians, and $r \in \mathbb{Z}^+$,

4.    find the minimum value of $r$ for the final design. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. To find the area of $X$, $A_X$, we need to subtract the area of the smaller sector from the area of the larger sector.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A_X&=\frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta)-\frac{1}{2}a^2\theta\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    &A_X=\dfrac{1}{2}\theta\,(r^2-a^2)\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2.  1. We need an equation which contains only $r$ and $\theta$ so let's set up a formula for the length of the fence, $b$.  

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        b&=AB+\overset{\large\frown}{BC}+CD+\overset{\large\frown}{AD}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Using the arc length formula and known values we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        b&=(r-a)+r\theta+(r-a)+a\theta\\[6pt]

        200&=2(r-15)+\theta(r+15)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Now we can rearrange this equation to make $r$ the subject.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        200&=2r-30+r\theta+15\theta\\[6pt]

        230-15\theta&=r(2+\theta)\\[6pt]

        r&=\dfrac{230-15\theta}{2+\theta}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. To maximise $A_X$ we need to eliminate one of the variables. 

        Substituting the expression for $r$ from part (b)(i) into the formula from part (a) we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        A_X&=\frac{1}{2}\theta\left(\left(\frac{230-15\theta}{2+\theta}\right)^2-15^2\right)\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Now we can find the maximum point of this function using our G.D.C.

        Note that it is useful to restrict the graph to the given domain of $0\leq \theta \leq \pi$.

        ![AA1334a1](images/AA1334a1.JPEG)

        Hence we obtain the answers

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \theta&=1.25\\[6pt]

        A&=2500\text{ m}^2\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

3.  1. In the second design we know that $r=90$ and $A_X=5\,000$.

        We can substitute these values into the same formula from part (a) and make $\theta$ the subject.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        A_X&=\frac{1}{2}\theta\,(r^2-a^2)\\[6pt]

        5\,000&=\frac{1}{2}\theta\,(90^2-a^2)\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[6pt]

        10\,000&=\theta\,(8\,100-a^2)\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[6pt]

        \theta&=\dfrac{10\,000}{8\,100-a^2}\hspace{2em}\text{as required}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. We want to find the minimum value of $b$ so let's again use the formula from part (b).

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        b&=2(r-a)+\theta(r+a)\\[6pt]

        &=2(90-a)+\theta(90+a)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Substituting the expression from part (d) we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        b&=2(90-a)+\left(\frac{10\,000}{8\,100-a^2}\right)(90+a)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[16pt]

        &=180-2a+\frac{900\,000+10\,000a}{8\,100-a^2}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Now we can find the minimum point of this function using our G.D.C.

        ![AA1334a2](images/AA1334a2.JPEG)

        Hence we obtain the answers

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        a&=19.3\text{ m}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[6pt]

        b&=283\text{ m}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

4. In the last scenario we are given that the ratio of $A_{OAD}:A_X=1:p$.

    If we divide both ratios by the element on the right we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{A_{OAD}}{A_x}:1&=\frac{1}{p}:1

    \end{aligned} 

    $$

    therefore we can write

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{A_{OAD}}{A_x}:1&=\frac{1}{p}:1\\[12pt]

    p \times A_{OAD}&=A_x\\[9pt]

    p\times\left(\frac{1}{2}a^2\theta\right)&=\frac{1}{2}\theta(r^2-a^2)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We are given $a=12$ and $\theta=1.3$, hence 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    p\times\left(\frac{1}{2}(12)^2(1.3)\right)&= \frac{1}{2}(1.3)(r^2-12^2)\\[6pt]

    p\times(93.6)&=0.65(r^2-144)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Next we can write $p$ as the subject and then use the G.D.C. to graph the equation.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    p&=\frac{0.65(r^2-144)}{93.6}\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Recall that $p>65$ and $r\in\mathbb{Z}^+$.

    

    Hence we will need to use the intersection of the function and $y=65$ to find our answer.

    ![AA1334a3](images/AA1334a3.JPEG)

    We need to find the first integer value of $r$ which is larger than $97.48...$.

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}r=98\text{ m}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    If you aren't sure this is the correct value you can always check the value on either side of your answer to see which one results in an answer where  $p>65$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    p(97)&=\frac{97^2-144}{144}\\[6pt]

    &=64.34...\\[10pt]

    p(98)&=\frac{98^2-144}{144}\\[6pt]

    &=65.69...\hspace{2em}\checkmark

    \end{aligned}

    $$
