# AA222

**Maximum mark: 15**

In a triangle XYZ, $\text{XY} = 9$ cm,
$\text{YZ} = x$ cm,
$\text{XZ} = y$ cm and
$\text{X\^{Y}Z} = \ang{45}$.

1.  Using the cosine rule, show that
    $x^2 - 9\sqrt{2}\hspace{0.05em}x + 81 - y^2 = 0$. [2 marks]

Consider the possible triangles with
$\text{XZ} = 7$ cm.

2.  Calculate the two corresponding values of YZ. [3 marks]

3.  Hence find the area of the smaller triangle. [3 marks]

Consider the case where $y$, the length of \[XZ\], is not fixed at
$7$ cm.

4.  Find the range of values of $y$ for which it is possible to
    form two triangles. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the cosine rule, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{11em} \text{XZ}^2 &= \text{XY}^2 + \text{YZ}^2 - 2(\text{XY})(\text{YZ})\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{X\^{Y}Z}) \\[7pt]
    y^2 &= 9^2 + x^2 - 2(9)(x)\cos \ang{45} \\[6pt]
    y^2 &= 81 + x^2 - 9\sqrt{2}\hspace{0.05em}x \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    x^2 - 9\sqrt{2}\hspace{0.05em}x + 81 - y^2 = 0  \hspace{11.4em}
    $$

2.  Substituting $y = 7$ in the equation $(1)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^2 - 9\sqrt{2}\hspace{0.05em}x + 81 - 7^2 &= 0 \hspace{11.4em} \\[6pt]
    x^2 - 9\sqrt{2}\hspace{0.05em}x + 32 &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{14em} x \approx 3.45 \text{ cm},\hspace{0.5em} 9.28 \text{ cm} \hspace{2em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    $$

3.  Using the area of a triangle formula, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.5em} A_{\triangle\text{XYZ}} &= \dfrac{1}{2}(\text{XY})(\text{YZ})\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{X\^{Y}Z}) \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{2}(9)(3.45) \sin \ang{45} \\[10pt]
    &\approx 11.0 \text{ cm}^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The quadratic polynomial
    $p(x) = x^2 - 9\sqrt{2}\hspace{0.05em}x + 81 - y^2$ has two roots
    when
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &> 0 \\[12pt]
    \hspace{2.35em} (-9\sqrt{2})^2 - 4(1)(81-y^2) &> 0 \hspace{3em} [\triangle  = b^2 - 4ac] \\[12pt]
    4y^2 - 162 &> 0 \\[9pt]
    y^2 &> \dfrac{81}{2} \\[3pt]
    y &> \dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $y > 0$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using Vieta's formulas, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{sum of roots} &= 9\sqrt{2} \\[6pt]
    \text{product of roots} &= 81-y^2 \hspace{1.1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence both roots of $p(x)$ are positive when
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    81 - y^2 &> 0 \\[6pt]
    y &< 9 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence it is possible to form two triangles
    when
     $\frac{9\sqrt{2}}{2} < y < 9$
