# AA223

**Maximum mark: 11**

In a triangle ABC,
$$
\begin{aligned}
5\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C}) - 6\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^{C}A}) &= 7, \\[6pt]
6\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^{C}A}) - 5\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C}) &=  \sqrt{2}. \\
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Show that
    $\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A}) = \dfrac{1}{6}$.
    [6 marks]

James claims that CÂB can have two possible values.

2.  Show that James is wrong by proving that CÂB has only one
    possible value.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    25\sin^2(\text{A\^BC}) - 60\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^BC})\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^CA}) + 36\cos^2(\text{B\^CA}) &= 49 \\[6pt]
    36\sin^2(\text{B\^CA}) - 60\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^CA})\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^BC}) + 25\cos^2\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^BC}) &= 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the Pythagorean identity and compound angle identity,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} 25 + 36 - 60\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A}) &= 51 \hspace{3em} [\text{sum of $(1)$ and $(2)$}] \\[10pt]
    10 &= 60\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A}) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A}) =\dfrac{1}{6} \hspace{4.3em}
    $$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.85em} \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{C\^{A}B}) &= \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\ang{180} - (\text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A})) \\[9pt]
    \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{C\^{A}B}) &= \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A}) \\[5pt]
    \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{C\^{A}B}) &= \dfrac{1}{6} \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (a)}] \\[6pt]
    \text{C\^{A}B} &= \arcsin\hspace{0.05em}(1/6), \hspace{0.25em} \ang{180} - \arcsin\hspace{0.05em}(1/6) \\[9pt]
    \text{C\^{A}B} &\approx \ang{10}, \hspace{0.25em} \ang{170} \hspace{3em} [\text{two possible values}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    For $\text{C\^{A}B} = \ang{170}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.3em} \text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A} &= \ang{180} - \text{C\^{A}B} \\[6pt]
    \text{A\^{B}C} + \text{B\^{C}A} &= \ang{10} \\[6pt]
    \text{B\^{C}A} &< \ang{10}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} 7 &= 5\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{B}C}) - 6\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^{C}A}) \\[6pt]  &< 5 - 6\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^{C}A}) \\[6pt]
    &< 5 - 6\cos \ang{10} \\[6pt]
    &< 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{contradiction!}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence there
    can be only one possible value of CÂB and James is wrong.
