# AA1286

**Maximum mark: 7**

A cyclist leaves town $A$ on a bearing of $240^\circ$ and rides $11$ kilometers to town $B$.

The cyclist then travels $d\,$km on a bearing of $090^\circ$ until he is exactly $6\,$km from town $A$.

Find the possible values of $d$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

First let's sketch a diagram to better understand the given information.

We can sketch the approximate positions of towns $A$ and $B$ and also indicate the stopping places of the cyclist by $D_1$ and $D_2$.

Any bearing measures start from the North and turn clockwise.

With all lengths in kilometres, our diagram will look like this.

![AA1286-a1](images/AA1286-a1.JPEG)

Notice that the dashed red circle, with radius $6$ centred on $A$, indicates that there are two places where the cyclist can stop that are $6$ kilometers from town $A$.

Using angle reasoning, we can determine $\angle ABD$.

![AA1286-a2](images/AA1286-a2.JPEG)

$$
\begin{aligned}
    240^\circ-180^\circ&=60^\circ\\[6pt]
    90^\circ-60^\circ&=30^\circ\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
When we add this to the diagram we notice that there are $2$ triangles which have sides of $11$ and $6$ and a non-included angle of $30^\circ$. This is an example of the ambiguous case of the sine rule.

![AA1286-a3](images/AA1286-a3.JPEG)

Using the sine rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sin A}{a}&=\frac{\sin B}{b}=\frac{\sin C}{c}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{\sin D}{11}&=\frac{\sin 30}{6}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
    \sin D&=0.9166...\\[6pt]
    D&=66.44...\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
As $D<90^\circ$, this must be the angle associated with triangle $2$.

Let's complete this triangle to find the distance $d_2$ associated with stopping at $D_2$.

![AA1286-a4](images/AA1286-a4.JPEG)

Finding angle $BAD_2$
$$
\begin{aligned}
    BAD_2&=180^\circ-30^\circ-66.44...^\circ\\[6pt]
    &=83.55...^\circ
\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using sine rule again
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{d_2}{\sin 83.55...^\circ}&=\frac{6}{\sin 30^\circ}\\[6pt]
    d_2&=11.92...\\[6pt]
&=11.9\text{ km}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can see that triangle $AD_1D_2$ is isosceles.

![AA1286-a5](images/AA1286-a5.JPEG)

Hence we can find angle $BD_1A$
$$
\begin{aligned}
    &=180^\circ-66.44...^\circ\\[6pt]
    &=113.5...\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finding angle $BAD_1$ in triangle $1$
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \angle BAD_1&=180^\circ-30^\circ-113.55...^\circ\\[6pt]
    &=36.44...^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

Using sine rule again to find the distance associated with stopping at $D_1$
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{d_1}{\sin 36.44...^\circ}&=\frac{6}{\sin 30^\circ}\\[6pt]
    d_1&=7.128...\\[6pt]
&=7.13\text{ km}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
