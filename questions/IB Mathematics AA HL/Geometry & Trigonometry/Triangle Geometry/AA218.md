# AA218

**Maximum mark: 6**

1.  Find the set of values of $h$ that satisfy the inequality
    $2h^2 - 3h - 14 > 0$. [2 marks]

2.  The triangle ABC is shown in the following diagram.

    ![280703425496e906bb7cacafeebe346ebdda7cce.svg](images/280703425496e906bb7cacafeebe346ebdda7cce.svg)

    Given that $\cos \theta > 0.25$, find the range of possible values
    of $a$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2h^2 - 3h - 14 &= 0 \\[9pt]
    (2h-7)(h+2) &= 0 \\[3pt]
    h &= -2,\hspace{0.25em}\dfrac{7}{2} \hspace{2em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, if we make a sign table, we get

    ![634f8d4b56a7defcf1dea745f8a9eb0cd21e5df5.svg](images/634f8d4b56a7defcf1dea745f8a9eb0cd21e5df5.svg)

    Hence the set of values of $h$ satisfying $2h^2 - 3h - 14 > 0$ is
     $\textstyle (-\infty,-2) \cup (\frac{7}{2},\infty)$

2.  Using the cosine rule, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{AB}^2 &= \text{AC}^2 + \text{BC}^2 - 2(\text{AC})(\text{BC})\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{A\^{C}B}) \\[12pt]
    4^2 &= a^2 + 3^2 - 2(3)(a)\cos\theta \\[12pt]
    16 &= a^2 + 9 - 6a\cos\theta \\[6pt]
    \cos\theta &= \dfrac{a^2 - 7}{6a} \\[4pt]
    \dfrac{a^2 - 7}{6a} &> \dfrac{1}{4} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\cos \theta > 0.25$}]  \\[9pt]
    2a^2 - 14 &> 3a \\[12pt]
    \hspace{3em} 2a^2 - 3a - 14 &> 0 \\[8pt]
    a &> \dfrac{7}{2} \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (a)}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since ABC is a triangle, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.5em} \text{BC} &< \text{AB} + \text{AC} \\[6pt]
    a &< 4 + 3 \\[6pt]
    a &< 7
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find
     $\dfrac{7}{2} < a < 7$
