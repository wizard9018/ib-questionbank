# AA1052

**Maximum mark: 6**

A cyclist leaves from a town situated at point $A$, she cycles $4$ kilometres on a bearing of $330^\circ$, to another town at point $B$.

She then cycles $6$ kilometres on a bearing of $220^\circ$, to a village at point $C$.

![q1052](images/q1052.png)

1.  Determine the size of angle $\angle ABC$. [1 mark]

The cyclist travels in a straight line from the village at point $C$ back to the town at point $A$ at an average speed of $22$ kilometres per hour.

2.  1.  Calculate how long will it take her to get home. Give your answer to the nearest minute.

    2.  Determine the bearing on which she will need to travel. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

![a1052-1](images/a1052-1.png)

1.  We can add the bearings to the diagram and through angle reasoning
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \angle ABC\ &=220-150\\
        &=70^\circ \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  1.  Using **the cosine rule (3.2)** we can find the length $AC$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        AC^2&=6^2+4^2-2\times 6 \times 4 \times \cos{70} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\
        AC&=5.965... \text{km} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Next we can calculate the time, $T$, to the nearest minute, to go from $C$ to $A$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        T&=\frac{5.965...}{22}\times 60\\[6pt]
        &=16 \text{ minutes} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    2.  We can use **the sine rule (3.2)** to find angle $$\angle ACB$$ ($=\theta$). We must then add $40^\circ$ to this to get the bearing the cyclist must head on.

        ![a1052-2](images/a1052-2.png)

        We calculated $\angle ABC = 70^{\circ}$ in part (a), and length $AC = 5.965...$ in part (b)(i).

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\sin{\theta}}{4}&=\frac{\sin{70}}{5.965...}  \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\
        \theta&=39.059...
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Using **the sine rule (3.2)**, we have

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \frac{a}{\sin A} &= \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}\\[8pt]
        \Rightarrow \frac{\sin A}{a} &= \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}\\[8pt]
        \frac{\sin \angle ACB}{AB} &= \frac{\sin \angle ABC}{AC} \\[8pt]
        \frac{\sin{\theta}}{4}&=\frac{\sin{70}}{5.965...}  \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[8pt]
        \theta&=39.059...
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Therefore the bearing of $A$ from $C$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=39.059...+40\\[6pt]
        &=\boxed{079^\circ}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
