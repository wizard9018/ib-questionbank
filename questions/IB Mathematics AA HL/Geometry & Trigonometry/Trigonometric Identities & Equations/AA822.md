# AA822

**Maximum mark: 7**

1.  Show that $2x-9+\dfrac{5}{x+1} = \dfrac{2x^2-7x-4}{x+1}$,
    $x\in \mathbb{R}$, $x\neq -1$.[2 marks]

2.  Hence, solve the equation
    $2\sin 2\theta - 9 + \dfrac{5}{\sin 2\theta + 1} =  0$ for
    $0\leq \theta \leq \pi$, $\theta \neq \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Expanding the left hand side, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x - 9 + \dfrac{5}{x+1} &= \dfrac{(2x - 9)(x+1)+5}{x+1} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{2x^2-7x-9+5}{x+1} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{2x^2-7x-4}{x+1}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using part (a), we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2\sin 2\theta - 9 + \dfrac{5}{\sin 2\theta + 1} &=  0 \\[12pt]
    \dfrac{2\sin^2 2\theta - 7 \sin 2\theta - 4}{\sin 2\theta + 1} &=  0 \\[12pt]
    %2\sin^2 2\theta - 7 \sin 2\theta - 4 &= 0 \\[12pt]
    (2\sin 2\theta + 1) (\sin 2\theta - 4) &= 0 \\[12pt]
    (2\sin 2\theta + 1) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{since }\sin 2\theta \neq 4] \\[12pt]
    \sin 2\theta &= -\dfrac{1}{2} \\[12pt]
    2\theta &= \dfrac{7\pi}{6}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6} \\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-0.5em}\theta = \dfrac{7\pi}{12}, \dfrac{11\pi}{12}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
