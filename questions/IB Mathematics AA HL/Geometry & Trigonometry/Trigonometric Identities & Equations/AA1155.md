# AA1155

**Maximum mark: 22**

1.  Show that $\cos{3\theta}=4\cos^3{\theta}-3\cos{\theta}$. [4 marks]

Consider the following function
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f(x)&=4x^3-3x-\cos{3k}
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $k$ is an angle in degrees such that $0 \leq k \leq 360^\circ$.

2.  Hence, verify that $\cos{k}$ is one of the roots of $f(x)$.[2 marks]

Let the remaining two roots of $f(x)$ be $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

3.  1.  Find $\alpha\beta$ in terms of $\cos{k}$.

    2.  Show that $\beta=-\alpha-\cos k$.[3 marks]

4.  Using your answers from part (c) show that $\alpha^2+\alpha \cos k +\cos^2 k -\tfrac{3}{4}=0$. [2 marks]

5.  Hence show that $\alpha = \frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}$ and $\beta = \frac{-\cos k - \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}$. [4 marks]

It is known that for $0 \leq k \leq 360^\circ$ $f(x)$ has either $3$ distinct roots or a single distinct root and a repeated root.

6.  Hence, find all of the values of $k$ for which $f(x)$ intersects the $x-$axis exactly $2$ times. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the compound angle identity from the formula booklet we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \cos{(A+B)}&=\cos A \cos B - \sin A \sin B\\[6pt]
        \cos{(2\theta+\theta)}&=\cos 2\theta \cos \theta - \sin 2\theta \sin \theta\\[6pt]
        \cos{3\theta}&=\cos 2\theta \cos \theta - \sin 2\theta \sin \theta \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now the use the double angle formula to rewrite $\cos {2\theta}$ and $\sin {2\theta}$ in terms of single angle functions.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \cos{3\theta}&=(2\cos^2{\theta}-1) \cos \theta - (2\sin{\theta}\cos{\theta}) \sin \theta \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        &=2\cos^3\theta-\cos \theta - 2\sin^2{\theta}\cos{\theta}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now rewrite $\sin^2{\theta}$ in terms of $\cos{\theta}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \cos{3\theta}&=2\cos^3\theta-\cos \theta - 2(1-\cos^2{\theta})\cos{\theta}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        &=2\cos^3\theta-\cos \theta - 2\cos{\theta}+2\cos^3{\theta}\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}4\cos^3\theta-3\cos \theta \hspace{2em}\text{... as required.}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  If $\cos{k}$ is a root of $f(x)$ then $f(\cos{k})=0$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f(\cos{k})&=4(\cos{k})^3-3(\cos{k})-\cos{3k}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using part (a)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f(\cos{k})&=\cos{3k}-\cos{3k} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        &=0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, $\cos k \text{ is a root of }f(x)$

3.  1.  Using the formula for the product of the roots of a polynomial
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \alpha\beta \cos{k} &=-\frac{(-1)^3\cos{3k}}{4}  \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Using part (a) we can rewrite $\cos{3k}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \alpha\beta \cos{k} &=\frac{4\cos^3{k}-3\cos{k}}{4}\\[6pt]
            \alpha\beta &= \frac{4\cos^2{k}-3}{4}\\[6pt]
            &= \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\cos^2{k}-\tfrac{3}{4}}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.  Using the formula for the sum of the roots of a polynomial (with the coefficient of $x^2$ being $0$)
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \alpha + \beta + \cos k &=-\frac{0}{4}\\[6pt]
            \beta= \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-\alpha - \cos k}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

4.  Combining our answers from part (c)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \alpha \beta &= \cos^2{k}-\tfrac{3}{4} \hspace{4em}\beta= -\alpha - \cos k\\[6pt]
        \alpha(-\alpha - \cos k) &= \cos^2{k}-\tfrac{3}{4} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
        -\alpha^2-\alpha \cos{k} &= \cos^2{k}-\tfrac{3}{4}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.4em}\boxed{0=\alpha^2+\alpha \cos{k}+\cos^2{k}-\tfrac{3}{4}} \hspace{2em} \text{... as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Use the quadratic formula with $a=1, b=\cos k$ and $c=\cos^2 k - \tfrac{3}{4}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \alpha &=\frac{-\cos k \pm \sqrt{\cos^2 k - 4\times 1 \times (\cos^2 k - \tfrac{3}{4})}}{2}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{-\cos k \pm \sqrt{\cos^2 k - 4\cos^2 k +3)}}{2}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{-\cos k \pm \sqrt{-3\cos^2 k +3)}}{2}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{-\cos k \pm \sqrt{3(1-\cos^2 k)}}{2}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{-\cos k \pm \sqrt{3\sin^2 k}}{2}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{-\cos k \pm \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let $\alpha = \frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}$, using the answer from part (c) (ii) we can find $\beta$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \beta &= -\alpha - \cos{k}\\[6pt]
        &= -\frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2} - \cos{k}\\[6pt]
        &= \frac{\cos k - \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2} - \frac{2\cos{k}}{2}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{-\cos k - \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence $\alpha = \frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2} \text{ and } \beta = \frac{-\cos k - \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note: also accept vice versa solutions for $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

6.  $f(x)$ must have a single distinct root and a repeated root to intersect the $x-$axis exactly twice.

    Recall the three roots to $f(x)$ are $\alpha, \beta$ and $\cos k$.

    We have a repeated root when any of these conditions are met.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \alpha &= \beta &[1]\\[6pt]
        \alpha &= \cos k &[2]\\[6pt]
        \beta &= \cos k &[3]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let's consider condition $[1]$ first
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \alpha &= \beta\\[6pt]
        \frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}&=\frac{-\cos k - \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        \frac{\sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}\\[6pt]
        \sin k&=0 \hspace{2em} \text{ where $0 \leq k \leq 360^\circ$}\\[6pt]
        k&=0, 180^\circ, 360^\circ \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now consider condition $[2]$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \alpha &= \cos k\\[6pt]
        \frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}&=\cos k \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        \frac{-\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}&=\frac{2\cos k}{2}\\[6pt]
        \frac{-3\cos k + \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}&=0\\[6pt]
        \sqrt{3}\sin k&=3\cos k\\[6pt]
        \tan k&= \sqrt{3}\hspace{2em} \text{ where $0 \leq k \leq 360^\circ$}\\[6pt]
        k&=60^\circ, 240^\circ \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Finally consider condition $[3]$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \beta &= \cos k\\[6pt]
        \frac{-\cos k - \sqrt{3}\sin k}{2}&=\cos k \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        \tan k&= -\sqrt{3}\hspace{2em} \text{ where $0 \leq k \leq 360^\circ$}\\[6pt]
        k&=120^\circ, 300^\circ \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence $f(x)$ will intersect the $x-$axis exactly $2$ times when
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \boxed{k=0^\circ, 60^\circ, 120^\circ, 180^\circ, 240^\circ, 300^\circ, 360^\circ}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
