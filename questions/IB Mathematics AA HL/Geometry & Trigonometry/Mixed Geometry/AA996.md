# AA996

**Maximum mark: 13**

A circle, with centre $O$, has a radius of $r\,$cm.

The triangle $OAB$ is drawn such that the vertices $A$ and $B$ lie on the circumference of the circle.

$\theta$ is the angle $\angle{AOB}$.

The segment formed is shaded as shown below

![Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 12.50.10 PM](<images/Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 12.50.10 PM.png>)

1. Show that the area of the shaded segment is given by $A_{\text{segment}}=\frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)$.
[2 marks]
2. Show that the length of $AB$ can be written as $AB=r\sqrt{2-2\cos\theta}$.
[4 marks]

A semi-circle is drawn with a diameter equal to the length of $AB$ with its centre at the point $C$, which is the mid-point of $AB$. This is shown below.

![Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 12.50.19 PM](<images/Screenshot 2023-09-15 at 12.50.19 PM.png>)

The blue area is the area of the semi-circle with the area of the segment removed.

The red area is the area of the triangle.

3. Find the value of $\theta$, where $0<\theta<\pi$, which makes the red area equal to the blue area. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the area of a triangle and area of a sector formula.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{\text{shaded}}&=A_{\text{sector}}-A_{\text{triangle}}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta-\frac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)}\hspace{0.525em}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As required.

2. Use the Cosine rule to find the side length $AB$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        AB^2&=r^2+r^2-2\times r\times r\times \cos\theta\\[6pt]
        &=2r^2-2r^2 \cos\theta\\[6pt]
        &=r^2(2-2\cos\theta)\\[6pt]
        AB&=\sqrt{r^2(2-2\cos\theta)}\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{r\sqrt{(2-2\cos\theta)}}\hspace{0.525em}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As required.

3. We need to first find the radius of the semi-circle in terms of $r$ and $\theta$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        r_{\text{semi}} &= \dfrac{AB}{2}\\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{r\sqrt{(2-2\cos\theta)}}{2}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now write the area of the semi-circle in terms of $r$ and $\theta$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{\text{semi}}&=\dfrac{\pi r_{\text{semi}}^2}{2}\\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\pi r^2(2-2\cos\theta)}{8}\\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\pi r^2(1-\cos\theta)}{4}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now subtract the area of the segment you found in part (a) to get the blue area.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{\text{blue}}&=\dfrac{\pi r^2(1-\cos\theta)}{4} - \left(\frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)\right)\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Form an equation using the area of the red triangle.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{\text{red}}&=A_{\text{blue}}\\[6pt]
        \cancel{\frac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta}&=\dfrac{\pi r^2(1-\cos\theta)}{4} - \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta+\cancel{\frac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta}\\[6pt]
        0&=\dfrac{\pi r^2(1-\cos\theta)}{4} - \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta\\[6pt]
        \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta&=\dfrac{\pi r^2(1-\cos\theta)}{4}\\[6pt]
        2\cancel{r^2}\theta&=\pi \cancel{r^2}(1-\cos\theta)\\[6pt]
        2\theta&=\pi(1-\cos\theta)\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The above equation has one unknown and can be solved on the G.D.C.

    The value of $\theta$, in the region $0<\theta<\pi$, for which the red and blue areas are equal is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ radians.
