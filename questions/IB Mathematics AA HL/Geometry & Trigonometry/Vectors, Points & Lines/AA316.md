# AA316

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the lines $\mathit{L}_1$ and $\mathit{L}_2$ defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{L}_1:\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -6 \\ 10 \\ c \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} +  \hspace{0.1em}\lambda\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{0.55em}\text{and}\hspace{0.8em} \mathit{L}_2:\hspace{0.5em} -\dfrac{x}{2} = y - 1 = \dfrac{z -13}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ and $c$ is a constant. Given that the
lines intersect at a point A,

1.  find the value of $c$ ; [4 marks]

2.  determine the coordinates of A. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the Cartesian equations of $\mathit{L}_2$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -x &= 2(y - 1)  \\[6pt]
    \hspace{1.1em} 3(y-1) &= z - 13
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    substituting $x = -6 + 3\lambda$ and $y = 10 - 3\lambda$ in the
    equation $(1)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.7em} -(-6 + 3\lambda) &= 2[(10 - 3\lambda) - 1] \\[6pt]
    6 - 3\lambda &= 18 - 6\lambda \\[6pt]
    3\lambda &= 12 \\[6pt]
    \lambda &= 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, substituting $y = 10-3(4)$ and
    $z = c + 4$ in the equation $(2)$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3[(10-3(4))-1] &= (c+4)-13 \hspace{1.4em} \\[6pt]
    3(-2-1) &= c - 9 \\[6pt]
    - 9 &= c - 9 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{1.1em} c = 0
    $$

2.  The coordinates of A are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.4em} (-6+3\lambda, 10-3\lambda, c+\lambda) &= (-6+3(4), 10-3(4), 0+4) \\[6pt]
    &= (6,-2,4)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
