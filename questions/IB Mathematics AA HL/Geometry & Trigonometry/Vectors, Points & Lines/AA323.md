# AA323

**Maximum mark: 18**

The equations of the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_1 &:\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{r}_1 = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.05em}s\hspace{-0.2em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.5em} s\hspace{-0.05em} \in \mathbb{R}, \\[20pt]
L_2 &:\hspace{0.5em} \mathbf{r}_2 = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.5em} t \in \mathbb{R}.
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Show that the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ are skew. [4 marks]

2.  Find the acute angle between $L_1$ and $L_2$, giving your answer
    correct to the nearest degree. [4 marks]

3.
    1.  Find a vector perpendicular to both lines.

    2.  Hence determine the equation of the line $L_3$ that is
        perpendicular to both $L_1$ and $L_2$ and
        intersects both lines. [10 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the direction vectors of $L_1$ and $L_2$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em} \neq k\hspace{-0.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} [\text{for any $k \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence $L_1$ and $L_2$ are not parallel. They are either skew or
    intersecting.

    Using the vector equations of $L_1$ and $L_2$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.7em} 2 + 4s &= 1 - 2t  \\[6pt]
    3 - 2s &= 3 + t   \\[6pt]
    1 + 4s &= 1 + 2t
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving the system of
    linear equations $(2)$-$(3)$ for $s$ and $t$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.75em} s = 0\hspace{0.75em}\&\hspace{0.75em} t = 0 \hspace{9.75em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we try to verify the equation $(1)$ for values $(4)$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.8em} 2 + 4(0) &\neq 1 - 2(0) \\[6pt]
    2 &\neq 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the system of linear equations
    $(1)$-$(2)$-$(3)$ has no solution.

    Therefore $L_1$ and $L_2$ are not intersecting. They are skew.

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6.25em} \mathbf{d}_1 &= \phantom{-}4\textbf{i} - 2\textbf{j} + 4\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{direction vector of $L_1$}] \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{d}_2 &= -2\textbf{i} + \phantom{2}\textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k} \hspace{3em} [\text{direction vector of $L_2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the angle between two vectors formula, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.15em} \cos \theta &= \dfrac{\mathbf{d}_1\hspace{-0.1em}\cdot\mathbf{d}_2}{|\mathbf{d}_1||\mathbf{d}_2|} \\[5pt]
    \cos \theta &= \dfrac{(4)(-2) + (-2)(1) + (4)(2)}{\big[\sqrt{4^2+(-2)^2+4^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]\big[\sqrt{(-2)^2+1^2+2^2}\hspace{0.1em}\big]} \\[9pt]
    \theta &\approx \ang{96} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the acute angle between $L_1$ and $L_2$ is
    $\ang{180} - \ang{96} =  \hspace{0.525em}\ang{84}$

3.
    1.  Using the vector product formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{5.1em} \mathbf{d}_1 \times \mathbf{d}_2 &= (4\textbf{i} - 2\textbf{j} + 4\textbf{k})\times(-2\textbf{i} + \textbf{j} + 2\textbf{k}) \\[6pt]
        &= \left|\begin{matrix*}[r] \textbf{i} & \textbf{j} & \hspace{0.7em}\textbf{k} \\ 4 & -2 & 4 \\ -2 & 1 & 2 \end{matrix*}\hspace{0.25em}\right| \\[6pt]
        &= [-4-4]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{i} - [8-(-8)]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{j} + [4-4]\hspace{0.05em}\textbf{k} \\[8pt]
        &= -8\mathbf{i} - 16\mathbf{j}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{10em} \mathbf{d}_3 &= 8\mathbf{i} + 16\mathbf{j}\phantom{-} \hspace{3em} [\text{direction vector of $L_3$}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        and

        ![52470a552fb1b070c5e32f42c88ee3747a6d6ab6.svg](images/52470a552fb1b070c5e32f42c88ee3747a6d6ab6.svg)

        The points A and B lie on $L_1$ and $L_2$ respectively.

        Hence, using the equations of $L_1$ and $L_2$, we have

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{6em} \vec{\text{OA}} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2+4s \\ 3-2s \\ 1 + 4s \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{0.75em} \& \hspace{1em} \vec{\text{OB}} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1-2t \\ 3+t \\ 1+2t \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{1.9em} [\text{for some $s, t \in \mathbb{R}$}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        The vectors $\vec{\text{AB}} = \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}}$
        and $\mathbf{d}_3 = 8\mathbf{i} + 16\mathbf{j}$ are parallel.

        Hence we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} &= p\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 8 \\ 16 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}  \\[3pt]
        \hspace{2.85em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -1-4s-2t \\ \hspace{0.3em}2s+t \\ -4s+2t \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} &= p\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 8 \\ 16 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2em} [\text{for some $p \in \mathbb{R}$}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Rewriting the vector equation $(6)$ as a system of linear
        equations, we get
        $$

        \begin{array}{rcccccl}
        8p &+& 4s &+& 2t &=&-1 \\[6pt]
        16p &-& 2s &-& t &=& 0 \\[6pt]
        && 4s &-& 2t &=& 0
        \end{array}
        $$
        Hence, solving this system of linear equations for
        $p$, $s$ and $t$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{7.4em} p = -0.025,\hspace{0.25em} s = -0.1,\hspace{0.5em} t = -0.2 \hspace{1.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{OA}} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1.6 \\ 3.2 \\ 0.6 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{0.9em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, using the vector equation of a line formula, we find

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{r}_3 &=  \vec{\text{OA}} + \lambda\hspace{0.05em}\mathbf{d}_3 \hspace{3em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{6.1em} \mathbf{r}_3 = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1.6 \\ 3.2 \\ 0.6 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} +  \lambda\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 8 \\ 16 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{3em} [\lambda \in \mathbb{R}]
        $$
