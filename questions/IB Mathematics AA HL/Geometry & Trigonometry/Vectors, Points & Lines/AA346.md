# AA346

**Maximum mark: 8**

A line $L$ passes through the points A$(6,5,0)$ and B$(8,6,1)$.

1.
    1.  Show that
        $\vec{\text{AB}} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}$ .
    2.  Write down a vector equation of $L$ in the form
        $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p} + t\hspace{0.05em}\mathbf{d}$.
        [2 marks]

The line $L$ also passes through the point C$(2,b,-2)$.

2.  Find the value of $b$. [2 marks]

The point D has coordinates $(p^2,2,5p)$.

3.  Given that $\vec{\text{CD}}$ is perpendicular to $L$, find
    the possible values of $p$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \vec{\text{AB}} &= \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 8 \\ 6 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} - \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 6 \\ 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} =  \hspace{0.525em} \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} \hspace{1em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.
        $\mathbf{r} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 6 \\ 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em},\hspace{0.5em} t \in \mathbb{R}$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.1em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ b \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 6 \\ 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{1.7em} [\text{for some $t \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, solving the vector equation $(1)$ for $t$ and $b$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    t &= -2 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{2.05em} b &= 5 + (-2) = \hspace{0.575em}3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \vec{\text{CD}} &= \vec{\text{OD}} - \vec{\text{OC}} \\[6pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] p^2 \\ 2 \\ 5p \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} - \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] p^2-2 \\ -1 \\ 5p+2 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the scalar product formula, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix*}[c] p^2-2 \\ -1 \\ 5p+2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} \cdot \hspace{-0.1em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.25em} &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\vec{\text{CD}} \perp L$}] \\[6pt]
    \hspace{3.5em} (p^2-2)(2) + (-1)(1) + (5p+2)(1) &= 0 \\[11pt]
    2p^2 + 5p - 3 &= 0 \\[11pt]
    (p+3)(2p-1) &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{11em} p = -3,\hspace{0.25em} \dfrac{1}{2}
    $$
