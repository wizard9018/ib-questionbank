# AA662

**Maximum mark: 11**

Two lines have vector equations given by

$\mathit{L}_1$ :
 $\mathbf{r}_1 = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.25em}t \in \mathbb{R}.$
  $\mathit{L}_2$ :
 $\mathbf{r}_2 = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}s\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em}, \hspace{0.25em}s \in \mathbb{R}.$

Point A is the point on $\mathit{L}_1$ that is closest to the origin.

1.  Find the coordinates of A. [4 marks]

2.  Find the shortest distance between $\mathit{L}_1$ and
    $\mathit{L}_2$. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.9em} \mathbf{d}_1 &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{direction vector of $\mathit{L}_1$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Point A lies on $\mathit{L}_1$.

    Hence, using the equation of $\mathit{L}_1$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7.5em} \vec{\text{OA}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 + t \\ 2t-1 \\ 2 + t \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{for some $\hspace{0.05em}t \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Point A is closest to the origin when
    $\vec{\text{OA}} \perp \mathbf{d}_1$.

    Hence, solving the equation $\vec{\text{OA}} \cdot \mathbf{d}_1 = 0$
    for $t$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 + t \\ 2t-1 \\ 2 + t \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{-0.15em}\cdot\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{3.8em} (3 + t)(1) + (2t - 1)(2) + (2 + t)(1) &= 0 \\[12pt]
    t &= -0.5 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, substituting $t = -0.5$ in the expression $(1)$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7em} \vec{\text{OA}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 2.5 \\ \hspace{-0.25em}-2 \\ 1.5 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore the coordinates of A are
     $(2.5, -2, 1.5)$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10.9em} \mathbf{d}_2 &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{direction vector of $\mathit{L}_2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we draw the shortest distance PQ between $\mathit{L}_1$ and
    $\mathit{L}_2$, we have

    ![d1298c64cb8be88af8fbc9efe70dd34fbb2aea76.svg](images/d1298c64cb8be88af8fbc9efe70dd34fbb2aea76.svg)

    Using the equations of $\mathit{L}_1$ and $\mathit{L}_2$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9em} \text{P}(3+t, 2t-1,2+t)& \hspace{3em} [\text{for some $\hspace{0.05em}t \in \mathbb{R}$}] \\[6pt]
    \text{Q}(4+s,3s-1,s)& \hspace{3em} [\text{for some $s\hspace{-0.06em} \in \mathbb{R}$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the coordinates of P and Q, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.15em} \vec{\text{PQ}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] s-t+1 \\ 3s-2t \\ s-t-2 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We have $\vec{\text{PQ}} \perp \mathbf{d}_1$ and
    $\vec{\text{PQ}} \perp \mathbf{d}_2$.

    Hence, using the equation $\vec{\text{PQ}} \cdot \mathbf{d}_1 = 0$,
    we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix*}[c] s-t+1 \\ 3s-2t \\ s-t-2 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{-0.15em}\cdot \hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} &= 0 \hspace{8em} \\[6pt]
    (s-t+1)(1) + (3s-2t)(2) + (s-t-2)(1) &= 0 \\[12pt]
    8s-6t &= 1  \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Similarly, using the
    equation $\vec{\text{PQ}} \cdot \mathbf{d}_2 = 0$, we deduce

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \begin{pmatrix*}[c] s-t+1 \\ 3s-2t \\ s-t-2 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{-0.15em}\cdot \hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} &= 0 \hspace{8em} \\[6pt]
     (s-t+1)(1) + (3s-2t)(3) + (s-t-2)(1) &= 0 \\[12pt]
     11s-8t &= 1  \\
     \end{aligned}
     $$
     Hence, solving the system
    of linear equations $(3)$-$(4)$ for $s$ and $t$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7.4em} s &= -1\hspace{0.75em}\&\hspace{0.75em} t = -1.5 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, substituting $t=-1.5$ & $s = -1$ in the expression $(2)$, we
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.2em} \vec{\text{PQ}} &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1.5 \\ 0 \\ -1.5 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore the shortest distance between $\mathit{L}_1$ and
    $\mathit{L}_2$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.4em} \text{PQ} &= \sqrt{(1.5)^2 + 0^2 + (-1.5)^2} \\[9pt]
    &\approx 2.12
    \end{aligned}
    $$
