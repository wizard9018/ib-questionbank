# AA333

**Maximum mark: 11**

A line $L_1$ passes through the points A$(-2,0,1)$ and B$(1,4,1)$.

1.  Show that
    $\vec{\text{AB}} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}$
    [1 mark]

2.  Hence write down:
    1.  a direction vector for $L_1$;

    2.  a vector equation for $L_1$ in the form
        $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + t\hspace{0.05em}\mathbf{b}$.[3 marks]

Another line $L_2$ is perpendicular to $L_1$ and has vector equation

$$
\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + s\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] k \\ -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em},\hspace{0.5em} s \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

3.  Find the value of $k$. [2 marks]

4.  Show that the point C$(-4,8,-5)$ lies on $L_2$. [2 marks]

5.  Let D be the point such that ABCD is a parallelogram. Find
    $\vec{\text{OD}}$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \vec{\text{AB}} &= \vec{\text{OB}} - \vec{\text{OA}} \\[6pt]
    &=\hspace{-0.15em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 1 \\ 4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} - \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} = \hspace{0.525em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.
        $\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}$

    2.
        $\mathbf{r} = \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + \hspace{0.1em}t\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em},\hspace{0.5em} t \in \mathbb{R}$

3.  Using the scalar product formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \begin{pmatrix*}[c] k \\ -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} \cdot \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 3 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*} &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $L_1 \perp L_2$}] \\[6pt]
    \hspace{3.1em} (k)(3) + (-3)(4) + (1)(0) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    3k - 12 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    3k &= 12 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{3.9em} k = 4
    $$

4.  Using the vector equation of $L_2$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \begin{pmatrix*}[c] -4 \\ 8 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix*} &= \begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} + s\hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] 4 \\ -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix*} \hspace{0.5em}\Longleftrightarrow\hspace{0.5em} s = -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence C$(-4,8,-5)$ lies on $L_2$.

5.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \vec{\text{CD}} &= - \vec{\text{AB}} \\[6pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -3 \\ -4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.3em} \vec{\text{OD}} &= \vec{\text{OC}} + \vec{\text{CD}} \\[6pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -4 \\ 8 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.2em} + \hspace{-0.15em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -3 \\ -4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix*}\hspace{-0.15em} = \hspace{0.525em}\begin{pmatrix*}[c] -7 \\ 4 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix*}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
