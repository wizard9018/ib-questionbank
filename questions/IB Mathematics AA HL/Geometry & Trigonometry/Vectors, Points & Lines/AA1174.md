# AA1174

**Maximum mark: 6**

In this question, all distances are measured in metres and time in seconds.

A seagull, positioned at $(5,9,15)$, is looking at a fish positioned at $(9, -23, -1)$.

At $t=0$, the seagull dives toward the fish with a speed of $12$ metres per second.

1.  Find the velocity vector of the seagull. [2 marks]

A duck, positioned at the point $(8, -10, 2)$, is also looking at the fish.

2.  At what time during the seagull's dive is it closest to the duck? [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We begin by working out the direction the seagull must fly. This can be done by subtracting the position vector of the seagull ($S$) from the fish ($F$)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \overrightarrow{SF}&=\begin{pmatrix}
            9 \\
            -23 \\
            -1
        \end{pmatrix} -
        \begin{pmatrix}
            5 \\
            9 \\
            15
        \end{pmatrix}\\[12pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix}
            4 \\
            -32 \\
            -16
        \end{pmatrix}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $\overrightarrow{SF}$ is shown below

    ![AA1174_a1](images/AA1174_a1.JPEG)

    However, this vector does not take into account the speed of the seagull.

    Lets work out the distance between $S$ and $F$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        |\overrightarrow{SF}|&=\left|
            \begin{array}{c}
            4 \\
            -32 \\
            -16
            \end{array}
            \right|\\[6pt]
            &=\sqrt{4^2+(-32)^2+(-16)^2}\\[6pt]
            &=36 \text{ metres}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We know the speed of the seagull is $12\,$ms$^{-1}$, therefore in $1$ second the velocity vector of the seagull is $\tfrac{1}{36}\times 12$ multiplied by the direction of $\overrightarrow{SF}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{1}{36}\times \overrightarrow{SF}\times 12\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix}
            4 \\
            -32 \\
            -16
        \end{pmatrix}   \\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix}
            4/3 \\
            -32/3 \\
            -16/3
        \end{pmatrix}\\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix}
            1.33 \\
            -10.7 \\
            -5.33
        \end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The seagull moves in the direction of the vector above every second, therefore

    $\text{The velocity vector of the seagull is } \begin{pmatrix}
            1.33 \\
            -10.7 \\
            -5.33
        \end{pmatrix}t$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The following diagram shows the approximate position of the duck ($D$) relative to $S$ and $F$

    ![AA1174_a2](images/AA1174_a2.JPEG)

    When $t>0$ the seagull moves toward the fish. The position of the seagull after some time is represented by $S'$.

    The direction between $S'$ and $D$, $\overrightarrow{S'D}$, is shown as the red vector below

    ![AA1174_a3](images/AA1174_a3.JPEG)

    The seagull is closest to the duck when $\overrightarrow{S'D}$ is perpendicular to the direction of the seagulls travel, $\overrightarrow{SF}$. Shown below

     ![AA1174_a4](images/AA1174_a4.JPEG)

    Therefore we need to solve the following equation for $t$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=\overrightarrow{SF} \cdot \overrightarrow{S'D} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We already have $\overrightarrow{SF}$ but we need $\overrightarrow{S'D}$ in terms of $t$.

    To find $\overrightarrow{S'D}$ we need to use the position vectors for $S'$ and $D$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \overrightarrow{S'D}&=\overrightarrow{OD} - \overrightarrow{OS'}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We know $\overrightarrow{OD}$, we were given that in the question. Using the answer to part(a), $\overrightarrow{v_S}$, we can write $\overrightarrow{OS'}$ relative to the origin after $t$ seconds as
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \overrightarrow{OS'}&=\overrightarrow{OS} + \overrightarrow{v_S}\\[6pt]
        \overrightarrow{OS'}&=\begin{pmatrix}
        5 \\
        9 \\
        15
    \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix}
        1.33 \\
        -10.7 \\
        -5.33
    \end{pmatrix}t\\[6pt]
    &=\begin{pmatrix}
        5+1.33t \\
        9-10.7t \\
        15-5.33t
    \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore we can find $\overrightarrow{S'D}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \overrightarrow{S'D}&=\overrightarrow{OD} - \overrightarrow{OS'}\\[6pt]
        &=\begin{pmatrix}
        8 \\
        -10 \\
        2
    \end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}
        5+1.33t \\
        9-10.7t \\
        15-5.33t
    \end{pmatrix}\\[6pt]
    &=\begin{pmatrix}
        3-1.33t \\
        -19+10.7t \\
        -13+5.33t
    \end{pmatrix} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Recall we need to solve the following and can use the result we have just found
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=\overrightarrow{SF} \cdot \overrightarrow{S'D}\\[6pt]
        0&=\begin{pmatrix}
        4 \\
        -32 \\
        -16
    \end{pmatrix}  \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
        3-1.33t \\
        -19+10.7t \\
        -13+5.33t
    \end{pmatrix}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can solve this directly using the numerical solver on the G.D.C.

    Or we can expand the dot product and then solve on the G.D.C. however it is always advisable to solve on the G.D.C. at the earliest opportunity
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=12-4t+608-\tfrac{1024}{3}t+208-\tfrac{256}{3}t
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore using a G.D.C.
    $\text{The seagull is closest to the duck when } t=1.92\,\text{seconds}$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
