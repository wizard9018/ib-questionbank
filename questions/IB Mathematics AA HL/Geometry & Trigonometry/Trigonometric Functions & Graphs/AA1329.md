# AA1329

**Maximum mark: 13**

A ceiling fan with centre $A$ is located $5$ metres from a beam $[PQ]$.

This is shown in the diagram.

![AA1329q1](images/AA1329q1.JPEG)

$C$ represents the tip of a blade on the fan where $AC=3$ metres.

The shortest distance from $C$ to $[PQ]$ is $h_C$, measured in metres.

1.    Find

    1.    the maximum value of $h_C$; 

    2.    the minimum value of $h_C$. [2 marks]

$h_C$ can be modelled by the function $h_C(t)=a\cos(bt)+c$ where $a$, $b$, $c\,\in\mathbb{R}$ and $t$ is time in seconds.

2.    Write down the value of

    1.    $a$ 

    2.    $c$ [2 marks]

The fan rotates clockwise at a rate of $1$ rotation per second.

3.    Write down the value of $b$. [1 mark]

At $t=0$ the value of $h_C$ is at a maximum value.

4.    Sketch the graph of $h_C(t)$ for $0\leq t\leq 1$ on the given axis. [2 marks]

![AA1329q2](images/AA1329q2.JPEG)

5.    Show that $\sin4\theta=2\sin2\theta\cos2\theta$. [1 mark]

Another ceiling fan, centred at $B$, is installed on the other side of $[PQ]$ and $D$ represents the tip of a blade on fan $B$.

The shortest distance from $D$ to $[PQ]$ is $h_D(t)$, measured in metres. It can be modelled by the function $h_D(t)=-\sqrt

3\sin(4\pi t)+5$ for $0\leq t\leq1$.

6.    Find the values of $t$ when $h_C(t)=h_D(t)$. [5 marks]

1.  We know that point $A$ is $5$ metres from $[PQ]$.

    We know that the blade on fan $A$ is $3$ metres long and that to be at a maximum (or minimum) the blade ($[AC]$) must be perpendicular to $[PQ]$.

        h_\text{C\,max}&=\textcolor{red}{3}+\textcolor{green}{5}\\[6pt]

        &=8\text{ metres}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. Showing values when $C$ is the minimum distance from $[PQ]$

        ![AA1329a2](images/AA1329a2.JPEG)

        we obtain

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        h_\text{C\,min}&=\textcolor{green}{5}-\textcolor{red}{3}\\[6pt]

        &=2\text{ metres}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2.  We can use the information from part (a) to solve for $a$ and $c$.

    1. The amplitude, $a$, is found using the formula 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        a&=\frac{\text{maximum}-\text{minimum}}{2}\\[9pt]

        &=\frac{8-2}{2}\\[9pt]

        &=3\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. The vertical shift of the cosine curve, $c$, can be found with the formula

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        c&=\frac{\text{maximum}+\text{minimum}}{2}\\[9pt]

        &=\frac{8+2}{2}\\[9pt]

        &=5\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

3. To find $b$ for a sine or cosine function we can use the formula

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \text{period}=\frac{2\pi}{b}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The period is the length of time it takes for the fan to complete a single rotation.

    We are told that fan $A$ rotates at a rate of $1$ rotation per second.

    Substituting this into the formula we can solve for $b$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    1&=\frac{2\pi}{b}\\[6pt]

    b&=2\pi\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. We are told that $h_C(t)=h_D(t)$ so we can use functions, $h_C$ and $h_D$, to form an equation.

    Using our values for $a$, $b$, and $c$ we have $h_C(t)=3\cos(2\pi t)+5$.

    

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    h_C(t)&=h_D(t)\\[6pt]

    3\cos(2\pi t)+5&=-\sqrt{3}\sin(4\pi t)+5\\[6pt]

    3\cos(2\pi t)&=-\sqrt{3}\sin(4\pi t)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Notice the angle measures are different.

    However, we can use the relationship from part (e) to rewrite the angle measure of $4\theta$ in terms of $2\theta$.

    Hence we can rewrite the R.H.S.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    3\cos(2\pi t)&=-2\sqrt{3}\sin(2\pi t)\cos(2\pi t)\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We don't want to divide by $\cos(2\pi t)$ as there are values of $t$ where this expression is $0$ and we cannot divide by $0$.

    Instead, let's rearrange the equation so that it equals $0$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    3\cos(2\pi t)+2\sqrt{3}\sin(2\pi t)\cos(2\pi t)&=0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can factorise the common term of $\cos(2\pi t)$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    [\cos(2\pi t)][3+2\sqrt{3}\sin(2\pi t)]&=0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    By the null factor theorem we can set each factor equal to $0$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \cos(2\pi t)&=0&&3+2\sqrt{3}\sin(2\pi t)=0\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    First let's work with the equation on the left.

    We know that $\cos\theta=0$ when $\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\,,\,\frac{3\pi}{2}$ hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2\pi t&=\frac{\pi}{2}&&2\pi t=\frac{3\pi}{2}\\[9pt]

    t_1&=\dfrac{1}{4}\text{ s}&&t_2=\dfrac{3}{4}\text{ s}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now let's consider the equation on the right. We can start by rearranging it.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2\sqrt{3}\sin(2\pi t)&=-3\\[6pt]

    \sin(2\pi t)&=-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{3}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    At first glance this doesn't look like a familiar trigonometric ratio but let's multiply by $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}$ to remove the root from the denominator.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \sin(2\pi t)&=-\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{6}\\[12pt]

    &=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    After simplifying, this now this looks more familiar.

    We know that the $\textcolor{green}{\text{principal}}$ angle for $\sin{\theta}=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is $\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

    Using a sketch (or the unit circle) of the sine curve we can identify the solutions for when $\sin\theta=-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

    ![AA1329a4](images/AA1329a4.JPEG)

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2\pi t&=\frac{4\pi}{3}&&2\pi t=\frac{5\pi}{3}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[12pt]

    t_3&=\dfrac{2}{3}\text{ s}&&t_4=\dfrac{5}{6}\text{ s}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
