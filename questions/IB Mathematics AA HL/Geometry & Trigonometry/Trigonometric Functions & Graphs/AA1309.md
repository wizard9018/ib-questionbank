## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The general form for a sinusoidal function is $y=a\sin{\left(b(x-c)\right)}+d$.
    1. Written in this form, the amplitude is equal to $\left|a\right|$. Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{amplitude} &= \left|-4\right| \\[6pt]
                         &= \boxed{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2. Written in this form, the period of a sine function is $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}$. Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{period} &= \dfrac{2\pi}{\tfrac{\pi}{2}} \\[6pt]
                      &= \boxed{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
2.  Compare with the parent function $y=-\sin{\theta}$, whose first minimum is at $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2},-1\right)$.

    ![Screenshot 2026-10-20 at 12.05.19](<images/Screenshot 2026-10-20 at 12.05.19.png>)

    Two of the transformations in $y=a\sin{\left(b(x-c)\right)}+d$ that affect the $x$-coordinates are $b$ and $c$. The factor $b$ is a horizontal stretch that multiplies all $x$-coordinates by its reciprocal, and $c$ is a horizontal translation that moves the entire graph $2$ units to the right.

    Applying these transformations to the first minimum of the parent graph, we take the $x$-coordinate $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, multiply by $\dfrac{2}{\pi}$ and add $2$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x &= \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{\pi}\right)+2 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
      &= 1+2 \\[6pt]
    x &= 3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    This means the first minimum at $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ has moved to $x=3$.

    Substituting $x=3$ into the sinusoidal function to find the corresponding $y$-coordinate.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= -4\sin{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}(3-2)\right)}-3 \\[6pt]
      &= -4\sin{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)}-3 \\[6pt]
      &= -4(1)-3 \\[6pt]
    y &= -7
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The coordinates of the first minimum are $\boxed{(3,-7)}$.

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Substituting $y=-3$ into the sinusoidal function, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -3 &= -4\sin{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}(x-2)\right)}-3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
    0 &= -4\sin{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}(x-2)\right)} \\[6pt]
    0 &= \sin{\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}(x-2)\right)} \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{\pi}{2}(x-2) &= \arcsin{0} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since the function is sinusoidal, there is likely more than one $x$ value giving $y=-3$ on $0\leq{x}\leq{8}$. Finding several values of $\arcsin{0}$ in each direction.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\pi}{2}(x-2) &= -2\pi,\,-\pi,\,0,\,\pi,\,2\pi,\,3\pi,\,4\pi \\[6pt]
    x-2 &= -4,\,-2,\,0,\,2,\,4,\,6,\,8 \\[6pt]
    x &= -2,\,0,\,2,\,4,\,6,\,8,\,10
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Rejecting any values outside the interval $0\leq{x}\leq{8}$, we have

    $x = \boxed{0,\,2,\,4,\,6,\,8}$

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
