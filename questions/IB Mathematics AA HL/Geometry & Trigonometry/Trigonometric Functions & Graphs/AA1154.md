# AA1154

[Maximum marks: 10]

The function $f(x)=a\sin{(bx)}+c$ where $0 \leq x \leq \pi$ and $a,b,c \in \mathrm{R}$ is shown below.

![AA1154](images/AA1154.JPEG)

The coordinates of the first two stationary points are $(\tfrac{\pi}{6}, -1)$ and $(\tfrac{\pi}{2},3)$ respectively.

1.  Find
    1.  a.

    2.  c.

    3.  b. [5 marks]

The domain of $f(x)$ is restricted to $\tfrac{\pi}{2}\leq x \leq k$, where $k \in \mathrm{R}$. This restriction allows an inverse to exist.

2.  Find the largest possible value of $k$. [2 marks]

Another function is defined as $g(x)=-2f(\tfrac{1}{2}x)+3$.

3.  Determine the coordinates of the __first__ maximum value of $g(x)$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  To find the value of $a$, which is known as the amplitude, we must find half of the vertical distance between the maximum and minimum values.

        ![AA1154a](images/AA1154a.JPEG)

        We can see from the diagram that the curve is a reflection of $\sin{x}$ in the $x-$axis, therefore the value of $a$ must be negative.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            a&=-\frac{3-(-1)}{2}\\[6pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}-2  \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  To find the  value of $c$ we must find the $y-$value half way between the maximum and minimum values.

        ![AA1154b](images/AA1154b.JPEG)

        Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            c&=\frac{3+(-1)}{2}\\[6pt]
            &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{1}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  To find $b$ we use the formula $T=\frac{2\pi}{b}$.

        We can use the diagram to find $T$ and see that $\frac{T}{2}$ is the $x-$distance between the maximum and minimum values.

        ![AA1154c (1)](<images/AA1154c (1).JPEG>)

        Hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \frac{T}{2}&=\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\tfrac{\pi}{6}\right)\\[6pt]
            T&=2\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\tfrac{\pi}{6}\right)\\[6pt]
            T&=\frac{2\pi}{3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Using this value of $T$ and the formula stated above we can find $b$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \frac{2\pi}{3}&=\frac{2\pi}{b}\\[6pt]
            b&=\hspace{0.525em}3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  For an inverse to exist the function must be one-to-one, this means it must pass the horizontal line test. We can see this is satisfied by the red part of $f(x)$  shown below
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![AA1154inv](images/AA1154inv.JPEG)

    We can see that if we add one complete time period to $\tfrac{\pi}{6}$ we will get $k$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        k&=\tfrac{\pi}{6}+\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\tfrac{5\pi}{6}}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  First we split the vertical and horizontal transformations.

    The inner function of $f$ is the horizontal transformations and everything else is vertical.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &-2f(\tfrac{1}{2}x)+3\\[6pt]
        &\text{Vertical}& \hspace{1em} & \text{Horizontal}\\[6pt]
        &\times -2 & \hspace{1em} & \div 2\\[6pt]
        &+3 & \hspace{1em} &
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Lets do the vertical transformations first. We don't need to transform the entire function. It is easier to identify a single point and transform that.

    Notice that we are multiplying by a negative number, that means $g$ will be a vertical reflection of $f$.

    Therefore the first maximum point of $g$ will be the image of the transformation of the first minimum point of $f$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore we need to apply our transformations to the point $(\tfrac{\pi}{6},-1)$.

    We transform the $y$-coordinate by $\times -2$ and then $+3$, therefore the transformed $y$-coordinate is $-1\times-2+3=5$.

    We transform the $x$-coordinate by $\times\tfrac{1}{1/2}$.therefore the transformed $x$-coordinate is $\tfrac{\pi}{6}\times2=\tfrac{\pi}{3}$.

    Therefore $\text{the first maximum of }g(x)\text{ has coordinates }(\tfrac{\pi}{3}, 5).$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack} \tag*{\textbf{A1A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
