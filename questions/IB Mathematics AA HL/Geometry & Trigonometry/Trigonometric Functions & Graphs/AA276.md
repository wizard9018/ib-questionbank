# AA276

**Maximum mark: 7**

Let $f(x) = \sin\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\Big) + q$. The
graph of $f$ passes through the point $\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3},5\Big)$.

1.  Find the value of $q$. [3 marks]

2.  Find the maximum value of $f$. [2 marks]

Let $g(x) = \sin x$. The graph of $g$ is translated to the graph of $f$
by
$\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$.

3.  Write down the values of $a$ and $b$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(\pi/3) &= 5 \hspace{6.8em} \\[9pt]
    \sin\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\Big) + q &= 5 \\[6pt]
    \sin\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Big) + q &= 5 \\[9pt]
    1 + q &= 5 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    q = 4 \hspace{0.2em}
    $$

2.  The maximum occurs when $\sin\hspace{0.05em}(x+\pi/6) = 1$.
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f_{\text{max}} &= 1 + 4 \\[6pt]
    &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.5em} f(x) &= \sin\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\Big) + 4 \\[6pt]
    &= g(x + \pi/6) + 4 \\[10pt]
    &= g(x - (-\pi/6)) + 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find
     $a = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
    and  $b = 4$
