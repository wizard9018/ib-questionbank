# AA159

**Maximum mark: 22**

Let
$f(x) = x^4 - 0.4\hspace{0.05em}x^3 - 2.85\hspace{0.05em}x^2 + 0.9\hspace{0.05em}x + 1.35$,
for $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Find the solutions for $f(x) > 0$. [3 marks]

2.  For the graph of $y = f(x)$,
    1.  find the coordinates of local minimum and maximum points.

    2.  find the $x$-coordinates of the points of inflexion. [5 marks]

The domain of $f$ is now restricted to $[a,b]$ where
$a, b \in \mathbb{R}^+$.

3.
    1.  Write down the smallest value of $a > 0$ and the largest value
        of $b > 0$ for which $f$ has an inverse. Give your answers
        correct to **three significant figures**.

    2.  For these values of $a$ and $b$, sketch the graphs of $y = f(x)$
        and $y = f^{-1}(x)$ on the same set of axes, showing clearly the
        coordinates of the end points of each curve.

    3.  Solve $f^{-1}(x) = 0.5$. [6 marks]

Let $g(x) = \dfrac{2}{3}\sin(2x - 1) + \dfrac{1}{2}$,
 $\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \leq x \leq \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

4.
    1.  Find an expression for $g^{-1}$ and state its domain.

    2.  Solve $(f^{-1}\circ g)(x) < 0.5$. [8 marks]

1.  If we sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$ \[by using G.D.C.\], we have

    From the diagram, we see that $f$ is positive on the intervals
    $(-\infty,-1.5)$, $(-0.6,1)$ and $(1.5,\infty)$, and negative on
    $(-1.5,-0.6)$ and $(1,1.5)$.

    Hence the set of solutions to the inequality $f(x) > 0$ is

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     (-\infty,-1.5)\cup(-0.6,1)\cup(1.5,\infty)
     \end{aligned}
     $$

2.
    1.  From the diagram, we see that the graph of $y = f(x)$ has a
        local\
        maximum at point A and local minimums at points B and C.

        Hence the coordinates of the local max and min points are

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{local max}:&\hspace{0.75em}\hspace{0.525em}(0.155, 1.42)  \\[10pt]
        \text{local min}:&\hspace{0.75em}\hspace{0.525em}(-1.13, -1.10)\hspace{0.525em}\text{ and }\hspace{0.525em}(1.28, -0.322)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(x) &= x^4 - 0.4x^3 - 2.85x^2 + 0.9x + 1.35 \\[6pt]
        f'(x) &= 4x^3 - 1.2x^2 - 5.7x + 0.9 \\[6pt]
        \hspace{1.5em} f''(x) &= 12x^2 - 2.4x - 5.7
        \end{aligned}
        $$

         Hence, if we solve the equation
        $f''(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        12x^2 - 2.4x - 5.7 &= 0 \hspace{9.8em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{12.95em} x \approx -0.596, \hspace{0.25em} 0.796 \hspace{2.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        $$

3.
    1.  From the diagram, we see that $f$ is a bijection if its domain
        is restricted to $[-1.13, 0.155]$ or $[0.155,1.28]$.

        As it is given that $a,b > 0$, hence $f$ has an inverse if
         $a = 0.155$
        and  $b = 1.28$

    2.  The graph of $y = f^{-1}(x)$ is the reflection of the graph of
        $y = f(x)$ across the line $y = x$.

        Hence, if we sketch the graphs of $y = f(x)$ and $y = f^{-1}(x)$
        on the same set of axes, we have

        ![524a320d1d2563d4a110444319e1f709eb5ee6c6.svg](images/524a320d1d2563d4a110444319e1f709eb5ee6c6.svg)

    3.  If we solve the equation $f^{-1}(x) = 0.5$ for $x$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(f^{-1}(x)) &= f(0.5) \hspace{4em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{10.8em} x = 1.1 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $f(f^{-1}(x)) = x$}]
        $$

4.
    1.  Solving the equation $(g\circ g^{-1})(x) = x$ for
        $y = g^{-1}(x)$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        g(g^{-1}(x)) &= x \\[14pt]
        g(y) &= x \\[10pt]
        \hspace{1.7em} \dfrac{2}{3}\sin(2y - 1) + \dfrac{1}{2} &= x \\[6pt]
        \dfrac{2}{3}\sin(2y - 1) &= \dfrac{2x-1}{2} \\[6pt]
        \sin(2y - 1) &= \dfrac{6x-3}{4} \\[4pt]
        2y - 1 &= \arcsin\bigg(\hspace{-0.05em}\dfrac{6x-3}{4}\bigg) \\[3pt]
        y &= \dfrac{1}{2}\arcsin\bigg(\hspace{-0.05em}\dfrac{6x-3}{4}\bigg) + \dfrac{1}{2} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence an expression for $g^{-1}$ is
         $g^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\arcsin\bigg(\hspace{-0.05em}\dfrac{6x-3}{4}\bigg) + \dfrac{1}{2}$
        The range of $g$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \left[-\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{2},\hspace{0.25em}\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{2}\right] = \left[-\dfrac{1}{6},\hspace{0.25em}\dfrac{7}{6}\right] \hspace{4.7em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the domain of $g^{-1}$ is
         $\bigg[-\dfrac{1}{6},\dfrac{7}{6}\bigg]$

    2.  Solving the inequality $(f^{-1}\circ g)(x) < 0.5$ for $x$, we
        get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{9.5em} f^{-1}(g(x)) &< 0.5 \\[6pt]
        g(x) &> f(0.5) \hspace{3em} [\text{since $f$ is decreasing}] \\[6pt]
        g(x) &> 1.1 \\[5pt]
        x &> g^{-1}(1.1) \hspace{2em} [\text{since $g^{-1}$ is increasing}] \\[6pt]
        x &> 1.06
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the set of solutions to the
        inequality $(f^{-1}\circ g)(x) < 0.5$ is

         $$
         \begin{aligned}
         \left(1.06,\hspace{0.25em}\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right] \hspace{2em}
         \end{aligned}
         $$
