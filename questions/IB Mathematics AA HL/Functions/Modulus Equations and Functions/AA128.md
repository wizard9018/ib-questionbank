# AA128

**Maximum mark: 17**

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = 1+ \dfrac{4x}{x+3}$, for
$x \neq -3$.

1.  Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$, indicating clearly any asymptotes
    and\
    points of intersection with the $x$ and $y$ axes. [4 marks]

2.  Find an expression for $f^{-1}$. [4 marks]

3.  Find all values of $x$ for which $f(x) = f^{-1}(x)$. [3 marks]

4.  Solve the inequality $|f(x)| < 2$. [4 marks]

5.  Solve the inequality $f(|x|) < 2$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we solve the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1+ \dfrac{4x}{x+3} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{5x+3}{x+3} &= 0 \\[11pt]
    5x + 3 &= 0 \\[10pt]
    x &= -\dfrac{3}{5} \hspace{2.1em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point
    P$(-3/5,0)$ is the $x$-intercept of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = 0$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} y &= f(0) \\[6pt]
    &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point Q$(0,1)$ is the $y$-intercept of
    the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \rightarrow\pm\infty} f(x) &= 1 + \dfrac{4}{1} \hspace{3.7em} \\[6pt]
    &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the line $y = 5$ is a horizontal asymptote
    of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    The denominator of $f$ is zero when $x = -3$. Hence the line
    $x = -3$\
    is a vertical asymptote of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

2.  If we solve the equation $(f\circ f^{-1})(x) = x$ for
    $y = f^{-1}(x)$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(f^{-1}(x)) &= x \hspace{3.7em}  \\[8pt]
    f\left(y\right) &= x
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.7em} 1+ \dfrac{4y}{y+3} &= x \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{5y+3}{y+3} &= x \\[10pt]
    5y+3 &= xy+3x \\[16pt]
    (5-x)y &= 3x-3 \\[10pt]
    y &= \dfrac{3x-3}{5-x} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence an expression for
    $f^{-1}$ is
     $f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{3x-3}{5-x}$

3.  If we solve the equation $f(x) = f^{-1}(x)$ for $x$, we get

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     f(x) &= f^{-1}(x) \\[9pt]
     1 + \dfrac{4x}{x+3} &= \dfrac{3x-3}{5-x} \\[6pt]
     \dfrac{5x+3}{x+3} &= \dfrac{3x-3}{5-x} \\[10pt]
     (5x+3)(5-x) &= (3x-3)(x+3) \\[12pt]
     \hspace{1.6em} 25x - 5x^2 + 15 -3x &= 3x^2 + 9x -3x - 9 \\[12pt]
     -8x^2 + 16x + 24 &= 0
     \end{aligned}
     $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^2 - 2x - 3 &= 0 \\[12pt]
    (x+1)(x-3) &= 0 \hspace{4em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{3.6em} x = -1,\hspace{0.25em}3
    $$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}

    \hspace{8.1em} \begin{array}{rcccl}
    && |f(x)| &<& 2 \\[13pt]
    -2 &<& f(x) &<& 2 \\[12pt]
    f^{-1}(-2) &<& x &<& f^{-1}(2) \hspace{2em} [\text{since $f^{-1}(f(x)) = x$}]
    \end{array}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating $y = f^{-1}(x)$ for $x = -2$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.4em} f^{-1}(-2) &= \dfrac{3(-2)-3}{5-(-2)} \\[3pt]
    &= -\dfrac{9}{7} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating $y = f^{-1}(x)$ for
    $x = 2$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.2em} f^{-1}(2) &= \dfrac{3(2)-3}{5-2}  \hspace{0.8em} \\[8pt]
    &= 1 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the set of solutions to the inequality
    $|f(x)| < 2$ is
     $-\frac{9}{7}<x<1$

5.  If we solve the inequality $f(|x|) < 2$ for $|x|$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.5em} 1+ \dfrac{4|x|}{|x|+3} &< 2 \\[5pt]
    \dfrac{4|x|}{|x|+3} &< 1 \\[10pt]
    4|x| &< |x|+3 \\[16pt]
    3|x| &< 3 \\[16pt]
    |x| &< 1 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the set of solutions to the
    inequality $f(|x|) < 2$ is
     $-1<x<1$
