# AA136

**Maximum mark: 6**

The quadratic equation $x^2 - kx + (k - 3) = 0$ has roots $\alpha$ and
$\beta$ such that $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 6$. Without solving the
equation, find the possible values of the real number $k$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Using Vieta's formulas, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha+\beta &=-\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} \\[4pt]
&= -\dfrac{(-k)}{1} \\[12pt]
&= k
\end{aligned}
$$
and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2.9em} \alpha\beta &= (-1)^2\bigg[\dfrac{a_0}{a_2}\bigg] \\[4pt]
&= \dfrac{k-3}{1} \\[12pt]
&= k-3
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\alpha + \beta)^2  &= k^2 \\[6pt]
\hspace{5.2em} \alpha^2 + \beta^2 + 2\alpha\beta &= k^2 \\[6pt]
6 + 2(k - 3)  &=  k^2  \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 6$}] \\[6pt]
\cancel{6} + 2k - \cancel{6}  &=  k^2 \\[6pt]
0 &= k^2 - 2k \\[7pt]
0 &= k(k - 2)\\
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\hspace{0.5em} k = 0,\hspace{0.25em}2
$$
