# AA1277

**Maximum mark: 4**

When the resulting product of $3x^2+7x-6$ multiplied by $ax+1$ is divided by $x-1$ the remainder is $-4$.

Find the integer $a$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Although not essential, we may notice that we can factorise the quadratic expression

$$

\begin{aligned}

    3x^2+7x-6&=(3x-2)(x+3)

\end{aligned}

$$

We will let the the product of the quadratic and linear expression be $p(x)$, hence

$$

\begin{aligned}

    p(x)&=(3x-2)(x+3)(ax+1)\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Now let's consider dividing $p(x)$ by $x-1$.

Using the remainder theorem we can form an equation

$$

\begin{aligned}

    p(1)&=-4\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Hence substituting $x=1$ into $p(x)$ the L.H.S. of above becomes

$$

\begin{aligned}

    (3(1)-2)(1+3)(a(1)+1)&=-4\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

We can now solve this for $a$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    1\times 4 \times (a+1)&=-4\\[6pt]

    a+1&=-1\\[6pt]

    a&=-2\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$
