# AA141

**Maximum mark: 12**

Consider the polynomial $p(z) = z^5 + z^4 - z^3 + z^2 + 4z + 2$, for
$z \in \mathbb{C}$.

1.  Write down the sum and product of the roots of $p(z) = 0$.
    [2 marks]

2.  Show that $(z+1)$ is a factor of $p(z)$. [2 marks]

The polynomial can be written in the form
$p(z) = (z+1)^3(z^2 + cz + d)$.

3.  Find the value of $c$ and the value of $d$. [5 marks]

4.  Hence find the complex roots of $p(z) = 0$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using Vieta's formulas, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{sum of roots} &= -\dfrac{a_4}{a_5} \hspace{1.1em} \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{1} \\[12pt]
    &= -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{product of roots} &= (-1)^5\bigg[\dfrac{a_0}{a_5}\bigg] \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{2}{1} \\[12pt]
    &= -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Evaluating $p(z)$ for $z = -1$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} p(-1) &= (-1)^5 + (-1)^4 - (-1)^3 + (-1)^2 + 4(-1) + 2 \\[6pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, by factor theorem, $(z+1)$ is a factor of
    $p(z)$.

3.  Evaluating $p(z)$ for $z = 0$ and $z = 1$, we have $p(0) = 2$ and
    $p(1) = 8$.

    Hence, substituting $z = 0,\hspace{0.1em}1$ in the equation
    $p(z) = (z+1)^3(z^2 + cz + d)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} p(0) &= (0+1)^3(0^2 + c(0) + d) \\[6pt]
    2 &= d \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    d = 2 \hspace{6.1em}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} p(1) &= (1+1)^3(1^2 + c(1) + 2) \\[6pt]
    8 &= 8(c+3) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    c = -2 \hspace{5.2em}
    $$

4.  The complex roots of $p(z) = 0$ are the complex roots of
    $z^2 - 2z + 2 = 0$.

    Hence, using the quadratic formula, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} z &= \dfrac{-(-2)\pm\sqrt{2^2-4(1)(2)}}{2(1)} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{2 \pm 2{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}}{2} \\[12pt]
    &= 1 \pm {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
