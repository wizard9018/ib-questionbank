# AA135

**Maximum mark: 7**

Let $f(x) = ax^3 + bx^2 + cx  +  d$, for $x \in \mathbb{R}$, where
$a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{Q}$. The diagram below shows part of the graph of
$y = f(x)$.

![f2e9d58912ae2f10d7fdf9d12ee3b7594c2c45fb.svg](images/f2e9d58912ae2f10d7fdf9d12ee3b7594c2c45fb.svg)

1.  Using the information shown in the diagram, find the values of $a$,
    $b$, $c$ and $d$.[4 marks]

2.  Let $g(x) = -\dfrac{3}{4}f(-2x+1)$.
    1.  Find the coordinates of the points where the graph of $y = g(x)$
        intercepts the $x$-axis.

    2.  Find the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = g(x)$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  From the diagram, we see that the roots of the polynomial equation
    $f(x) = 0$ are $-3$, $-1$ and $3$.

    Hence, using the factor theorem, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= a(x-3)(x+1)(x+3) \\[6pt]
    &= a(x^2-9)(x+1) \\[6pt]
    &= a(x^3 + x^2 - 9x - 9)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we substitute
    $x = 0$ in the expression $(1)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.6em} f(0) &= a(0^3 + 0^2 - 9(0) - 9) \\[12pt]
    -3 &= -9a \hspace{3em} [\text{since $f(0) = -3$}] \\[6pt]
    a &= \dfrac{1}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we substitute $a = 1/3$ in the
    expression $(1)$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= \dfrac{1}{3}(x^3 + x^2 - 9x - 9) \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{3}x^3 + \dfrac{1}{3}x^2 - 3x - 3 \hspace{1.1em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we find
     $a = b = \dfrac{1}{3}$
    and  $c = d = -3$

2.
    1.  Solving the equation $g(x) = 0$ for $x$, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        -\dfrac{3}{4}f(-2x + 1) &= 0 \\[6pt]
        f(-2x + 1) &= 0 \\[12pt]
        -2x + 1 &= -3,\hspace{0.25em} -1,\hspace{0.25em} 3 \hspace{4em} \\[12pt]
        -2x &= -4,\hspace{0.25em} -2,\hspace{0.25em} 2 \\[12pt]
        x &= -1,\hspace{0.25em}1,\hspace{0.25em}2
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence
        the coordinates of the $x$-intercepts of the graph of $y = g(x)$
        are
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        (-1,0),\hspace{0.25em}(1,0),\hspace{0.25em}(2,0)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Evaluating $y=g(x)$ for $x = 0$, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{10.9em} y &= g(0) \\[10pt]
        &= -\dfrac{3}{4}f(2(0)+1) \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{3}{4}f(1) \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{3}{4}\hspace{-0.2em}\left[\dfrac{1}{3}(1)^3 + \dfrac{1}{3}(1)^2 - 3(1) - 3\right] \\[4pt]
        &=-\dfrac{3}{4}\hspace{-0.2em}\left[-\dfrac{16}{3}\right] \\[10pt]
        &= 4
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the $y$-intercept of the graph of
        $y = g(x)$ is
         $\mathrm{P}(0,4)$
