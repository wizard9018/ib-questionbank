# AA1316

**Maximum mark: 7**

A function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=\dfrac{a-3x}{x-2}$, where $x\in \mathbb{R}$, $x\neq 2$ and $a \in \mathbb{Z}$.

1.    For the graph of $y=f(x)$, write down the equation of

    1.    the vertical asymptote; 

    2.    the horizontal asymptote. [2 marks]

It is known that $f(1)=f^{-1}(1)$.

2.    Find the value of $a$. [3 marks]

3.    Hence, sketch the graph of $f$ on the axes below. On your sketch, indicate the values of any axis intercepts and label any asymptotes with their equations.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  $f$ is a rational function. This means it can be written as a fraction with a function of $x$ in the numerator and the denominator.

    1. The vertical asymptote occurs when $f$ is undefined.

        $f$ is undefined for any $x$-value that causes the denominator to equal zero.

        Hence, we can form and solve an equation to find the value of $x$ that makes $f$ undefined.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x-2&=0\\[6pt]

        x&=2

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The answer must be written as an equation. Hence  $x=2$.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. The horizontal asymptote of $f$ can be found by considering extremely large positive and negative values.

        Let's consider the numerator $a-3x$ to begin with.

        If $x$ becomes extremely large in the positive or negative direction, then the value of $a-3x$ becomes closer to $-3x$. The value of $a$ is less and less significant to the value of $a-3x$ as the size of $x$ grows.

        

        Similarly, considering the denominator, the value of $x-2$ approaches $x$ as the size of $x$ grows.

        Hence, if we put that together, for large $x$ values we get

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        f(x)&=\frac{-3\cancel{x}}{\cancel{x}}\\[6pt]

        &=-3

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Notice that, as the $x$'s cancel out, we don't need to worry about $x$ being positively or negatively large.

        Therefore the equation of the horizontal asymptote is $y=-3$

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. We know that a function and its inverse will be equivalent on the line $y=x$. 

    It is known that the inverse functions are equivalent when $x=1$. Hence we also know that $f(x)=1$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{\textbf{{R1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can substitute $1$ in place of $x$ and $f(x)$ to solve for $a$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{a-3(1)}{(1)-2}&=1\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[6pt]

    -a+3&=1\\[6pt]

    a&=2\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. First, we can find the axis intercepts.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y&\text{-intercept}&&&x&\text{-intercept}\\[6pt]

    f(0)&=\frac{2-3(0)}{(0)-2}&&&0&=\frac{2-3x}{x-2}\\[6pt]

    &=-1&&&&=\frac{2}{3}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the answers from part (a) we can draw the asymptotes and also plot the intercepts.
