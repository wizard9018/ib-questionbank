# AA185

**Maximum mark: 8**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{3x-6}{x+1}$, for $x\neq-1$.

1.  For the graph of $y = f(x)$, find the coordinates of:
    1.  the $x$-intercept;

    2.  the $y$-intercept. [3 marks]

2.  For the graph of $y = f(x)$, find the equation of:
    1.  the horizontal asymptote;

    2.  the vertical asymptote. [3 marks]

3.  Hence sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Solving the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{3x-6}{x+1} &= 0 \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
        3x-6 &= 0 \\[10pt]
        x &= 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the coordinates of the $x$-intercept
        are  $(2,0)$

    2.  Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = 0$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{4.8em} y &= f(0) \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{3(0)-6}{0+1} \\[6pt]
        &= -6
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the coordinates of the $y$-intercept
        are  $(0,-6)$

2.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \lim_{x \rightarrow \pm \infty} f(x) &= \dfrac{3}{1} \hspace{2.5em} \\[6pt]
        &= 3
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the equation of the horizontal
        asymptote is
         $y = 3$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &= 0 \hspace{6em} \\[6pt]
        x+1 &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x &= -1
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the equation of the vertical
        asymptote is
         $x = -1$
