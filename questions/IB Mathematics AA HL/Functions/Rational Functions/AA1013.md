# AA1013

**Maximum mark: 17**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{3x+4}{2x-5}$, for $x\neq\dfrac{5}{2}$. 

1. For the graph of $y = f(x)$, find the coordinates of:

    1. the $x$-intercept;

    2. the $y$-intercept. [3 marks]

2. For the graph of $y = f(x)$, find the equation of:

    1. the horizontal asymptote;

    2. the vertical asymptote. [3 marks]

3. Hence sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$.  [2 marks]

4. Find $f^{-1}(x)$. [3 marks]

The graphs of $f(x)$ and $f^{-1}(x)$ have two points of intersection.

5. Find the coordinates of the two points of intersection. **Give your answers to two decimal places**. [3 marks]

6. Hence find the equation of the perpendicular bisector to the two points of intersection. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Solving the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \dfrac{3x+4}{2x-5} &= 0 \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]

        3x+4 &= 0 \\[10pt]

        x &= -\dfrac{4}{3}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence the coordinates of the $x$-intercept are $\left(-\dfrac{4}{3},0\right)$

    

    2. Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = 0$, we have 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        y &= f(0) \\[6pt]

        &= \dfrac{3(0)+4}{2(0)-5} \\[6pt]

        &= -\dfrac{4}{5}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence the coordinates of the $y$-intercept are $\left(0,-\dfrac{4}{5}\right)$

2. 1. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \lim_{x \rightarrow \pm \infty} f(x) &= \dfrac{3}{2} \hspace{2.5em} \\[6pt]

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence the equation of the horizontal asymptote is $y = \dfrac{3}{2}$

    2. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        [\text{denominator of $f$}] &= 0 \hspace{6em} \\[6pt]

        2x-5 &= 0 \\[6pt]

        x &= \dfrac{5}{2}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence the equation of the vertical asymptote is $x = \dfrac{5}{2}$

4. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y &= \dfrac{3x+4}{2x-5} \hspace{5.5em} \left[\text{where } y = f(x)\right]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x &= \dfrac{3y+4}{2y-5} \hspace{3em} \left[\text{where } y = f^{-1}(x)\right] \\[8pt]

    x(2y-5) &= 3y+4 \\[8pt]

    2xy - 5x &= 3y +4 \\[8pt]

    2xy - 3y &= 5x + 4 \\[8pt]

    y(2x - 3) &= 5x + 4 \\[8pt]

    y &= \dfrac{5x+4}{2x-3} \\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-3em}\boxed{f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{5x+4}{2x-3}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. The midpoint of AB is given by

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2} , \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right) &= \left(\dfrac{-0.45+4.45}{2} , \dfrac{-0.45+4.45}{2}\right) \\[8pt]

    &= (2,2)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Point A and point B both lie on the line $y=x$, which has gradient $m=1$. Therefore the perpendicular bisector has gradient $m=-1$.

    The equation of the perpendicular bisector is given by

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[8pt]

    y - 2 &= -(x - 2) \\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-0.525em}\boxed{y = -x + 4}

    \end{aligned}
