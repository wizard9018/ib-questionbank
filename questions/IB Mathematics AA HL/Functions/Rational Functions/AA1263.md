# AA1263

**Maximum mark: 10**

Consider the functions $f$ and $g$ such that $f(x)=x^2-5x+4$ and $g(x)=\dfrac{x-2}{f(x)}$.

1. Evaluate $g(3)$.[2 marks]

For the graph of $g$,

2. Determine the equations of the vertical asymptotes.[2 marks]

3. Find the coordinates of any axes intercepts.[2 marks]

4. Determine the equation of the horizontal asymptote.[1 mark]

5. Hence sketch the graph of $g$, labelling any asymptotes and any axes intercepts.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Substituting $f(x) = x^2-5x+4$ into $g(x)$, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        g(x) &= \dfrac{x-2}{x^2-5x+4}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Now substituting $x=3$,

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        g(3)&=\dfrac{3-2}{(3)^2-5(3)+4}\\[6pt]

        g(3)&=\boxed{-\dfrac{1}{2}}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Factorising the denominator of $g(x)$ from part (a), we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        g(x) &= \dfrac{x-2}{(x-1)(x-4)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The vertical asymptotes occur at $x$-values that make the denominator equal to zero.

    

    Setting the denominator equal to zero and solving, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        (x-1)(x-4) &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    By the null factor theorem, this leads to $x=1$ and $x=4$ and therefore the equations of the vertical asymptotes are 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \boxed{x=1} \hspace{1em}\text{ and }\hspace{1em}\boxed{x=4} \tag*{\textbf{A1A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. The $y$-intercept occurs when $x=0$. Evaluating $g(x)$ when $x=0$, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        g(0)&=\dfrac{0-2}{(0)^2-5(0)-4}\\[6pt]

        g(0)&=\dfrac{-2}{-4}\\[6pt]

        g(0)&=-\dfrac{1}{2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Hence the coordinates of the $y$-intercept are $\left(0,-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

    $$\\[-3em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The $x$-intercepts occur when the function value is 0. Solving the equation $g(x)=0$ for $x$, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        0&=\dfrac{x-2}{x^2-5x-4}\\

        0&=x-2\\

        x&=2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Hence the coordinates of the $x$-intercept are $\left(2,0\right)$

    $$\\[-2.7em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

  

4. To find the equation of the horizontal asymptote, we examine the behaviour of the function as $x$ approaches $\pm \infty$. As $x\rightarrow\infty$, $g(x)\rightarrow0^+$. As $x\rightarrow-\infty$, $g(x)\rightarrow0^-$.

  

    Therefore the equation of the horizontal asymptote is $y=0$

    $$\\[-2.7em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. The left branch of the equation passing through $\big(0,-\frac{1}{2}\big)$ is bounded by the asymptotes and easy to graph. 

  

    The middle and right branches require additional consideration.

    As $x\rightarrow1^+$, $g(x)\rightarrow+\infty$.

  

    As $x\rightarrow4^-$, $g(x)\rightarrow-\infty$.

    When $x=5$, $g(x)=\frac{3}{4}$. This branch is above the $x$-axis and bounded by the asymptotes.
