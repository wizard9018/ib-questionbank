# AA1042

**Maximum mark: 7**

The brightness of a star can be measured in terms of its apparent magnitude as seen from Earth. The apparent magnitude $M$ of a star is given by the formula
$$
M = -2.5 \log\left(\dfrac{F}{F_0}\right)
$$
where $F$ is the observed flux density and $F_0$ is a reference flux density.

1. Solve for $F$ in terms of $M$.[2 marks]

2. Suppose that $M(F_1)=1$ and $M(F_6)=6$. Find the value of $\dfrac{F_1}{F_6}$.[1 mark]

Now, let's look at the difference in magnitude of two stars. Suppose that star $1$ has observed flux density of $F_1$ with magnitude $M_1$ while star $2$ has flux density $F_2$ with magnitude $M_2$.

3. Find an expression for the difference in magnitude $M_2-M_1$.[2 marks]

The apparent magnitude of the sun is $M_s=-26.8$ and the apparent magnitude of the full moon is $M_m=-12.7$.

4. Their brightness factor is $\dfrac{F_s}{F_m}=10^{\tfrac{a}{b}}$ where $a,b \in \mathbb{Q^+}$. Find $a$ and $b$.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. By applying logarithm and exponential properties, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    M &= -2.5\big[\log(F)-\log(F_0)\big] \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[10pt]
    M &= -2.5\log(F)+2.5\log(F_0) \\[11pt]
    2.5\log(F) &= 2.5\log(F_0) - M \\[8pt]
    \log(F) &= \log(F_0)- \dfrac{M}{2.5} \\[7pt]
    &\hspace{-1.2em}F = F_0 \cdot 10^{-\frac{M\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. Using the previous answer, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{F_1}{F_6} &= \dfrac{\cancel{F_0} \cdot 10^{-\frac{1\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}}}{\cancel{F_0}\cdot  10^{-\frac{6\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}}} \\[10pt]
    &= \dfrac{10^{-\frac{1\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}}}{10^{-\frac{6\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}}} \\[10pt]
    &= 10^{\frac{6\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}-\frac{1\rule[-1pt]{0pt}{6pt}}{2.5}}\\[10pt]
    &= 10^{2} \\[10pt]
    \dfrac{F_1}{F_6} &=100 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. Using logarithm properties again, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    M_2-M_1 &= -2.5\log\left(\dfrac{F_2}{F_0}\right) - \left[-2.5\log\left(\dfrac{F_1}{F_0}\right)\right] \\[10pt]
    &= -2.5\left[\log\left(\dfrac{F_2}{F_0}\right)-\log\left(\dfrac{F_1}{F_0}\right)\right]\tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[10pt]
    &= -2.5\left[\log\left(\dfrac{F_2}{F_0}\div\dfrac{F_1}{F_0}\right)\right] \\[10pt]
    &= -2.5\left[\log\left(\dfrac{F_2}{F_0}\div\dfrac{F_1}{F_0}\right)\right] \\[10pt]
    M_2-M_1 &=-2.5 \log\left(\dfrac{F_2}{F_1}\right)\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. From part (c), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -2.5 \log\left(\dfrac{F_s}{F_m}\right) &= M_s-M_m \\[10pt]
    \log\left(\dfrac{F_s}{F_m}\right) &= \dfrac{-26.8-(-12.7)}{-2.5} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[10pt]
    \dfrac{F_s}{F_m} &=10^{\tfrac{-14.1}{-2.5}} \\[10pt]
    &\hspace{-2.8em}a=14.1 \text{ and }b=2.5 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
