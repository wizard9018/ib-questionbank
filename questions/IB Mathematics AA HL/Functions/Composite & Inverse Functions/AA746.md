# AA746

**Maximum mark: 7**

A town is planning to construct a jogging path in a grass field
$170$ m long and $70$ m
wide. The path is to be the shape of a rectangle with two semicircles of
radius $x$, as shown in the diagram. The sides of the rectangle
connecting the circles are to be $100$ m long.

![22448faee8b9c6574b3fdefa8505f67030a94583.svg](images/22448faee8b9c6574b3fdefa8505f67030a94583.svg)

1.  Write down a function, $P$, (in metres) for the perimeter of the
    jogging path, in terms of the radius, $x$.[1 mark]

2.  Determine the domain and range of $P$, taking into consideration the
    dimensions of the grass field.[2 marks]

3.  Find an equation for the inverse function $P^{-1}(x)$. Express your
    answer in the form $P^{-1}(x)=mx+c$.[2 marks]

The designers of the path are deciding whether the total length of the
path should be $300$ m,
$400$ m, or $500$ m. The
designers want to maximise the perimeter of the path, but fit the path
in the grass field.

4.  Determine which length is most suitable, given the
    dimensions of the grass field.[2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The perimeter for the path is the sum of the two straight sides
    connecting the circles and the perimeter of a complete circle of
    radius $x$. We get
    $$
    P(x) = 200+2\pi x
    $$

2.  The length of the path will be $100+2x$ and its width $2x$.
    Therefore we require $100+2x\leq 170$ and $2x\leq 70$. Hence

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Domain: } 0 < x \leq 35.\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    For
    the range, the function $P(x)$ is increasing and $P(0)=200$ and\
    $P(35)=200+70\pi$. We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Range: } 200\leq P(x) \leq 200+70\pi.\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Let's denote $P(x)$ by $y$. To find the inverse we can interchange
    the variables $x$ and $y$ and rearrange for $y$, as follows

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= 200+2\pi x\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    x &= 200+2\pi y\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    y &= \dfrac{x-200}{2\pi}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    &\hspace{-3.2em}P^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\pi}x - \dfrac{100}{\pi}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  For each of the proposed perimeters $P$, we can find the radius
    value $x$ as $x=P^{-1}(P)$, then the length and width of the running
    track can be calculated, as shown in the following table.

    ![8985560368df68be350c8eca5afbe5b19a733963.svg](images/8985560368df68be350c8eca5afbe5b19a733963.svg)

    From the table, we can see that the largest perimeter of the options
    that still fit inside the grass field dimensions is

    $$
    P=400\hspace{0.25em}\text{m}.
    $$
