# AA191

**Maximum mark: 8**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{4}x^2 - 2$ and $g(x) = x^2 - 4$, for
$x \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Show that $(f\circ g)(x) = \dfrac{1}{4}x^4 - 2x^2 + 2$. [2 marks]

2.  On the following grid, sketch the graph of $y = (f \circ g)(x)$, for
    $0 \leq x \leq 3$. [3 marks]

![b4ef7d37b745cb564b996b9d79dff67b636cf799.svg](images/b4ef7d37b745cb564b996b9d79dff67b636cf799.svg)

3.  The equation $(f\circ g)(x) = k$ has exactly two solutions,
    for $0 \leq x \leq 3$. Find\
    the possible values of $k$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (f\circ g)(x) &= f(g(x)) \\[6pt]
    &= f(x^2 - 4) \\[6pt]
    \hspace{3em} &= \dfrac{1}{4}(x^2 - 4)^2 - 2 \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{4}(x^4 - 8x^2+16) - 2 \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{4}x^4 - 2x^2 + 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The line $y = k$ intersects the graph of $y = (f \circ g)(x)$
    exactly at two points when $-2 < k \leq 2$.

    Hence the equation $(f \circ g)(x) = k$ has exactly two solutions
    when  $-2 < k \leq 2$
