# AA196

**Maximum mark: 14**

A quadratic function $f$ can be written in the form
$f(x) = p(x-1)(x-q)$.

The graph of $y = f(x)$ has axis of symmetry $x = 2$ and $y$-intercept
at $(0,-3)$.

1.  Find the value of $q$. [3 marks]

2.  Find the value of $p$. [3 marks]

The line $y = kx + 6$ is a tangent to the graph of $y = f(x)$.

3.  Find the possible values of $k$. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The $x$-coordinates of the $x$-intercepts are $x=1$ and $x=q$.
    Hence, using the equation of the axis of symmetry, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1+q}{2} &= 2 \\[6pt]
    1+q &= 4 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{2em} q = 3
    $$

2.  Using the coordinates of the $y$-intercept, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(0) &= -3 \hspace{3.3em} \\[6pt]
    p(0-1)(0-3) &= -3 \\[6pt]
    3p &= -3 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{2.7em} p = -1
    $$

3.  If we rewrite the equation $f(x) = kx+6$ in the form
    $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} -(x-1)(x-3) &= kx+6 \\[6pt]
    -x^2+4x-3 &= kx+6 \\[6pt]
    0 &= x^2+(k-4)x+9
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the quadratic function
    $q(x) = x^2+(k-4)x+9$ has two equal roots.

    Therefore we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.2em} [\text{discriminant of $q$}] &= 0 \\[6pt]
    (k-4)^2 - 4(1)(9) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\triangle  = b^2 - 4ac]  \\[6pt]
    (k-4)^2 &= 36 \\[7pt]
    k-4 &= \pm 6 \\[7pt]
    k &= 4 \pm 6 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{4.75em} k=-2,\hspace{0.25em} 10
    $$
