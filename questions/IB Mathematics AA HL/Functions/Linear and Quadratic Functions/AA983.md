# AA983

**Maximum mark: 16**

The graph of a function $f$ has its vertex at the point $(1,7)$ and intersects the $x$-axis at $x=-3$. 

1. Find $f$ in the form $f(x)=a\left(x-h\right)^2+k$. [3 marks]

The function $g$ is defined by $g(x)=mx^2-2nx+\dfrac{2n^2}{m}$ where $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $m \in \mathbb{R}^+$ and $n \in \mathbb{R}$.

2. In the case where $g(-2)=g(1)=3$,

    1. find the value of $m$ and the value of $n$;

    2. Find the range of $g$.[7 marks]  

The linear function $h$ is defined by $h(x)=nx+1$ where $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

3. Show that the graphs of $h$ and $g$ have two distinct points of intersection for every possible value of $m \in \mathbb{R}^+$ and $n \in \mathbb{R}$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. In the form $f(x)=a\left(x-h\right)^2+k$, $h$ is the $x$-coordinate of the vertex, and $k$ is the $y$-coordinate. Hence $h=1$ and $k=7$, and $f(x)=a\left(x-1\right)^2+7$.

    The graph of $f$ passes through the point $(-3,0)$, so we have $f(-3)=0$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(-3) &= a\left(-3-1\right)^2+7 \\[8pt]

    0 &= 16a + 7 \\[8pt]

    a &= -\dfrac{7}{16}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \boxed{f(x)=-\dfrac{7}{16}\left(x-1\right)^2+7}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. 1. We have that $g(-2)=g(1)=3$, so $g$ passes through the points $(-2,3)$ and $(1,3)$. Given that $g$ is a quadratic and hence its graph is a parabola, we have

        ![aa983a](images/aa983a.PNG)

        The axis of symmetry must be halfway between these points and has equation $x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Using the formula for the axis of symmetry, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x &= -\dfrac{b}{2a} \\[8pt]

        -\dfrac{1}{2} &= -\dfrac{-2n}{2m} \\[8pt]

        m &= -2n

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        

        Substituting $-2n$ in place of $m$, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        g(x) &= -2nx^2 -2nx + \dfrac{2n^2}{-2n} \\[8pt]

        &= -2nx^2 - 2nx - n

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        And given that $g(1)=3$,

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        g(1) &= -2n(1)^2 - 2n(1) - n \\[8pt]

        3 &= -5n \\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{n = -\dfrac{3}{5}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        And calculating the value of $m$,

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        m &= -2n \\[8pt]

        &= -2\left(-\dfrac{3}{5}\right) \\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{m = \dfrac{6}{5}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. As stated in part (b)(i), the $x$-coordinate of the vertex is $x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The $y$-coordinate will therefore be equal to $g\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$:

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        g(x) &= \dfrac{6}{5}x^2 + \dfrac{6}{5}x + \dfrac{3}{5} \\[6pt]

        g\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) &= \left(\dfrac{6}{5}\right)\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{6}{5}\right)\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) + \dfrac{3}{5} \\[6pt]

        &= \dfrac{3}{10} - \dfrac{6}{10} + \dfrac{6}{10} \\[6pt]

        &= \dfrac{3}{10}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The vertex is at $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{3}{10}\right)$ and the graph of $g$ is as follows:

        ![aa983b](images/aa983b.PNG)

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \boxed{\text{The range of $g$ is $g(x) \geq \dfrac{3}{10}$.}}  

        \end{aligned}

        $$

3. If $g$ and $h$ always have two distinct points of intersection, then the equation $g(x)=h(x)$ must have two unique solutions. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    g(x) &= h(x) \\[8pt]

    mx^2-2nx+\dfrac{2n^2}{m} &= nx + 1 \\[8pt]

    mx^2 - 3nx + \dfrac{2n^2}{m} -1 &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can use the discriminant to check the number of solutions. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \Delta &= b^2 - 4ac \\[8pt]

    &= (-3n)^2 - 4(m)\left(\dfrac{2n^2}{m} -1\right)\\[8pt]

    &=(-3n)^2 - 4(m)\left(\dfrac{2n^2}{m} -1\right) \\[8pt]

    &=9n^2 - 8n^2 + 4m \\[8pt]

    &=n^2 + 4m 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Given that $m \in \mathbb{R}^+$, $4m>0$. $n^2 \geq 0$. Hence $\Delta>0$. Thus

    $$\boxed{\text{The graphs of $h$ and $g$ have two distinct points of intersection for all $m \in \mathbb{R}^+$ and $n \in \mathbb{R}$.}}$$
