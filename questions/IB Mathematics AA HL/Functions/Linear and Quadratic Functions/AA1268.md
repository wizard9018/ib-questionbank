# AA1268

**Maximum mark: 4**

Consider the line $L_1$ which passes through the points $A (-4,-4)$ and $B(8,-1)$.

1. Find the gradient of the line $L_1$. [1 mark]

Line $L_2$ is perpendicular to $L_1$ and passes through the point $(-a,2a)$ where $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

2. Given that $L_2$ intersects the $y$-axis at $y=2a-3$, find the value of $a$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the formula for the gradient of a line

    $$

     \begin{aligned}

        m&=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    we can find the gradient of $L_1$

    $$

     \begin{aligned}

        m_1&=\frac{-1-(-4)}{8-(-4)}\\[9pt]

       &=\frac{3}{12}\\[9pt]

       &=\dfrac{1}{4}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

    $$

2.  As $L_2$ is perpendicular to $L_1$ we can find the gradient of $L_2$ using the formula

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        m_1\times m_2&=-1\\[9pt]

        \frac{1}{4}\times m_2&=-1\\[9pt]

        m_2&=-4\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can form an equation, using the gradient formula, and solve for $a$.

    Substituting in $m_2$ and the points $(-a,2a)$ and $(0,2a-3)$ we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    m_2&=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\\[9pt]

    -4&=\frac{(2a-3)-2a}{0-(-a)}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(M1)}}}\\[9pt]

      -4 &=\frac{-3}{a}\\[9pt]

    -4a&=-3\\[6pt] 

    a&=\dfrac{3}{4}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
