# AA573

**Maximum mark: 6**

The following diagram shows the graph of $f(x) = \dfrac {4x}{x^2 + 1}$,
for $0 \leq x \leq 6$, and the line $x = 6$.

![2c4cfb84f6a2023641db5a3e6fb90100b872411a.svg](images/2c4cfb84f6a2023641db5a3e6fb90100b872411a.svg)

Let $R$ be the region enclosed by the graph of $f$, the $x$-axis and the
line $x = 6$.

Find the area of $R$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

The area of $R$ is given by the integral
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_R         & = \int_0^6 f(x)\,\textrm dx\\[8pt]
& = \int_0^6 \dfrac {4x} {x^2 + 1}\,\textrm d x
\end{aligned}
$$
\
Integrating by substitution with $u = x^2 + 1$ and
$\textrm du = 2 x \,\textrm d x$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{5mm}
A_R         & = \int \dfrac 2 u \,\textrm du\\[8pt]
& =  2 \ln (u)\\[6pt]
& =  2 \ln (x^2 + 1)  \Big|_0^{6}\\[8pt]
& = 2 \ln(37) - 2 \ln(1)\\[10pt]
&\hspace{.275em} = 2 \ln 37
\end{aligned}
$$
