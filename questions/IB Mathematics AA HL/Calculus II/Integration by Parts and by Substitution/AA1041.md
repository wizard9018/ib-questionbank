# AA1041

**Maximum mark: 18**

Consider the function $f_n(x)=e^{x}\cos{nx}$, where $n\in \mathbb{Z}$.

1.  1.  Show that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi} f_2(x) \,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{5}(e^{2\pi}-1)$.

    2.  Show that $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi} f_n(x) \,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{1+n^2}(e^{2\pi}-1)$. [7 marks]

2.  1.  Expand and simplify $\cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)$.

    2.  Hence, write $\cos 3x\cos x$ as the sum of two cosine functions.

    3.  Hence, find $a$, where $a\in \mathbb{Q}$, if $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{x}\cos{3x}\cos{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=a(e^{2\pi}-1)$. [6 marks]

3.  Find the value of $b$, where $b\in \mathbb{Q}$, if $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{x}\sin{4x}\sin{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x=b(e^{2\pi}-1)$. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  As $n=2$, we need to integrate the following function
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{0}^{2\pi} f_2(x) \,\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        The integral is a product of two functions of $x$, this suggests we use integration by parts (IbP).

        We need to choose a function for $u$ and $v'$, it is a good strategy to pick a function that is easy to integrate for $v'$. Hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            u&=\cos{2x} \hspace{2em} \text{ therefore } u'=-2\sin{2x} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\
            v'&=e^x \hspace{3.6em} \text{ therefore } v=e^x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can then substitute these expressions into the formula for $\textbf{integration by parts}$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\Big[e^x\cos{2x}\Big]_{0}^{2\pi}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}-2e^x\sin{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Next resolve the limits and simplify the integral
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\Big[e^{2\pi}\cos4\pi -e^0\cos{0}\Big]+2\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\sin{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x\\[6pt]
            &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)+2\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\sin{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now we need to use (repeat) integration by parts again.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            u&=\sin{2x} \hspace{2em} \text{ therefore } u'=2\cos{2x} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\
            v'&=e^x \hspace{3.6em} \text{ therefore } v=e^x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Substituting them into the integral we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)+2\left(\Big[e^x\sin{2x}\Big]_{0}^{2\pi}-2\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x\right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        In a similar fashion to earlier, we can resolve the limits and simplify
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)+2\left(\Big[e^{2\pi}\sin{4\pi}-e^{0}\sin{0}\Big]-2\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
        &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)-4\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Notice that the integral is a multiple of our original integral
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x&=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)-4\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence $\displaystyle5\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)$

        $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{2x} \,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{5}\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right) \hspace{2em}$ ...as required

    2.  The integral for $n$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{0}^{2\pi} f_n(x) \,\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{nx}\,\mathrm{d}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can use a very similar method to part (a). Notice the coefficient changes to $n$. Use previous workings to help
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\left[e^{2\pi}\cos2n\pi -e^0\cos{0}\right]+n\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\sin{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x\\[6pt]
            &(\cos{2n\pi=1} \text{ for all }n\in \mathbb{Z})\\
            &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)+n\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\sin{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We again repeat IbP, in a similar fashion to part (a)
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)+n\left(\Big[e^x\sin{nx}\Big]_{0}^{2\pi}-n\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x\right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Resolve the limits and simplify
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)+n\left(\Big[e^{2\pi}\sin{2n\pi}-e^{0}\sin{0}\Big]-n\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
        &(\sin{2n\pi=0} \text{ for all }n\in \mathbb{Z})\\
        &=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)-n^2\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence $\displaystyle(1+n^2)\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x=\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right)$

        $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^x\cos{nx} \,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{1+n^2}\left(e^{2\pi}-1\right) \hspace{2em}$ ...as required

2.  1.  Using the compound angle formulae for cosine

        $\cos{(A+B)}+\cos{(A-B)}$

        $=\cos A\cos B -\sin A\sin B+(\cos A\cos B+\sin A\sin B)$

        $=\cos A\cos B-\cancel{\sin A\sin B}+\cos A\cos B+\cancel{\sin A\sin B}$

        $=2\cos A\cos B$
        $$
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        $$
        $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

    2.  By comparing $\cos 3x\cos x$ with the previous result we can see that $A=3x$ and $B=x$. Hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            2\cos 3x\cos x &= \cos(3x+x)+\cos(3x-x) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
            \cos 3x\cos x &= \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos{4x}+\cos{2x}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    3.  Use the previous answer to rewrite the integral
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{x}\cos{3x}\cos{x}\,\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tfrac{1}{2}e^{x}\left(\cos{4x}+\cos{2x}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Integrate each term individually
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{x}\cos{4x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{x}\cos{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now use the formula from (a)(ii), where $n=4$ and $n=2$ respectively
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+4^2}(e^{2\pi}-1)+ \dfrac{1}{1+2^2}(e^{2\pi}-1)\right) \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[12pt]

            &\text{(Write $\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{1}{17}+\tfrac{1}{5}\right)$ as a single fraction)}\\[12pt]

            &=\tfrac{11}{85}(e^{2\pi}-1)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Therefore $\hspace{0.525em}a=\tfrac{11}{85}$
        $$
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        $$
        $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

3.  In a similar way to part (b) we can find $\sin{4x}\sin{2x}$ as the sum of two cosine functions.

    $\cos{(A+B)}-\cos{(A-B)}=\cos A\cos B -\sin A\sin B-(\cos A\cos B+\sin A\sin B)$

    $=\cancel{\cos A\cos B}-\sin A\sin B-\cancel{\cos A\cos B}-\sin A\sin B$

    $=-2\sin A\sin B$
    $$
     \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

    Comparing this result to the expression $\sin{4x}\sin{2x}$, we can see that $A=4x$ and $B=2x$, therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -2\sin 4x\sin 2x &= \cos(4x+2x)-\cos(4x-2x)\\[6pt]
         \sin 4x\sin 2x &= -\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\cos6x-\cos2x\right)  \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the integral becomes
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{x}\sin{4x}\sin{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{x}\left(\cos6x-\cos2x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now use the formula from (a)(ii), where $n=6$ and $n=2$ respectively to evaluate the integral
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+6^2}(e^{2\pi}-1)- \dfrac{1}{1+2^2}(e^{2\pi}-1)\right) \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{16}{185}(e^{2\pi}-1)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $\hspace{0.525em}b=\tfrac{16}{185}$
    $$
     \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$
