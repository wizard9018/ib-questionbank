# AA1062

**Maximum mark: 12**

The function $f(x)$ lies entirely to the right of the $y-$axis, it has no $y-$intercept and it has a derivative of $f'(x)=\dfrac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^4-27}}$.

1. 1. Show that $f''(x)=\dfrac{x^2(x^4-81)}{\sqrt{(x^4-27)^3}}$.

    

    2. Hence justify that $f$ has a single point of inflection when $x=3$. [6 marks]

    

2. Find $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^4-27}}\,\mathrm{d}x$. [3 marks]

    

It is known that $\displaystyle\int_{3}^{6} f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=18.33$, to four significant figures.

3. Find $f(x)$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Use the quotient rule to differentiate $f'(x)$

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            f''(x)&=\frac{3x^2\sqrt{x^4-27}-\tfrac{1}{2}\times4x^3\times(x^4-27)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}} \times x^3}{x^4-27} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[3pt]

            &=\frac{3x^2\sqrt{x^4-27}-2x^6(x^4-27)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}}{x^4-27} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[3pt]

            &=\frac{x^2(x^4-27)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}(3(x^4-27)-2x^4)}{x^4-27} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[3pt]

            &\boxed{=\dfrac{x^2(x^4-81)}{\sqrt{(x^4-27)^3}}} \hspace{3em}\text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. At a point of inflection $f''(x)=0$ and the concavity changes.

        $$                        

        \begin{aligned}                

        \tag*{\textbf{M1}}            

        \end{aligned}                

        $$

        Using the numerator from the previous result we can write

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x^2(x^4-81)&=0 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[3pt]

        \implies \,\, x^2&=0 &x^4-81=0\\[3pt]

        x&=0, \pm 3.

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        As $f(x)$ lies entirely to the right of the $y-$axis and has no $y-$interpect the only solution to $f''(x)=0$ is $x=3$. 

        $$                        

        \begin{aligned}                

        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}            

        \end{aligned}                

        $$

        By graphing $f''(x)$ on the G.D.C., we can see that $f''(x)<0$ for $x<3$ (red part) and $f''(x)>0$ for $x>3$ (blue part), confirming a change in concavity.

        ![AA1062](images/AA1062.png)

        $$\text{Hence $f(x)$ has a single point of inflection at $x=3$.}$$

    

2. We can use a substitution of $u=x^4-27$ to find the integral.

    Hence $\tfrac{du}{dx}=4x^3$, therefore $dx=\tfrac{1}{4x^3}du$. We can now form the integral using this information

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        &=\int \frac{\cancel{x^3}}{\sqrt{u}}\times\frac{1}{4\cancel{x^3}}du \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[12pt]

        &=\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}du

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now integrate the expression ($u^{-\frac{1}{2}}$) using the inverse power rule.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        &=\frac{1}{2}u^{\tfrac{1}{2}}+c \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[6pt]

        &=\hspace{0.275em}\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{x^4-27}+c} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
