# AA569

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f'(x) = \dfrac x {\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$. Given that $f(0) = 7$, find
$f(x)$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{3.5cm}
f(x)        & = \int f'(x)\,\textrm d x\\[6pt]
& = \int \dfrac x {\sqrt {x^2 + 1}}\,\textrm d x\\[6pt]
& = \int \dfrac 1 {2\sqrt u} \,\textrm d u\qquad\quad
\mbox{[where $u = x^2 + 1$, $\textrm d u = 2 x \,\textrm d x$]} \\[6pt]
& = \dfrac 1 2 \dfrac {u^{1/2}} {\frac 1 2} + C\\[6pt]
& = u^{1/2} + C\\[6pt]
& = \sqrt{x^2 + 1} + C
\end{aligned}
$$

Using $f(0) = 7$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
7   & = \sqrt {0^2 + 1} + C\hspace{1.5cm}\\[5pt]
7   & = 1 + C\\[5pt]
C   & = 6
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
  $f(x) = \sqrt {x^2 + 1} + 6$
