# AA488

**Maximum mark: 6**

Using the substitution $u = 1-\sqrt{2x}$, find
$\displaystyle \int \dfrac{\sqrt{2x}}{1-\sqrt{2x}}\,\textrm dx$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
u   & =  1-\sqrt{2x} \\[6pt]
\textrm du  & =  -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x}}\,\textrm dx \\[6pt]
-\sqrt{2x}\,\textrm du & =  \textrm dx \\[6pt]
(u-1)\,\textrm du   & =  \textrm dx. \\
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the
substitution $u = 1-\sqrt{2x}$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \dfrac{\sqrt{2x}}{1-\sqrt{2x}}\,\textrm dx & =  -\int \dfrac{(u-1)^2}{u}\,\textrm du \\[3pt]
& =  - \int \dfrac{u^2 - 2u + 1}{u}\,\textrm du \\[6pt]
& =  \int \left[-u + 2 - \dfrac{1}{u} \right]\textrm du \\[6pt]
& =  2u - \dfrac{u^2}{2} - \ln |u| + C \\[6pt]
& =  2(1 - \sqrt{2x}) - \dfrac{(1-\sqrt{2x})^2}{2} - \ln|1-\sqrt{2x}| + C \\[6pt]
\int \dfrac{\sqrt{2x}}{1-\sqrt{2x}}\,\textrm dx&=\dfrac{3}{2} - \sqrt{2x} - x - \ln|1 - \sqrt{2x}| + C
\end{aligned}
$$

__Note, we could omit the $\bm{\frac{3}{2}}$ term from our answer as this value could be absorbed by the constant $\bm{C}$.__

So a correct final answer is also

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \dfrac{\sqrt{2x}}{1-\sqrt{2x}}\,\textrm dx & =- \sqrt{2x} - x - \ln|1 - \sqrt{2x}| + C
\end{aligned}
$$
