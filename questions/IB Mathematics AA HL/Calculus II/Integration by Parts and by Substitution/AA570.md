# AA570

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f'(x)  = \dfrac {12x} {(x^2 - 1)^4}$. Given that $f(0) = 3$, find
$f(x)$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{3.5cm}
f(x)        & = \int f'(x)\,\textrm d x\\[6pt]
& = \int \dfrac {12x} {(x^2 - 1)^4}\,\textrm d x\\[6pt]
& = \int \dfrac 6 {u^4} \,\textrm d u\qquad\quad
\mbox{[where $u = x^2 - 1$, $\textrm d u = 2 x \,\textrm d x$]} \\[6pt]
& = \int 6 u^{-4}\,\textrm d u\\[6pt]
& =  \dfrac {6 u^{-3}} {-3} + C\\[6pt]
& = -2u^{-3} + C\\[6pt]
& = -\dfrac 2 {(x^2 - 1)^3} + C
\end{aligned}
$$

Using $f(0) = 3$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
3   & = -\dfrac 2 {(0^2 - 1)^3} + C\hspace{.7cm}\\[5pt]
3   & = -\dfrac 2 {(-1)^3} + C\\[5pt]
3   & = 2 + C\\[6pt]
C   & = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
  $f(x) = -\dfrac 2 {(x^2 - 1)^3} + 1$
