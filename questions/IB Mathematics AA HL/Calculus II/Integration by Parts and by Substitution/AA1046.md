# AA1046

**Maximum mark: 6**

The following diagram shows part of the graph of $y=\dfrac{6x^2}{1+x^3}$ for $x\geq 0$.

![Screenshot 2024-03-08 at 10.24.07 AM](<images/Screenshot 2024-03-08 at 10.24.07 AM.png>)

The area bounded by the curve, the $x$-axis and the line $x=a$, shaded in grey, equals $2\ln(65)$.

Find the value of $a$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

The area of the region shaded is given by the integral
$$
\begin{aligned}
    &= \int_0^a \dfrac{6x^2}{1+x^3} \,\mathrm{d}x \\[9pt]
\end{aligned}
$$
We can integrate this quotient using a substitution of $u=1+x^3$.
$$
\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
$$

Therefore, $\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} = 3x^2$, hence $\mathrm{d}x=\tfrac{1}{3x^2}\mathrm{d}u$

We can also transform the limits. When $x=0$, $u=1$ and when $x=a$, $u=1+a^3$.

Hence, the integral becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
    &=  \int_1^{1+a^3} \dfrac{6x^2}{u}\times \dfrac{1}{3x^2}\mathrm{d}u\hspace{3em}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[9pt]
    &=  2\int_1^{1+a^3} \dfrac{1}{u}\mathrm{d}u\hspace{3em}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[9pt]
    &= 2\big[\ln(1+a^3)-\ln(1)\,\big] \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[10pt]
    &= 2\ln(1+a^3)\\[-16pt]
\end{aligned}
$$
Now, solving for $a$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \cancel{2}\ln(1+a^3) &= \cancel{2}\ln(65) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[8pt]
    1+a^3 &= 65 \\[8pt]
    a^3 &= 64 \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-0.53em}a = 4 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
