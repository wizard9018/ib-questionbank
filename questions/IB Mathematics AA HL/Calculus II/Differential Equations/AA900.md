# AA900

**Maximum mark: 18**

The population $P$ of fish in a lake after $t$ weeks can be modelled by
the differential equation.

$$
\hspace{5mm}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}t} = k\sqrt{P}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{9mm}, \qquad k,\,t>0
$$

1.  Show that the population of fish is given by
    $$
    P(t) = \left(\dfrac{kt}{2}+\sqrt{P_0}\right)^2\rule[-4.5mm]{0pt}{10.5mm}, \quad t>0
    $$
    where $P_0$ is the initial fish population.[4 marks]

It is known that the initial fish population was $3\hspace{0.1em}000$,
and that $24$ weeks later the population had doubled in
size.

2.  Find the value of $k$ to three significant
    figures.[2 marks]

3.  Estimate the number of fish after $30$ weeks to the nearest
    integer.[2 marks]

After a careful adjustment it is found that the model that best
describes the fish population is given by

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}P_2}{\mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t} = \big(1.89+3\cos(0.2\pi t)\big)\sqrt{P_2}%\rule[-4.5mm]{1pt}{10mm}
$$

where $t$ is the time measured in weeks,
$t \geq 0$.

4.  Verify that
    $P_2=\left(\dfrac{1.89\hspace{0.05em}t}{2}+\dfrac{30\sin(0.2\pi t)}{4\pi}+\sqrt{3\hspace{0.15em}000}\right)^2$
    is the solution of this new
    differential equation.
    [5 marks]

5.  Sketch the graph of $P_2(t)$ and the graph of $P(t)$ found
    in parts (a) and (b) on the same axes, for $0 \leq t \leq 50$.
    [3 marks]

6.  Use $P_2(t)$ to estimate the number of **whole** weeks it
    takes for the population to reach $5\,000$ fish.[2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Solving the separable differential equation, we obtain

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t} &= k\sqrt{P} \\[12pt] 

    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}P}{\sqrt{P}} &= \int k \, \mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t \\[12pt]

    2\sqrt{P} &= k\hspace{0.05em}t+C \\[12pt]

    \sqrt{P} &= \dfrac{k\hspace{0.05em}t}{2} + A \hspace{4.85em}{(1)}\\[12pt]

    P &= \bigg(\dfrac{k\hspace{0.05em}t}{2}+A\bigg)^2 \hspace{3em}{(2)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Solving (1) for the initial value $P(0)=P_0$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A &= \sqrt{P_0} \hspace{8.2em}{(3)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting (3) in (2), we obtain

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &P(t) = \bigg(\dfrac{kt}{2}+\sqrt{P_0}\bigg)^2 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Solving for $k$, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    6\hspace{0.15em}000 &= \left(\dfrac{k\hspace{0.05em}(24)}{2}+\sqrt{3\hspace{0.15em}000}\right)^2 \\[12pt]

   &\hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{k \approx 1.89} \hspace{4em}[\text{by using G.D.C.}] 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Evaluating $P(t)$ at $t=30$, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    P(30) &= \left(\dfrac{(1.89)(30)}{2}+\sqrt{3\hspace{0.15em}000}\right)^2 \\[12pt]

   &\hspace{0.285em}\boxed{= 6\hspace{0.15em}909} \hspace{5em}[\text{by using G.D.C.}]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. Taking the square root of both sides and differentiating implicitly, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \sqrt{P_2} &= \dfrac{1.89\hspace{0.05em}t}{2}+\dfrac{30\sin(0.2\pi t)}{4\pi}+\sqrt{3\hspace{0.15em}000} \hspace{5em}{(4)}\\[12pt]

   \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t} \sqrt{P_2} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t} \left(\dfrac{1.89\hspace{0.05em}t}{2}+\dfrac{30\sin(0.2\pi t)}{4\pi}+\sqrt{3\hspace{0.15em}000}\right) \\[12pt]

   \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{P_2}}\dfrac{\mathrm{d} P_2}{\mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t} &= \dfrac{1.89}{2} + \dfrac{3\cos(0.2\pi t)}{2} \\[12pt]

   \dfrac{\mathrm{d}P_2}{\mathrm{d}\hspace{0.05em}t} &= \left(1.89+3\cos(0.2\pi t)\right) \sqrt{P_2} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$
