# AA606

**Maximum mark: 12**

Consider the differential equation
$x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+y=x^{p+1}$ where $x\in\mathbb{R}$,
$x\neq 0$ and $p$ is a positive integer, $p>0$.

1.  Solve the differential equation given that $y=1$ when $x=1$. Give
    your answer in the form $y=f(x)$.[8 marks]

2.
    1.  Show that the $x$-coordinate(s) of the points on the curve
        $y=f(x)$ where $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$ satisfy the
        equation $x^{p+2}=1$.

    2.  Deduce the set of values for $p$ such that there are two points
        on the curve $y=f(x)$ where
        $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$. Give a reason for your
        answer.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The differential equation is equivalent to
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+\dfrac{y}{x}=x^p \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the formula to find an integrating factor of (1).

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    I(x) &= e^{\footnotesize\raisebox{0.3em}{\mbox{$\int \frac{1}{x}\, \mathrm{d}x$}}}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    &= e^{\ln x}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    &= x\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Multiplying both sides
    of (1) by the integrating factor $I(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+y&=x^{p+1}\\\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{4mm}\
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}[xy] &= x^{p+1}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}[xy]\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int x^{p+1}\,\mathrm{d}x\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    xy &= \dfrac{1}{p+2}x^{p+2}+C\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    y &= \dfrac{1}{p+2}x^{p+1}+\dfrac{C}{x}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $x=1$ and $y=1$ in equation (2), we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1 &= \dfrac{1}{p+2}+C \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{4mm}\\
    C &= 1-\dfrac{1}{p+2}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{p+1}{p+2}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore
    the solution to the differential equation is

    $$
    y=\dfrac{1}{p+2}\left(x^{p+1}+\dfrac{p+1}{x}\right)
    $$

2.
    1.  The derivative of $y$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=\dfrac{1}{p+2}\left((p+1)x^{p}-\dfrac{p+1}{x^2}\right)\rule[-4mm]{0mm}{4mm}\\
        &= \dfrac{p+1}{p+2}\left(x^p-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\right)\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Setting the derivative equal to zero in (3), we have

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        0 &= \dfrac{p+1}{p+2}\left(x^p-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\right)\rule[-4mm]{0mm}{4mm}\\
        0&= x^p-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\rule[-4mm]{0mm}{9mm}\\
        x^p &= \dfrac{1}{x^2}\rule[-4mm]{0mm}{9mm}\\
        &\hspace{-7.25mm}x^{p+2} = 1\rule[0mm]{0mm}{5mm}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The equation (4) has two solutions if and only if $p+2$ is even
        and therefore (4) has two solutions only when $p$ is even.
