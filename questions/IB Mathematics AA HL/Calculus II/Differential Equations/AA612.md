# AA612

**Maximum mark: 9**

Consider the differential equation $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+\bigg(\dfrac{6x}{3x^2-2}\bigg)\,y=4x$, given that $y=4$ when $x=0$.

1.  Show that $3x^2-2$ is an integrating factor for this differential
    equation.[4 marks]

2.  Hence solve this differential equation. Give the answer in the form
    $y=f(x)$.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the formula to find an integrating factor
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    I(x) &= e^{\footnotesize\raisebox{0.5em}{\mbox{$\int\frac{6x}{3x^2-2}\, \mathrm{d}x$}}}\rule[-1mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    &= e^{\ln(3x^2-2)}\rule[-1mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    &= 3x^2-2\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $3x^2-2$
    is an integrating factor.

2.  Multiplying both sides of the differential equation by the
    integrating factor, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \left(3x^2-2\right)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+6xy &= 12x^3-8x \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[\left(3x^2-2\right)y\right] &= 12x^3-8x \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[\left(3x^2-2\right)y\right] \mathrm{d}x &= \int (12x^3-8x) \mathrm{d}x \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \left(3x^2-2\right)y &= 3x^4-4x^2+C\rule[-2.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $x=0$ and $y=4$ in (1), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (3(0)^2 -2)(4) &= 3(0)^4-4(0)^2+C \\[7pt]
    C &= -8
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, the solution is

    $$
    \hspace{2.7em}y = \dfrac{3x^4-4x^2-8}{3x^2-2}
    $$
