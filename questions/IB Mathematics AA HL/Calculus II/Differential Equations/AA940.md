# AA940

**Maximum mark: 20**

A video streaming service company are monitoring their market share in a region in which they have recently commenced operations.

The number of households, $N$, they predict will subscribe to the streaming service can be modelled by the logistic differential equation

$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}=\dfrac{3kN(L-N)}{2L}$

where $t$ is time measured in years and $k$, $L$ are positive constants.

The constant $L$ represents the total number of households in the region who could possibly subscribe to the streaming service.

1. Show that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2}=\bigg(\dfrac{3k}{2L}\bigg)^2(N)(L-N)(L-2N)$. [4 marks]

2. Hence show that the number of households subscribing to the streaming service is predicted to increase at its maximum rate when $N=\dfrac{L}{2}$. [5 marks]

3. Hence determine the maximum value of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}$ in terms of $k$ and $L$. [2 marks]

Let $N_0$ be the number of households who have subscribed to the streaming service at the time the company start monitoring their market share. 

4. By solving the logistic differential equation, show that its solution can be expressed in the form

    $kt=\bigg(\dfrac{2}{3}\bigg)\mathrm{ln}\Bigg(\dfrac{N(L-N_0)}{N_0(L-N)}\Bigg)$

    [7 marks]

After 12 years, the number of subscribed households is predicted to be $4N_0$. It is known that $L=5N_0$.

5. Find the value of $k$ for this model. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the product rule, we can differentiate $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}$ implicitly.

    Let $u = \dfrac{3kN}{2L}$ and $v = L - N$, so that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} = uv$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}{t}} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}N} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{3k}{2L}\times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} \\[10pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}{t}} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}N} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} \\[10pt]

    &= -1\times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting into the product rule formula, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2} &= u \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}{t}} + v \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}{t}} \\[10pt]

    &= \bigg(\dfrac{3kN}{2L}\bigg)\bigg(-1\times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg) + (L - N)\bigg(\dfrac{3k}{2L}\times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg) \\[10pt]

    &= \bigg(\dfrac{3k}{2L}\times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)(L-2N)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can also substitute in $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}=\dfrac{3kN(L-N)}{2L}$, giving

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2} &= \bigg(\dfrac{3k}{2L}\times \dfrac{3kN(L-N)}{2L}\bigg)(L-2N) \\[10pt]

    &  \hspace{-2.1em}\boxed{\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = \bigg(\dfrac{3k}{2L}\bigg)^2(N)(L-N)(L-2N)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. The maximum value for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}$ will occur when $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = 0$ and changes sign from positive to negative. There are three values of $N$ for which $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = 0$:

    $\bullet$ When $N=0$ $\\[10pt]$

    $\bullet$ When $L - N = 0 \hspace{0.3em}\implies N = L$ $\\[10pt]$

    $\bullet$ When $L - 2N = 0 \hspace{0.3em}\implies N = \dfrac{L}{2}$ $\\[10pt]$

    The following table shows how the sign of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$ changes between these values:

    ![AA940a](images/AA940a.PNG)

    When $N=\dfrac{L}{2}$, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2N}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = 0$ and changes sign from positive to negative.

    Therefore $\text{the maximum value for }\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}\text{ occurs at }N=\dfrac{L}{2}$

3. When $N=\dfrac{L}{2}$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{3kN(L-N)}{2L} \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{3k\bigg(\dfrac{L}{2}\bigg)\bigg(L-\dfrac{L}{2}\bigg)}{2L} \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{3k\bigg(\dfrac{L}{2}\bigg)\bigg(\dfrac{L}{2}\bigg)}{2L} \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{3kL^2}{8L} \\[10pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{3kL}{8}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The maximum value of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}t}$ is $\dfrac{3kL}{8}$

4. By separating the variables, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{1}{N(L-N)}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}N &= \dfrac{3k}{2L}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}t

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    And therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \int \dfrac{1}{N(L-N)}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}N &= \int \dfrac{3k}{2L}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}t

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The left hand side can be expressed in partial fraction form:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{1}{N(L-N)} &\equiv \dfrac{A}{N} + \dfrac{B}{L-N} \\[10pt]

    1 &\equiv A(L-N) + BN

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting $N=0$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    1 &= AL \\[10pt]

    A &= \dfrac{1}{L}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting $N=L$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    1 &= A(L-L) + BL \\[10pt]

    1 &= BL \\[10pt]

    B &= \dfrac{1}{L}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{1}{N(L-N)} &= \dfrac{1}{L}\bigg(\dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{L-N}\bigg)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{1}{L}\hspace{0.15em}\int \dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{L-N}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}N &= \int \dfrac{3k}{2L}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}t \\[10pt]

    \int \dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{L-N}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}N &= \int \dfrac{3k}{2}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{d}t

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Integrating both sides of the equation, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{ln}|N| - \mathrm{ln}|L-N| &= \dfrac{3kt}{2} + c \\[10pt]

    \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N}{L-N}\bigg) &= \dfrac{3kt}{2} + c \hspace{3em} [\text{since }N>0 \text{ and }L>N]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Given that $N=N_0$ when $t=0$, we can find the value of c:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N_0}{L-N_0}\bigg) &= c

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N}{L-N}\bigg) &= \dfrac{3kt}{2} + \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N_0}{L-N_0}\bigg) \\[10pt]

    \dfrac{3kt}{2} &= \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N}{L-N}\bigg) - \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N_0}{L-N_0}\bigg) \\[10pt]

    &= \mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N(L-N_0)}{N_0(L-N)}\bigg) \\[10pt]

    &  \hspace{-0.9em}\boxed{kt = \dfrac{2}{3}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{N(L-N_0)}{N_0(L-N)}\bigg)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. Substituting $t=12$, $N=4N_0$ and $L=5N_0$ into the equation from part (d), we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    12k &= \dfrac{2}{3}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{4N_0(5N_0-N_0)}{N_0(5N_0-4N_0)}\bigg) \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{2}{3}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ln}\bigg(\dfrac{4(4N_0)}{(N_0)}\bigg) \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{2}{3}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ln}\hspace{0.15em}16 \\[10pt]

    k &= \dfrac{1}{18}\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ln}\hspace{0.15em}16 \\[10pt]

    &  \hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{k \approx 0.154}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
