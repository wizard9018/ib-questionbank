# AA904

**Maximum mark: 21**

The acceleration, $a\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ms}^{-2}$ of a particle
moving in a vertical trajectory at time $t$ seconds, $t\geq 0$, is given
by $a(t)=-(3+v)$ where $v$ is the particle's velocity in ms$^{-1}$.

At $t=0$, the particle is at a fixed origin O and has an initial
velocity of $v_0\hspace{0.15em}\text{ms}^{-1}$.

1.  By solving an appropriate differential equation, show that the
    particle's\
    velocity is given by $v(t)=(v_0+3)e^{-t}-3$.[6 marks]

The particle initially moves upwards until it reaches its maximum height
from O, and then returns to O.

Let $s$ metres represent the particle's displacement from O, and
$s_{\text{max}}$ the maximum displacement from
O.

2.
    1.  Show that the time $T$ taken for the particle to reach
        $s_\text{max}$ satisfies the equation
        $e^{-T} = \dfrac{3\rule[-0mm]{0pt}{4.5mm}}{v_0+3}.$
    2.  Hence, solve for $T$ in terms of $v_0$.

    3.  By solving an appropriate differential equation and using the
        results from **part (b) (i)** and **(ii)**, find an expression
        for $s_{\text{max}}$ in terms of $v_0$.[8 marks]

Let $v(T-k)$ represent the particle's velocity $k$ seconds before it
reaches $s_{\text{max}}$, where

$$
v(T-k) = (v_0+3)e^{-(T-k)}-3\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}
$$

3.  By using the result from **part (b) (i)**, show that
    $v(T-k) = 3e^k-3$.[2 marks]

Similarly, let $v(T+k)$ represent the particle's velocity $k$ seconds
after it reaches $s_{\text{max}}$.

4.  Deduce a similar expression for $v(T+k)$ in terms of $k$.
    [2 marks]

5.  Hence, show that $v(T-k)+v(T+k)\geq 0$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Solving the separable differential equation for $v(t)$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    a &= -(3+v) \\[12pt]
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} &= -(3+v) \\[12pt]
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{3+v} &= - \int \mathrm{d}t \\[12pt]
    \ln(3+v) &= -t + C\\[12pt]
    3 + v &= Ae^{-t} \\[12pt]
    v &= Ae^{-t}-3\\[8pt]\\
    \intertext{Using the initial value $v(0)=v_0$, we get}\\
    v_0 &= A-3 \\[12pt]
    A &= v_0+3\\[8pt]\\
    \intertext{Hence, the particle's velocity is}\\
    &\hspace{-1.87mm}v(t) = \left(v_0+3\right)e^{-t}-3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  The time $T$ satisfies $v(T)=0$, hence, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        (v_0+3)e^{-T} - 3 &= 0 \\[13pt]
        (v_0+3)e^{-T} &= 3 \\[10pt]
        &\hspace{-6mm}e^{-T} = \dfrac{3}{v_0+3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Solving equation (1) for $T$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        e^{-T} &= \dfrac{3}{v_0+3} \\[12pt]
        e^T &= \dfrac{v_0+3}{3} \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-2.6mm} T = \ln\left(\dfrac{v_0+3}{3}\right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Solving the differential equation for $s(t)$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        v &= (v_0+3)e^{-t} - 3 \\[12pt]
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= (v_0+3)e^{-t} - 3 \\[12pt]
        s &= \int \big[(v_0+3)e^{-t} - 3\big]\,\mathrm{d}t \\[12pt]
        s &= -(v_0+3)e^{-t}-3t + C
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Using the initial condition $s(0)=0$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        s(0) &= -(v_0+3) + C \\[12pt]
        C &= v_0+3
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Substituting the value of $C$ in (2), we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        s &= -(v_0+3)e^{-t}-3t + v_0+3
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Evaluating at $T$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        s_{\text{max}} &= -(v_0+3)e^{-T}-3T+v_0+3 \\[12pt]
        &= -3-3\ln\left(\dfrac{v_0+3}{3}\right)+v_0+3\hspace{2em}\big[\text{by (b) (i) and (ii)}\big] \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-6.69mm} s_{\text{max}} =v_0-3\ln\left(\dfrac{v_0+3}{3}\right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Using part (b) (i), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    v(T-k) &= (v_0+3)e^{-T}e^k-3\\[12pt]
    &= (v_0+3)\left(\dfrac{3}{v_0+3}\right)e^k-3 \\[12pt]
    &= 3e^{k}-3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using part (b) (i), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    v(T+k) &=(v_0+3)e^{-(T+k)}-3\\[12pt]
    &=(v_0+3)e^{-T}e^{-k}-3\\[12pt]
    &= (v_0+3)\left(\dfrac{3}{v_0+3}\right)e^{-k}-3 \\[12pt]
    &= 3e^{-k}-3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Using parts (c) and (d), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    v(T-k) + v(T+k)  &= 3e^{k}-3 + 3e^{-k}-3 \\[12pt]
    &= 3e^{k}-6 + 3e^{-k} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{3e^{2k}-6e^{k} + 3}{e^{k}} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{3(e^{2k}-2e^{k} + 1)}{e^{k}} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{3(e^{k}-1)^2}{e^{k}} \geq 0 \hspace{3em} \big[\text{since }e^{k}> 0\big]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
