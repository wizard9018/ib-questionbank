# AA1383

**Maximum mark: 6**

Solve the differential equation

$$

\begin{aligned}

    (1+x^2)\hspace{0.15em}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=1+y^2

\end{aligned}

$$

for $y$, which satisfies the initial condition $y(0)=\sqrt{3}$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Rewriting the differential equation and integrating with respect to $x$ and $y$, we have 

$$

\begin{aligned}

\textcolor{white}{placeholder}\tag*{{\textbf{(M1)}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{1+y^2} &=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{1+x^2}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\[8pt]

    \int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{1+y^2} &=\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{1+x^2}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\[4pt]

    \arctan y &= \arctan x+C \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm} \tag*{\textbf{{A1A1}}}\\

    y &= \tan(\arctan x + C)\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm} \hspace{1em}[1]

\end{aligned}

$$

Substituting $x=0$ and $y=\sqrt{3}$ in [1], we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

\textcolor{white}{placeholder}\tag*{{\textbf{(M1)}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \sqrt{3} &= \tan(\arctan{0}+C) \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{4mm}\\

    \sqrt{3} &= \tan (C)\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\

    C &= \arctan{\left(\sqrt{3}\right)}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\

    C &= \frac{\pi}{3}\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore, the solution is 

$$

\begin{aligned}

    y=\tan\left(\arctan x+\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

\end{aligned}

$$
