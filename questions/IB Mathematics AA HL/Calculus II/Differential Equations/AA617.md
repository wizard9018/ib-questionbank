# AA617

**Maximum mark: 14**

The curves $y=f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$ both pass through the point $(1,0)$ and
are defined by the differential equations
$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2x-y^2$ and
$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=3y-\dfrac{x}{2}$
respectively.

1.  Show that the tangent to the curve $y=f(x)$ at the point $(1,0)$ is
    normal to the curve $y=g(x)$ at the point $(1,0)$.[2 marks]

2.  Find $g(x)$.[7 marks]

3.  Use Euler's method with steps of $0.2$ to estimate $f(2)$ to 5
    decimal places.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The gradient of $f$ at $(1,0)$ is $2(1)-(0)^2=2$ whereas the
    gradient of $g$ at $(1,0)$ is
    $3(0)-\dfrac{1}{2}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{5mm}$. Therefore,
    as one gradient is the negative reciprocal of the other, we know
    that $f(x)$ is normal to $g(x)$ at the point $(1,0)$.

2.  An integrating factor for
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-3y=-\dfrac{x}{2}$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    I(x) &= e^{\footnotesize\mbox{$\int -3\, \mathrm{d}x$}}\\[7pt]
    &= e^{-3x}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Multiplying both sides by the integrating factor, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    e^{-3x}\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-3y\right)&= e^{-3x}\left(-\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\\[2pt]
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[ye^{-3x}\right] &= -\dfrac{xe^{-3x}}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Integrating both sides of the equation (1) with respect to $x$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[ye^{-3x}\right] \mathrm{d}x &= \int -\dfrac{xe^{-3x}}{2}\, \mathrm{d}x\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    ye^{-3x} &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\int xe^{-3x}\, \mathrm{d}x \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    &=-\dfrac{1}{2}\left[-\dfrac{1}{3}xe^{-3x}+\dfrac{1}{3}\int e^{-3x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    &=\dfrac{1}{6}xe^{-3x}-\dfrac{1}{6}\int e^{-3x}\,\mathrm{d}x\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    &=\dfrac{1}{6}x e^{-3x}+\dfrac{1}{18}e^{-3x}+C\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    &= \dfrac{1}{18} e^{-3x}(3x+1)+C\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $x=1$ and $y=0$ in (2), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    0 &= \dfrac{1}{18} e^{-3}(4)+C \\[3pt]
    C &= -\dfrac{4}{18} e^{-3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore an
    expression for $g(x)$ is

    $$
    \hspace{11.5mm}y=\dfrac{e^{-3x}(3x+1)-4e^{-3}}{18e^{-3x}}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 2x-y^2 \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    y(1) &= 0 \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    h &= 0.2\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7em} x\text{-iteration:}\hspace{0.75em} x_{n+1} &= x_n + 0.2 \\[2pt]
    y\text{-iteration:}\hspace{0.75em} y_{n+1} &= y_n +0.2\times\big[2x_n-y_n^2\big]\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using Euler's method, we obtain

    ![68cb167d79a9e181f6de8c131e519dc8f409146a.svg](images/68cb167d79a9e181f6de8c131e519dc8f409146a.svg)

    Hence we find
     $y(2)\approx 1.80239$
