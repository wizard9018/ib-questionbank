# AA1655

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the differential equation $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{2y}{1 + x^2}$, where $y=1$ when $x=0$.

Solve the differential equation, giving your answer in the form $y=f(x)$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

In this differential equation, we can separate the variables. Hence, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y &= \dfrac{2}{1 + x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Next we need to integrate both sides. Notice that we can use the standard integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x = \arctan x$ on the R.H.S. giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \int \dfrac{1}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y &= \int \dfrac{2}{1 + x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[10pt]
    \ln|y| &= 2\arctan x + C \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $y = 1$, we may drop the modulus, and then raise $e$ to the power of both sides giving
$$
\begin{aligned}
    e^{\ln y} &= e^{(2\arctan x + C)}\\[6pt]
    y &= e^{(2\arctan x + C)}
\end{aligned}
$$
To find the value of $C$ we can apply the initial condition $y = 1$ when $x = 0$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    1&=e^{(2\arctan0+C)}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
    1 &= e^{2(0) + C} \\[6pt]
    1 &= e^{C} \\[6pt]
    C &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, writing the result in the required form,
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \boxed{y=e^{2\arctan x}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
