# AA1256

**Maximum mark: 8**

A ball is dropped from a certain height and falls vertically. The velocity $v$ of the ball at time $t$ is such that the rate of change of velocity is proportional to the difference between the terminal velocity $v_T$ and the current velocity $v$. This is modelled by the differential equation $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = k(v_T - v)$, where $k$ is a positive constant. The ball is released from rest, so $v = 0$ when $t = 0$.

1.  Solve the differential equation to find an expression for $v$ in terms of $v_T$, $k$ and $t$.[4 marks]

2.  Find the time taken for the velocity to reach $\dfrac{3}{4}v_T$.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Treating this as a first-order differential equation, separating out the variables, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} &= k(v_T - v)\\[4pt]
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{v_T - v} &= k\,\mathrm{d}t \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Integrating both sides using the **standard integral (5.10)** $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{1}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x = \ln|x| + C$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -\ln|v_T - v| &= kt + C \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting the initial condition $v = 0$ when $t = 0$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -\ln|v_T - 0| &= k(0) + C \\[6pt]
        C &= -\ln v_T
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Rearranging, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \ln\left(\dfrac{v_T}{v_T - v}\right) &= kt \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Exponentiating both sides and isolating $v$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{v_T}{v_T - v} &= \mathrm{e}^{kt} \\[10pt]
        \dfrac{v_T - v}{v_T} &= \mathrm{e}^{-kt} \\[6pt]
        v &= \boxed{v_T\left(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-kt}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Setting $v = \dfrac{3}{4}v_T$ in the result from part (a), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{3}{4}v_T &= v_T\left(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-kt}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Dividing through by $v_T$ and rearranging, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{e}^{-kt} &= \dfrac{1}{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Taking natural logarithms of both sides, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \ln\left(\mathrm{e}^{-kt}\right) &= \ln\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Applying the **laws of logarithms (1.7)**, $\log_a x^m = m\log_a x$, to bring the power to the front, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -kt\ln(\mathrm{e}) &= \ln\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right) \\[6pt]
        -kt &= \ln\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Dividing both sides by $-k$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        t &= -\dfrac{1}{k}\ln\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right) \\[10pt]
        t &= \boxed{\dfrac{\ln 4}{k}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
