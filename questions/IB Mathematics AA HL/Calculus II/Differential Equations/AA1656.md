# AA1656

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider the differential equation $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 3x^2 y^3$.

Given that $y = 2$ when $x = 1$, solve the differential equation, giving your answer in the form $y = f(x)$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let's start by separating the variables and then integrate both sides.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{1}{y^3}\,\mathrm{d}y&=3x^2\,\mathrm{d}x\\[10pt]
    \int y^{-3}\,\mathrm{d}y &= \int 3x^2\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[10pt]
    -\dfrac{1}{2y^2} &= x^3 + C \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Then we can apply the initial condition $y = 2$ when $x = 1$ to solve for $C$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac{1}{2(2)^2} &= 1 + C \tag*{(\textbf{(M1)})} \\[6pt]
    C &= -\dfrac{9}{8} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac{1}{2y^2} &= x^3 - \frac{9}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$
To give the equation in the required form we need to isolate $y$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{y^2} &= \dfrac{9}{4} - 2x^3 \\[10pt]
    y^2&=\frac{4}{9-8x^3}
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the positive root, since $y(1) = 2 > 0$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \boxed{y=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{9 - 8x^3}}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
