# AA607

**Maximum mark: 10**

Consider the differential equation
$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+\dfrac{2x^3}{1+x^4}y=4x^3$ where $y=1$
when $x=0$.

1.  Show that $\sqrt{1+x^4}$ is an integrating factor for this
    differential equation.[4 marks]

2.  Solve the differential equation giving your answer in the form
    $y=f(x)$.[6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the formula to find an integrating factor, we get

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     I(x)&=e^{\raisebox{0.35em}{\mbox{$\int$}}\hspace{-0.1em}\footnotesize\raisebox{0.42em}{\mbox{$ \frac{2x^3\rule[-0.5mm]{0pt}{2.25mm}}{1+x^4\rule[-0.5mm]{0pt}{2.3mm}}\, \mathrm{d}x$}}}\\
     &= e^{\raisebox{0.1em}{\footnotesize\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+x^4\right)$}}}\\
     &= e^{\raisebox{0.1em}{\footnotesize\mbox{$\ln\left(1+x^4\right)^{1/2}$}}}\\
     &\hspace{0.3em}= \sqrt{1+x^4}
     \end{aligned}
     $$

2.  Multiplying both sides of the differential equation by the
    integrating factor, $I(x)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sqrt{1+x^4}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+\dfrac{2x^3y\sqrt{1+x^4}}{1+x^4}&=4x^3\sqrt{1+x^4}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{3.5mm}\\
    \sqrt{1+x^4}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+\dfrac{2x^3y}{\sqrt{1+x^4}}&=4x^3\sqrt{1+x^4}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[y\sqrt{1+x^4}\right]&=4x^3\sqrt{1+x^4}\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[y\sqrt{1+x^4}\right]\, \mathrm{d}x &=\int 4x^3\sqrt{1+x^4}\,\mathrm{d}x\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    y\sqrt{1+x^4} &= \dfrac{2}{3}\left(1+x^4\right)^{3/2}+C\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    y &= \dfrac{2}{3}\left(1+x^4\right)+\dfrac{C}{\sqrt{1+x^4}}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{6mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $x=0$ and $y=1$ in equation (1), we have as expression
    for $y$

    $$
    y=\dfrac{2}{3}\left(1+x^4\right)+\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{1+x^4}}
    $$
