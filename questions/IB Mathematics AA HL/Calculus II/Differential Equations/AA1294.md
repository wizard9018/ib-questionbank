# AA1294

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the differential equation
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d^2}y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}&=-10xe^{-x^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $y=-1$ and $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2$ when $x=0$.
1.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ in terms of $x$.[2 marks]

2.  Use Euler's method with a step length of $0.1$ to estimate a value for $y$ when $x=0.5$.[4 marks]

3.  Hence justify whether your answer to part (b) is an overestimate or an underestimate. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We can find the first derivative by integrating the second derivative.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int{\frac{\mathrm{d^2}y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}}&=\int{-10xe^{-x^2}}\mathrm{d}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using integration by substitution
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Let }u&=-x^2\\[6pt]
    \text{Therefore }\mathrm{d}u&=-2x\mathrm{d}x\\[6pt]
    \text{and }\mathrm{d}x&=-\frac{1}{2x}\mathrm{d}u
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Replacing  $\mathrm{d}x$ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int{\frac{\mathrm{d^2}y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}}&=\int{-10xe^{u}\times -\frac{1}{2x}}\,\mathrm{d}u\\[6pt]
    &=\int{5e^{u}\mathrm{d}u}\\[6pt]
    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=5e^u+c\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    To find the value of $c$, we can use the given information of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2$ when $x=0$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2&=5e^0+c\\[6pt]
    2&=5+c\\[6pt]
    c&=-3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=5e^{-x^2}-3\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We need to decide what sign the second derivative takes near to our estimated value.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}

    \end{aligned}
    $$
