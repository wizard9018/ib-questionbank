# AA666

**Maximum mark: 14**

Consider the differential equation
$\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} -(\tan x)\hspace{0.05em}y = 1$, where
$x \neq \dfrac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}$, for any integer $n$.

1.  Given that $y(0) = 1$, use Euler's method with step length $h = 0.2$
    to find an approximation for $y(1)$. Give your answer correct to two
    decimal places. [6 marks]

2.  Solve the equation
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} -(\tan x)\hspace{0.05em}y = 1$.
    Give your answer in the form $y=f(x)$.
    [6 marks]

3.  Find the percentage error when $y(1)$ is approximated by the final
    rounded value found in part (a). Give your answer correct to two
    significant figures. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 1 + (\tan x)y \hspace{3em} \bigg[x \neq \dfrac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}\bigg] \hspace{1.6em} \\[6pt]
    \hspace{6.25em} y(0) &= 1 \\[6pt]
    h &= 0.2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x\text{-iteration:}\hspace{0.75em} x_{n+1} &= x_n + 0.2 \\[2pt]
    y\text{-iteration:}\hspace{0.75em} y_{n+1} &= y_n +0.2\times\bigg[1 + (\tan x_n)y_n \bigg] \hspace{2em} [\text{for $n \in \mathbb{N}$}] \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using Euler's method, we obtain

    ![ba982c50d6f744daecad4d742b810439a420b395.svg](images/ba982c50d6f744daecad4d742b810439a420b395.svg)

    Hence we find
     $y(1) \approx 2.87$

2.  An integrating factor is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    I(x) &= e^{\footnotesize\raisebox{0.2em}{\mbox{$\int-\tan x\,\mathrm{d}x$}}} \\[7pt]
    &= e^{\ln (\cos x)} \\[5pt]
    &= \cos x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, multiplying both sides of the
    differential equation by $\cos x$, we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos x\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} - (\cos x)(\tan x)y &= \cos x \\[6pt]
    \cos x\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} + (-\sin x)y &= \cos x \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[(\cos x)y\bigg] &= \cos x
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Integrating both sides of the equation $(1)$ with respect to $x$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[(\cos x)y\bigg]\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{d}x &= \int\cos x\,\mathrm{d}x \\[6pt]
    (\cos x)y &= \sin x + C  \\[8pt]
    y &= \tan x + C\sec x\hspace{0.05em} \hspace{1.5em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    the last equation is possible since $x\neq \dfrac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}$.

    Substituting $x = 0$ and $y = 1$ in the equation $(2)$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.4em} 1 &= \tan (0)+C\sec(0) \\[6pt]
    C &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.15em} y = \tan x +\sec x
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Evaluating $y(x)$ for $x = 1$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y(1) &= \tan(1)+\sec(1) \\[9pt]
    &= 3.408\dots \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the percentage error
    formula
    $\epsilon = \bigg|\dfrac{v_\mathrm{A} - v_\mathrm{E}}{v_\mathrm{E}}\bigg|\times 100\hspace{0.05em}\%$
    with $v_\mathrm{E} = (3.408\dots)$ and $v_\mathrm{A} = 2.87$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \epsilon &= \bigg|\dfrac{2.87 - (3.408\dots)}{(3.408\dots)}\bigg|\times 100\hspace{0.05em}\% \\[9pt]
    &\approx 16\hspace{0.05em}\%
    \end{aligned}
    $$
