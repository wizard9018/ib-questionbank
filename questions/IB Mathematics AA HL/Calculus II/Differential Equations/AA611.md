# AA611

**Maximum mark: 13**

1.  Consider the differential equation
    $$
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = f\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right), \quad x>0.

    $$
    Use the substitution $v=\dfrac{y}{x}$ to show that
    the general solution of this\
    differential equation is

    $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{f(v)-v} = \ln x + C.$[4 marks]

2.  Hence, or otherwise, solve the differential
    equation

    $$
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{4x^2+5xy+y^2}{x^2}, \quad x>0,

    $$
    given that $y=2$ when $x=1$. Give your answer in
    the form $y=g(x)$.[9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Introducing the new variable $v=y/x$ or $y=vx$ and taking the
    derivative with respect to $x$ using the product rule, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}  (vx) \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    &= v +x \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Then, the differential equation becomes
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= f(v) \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    v+x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} &= f(v) \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{f(v)-v}&= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{x} \rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\[4pt]
    \int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{f(v)-v}&= \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{9mm} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-18mm}\int\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{f(v)-v}= \ln x+c \rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  First we rearrange the differential equation
    $$
    \hspace{14mm}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 4+5\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)+\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)^2
    $$

    Then, $f(v)=4+5v+v^2$ and using the general solution from (a)

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{4+5v+v^2-v} &= \ln x+C \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{(2+v)^2} &= \ln x+C \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    -\dfrac{1}{2+v} &= \ln x+C \rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using that $y=2$ when $x=1$ and therefore $v=2$, we get

    $$
    C=-\dfrac{1}{4}
    $$
    Substituting the variable $v$ we obtain an expression for $y$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ln x-\dfrac{1}{4} &= -\dfrac{1}{2+v}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    2+v &= \dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{4}-\ln x}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{10mm} \\
    2+\dfrac{y}{x} &= \dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{4}-\ln x}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\[4pt]
    y &= x \left(\dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{4}-\ln x}-2\right)\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-2mm}y = x \left(\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}+2\ln x}{\frac{1}{4}-\ln x}\right)\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
