# AA995

**Maximum mark: 16**

A particle moves in a straight line.
Its displacement, $s$ centimetres, from the origin at time $t$ seconds is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
    s(t)=(3t-\frac{1}{2}t^3)\sin^2(2t) \text{ for } 0 \leq t \leq \pi
\end{aligned}
$$

As shown on below.

![q1](images/q1.png)

1. What is the furthest distance the particle is from the origin? [2 marks]

2. For how many seconds is the particle further than $1\,$cm from the origin? [3 marks]

3. For what interval(s) of $t$ is the particle moving away from the origin? [3 marks]

4. Find the particle's velocity when $t=1$. [1 mark]

5. What is the maximum speed of the particle? [3 marks]

The particle arrives back to the origin for the first time at point $A$.

![AA995a2](images/AA995a2.JPEG)

It takes the particle $a$ seconds to travel a further distance of $2.5\,$cm.

6. Find $a$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The point shown below is the furthest from the origin. Using the G.D.C. we can find the $y$-coordinate.

    ![a1](images/a1.png)

    The furthest the particle gets from the origin is $2.22\,\text{cm}$

2. The three regions shown below is the total time when the particle is over $1\,$cm from the origin.

    ![a2](images/a2.png)

    Use your G.D.C. to find intersection points and sum the three times
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \text{T} &= (1.248...-0.496...)+(2.2522...-1.945...)+(2.8151...-2.676...)\\[6pt]
        &=1.1985...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The particle is further than $1$cm from the origin for $1.20\,\text{seconds}$.

3. The following graph identifies the regions in which the particle is moving away from the origin.

    ![a3](images/a3.png)

    By using a G.D.C. the three regions can be found.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \boxed{0<x<0.883 \text{ and }1.57<x<2.10 \text{ and }2.45<x<2.75}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4. Recall $v(t)=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}$, use G.D.C. to plot the function $v(t)$.

    ![a4](images/a4.png)

    Use your G.D.C. to resolve $v(1)=-2.54\,\text{cm}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

5. Recall the formula $\text{speed}=|v(t)|$.

    ![a5](images/a5.png)

    The highest speed occurs at the point identified on the diagram above. The coordinates are $(2.44, 5.80)$.

    Therefore the maximum speed of the particle is $5.80\,\text{cm}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

6. The formula for the distance travelled from time $t_1$ to $t_2$ is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        D=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|v(t)|dt\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $t_1$ is the $x$-coordinate of $A$.

    Using a G.D.C. the coordinates of $A$ can be found to be $(1.57..., 0)$.

    This information is shown below on the graph of $y=|v(t)|$. The area under this graph is the distance travelled **from** point $A$.

    ![Screenshot 2023-09-16 at 8.22.28 AM](<images/Screenshot 2023-09-16 at 8.22.28 AM.png>)

    To find $t_2$ solve the following equation. Note, the function $|v(t)|$ is found using the G.D.C.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2.5&=\int_{1.57...}^{t_2}|v(t)|dt\\[6pt]
            t_2&=2.4431.... &\text{[By using G.D.C.]}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    To find $a$ we must calculate the time **after** point $A$. Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        a&=2.4431....-1.57...\\[3pt]
        a&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{0.872\,\text{s}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
