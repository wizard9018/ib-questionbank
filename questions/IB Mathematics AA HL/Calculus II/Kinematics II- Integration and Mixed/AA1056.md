# AA1056

**Maximum mark: 6**

The equation of the movement of a bouncing ball is given by the equation
$$
h(t) = \left\lvert 3e^{-t}\cos\left(\pi t + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) \right\rvert.
$$
The graph of $h$ is shown on the diagram below.

![Screenshot 2024-03-06 at 8.03.02 PM](<images/Screenshot 2024-03-06 at 8.03.02 PM.png>)

1. Find the first two times $t_1$ and $t_2$ at which the ball hits the ground. [2 marks]

2. Determine an expression for $h(t)$ **without** modulus signs for $t_1<t<t_2$.[2 marks]

Let $t_M$ be the time at which the maximum between $t_1$ and $t_2$ occurs.

3. Using the G.D.C., find the value of $t_M$.[2 marks]

2. Let's remove the modulus signs from $h(t)$ and call this $g(t)$. Therefore

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g(t) &= 3e^{-t}\cos\left(\pi t + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    On the G.D.C. we can compare $h(t)$ with our new function $g(t)$ for $t_1 < t < t_2$. We have

    ![1056-2](images/1056-2.png)

    The parts of the graph in red and blue show where $g(t)$ and $h(t)$ are different.

    The parts in green show where the graphs are the same.

    Between $t_1$ and $t_2$, $h(t)$ is a reflection of $g(t)$ in the horizontal axis. This is the effect of the modulus signs.

    That is, for $t_1 < t < t_2$, $h(t) = -g(t)$.

    $$\\[-2.3em]$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(R1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    \end{aligned}
    $$
