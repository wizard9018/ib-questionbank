# AA1166

**Maximum mark: 5**

A particle is travelling along the $x-$axis.

Its position in centimetres relative to the origin after $t$ seconds is given by the function $s(t)=2t^4-3t^3-t+1$. Where $t>0$.

The particle changes direction at $k\,$seconds.

1.  Find $k$. [2 marks]

At $t=1\,$s an observer begins measuring the distance travelled by the particle.

They stop observing after $b\,$seconds when the particle has travelled a further $3\,$cm, where $b>1$.

2.  Find $b$.[3 marks]

2.  Using the formula for the distance travelled we know
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        D&=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|v(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The initial time is $t_1=1\,$s, $D=3$ and $t_2=b$. Hence we need to solve the following equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        3&=\int_{1}^{b}|s'(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t  \tag*{\textbf{A1M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can also show this graphically

    Solving for $b$ on the G.D.C. gives $\hspace{0.525em}b=1.72\,s$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Note: When solving on the G.D.C. you may need to specify that $b>1$.
