# AA1287

**Maximum mark: 6**

A particle $P$ moves along a straight line such that its displacement, in metres, after $t$ seconds, from a fixed point $O$ is given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

    s(t)&=3e^{-\left(t+1\right)}\sin{(4t+4)},\hspace{1em} 0\leq t \leq 2

\end{aligned}

$$

1.  Sketch the graph of the velocity of $P$ against $t$ on the axes below.[2 marks]

![AA1287](images/AA1287.JPEG)

$P$ reaches its maximum speed when $t=a\,$seconds.

2.  Find $a$.[1 mark]

3.  Hence or otherwise, find the distance travelled whilst the acceleration of $P$ is negative.[3 marks]

2.  Recall speed$=|v(t)|$.

    The maximum speed occurs when $|v(t)|$ is at a maximum.

    In this case, the maximum speed is also the maximum positive value of $v(t)$, which we find using a calculator to be $(0.4483..., 2.819...)$. 

    Hence, the maximum speed is $2.82\text{ m\,s}^{-1}$ and this occurs at $t=0.448\,\text{s}$

3.  Recall

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        a&=\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore, the acceleration of $P$ is negative when the gradient of the velocity function, $v(t)$, is negative.

    From part (b) we know the local maximum occurs when $t=0.448$ seconds. 

    Using technology we find the local minimum point of the velocity function to be $(1.233...,-1.285...)$. Hence, the minimum occurs when $t=1.23$ seconds.
