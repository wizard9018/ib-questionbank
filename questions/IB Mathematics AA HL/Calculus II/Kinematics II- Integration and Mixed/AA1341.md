# AA1341

**Maximum mark: 7**

A particle moves in a straight line about a fixed point $O$.  The particle's velocity, $v$ m\,s$^{-1}$, after $t$ seconds is given by $v(t)=\left(\frac{1}{2}t\right)^3+\sin(3t)$ for $0\leq t \leq 3$.

1.    Given that the initial displacement is $-\frac{1}{6}$ metres find an expression for the displacement, $s$ m, of the particle at time $t$. [4 marks]

2.    Hence determine the total time that the particle is moving toward the fixed point $O$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. To find an expression for the displacement $s(t)$ we need to integrate the velocity function.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    s(t)&=\int\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}t\right)^3+\sin(3t)\right]\mathrm{d}t\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Let's rewrite the integral and then use the inverse power rule and the integral of sine to integrate.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    s(t)&=\int\frac{1}{8}t^3\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\sin(3t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\[6pt]

    &=\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)t^4-\frac{1}{3}\cos(3t)+C\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We know that the initial displacement is $-\frac{1}{6}$ metres. This means that when $t=0$ then $s(t)=-\frac{1}{6}$.

    We can use this information to solve for $C$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    -\frac{1}{6}&=\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{1}{4}(0)^4\right)-\frac{1}{3}\cos(3(0))+C\\[6pt]

    -\frac{1}{6}&=-\frac{1}{3}+C\\[6pt]

    \frac{1}{6}&=C\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence the expression for displacement at time $t$ seconds is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}s(t)=\dfrac{1}{32}t^4-\dfrac{1}{3}\cos(3t)+\dfrac{1}{6}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Now that we have an expression for displacement, let's use our G.D.C. to graph it.

    The particle is moving in a straight line in relation to the point $O$. This point is represented by the $x$-axis (i.e. when $y=0$).

    When the graph is below the axis it is on one side of point $O$. When the graph is above the axis it is on the other side of point $O$.

    Hence, for the function to be moving toward the $x$-axis the function needs to be negative and increasing or positive and decreasing.

    The function is negative and increasing from $t=0$ until it crosses the $x$-axis.

    Using the G.D.C. to find the first $x$-intercept we get $t=0.3485...$.

    The function is positive and decreasing between the local maximum and local minimum shown above. Using the G.D.C. we find the local maximum occurs when $t=1.103...$ and the local minimum occurs when $t=1.813...$.

    

    Hence the total time moving toward the point $O$ can be written

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    T&=(0.3458...-0)+(1.813...-1.103...)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    &=1.058...\\[6pt]

    &=1.06\text{ s}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
