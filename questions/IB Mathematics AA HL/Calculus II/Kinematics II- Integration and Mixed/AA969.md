# AA969

**Maximum mark: 16**

Consider a particle P, moving in a straight line, relative to an origin O.

The displacement, in metres, of the particle from the origin at time $t$ seconds is given by $s(t) = t \sin 2t - 7 \sin t \cos t$, for $0 \leq t \leq 4$.

This is shown in the following diagram. Point A shows the particle's displacement after $t=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ seconds and point B shows the particle's displacement after $t=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ seconds.

![q](images/q.png)

1. Show that the displacement of P from O can be expressed as $\left(\sin 2t \right)\left(t - \dfrac{7}{2}\right)$. [3 marks]

2. Show that for $t>0$, the particle first passes through the origin
at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. [4 marks]

3. Find the velocity of the particle at this time. [4 marks]

4. Find the total distance travelled by P between point A and point B. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the double angle identity for sine, $\sin\,2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    s(t) &= t \sin 2t - 7 \sin t \cos t \\[8pt]
    &= t \sin 2t - \dfrac{7}{2}\left(2 \sin t \cos t\right) \\[8pt]
    &= t \sin 2t - \dfrac{7}{2}\sin 2t  \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-1.535em}\boxed{s(t) = \left(\sin 2t \right)\left(t - \dfrac{7}{2}\right)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. If the particle is at the origin, $s(t)=0$. We must solve
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        s(t) &= 0 \\[8pt]
        \left(\sin 2t \right)\left(t - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore either $\sin 2t=0$ or $t - \dfrac{7}{2}=0$.

    For $0 < t \leq 4$, we have $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2} , \pi$ or $\dfrac{7}{2}$.

    $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \pi < \dfrac{7}{2}$ therefore,

    The first time the particle passes through the origin for $t>0$ is when $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

3. Given that $v = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}$, we must find the value of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        v(t) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg[\left(\sin 2t \right)\left(t - \dfrac{7}{2}\right)\bigg] \\[8pt]
        v(t) &= 2\cos 2t \left(t - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) + \sin 2t \hspace{3em}\text{[By the chain rule]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        v\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) &= 2\cos \left(2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\right) \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) + \sin \left(2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\right) \\[8pt]
        &= 2\cos \pi \left(\dfrac{\pi - 7}{2}\right) + \sin \pi \\[8pt]
        &= -2\left(\dfrac{\pi - 7}{2}\right) \\[8pt]
        &\hspace{-2.7em}\boxed{v\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 7 - \pi}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4. Given that between point A and point B, $s'(t)>0$, the particle is moving in the same direction for all of this time. Hence the total distance travelled in this time is the difference in displacement between points A and B.

    Given that at point A, $t=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and at point B, $t=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \text{Total distance} &= s\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) - s\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) \\[8pt]
        &= \left(\sin 2\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) \right)\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) - \left(\sin 2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) \right)\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) \\[8pt]
        &= \sin\left(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) - \sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ , and in general $\sin(\pi + \theta) = -\sin\theta$ and $sin\left(\pi - \theta\right) = \sin\theta$.

    Hence, $\sin\left(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

    We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \text{Total distance} &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{7}{2}\right) \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + \dfrac{7}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{7}{2}\right) \\[8pt]
        &\hspace{-6.2em}\boxed{\text{Total distance} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(7 - \pi)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
