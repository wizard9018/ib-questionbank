# AA605

**Maximum mark: 14**

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=(\arccos x)^2$,
$-1\leq x\leq 1$.

1.  Show that $f'(0)=-\pi$ [2 marks]

The function $f$ satisfies the equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1-x^2)f''(x)-xf'(x)=2.\\
\end{aligned}
$$

2.  By differentiating the above equation twice, show that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (1-x^2)f^{(4)}(x)-5xf'''(x)=4f''(x)\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    where $f^{(n)}(x)$
    denotes the $n$th derivative of $f(x)$.[4 marks]

3.  Hence show the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$ up to and
    including the term in $x^4$ is
    $\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}-\pi x+x^2-\dfrac{\pi}{6}x^3+\dfrac{x^4}{3}$.[5 marks]

4.  Use this series approximation for $f(x)$ with
    $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$ to find an approximate value for
    $25\pi-\dfrac{20}{3}\pi^2$.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The first derivative of $f(x)$ and its value at $0$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= 2\arccos{x}(\arccos(x))'\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    &=-\dfrac{2\arccos{x}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8.5mm}\\
    f'(0) &= -2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8.5mm}\\
    &\hspace{0.3em}= -\pi\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Differentiating both sides of the equation with respect to $x$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[(1-x^2)f''(x)-xf'(x)\right] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} [2]\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{4mm}\\
    (1-x^2)f'''(x)-3xf''(x)-f'(x) &=0\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Differentiating again with respect to $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[(1-x^2)f'''(x)-3xf''(x)-f'(x)\right] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} [0]\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    (1-x^2)f^{(4)}(x)-5xf'''(x)-4f''(x) &=0\rule[-4mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    (1-x^2)f^{(4)}(x)-5xf'''(x)=4f''(x)\hspace{-14.6mm}&\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}\hspace{15mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  By direct evaluation we have
    $f(0)=(\arccos{0})^2 = \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)^2=\dfrac{\pi^2}{4}$.

    From (a) we have $f'(0)=-\pi$ and substituting $x=0$ in the
    equation\
    $f$ satisfies, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (1-(0)^2)f''(0)-(0)f'(0) &= 2 \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{2mm}\\
    f''(0)&= 2\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting $x=0$
    in equation (1), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (1-0^2)f'''(0)-3(0)f''(0)-f'(0)&= 0 \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{2mm}\\
    f'''(0)-(-\pi) &= 0 \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    f'''(0) &= -\pi\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Finally,
    substituting $x=0$ in equation (2), we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (1-(0)^2)f^{(4)}(0)-5(0)f'''(0)&= 4f''(0)\rule[-2mm]{0pt}{2mm}\\
    f^{(4)}(0) &= 4(2) \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &= 8\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, the Maclaurin
    series for $f(x)$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= f(0)+\dfrac{f'(0)}{1!}x+\dfrac{f''(0)}{2!}x^2+\dfrac{f'''(0)}{3!}x^3+\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{4!}x^4+\cdots \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.3em}= \dfrac{\pi^2}{4} -\pi x+x^2-\dfrac{\pi}{6}x^3+\dfrac{1}{3}x^4+\cdots
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Substituting $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$ in equation (3), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) &= \dfrac{\pi^2}{4} -\pi \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3+\dfrac{1}{3}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4+\cdots \rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \left(\arccos{\dfrac{1}{2}}\right)^2 &\approx \dfrac{\pi^2}{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{48}+\dfrac{1}{48}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)^2&\approx \dfrac{12\pi^2-25\pi}{48}+\dfrac{13}{48}\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    \dfrac{48\pi^2}{9}-12\pi^2+25\pi &\approx 13\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{9mm}\\
    25\pi-\dfrac{20}{3}\pi^2 \approx 13\hspace{-8mm}&\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5.5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
