# AA614

**Maximum mark: 9**

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=e^x\cos x$, $x\in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  By finding a suitable number of derivatives of $f$, determine the
    Maclaurin series for $f(x)$ as far as the term
    $x^4$.[6 marks]

2.  Hence, or otherwise, determine the exact value of $\displaystyle
    \lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{e^x\cos x-x-1}{x^3}$.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Taking the first four derivatives using the product rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)&=e^x\cos x-e^x\sin x & f'(0)&= 1\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\[1pt]
    f''(x)&=-2e^x\sin x & f''(0)&= 0\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\[1pt]
    f'''(x)&=-2e^x\cos x-2e^x\sin x & f'''(0)&= -2\rule[-1.5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\[1pt]
    f^{(4)}(x)&= -4e^x\cos x&f^{(4)}(0)&=-4\rule[0mm]{0pt}{3.75mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore the first terms of the Maclaurin series are

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x)&= f(0)+\dfrac{f'(0)}{1!}x+\dfrac{f''(0)}{2!}x^2+\dfrac{f'''(0)}{3!}x^3+\dfrac{f''''(0)}{4!}x^4+\cdots \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{0.3em}= 1+x-\dfrac{1}{3}x^3-\dfrac{1}{6}x^4+\cdots
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  **Method 1:** Using the Maclaurin series found in (a), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{e^x\cos x-x-1}{x^3} &= \dfrac{\cancel{1}+\cancel{x}-\dfrac{1}{3}x^3-\dfrac{1}{6}x^4+\cdots -\cancel{x}-\cancel{1}}{x^3} \\[6pt]
    \lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{e^x\cos x-x-1}{x^3} &= \lim_{x\to 0} \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{6}x+\cdots\right) \\[10pt]
    &\hspace{0.3em}= -\dfrac{1}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    \
    **Method 2:** Using L'Hôpital's rule repeatedly, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{e^x\cos x-x-1}{x^3} &= \lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{e^x\cos x-e^x\sin x-1}{3x^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{-2e^x\sin x}{6x}\\[6pt]
    &= \lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{-2e^x\cos x-2e^x\sin x}{6} \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{2}{6}=-\dfrac{1}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, the exact
    value of $\displaystyle
    \lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{e^x\cos x-x-1}{x^3}$ is
    $-\dfrac{1}{3}$
