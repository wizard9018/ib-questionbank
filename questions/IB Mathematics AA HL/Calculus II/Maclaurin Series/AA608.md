# AA608

**Maximum mark: 15**

Consider the function $f(x)=\cos(p\arccos x)$, $-1<x<1$ and
$p\in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Show that
    $f'(0)=p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)$.[2 marks]

The function $f$ and its derivative satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1-x^2)f^{(n+2)}(x)=(2n+1)xf^{(n+1)}(x)+\left(n^2-p^2\right)f^{(n)}(x), \quad n\in \mathbb{Z^+}\\
\end{aligned}
$$

where $f^{(n)}(x)$ denotes the $n$th derivative of $f(x)$ and
$f^{(0)}(x)$ is $f(x)$.

2.  Show that $f^{(n+2)}(0)=(n^2-p^2)f^{(n)}(0)$.[1 mark]

3.  For $p\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\pm 3\}$, show
    that the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$, up to and including the $x^4$
    term, is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &\hspace{-6em}\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)+p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)x-\dfrac{p^2\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{2}x^2\\[12pt]
    &+\dfrac{(1-p^2)p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{6}x^3-\dfrac{(4-p^2)p^2\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{24}x^4\hspace{8mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    [5 marks]

4.  Hence or otherwise, find
    $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{\cos(p\arccos(x))}{x}$ where $p$
    is an odd integer. [3 marks]

5.  If $p$ is an integer, prove the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$
    is a polynomial of degree $p$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Differentiating with respect to $x$ and evaluating at $x=0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{p\sin(p\arccos x)}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
    f'(0) &= \dfrac{p\sin(p\arccos(0))}{\sqrt{1-(0)^2}}\rule[-5mm]{0pt}{10mm}\\
    &\hspace{0.3em}= p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Evaluating $x=0$ in the equation $f$ satisfy, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (1-(0)^2)f^{(n+2)}(0)&=(2n+1)(0)f^{(n+1)}(0)+\left(n^2-p^2\right)f^{(n)}(0)\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-14mm}f^{(n+2)}(0) = (n^2-p^2)f^{(n)}(0)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Considering $f$ and its derivatives at $x=0$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(0) &= \cos(p\arccos(0))=\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f'(0) &= p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)&[\text{by part (a)}]\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f''(0)&=(0^2-p^2)f^{(0)}(0)=(-p^2)\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right) &[\text{by part (b)}]\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f'''(0) &= (1^2-p^2)f^{(1)}(0)=(1-p^2)p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)&[\text{by part (b)}]\rule[-3.5mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f^{(4)}(0)&= (2^2-p^2)f^{(2)}(0) = (4-p^2)(-p^2)\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right) &[\text{by part (b)}]\rule[-0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x)&= f(0)+f'(0)x+\dfrac{f''(0)}{2!}x^2+\dfrac{f'''(0)}{3!}x^3+\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{4!}x^4+\cdots \\
    &= \cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)+p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)x-\dfrac{p^2\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{2}x^2\\
    &\hspace{5.5em}+\dfrac{(1-p^2)p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{6}x^3-\dfrac{(4-p^2)p^2\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{24}x^4+\cdots
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Using the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\cos(p\arccos(x))}{x} &= \lim_{x\to 0}\, \dfrac{\cancel{\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}+p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)x-\dfrac{p^2\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{2}x^2+\cdots}{x}\\
    &= \lim_{x\to 0}\  p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\pi}{2}\right)-\dfrac{p^2\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)}{2}x+\cdots\\[10pt]
    &\hspace{0.3em}= p\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  From the recursive equation (1) we deduce $f^{p+2}(0)=0$ and
    therefore $f^{(n)}=0$ for all $n\geq p+2$. For the $p+1$ term we
    have two options:

    -   $p$ is odd. Then
        $\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)$ is zero and
        $\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)$ is non zero,
        therefore the $p+1$ coefficient is zero and the $p$ term is non
        zero.

    -   $p$ is even. Then
        $\cos\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)$ is non zero
        and $\sin\left(\dfrac{p\hspace{0.05em}\pi}{2}\right)$ is zero,
        therefore the $p+1$ coefficient is zero and the $p$ term is non
        zero.

    In both cases the $n+1$ term vanishes and the $n$ term does not.
    Therefore $f(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $p$.
