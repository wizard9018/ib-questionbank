# AA618

**Maximum mark: 7**

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=e^{-x} \sin x -x+x^2$.

By finding a suitable number of derivatives of $f$, determine the first
non-zero term in its Maclaurin series.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Differentiating with respect to $x$, and evaluating the first
derivatives at $0$ we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)&=e^{-x}\sin x-x+x^2 & f(0)&=0\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{5mm}\\
f'(x)&=-e^{-x}\sin x + e^{-x}\cos x-1+2x & f'(0)&=0\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
f''(x)&=\cancel{e^{-x}\sin x}-e^{-x}\cos x-e^{-x}\cos x-\cancel{e^{-x}\sin x} + 2 & f''(0)&=0\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
f'''(x)&= 2e^{-x}\cos x + 2e^{-x}\sin x & f'''(0)&=2\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4mm}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the first non-zero term is

$$
\dfrac{2}{3!}x^3=\hspace{0.4em}\dfrac{1}{3}x^3
$$
