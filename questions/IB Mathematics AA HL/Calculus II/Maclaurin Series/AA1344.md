# AA1344

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the function $f(x)=m+\dfrac{\sin{3x}}{x^3}+\dfrac{n}{x^2}$ where, $m,n \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.    Show that a finite limit for $\displaystyle \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]$ can only exist when $n=-3$. [3 marks]

2.    Find the value of $m$ for which $\displaystyle \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]=5$. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let's begin by trying to find the limit of $f$ as $x \rightarrow 0$ by direct substitution.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]&=\lim_{x \rightarrow 0}\left[m+\dfrac{\sin{3x}}{x^3}+\dfrac{n}{x^2}\right]\\[12pt]
    &=m+\dfrac{\sin{3(0)}}{(0)^3}+\dfrac{n}{(0)^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The limit is undefined as we are dividing by zero.

    However, we may be able to use $\text{L'H\^opital's Rule}$.

    Let's write $f$ as a single fraction.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x)=\frac{mx^3+\sin{3x}+nx}{x^3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now try to find the limit as $x\rightarrow 0$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]&=\frac{m(0)^3+\sin{3(0)}+n(0)}{(0)^3}\\[6pt]
    &=\frac{0}{0}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    This is an indeterminate form. Therefore we can attempt to find a limit by using $\text{L'H\^opital's Rule}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{(R1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Differentiating the numerator and denominator independently and evaluating the limit we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &=\lim_{x \rightarrow 0}\left[\frac{3mx^2+3\cos{3x}+n}{3x^2}\right]\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{3m(0)^2+3\cos{3(0)}+n}{3(0)^2}\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{3+n}{0}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Again, as we are dividing by zero, a finite limit can only exist if the fraction is in an indeterminate form.
    Hence we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{3+n}{0}&\equiv\frac{0}{0}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore $n=-3$ ...as required.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. In this part, we must use the value of $n=-3$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{(R1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using part of our previous answer we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]&=\lim_{x \rightarrow 0}\left[\frac{3mx^2+3\cos{3x}-3}{3x^2}\right]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As shown previously, this leads to an indeterminate form hence we can use $\text{L'H\^opital's Rule}$ and evaluate the limit to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]&=\lim_{x \rightarrow 0}\left[\frac{6mx-9\sin{3x}}{6x}\right]\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{6m(0)-9\sin{3(0)}}{6(0)}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{0}{0}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Once again we use \text{L'H\^opital's Rule} and evaluate the limit.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \rightarrow 0}[f(x)]&=\lim_{x \rightarrow 0}\left[\frac{6m-27\cos{3x}}{6}\right]\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{6m-27\cos{3(0)}}{6}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{6m-27}{6}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now use the information given in the question to form an equation which we can solve to find $m$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{6m-27}{6}&=5\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[12pt]
    m&=9.5\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
