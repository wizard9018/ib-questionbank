# AA613

**Maximum mark: 10**

1.  By successive differentiation find the first five non-zero terms in
    the Maclaurin series for $f(x)=(2-2x)\ln(1-x)+2x$.[8 marks]

2.  Deduce that, for $n\geq 2$, the coefficient of $x^n$ in this series
    is $\dfrac{2}{n(n-1)}$.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We find the first six derivatives and their evaluations at $x=0$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= -\dfrac{2-2x}{1-x}-2\ln(1-x)+2=-2\ln(1-x) & f'(0)&= 0 \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{3mm}\\
    f''(x) &= \dfrac{2}{1-x} & f''(0)&=2\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f'''(x)&=\dfrac{2}{(1-x)^2} & f'''(0) &= 2=2(1)\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f^{(4)}(x) &= \dfrac{4}{(1-x)^3} & f^{(4)}(0)&=4=2(2)\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f^{(5)}(x) &= \dfrac{12}{(1-x)^4} & f^{(5)}(0) &= 12=2(6)\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{8mm}\\
    f^{(6)}(x) &= \dfrac{48}{(1-x)^5} & f^{(6)}(0)&= 48=2(24)\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    thus, the first five non-zero terms are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{-1.4em}f(x) &= \dfrac{f''(0)}{2!}x^2+\dfrac{f'''(0)}{3!}x^3+\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{4!}x^4+\dfrac{f^{(5)}(0)}{5!}x^5+\dfrac{f^{(6)}(0)}{6!}x^6+\cdots \\[8pt]
    \hspace{-0.3em}&= x^2+\dfrac{1}{3}x^3+\dfrac{1}{6}x^4+\dfrac{1}{10}x^5+\dfrac{1}{15}x^6\cdots
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  From the terms of $f^{(n)}(0)$ in (a) we can infer the $n$-th term
    is $2(n-2)!$. Therefore, the coefficient of $x^n$ in the Maclaurin
    series is

    $$
    \dfrac{2(n-2)!}{n!} =\enskip\dfrac{2}{n(n-1)}
    $$
