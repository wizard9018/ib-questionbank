# AA1059

**Maximum mark: 5**

1. Find the first three non-zero terms of the Maclaurin series for $\ln(1+3x)\sin{x}$.[3 marks]

2. Hence find $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+3x)\sin{x}-3x^2}{x^3}$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the formula booklet we can write down

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \ln(1+3x)\sin{x}&=\left(3x-\tfrac{(3x)^2}{2}+\tfrac{(3x)^3}{3}+...\right)\left(x-\tfrac{x^3}{3!}+\tfrac{x^5}{5!}-...\right)\\

    &=\left(3x-\tfrac{9x^2}{2}+9x^3+...\right)\left(x-\tfrac{x^3}{3!}+\tfrac{x^5}{5!}-...\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Recognise that we need, up to and including, the $x^4$ terms

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(R1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=3x^2-\tfrac{9x^3}{2}+9x^4-\tfrac{3x^4}{3!}\\[6pt]

    &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{3x^2-\tfrac{9x^3}{2}+\tfrac{17x^4}{2}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Using the result from part (a) we can rewrite the quotient

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+3x)\sin{x}-3x^2}{x^3}&=\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{3x^2-\tfrac{9x^3}{2}+\tfrac{17x^4}{2}-3x^2}{x^3} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &=\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{-\tfrac{9x^3}{2}+\tfrac{17x^4}{2}}{x^3}\\[6pt]

    &=\lim_{x \to 0}\left(\frac{-9}{2}+\frac{17x}{2}\right)\\[6pt]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    All of the remaining terms of the expansion will evaluate to zero hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-\frac{9}{2}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
