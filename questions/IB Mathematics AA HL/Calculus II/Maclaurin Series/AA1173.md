# AA1173

**Maximum mark: 9**

Consider the following differential equation
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=x-2xy
\end{aligned}
$$

It is known that the point $(0, 2)$ lies on the solution curve.

1.  1.  Show that $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}=1-2y-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$.

    2.  Hence write down the value of $\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}$ at $x=0$. [3 marks]

2.  1.  Find the values of $\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}$ and $\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^4y}{\mathrm{d}x^4}$ at $x=0$.

    2.  Hence, find the first three non-zero terms of the Maclaurin Series for the solution curve of the differential equation. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  We can use the product rule to differentiate
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}&=(x-2xy)' \\[6pt]
        &=1-\left(2y+2x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &=1-2y-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} \hspace{2em} \text{... as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    2.  We know from the question that when $x=0, y=2$. We can also see that $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$ when $x=0$. Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\bigg\lvert_{x=0,y=2}&=1-2\times 2- 2\times 0 \times 0\\[6pt]
            &=-3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  1.  To find $\frac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}$ we can use the product rule to differentiate $\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \frac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}&=\left(1-2y-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)'\\[12pt]
            &=-2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-\left(2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+2x\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\right)\\[12pt]
            &=-2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-2\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-2x\frac{\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\\[6pt]
            &=-4\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[6pt]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
           \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}\bigg\lvert_{x=0,y=2} &=-4\times 0 - 2 \times 0 \times -3 \\[6pt]
           &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{0} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We use a similar method to find $\frac{\mathrm{d}^4y}{\mathrm{d}x^4}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \frac{\mathrm{d}^4y}{\mathrm{d}x^4}&=\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}\right)'\\[12pt]
           &=\left(-4\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}\right)'\\[12pt]
           &=-4\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}-\left(2\frac{d^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}+2x\frac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}\right)\\[12pt]
           &=-6\frac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}^3y}{\mathrm{d}x^3}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[12pt]
           &=-6\times (-3)-2\times 0 \times 0\\[12pt]
           &=18 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Here is a summary of what we have found in the previous part.

        Assuming the solution curve is of the form $y=f(x)$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            f(0)=2 \hspace{2em} f'(0)=0 \hspace{2em} f''(0)=-3 \hspace{2em} f'''(0)=0 \hspace{2em} f''''(0)=18
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now using the definition of the Maclaurin Series from the formula booklet we can write the first three non-zero terms
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            f(x)&=2+x \times 0 + \frac{x^2}{2!}\times (-3) + \frac{x^3}{3!}\times 0+ \frac{x^4}{4!}\times 18 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
            &=2-\frac{3}{2}x^2+\frac{3}{4}x^4 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
