# AA596

**Maximum mark: 20**

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = e^{\arctan x}$.

1.  Find the first two derivatives of $f(x)$ and hence find the
    Maclaurin series for $f(x)$ up to and including the $x^2$ term.
    [6 marks]

2.  Show that the coefficient of $x^3$ in the Maclaurin series for
    $f(x)$ is $-\dfrac 1 6$. [6 marks]

3.  Using the Maclaurin series for $\sin x$ and $\ln(2x + 1)$, find the
    Maclaurin series for $\sin(\ln(2x + 1))$ up to and including the
    $x^3$ term. [6 marks]

4.  Hence, or otherwise, find
    $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\, \dfrac {f(x) - 1} {\sin(\ln(2x + 1))}$.
    [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the chain and quotient rules, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = \dfrac {e^{\arctan x}} {1 + x^2}\\[6pt]
    f''(x)  & =     \dfrac {e^{\arctan x} - 2 x e^{\arctan x}} {(1 + x^2)^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence $f(0) = 1$, $f'(0) = 1$, $f''(0)    = 1$.

    Substituting the above result into the Maclaurin formula

    $$
    f(x) = f(0) + x f'(0) + \dfrac {x^2} {2!} f''(0) + \dots,

    $$
    we get
    the Maclaurin series for $f(x)$ up to and including the $x^2$ term

    $$
    1 +  x + \dfrac {x^2} 2
    $$

2.  Let's find $f'''(0)$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'''(x)     & = \dfrac {(6x^2 - 6x - 1) e^{\arctan x}} {(1 + x^2)^3}\\[6pt]
    f'''(0) & = -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, the coefficient of $x^3$ in the
    Maclaurin series is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    c_3     & = \dfrac {-1} {3!} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{.275em} = -\dfrac 1 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Substituting $2x$ into the Maclaurin series for $\ln(1 + x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ln(1 + 2x)     & = 2x - \dfrac {(2x)^2} {2} + \dfrac {(2x)^3} {3} + \dots\\[4pt]
    & = 2x - 2x^2 + \dfrac {8x^3} 3 + \dots
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $\ln(1 + 2x)$ into the Maclaurin series for $\sin x$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin (\ln(1 + 2x))  & = \ln(1 + 2x)  - \dfrac {(\ln(1 + 2x) )^3} {3!} + \dots\\[4pt]
    & = \left(2x - 2x^2 + \dfrac {8x^3} 3 + \dots\right)
    - \dfrac {\left(2x - 2x^2 + \dfrac {8x^3} 3\right)^3} 6 + \dots\\[6pt]
    & = \left(2x - 2x^2 + \dfrac {8x^3} 3\right) - \dfrac{(2x)^3} 6 + \dots
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the Maclaurin series for $\sin(\ln(2x + 1))$ up to and
    including the $x^3$ term is

    $$
    2 x - 2x^2 + \dfrac {4x^3} 3
    $$

4.  Substituting the results from parts (a) and (c), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \to 0} \,\dfrac {f(x) - 1} {\sin(\ln(2x + 1))}
    & = \lim_{x \to 0} \,\dfrac {x + \dfrac {x^2} 2 + \dots} {2x - 2x^2 + \dots}\hspace{4em}\\[6pt]
    & = \lim_{x \to 0} \,\dfrac {1 + \dfrac {x} 2 + \dots} {2 - 2x + \dots}\\[8pt]
    &\hspace{.275em} = \dfrac 1 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
