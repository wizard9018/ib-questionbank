# AA616

**Maximum mark: 9**

Let $f(x)=e^x\cos x$.

1.  Show that $f''(x)=2\left(f'(x)-f(x)\right)$.[4 marks]

2.  By further differentiation of the result in part (a), find the
    Maclaurin\
    expansion of $f(x)$, as far as the term in $x^5$.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Differentiating with respect to $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= -e^x\sin x + e^x\cos x \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{2mm}\\
    f''(x)&= -\cancel{e^x\cos x}-e^x\sin x-e^x\sin x+\cancel{e^x\cos x} \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &= 2(-e^x\sin x) \rule[-2mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &= 2(-e^x\sin x+e^x\cos x-e^x\cos x)\rule[-2mm]{0pt}{6mm}\\
    &= 2(f'(x)-f(x))\rule[0mm]{0pt}{4mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Given that $f(0)=1$, $f'(0)=1$ and $f''(0)=0$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'''(0) &= 2(f''(0)-f'(0)) = 2(0-1) = -2\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{2mm}\\
    f^{(4)}(0) &= 2(f'''(0)-f''(0)) = 2(-2-0)=-4 \rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}\\
    f^{(5)}(0)&=2(f^{(4)}(0)-f'''(0))=2(-4-(-2))=-4\rule[0mm]{0pt}{5mm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the Maclaurin expansion of $e^x\cos x$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &= f(0)+\dfrac{f'(0)}{1!}x+\dfrac{f''(0)}{2!}x^2 + \dfrac{f'''(0)}{3!}x^3+\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{4!}x^4+\dfrac{f^{(5)}(0)}{5!}x^5+\cdots\\[10pt]
    &\hspace{0.3em}= 1+x-\dfrac{1}{3}x^3-\dfrac{1}{6}x^4-\dfrac{1}{30}x^5+\cdots
    \end{aligned}
    $$
