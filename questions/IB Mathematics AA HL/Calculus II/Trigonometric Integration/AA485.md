# AA485

**Maximum mark: 7**

Using the substitution $x^3 = 3\sec \theta$, show that
$\displaystyle \int \dfrac{\textrm dx}{x\sqrt{x^6 - 9}} = \dfrac{1}{9}\arccos\left(\dfrac{3}{x^3}\right) + C$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Using the substitution $x^3 = 3\sec \theta$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \dfrac{\textrm dx}{x\sqrt{x^6 - 9}}    & =  \int \dfrac{x^2\,\textrm dx}{x^3\sqrt{x^6 - 9}}                                     \quad \qquad [\text{replace $x^2\,\textrm dx = \tan \theta \sec \theta\,\textrm d\theta$}]  \\[8pt]
& =  \int \dfrac{\tan \theta \cancel{\sec \theta}\,\textrm d\theta}{3\cancel{\sec\theta}\sqrt{9\sec^2\theta - 9}}   \quad \qquad [\text{replace $\sec^2 \theta -1 = \tan^2 \theta$}]  \\[8pt]
& =  \int \dfrac{\cancel{\tan \theta}\,\textrm d\theta}{9\cancel{\tan \theta}} \\[8pt]
& =  \dfrac{\theta}{9} + C \qquad  \Big[\text{replace $\theta = \arccos\left(\dfrac{3}{x^3}\right)$} \Big]  \\[10pt]
&\hspace{.275em} =  \dfrac{1}{9} \arccos \left(\dfrac{3}{x^3}\right) + C
\end{aligned}
$$
