# AA508

**Maximum mark: 8**

By using the substitution $s = \tan x$, find
$\displaystyle \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d} x}{2+\cos 2x}$.

Express your answer in the form $A \arctan (B \tan x) + C$, where $A, B$
are constants to be determined.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Letting $s = \tan x$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
x   & =  \arctan s \\[11pt]
\dfrac{\mathrm{d} x}{\mathrm{d} s} & =  \dfrac{1}{1 + s^2} \\[11pt]
\mathrm{d} x & =  \dfrac{\mathrm{d} s}{1 + s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the triangle

![AA508a1](images/AA508a1.JPEG)

we deduce that $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + s^2}}$

Hence making the substitution $s = \tan x$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \dfrac{\mathrm{d} x}{2+\cos 2x}
& =  \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d} x}{1+2\cos^2 x} \quad \quad \quad [\text{using $\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1$}] \\[8pt]
& =  \int \dfrac{\dfrac{\mathrm{d} s}{1 + s^2}}{1+2\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + s^2}}\right)^2} \\[12pt]
& =  \int \dfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{1 + s^2 + 2} \\[12pt]
& =  \int \dfrac{1}{3 + s^2} \,\mathrm{d} s \\[12pt]
& =  \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \arctan \left(\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{3}}\right) + C \\[14pt]
&  =   \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \arctan \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \tan x\right) + C
\end{aligned}
$$
