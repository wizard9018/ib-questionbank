# AA509

**Maximum mark: 16**

Consider the functions $f(x) = \sin x$,
$-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and
$g(x) = \dfrac{2x\sqrt{1-x^2}}{1 - 2x^2}$,
$x \in \mathbb{R}, \, x \neq \pm \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

1.  Find an expression for $(g \circ f)(x)$, stating its domain.
    [2 marks]

2.  Hence show that $(g \circ f)(x) = \tan 2 x$. [2 marks]

3.  Find the exact value of
    $(g\circ f)'(x)$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
    [6 marks]

4.  Show that the area bounded by the graph of $y = (g\circ f)(x)$, the
    $x$-axis and the lines $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is
    $\dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (g \circ f)(x)  & =  g\big(f(x)\big) \\[6pt]
    & =  g(\sin x) \\[10pt]
    (g \circ f)(x)&= \dfrac{2\sin x\sqrt{1-\sin^2 x}}{1 - 2\sin^2 x}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    and its domain is
     $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \,\, x \neq \pm\dfrac{\pi}{4}$

2.  Simplifying the above expression for $(g \circ f)(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (g \circ f)(x)  & =  \dfrac{2\sin x\sqrt{\cos^2 x}}{1 - 2\sin^2 x} \\[8pt]
    & =  \dfrac{2 \sin x |\cos x|}{1 - 2\sin^2 x} \\[8pt]
    & =  \dfrac{2 \sin x \cos x}{1 - 2\sin^2 x} \quad \quad \quad [\text{since $\cos x > 0$}] \\[8pt]
    & =  \dfrac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x} \\[8pt]
    &= \tan 2x  \hspace{0.2em} \text{... as required.}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (g \circ f)(x) &= \tan 2x \\[8pt]
    (g \circ f)'(x) &= 2 \sec ^2 2x \\[8pt]
    &= \dfrac{2}{\cos ^2 2x}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ into the above expression for
    $(g \circ f)'(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (g \circ f)'\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)
    & =  \dfrac{2}{\left(\cos \frac{\pi}{3}\right)^2} \\[5pt]
    & =  \dfrac{2}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2} \\[12pt]
    &=  8
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Area}     & =  \int_0^\frac{\pi}{6} (g \circ f)(x)\,\mathrm{d} x \\[10pt]
    & =  \int_0^\frac{\pi}{6}  \tan 2x \,\mathrm{d} x \\[10pt]
    &= \int_0^\frac{\pi}{6}  \dfrac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x} \,\mathrm{d} x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let $u = \cos 2x$. Therefore $\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=-2 \sin 2x$.

    We can substitue $-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u$ in place of $\sin 2x \, \mathrm{d}x$. Hence we can write

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_0^\frac{\pi}{6} (g \circ f)(x)\,\mathrm{d} x &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{x=0}^{x=\frac{\pi}{6}} \dfrac{1}{u}\, \mathrm{d} u \\[10pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2} \bigg(\ln |u| \bigg)\bigg|_{x=0}^{x=\frac{\pi}{6}} \\[10pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2} \bigg(\ln |\cos 2x| \bigg)\bigg|_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \\[10pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\ln \cos \left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)- \ln \cos 0\right) \\[10pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\ln \frac{1}{2} - \ln 1\right) \\[10pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\ln 2^{-1}\right) \\[10pt]
    &\hspace{-7.35em}\displaystyle{\int_0^\frac{\pi}{6}} (g \circ f)(x)\,\mathrm{d} x = \frac{1}{2} \ln 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
