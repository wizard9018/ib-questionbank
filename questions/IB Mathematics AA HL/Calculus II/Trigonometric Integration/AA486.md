# AA486

**Maximum mark: 7**

1.  Using the substitution $x = \cot \theta$, show that
    $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \dfrac{1}{(x^2+1)^2}\,\textrm dx = \int_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2} \sin^2\theta \,\textrm d\theta$.
    [4 marks]

2.  Hence find the value of
    $\displaystyle \int_0^1 \dfrac{1}{(x^2+1)^2}\,\textrm dx$.
    [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the substitution $x = \cot \theta$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_0^1 \dfrac{1}{(x^2+1)^2}\,\textrm dx   & =  -\int_\frac{\pi}{2}^\frac{\pi}{4}\dfrac{\csc^2 \theta}{(\cot^2 \theta+1)^2}\,\textrm d\theta \qquad \quad  [\text{since }\textrm  dx = -\csc^2 \theta\,\textrm d\theta] \\[6pt]
    & =  \int_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2}\dfrac{\csc^2 \theta}{\csc^4 \theta}\,\textrm d\theta \qquad \quad [\text{since } \cot^2 \theta + 1 = \csc^2 \theta] \\[6pt]
    & =  \int_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2}\dfrac{1}{\csc^2 \theta}\,\textrm d\theta \\[10pt]
    & \hspace{.275em} =  \int_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2} \sin^2\theta \,\textrm d\theta
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using double-angle formula $\cos 2\theta  = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_0^1 \dfrac{1}{(x^2+1)^2}\,\textrm dx  & =  \int_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2} \sin^2\theta \,\textrm d\theta \quad \quad \quad [\text{by part (a)}] \\[6pt]
    & =  \dfrac{1}{2}\int_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2} (1-\cos 2\theta)\,\textrm d\theta \\[6pt]
    & =  \dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\theta-\dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta\Big)\Big|_\frac{\pi}{4}^\frac{\pi}{2} \\[8pt]
    & =  \dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\Big[\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(\pi) \Big] - \Big[\dfrac{\pi}{4} - \dfrac{1}{2}\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\Big]\Big) \\[8pt]
    & =  \dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2}\Big) \\[10pt]
    &\hspace{.275em}  =  \dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{1}{4}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
