# AA481

**Maximum mark: 24**

Let $y = e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$.

1.  Find an expression for $\dfrac{\textrm dy}{\textrm dx}$. [2 marks]

2.  Show that
    $\dfrac{\textrm d^2y}{\textrm dx^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$.
    [2 marks]

Consider the function $f$ defined by
$f(x) =  e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$,
$-\pi \leq x \leq \pi$.

3.  Show that the function $f$ has a local maximum value when
    $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. [4 marks]

4.  Find the $x$-coordinate of the point of inflexion of the
    graph of $y = f(x)$. [2 marks]

5.  Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$, clearly indicating the
    positions of the\
    local maximum point, the point of inflexion and the intercepts with\
    the axes. [3 marks]

6.  Find the area of the region enclosed by the graph of
    $y = f(x)$ and\
    the $x$-axis.[6 marks]

The curvature at any point $(x,y)$ on a graph is defined as
$\kappa = \dfrac{\left|\dfrac{\textrm d^2y} {\textrm dx^2} \right|} {\left[1 + \left[ \dfrac{\textrm dy} {\textrm dx}\right]^2\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}}$.

7.  Find the value of the curvature of the graph of $y = f(x)$
    at the local maximum point. [3 marks]

8.  Find the value of $\kappa$ for $x = 0$ and comment on its
    meaning with respect to the shape of the graph. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Calculating the $1$st derivative of $y$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\textrm dy}{\textrm dx}  & =  -\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) - \dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) \\[8pt]
    &  =  -\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\left[\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Calculating the $2$nd derivative of $y$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\textrm d^2y}{\textrm dx^2} & =  \dfrac{1}{4}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\left[ \cancel{\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)} + \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right] - \dfrac{1}{4}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\left[-\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + \cancel{\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}\right] \\[8pt]
    &  =  \dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    as required.

3.  Solving $f'(x) = 0$ for the stationary points, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\left[\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right] & =  0 \\[6pt]
    \cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) & =  -\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) \\[6pt]
    \tan\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) & =  -1 \quad \quad \quad \quad   [\text{for $x \neq 0$}] \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{x}{2} & =  -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \\[6pt]
    x & =  -\dfrac{\pi}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Evaluating $f''(x)$ at $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f''\Big(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Big) & =  \dfrac{1}{2}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \sin\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) \\[6pt]
    & =  -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}}}{4} \\[10pt]
    & <  0.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence  $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$  is the
    $x$-coordinate of a local maximum point.

4.  Solving the equation $f''(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)     & =  0 \\[6pt]
    \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)                          & =  0 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{x}{2}                                    & =  0 \\[6pt]
    x                                           & =  0.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since $f''(x)$ has opposite signs on either side of $x = 0$,
     $x = 0$  is the $x$-coordinate of a
    point of inflexion on the graph of $y = f(x)$.

5.  Solving $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) & =  0 \\[6pt]
    \cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) & =  0 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{x}{2} & =  \pm \dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[6pt]
    x & =  \pm \pi
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating $f(x)$ at $x = 0$, we get
    $f(0) = 1$. Hence the points $(\pm \pi, 0)$ are the $x$-intercepts
    and the point $(0,1)$ is the $y$-intercept of the graph of
    $y = f(x)$.

6.  The area of the region enclosed by the graph of $y = f(x)$ and the
    $x$-axis is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A & =  \int_{-\pi}^\pi e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\, \textrm dx \quad \quad \quad \left[\text{let $u = -\dfrac{x}{2}$}\right]\\[6pt]
    A & =  -2\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}} e^u \cos(-u)\, \textrm du \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{A}{2} & =  \int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} e^u \cos u\, \textrm du \quad \quad \quad [\text{integrate by parts}] \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{A}{2} & =  e^u \sin u \Big|_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}  -  \int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} e^u \sin u\, \textrm du \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{A}{2} & =  \big(e^u \sin u\big)\Big|_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}  - \Big[\big(-e^u \cos u\big) \Big|_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} + \int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} e^u \cos u\,\textrm du \Big] \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{A}{2} & =  \big(e^\frac{\pi}{2} + e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}}\big) - \left[0 +\dfrac{A}{2}\right]\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    A  =  e^\frac{\pi}{2} + e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}}
    $$

7.  The value of the curvature of the graph of $y = f(x)$ at
    $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \kappa &= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right|}{\left[1 + \dfrac{1}{4}e^{-x}\left[\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]^2\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \\[6pt]
    %
    \end{aligned}
    %\begin{aligned}
    &= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}} \left|\sin\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\right|}{\left[1 + \dfrac{1}{4}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left[\cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) + \sin\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\right]^2\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \\[6pt]
    %
    \end{aligned}
    %\begin{aligned}
    &= \dfrac{\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}}}{\left[1 + \dfrac{1}{4}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left[\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right]^2\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \\[10pt]
    & =  \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

8.  The value of the curvature of the graph of $y = f(x)$ at $x = 0$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \kappa &= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{x}{2}} \left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right|}{\left[1 + \dfrac{1}{4}e^{-x}\left[\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + \sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]^2\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}\left|\sin\left(0\right)\right|}{\left[1 + \dfrac{1}{4}\left[\cos\left(0\right) + \sin\left(0\right)\right]^2\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}} \\[12pt]
    &  =  0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the graph
    of $y = f(x)$ is a straight line in the neighborhood of the point
    $(0,1)$.
