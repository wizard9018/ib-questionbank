# AA567

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f'(x) = \sin(2x) \cos (2x)$. Find $f(x)$, given that $f(\pi) = 3$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Substituting $u = \sin(2x)$ and $\textrm d u = 2 \cos (2x) \textrm d x$,
we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)        & = \int \sin(2x) \cos(2x)\,\textrm dx\\[6pt]
& = \int \dfrac u 2 \,\textrm du\\[6pt]
& = \dfrac {u^2} 4 + C\\[6pt]
& = \dfrac {\sin^2(2x)} 4 + C
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's determine $C$. Substituting $f(\pi) = 3$ into the expression for
$f$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
3       & = \dfrac {\sin^2(2\pi)} 4+ C\hspace{.8cm}\\[6pt]
C       & = 3
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence
  $f(x) = \dfrac {\sin^2(2x)} 4 + 3$
