# AA1327

**Maximum mark: 9**

1.    Show that $\sin^3{\theta} = \sin{\theta} - \sin{\theta}\cos^2{\theta}$. [1 mark]

The function $f$ is defined as $f(x) = \sin^3{2x}$.

2.    Hence, show that $\displaystyle \int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{6}\cos^3{2x} - \frac{1}{2}\cos{2x} + C$. [4 marks]

A continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function $g$ defined by
$$
\begin{aligned}
    g(x) =
    \begin{cases}
        af(x), & 0 \leq x \leq \frac{\pi}{4} \\
        0, & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

3.    Find $\mathrm{P}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{6} < X < \tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Rewriting and then using the Pythagorean identity, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin^3{\theta} &= \sin{\theta} \times \sin^2{\theta} \\[6pt]
    &= \sin{\theta}(1 - \cos^2{\theta}) \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]
    &= \sin{\theta}- \sin{\theta}\cos^2{\theta}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required.}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. We need to integrate the function $f$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int \sin^3{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Notice how the question uses the word _hence_; this means we need to use the previous part to help us.

    Let’s rewrite $\sin^3{2x}$, with $\theta = 2x$, using the identity from part (a)(i).
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \sin^3{2x}\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int \left( \textcolor{red}{\sin{2x}} \textcolor{blue}{\,-\,\sin{2x}\cos^2{2x}} \right)\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now integrate both terms separately.
    For the first term, we use the standard integral for sine
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \textcolor{red}{\sin{2x}}\,\mathrm{d}x &= -\frac{1}{2}\cos{2x} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    And for the second term, $\textcolor{blue}{\,-\,\sin{2x}\cos^2{2x}}$, we use the substitution
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    u &= \cos{2x} \\[6pt]
    \frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} &= -2 \sin{2x} \\[6pt]
    \mathrm{d}x &= -\dfrac{1}{2 \sin{2x}}\,\mathrm{d}u \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, writing the second term in terms of $u$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int \textcolor{blue}{\,-\,\sin{2x}\cos^2{2x}}\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int -\sin{2x} \times u^2 \times \left( -\dfrac{1}{2 \sin{2x}} \right)\,\mathrm{d}u
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Notice, we can cancel out the sine functions! But be careful with the two negative signs.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= \frac{1}{2} \int u^2\,\mathrm{d}u
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We now have a single function of $u$, which we are integrating with respect to $u$. Excellent!
    Using the inverse power rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} u^3 \\[12pt]
    &= \frac{1}{6} u^3 \\[12pt]
    &= \frac{1}{6} \cos^3{2x} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We will add the constant of integration in the next step.
    Putting the results for the two terms back together, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x &= \frac{1}{6} \cos^3{2x} - \frac{1}{2} \cos{2x} + C\hspace{1em}\text{...as required.}\tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  We have been asked to find the probability of the continuous random variable  $X$ taking a value from $\tfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$.

    As this is a continuous distribution, we need to find the area under the probability density function from $\tfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$.

    Hence, we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}\left( \tfrac{\pi}{6} < X < \tfrac{\pi}{4} \right) &= \int_{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{6}}^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{4}} g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let’s write $g$ in terms of $f$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= \int_{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{6}}^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{4}} a f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, we need to evaluate the definite integral shown above.

    However, we don't know the value of $a$.

    But we do know that the area under a valid probability distribution must be equal to $1$.

    Hence, using the given domain of $g$ which is $0 < x < \frac{\pi}{4}$ we can form an equation which we can solve to find $a$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_0^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{4}} a f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = 1 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using our answer from part (b), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    a \left[ \frac{1}{6} \cos^3{2x} - \frac{1}{2} \cos{2x} \right]_0^{\textstyle\frac{\pi}{4}} = 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting in the limits and solving for $a$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    a \left[ \left( \frac{1}{6} \times \cos^3{(2 \times \tfrac{\pi}{4})} - \frac{1}{2} \times \cos{(2 \times \tfrac{\pi}{4})} \right) - \left( \frac{1}{6} \times \cos^3{(2 \times 0)} - \frac{1}{2} \times \cos{(2 \times 0)} \right) \right] &= 1 \\[12pt]
    a \left[ \left( \frac{1}{6} \times \cos^3{ \tfrac{\pi}{2}} - \frac{1}{2} \times \cos{\tfrac{\pi}{2}} \right) - ... \\
    ...\left( \frac{1}{6} \times \cos^3{0} - \frac{1}{2} \times \cos{0} \right) \right] &= 1 \\[12pt]
    a \left[ \left( \frac{1}{6} \times 0^3 - \frac{1}{2} \times 0 \right) - \left( \frac{1}{6} \times 1^3 - \frac{1}{2} \times 1 \right) \right] &= 1 \\[12pt]
    -a \left( \frac{1}{6} - \frac{3}{6} \right) &= 1 \\[12pt]
    \frac{2a}{6} &= 1 \\[12pt]
    a &= 3 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Now we have $a$, we can find the probability.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}\left( \tfrac{\pi}{6} < X < \tfrac{\pi}{4} \right)&=\int_{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{6}}^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{4}}3 f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\[12pt]
    &= 3 \left[ \frac{1}{6} \cos^3{2x} - \frac{1}{2} \cos{2x} \right]_{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{6}}^{\textstyle \frac{\pi}{4}} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting in the limits and simplifying, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= 3 \left[ \left( \frac{1}{6} \times ( 0 )^3 - \frac{1}{2} \times ( 0)  \right) - \left( \frac{1}{6} \times \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^3 - \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \right) \right] \\[12pt]
    &= -3 \left( \frac{1}{48} - \frac{1}{4} \right) \\[12pt]
    &= -\frac{1}{16} + \frac{3}{4} \\[12pt]
    \mathrm{P}\left( \tfrac{\pi}{6} < X < \tfrac{\pi}{4} \right)&= \dfrac{11}{16} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
