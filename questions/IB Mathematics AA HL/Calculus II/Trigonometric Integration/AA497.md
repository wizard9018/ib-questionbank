# AA497

**Maximum mark: 5**

1.  Show that $\cos \theta = \sin \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)$
    for $0 < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. [1 mark]

2.  Find
    $\displaystyle \int_{\sin \theta}^{\cos \theta} \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}\,\textrm dx$
    where $0 < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)
    & =   \sin \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) \cos\theta -  \cos \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) \sin \theta \\[6pt]
    & =  1\cdot\cos\theta - 0\cdot\sin \theta \\[8pt]
    & \hspace{.275em}=  \cos \theta
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using part (a), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_{\sin \theta}^{\cos \theta} \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}\,\textrm dx
    & =  (\arcsin x)\Big|_{\sin \theta}^{\cos \theta} \\[3pt]
    & =  \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) - \theta \\[10pt]
    & \hspace{.275em}= \dfrac{\pi}{2} - 2\theta
    \end{aligned}
    $$
