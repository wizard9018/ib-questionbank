# AA807

**Maximum mark: 18**

Consider the functions $f$ and $g$ defined on the domain $0< x< 2\pi$ by

$$
f(x)=4\cos 2x \quad\text{and}\quad g(x)=2-8\cos x.
$$
The following
diagram shows the graphs of $y=f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$.

![e700b521092372413c6cbd43f59c1c9797a22356.svg](images/e700b521092372413c6cbd43f59c1c9797a22356.svg)

1.  Find the $x$-coordinates of the points of intersection of the two
    graphs.[7 marks]

2.  Find the exact area of the shaded region, giving your answer in the
    form $a\pi + b\sqrt{3}$, where $a$, $b\in \mathbb{Q}$.[6 marks]

At the points P and Q on the diagram, the gradients of the two graphs
are equal.

3.  Determine the $y$-coordinate of P on the graph of
    $g$.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $x$ be an intersection point of the two graphs, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    4\cos(2x) &= 2 - 8\cos x \\[12pt]
    4\left(2\cos^2 x - 1 \right)  &= 2-8\cos x\\[12pt]
    8\cos^2 x - 4 &= 2 - 8\cos x \\[12pt]
    8\cos^2 x + 8\cos x -6 &= 0 \\[12pt]
    4\cos^2 x + 4\cos x -3 &= 0\\
    \intertext{Using the quadratic formula, we get}
    \cos x &= \dfrac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16-4(4)(-3)}}{2(4)} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{-4\pm \sqrt{64}}{8} \\[12pt]
    &= -\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\\
    \intertext{Using that $-1\leq \cos x \leq 1$ the solutions are}
    \cos x &= \dfrac{1}{2} \\[12pt]
    & \hspace{-0.52em}x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{5\pi}{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The area is given by the integral
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_{{\pi}/{3}}^{{5\pi}/{3}} \big[\hspace{0.1em} g(x)-f(x) \hspace{0.1em}] \, \mathrm{d}x
    &= \int_{{\pi}/{3}}^{{5\pi}/{3}}  \big[\hspace{0.1em} 2-8\cos x - 4\cos 2x \hspace{0.1em}\big] \\[12pt]
    &= \bigg[\hspace{0.1em} 2x-8\sin x-2\sin 2x \hspace{0.1em}\bigg]_{\pi/3}^{5\pi/3}\\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{10\pi}{3} - 8\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) - 2\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) \\[9pt]
    & \qquad -\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 8\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + 2\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{8\pi}{3} + 10\sqrt{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Let $x$ be the point at which the gradients are equal, thus, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= g'(x) \\[12pt]
    -8\sin 2x &= 8\sin x \\[13pt]
    -16\sin x\cos x &= 8 \sin x \\[10pt]
    \cos x &= -\dfrac{1}{2} \hspace{3em}[\hspace{0.1em}\text{since } \sin x \neq 0\hspace{0.1em}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, using (1), the $y$-coordinate of P is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g(x) &= 2-8 \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) \\[12pt]
    &= 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$
