# AA780

**Maximum mark: 5**

Let $f(x) = -x^2+2x+3$. The graph of $f$ is shown in the following
diagram.

![9b151d369be7a9d5947f25e37a1d88fcf6e38455.svg](images/9b151d369be7a9d5947f25e37a1d88fcf6e38455.svg)

1.  Find $\displaystyle \int (-x^2+2x+3) \,\mathrm{d}x$. [2 marks]

2.  Find the area of the shaded region. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Integrating term by term, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \displaystyle \int (-x^2+2x+3) \,\mathrm{d}x & = -\dfrac {x^3} 3 + \dfrac {2x^2} 2 + 3x + C \\[12pt]
    & \hspace{.275em}= -\dfrac {x^3} 3 +x^2 + 3x + C
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_{1}^{2} (-x^2+2x+3) \,\textrm d x &=\left(-\dfrac{x^3} 3 +x^2+ 3x\right)\Bigg|_{1}^{2}  \\[12pt]
    & = \left[-\dfrac{(2)^3} 3 + (2)^2+3(2)\right]-\left[-\dfrac{(1)^3} 3 + (1)^2 +3(1)\right]\\[12pt]
    & = \dfrac{22}{3} - \dfrac{11}{3} \\[12pt]
    &\hspace{.275em}=\dfrac{11}{3}
    %&\hspace{.275em} = 32
    \end{aligned}
    $$
