# AA1064

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the two curves $y=2x^2$ and $y=x^2$.

Region $A$ is the area enclosed by the two curves, from the origin to the line $x=a$, where $a\in \mathbb{R^+}$. This is shown below.

![aa1064a](images/aa1064a.PNG)

1. Show that the area of $A$ is $\frac{1}{3}a^3$.[1 mark]

Consider a third curve $y=kx^2$,  where $k>2, k\in \mathbb{R}$.

Region $B$ is the  area enclosed by the new curve and $y=2x^2$, from the origin to the line $y=b$, where  $b\in \mathbb{R}$. This is shown below.

![aa1064b](images/aa1064b.PNG)

2. Find $k$, where $k \in \mathbb{Q}$, such that the area of region B equals the area of region A for all values of $a$.[7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. We can find area $A$ by evaluating the following definite integral

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \int_{0}^{a} \left(2x^2-x^2\right)dx&= \int_{0}^{a} x^2dx\\[3pt]

        &=\left[\frac{1}{3}x^3\right]_{0}^{a} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\tfrac{1}{3}a^3} \hspace{3em}\text{...as required}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. We need to find a function for the area of region $B$ in terms of $a$.

    To do this we need to integrate up the $y-$axis. We can find $x(y)$ for the two curves forming region $B$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        y&=2x^2 \hspace{3em} \text{becomes } x=\sqrt{\frac{y}{2}}\\

        y&=kx^2 \hspace{3em} \text{becomes } x=\sqrt{\frac{y}{k}}\\

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence we can represent the area of region $B$ with the following integral

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \int_{0}^{b} \left(\sqrt{\frac{y}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{y}{k}}\right)dy \tag*{\textbf{(M1)(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    However, before we integrate we need to change the limits so that they are in terms of $a$.

    The point $(a, b)$ is on the curve $y=2x^2$, hence $b=2a^2$. We can now evaluate the definite integral

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        &=\int_{0}^{2a^2} \left(\sqrt{\frac{y}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{y}{k}}\right)dy \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[3pt]

        &=\int_{0}^{2a^2} \left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{y} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sqrt{y}\right)dy\\[3pt]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    By factorising the integral it is easier to integrate

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        &=\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}- \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\int_{0}^{2a^2} \sqrt{y}\,\,dy\\[3pt]

        &=\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}- \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\left[\tfrac{2}{3}y^{\tfrac{3}{2}}\right]_{0}^{2a^2} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can substitute the limits into the integral to find the area of region $B$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        &=\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}- \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\left(\tfrac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}a^3\right)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Form an equation such that the area of region $A$ equals the area of region $B$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{1}{3}a^3&=\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}- \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\left(\tfrac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}a^3\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Solve for $k$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{1}{3}a^3&=\frac{4}{3}a^3- \frac{4\sqrt{2}a^3}{3\sqrt{k}} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Rearrange and factorise

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        0&=a^3-\frac{4\sqrt{2}a^3}{3\sqrt{k}}\\[3pt]

        0&=a^3\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{k}}\right)\\[3pt]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We are told in the question that $a\neq0$ therefore the only solution is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        1-\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{k}}&=0\\[3pt]

        1&=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{k}}\\[3pt]

        3\sqrt{k}&=4\sqrt{2}\\[3pt]

        9k&=16\times 2\\[3pt]

        k&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\frac{32}{9}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
