# AA1176

**Maximum mark: 17**

The following implicit relation describes the ellipse shown below
$$
\begin{aligned}
    x^2-2xy+4y^2&=8
\end{aligned}
$$

![AA1176_a](images/AA1176_a.JPEG)

The ellipse is centered on the origin and has rotational symmetry of order two.

1.  Show that $\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{x-y}{x-4y}$. [3 marks]

2.  1.  Hence show that the equation of the horizontal lines which are tangent to the ellipse are $y=\pm\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}$.

    2.  Use a similar method to find the equation of the  vertical lines which are tangent to the ellipse. [4 marks]

Consider the top part of the ellipse shown below

![AA1176_q2](images/AA1176_q2.JPEG)

3.  Show that this can be explicitly defined as the following function of $x$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f(x) = \frac{x}{4} + \sqrt{\frac{32-3x^2}{16}} \tag*{[4]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

Region $A$ is above the $y-$axis enclosed by the ellipse, this is shown below.

![AA1176_q3](images/AA1176_q3.JPEG)

4.  Find the area of region $A$. [3 marks]

A company wants to create a logo by inscribing the ellipse inside a rectangle.

The region inside the rectangle but outside of the ellipse is labeled as region $B$, this is shown below.

![AA1176_q4](images/AA1176_q4.JPEG)

5.  Find the size of region $B$. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We must use implicit differentiation and the product rule
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (x^2-2xy+4y^2)'&=(8)'\\[6pt]
        2x-\left(2y+2x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)+8y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=0 \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}\\[6pt]
        2x-2y-2x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+8y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=0\\[6pt]
        \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}(8y-2x)&=2y-2x\\[6pt]
        \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=\frac{2y-2x}{8y-2x}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &=\displaystyle\frac{x-y}{x-4y} \hspace{2em} \text{... as required.}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  1.  Recognise that for horizontal tangents $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{x-y}{x-4y}&=0\\[12pt]
        x-y&=0\\[6pt]
        x&=y \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can substitute this into the relation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y^2-2y \times y+4y^2&=8\\[6pt]
    y^2-2y^2+4y^2&=8 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3y^2&=8\\[6pt]
    y&=\pm \sqrt{\frac{8}{3}}\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.525em}y=\pm \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{6} \hspace{2em} \text{... as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Both of these tangents are shown in the image below, recall that $x=y$ when $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$

    ![AA1176_a1](images/AA1176_a1.JPEG)

    2.  Recognise that for vertical tangents $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ is undefined. This means the denominator of the quotient must be zero, hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            x-4y&=0 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
            y&=\tfrac{1}{4}x
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Again, lets substitute this into the relation
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        x^2-2x \times \left(\tfrac{1}{4}x\right)+4\left(\tfrac{1}{4}x\right)^2&=8 \\[6pt]
        x^2-\tfrac{x^2}{2}+\tfrac{x^2}{4}&=8\\[6pt]
        4x^2-2x^2+x^2&=32\\[6pt]
        x&=\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{32}{3}}\\[6pt]
        &=3.265...\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{-0.555em}x=\pm \,3.27 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        These two new tangents are added to the previous image and shown again below, recall that $y=\tfrac{1}{4}x$ when $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ is undefined.

        ![AA1176_a2](images/AA1176_a2.JPEG)

3.  Considering the original relation, we need to make $y$ the subject
     $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x^2-2xy+4y^2&=8\\[6pt]
        4y^2-2xy&=8-x^2\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now complete the square on the L.H.S. to isolate the $y$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        4\left(y^2-\tfrac{1}{2}xy\right)&=8-x^2\\[6pt]
        4\left[\left(y-\tfrac{x}{4}\right)^2-\tfrac{x^2}{16}\right]&=8-x^2 \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}\\[6pt]
        4\left(y-\tfrac{x}{4}\right)^2-\tfrac{x^2}{4}&=8-x^2\\[6pt]
        4\left(y-\tfrac{x}{4}\right)^2&=8-\tfrac{3}{4}x^2\\[6pt]
        \left(y-\tfrac{x}{4}\right)^2&=2-\tfrac{3}{16}x^2\\[6pt]
        \left(y-\tfrac{x}{4}\right)^2&=\frac{32-3x^2}{16}\\[6pt]
        y-\frac{x}{4}&=\pm \sqrt{\frac{32-3x^2}{16}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As we want the upper part of the ellipse we must take the positive square root, therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x}{4}+\sqrt{\frac{32-3x^2}{16}} \hspace{2em} \text{... as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Consider the following image which has the two points on the vertical tangents labelled $S$ and $T$ and it shows the area enclosed by $f(x)$ and the $x-$axis.

    ![AA1176_a3](images/AA1176_a3.JPEG)

    If we integrate $f(x)$ from $S$ to $T$ we obtain the signed area. Notice in the image below that the red region will be negative and the blue region positive.

    ![AA1176_a4 (1)](<images/AA1176_a4 (1).JPEG>)

    As the ellipse is symmetrical centred at the origin the red region is the same size as the blue one.

    Therefore the red region cancels out the blue region. Meaning the signed area is the area of region $A$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A&=\int_{-\sqrt{\tfrac{32}{3}}}^{\sqrt{\tfrac{32}{3}}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\[12pt]
        &=\int_{-\sqrt{\tfrac{32}{3}}}^{\sqrt{\tfrac{32}{3}}}\left(\frac{x}{4}+\sqrt{\frac{32-3x^2}{16}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}\\[12pt]
        &=7.255...\\[6pt]
        &=7.26 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
5.  To find the area of region $B$ we must subtract the area of the ellipse from the area of the rectangle.

    Using the answers to part (b) we can find the dimensions of the rectangle.

    ![Screenshot 2024-08-28 at 10.51.58 PM](<images/Screenshot 2024-08-28 at 10.51.58 PM.png>)

    The width of the rectangle is $2 \times \sqrt{\frac{32}{3}}$ and the height is $2 \times \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}$. Therefore we can find the area
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{\text{rectangle}}&=2 \times \sqrt{\tfrac{32}{3}} \times 2 \times \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{6} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        &=6.531... \times 3.265...\\[6pt]
        &=21.33...
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the answer to part (d) we can find the area of the ellipse.

    Due to symmetry the area of the ellipse is $2\times A$. Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        B&=A_{\text{rectangle}}-2 \times A\\[6pt]
        &=21.33... - 2 \times 7.255... \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &=6.822...\\[6pt]
        &=6.82\hspace{0.525em} \,\text{units}^2 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
