# AA531

**Maximum mark: 8**

Let $g(x) = \dfrac{8-2x}{\sqrt{9+8x-x^2}}$. The following diagram shows
part of the graph of $g$.

![5fa0f1e41fdbe2be7df3ce222628aa65ca20900b.svg](images/5fa0f1e41fdbe2be7df3ce222628aa65ca20900b.svg)

The region $R$ is enclosed by the graph of $g$, the $x$-axis, and the
$y$-axis. Find the area of $R$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

First, we need to find a point where $g$ intersects the $x$-axis

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{8-2x}{\sqrt{9+8x-x^2}}   & = 0\hspace{2.5cm}\\[3pt]
8 - 2 x                     & = 0\\[3pt]
x                       & = 4.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence the area of $R$ is
given by the integral
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.5cm}
A       & = \int_0^4 \dfrac{8-2x}{\sqrt{9+8x-x^2}}\,\textrm dx\\[6pt]
& = \int_0^4 u^{-1/2}\,\textrm d u \qquad\quad
[\mbox{with } u = 9+8x-x^2]\\[6pt]
& = 2 u^{1/2}\Big|_0^4\\[6pt]
& = 2 \sqrt{9+8x-x^2}\Big|_0^4\\[6pt]
& = 2 \sqrt{25} - 2 \sqrt 9\\[10pt]
&\hspace{.275em} = 4
\end{aligned}
$$
