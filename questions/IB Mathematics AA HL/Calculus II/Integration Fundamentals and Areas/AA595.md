# AA595

**Maximum mark: 18**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac {\ln (8 x^3)} {k x}$ where $x > 0$,
$k \in \mathbb R^+$.

1.  Show that $f'(x) = \dfrac {3 - \ln(8 x^3)} {k x^2}$. [4 marks]

The graph of $f$ has exactly one maximum point A.

2.  Find the $x$-coordinate of A.[3 marks]

The second derivative of $f$ is given by
$f''(x) = \dfrac {2 \ln (8 x^3) - 9} {kx^3}$. The graph of $f$ has
exactly one point of inflexion B.

3.  Show that the $x$-coordinate of B is
    $\dfrac {e^{3/2}} {2}$. [3 marks]

The region $R$ is enclosed by the graph of $f$, the $x$-axis, and the
vertical lines through the maximum point A and the point of inflexion B.

![b5db3dac8f98ade85e6b992c4d531177ea19402b.svg](images/b5db3dac8f98ade85e6b992c4d531177ea19402b.svg)

4.  Given that the area of $R$ is $5$, find the value of $k$.
    [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the quotient rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = \dfrac {kx \cdot \dfrac {8(3x^2)} {8 x^3} - k \cdot \ln(8x^3)} {(kx)^2}\\[8pt]
    & = \dfrac {3k - k \ln(8x^3)} {k^2x^2}\\[15pt]
    &\hspace{.275em}  = \dfrac {3 - \ln(8x^3)} {kx^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  A maximum on $f$ occurs when $f'(x) = 0$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac {3 - \ln(8x^3)} {kx^2}   & = 0\hspace{2.6cm}\\[8pt]
    \ln(8x^3)                   & = 3\\[8pt]
    8x^3                    & = e^3\\[8pt]
    x^3                     & = \dfrac {e^3} {8}\\[10pt]
    & \hspace{-2mm} x = \dfrac e 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The point of inflection occurs when $f''(x) = 0$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac {2 \ln (8 x^3) - 9} {kx^3}  & = 0\\[6pt]
    2 \ln (8 x^3) - 9  & = 0\\[6pt]
    \ln(8x^3)                      & = \dfrac 9 2\\[6pt]
    8 x^3                      & = e^{9/2}\\[6pt]
    x^3                            & = \dfrac {e^{9/2}} 8\\[10pt]
    &  \hspace{-2mm}
    x = \dfrac {e^{3/2}} 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  An expression for the area of $R$ can be found by the integral
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_R         & = \int_{\frac e 2}^{\frac {e^{3/2}}2} \dfrac {\ln(8x^3)} {kx}\,
    \textrm d x \hspace{3cm}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Integrating by
    substitution with $u = \ln(8x^3)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_R     & = \int_3^{\frac9 2} \dfrac u {3k}\,\textrm d u \hspace{4cm}\\[6pt]
    & = \dfrac 1 {3k} \left[\dfrac {u^2}2\right]_3^{\frac 9 2}\\[6pt]
    & = \dfrac 1 {3k} \left[ \dfrac {81}8 - \dfrac 9 2\right]\\[6pt]
    & = \dfrac {15} {8k}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    It was given that $A_R = 5$. Hence we can solve for $k$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac {15} {8k}    & = 5\hspace{5cm}\\[6pt]
    8k              & = 3\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-2mm}  k  = \dfrac 3 8
    \end{aligned}
    $$
