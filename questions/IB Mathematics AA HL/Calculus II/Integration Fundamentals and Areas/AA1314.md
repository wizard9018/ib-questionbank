# AA1314

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the functions $f(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}$ and $g(x)=6-8e^{-x}$, where $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

It is known when $x=a$, where $a>0$, that $f'(a)=g'(a)$.

1.    Show that $a=\ln 2$. [3 marks]

The functions $f$ and $g$ have one intersection point at $x=\ln 2$.

2.    Find the area enclosed by the line $x=0$, the graph of  $f$ and the graph of $g$.

     Express your answer in the form $a-b\ln{c}$ where $a \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $b,c \in \mathbb{Z}$. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. First let's differentiate $f(x)$ and $g(x)$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(x)&=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}&&g(x)=6-8e^{-x}\\[6pt]

    f'(x)&=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2x}\times 2&&g'(x)=-8e^{-x}\times (-1)\\[6pt]

    &=e^{2x}&&\hspace{1em}=8e^{-x}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now, because we know that $f'(a)=g'(a)$, lets set the derivative equations equal and substitute $x=a$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    e^{2a}&=8e^{-a}\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    To solve for $a$ let's apply the natural logarithm to both sides.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \ln e^{2a}&=\ln (8e^{-a})

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Then we can simplify using logarithm rules.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2a&=\ln 8+\ln e^{-a}\\[6pt]

    2a&=\ln 8-a\\[6pt]

    3a&=\ln 8\\[6pt]

    3a&=\ln 2^3\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[6pt]

    3a&=3\ln 2\\[6pt]

    a&=\ln 2\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. To find the area between two curves we use the formula 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A=\int_a^b{(\text{upper curve}-\text{lower curve})}\,\mathrm{d}x

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    where $a$ and $b$ are the upper and lower limits.

    As we are told that the two curves intersect only once, at $x=\ln{2}$, we can be confident that one curve consistently lies above the other between the limits of $x=0$ and $x=\ln{2}$.

    This means the graphs do not cross again between $x=0$ and $x=\ln2$ and we do not need to worry about the area being partially negative, the difference between the upper and lower curves will remain positive throughout.

    Let's consider the $y$-values at $x=0$ so we can determine which function is the upper curve.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(0)&=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2(0)}&&g(0)=-8e^{-(0)}+6\\[9pt]

    &=\tfrac{1}{2}&&\hspace{1.7em}=-2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Since $f(0)>g(0)$ this means $f(x)$ is the upper function.

    Now we can write the equation to find the area.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A&=\int_a^b{\bigg(f(x)-g(x)}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x\\[12pt]

    &=\int_0^{\ln2}\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}-(-8e^{-x}+6)\right)\mathrm{d}x\tag*{\textbf{{(M1A1)}}}\\[12pt]

    &=\int_0^{\ln 2}\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}+8e^{-x}-6\right)\mathrm{d}x

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Next we can integrate using the inverse power rule and the standard integral

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \int e^{x}\ \mathrm{d}x&=e^{x}+c

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    and accounting for the coefficients in the exponents we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A&=\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{2x}+8\left(\frac{1}{-1}\right)e^{-x}-6x\right]_0^{\ln 2}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Evaluating the upper and lower boundaries

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    A&=\left[\frac{1}{4}e^{2(\ln2)}-8e^{-\ln2}-6(\ln2)\right]-\left[\frac{1}{4}e^{2(0)}-8e^{0}-6(0)\right]\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Applying the logarithm rule from part (a) to the exponents

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\left[\frac{1}{4}e^{\ln2^2}-8e^{\ln2^{-1}}-6\ln2\right]-\left[\frac{1}{4}-8\right]\\[12pt]

    &=\left[\frac{1}{4}e^{\ln4}-8e^{\ln{\normalsize\frac{1}{2}}}-6(\ln2)\right]-\left[-\frac{31}{4}\right]

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the fact that $e^{\ln a}=a$ we can simplify further

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\frac{1}{4}(4)-8\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)-6\ln2+\frac{31}{4}\\[6pt]

    &=1-4-6\ln2+\frac{31}{4}\\[6pt]

    &=\dfrac{19}{4}-6\ln2\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This is the form that the question has asked for therefore $a=\tfrac{19}{4}$, $b=6$ and $c=2$.
