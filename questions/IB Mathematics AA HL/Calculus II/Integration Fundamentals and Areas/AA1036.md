# AA1036

**Maximum mark: 17**

The quadratic function $f(x)$ has a vertex at $(h, -2)$.

1.  The function can be written in the form of $f(x) = a(x-h)^2+k$.

    1.  If the equation of the axis of symmetry of $f(x)$ is $x=3$, write down the values of $h$ and $k$.

    2. If the $y-$intercept of $f(x)$ is $-11$, find $a$. [4 marks]

A new function $g(x)$ is defined such that $g'(x)=f(x)$.

2.  1.  Show that the function $f(x)$ has no real roots.

    2.  Hence, or otherwise, justify that $g(x)$ is a decreasing function. [3 marks]

The function $f(x)$ is vertically translated such that it now has $2$ distinct roots, $x_1$ and $x_2$, where $x_2>x_1$. The coordinates of the new vertex is $(3, m)$. Where $m>0$.

3.  Show that $x_1=3- \sqrt{m}$ and $x_2=3+ \sqrt{m}$. [2 marks]

The area enclosed by $f(x)$ and the $x-$axis is $\frac{32}{3}$ units.

4.  1.  Use the substitution $u=x-3$ to find $\displaystyle\int f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x$ in terms of $m$.

    2.  Hence find $m$. [6 marks]

5.  Hence, or otherwise, find the region(s) of $x$, for which $g(x)$ is a decreasing function. [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  Written in vertex form the point $(h, k)$ is the location of the vertex, additionally, the vertex lies on the axis of symmetry.

        Hence $\hspace{0.525em}h=3\hspace{0.525em}$ and $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{k=-2}\hspace{0.525em}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \tag*{\textbf{A1A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.  From the question we are told $f(0)=-11$, hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            -11&=a(0-3)^2-2  \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
            -9&=9a\\[6pt]
            &\hspace{-0.525em}a=-1\hspace{0.525em}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  1.  We can use the discriminant to show that $f(x)$ has no real roots.

        But first, we need to expand and simplify $f(x)$ into the general form.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            f(x)&=-(x-3)^2-2\\[6pt]
            &=-(x^2-6x+9)-2\\[6pt]
            &=-x^2+6x-11
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now using the coefficients $a, b$ and $c$ we can find the value of the discriminant
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \triangle &= b^2-4ac  \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
            &= (6)^2-4(-1)(-11)\\[6pt]
            &=36-44\\[6pt]
            &=-8
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence $\hspace{0.525em}f(x) \text{ has no real roots as } \triangle < 0$.
        $$
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        $$
        $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

    2.  As $f(x)$ has no real roots and $a<0$ we can say that $f(x)$ is entirely below the $x-$axis.

        Here is a sketch to help visualise the function

        ![a1036-1](images/a1036-1.png)

        As $g'(x)=f(x)$, $\text{the gradient of } g(x) \text{ is always negative}$ making $g(x)$ a decreasing function.
        $$
        \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        $$
        $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

3.  After the translation we can write $f(x)=-(x-3)^2+m$.

    Here is a new sketch of $f(x)$ with the new features identified.

    ![a1036-2](images/a1036-2.png)

    The roots are found by solving $f(x)=0$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=-(x-3)^2+m \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        (x-3)^2&=m\\[6pt]
        x-3&=\pm \sqrt{m}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{-0.525em}x_1=3 - \sqrt{m} \text{ and } x_2=3 + \sqrt{m}\hspace{0.525em} \hspace{3em} \text{...as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
4.  1.  Using the substitution $u=x-3$ we find that $\frac{du}{\mathrm{d}x}=1$, hence $\mathrm{d}u=\mathrm{d}x$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
           \int f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x&= \int \left(-(x-3)^2+m\right)\,\,\mathrm{d}x \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
           &=\int \left(-u^2+m\right)\,\,\mathrm{d}u\\[6pt]
           &=-\frac{1}{3}u^3+mu+c\\[6pt]
           &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-\frac{1}{3}(x-3)^3+m(x-3)+c} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.  We are told that the area enclosed by $f(x)$ and the $x-$axis is $\frac{32}{3}$ we can visualise this, with the shaded area below being equal to $\dfrac{32}{3}$

        ![a1036-3](images/a1036-3.png)

        hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{x_1}^{x_2}f(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x&=\frac{32}{3} \\[6pt]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Using the integral we found in part (i) and the roots we find in part (c) we can write
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \left[-\frac{1}{3}(x-3)^3+m(x-3)\right]_{3-\sqrt{m}}^{3+\sqrt{m}}&=\dfrac{32}{3} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Note, we do not need to include the constant of integration $c$, as this is a definite integral.\\[6pt]
        Next we can substitute in the limits of integration, simplify and then solve for $m$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            -\frac{1}{3}(3+\sqrt{m}-3)^3+m(3+\sqrt{m}-3) -&\\[6pt]
            \left(-\frac{1}{3}(3-\sqrt{m}-3)^3+m(3-\sqrt{m}-3)\right) &=\frac{32}{3}\\[6pt]
            -\frac{1}{3}(\sqrt{m})^3+m\sqrt{m}-\left(-\frac{1}{3}(-\sqrt{m})^3-m\sqrt{m}\right)&=\frac{32}{3} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
            -\frac{2}{3}(\sqrt{m})^3+2m\sqrt{m}&=\frac{32}{3}\\[6pt]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Rewrite the radicals into index form
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            -\frac{2}{3}m^{\frac{3}{2}}+2m^{\frac{3}{2}}&=\frac{32}{3} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[6pt]
            -2m^{\frac{3}{2}}+6m^{\frac{3}{2}}&=32\\[6pt]
            4m^{\frac{3}{2}}&=32\\[6pt]
            m^{\frac{3}{2}}&=8\\[6pt]
            m&=8^{\frac{2}{3}}\\[6pt]
            &\hspace{-0.525em}\boxed{m=4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
5.  Using the answer from part (c) and (d)(ii) we know that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x_1&=3-\sqrt{4}\\
        &=1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x_2&=3+\sqrt{4}\\
        &=5 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $g(x)$ is a decreasing function when the value of its gradient is negative.

    Here is a sketch of $f(x)$ with the roots identified

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
         \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{x<1}\hspace{0.525em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{x>5}\hspace{0.525em} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
