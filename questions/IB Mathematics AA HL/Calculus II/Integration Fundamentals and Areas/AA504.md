# AA504

**Maximum mark: 16**

Consider the function $f(x) = \dfrac{ae^{-x}}{b - ae^{-x}}$ where
$a > 0$, $b > 0$.

1.  Show that $f'(x) = \dfrac{-abe^{-x}}{(b-ae^{-x})^2}$. [3 marks]

2.  Explain why $f''(x)$ is never zero. [5 marks]

3.  Find the equation of:
    1.  the vertical asymptote of $f$;

    2.  the horizontal asymptote of $f$. [2 marks]

4.  Draw a sign diagram for $f'(x)$. [1 mark]

5.  If $a = 3$ and $b = 1$,
    1.  sketch the graph of $f$ labelling all asymptotes;

    2.  find the area of the region enclosed by $f$, the $x$ and $y$
        axes and the line $x = \ln 2$. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the quotient rule to differentiate $f(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = \dfrac{(-ae^{-x})(b-ae^{-x}) - (ae^{-x})(ae^{-x})}{(b-ae^{-x})^2} \\[3pt]
    & =  \dfrac{-abe^{-x} + \cancel{a^2e^{-2x}} - \cancel{a^2e^{-2x}}}{(b-ae^{-x})^2} \\[3pt]
    & =  \dfrac{-abe^{-x}}{(b-ae^{-x})^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Using the quotient rule to differentiating $f'(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f''(x)      & = \dfrac{(abe^{-x})(b-ae^{-x})^2 - (-abe^{-x})\big[2(b-ae^{-x})(ae^{-x})\big]}{(b-ae^{-x})^4} \\[3pt]
    & = \dfrac{(abe^{-x})(b-ae^{-x}) - (-abe^{-x})(2ae^{-x})}{(b-ae^{-x})^3} \\[3pt]
    & = \dfrac{ab^2e^{-x} - a^2be^{-2x} + 2a^2be^{-2x}}{(b-ae^{-x})^3} \\[3pt]
    & = \dfrac{ab^2e^{-x} + a^2be^{-2x}}{(b-ae^{-x})^3} \\[3pt]
    & = \dfrac{abe^{-x}(b + ae^{-x})}{(b-ae^{-x})^3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since $a > 0$, $b > 0$, $e^{-x} > 0$, we can see that $f''(x)$
    cannot be zero for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

3.
    1.  The equation of the vertical asymptote of $f$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        b - ae^{-x}         & = 0 \\[6pt]
        b               & = ae^{-x} \\[6pt]
        be^x            & = a \\[6pt]
        e^{x}           & = \dfrac{a}{b} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{1.4cm}x  = \ln\Big(\dfrac{a}{b}\Big)
        $$

    2.  Note that
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(x)        & = \dfrac{ae^{-x}}{b - ae^{-x}} \cdot \dfrac{e^x}{e^x} \\[3pt]
        & = \dfrac{a}{be^x - a}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence we have

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(x) \rightarrow 0  &\text{ as } x \rightarrow \infty \\[3pt]
        &\text{ and } \\[3pt]
        f(x) \rightarrow -1     &\text{ as }  x \rightarrow -\infty
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the equations of the horizontal asymptotes are
          $y = 0 \mbox{ and } y = -1$

4.  Here is the sign diagram for $f'(x)$:

    |   $x$   | $-\infty < x < \ln\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)$ | $\ln\left(\frac{a}{b}\right) < x < \infty$ |
    |:-------:|:-----------------------------------------------------------:|:----------------------------------------------------------:|
    | $f'(x)$ |                             $-$                             |                            $-$                             |

5.  For $a = 3$ and $b = 1$, we have
    $\boxed{f(x) = \dfrac{3e^{-x}}{1 - 3e^{-x}}}$

    1.  Here is the sketch of the graph of $y = f(x)$:

        ![112ee9b78075e5e4511dd3aaefa8ffc03c70a8f1.svg](images/112ee9b78075e5e4511dd3aaefa8ffc03c70a8f1.svg)

    2.  We have
        $$
        {
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{Area}     & =  - \int_0^{\ln 2} f(x)\, \textrm dx \\[3pt]
        & =  -\int_0^{\ln 2} \dfrac{3e^{-x}}{1 - 3e^{-x}}\, \textrm dx \quad \quad \quad [\text{let $u = 1 - 3e^{-x}$}] \\[3pt]
        & = -\int_{-2}^{-\frac{1}{2}} \dfrac{1}{u}\, \textrm du \\[3pt]
        & =  - \Big[\ln |u|\Big] \Big|_{-2}^{-\frac{1}{2}} \\[8pt]
        %           & = - \Big[\ln \Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big) - \ln 2\Big] \\[9pt]
        & = \ln 2 - \ln \Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big) \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{.275em} = \ln 4 \text{ unit}^2
        \end{aligned}
        }
        $$
