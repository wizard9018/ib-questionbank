# AA727

**Maximum mark: 18**

A cannonball is fired from the top of a tower. The rate of change of the height, $h$, of the cannonball above the ground is modelled by

$$
\begin{aligned}
h'(t) = -4t + 20, \hspace{0.5em} t \geq 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
where $h$ is in metres and $t$ is the time, in seconds, since the moment the cannonball was fired.

1.  Determine the time $t$ at which the cannonball reached its maximum height. [2 marks]

After one second, the cannonball is 26 metres above the ground.

2.  1. Find an expression for $h(t)$, the height of the cannonball above the ground at time $t$.

    2. Hence, determine the maximum height reached by the cannonball. [5 marks]

3.  Write down the height of the tower. [1 mark]

4.  Calculate the height of the cannonball $4$ seconds after it was fired. [2 marks]

The cannonball hits its target on the ground $n$ seconds after it was fired.

5.  Find the value of $n$. [2 marks]

6.  Determine the total time the cannonball was above the height of the tower.[3 marks]

A second cannonball is fired from exactly halfway up the tower, with the same projectile motion as the first cannonball.

7. Given that both cannonballs land at the same time, determine the length of time between the first cannonball and the second cannonball being fired. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The time at which the cannonball reaches maximum height is found by solving $h'(t)=0$. We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    h'(t) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    -4t + 20 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.275em}\boxed{t = 5 \text{ s}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  1. Given that $h(t)=\int h'(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        h(t) &= \int{-4t+20}\,\mathrm{d}t \\[6pt]
        &= -2t^2 +20t + C
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Given that $h=26$ when $t=1$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        h(1) &= -2(1)^2 +20(1) + C \\[6pt]
        26 &= -2 + 20 + C \\[6pt]
        C &= 8
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Therefore $\hspace{0.575em}\boxed{h(t) = -2t^2 +20t + 8}$.

    2. We must calculate $h(5)$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        h(5) &= -2(5)^2 +20(5) + 8 \\[6pt]
        &= 58
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        The maximum height reached is $\hspace{0.575em}\boxed{58 \text{ metres}}$.

3.  Evaluating $h(t)$ for $t = 0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.7em} h(0) &= -2(0)^2+20(0)+8 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.275em}\boxed{= 8 \text{ m}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Evaluating $h(t)$ for $t = 4$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.7em} h(5) &= -2(4)^2+20(4)+8 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{0.275em}\boxed{= 56 \text{ m}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Solving the equation $h(n) = 0$ for $n > 0$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -2n^2+20n+8 &= 0 \hspace{9em} \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.8em}\boxed{n \approx 10.4} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  Solving the equation $h(t) = 8$ for $t > 0$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -2t^2+20t+8 &= 8 \hspace{8.6em} \\[6pt]
    -2t^2+20t &= 0 \\[6pt]
    &\hspace{-0.8em} \boxed{t = 10 \text{ seconds}} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7. Given that the second cannonball follows the same projectile motion as the first cannonball, the equation is the same as $h(t)$, but with a vertical translation of 4 units down (the second cannonball is launched from a height of 4 metres instead of 8 metres). We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    h_2(t) &= -2t^2 +20t + 4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Where $h_2(t)$ is the height of the second cannonball above the ground, in metres, at time $t$ seconds.

    Solving the equation $h_2(t) = 0$ for $t > 0$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -2t^2+20t+4 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    t &\approx 10.20 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The second cannonball will take $10.2$ seconds to land, whereas the first cannonball will take $10.4$ seconds.

    Therefore, the second cannonball was fired $\hspace{0.25em}\boxed{0.2 \text{ seconds later}}$.
