# AA480

**Maximum mark: 20**

Consider the function defined by $f(x) = (1-x)\sqrt{2x - x^2}$ where
$0 \leq x \leq 2$.

1.  Show that $f(1 - x) = - f(1 + x)$, for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$.
    [3 marks]

2.  Find $f'(x)$. [4 marks]

3.  Hence find the $x$-coordinates of any local minimum or maximum
    points. [3 marks]

4.  Find the range of $f$. [3 marks]

5.  Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$, indicating clearly the coordinates
    of
    $\text{the \(x\)-intercepts}$ and any local maximum or minimum
    points. [3 marks]

6.  Find the area of the region enclosed by the graph of $y = f(x)$ on
    the $x$-axis, for $0 \leq x \leq 1$. [2 marks]

7.  Show that
    $\displaystyle \int_0^2 \left|f(x)\right|\,dx > \left|\int_0^2 f(x)\,dx\right|$.
    [2 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  For any $-1 \leq x \leq 1$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} f(1-x) &= (1-(1-x))\sqrt{2(1-x) - (1-x)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= x\sqrt{(2-2x) - (1-2x + x^2)} \\[6pt]
    &= x\sqrt{1 - x^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Similarly, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} f(1+x) &= (1-(1+x))\sqrt{2(1+x) - (1+x)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= -x\sqrt{(2+2x) - (1+2x + x^2)} \\[6pt]
    &= -x\sqrt{1- x^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, for any $-1 \leq x \leq 1$,
    we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(1-x) &= x\sqrt{1 - x^2} \\[6pt]
    &= -\big[\hspace{-0.15em}-x\sqrt{1 - x^2}\big] \hspace{0.4em} \\[9pt]
    &= - f(1+x)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Differentiating $f$ with respect to $x$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[(1-x)\sqrt{2x - x^2}\big] \\[10pt]
    [\text{by differentiation rules}] \hspace{2em} &= -\sqrt{2x-x^2} + \dfrac{(1-x)(2-2x)}{2\sqrt{2x-x^2}} \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
    &= -\sqrt{2x-x^2} + \dfrac{(1-x)^2}{\sqrt{2x-x^2}} \\[6pt]
    [\text{since $0 < x < 2$}] \hspace{2em} &= \dfrac{-(2x-x^2) + (1-2x + x^2)}{\sqrt{2x-x^2}} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{2x^2 - 4x + 1}{\sqrt{2x-x^2}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Solving the equation $f'(x) = 0$ for $x$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{2x^2 - 4x + 1}{\sqrt{2x-x^2}} &= 0 \hspace{9em} \\[6pt]
    2x^2 - 4x + 1 &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{11.1em} x \approx 0.293,\hspace{0.25em} 1.71 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    $$

4.  Evaluating $f(x)$ for the critical numbers $x = 0.293$ and
    $x = 1.71$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.2em} f(0.293) &= (1-0.293)\sqrt{2(0.293) - (0.293)^2} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.5 \\[12pt]
    f(1.71) &= (1-1.71)\sqrt{2(1.71) - (1.71)^2} \\[6pt]
    &\approx -0.5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Evaluating $f(x)$ for the end points
    $x = 0$ and $x = 2$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.5em} f(0) &= (1-0)\sqrt{2(0) - (0)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= 0 \\[12pt]
    f(2) &= (1-2)\sqrt{2(2) - (2)^2} \\[6pt]
    &= 0 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the range of $f$ is
     $[-0.5, 0.5]$

5.  The local maximum and minimum points on the graph of $y = f(x)$
    are $\mathrm{P}(0.293,0.5)$ and
    $\mathrm{Q}(1.71,-0.5)$, respectively.
    Solving the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8em} (1-x)\sqrt{2x - x^2} &= 0 \\[8pt]
    x &= 0,\hspace{0.25em} 1,\hspace{0.25em} 2 \hspace{2.8em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the $x$-intercepts of the graph of $y = f(x)$ are
    $\mathrm{A}(0,0)$, $\mathrm{B}(1,0)$
    and $\mathrm{C}(2,0)$.

    P and Q, we have

6.  Evaluating the integral of $f(x)$ from $a = 0$ to $b = 1$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A &= \int_0^1 (1-x)\sqrt{2x-x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{3} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.5em} \int_0^2 f(x)\,dx &= \int_0^2 (1-x)\sqrt{2x-x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x \\[12pt]
    &= 0 \hspace{3.45em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.2em} \int_0^2 |f(x)|\,dx &= \int_0^2 |1-x|\sqrt{2x-x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{2}{3} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \int_0^2 \left|f(x)\right|\,dx = \dfrac{2}{3} > \left|\int_0^2 f(x)\,dx\right| = 0 \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
