# AA530

**Maximum mark: 15**

Let $f(x) = 2e^{\frac{x}{5}}$ and $g(x) = mx$, where $m\geq0$, and
$-6\leq x \leq 6$. Let $R$ be the region enclosed by the $y$-axis, the
graph of $f$, and the graph of $g$.

1.  Let $m=2$.
    1.  Sketch the graphs of $f$ and $g$ on the same axes.

    2.  Find the area of $R$. [7 marks]

2.  Consider all values of $m$ such that the graphs of $f$ and $g$
    intersect. Find the value of $m$ that gives the greatest value for
    the area of $R$. [8 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Graphics of $f$ and $g$ are shown in the diagram below

        ![07778bd6161f620485d0a1023e79e4d2a671e73f.svg](images/07778bd6161f620485d0a1023e79e4d2a671e73f.svg)

    2.  First let's find the $x$-coordinate of the point of intersection
        of $f$ and $g$, A, shown in the diagram
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3cm}
        2 e^{\frac x 5}         & = 2 x\\[5pt]
        x               & = 1.296 \qquad\quad \mbox{[by using G.D.C.]}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        We choose the value of $x$ that is closer to the $y$-axis,
        $x = 1.296$.
        Hence, the area of $R$ is given by the integral

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{.5cm}
        A_R         & = \int_0^{1.296} \left(2 e^{\frac x 5} - 2x\right)\,\textrm d x\\[8pt]
        &\hspace{.275em} = 1.28
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  The area of $R$ gets its maximum when $g$ is a tangent line to $f$
    and $m = f'(x_1)$, where $(x_1,y_1)$ is a point on $f$ and $g$. We
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = \dfrac 2 5 e^{\frac x 5}\hspace{1.6cm}\\[5pt]
    m       & = \dfrac 2 5 e^{\frac {x_1} 5}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the fact that $f(x_1) = g(x_1)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2cm}
    2e^{\frac {x_1} 5}  & = \dfrac 2 5 e^{\frac {x_1} 5}x_1\\[5pt]
    x_1             & = 5\\[5pt]
    m               & = \dfrac 2 5 e^{\frac 5 5}\\[5pt]
    &\hspace{.275em}  = 1.09
    \qquad\quad \mbox{[by using G.D.C.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
