# AA544

**Maximum mark: 16**

Let $f(x) = x^2 - 3x + 2$, for $x \in \mathbb{R}$. The following diagram
shows part of the graph of $f$.

![d183574a6a692dbdc1dc3e0fc2c20b11fc58cca9.svg](images/d183574a6a692dbdc1dc3e0fc2c20b11fc58cca9.svg)

The graph of $f$ crosses the $x$-axis at the point P$(1,0)$ and at the
point Q$(2,0)$.

1.  Show that $f'(1) = -1$. [3 marks]

The line $L$ is the normal to the graph of $f$ at P.

2.  Find the equation of $L$ in the form $y = mx + c$.
    [3 marks]

The line $L$ intersects the graph of $f$ at another point R, as shown in
the following diagram.

![34a951b9f58cf4f39cef2ae8f1bab13da582edcc.svg](images/34a951b9f58cf4f39cef2ae8f1bab13da582edcc.svg)

3.  Find the $x$-coordinate of R. [4 marks]

4.  Find the area of the region enclosed by the graph of $f$
    and the line $L$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)   & = 2 x - 3\\[6pt]
    f'(1)   & = 2(1) - 3\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{.275em} = -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The equation of the normal $L$ is $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ with
    $m = -\dfrac {1}{-1}$ (by part (a)), $y_1 = 0$, and $x_1 = 1$, hence
      $y = x - 1$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^2 - 3x + 2    & = x - 1\\[6pt]
    x^2 - 4x + 3    & = 0\\[6pt]
    (x - 3) (x - 1)     & = 0\\[6pt]
    x           & = 3,\,1\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{.275em}x = 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  The area of the required region is given by the integral
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A       & = \int_1^3 (x - 1) - (x^2 - 3x + 2)\,\textrm d x\\[6pt]
    & = \int_1^3 -x^2 + 4x - 3\,\textrm d x\\[6pt]
    & = \left[-\dfrac {x^3} 3 + 2x^2 - 3 x\right]_1^3\\[6pt]
    & = [-9 + 18 - 9] - \left[-\dfrac 1 3 + 2 - 3 \right]\\[6pt]
    & \hspace{.275em} = \dfrac 4 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
