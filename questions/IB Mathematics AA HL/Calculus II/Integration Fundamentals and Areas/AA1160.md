# AA1160

**Maximum mark: 19**

Consider the function
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f(x)&=\tfrac{1}{4}x^2-x+3
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  Write $f(x)$ in the form $a(x-h)^2+k$. Where $a, h, k \in \mathbb{Q}$. [3 marks]

2.  Hence, find $\displaystyle \int f(x)\,dx$. [2 marks]

A rectangle $ABCD$ has vertices shown below.

![grid](images/grid.JPEG)

$f(x)$ cuts the rectangle into two parts.

The part above the curve is labelled $U$ and the part below the curve is labelled $L$.

3.  1.  Verify the point (4,3) lies on $f(x)$.

    2.  Hence, or otherwise, sketch $f(x)$ on the diagram above and identify the parts $U$ and $L$. [2 marks]

4.  1.  Write down the area of rectangle $ABCD$.

    2.  Hence, show that the area of $U$ is twice the area of $L$. [5 marks]

The rectangle $ABCD$ is modified such that point $C(b, f(b))$ is a variable point on the function $f(x)$. Where $b>2$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}$.

The point $A(2, 2)$ is fixed in the same position as previously.

5.  Find the area of the new rectangle in terms of $b$.[2 marks]

6.  Find $\displaystyle \int_{2}^{b}f(x)\,dx$ in terms of $b$.[1 mark]

7.  Hence, or otherwise, prove that the area of $U$ is twice the area of $L$ for any position of point $C$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  By completing the square, we can rewrite $f(x)$ into the desired form
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f(x)&=\tfrac{1}{4}(x^2-4x)+3\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{4}[(x-2)^2-2^2]+3\tag*{\textbf{(M1)A1}}\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{4}(x-2)^2-1+3\\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.2em}=\tfrac{1}{4}(x-2)^2+2\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  We can use the inverse power rule to integrate the function
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \int f(x)\,dx&=\int {\left[\tfrac{1}{4}(x-2)^2+2\right]}\,dx\\[12pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{12}(x-2)^3+2x+C \tag*{\textbf{A1A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    2.  We can recognise that the area of region $U$ can be found by subtracting the area under the curve from $A$ to $C$ from the red dashed rectangle, this is shown below

    Using the answer from part (b) and remembering that for definite integrals the constant $C$ cancels out

5.  Here is a sketch of the new rectangle with the variable point $C$.

    ![AA1160_q (1)](<images/AA1160_q (1).JPEG>)

    Let the area of the new rectangle be $A_{R}$.

    The width of the rectangle is $b-2$ and the height is $f(b)-2$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_{R}&=(f(b)-2)(b-2)\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
        &=(\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^2+2-2)(b-2)\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^2(b-2)\\[6pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^3\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  Using the answer to part (b) we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \int_{2}^{b}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x&=\left[\tfrac{1}{12}(x-2)^3+2x\right]_{2}^{b}\\[6pt]
        &=\left(\tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3+2b\right)-\left(\tfrac{1}{12}(2-2)^3+2\times 2\right)\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3+2b-4 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  Consider the red rectangle shown in the following image

    ![AA1160_a3](images/AA1160_a3.JPEG)

    Using our answer from part (a) we can evaluate $f(b$) and see that the area of the red rectangle in terms of $b$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (b-2)f(b)&=(b-2)\left(\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^2+2\right)\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^3+2(b-2)\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^3+2b-4 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the answer from part (f) we can now write an expression for the area of region $U$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_U&=\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^3+2b-4 - \int_{2}^{b}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\[6pt]
    &=\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^3+2b-4 - \left(\tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3+2b-4\right)\\[6pt]
    &=\tfrac{1}{4}(b-2)^3\cancel{+2b-4} - \tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3\cancel{-2b+4}\\[6pt]
    &=\tfrac{1}{6}(b-2)^3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Consider the blue rectangle shown in the image below

    ![AA1160_a4](images/AA1160_a4.JPEG)

    We can see that the area is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2(b-2)&=2b-4
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We can find the area of region $L$ by subtracting the area of the blue triangle from the area under the curve.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A_L&=\int_{2}^{b}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x - (2b-4)\\[6pt]
        &=\left(\tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3+2b-4\right)-(2b-4)\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3\cancel{+2b-4}\cancel{-2b+4}\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We now have expressions for $A_U$ and $A_L$ in terms of $b$, which is the $x-$coordinate of point $C$. Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tfrac{1}{6}(b-2)^3&=2 \times \tfrac{1}{12}(b-2)^3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
        A_U&=2 \times A_L
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    $\text{The area of region }U\text{ is twice the area of region }L\text{ for any position of point C.}$
