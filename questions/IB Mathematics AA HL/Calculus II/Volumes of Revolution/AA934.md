# AA934

**Maximum mark: 20**

Consider the function $f(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{10}{x^2}-1}$, where $1 \leq x \leq \sqrt{10}$.

1. Sketch the curve $y=f(x)$, indicating the coordinates of the endpoints. [2 marks]

2. 1. Show that $f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{10}{x^2+1}}$.

    2. State the domain and range of $f^{-1}$. [5 marks]

The curve $y=f(x)$ is rotated through $2\pi$ about the $y$-axis to form a solid of revolution that is used to model a vase.

3. 1. Show that the volume $V$ cm$^3$, of liquid in the vase when it is filled to a height of $h$ centimetres is given by $V=10\pi\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(h)$.

    2. Hence, determine the volume of the vase. [5 marks]

At $t=0$, the vase is filled to its maximum volume with water. Water is then removed from the vase at a constant rate of $4\hspace{0.15em}\text{cm}^3\hspace{0.15em}\text{s}^{-1}$.

4. Find the time it takes to completely empty the vase. [2 marks]

5. Find the rate of change of the height of the water when half of the water has been emptied from the vase. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using G.D.C., we get

2. 1. If we interchange $x$ and $y$, we have $x = \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{y^2}-1}$.

        Rearranging this equation to make $y$ the subject we get

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x^2 &= \dfrac{10}{y^2}-1 \\[10pt]

        x^2 + 1 &= \dfrac{10}{y^2} \\[10pt]

        y^2 &= \dfrac{10}{x^2+1} \\[10pt]

        y &= \pm\sqrt{\dfrac{10}{x^2+1}} \\[10pt]

        &\hspace{-3em}\boxed{f^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{x^2+1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. From part (a), we note that the domain of $f$ is $1 \leq x \ \leq \sqrt{10}$ and the range of $f$ is $0 \leq f(x) \leq 3$. 

        Given that the range of a function is equal to the domain of its inverse, the domain of $f^{-1}$ is $0 \leq x \leq 3$

        Given that the domain of a function is equal to the range of its inverse, the range of $f^{-1}$ is $1 \leq f^{-1}(x) \leq \sqrt{10}$

3. 1. The volume of the solid formed by rotating $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis is given by

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        V &= \int_a^b\pi\hspace{0.15em}x^2\,\mathrm{d}y

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Rearranging $f(x)$ to make $x^2$ the subject, we get

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        y &= \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{x^2}-1} \\[10pt]

        y^2 &= \dfrac{10}{x^2}-1 \\[10pt]

        y^2 + 1 &= \dfrac{10}{x^2} \\[10pt]

        x^2 &= \dfrac{10}{y^2+1}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Substituting this expression for $x^2$ into the volume formula, with $a=0$ and $b=h$, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        V     & = \int_0^h \dfrac{10\pi}{y^2+1}\,\mathrm{d}y \\[8pt]

        & = 10\pi  \int_0^h \dfrac{1}{y^2+1}\,\mathrm{d}y  \\[8pt]

        & =  10\pi \bigg[\hspace{0.15em} \mathrm{arctan}(y) \hspace{0.15em}\bigg]_{0}^{h}\\[8pt]

        & = 10\pi \big[\hspace{0.15em} \mathrm{arctan}(h) -  \mathrm{arctan}(0)\hspace{0.15em}\big] \\[8pt]

        &  \hspace{-1.1em}\boxed{V=10\pi\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(h)}\hspace{2em}\text{...as required}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. The volume of the vase is found by substituting the value $h=3$ into the equation found in part (c)(i).

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        V & = 10\pi\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(3)  \\[8pt]

        & \approx  39.2

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The volume of the vase is approximately $39.2\,\text{cm}^3$

4. The time taken to empty the vase is found by dividing the volume by the rate at which it is emptying. We have $V\approx39.2$ and rate $=4$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    t &= \dfrac{39.2}{4}\\[8pt]

    &= 9.8

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    It takes $9.8\, \text{seconds}\,$ to completely empty the vase.

5. When half the water has been emptied, there is $19.6\,\text{cm}^3$ remaining. The height of the water at this time can be found using the volume equation from part (c)(i).

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    V & = 10\pi\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(h)  \\[8pt]

    19.6 &= 10\pi\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(h)  \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{19.6}{10\pi} &= \text{arctan}(h) \\[8pt]

    h &= \text{tan}\bigg(\dfrac{19.6}{10\pi}\bigg) \\[8pt]

    &= 0.7197...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We need to find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $h=0.7197...$. We have $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}=-4$, given that the volume of water in the vase is reducing. 

    By differentiating our volume equation we can obtain an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h}$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    V & = 10\pi\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(h)  \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \dfrac{10\pi}{1 + h^2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Relating $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h}$ and $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}$, we obtain an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}V} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}\\[8pt]

    &= \dfrac{1+h^2}{10\pi} \times -4

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When $h=0.7198$ we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{1+(0.7197...)^2}{10\pi} \times -4\\[8pt]

    &= -0.1932...\\[6pt]

    &=-0.193

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    At the time when the vase has been half emptied of water, the rate of change of the height of the water is approximately $-0.193\, \text{cm}\,\text{s}^{-1}$
