# AA470

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the ellipse defined by the equation $x^2 + 3y^2  = 12$.

1.  Find the equation of the normal to the ellipse at the point
    P$(3,1)$. [5 marks]

2.  Find the volume of the solid formed when the region bounded by the
    ellipse, the $x$-axis for $x\geq0$ and the $y$-axis for $y\geq0$ is
    rotated through $2\pi$ about the $y$-axis. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using implicit differentiation, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.3em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[x^2 + 3y^2\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[12\big] \\[6pt]
    2x + 6yy' &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by chain rule}] \\[9pt]
    6yy' &= -2x \\[6pt]
    y' &= -\dfrac{x}{3y} \hspace{3em} [\text{for $y \neq 0$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Evaluating the gradient of the tangent to the ellipse at P, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg|_{\substack{x=3\\[1pt]\hspace{0.025em}y=1}} &= -\dfrac{3}{3(1)} \hspace{1em} \\[6pt]
    &= -1 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the gradient of the normal to the
    ellipse at P is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.6em} m &= -\dfrac{1}{(-1)} \\[6pt]
    &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the point-gradient formula with
    P$(3,1)$ and $m = 1$, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.1em} y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[6pt]
    y - 1 &= (1)(x - 3) \\[6pt]
    y - 1 &= x - 3 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{1.1em} y = x - 2
    $$

2.  If we draw the region bounded by the ellipse, the $x$-axis for
    $x\geq0$ and the $y$-axis for $y\geq0$, we
    have

    ![419ccb0adc60ba05e9238d66ea775e2ae02b3750.svg](images/419ccb0adc60ba05e9238d66ea775e2ae02b3750.svg)

    Hence, using the volume of revolution formula, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.3em} V &= \int_0^2 \pi x^2\,\mathrm{d}y \\[6pt]
    &= \pi \int_0^2 \big(12- 3y^2\big)\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{d}y \\[12pt]
    &= \pi(16) \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[17pt]
    &= 16\pi
    \end{aligned}
    $$
