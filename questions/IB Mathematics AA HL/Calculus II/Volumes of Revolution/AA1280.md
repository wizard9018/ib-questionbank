# AA1280

**Maximum mark: 8**

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=\displaystyle \frac{\sqrt{1-9x^2}}{2x}$ for $x> 0$.

The region $R$ is bounded by the curves $y=f(x)$ and the lines $x=0$ and $y=0$ as shown in the following diagram.

![AA1280q](images/AA1280q.JPEG)

The shape of a solid clay sculpture can be modeled by rotating the region $R$ though $2\pi$ radians about the $y-$axis.

The top edge of the sculpture has coordinates of $\left(\tfrac{1}{6},k\right)$.

The volume of clay used to make the sculpture is $a\pi^2\,\text{units}^2$. Where $a \in \mathbb{Q}$.

Find a.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

In this question we are rotating region $R$ about the $y-$axis.

According to the formula booklet rotation about the $y-$axis requires the following formula

$$

\begin{aligned}

    V&=\pi \int_a^b x^2\,\mathrm{d}y

\end{aligned}

$$

This means we need to find $x=f(y)$ hence we need to rewrite $y=f(x)$ to make $x$ the subject.

$$

\begin{aligned}

     \tag*{\textbf{M1}}

\end{aligned}

$$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    y&=f(x)\\[12pt]

    y&=\frac{\sqrt{1-9x^2}}{2x}

\end{aligned}

$$

Let's begin by squaring both sides and then rewriting the expression

$$

\begin{aligned}

    y^2&=\frac{1-9x^2}{4x^2}\\[12pt]

    4x^2y^2&=1-9x^2\\[12pt]

    1&=4x^2y^2+9x^2

\end{aligned}

$$

Now we can factorise the R.H.S. and make $x$ the subject

$$

\begin{aligned}

    1&=x^2(4y^2+9)\\[12pt]

    x^2&=\frac{1}{4y^2+9}\\[12pt]

    x&=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4y^2+9}}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}\\[6pt]

    &=f(y)

\end{aligned}

$$

Now substitute this expression into the formula mentioned to get

$$

\begin{aligned}

    V&=\pi \int_a^{b} \left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{4y^2+9}}\right)^2\,\mathrm{d}y\\[12pt]

    &=\pi \int_a^{b} \frac{1}{4y^2+9}\,\mathrm{d}y

\end{aligned}

$$

Next let's consider the limits.

We have been given the $x-$coordinate of the edge of the vase but, as we are integrating up the $y-$axis, we need to find $k$, the $y-$coordinate.

Hence, we find this by evaluating $f$ at $x=\tfrac{1}{6}$

$$

\begin{aligned}

    k&=f(\tfrac{1}{6})\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}\\[6pt]

    &=\frac{\sqrt{1-9\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right)^2}}{2\left(\tfrac{1}{6}\right)}\\[12pt]

    &=\frac{\sqrt{1-9\times\tfrac{1}{36}}}{\tfrac{1}{3}}\\[12pt]

    &=3\sqrt{1-\tfrac{1}{4}}\\[6pt]

    &=3\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{4}}\\[6pt]

    &=\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{3}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Hence our expression for the volume with the correct limits is

$$

\begin{aligned}

    V&=\pi \int_0^{\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{3}} \frac{1}{4y^2+9}\,\mathrm{d}y

\end{aligned}

$$

We now have an expression, with limits in terms of $y$, which we are integrating with respect to $y$. 

Notice that the integral looks similar to the following standard integral in the formula booklet

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \int \frac{1}{a^2+x^2}\,\mathrm{d}x&=\frac{1}{a}\arctan{\frac{x}{a}}+C

\end{aligned}

$$

However, there are some differences, the first one is that our integral is a function of $y$ but, as we are integrating with respect to $y$ then we can ignore this difference.

The other difference is we have a composite function for $y$. As we need to be more careful here, we should use a substitution to make sure we perform the integral correctly.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    V&=\pi \int \frac{1}{3^2+(2y)^2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\end{aligned}

$$

Let $u=2y$ therefore $\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}y}=2$

Ignoring the limits for now and using $\mathrm{d}y=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u$ we can rewrite the integral in terms of $u$ to get

$$

\begin{aligned}

     V&=\pi \int \frac{1}{3^2+u^2}\times \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}u\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}\\[12pt]

     &=\frac{\pi}{2} \int \frac{1}{3^2+u^2}\,\mathrm{d}u

\end{aligned}

$$

Using the standard integral with $a=3$ we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

     &=\frac{\pi}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}\arctan{\left(\frac{u}{3}\right)}+C

\end{aligned}

$$

Writing our answer back in terms of $y$ we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

     &=\frac{\pi}{6} \arctan{\left(\frac{2y}{3}\right)}+C\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Let's now find the volume by re-introducing the limits.

$$ 

\begin{aligned}

     &=\frac{\pi}{6} \left[\arctan{\left(\frac{2y}{3}\right)}\right]_0^{\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{3}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Recall, the constant of $C$ will cancel out and we can factorise out any common factors.

Substituting in the upper and lower limits we get

$$

\begin{aligned}

     &=\frac{\pi}{6} \left[\arctan{\left(\frac{2(\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{3})}{3}\right)}-\arctan{\left(\frac{2(0)}{3}\right)}\right]\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}\\[12pt]

     &=\frac{\pi}{6}\arctan{\sqrt{3}}\\[6pt]

     &=\frac{\pi}{6} \times \frac{\pi}{3}\\[6pt]

     &=\frac{\pi^2}{18}\text{ units}^3

\end{aligned}

$$

Therefore $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{a=\dfrac{1}{18}}$

$$

\begin{aligned}

     \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$
