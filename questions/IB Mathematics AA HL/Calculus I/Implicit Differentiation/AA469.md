# AA469

**Maximum mark: 7**

The curve $C$ is defined by the equation $x^2y+\ln(xy)=1$, $x>0$, $y>0$.

1.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ in terms of $x$ and $y$.
    [4 marks]

2.  Determine the equation of the tangent to $C$ at the point P$(1,1)$.
    [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using implicit differentiation, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[x^2y+\ln (xy)\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[1\big] \\[6pt]
    2xy + x^2y' + \dfrac{y + xy'}{xy} &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by differentiation rules}] \\[9pt]
    \hspace{3em} 2x^2y^2 + x^3yy' + y + xy' &= 0 \\[15pt]
    (x + x^3y)y' &= -(y + 2x^2y^2) \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{3.9em} y' = -\dfrac{y + 2x^2y^2}{x + x^3y}
    $$

2.  Evaluating the gradient of the tangent to $C$ at P, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.9em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg|_{\substack{x=1\\[1pt]\hspace{0.025em}y=1}} &= -\dfrac{1 + 2(1)^2(1)^2}{1 + (1)^3(1)} \\[5pt]
    &= -\dfrac{1+2}{1+1} \\[11pt]
    &= -\dfrac{3}{2} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the point-gradient
    formula with P$(1,1)$ and $m = -3/2$, we
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.3em} y - y_1 &= m(x - x_1) \\[10pt]
    y - 1 &= -\dfrac{3}{2}(x - 1) \\[6pt]
    y - 1 &= -\dfrac{3}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{2} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{2.9em} y =  -\dfrac{3}{2}x + \dfrac{5}{2}
    $$
