# AA1345

**Maximum mark: 9**

The curve $C$ has equation $2x^2-xy+y^2+ax=3$. Where $a \in \mathbb{R}^+$.

This is shown in the following diagram.

![AA1345-q](images/AA1345-q.JPEG)

1.    Show that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{y-4x-a}{2y-x}$ [3 marks]

The domain of $C$ is $p \leq x \leq q$ where $p,q \in \mathbb{Q}$.

Given that $q-p=\dfrac{20}{7}$

2.    find the value of $a$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. This is an implicitly defined relation.

    Hence, to differentiate it, we must use implicit differentiation.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Recall, that if $y=f(x)$ then due to the chain rule, when we differentiate a function of $y$ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}[f(y)]&=f'(y)\times\frac {\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using this, we differentiate the relation term by term, to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(2x^2)-\textcolor{red}{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(xy)}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(y^2)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(ax)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(3)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note we will need to use the product rule for the term in $\textcolor{red}{\text{red}}$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    4x-\left(y+x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)+2y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+a&=0\\[12pt]
    4x-y-x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+2y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+a&=0\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next we collect the terms involving $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ to one side and factorise
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -x\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}+2y\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=y-4x-a\\[12pt]
    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}(2y-x)&=y-4x-a\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-1.7em}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{y-4x-a}{2y-x}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required.}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. The first important thing to note about curve $C$ is that the boundaries of its domain occur when $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ is undefined.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{(R1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    ![AA1345-a1](images/AA1345-a1.JPEG)

    Here, $p$ and $q$ represent the lower and upper boundary respectively.
    Using the answer from part (a)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=\frac{y-4x-a}{2y-x}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    we can see that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ is undefined when
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2y-x&=0\\[6pt]
    y&=\frac{1}{2}x\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    To find the $x-$coordinates of the domain boundary we must substitute $ y=\dfrac{1}{2}x$ into the equation for $C$ and solve for $x$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2x^2-x\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)^2+ax&=3\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[12pt]
    2x^2-\frac{1}{2}x^2+\frac{1}{4}x^2+ax-3&=0\\[12pt]
    \frac{7}{4}x^2+ax-3&=0\\[12pt]
    7x^2+4ax-12&=0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the quadratic formula we can find the two roots of $x$ in terms of $a$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x_{1,2}&=\frac{-4a\pm\sqrt{(4a)^2-4 \times 7 \times (-12)}}{2 \times 7}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{-4a\pm\sqrt{16a^2+336}}{14}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    In the question, we are told that $q-p=\dfrac{20}{7}$.
    The smaller root is $p$, and the larger one is $q$.
    We can find the difference of the two roots
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    q-p&=x_2-x_1\\[6pt]
    &=\frac{-4a+\sqrt{16a^2+336}}{14}-\left(\frac{-4a-\sqrt{16a^2+336}}{14}\right)\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{-4a+\sqrt{16a^2+336}+4a+\sqrt{16a^2+336}}{14}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{\sqrt{16a^2+336}}{7}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using this result with the information given, we form the equation
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\sqrt{16a^2+336}}{7}&=\frac{20}{7}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    which we can solve using a G.D.C., using $x$ instead of $a$, to get

    ![AA1345-a3](images/AA1345-a3.JPEG)

    Recall that $a \in \mathbb{R}^+$, hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    a&=2\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
