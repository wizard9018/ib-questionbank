# AA978

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the curve, $C$, described by the equation: 

$$

\begin{aligned}

(x^2+y^2)(y^2-9) + 9y^2=0  \text{ where }x > 0 \text{ and } y > 0.

\end{aligned}

$$

Show that the curve has no local maximum or local minimum points.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Expanding the brackets on the left hand side, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    x^2y^2 - 9x^2 + y^4 \cancel{- 9y^2 + 9y^2} = 0

\end{aligned}

$$

We can differentiate implicitly, term by term, to find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(x^2y^2 - 9x^2 + y^4\right) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}0 \\[8pt]

    2xy^2 + 2x^2y\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} - 18x + 4y^3\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 0 \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(2x^2y + 4y^3\right) &= 18x - 2xy^2 \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{18x - 2xy^2}{2x^2y + 4y^3}

\end{aligned}

$$

In order for a local maximum or local minimum to exist, we must have $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{18x - 2xy^2}{2x^2y + 4y^3} &= 0 \\[8pt]

    18x - 2xy^2 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    2x(9-y^2) &= 0

\end{aligned}

$$

This means possible maxima or minima when $2x=0$ or $9-y^2=0$. But $x>0$ hence $2x\neq0$, so the only possibility is $9-y^2=0$.

$$

\begin{aligned}

    y^2 &= 9 \\[8pt]

    y &= 3 \hspace{3em}\text{[$y>0$]}

\end{aligned}

$$

Substituting $y=3$ into the original equation, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    x^2(3)^2 - 9x^2 + (3)^4 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    9x^2 - 9x^2 + 81 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    81 &\neq 0

\end{aligned}

$$

We have a contradiction. 

Therefore $\boxed{\text{the curve has no local maximum or local minimum points.}}$
