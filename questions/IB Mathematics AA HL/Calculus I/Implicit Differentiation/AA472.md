# AA472

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the curve defined by the equation $x^5 + y^5 = 5x^2y$.

1.  Show that
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{2xy - x^4}{y^4 - x^2}$.
    [3 marks]

The normal to this curve is parallel to the $y$-axis at the point where
$x = h$, $h > 0$.

2.  Find the value of $h$, giving your answer correct to two
    decimal places. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using implicit differentiation, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[x^5 + y^5\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[5x^2y\big] \\[10pt]
    5x^4 + 5y^4y' &= 5(2xy + x^2y') \hspace{2em} [\text{by differentiation rules}] \\[12pt]
    x^4 + y^4y' &= 2xy + x^2y' \\[12pt]
    (y^4 - x^2)y' &= 2xy - x^4 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{0.15em} y' = \dfrac{2xy - x^4}{y^4 - x^2}
    $$

2.  The normal to the curve that is parallel to the $y$-axis has
    gradient $0$.

    Hence we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{2xy - x^4}{y^4 - x^2} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{4.3em} 2xy - x^4 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    y &= \dfrac{x^3}{2} \hspace{3em} [\text{for $x \neq 0$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, substituting $x = h$ and $y = h^3\hspace{-0.15em}/2$ in the
    equation of the curve, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^5 + y^5 &= 5x^2y \\[10pt]
    \hspace{4.7em}  h^5 + \dfrac{h^{15}}{32} &= \dfrac{5h^5}{2} \\[6pt]
    1 + \dfrac{h^{10}}{32} &= \dfrac{5}{2} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $h > 0$}] \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{11.3em} h \approx 1.47 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    $$
