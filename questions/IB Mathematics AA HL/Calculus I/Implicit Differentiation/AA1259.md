# AA1259

**Maximum mark: 10**

Consider the curve $y^2 - xy = x^3$, where $x \neq 0$ and $y \neq 0$.

1.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$.[3 marks]

2.  Find the coordinates of the point on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the $x$-axis.[3 marks]

3.  Find the coordinates of the point on the curve where the tangent is parallel to the $y$-axis.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Differentiating both sides implicitly with respect to $x$ and applying the **product rule (5.6)**, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(uv) = u\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} + v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}$, to the term $xy$ with
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        u &= x, & \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 1 \\[6pt]
        v &= y, & \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2y\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} - \left(x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} + y\right) &= 3x^2 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Collecting the derivative terms on one side, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (2y - x)\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 3x^2 + y \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Making the derivative the subject, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \boxed{\dfrac{3x^2 + y}{2y - x}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The tangent is parallel to the $x$-axis where $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 0$, so we let the numerator $3x^2 + y = 0$,  giving $y = -3x^2$. Substituting into the equation of the curve, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (-3x^2)^2 - x(-3x^2) &= x^3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
        9x^4 + 3x^3 &= x^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Rearranging, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        9x^4 + 2x^3 &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x^3(9x + 2) &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since $x \neq 0$, we have $x = -\dfrac{2}{9}$. Substituting into $y = -3x^2$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y &= -3\left(-\dfrac{2}{9}\right)^2 \\[10pt]
        &= -3 \cdot \dfrac{4}{81} \\[10pt]
         y &= -\dfrac{4}{27}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    so the point is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \boxed{\left(-\dfrac{2}{9},\,-\dfrac{4}{27}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  The tangent is parallel to the $y$-axis where $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ is undefined, so we let the denominator $2y - x = 0$, giving $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$. Substituting into the equation of the curve, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)^2 - x\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) &= x^3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[10pt]
        \dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{x^2}{2} &= x^3 \\[10pt]
        -\dfrac{x^2}{4} &= x^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Rearranging, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x^3 + \dfrac{x^2}{4} &= 0 \\[10pt]
        x^2\left(x + \dfrac{1}{4}\right) &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since $x \neq 0$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x &= -\dfrac{1}{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting into $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y &= \dfrac{1}{2}\left(-\dfrac{1}{4}\right) \\[10pt]
        &= -\dfrac{1}{8}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    so the point is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \boxed{\left(-\dfrac{1}{4},\,-\dfrac{1}{8}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
