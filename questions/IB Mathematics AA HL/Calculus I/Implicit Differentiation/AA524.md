# AA524

**Maximum mark: 19**

A curve $C$ is given by the implicit equation $x - y + \sin(xy) = 0$.

1.  Show that
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{1+y\cos(xy)}{1-x\cos(xy)}$.
    [5 marks]

2.  The curve $xy = \pi$ intersects $C$ at P and Q.
    1.  Find the coordinates of P and Q.

    2.  Given that the gradients of the tangents to $C$ at P and Q are
        $m_1$ and $m_2$ respectively, show that
        $m_1\cdot\hspace{0.1em} m_2 = 1$. [7 marks]

3.  Find the coordinates of the three points on $C$, nearest to the
    origin, where the tangent $\text{is~parallel}$ to the line
    $y = x$. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we dif$\text{f}$erentiate the equation of the curve $C$
    implicitly with respect to $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[x - y + \sin(xy)\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[0\big] \\[8pt]
    \hspace{0.3em} 1 - \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} + \cos(xy)\bigg[y + x\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg] &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by chain rule}] \\[6pt]
    (1 + y\cos(xy)) &= (1 - x\cos(xy))\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} \\[8pt]
    &\hspace{-1.3em}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{1 + y\cos(xy)}{1 - x\cos(xy)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  If we substitute $xy = \pi$ in the equation of the curve $C$, we
        have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        x - y + \sin(xy) &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x - y + \sin(\pi) &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x - y &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x^2 - xy &= x(0) \\[6pt]
        x^2 - \pi &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x &= \pm\sqrt{\pi} \hspace{3.2em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence,
        evaluating $y = \dfrac{\pi}{x}$ for $x = \pm\sqrt{\pi}$, we
        obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3.1em} y &= \dfrac{\pi}{\pm \sqrt{\pi}} \\[6pt]
        y &= \pm\sqrt{\pi}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence we find
          $\mbox P \left(-\sqrt{\pi},-\sqrt{\pi}\right) \mbox{ and Q}\left(\sqrt{\pi},\sqrt{\pi}\right)$

    2.  Evaluating $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ at P and Q, we
        have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        m_1 &= \dfrac{1 - \sqrt{\pi}\cos(\pi)}{1 + \sqrt{\pi}\cos(\pi)} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1+\sqrt{\pi}}{1-\sqrt{\pi}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        and

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        m_2 &= \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{\pi}\cos(\pi)}{1 - \sqrt{\pi}\cos(\pi)} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1 - \sqrt{\pi}}{1+\sqrt{\pi}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the
        product of the gradients $m_1$ and $m_2$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2em} m_1\cdot\hspace{0.1em} m_2 &= \bigg(\dfrac{\cancel{1+\sqrt{\pi}}}{\cancel{1-\sqrt{\pi}}}\bigg)\hspace{-0.04em}\cdot\bigg(\dfrac{\cancel{1 - \sqrt{\pi}}}{\cancel{1+\sqrt{\pi}}}\bigg) \\[8pt]
        &= 1
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Let A, B and C be the three points on $C$, nearest to the origin,
    where the tangent $\text{has gradient \(1\)}$.

    We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 1 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{1+y\cos(xy)}{1-x\cos(xy)} &= 1 \\[10pt]
    \hspace{4.1em} (x+y)\cos(xy) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by rearranging terms}] \\[16pt]
    x + y = 0 \hspace{0.5em} &\hspace{0.25em}\text{or} \hspace{0.5em} \cos(xy) = 0 \\[11pt]
    y = -x \hspace{0.5em} &\hspace{0.25em}\text{or} \hspace{0.5em} xy = \dfrac{\pi}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we substitute $y = -x$ in the equation of the curve $C$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x - y + \sin(xy) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x - (-x) + \sin(x(-x)) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    2x + \textstyle \sin(-x^2) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x &= 0 \hspace{3.3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we find  $\mbox A (0,0)$\
    If we substitute $xy = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ in the equation of the curve
    $C$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x - y + \sin(xy) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{6.6em} x - y + \sin(\pi\hspace{-0.05em}/2) &= 0 \\[10pt]
    x - y + 1 &= 0 \\[9pt]
    x^2 - xy + x &= x(0) \\[7pt]
    x^2 + x - \dfrac{\pi}{2} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x&\approx -1.85,\hspace{0.4em} 0.849 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, evaluating $y = \dfrac{\pi}{2x}$ for
    $x = -1.85,\hspace{0.4em} 0.849$, we obtain

     $$
     \begin{aligned}
     \hspace{13.2em} y &\approx -0.849,\hspace{0.4em} 1.85 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
     \end{aligned}
     $$

    Hence we find
     $\mbox B (-1.85,-0.849) \mbox{ and C}(0.849,1.85)$
