# AA474

**Maximum mark: 7**

A ladder of length $13$ m rests on horizontal
ground and leans against a vertical wall. The bottom of the ladder is
pulled away from the wall at a constant speed of
$1.2\hspace{0.25em}\text{m}\hspace{0.1em}\text{s}^{-1}$. Calculate the
speed of descent of the top of the ladder when the bottom is
$5$ m away from the wall.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let $y$ be the distance of the top of the ladder from the ground at time
$t$ seconds.

Using the Pythagorean theorem, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + y^2 &= 13^2 \\[6pt]
x^2 + y^2 &= 169
\end{aligned}
$$
Using implicit
differentiation, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[x^2 + y^2\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[169\big] \\[6pt]
\hspace{5.4em} 2x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}  + 2y\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by chain rule}] \\[6pt]
x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}  + y\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, substituting $x = 5$ in the equation $(1)$, we obtain

 $$
 \begin{aligned}
 \hspace{7.8em} 25 + y^2 &= 169 \\[6pt]
 y^2 &= 144 \\[7pt]
 y &= 12 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $y > 0$}]
 \end{aligned}
 $$
 Hence,
substituting $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = 1.2$, $x = 5$ and
$y = 12$ and in the equation $(2)$, we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5)(1.2) + 12\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 0 \\[3pt]
6 + 12\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 0 \\[3pt]
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t} &= - 0.5 \hspace{3.2em} \\
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence the speed of descent of the top of the ladder when $x = 5$ is
 $0.5\hspace{0.25em}\text{m}\hspace{0.1em}\text{s}^{-1}$
