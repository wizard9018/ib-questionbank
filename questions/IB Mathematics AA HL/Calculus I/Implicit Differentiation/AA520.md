# AA520

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the curves $C_1$ and $C_2$ defined as follows
 $$
 \begin{array}{rl}
 C_1: & 3y^2 + 2x^2 = 5,\hspace{0.25em} y > 0, \\[10pt]
 C_2: & y^2 - 5x^3 = 0,\hspace{0.25em} y > 0. \\
 \end{array}
 $$

1.  Using implicit dif$\text{f}$erentiation, or otherwise, find
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ for each curve in terms of $x$
    and $y$. [4 marks]

Let P$(a,b)$ be the unique point where the curves $C_1$ and $C_2$
intersect.

2.  Show that the tangent to $C_1$ at P is perpendicular to the
    tangent to $C_2$ at P.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using implicit dif$\text{f}$erentiation, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[3y^2 + 2x^2\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[5\big] \\[7pt]
    6y\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} + 4x &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by chain rule}]
    \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = -\dfrac{2x}{3y}
    $$
    and
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[y^2 - 5x^3\big] &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[0\big] \\[7pt]
    2y\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} - 15x^2 &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by chain rule}]  \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
     \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{15x^2}{2y}
    $$

2.  The slope of the tangent to $C_1$ at P is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m_1 &= -\dfrac{2a}{3b} \hspace{0.2em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The slope of
    the tangent to $C_2$ at P is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    m_2 &= \dfrac{15a^2}{2b}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the product of the
    slopes at P is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.6em} m_1m_2 &= \bigg(\hspace{-0.3em}-\dfrac{2a}{3b}\bigg)\bigg(\dfrac{15a^2}{2b}\bigg) \\[6pt]
    &= -\dfrac{5a^3}{b^2} \\[7pt]
    &= -\dfrac{5a^3}{5a^3} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $b^2 = 5a^3$}] \\[14pt]
    &= -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the tangent to $C_1$ at P is
    perpendicular to the tangent to $C_2$ at P.
