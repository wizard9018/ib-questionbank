# AA770

**Maximum mark: 17**

Consider the function $g(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$, where $x\in \mathbb{R}$, $a \neq 0$ and
$a$, $b$, $c$, $d \in \mathbb{R}.$

1.
    1.  Write down an expression for $g'(x)$.

    2.  Hence, given that $g^{-1}$ does not exist, show that
        $b^2-3ac>0.$[3 marks]

Consider the function
$f(x)=\dfrac{x^3}{2}+3x^2+6x+\dfrac{9}{2}$

2.
    1.  Show that $f^{-1}$ exists.

    2.  $f(x)$ can be written in the form $p\hspace{0.05em}(x+2)^3+q$,
        where $p$, $q\in \mathbb{R}$. Find the value of $p$ and the
        value of $q$.

    3.  Hence, find $f^{-1}(x)$.[7 marks]

The graph of $f(x)$ may be obtained by transforming the graph of $y=x^3$
using a sequence of three transformations.

3.  State each of the transformations in the order in which
    they are applied.[3 marks]

4.  Sketch the graphs of $y=f(x)$ and $y=f^{-1}(x)$ on the same
    set of axes, indicating the points where each graph crosses the
    coordinate axes.[4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Taking the derivative with respect to $x$, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        g'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\hspace{0.1em} \big[ ax^3+bx^2+cx+d \big] \\[12pt]
        &= 3ax^2+2bx+c
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Since $a \neq 0$, $g(x)$ is a cubic polynomial. For $g^{-1}$ not to exist, $g(x)$ cannot be one-to-one. Thus, $g'(x)$ must have **two distinct real roots** (so that the graph has two stationary points, a local maximum and a local minimum) which requires the discriminant of $g'(x)$ to be strictly positive $\left(\triangle > 0 \right)$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \big[\text{discriminant of $g'$}\big] &> 0 \\[12pt]
        (2b)^2 - 4(3a)(c) &> 0 \hspace{3.5em} [\triangle  = b^2 - 4ac] \\[12pt]
        4b^2 - 12ac &> 0 \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-1.25cm}b^2-3ac > 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  Using part (a) (ii), we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        (3)^2-3\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(6\right) & \ngtr 0.
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Therefore, by the contrapositive statement to part (a) (ii), we
        deduce $f^{-1}$ exists.

    2.  Factorizing the expression for $y=f(x)$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(x) &= \dfrac{1}{2}\left(x^3+6x^2+12x+9\right) \\[10pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}\left(x^3+6x^2+12x+8+1\right)  \\[10pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}\left(x+2\right)^3 + \dfrac{1}{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Therefore
        $p=\dfrac{1}{2}, \, q=\dfrac{1}{2}$

    3.  If we solve the equation $(f\circ f^{-1})(x) = x$ for
        $y = f^{-1}(x)$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(f^{-1}(x)) &= x \\[12pt]
        f(y) &= x \\[10pt]
        \dfrac{1}{2}(y+2)^3+\dfrac{1}{2} &= x \\[10pt]
        (y+2)^3 &= 2x - 1 \\[12pt]
        y+2 &= \sqrt[3]{2x-1} \\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-10.5mm}f^{-1}(x) = \sqrt[3]{2x-1} - 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  We get the following transformations
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{transformation $1$}&: \hspace{0.5em}\hspace{0.525em}\text{horizontal translation $2$ units left} \\[10pt]
    \text{transformation $2$}&:
    \hspace{0.5em}\hspace{0.525em}\text{vertical stretch by a factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$}\\[10pt]
    \text{transformation $3$}&: \hspace{0.5em}\hspace{0.525em}\text{vertical translation $\dfrac{1}{2}$ units up}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    *Note:* The horizontal translation can be performed in any step but
    the\
    vertical stretch should be performed before the vertical
    translation.
