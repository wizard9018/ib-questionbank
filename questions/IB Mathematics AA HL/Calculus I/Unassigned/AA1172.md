# AA1172

**Maximum mark: 6**

A company is designing an animated logo for its website.

The logo is created by drawing a rectangle around a sector with radius $r$ centimetres and angle $\theta$ radians. This is shown below.

![AA1172_q](images/AA1172_q.JPEG)

Let the area of rectangle $RSTU$ be $A$.

1.  Show that $A=r\sqrt{2r^2(1-\cos{\theta})}$ [2 marks]

To animate the logo the designer increases the angle $\theta$ by a constant rate of $0.5$ radians per second.

For the final logo design $r$ is fixed at $4\,$cm.

2.  If
    $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}=a\sqrt{b}$ where $a, b\in \mathbb{Z}$.

    Find $a$ and $b$ when $\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Recognise that the area of the rectangle is given by
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A&=VW\times r \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can use the Cosine rule to find $VW$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        VW^2&=r^2+r^2-2r^2\cos{\theta}\\[6pt]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        VW&=\sqrt{2r^2(1-\cos{\theta})}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A&=r\sqrt{2r^2(1-\cos{\theta})} \hspace{2em} \text{...as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  We can recognise that the three quantities $A, \theta$ and $t$ are changing.

    Hence we can relate them using the chain rule.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}&=\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\times \frac{\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We know from the question that $\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t}=0.5$ for all $\theta$.

    We need to find the value of $\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}\theta}$ when $\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}$.

    Recall that $r=4$ hence $A=4\sqrt{32(1-\cos{\theta})}$

    We can use the power and chain rule to differentiate $A$ with respect to $\theta$.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A&=4(32(1-\cos{\theta}))^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\\[6pt]
    \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}\theta}&=4(32(1-\cos{\theta}))^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\times \frac{1}{2} \times (32(1-\cos{\theta}))' \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The derivative of the inner function of $A$ shown above is $(32-32\cos{\theta})'=32\sin{\theta}$

    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}\theta}&=4(32(1-\cos{\theta}))^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\times \frac{1}{2} \times 32\sin{\theta}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{64\sin{\theta}}{\sqrt{32(1-\cos{\theta})}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We must evaluate this expression when $\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}$.

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}\theta}&=\frac{64\sin{\tfrac{\pi}{3}}}{\sqrt{32(1-\cos{\tfrac{\pi}{3}})}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{64\times \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{\sqrt{32(1-\tfrac{1}{2})}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{32\sqrt{3}}{4}\\[6pt]
    &=8\sqrt{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Recall
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}&=\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\times \frac{\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t}\\[12pt]
        &=8\sqrt{3}\times 0.5\\[6pt]
        &=4\sqrt{3} \,\text{cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore $a=4 \text{ and } b=3$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
