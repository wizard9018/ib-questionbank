# AA609

**Maximum mark: 7**

Use L'Hôpital's rule to determine the value of
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\to 0} \dfrac{2\sin^2(x)}{\ln(1+x^2)}
\end{aligned}
$$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0} 2\sin^2(x)&= 0 \\[7pt]
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0} \ln(1+x^2) &= 0\\
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, using
L'Hôpital's rule, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{2\sin^2(x)}{\ln(1+x^2)}  &= \lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{4\sin(x)\cos(x)}{\dfrac{2x\rule[0pt]{0pt}{3mm}}{1+x^2}} \\[6pt]
&= \lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{(1+x^2)2\sin(2x)}{2x} \\
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0} (1+x^2)2\sin(2x) &= 0 \\[7pt]
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0} 2x &= 0 \\
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, using L'Hôpital's rule, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{(1+x^2)2\sin(2x)}{2x}  &= \lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{(1+x^2)4\cos(2x)+4x\sin(2x)}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \lim_{x\rightarrow0} \dfrac{(1+0^2)4\cos(0)+4(0)\sin(0)}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{4}{2} \\[8pt]
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
