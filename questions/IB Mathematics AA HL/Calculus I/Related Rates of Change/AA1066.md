# AA1066

**Maximum mark: 17**

The shape $OAB$ shows a quarter circle with a radius of $8$ centimetres. The vertex $C$ lies on $OB$ and the vertex $D$ lies on the arc $AB$.

![aa1066a](images/aa1066a.PNG)

$\angle OCD$ is $90^\circ$ and $\angle COD$ is $\theta$.

The length $CB$ is $h$.

 

1. Find the following in terms of $h$.

    

    1. $OC$

    

    2. $CD$[3 marks]

    

2. Hence, show that the area of the region $CBD$ is 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        A_{CDB}&=32\arccos{\left(\frac{8-h}{8}\right)}+\tfrac{1}{2}(h-8)\sqrt{16h-h^2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    [5 marks]

A rain drainage gutter is modelled by a prism with the cross-sectional area $A_{CBD}$ and a length of $1\textbf{ metre}$.

![aa1066b](images/aa1066b.PNG)

On a dry day, in which it rained over night, the gutter was partially filled with water and the depth of it was is given by $h$. 

During the day water flowed out of the bottom of the gutter at a rate of $250\sqrt{h}$ cm$^3$ per second.

3. Find the rate of change of $h$ when the depth of the water is $3$ cm.[9 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. We have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            OB &= 8 \hspace{2em} \text{ therefore } \hspace{2em} OC = \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{8-h} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. We know that $OD=8$, therefore using Pythagoras' theorem we can write

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        CD^2&=8^2-(8-h)^2 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[3pt]

        CD&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{\sqrt{16h-h^2}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. We must find the area of sector $ODB$ then subtract from it the area of $\triangle OCD$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the the results from part (a) we can know the area of $\triangle OCD=\tfrac{1}{2}(8-h)\sqrt{16h-h^2}$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The area of the sector $ODB$ is $\tfrac{1}{2}\times 8^2 \times \theta$, hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        A_{CDB} &=\tfrac{1}{2}\times 8^2 \times \theta - \tfrac{1}{2}(8-h)\sqrt{16h-h^2}\\

        &=32\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}(h-8)\sqrt{16h-h^2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using $\triangle OCD$ we can write

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \cos{\theta}&=\frac{8-h}{8} \hspace{2em} \text{ hence}\\

        \theta&=\arccos{\left(\frac{8-h}{8}\right)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \hspace{0.525em}&\boxed{A_{CDB}=32\arccos{\left(\frac{8-h}{8}\right)} + \tfrac{1}{2}(h-8)\sqrt{16h-h^2}} \hspace{2em} \text{as required} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. First we should find the volume of rain in gutter in terms of $h$. 

    

    The gutter is a prism with a cross-sectional area $CDB$ therefore, using the depth of the prism to be $100$ cm we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        V&=100 \times A_{CDB}\\

        &=3200\arccos{\left(\frac{8-h}{8}\right)} + 50(h-8)\sqrt{16h-h^2} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We want to find $\frac{dh}{dt}$ when $h=3$ cm. 

    Using the chain rule we can write

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{dh}{dt}&=\frac{dh}{dV} \times \frac{dV}{dt} \tag*{(1)}\\

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When $h=3$, we know that

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \tfrac{dV}{dt}&=-250\sqrt{3}\\

        &=-433.0127...\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence substituting this into equation $(1)$ we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{dh}{dt}&=\frac{dh}{dV} \times -433.0127...\tag*{(2)}\\

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the G.D.C and the formula we found for $V$ we can find 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \left. \frac{dV}{dh} \right|_{h=3}=624.49... \tag*{\textbf{(M1)(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{dh}{dV} &=\frac{1}{624.49...}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[3pt]

        &=0.0016012...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting this into equation $(2)$ gives

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{dh}{dt}&=0.0016012... \times -433.0127...\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\

        &=-0.693375...\\

        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-0.693 \text{ cm/second}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
