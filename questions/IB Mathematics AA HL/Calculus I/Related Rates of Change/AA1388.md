# AA1388

**Maximum mark: 9**

A hemispherical bowl of radius $6 \text{ cm}$ is initially empty. Water is poured into the bowl at a constant rate of $2\pi \text{ cm}^3 \, \text{s}^{-1}$.

The volume of water, $V \text{ cm}^3$, in a hemispherical bowl of radius $R$ when the water depth is $h$ is given by the formula $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi h^2 (3R - h)$.

Let $A$ be the area of the flat circular surface of the water at time $t$. Find the rate at which $A$ is increasing when the depth of the water is $2 \text{ cm}$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let's first of all write down what we know.

- We were given that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}=2\,\pi$.

- In this case, $R=6$, hence 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        V&=\frac{1}{3}\pi h^2\left(18-h\right) \\[6pt]

        &= 6\pi h^2 - \frac{1}{3}\pi h^3 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

- Letting $r$ be the radius of the flat circular surface of the water at time $t$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        A &= \pi r^2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

What we are looking for is the value of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $h=2$.

Using related rates of change, we can form the following equation

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}V} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

We have $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = 2 \pi$.

Given that $A = \pi r^2$, we can say that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}r} = 2 \pi r$.

Given that $V = 6\pi h^2 - \dfrac{1}{3}\pi h^3$ we can say that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h} = 12\pi h - \pi h^2$, and therefore

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}V} &= \dfrac{1}{12\pi h - \pi h^2}

\end{aligned}

$$

Substituting these expressions into our related rates equation, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 2 \pi r \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h} \times \dfrac{1}{12\pi h - \pi h^2} \times 2 \pi \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

What we still require is an equation relating $h$ and $r$, so that we can differentiate it to get an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h}$. 

To do this, let's consider the hemispherical bowl, and note that the distance from the centre of a sphere to any point on the surface is equal to the radius, in this case, $6\,\text{cm}$.

![AA1388](images/AA1388.png)

Using the Pythagorean theorem, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    r^2 + (6-h)^2 &= 6^2 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Rearranging and simplifying, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    r^2 &= 6^2 - (6-h)^2 \\[6pt]

    &= 36 - \left(36 - 12h + h^2\right) \\[6pt]

    r^2 &= 12h - h^2 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

We can differentiate this equation with respect to $h$, to obtain an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h}$. We have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\left(r^2\right) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\left(12h - h^2\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt] 

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left(r^2\right) \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\left(12h - h^2\right) \\[6pt]

    2r\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h} &= 12 - 2h \\[6pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \dfrac{12 - 2h}{2r} \\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{6-h}{r} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

\end{aligned}

$$

Note that we differentiated the left hand side implicitly.

Now we can substitute this into our related rates equation. We have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 2 \pi r \times \dfrac{6-h}{r} \times \dfrac{1}{12\pi h - \pi h^2} \times 2 \pi \\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{4\pi^2 r(6-h)}{\pi rh(12-h)} \\[6pt]

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{4\pi (6-h)}{h(12-h)}

\end{aligned}

$$

We have been asked to find the rate

at which $A$ is increasing when the depth of the water is $2\,\text{cm}$, that is, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $h=2$. Substituting $h=2$, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{4\pi (6-2)}{2(12-2)} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= \dfrac{16\pi}{20} \\[6pt]

    \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{4\pi}{5} \text{ cm}^2 \, \text{s}^{-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

\end{aligned}

$$
