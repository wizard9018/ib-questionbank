# AA1386

**Maximum mark: 14**

An inverted conical funnel is being filled with water. The funnel has a semi-vertical angle of $30^{\circ}$. Let $h$ be the depth of the water and $r$ be the radius of the water surface at time $t$.

This is shown in the following diagram.

![AA1386](images/AA1386.png)

Water flows into the funnel at a constant rate of $\pi \text{ cm}^3 \text{s}^{-1}$.

1. Show that the volume of water in the funnel, $V$, at time $t$, can be expressed as $V = \frac{\pi}{9}h^3$. [2 marks]

2. Find the rate of change of the water's depth when the depth is $3 \text{ cm}$. [5 marks]

3. Let $A$ be the area of the curved surface of the cone that is in contact with the water. Find the rate of change of $A$ when the depth is $3 \text{ cm}$. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. We can start by using **the formula for the volume of a right cone (3.1)**.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        V &= \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This is expressed in terms of two variables, $r$ and $h$. We need an expression in terms of $h$ only, so we need to express $r$ in terms of $h$.

    To do this, we could note from the right-angled triangle in the diagram that the ratio of $r$ to $h$ is always $\tan 30^{\circ}$. That is, 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \tan 30^{\circ} &= \frac{r}{h}

    \end{aligned}

    $$  

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        r &= h \tan 30^{\circ}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Further, we could note that $\tan 30^{\circ} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ so we have $r = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \textcolor{white}{placeholder}\tag*{{\textbf{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting this expression into our volume formula for the cone in place of $r$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 h \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]

    V &= \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h^2}{3}\right) h \\[6pt]

    V &= \hspace{0.5em} \frac{\pi}{9}h^3 \hspace{1em}\text{... as required.} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. We want to find a value for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $h=3$.

    We were given that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = \pi$ and we have an expression for $V$ in terms of $h$ from part (a). We can differentiate this expression with respect to $h$ to obtain an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h}$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        V &= \frac{\pi}{9}h^3 \\[6pt]

        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \frac{\pi}{3}h^2 \\[6pt]

        &= \frac{\pi h^2}{3} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now use related rates of change to find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}$. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \textcolor{white}{placeholder}\tag*{{\textbf{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}V} \\[6pt]

        &= \pi \times \frac{3}{\pi h^2} \\[6pt]

        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{3}{h^2} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Note that we took the reciprocal of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}h}$ to use in this equation.

    Substituting $h=3$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{3}{3^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    \frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \hspace{0.5em}\frac{1}{3}\, \text{cm}\,\text{s}^{-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Using \textbf{the formula for the curved surface area of a cone (3.1)}, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        A &= \pi r l

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    This is at present expressed in terms of the variables $r$ and $l$, but ideally we'd like it expressed in terms of the variable $h$ only. 

    Referring to our diagram,

    ![aa1386c](images/aa1386c.png)

    We could use the Pythagorean theorem to express $l$ in terms of $h$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        l &= \sqrt{h^2 + r^2} \\[6pt]

        &= \sqrt{h^2 + \left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

        &= \sqrt{h^2 + \dfrac{h^2}{3}} \\[6pt]

        &= \sqrt{\dfrac{4h^2}{3}} \\[6pt]

        l &= \dfrac{2h}{\sqrt{3}} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We know from our work in part (a) that $r=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$.

    Substituting our expressions for $r$ and $l$ into the area formula, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        A &= \pi \left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}\right) \left(\frac{2h}{\sqrt{3}}\right) \\[6pt] 

        &= \frac{2 \pi h^2}{3} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    If we differentiate this with respect to $h$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

       \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \frac{4 \pi h}{3} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When $h=3$,

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}h} &= \frac{4 \pi (3)}{3} \\[6pt]

        &= 4\pi \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We are looking for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $h=3$. As we did in part (b), we can set up a related rates equation. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}h} \times \frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting in our value for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}h}$, and our value for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}$ from part (b), we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 4\pi \times \frac{1}{3} \\[6pt]

        &= \hspace{0.5em}\frac{4\pi}{3} \text{ cm}^2 \text{s}^{-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
