# AA1387

**Maximum mark: 13** 

An observer stands at point $O$, $300 \text{ m}$ horizontally from a launch pad $L$. A rocket is launched vertically upwards from $L$. Let $h$ be the height of the rocket above the ground and $\theta$ be the angle of elevation of the rocket from the observer at time $t$.

The rocket rises such that its vertical speed is constant at $40 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.

1. Express $h$ in terms of $\theta$. [2 marks]

2. Find the rate of change of the angle of elevation, $\theta$, when the rocket is at a height of $400 \text{ m}$. Give your answer in radians per second. [7 marks]

3. Let $s$ be the straight-line distance from the observer to the rocket. Find the rate of change of $s$ when the height of the rocket is $400 \text{ m}$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. From the right-angled triangle formed by the observer, the launch pad, and the rocket:

    ![AA1387a](images/AA1387a.png)

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tan \theta &= \frac{h}{300} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

    h &= 300 \tan \theta \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. We want to find a value for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t}$ when $h=400$.

    We were given that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} = 40$ and we have an expression for $h$ in terms of $\theta$ from part (a). Using **the formula for the derivative of tan *x* (5.15)**, we can differentiate this expression with respect to $\theta$ to obtain an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}\theta}$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        h &= 300 \tan \theta \\[6pt]

        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}\theta} &= 300 \sec^2 \theta \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We can now use related rates of change to find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t}$. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \textcolor{white}{placeholder}\tag*{{\textbf{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} \times \dfrac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}h} \\[6pt]

        &= 40 \times \frac{1}{300 \sec^2 \theta} \\[6pt]

        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{2 \cos^2 \theta}{15} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Note that we took the reciprocal of $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}\theta}$ to use in this equation.

    To substitute $h=400$, we first need to evaluate $\cos^2 \theta$ at this height. 

    When $h = 400$, the right-angled triangle has sides adjacent $= 300$ and opposite $= 400$.

    By the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{300^2 + 400^2} = 500$ (a 3-4-5 triangle). 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \textcolor{white}{placeholder}\tag*{{\textbf{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \cos \theta &= \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} = \frac{300}{500} = \frac{3}{5} \\[6pt]

        \cos^2 \theta &= \frac{9}{25} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting $\cos^2 \theta = \frac{9}{25}$, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{2}{15} \left(\frac{9}{25}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= \frac{18}{375} \\[6pt]

    \frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{6}{125} \text{ rad s}^{-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Using the Pythagorean theorem, we can relate distance $s$ and height $h$. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        s^2 &= 300^2 + h^2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Differentiating with respect to time $t$ (implicit differentiation), we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        2s \frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 2h \frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

        s \frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= h \frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When $h = 400$, we know from part (b) that $s = 500$.

    We are given $\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{\mathrm{d}t} = 40$.

    Substituting these values:

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        500 \frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 400(40) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

        500 \frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 16000 \\[6pt]

        \frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \frac{16000}{500} \\[6pt]

        \frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}t} &= 32 \text{ m s}^{-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
