# AA1182

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the function $y=x^{-2x}$.

1.  Show $\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=-2x^{-2x}(\ln{x}+1)$. [3 marks]

2.  Hence, evaluate $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{1-\left(\tfrac{1}{x^2}\right)^x}{\tfrac{1}{2}x(x-1)}\right)$ [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let's first take the natural logarithm of both sides
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        y&=x^{-2x}\\[6pt]
        \ln{y}&=\ln{x^{-2x}}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
        \ln{y}&=-2x\ln{x} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now implicitly differentiate also using the product rule for the R.H.S.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{1}{y}\times \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=-2\ln{x}-2x\times\frac{1}{x} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[12pt]
        \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=-2y(\ln{x}+1)\\[12pt]
        \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{-2x^{-2x}(\ln{x}+1)}\hspace{2em}\text{...as required.}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  First attempt to evaluate the limit by substituting in $x=1$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{1-\left(\tfrac{1}{x^2}\right)^x}{\tfrac{1}{2}x(x-1)}\right)&=\left(\frac{1-\left(\tfrac{1}{1^2}\right)^1}{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-1)}\right)\\[12pt]
        &=\left(\frac{1-1}{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-1)}\right)\\[12pt]
        &=\frac{0}{0} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We have an indeterminate form, therefore we can use L'Hôpital's rule, we also notice that we can rewrite the numerator in a form similar to part (a)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{1-\left(\tfrac{1}{x^2}\right)^x}{\tfrac{1}{2}x(x-1)}\right)&=\lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{(1-x^{-2x})'}{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}x(x-1)\right)'}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can use the result of part (a) to differentiate the numerator. The derivative of the denominator can be found by expanding and then using the power rule.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &=\lim_{x \to 1} \left(\frac{0-(-2x^{-2x}(\ln{x}+1))}{x-\tfrac{1}{2}}\right) \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now attempt to evaluate the limit at $x=1$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &= \frac{0-(-2(1)^{-2(1)}(\ln{1}+1))}{1-\tfrac{1}{2}}\\[12pt]
    &=\frac{2}{\tfrac{1}{2}}\\[12pt]
    &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
