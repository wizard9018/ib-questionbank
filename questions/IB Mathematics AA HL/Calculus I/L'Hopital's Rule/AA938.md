# AA938

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider 

$\displaystyle{\lim_{x \to 0}} \dfrac{2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-c}{3x}$

where $c \in \mathbb{R}$.

1. Show that a finite limit only exists for $c = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. [2 marks]

2. Using l'H$\hat{\text{o}}$pital's rule, show algebraically that the value of the limit is $\dfrac{1}{3}.$ [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Given that $3x\rightarrow{0}$ as $x\rightarrow{0}$, a finite limit can only exist if the numerator also approaches $0$, so that the limit takes the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$.

    We must have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \lim_{x \to 0} 2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-c &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting the value $x=0$ directly, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^0)-c &= 0 \\[10pt]

    2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(1)-c &= 0 \\[10pt]

    2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(1) &= c \\[10pt]

    2\bigg(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\bigg) &= c \\[10pt]

    &  \hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{c = \dfrac{\pi}{2}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. According to l'H$\hat{\text{o}}$pital's rule, for the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$,

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \lim_{x \to c} \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} &= \lim_{x \to c} \dfrac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Letting $f(x)=2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $g(x)=3x$, we find the derivatives.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(x) &= 2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[8pt]

    f'(x) &= \dfrac{2e^x}{1+e^{2x}} \\[8pt]

    g(x) &= 3x \\[8pt]

    g'(x) &= 3

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-\dfrac{\pi}{2}}{3x} &= \lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\dfrac{2e^x}{1+e^{2x}}}{3} \\[8pt]

    &= \lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{2e^x}{3(1+e^{2x})}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting the value $x=0$ directly on the RHS, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-\dfrac{\pi}{2}}{3x} &= \dfrac{2e^0}{3(1+e^{2(0)})} \\[8pt]

    &= \dfrac{2}{3(1+1)} \\[8pt]

    &  \hspace{-9.25em}\boxed{\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{2\hspace{0.15em}\text{arctan}(e^x)-\dfrac{\pi}{2}}{3x} = \dfrac{1}{3}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
