# AA604

**Maximum mark: 6**

The function $f$ is defined by
$$
f(t) = \left\{
\begin{array}{ccl}
at^2 + bt, & t \leq 4, \\[9pt]
|t-4| + 2, & t > 4.
\end{array} \right.
$$
where $a$ and $b$ are real constants.

Given that both $f$ and its derivative are continuous at $t = 4$, find
the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Evaluating the left-sided limit of $f$ at $t = 4$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2.2em} \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^-} f(t) &= \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^-} (at^2 + bt) \\[6pt]
&= a(4)^2 + b(4) \\[11pt]
&= 16a + 4b
\end{aligned}
$$
Evaluating the right-sided limit of $f$ at
$t = 4$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{3.25em} \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^+} f(t) &= \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^+} (|t-4| + 2) \\[6pt]
&= |4-4| + 2 \\[11pt]
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, using the continuity of $f$ at $t = 4$, we
deduce
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t \rightarrow 4^-} f(t) &= \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^+} f(t) \\[6pt]
16a + 4b &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
For $t > 4$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{0.2em} |t-4| + 2 &= (t-4) + 2 \\[6pt]
&= t-2
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, differentiating $f$ with respect to $t$, we
find
$$
f'(t) = \left\{
\begin{array}{ccl}
2at + b, & t \leq 4, \\[9pt]
1, & t > 4.
\end{array} \right.
$$
Evaluating the left-sided limit of $f'$ at
$t = 4$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.6em} \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^-} f'(t) &= \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^-} (2at + b) \\[6pt]
&= 2a(4) + b \\[11pt]
&= 8a + b
\end{aligned}
$$
Evaluating the right-sided limit of $f'$ at
$t = 4$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t \rightarrow 4^+} f'(t) &= \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^+} 1 \hspace{1.5em} \\[6pt]
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, using the continuity of $f'$ at $t = 4$, we
deduce
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t \rightarrow 4^-} f'(t) &= \lim_{t \rightarrow 4^+} f'(t) \\[6pt]
8a + b &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore, solving the system of
linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$ for $a$ and $b$, we find

 $$
 \begin{aligned}
 a = \dfrac{1}{8}\hspace{0.525em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.525em}b = 0
 \end{aligned}
 $$
