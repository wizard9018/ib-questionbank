# AA533

**Maximum mark: 12**

Let $f(x) = x^3+2x^2+kx-3$. Part of the graph of $f$ is shown in the
following diagram.

![8a31bfc24db155e41ebd397f7655348ba4cac6ac.svg](images/8a31bfc24db155e41ebd397f7655348ba4cac6ac.svg)

The graph of $f$ crosses the $y$-axis at the point Q. The line $L$ is
tangent to the graph of $f$ at Q.

1.  Find the coordinates of Q. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Find $f'(x)$.

    2.  Hence find the equation of $L$ in terms of $k$. [4 marks]

The graph of $f$ has a local maximum at the point P. The line $L$ passes
through P.

3.  Find the value of $k$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The point Q lies on $f(x)$ and has $x = 0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.5cm}
    f(0)        & = 0^3 + 2(0^2) + k(0) - 3\\[3pt]
    & = -3.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence Q has coordinates
       $(0,-3)$

2.
    1.  We have
           $f'(x)  = 3x^2 + 4 x + k$

    2.  By part (i) we have $f'(0) = k$. Hence the equation of $L$ is
        $y = kx + b$. To find $b$ we substitute the coordinates of Q
        into the equation
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        -3  & = k(0) + b\hspace{1cm}\\
        b   & = -3.
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        The equation of L is
          $y = kx -3$

3.  Let's find the $x$-coordinate of P
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^3+2x^2+kx-3   & = kx - 3\\[3pt]
    x^3 + 2x^2          & = 0\\[3pt]
    x^2(x + 2)          & = 0\\[3pt]
    x               & = 0,\,-2.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The $x$-coordinate of P
    is $-2$. We know that P is a local maximum, hence $f'(-2) = 0$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3(-2)^2 + 4 (-2) + k    & = 0\\[3pt]
    12 - 8 + k          & = 0\\[3pt]
    &\hspace{-0.8em}k = -4
    \end{aligned}
    $$
