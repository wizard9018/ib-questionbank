# AA518

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the curve $y = \dfrac{9}{5-x} + \dfrac{1}{x-1}$.

Find the $x$-coordinates of the points on the curve where the gradient
is zero.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

If we calculate the derivative of $y$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[\dfrac{9}{5-x} + \dfrac{1}{x-1}\bigg] \\[8pt]
&= \dfrac{9}{(5-x)^2} - \dfrac{1}{(x-1)^2} \hspace{3em}  \\
\end{aligned}
$$

If we solve the equation $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 0$ for $x$,
we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{9}{(5-x)^2} - \dfrac{1}{(x-1)^2} &= 0 \\[6pt]
9(x-1)^2 &= (5-x)^2 \\[10pt]
9x^2-18x+9 &= 25-10x+x^2 \\[10pt]
8x^2-8x-16 &= 0 \\[10pt]
x^2-x-2 &= 0 \\[11pt]
(x+1)(x-2) &= 0 \\[11pt]
x&= -1,\,2
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence the $x$-coordinates of the points on the
curve where the gradient is zero are

$x = -1\ \mbox {and}\  x = 2$
