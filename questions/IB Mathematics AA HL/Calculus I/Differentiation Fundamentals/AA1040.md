# AA1040

**Maximum mark: 20**

The function $f$ is defined as $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-(a+3)x+3a}{x-2}$, where $x\in \mathbb{R}, x\neq 2$.
The parameter $a$ can be any real number.

1.  For this part of the question $a=4$.

    1.  Find the equations of the vertical and oblique asymptotes of $f(x)$.

    2.  Justify that $f$ has two stationary points. Note: you do not need to find the coordinates of these points.

    3.  Hence, sketch $f(x)$ and identify the asymptotes and axis intercepts.[9 marks]

2.  Show that $f(x)$ will always have at least one root for any value of $a$. [3 marks]

3.  Find the range of values of $a$ for which $f$ has no stationary points. [4 marks]

4.  Show that the root of the equation of the oblique asymptote is always exactly 1 unit away from one of the roots of $f(x)$. [4 marks]

3.  Find the derivative of $f(x)$ using the quotient rule
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x)&=\frac{(2x-(a+3))(x-2)-(1)(x^2-(a+3)x+3a)}{(x-2)^2}\\
        &=\frac{2x^2-4x\cancel{-ax}+2a\cancel{-3x}+6-x^2\cancel{+ax}\cancel{+3x}-3a}{(x-2)^2}\\
        &=\frac{x^2-4x-a+6}{(x-2)^2}\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We must find the range of values of $a$ for which there are no solutions to the equation $f'(x)= 0$. We can find the discriminant of the numerator found above
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \triangle&=(-4)^2-4(1)(6-a) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\
        &=4a-8
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore there are no solutions to $f'(x)= 0$ when $4a-8<0$, so we have no stationary points for $a<2$.
    $$
     \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$

    Also, when $a=2$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \frac{x^2-4x+4}{(x-2)^2}\\
        &= \frac{(x-2)^2}{(x-2)^2}\\
        &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence when $a=2$, we have a constant gradient of $1$, and $f(x)$ is a linear function (with a hole at $x=2$) and hence has no stationary point.

    Hence $f$ has no stationary points for $a \leq 2.$
    $$
     \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    $$
    $\textcolor{white}{gap}$
