# AA588

**Maximum mark: 15**

Consider $f(x) = x\ln(x) - x$, for $x>0$

1.  Find $f(1)$. [2 marks]

2.  Solve $f(x) = 0$. [3 marks]

The graph of $f$ has a local minimum at point P$(x,y)$.

3.  Find the coordinates of point P and explain why it is a
    local minimum. [5 marks]

4.  Find the set of $x$ values for which $f$ is increasing.
    [2 marks]

5.  Hence, sketch the graph of $f$, for $x>0$.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Substituting $x = 1$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.4cm}
    f(1)        & = 1 \ln (1) - 1\\[5pt]
    &\hspace{.275em} = -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x\ln x - x      & = 0\\[5pt]
    x(\ln x - 1)    & = 0\\[5pt]
    \ln x - 1       & = 0 \\[5pt]
    \ln x           & = 1\\[5pt]
    & \hspace{-1.8mm}x  = e
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  A local minimum occurs when $f'(x) = 0$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = x\,\dfrac 1 x + \ln x - 1\\[6pt]
    & = \ln x\\[6pt]
    \ln x       & = 0\\[6pt]
    x       & = e^0\\[6pt]
    x       & = 1\\[6pt]
    f(1)        & = -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
      $\mbox P(1,-1)$

    To show that $x = 1$ is a local minimum we need to check the sign of
    the second derivative of $f$ at this point. We get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f''(x)  & = \dfrac 1 x \\[6pt]
    f''(1)      & = 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since $f''(1) > 0$, we conclude
    that P is a local minimum.

4.  $f$ is increasing when $f'(x) > 0$. We know that $f'(x) = 0$ at
    $x = 1$. Hence, we

    find that $f'(x) > 0$ for   $x > 1$
