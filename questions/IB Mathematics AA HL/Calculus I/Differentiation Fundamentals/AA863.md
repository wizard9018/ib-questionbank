# AA863

**Maximum mark: 8**

Consider the function $f$ defined by $f(x) = \ln \left( 4x^2 - 9\right)$
for $x > \dfrac{3}{2}$.

The following diagram shows part of the graph of $f$ which crosses the
$x$-axis at point A, with coordinates $(a,0)$. The line $L$ is the
tangent to the graph of $f$ at the point B.

![6a470451e3d20e2264b1afd77591f138e5b01904.svg](images/6a470451e3d20e2264b1afd77591f138e5b01904.svg)

1.  Find the exact value of $a$. [3 marks]

2.  Given that the gradient of $L$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, find the
    $x$-coordinate of B. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we solve the equation $f(a) = 0$ for $a$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \ln \left(4a^2 - 9\right) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    4a^2 - 9 &= 1 \\[5pt]
    4a^2 &= 10 \\[5pt]
    a^2 &= \dfrac{5}{2} \\[5pt]
    a &= \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}} \hspace{3em}[a>0]\\[5pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{2}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} \\[5pt]
    &\hspace{-0.525em}a = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2} \hspace{5.25em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Differentiating $f(x) = \ln \left(4x^2 - 9\right)$, we get
    $f'(x) = \dfrac{8x}{4x^2 - 9}$

    If we solve $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for $x$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{8x}{4x^2 - 9} &= \dfrac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
    4x^2 - 9 &= 16x \\[6pt]
    4x^2 - 16x - 9 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{6.75em} (2x + 1)(2x - 9) &= 0 \\[2pt]
    2x - 9 &= 0 \hspace{3em} \left[\text{since } 2x + 1 \neq 0 \text{ for } x > \dfrac 3 2 \right]\\[4pt]
    x &= \dfrac{9}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the $x$-coordinate of B is
     $x = \dfrac{9}{2}$
