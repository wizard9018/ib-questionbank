# AA1641

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the function $f(x) = 2x^3 - 4x$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Find $f'(x)$ from first principles.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Applying the **derivative of $f(x)$ from first principles (5.12)**
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $f(x) = 2x^3 - 4x$, and $f(x+h) = 2(x+h)^3 - 4(x+h)$ we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{\bigl[2(x+h)^3 - 4(x+h)\bigr] - \bigl[2x^3 - 4x\bigr]}{h} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Expanding $(x+h)^3$ using the **binomial theorem (1.9)**,
$$
\begin{aligned}
    (a+b)^n = \displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r,
\end{aligned}
$$
with $n = 3$, we expand to get
$$
\begin{aligned}
    (x+h)^3 &= x^3 + 3x^2 h + 3xh^2 + h^3
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting this in and simplifying the numerator gives us
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{2x^3 + 6x^2 h + 6xh^2 + 2h^3 - 4x - 4h - 2x^3 + 4x}{h} \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[12pt]
    &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{6x^2 h + 6xh^2 + 2h^3 - 4h}{h} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Dividing each term by $h$, we then have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \left(\dfrac{6x^2 h}{h} + \dfrac{6xh^2}{h} + \dfrac{2h^3}{h} - \dfrac{4h}{h}\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[12pt]
    &= \lim_{h \to 0} \bigl(6x^2 + 6xh + 2h^2 - 4\bigr) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the limit as $h \to 0$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \boxed{6x^2 - 4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
