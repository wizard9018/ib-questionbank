# AA1262

**Maximum mark: 5**

The tangent to the curve $y = x^3 - 3x$ at the point $G(2, 2)$ meets the $x$-axis at $H$ and the $y$-axis at $J$. Find the ratio $GH : HJ$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Differentiating to find the gradient function of the tangent, we have $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 3x^2 - 3$. Evaluating at $x = 2$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \left.\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\right|_{x=2} &= 3(2)^2 - 3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
    &= 3(4) - 3 \\[6pt]
    &= 9 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the **equation of a straight line (2.1)**, $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$, with $m = 9$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (2, 2)$, the tangent at $G$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
    y - 2 &= 9(x - 2) \\[6pt]
    y-2 &= 9x - 18 \\[6pt]
    y &= 9x - 16
\end{aligned}
$$
Finding $H$ by setting $y = 0$ gives $0 = 9x - 16$, so $x = \dfrac{16}{9}$. Finding $J$ by setting $x = 0$ gives $y = -16$. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
    H = \left(\dfrac{16}{9},\,0\right), \qquad J = (0,\,-16) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $G$, $H$ and $J$ are collinear, the ratio of the segments equals the ratio of the differences in their $x$-coordinates. Hence we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
    GH : HJ &= \left(2 - \dfrac{16}{9}\right) : \left(\dfrac{16}{9} - 0\right) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[10pt]
    &= \dfrac{2}{9} : \dfrac{16}{9} \\[10pt]
    &= 2 : 16 \\[6pt]
    GH : HJ &= \boxed{1 : 8} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
