# AA599

**Maximum mark: 16**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac 1 3 x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 10$.

1.  Find $f'(x)$. [2 marks]

The graph of $f$ has horizontal tangents at the points where $x = a$ and
$x = b$, $a <b$.

2.  Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.[3 marks]

3.
    1.  Sketch the graph of $y = f'(x)$.

    2.  Hence explain why the graph of $f$ has a local maximum point at
        $x = a$.[2 marks]

4.
    1.  Find $f''(b)$.

    2.  Hence, use your answer to part (d) (i) to show that the graph of
        $f$\
        has a local minimum point at $x = b$. [4 marks]

The tangent to the graph of $f$ at $x = a$ and the normal to the graph
of $f$ at $x = b$ intersect at the point $(p,q)$.

5.  Find the value of $p$ and the value of $q$. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    $f'(x) = x^2 + 4x - 5$

2.  Horizontal tangents occur when $f'(x) = 0$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^2 + 4x - 5    & = 0\\[6pt]
    (x - 1)(x+5)    & = 0\\[6pt]
    x           & = -5,\,1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
      $a = -5,\ b = 1$

3.
    1.  The sketch of $f'$ is

        ![1841a9522e22450b6ba89a4cef01640f127977b9.svg](images/1841a9522e22450b6ba89a4cef01640f127977b9.svg)

    2.  The first derivative changes from positive to negative at
        $x = a$ so the local maximum is at $x = a$.

4.
    1.  The second derivative of $f$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f''(x)  & = 2 x +4
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Substituting $b = 1$ into
        $f''$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f''(1)  & = 2(1) + 4\\[5pt]
        &\hspace{.275em} = 6\\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  $f''(b)$ is positive, therefore the function is concave up at
        $x = b$ and, by the second derivative test, we conclude that $f$
        has a local minimum at $x = b$.

5.  The tangent to $f$ at $x = a$ is the horizontal line $y = f(a)$. We
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(a)    & = \dfrac 1 3(-5)^3 + 2(-5)^2 - 5(-5) + 10\\[5pt]
    & = \dfrac {130}3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    The normal to $f$ at $x = b$ is the vertical line $x = 1$. Hence the
    intersection is at
    $$
    \left(1,\dfrac{130} 3\right).
    $$

    It follows that
      $p = 1,\ \ q = \dfrac {130}3$
