# AA581

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f(x) = x^2 e^x$ and $g(x) = 4x - x^2$.

1.  Find $f'(x)$.[3 marks]

2.  Find the $x$-coordinate where the tangents of $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are
    parallel. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the product rule $uv = u'v + uv'$ with $u = x^2$ and
    $v = e^x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = 2 x e^x + x^2 e^x\\[6pt]
    &\hspace{.275em}  = x e^x (x + 2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The tangents are parallel when the gradients are the same.
    First, let's find $g'(x)$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g'(x)   & = 4 - 2x\hspace{.8cm}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Next

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.7cm}
    f'(x)           & = g'(x)\\[6pt]
    x e^x (x + 2)   & = 4 - 2x\\[6pt]
    & \hspace{-2mm} x = 0.596 \qquad\quad\mbox{[by using G.D.C.]}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
