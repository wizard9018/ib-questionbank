# AA898

**Maximum mark: 22**

Consider the function defined by $f(x) = (2x-6)\ln(x+3)+x$ for
$x \in \mathbb{R}$, $x>p$.

1.  Find the value of $p$. [2 marks]

2.  Find an expression for $f'(x)$. [3 marks]

The graph of $y=f(x)$ has no points of
inflexion.

3.  Determine if the graph of $y=f(x)$ is concave down or
    concave up over its domain. [3 marks]

The function $g$ is defined by $g(x)=3\ln\left(\dfrac{1}{x+3}\right)+x$,
for $x\in \mathbb{R}$, $x>-3$.

4.  Find an expression for $g'(x)$. [4 marks]

5.  Find the horizontal and vertical asymptotes of
    $g'(x)$.[2 marks]

6.  Find the exact value of the minimum of $y=g(x)$.[3 marks]

7.  Solve $f(x)<g(x)$ for $x\in \mathbb{R}$, $x>-3$.[5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Due to the natural log term, $x+3>0$. Therefore
    $ p=-3$

2.  Differentiating $f$ with respect to $x$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[(2x-6)\ln(x+3)+x\big] \\[10pt]
    & =\dfrac{2x-6}{x+3}+2\ln(x+3)+1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Differentiating $f'$ with respect to $x$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f''(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[\dfrac{2x-6}{x+3}+2\ln(x+3)+1\right] \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{(x+3)(2)-(2x-6)}{\left(x+3\right)^2} + \dfrac{2}{x+3} \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{2x+18}{(x+3)^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, over $x>-3$, we have $f''(x)>0$. Therefore,
    $f \text{ is concave up}$

4.  Differentiating $g$ with respect to $x$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left[3\ln\left(\dfrac{1}{x+3}\right)+x\right] \\[12pt]
    &=3(x+3)\left(-\dfrac{1}{(x+3)^2}\right)+1  \\[8pt]
    & =\dfrac{-3}{x+3}+1 \\[10pt]
    & =\dfrac{x}{x+3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  The denominator of $g'$ is zero when $x=-3$.\
    Hence,
    $\text{the line \(x=-3\) is a vertical asymptote of the graph of \(y=g'(x)\)}$\
    If we take the limit as $x$ tends to $\infty$, we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\to \infty} g'(x) &= \lim_{x\to \infty} \dfrac{x}{x+3}%\cdot \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{x}}{\dfrac{1}{x}}
    \\[8pt]
    & = \lim_{x\to \infty} \dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{3}{x}\rule[-2mm]{0pt}{7.2mm}} \\[6pt]
    & = 1 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    $\text{the line \(y=1\) is a horizontal asymptote of the graph of \(y=g'(x)\)}$

6.  From part (d) we obtain a stationary point at $x=0$.
    The second derivative of $g$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g''(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[\dfrac{x}{x+3}\right] \\[12pt]
    &= \dfrac{3}{(x+3)^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, $g''(x)>0$ which implies that at $x=0$ the function $g$
    reaches its minimum. Therefore, the minimum value of $g$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g(0) &= 3\ln\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)+0 \\[12pt]
    &= -3\ln(3)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

7.  Using logarithm properties, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(x) &< g(x) \\[12pt]
    (2x-6)\ln(x+3) + x &< 3\ln\left(\dfrac{1}{x+3}\right) + x \\[12pt]
    (2x-6)\ln(x+3) &< -3\ln(x+3) \\[15pt]
    (2x-3)\ln(x+3) &< 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Thus, we have two options
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \left[2x-3<0\enskip\&\enskip \ln(x+3)>0\right] &\text{  or  } \left[2x-3>0 \enskip\&\enskip \ln(x+3)<0\right] \\[12pt]
    \left[x<1.5\enskip\&\enskip\ln(x+3)>0\right]&\text{ or } \left[x>1.5\enskip\&\enskip \ln(x+3)<0\right] \\[12pt]
    x < 1.5\enskip & \&\enskip 1<x+3 \hspace{2em}[\text{since }\ln(x+3)>0\text{ for }x>1.5]\\[8pt]
    -2 <& \hspace{0.1em}x < 1.5 \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore,
    $f(x)<g(x)$ over $-2 <  x < 1.5$
