# AA1643

**Maximum mark: 15**

Let $f(x) = -x^3 + 3x + 5$.

1.  Expand $(x + h)^3$.[2 marks]

2.  Use the formula $f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ to show that the derivative of $f(x)$ is $-3x^2 + 3$.[4 marks]
3.  The tangent to the curve of $f$ at the point $A(2,\,3)$ is parallel to the tangent at a point $B$. Find the coordinates of $B$.[4 marks]
4.  The graph of $f$ is increasing for $p < x < q$. Find the value of $p$ and of $q$.[3 marks]
5.  Write down the range of values for the gradient of $f$.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Applying the **binomial theorem (1.9)**, $(a+b)^n = \displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r$, with $n = 3$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (x+h)^3 &= \binom{3}{0}x^3 + \binom{3}{1}x^2 h + \binom{3}{2}x h^2 + \binom{3}{3}h^3 \\[14pt]
        &= \boxed{x^3 + 3x^2 h + 3xh^2 + h^3} \tag*{\textbf{A1\;A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  Applying the **derivative of $f(x)$ from first principles (5.12)**,
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h},
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    with $f(x) = -x^3 + 3x + 5$ and $f(x+h) = -(x+h)^3 + 3(x+h) + 5$, subbing in we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{\bigl[-(x+h)^3 + 3(x+h) + 5\bigr] - \bigl[-x^3 + 3x + 5\bigr]}{h} \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting the expansion of $(x+h)^3$ from part (a) and simplifying the numerator, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{-x^3 - 3x^2 h - 3xh^2 - h^3 + 3x + 3h + 5 + x^3 - 3x - 5}{h} \\[6pt]
        &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{-3x^2 h - 3xh^2 - h^3 + 3h}{h} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Factoring $h$ from the numerator and cancelling, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{h(-3x^2 - 3xh - h^2 + 3)}{h} \tag*{\textbf{M1}} \\[12pt]
        &= \lim_{h \to 0} (-3x^2 - 3xh - h^2 + 3) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Taking the limit as $h \to 0$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \boxed{-3x^2 + 3}\hspace{1em}\text{... as required.} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
3.  Evaluating the derivative at $A$, the gradient of the tangent at $A$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        f'(2) &= -3(2)^2 + 3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[12pt]
        &= -9 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Setting $f'(b) = -9$ to find the $x$-coordinate of $B$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -3b^2 + 3 &= -9 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[12pt]
        b^2 &= 4 \\[6pt]
        b &= \pm 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since $B \neq A$, we take $b = -2$, and $f(-2) = 8 - 6 + 5 = 7$, so
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        B &= \boxed{(-2,\, 7)} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
4.  The graph is increasing where $f'(x) > 0$. Setting up the inequality, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        -3x^2 + 3 &> 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Dividing by $-3$ (and flipping the inequality), we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        x^2 &< 1 \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[12pt]
        -1 &< x < 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    therefore we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        p = \boxed{-1}, \quad q = \boxed{1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
5.  The gradient function $f'(x) = -3x^2 + 3$ is an inverted parabola with maximum at $x = 0$, giving $f'(0) = 3$. Since $f'(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, the range of the gradient is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &\boxed{f'(x) \leq 3} \tag*{\textbf{A1\;A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
