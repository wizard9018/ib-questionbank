# AA593

**Maximum mark: 16**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac {2x + 1} {x - 3}$, $x \in \mathbb R$, $x \neq 3$

1.
    1.  Find the equation of the vertical and horizontal asymptote of
        $f$.

    2.  Write down the coordinates of the point Q at which the
        asymptotes intersects.

    3.  Find the $x$ and $y$ intercepts of $f$. [5 marks]

2.  Show that $f'(x) = -\dfrac 7 {(x-3)^2}$. [2 marks]

3.  Hence, find the equation of the tangent at point
    P$(4,y)$.[3 marks]

4.  The point S also lies on the graph of $f$. The tangent to S is
    parallel to the\
    tangent at point P. Find the coordinates of S. [4 marks]

5.  Show that Q is the midpoint of \[PS\]. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Vertical asymptote:   $ x = 3$\
        Horizontal asymptote:   $ y = 2$

    2.    $\text{Q}(3,2)$
    3.  To find the $y$-intercept of $f$, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y   & = f(0)\\[8pt]
        y   & = \dfrac {2(0) + 1} {0 - 3}\hspace{1cm}\\[10pt]
        &\hspace{-2mm}y = -\dfrac 1 3
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        To find the $x$-intercepts of $f$, we solve $f(x)=0$

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac {2x + 1} {x - 3}     & = 0\hspace{3cm}\\[8pt]
        2x + 1              & = 0\\[10pt]
        &\hspace{-2mm}x = -\dfrac 1 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  Using the quotient rule, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{.9cm}
    f'(x)       & = \dfrac {2(x - 3) - (2x + 1)} {(x - 3)^2}\\[10pt]
    &\hspace{.275em} = -\dfrac 7 {(x - 3)^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  First we find the $y$ coordinate of P$(4,y)$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3mm}
    y   & = \dfrac {2(4) + 1} {4 - 3}\\[5pt]
    y   & = 9\\[1pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The gradient of the tangent of $f$
    at P$(4,9)$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4mm}
    m   & = f'(4)\\[5pt]
    & = -\dfrac 7 {(4 - 3)^2}\\[5pt]
    & = -7\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the equation of the tangent
    $y - y_1 = m (x - x_1)$ with $m = -7$, $x_1 = 4$, and $y_1 = 9$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y - 9   & = -7 (x - 4)\\[9pt]
    &\hspace{-2mm} y = -7x + 37
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Parallel lines have the same gradient. Hence, the gradient of the
    tangent at\
    both P and S is $m = -7$.\
    Hence, solving the equation $f'(x) = -7$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac 7 {(x - 3)^2}   & = -7\\[6pt]
    (x - 3)^2           & = 1\\[6pt]
    x - 3           & = \pm 1\\[6pt]
    x               & = 2\\[6pt]
    f (2)               & = \dfrac {2(2) + 1}{2 - 3}\\[6pt]
    & = -5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
      $\mbox S(2,-5)$

5.  A midpoint has the coordinates
    M$\left(\dfrac {x_1 + x_2} 2, \dfrac {y_1 + y_2} 2\right)$.\
    Substituting the coordinates of P and S into M, we get
    M$\left(\dfrac {4 + 2} 2, \dfrac {9 + (-5)} 2\right)$.\
    Therefore, we find M$(3,2)$ is the midpoint of \[PS\].\
    $\mbox{Hence, Q\((3,2)\) is the midpoint of [PS]}$
