# AA782

**Maximum mark: 7**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{g(x)}{h(x)}+15$, where $g(5) = 30$, $h(5) = 10$,
$g'(5) = 20$ and $h'(5) = 5$.

Find the equation of the normal to the graph of $f$ at $x = 5$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

First we find the $y$-coordinate of the graph of $f$ at $x=5$, as
follows
$$
\begin{aligned}
y   & =  f(5) \\[12pt]
& =  \dfrac{g(5)}{h(5)}+15 \\[12pt]
& =  \dfrac{30}{10}+15  \\[12pt]
& =  18
\end{aligned}
$$
and the gradient of the tangent line of the
graph at $x=5$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(5) & =  \dfrac{h(5)g'(5) - g(5)h'(5)}{[h(5)]^2}+0 \\[12pt]
& = \dfrac{(10)(20)-(30)(5)}{(10)^2} \\[12pt]
% & =  \dfrac{200-150}{100} \\[7pt]
& =\dfrac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, the slope of the normal to the
curve at $(5,18)$ is $m = -2$ and the normal line is given by the
equation
$$
\begin{aligned}
%y - y_1    & =  m(x - x_1) \hspace{4mm}\\[8pt]
y - 18  & =   -2(x - 5) \\[13pt]
&\hspace{-0.5em}y   =  -2x +28
\end{aligned}
$$
