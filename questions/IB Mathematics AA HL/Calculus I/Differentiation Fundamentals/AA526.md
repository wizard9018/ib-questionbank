# AA526

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f(x) = kx^3$.

1.  Show that the point P$(2,8k)$ lies on the curve of $f$. [1 mark]

At P, the normal to the curve is parallel to $y = \dfrac{1}{6}x$.

2.  Find the value of $k$. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  To show that the point P lies on the curve we substitute $x = 2$ to
    the equation of $f$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(2)        & = k (2)^3\\[3pt]
    & = 8 k
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    as required.

2.  Let's find $f'(2)$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x)       & = 3k x^2\\[3pt]
    f'(2)       & = 3k(2^2)\\[3pt]
    & = 12k
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the slope of of the normal to
    the curve at P is $m = -\dfrac 1 {12k}$. On the other hand we know
    that the normal is parallel to $y = \dfrac 1 6 x$, hence

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    -\dfrac 1 {12k}         & = \dfrac 1 6\hspace{8mm} \\[10pt]
    & \hspace{-.5em}k = -\dfrac 1 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
