# AA467

**Maximum mark: 7**

Find the $x$-coordinates of all the points on the curve
$y = 2x^4 - \dfrac{34}{3}x^3 + \dfrac{41}{2}x^2 - 10x$

at which the tangent to the curve is parallel to the tangent at
P$\bigg(1,\dfrac{7}{6}\bigg)$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{4em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[2x^4 - \dfrac{34}{3}x^3 + \dfrac{41}{2}x^2 - 10x\bigg] \\[6pt]
&= 8x^3 - 34x^2 + 41x - 10 \\
\end{aligned}
$$
Evaluating the gradient of
the tangent to the curve at P, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{4em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg|_{x=1} &= 8(1)^3 - 34(1)^2 + 41(1) - 10 \\[5pt]
&= 8 - 34 + 41 - 10 \\[12pt]
&= 5 \\
\end{aligned}
$$
Tangents to the curve that are parallel have the
same gradient.

Hence we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg|_{x=1} \\[6pt]
8x^3 - 34x^2 + 41x - 10 &= 5 \\[10pt]
\hspace{2em} 8x^3 - 34x^2 + 41x - 15 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

If we let $p(x) = 8x^3 - 34x^2 + 41x - 15$, we obtain $p(1) = 0$.

Hence, by the factor theorem, $(x-1)$ is a factor of $p(x)$.

\begin{aligned}
(x - 1)(8x^2 - 26x +15) &= 0 \hspace{8.2em} \\[10pt]
(x-1)(4x-3)(2x - 5) &= 0 \\
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
