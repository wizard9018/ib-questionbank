# AA571

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $f(x) = p x^3 - qx$. At $x = 0$, the gradient of the curve of $f$ is
$2$.

Given that
$f^{-1} (12) = 2$, find the value of $p$ and $q$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x)       & = 3 p x^2 - q\\[8pt]
2       & = 3 p (0)^2 - q \hspace{.8cm}\\[12pt]
&\hspace{-1.5mm}q = -2
\end{aligned}
$$
\
Since $f^{-1}(12) = 2$, it follows $f(2) = 12$. Substituting $f(2) =12$,
we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
12  & = p(2^3) - 2(-2)\\[5pt]
12  & = 8 p +4\\[5pt]
8   & = 8p\\[6pt]
& \hspace{-1.5mm}p = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
