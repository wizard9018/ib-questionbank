# AA1640

**Maximum mark: 7**

Consider the function $y = \dfrac{2x + 3}{x + 1}$. The graph of this function has a vertical and a horizontal asymptote.

1.  Write down the equations of the asymptotes.[2 marks]

2.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$, giving your answer in the form $\dfrac{a}{(x+b)^2}$ where $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$.[3 marks]

3.  State the number of points of inflexion on the graph of this function, justifying your answer.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The vertical asymptote occurs where the denominator is zero, $x + 1 = 0$, so we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \boxed{x=-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As $x \to \pm\infty$ the function approaches the ratio of leading coefficients, so the horizontal asymptote is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \boxed{y = 2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Applying the **quotient rule (5.6)**, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\dfrac{u}{v}\right) = \dfrac{v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} - u\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}}{v^2}$, with
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        u &= 2x + 3, & \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 2 \\[6pt]
        v &= x + 1, & \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{(x+1)(2) - (2x+3)(1)}{(x+1)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)\;A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Simplifying the numerator, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{2x + 2 - 2x - 3}{(x+1)^2} \\[10pt]
        &= \boxed{\dfrac{-1}{(x+1)^2}} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    so $a = -1$ and $b = 1$.

3.  Differentiating the result from part (b), we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2 y}{\mathrm{d}x^2} &= \dfrac{2}{(x+1)^3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since the numerator is a non-zero constant, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2 y}{\mathrm{d}x^2}$ is never equal to zero for any $x$ in the domain of $y$, so there can be no point of inflexion.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the number of points of inflexion is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &\boxed{0} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
