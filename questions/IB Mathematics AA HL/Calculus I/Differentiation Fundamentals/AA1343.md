# AA1343

**Maximum mark: 14**

A scientist is studying the effect of a supplement on the growth of a certain species of bacteria in a container. 

A function is found which models the population $P$ of bacteria after $t$ minutes such that $P_A(t)=a\times 3^{kt}$ where $t\geq0$ and $a,k\in\mathbb{R}$.

The initial population of the bacteria is known to be $600$ and the scientist finds that when $t=3$ the population has tripled.

1.    

    1.    Write down the value of $a$. 

    2.    Hence show that the value of $k=\dfrac{1}{3}$. [3 marks]

2.    Calculate the population after $10$ minutes. Give your answer to the nearest integer. [1 mark]

3.    Use your model to determine the rate of change of $P_A$ when $t=20$ minutes. [2 marks]

At $10$ minutes the scientist adds a supplement to the container. 

The model after adding the supplement can be expressed in the form $P_B(t)=be^{c(t-10)}$ where $b,c\in\mathbb{R}$ and $t>10$.

4.    Write down the value of $b$. [1 mark]

The scientist calculates that the population when $t=15$ minutes is decreasing by $74$ bacteria per minute.

5.    Given that $c<-1$, find the value of $c$. [4 marks]

6.    Determine the time when the population of bacteria first drops below $1\%$ of the maximum population. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1. To find $a$ we can use the initial population $P=600$ which is when $t=0$. Hence 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        600&=a(3^{k(0)})\\[6pt]

        600&=a(1)\\[6pt]

        a&=600\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. Using the value from part (a)(i) and that the population has tripled when $t=3$, we can solve for $k$. 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        P_A(t)&=600(3)^{kt}\\[10pt]

        3(600)&=600(3)^{3k}\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        As this is a 'show that' question we need to show algebraic working.

        Diving by $600$ and using the exponent of $3$ we get

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        3&=3^{3k}\\[6pt]

        3^1&=3^{3k}\\[6pt]

        1&=3k\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[6pt]

        k&=\dfrac{1}{3}\hspace{2em}\text{...as required}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. To find the population when $t=10$ we substitute this into the function using the parameters we found in part (a). We must also remember to round our answer to the nearest integer.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    P_A(10)&=600(3)^{\normalsize\frac{1}{3}(10)}\\[6pt]

    &=23\,364.4...\\[6pt]

    &=23\,364\text{ bacteria}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. We are not expected to know how to find the derivative of the given function but we can use our G.D.C. to help.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the derivative function on the G.D.C. and evaluating when $t=20$ we obtain

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    P_A'(20)&=333\,182.9...\\[6pt]

    &=333\,000\text{ bacteria per minute}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. We know the population of bacteria in the container when the supplement is added is $23\,364$, from part (b).

    Hence we can substitute this value and the initial value of $t=10$ into the equation to solve for $b$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    23\,364&=be^{c(10-10)}\\[6pt]

    23\,364&=be^0\\[6pt]

    b&=23\,364\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. We are given that the population is $\textbf{decreasing}$ when $t=15$. 

    The given value is the $\textit{rate of change}$ of $P_B$ so we need to find the derivative function.

    Using the chain rule with the standard derivative of $y=e^x$ we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    P_B'(t)&=23\,364e^{c(t-10)}(c)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    &=23\,364c\cdot e^{c(t-10)}\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Setting this equal to $-74$ when $t=15$ we can solve for $c$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    -74&=23\,364c\cdot e^{c(15-10)}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    -74&=23\,364c\cdot e^{5c}\\[6pt]

    c=-1.184...&\hspace{3em}c=-0.00322...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    As $c<-1$ then our answer is $c=-1.18$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

6. As this is an exponential function and the population is decreasing after the supplement is added to the container, the maximum value is the initial value. 

    Hence $1\%$ of $23\,364$ is $233.64...$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Setting the equation equal to this value we can solve for $t$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    233.364...&=23\,364...e^{-1.184...(t-10)}\\[6pt]

    0.01&=e^{-1.184...(t-10)}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the G.D.C. we can solve for $t$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    t&=13.88...\\[6pt]

    &=13.9\text{ minutes}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
