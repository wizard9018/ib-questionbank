# AA805

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the function $f(x)=\dfrac{4x^2+px}{x+1}$, where $x\neq -1$ and $p \in \mathbb{R}$.

Find the set of values of $p$ for which the graph of $f$ has exactly two stationary points.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Using the **quotient rule (5.6)**, to differentiate $f(x)$, $y = \dfrac{u}{v} \Rightarrow \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{v\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} - u\tfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}}{v^2}$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x) &= \dfrac{(x+1)(8x+p) - (4x^2+px)(1)}{(x+1)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[12pt]
      &= \dfrac{8x^2 + px + 8x + p - 4x^2 - px}{(x+1)^2} \\[12pt]
f'(x) &= \dfrac{4x^2 + 8x + p}{(x+1)^2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
For $f$ to have exactly two stationary points, the quadratic $4x^2 + 8x + p = 0$ must have two distinct real roots, and neither root can equal $-1$ (which is excluded from the domain).

Applying the **discriminant (2.7)**, with the condition $\Delta > 0$ for two distinct real roots, $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
8^2 - 4(4)(p) &> 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]
64 - 16p &> 0 \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Solving the inequality gives $p < 4$.

Checking whether $x = -1$ can be a root of $4x^2 + 8x + p = 0$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
4(-1)^2 + 8(-1) + p &= p - 4
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $p < 4$ gives $p - 4 < 0$, the value $x = -1$ is not a root of the quadratic, so both stationary points lie in the domain.

$$\\[-2.5em]$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence $\boxed{p < 4}$.

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
