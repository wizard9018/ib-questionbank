# AA521

**Maximum mark: 17**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{x^3+x-2}{2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $x \neq 0$.

1.  The graph of $y = f(x)$ has a local minimum at A. Find the
    coordinates of A.[5 marks]

2.
    1.  Show that there is exactly one point of inflexion, B, on the
        graph of $y = f(x)$.

    2.  The coordinates of B can be expressed in the form B$(2^p, 2^q)$,
        where $p,q \in \mathbb{Q}$. Find the value of $p$ and the value
        of $q$. [8 marks]

3.  Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$ showing clearly the position of the
    points A and B. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we calculate the $1$st derivative of $f$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.5cm}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[\dfrac{x^3+x-2}{2x}\bigg] \\[4pt]
    &= \dfrac{(3x^2+1)(2x) - (x^3+x-2)(2)}{(2x)^2} \hspace{1.5em} [\text{by quotient rule}]\\[4pt]
    &= \dfrac{6x^3+\cancel{2x} - 2x^3-\cancel{2x}+4}{4x^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{4x^3 + 4}{4x^2} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{x^3+1}{x^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we solve the equation
    $f'(x) = 0$ for $x \neq 0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{x^3+1}{x^2} &= 0  \\[5.5pt]
    x^3+1 &= 0 \\[10pt]
    x &= -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence $-1$ is the only critical number of
    $f$.

    Using the ID Test, we have

    ![9e92d9e245c8060950431511bb61a4076fa9daaf.svg](images/9e92d9e245c8060950431511bb61a4076fa9daaf.svg)

    The sign of $f'$ changes from negative to positive at $-1$.

    Hence, by the First Derivative Test, $f$ has a local minimum at
    $-1$.

    Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = -1$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(-1) \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{(-1)^3+(-1)-2}{2(-1)} \\[6pt]
    &= 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the graph of $y = f(x)$ has a local
    minimum at  $ \mbox A(-1,2)$

2.
    1.  If we calculate the $2$nd derivative of $f$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.7cm}
        f''(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[\dfrac{x^3+1}{x^2}\bigg] \\[4pt]
        &= \dfrac{(3x^2)(x^2) - (x^3+1)(2x)}{(x^2)^2} \hspace{3em} [\text{by quotient rule}] \\[4pt]
        &= \dfrac{x^4 - 2x}{x^4}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        If we solve the equation
        $f''(x) = 0$ for $x \neq 0$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{x^4 - 2x}{x^4} &= 0 \\[6pt]
        x^4 - 2x &= 0 \\[9.5pt]
        x^3 - 2 &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $x \neq 0$}] \\[8pt]
        x &= \sqrt[3]{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence there is at most one point
        of inflexion on the graph of $y = f(x)$.

        Using the Concavity Test, we have

        ![8d86187a467f2a2771ca3a277926d4698a2c343b.svg](images/8d86187a467f2a2771ca3a277926d4698a2c343b.svg)

        The graph of $y = f(x)$ changes from concave downward to concave
        upward at $\sqrt[3]{2}$.

        Hence the graph of $y = f(x)$ has an inflexion point at
        $\sqrt[3]{2}$.

        $
        \mbox{Hence there is exactly one point of inflexion on the graph of \(y = f(x)\).}$

    2.  Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = \sqrt[3]{2}$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        y &= f(\sqrt[3]{2}) \\[9pt]
        &= \dfrac{(\sqrt[3]{2})^3+\sqrt[3]{2}-2}{2\sqrt[3]{2}} \\[4pt]
        &= \dfrac{\cancel{2}+\sqrt[3]{2}-\cancel{2}}{2\sqrt[3]{2}} \\[4pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the graph of $y = f(x)$ has
        the inflexion point at B$(2^\frac{1}{3},2^{-1})$.

        $
        \mbox{Hence \(p = \dfrac 1 3\) and \(q = -1\)}$

3.  We first note that the domain of the function $f$ is
    $\mathbb{R}/\{0\} = (-\infty,0)\cup(0,+\infty)$.

    Since $f(0)$ is not defined, the graph of $y = f(x)$ has no
    $y$-intercept.

    Solving the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x\neq 0$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{x^3+x-2}{2x} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x^3+x-2 &= 0 \\[10pt]
    x &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point P$(1,0)$ is the $x$-intercept
    of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\rightarrow0^-} f(x) &= \lim_{x\rightarrow0^-} \bigg[\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{x}\bigg] \\[6pt]
    &= +\infty
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\rightarrow0^+} f(x) &= \lim_{x\rightarrow0^+} \bigg[\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{x}\bigg] \\[6pt]
    &= -\infty
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the line $x = 0$ (or the $y$-axis)
    is a vertical asymptote of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    Since
    $\displaystyle \lim_{x \rightarrow \pm \infty} f(x) = + \infty$, the
    graph of $y = f(x)$ has no horizontal asymptotes.
