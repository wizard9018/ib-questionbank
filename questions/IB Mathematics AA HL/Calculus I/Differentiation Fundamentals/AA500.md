# AA500

**Maximum mark: 7**

Let $f(x) = \dfrac{\ln x}{x}$, for $x > 0$.

1.  Find $f'(x)$. [3 marks]

2.  The graph of $f$ has a maximum at point P. Find the coordinates of
    P.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the quotient rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) & =  \dfrac{(\ln x)'(x) - (\ln x)(x)'}{x^2} \\[3pt]
    & =  \dfrac{\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)(x) - (\ln x)(1)}{x^2} \\[8pt]
    &  \hspace{.275em}=  \dfrac{1 - \ln x}{x^2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Solving $f'(x) = 0$ for stationary points, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1 - \ln x}{x^2} & =  0 \\[6pt]
    1 - \ln x & =  0 \\[9pt]
    1 & =  \ln x \\[9pt]
    e^1 & =  x \\[9pt]
    x & =  e.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Since $f'(x) > 0$ for $x < e$ and
    $f'(x) < 0$ for $x > e$, $f$ has a maximum when $x = e$.

    The $y$-coordinate of P is $f(e) = \dfrac{1}{e}$. Hence $f$ has a
    maximum at   $\textrm{P}\Big(e,\dfrac{1}{e}\Big)$
