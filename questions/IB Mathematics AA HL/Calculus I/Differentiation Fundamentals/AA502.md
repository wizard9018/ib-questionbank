# AA502

**Maximum mark: 7**

Let $f(x) = g(x)h(x)$, where $g(3) = 6$, $h(3) = 2$, $g'(3) = 4$ and
$h'(3) = 1$.

Find the equation of the normal to the graph of $f$ at $x = 3$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
y   & =  f(x) \\[5pt]
& =  f(3) \\[5pt]
& =  g(3)h(3) \\[5pt]
& =  6\cdot 2 \\[5pt]
& =  12
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.3cm}
f'(3)       & =  g'(3)h(3) + g(3)h'(3) \\[5pt]
& = 4\cdot2 + 6\cdot 1 \\[5pt]
& =  14
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence the slope of the normal to the
curve at $(x_0,y_0) = (3,12)$ is $m = -\dfrac{1}{14}$ and the equation
of the normal is given by
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_0     & =  m(x - x_0) \hspace{4mm}\\[8pt]
y - 12  & =   -\dfrac{1}{14}(x - 3) \\
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\hspace{.7cm}y      =  -\dfrac{1}{14}x + \dfrac{171}{14}
$$
