# AA1258

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider the curve with equation $y = (x + 2)\mathrm{e}^{kx}$, where $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and $k \in \mathbb{Q}$. The tangent to the curve at the point where $x = 0$ is parallel to the line $y = -3x$. Find the value of $k$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Differentiating using the **product rule (5.6)**, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(uv) = u\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} + v\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
    u &= x + 2, & \dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} &= 1 \\[6pt]
    v &= \mathrm{e}^{kx}, & \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} &= k\mathrm{e}^{kx}
\end{aligned}
$$
we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= (x+2)(k\mathrm{e}^{kx}) + \mathrm{e}^{kx}(1) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)\;A1}} \\[6pt]
    &= \mathrm{e}^{kx}\bigl(1 + k(x+2)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$
Evaluating the derivative at $x = 0$, the gradient of the tangent is
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \left.\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\right|_{x=0} &= \mathrm{e}^{0}\bigl(1 + k(0+2)\bigr) \\[6pt]
    &= 1(1 + 2k) \\[6pt]
    &= 1 + 2k \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Since the tangent is parallel to $y = -3x$, its gradient is $-3$, so we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    1 + 2k &= -3 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Solving for $k$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
    2k &= -4 \\[6pt]
    k &= \boxed{-2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
