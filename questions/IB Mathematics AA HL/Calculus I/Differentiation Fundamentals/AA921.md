# AA921

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the curve $y=(kx-1)\ln(2x)$ where $k\in \mathbb{R}$ and $x>0$. 

The tangent to the curve at $x=2$ is perpendicular to the line $y=\dfrac{2}{5+4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4}x$. 

Find the value of $k$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

First we find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ using the product rule.

Let $u=kx-1$ and $v=\mathrm{ln}(2x)$ so that $y=uv$. Therefore $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} = k$ and $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

Using the product rule, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= u\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} + v\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x} \\[10pt]

&= (kx-1)\cdot \dfrac{1}{x} + \mathrm{ln}(2x)\cdot k \\[10pt]

&= k - \dfrac{1}{x} + k\hspace{0.15em}\mathrm{ln}(2x)

\end{aligned}

$$

Thus, the derivative at $x=2$ is

$$

\begin{aligned}

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg\lvert_{x=2} &= k-\dfrac{1}{2}+k\ln 4\\[-13pt]

\end{aligned}

$$

The line $y=\dfrac{2}{5+4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4}x$ has gradient $m=\dfrac{2}{5+4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4}$. 

Given that the tangent to the curve at $x=2$ is perpendicular to this line, the tangent must have a gradient of $m=-\dfrac{5+4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4}{2}$. 

Therefore, we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg\lvert_{x=2} &= -\dfrac{5+4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4}{2} \\[8pt]

k-\dfrac{1}{2}+k\ln 4 &= \dfrac{-5-4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4}{2} \\[10pt]

2k-1+2k\ln 4 &= -5-4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4 \\[10pt]

2k+2k\ln 4 &= -4-4\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4 \\[10pt]

k(2+2\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4) &= -2(2+2\hspace{0.15em}\ln 4) \\[10pt]

& \hspace{-0.528em}\boxed{k = -2}

\end{aligned}

$$
