# AA1264

**Maximum mark: 17**

Consider the function $f$ such that $f(x)=\dfrac{2x^2-10x+20}{x-3}$.

1. Write down the equation of the vertical asymptote.[1 mark]

2. Find the coordinates of any maxima and minima on the graph of $f$. [6 marks]

The graph of $f$ approaches an oblique asymptote as $x\rightarrow \pm \infty$.

3. Find the equation of the oblique asymptote.[4 marks]

4. Sketch the graph of $f$ on the axes provided, clearly indicating any asymptotes, axes intercepts, maxima and minima. [5 marks]

![1264-1](images/1264-1.png)

5. State the range of $f$. [1 mark]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Since the function is undefined when the denominator is zero, and the numerator is non-zero at this value, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x-3 &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Therefore the equation of the vertical asymptote is

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \boxed{x = 3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. Using **the quotient rule (5.6)**, with $u=2x^2-10x+20$ and $v=x-3$, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \frac{v\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}-u\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}}{v^2} \\[6pt]

    f'(x) &= \frac{(x-3)(4x-10)-(2x^2-10x+20)(1)}{(x-3)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

    &= \frac{4x^2-22x+30-2x^2+10x-20}{(x-3)^2} \\[6pt]

    f'(x) &= \frac{2x^2-12x+10}{(x-3)^2} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Setting $f'(x)=0$ to find the stationary points, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    2x^2-12x+10 &= 0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

    x^2-6x+5 &= 0 \\[6pt]

    (x-1)(x-5) &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    So the $x$-coordinates of the stationary points are

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x &= 1 \text{ or } x = 5 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting these values into $f(x)$, we obtain

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(1) &= \frac{2(1)^2-10(1)+20}{1-3} \\[6pt]

    &= -6 \\[12pt]

    f(5) &= \frac{2(5)^2-10(5)+20}{5-3} \\[6pt]

    &= 10

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We therefore have two stationary points, $(1,-6)$ and $(5,10)$. 

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    By performing a sign-change test, we can determine whether these are maxima or minima. Let's find the value of the gradient at $x=0$, $x=2$, $x=4$ and $x=6$. 

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f'(0)&= \dfrac{2(0)^2-12(0)+10}{9} \hspace{3em}\bm{\left[f'(0)>0\right]} \\[6pt]

    f'(2) &= \dfrac{2(2)^2-12(2)+10}{1} \hspace{3em}\bm{\left[f'(2)<0\right]}\\[12pt]

    f'(4) &= \dfrac{2(4)^2-12(4)+10}{1} \hspace{3em}\bm{\left[f'(4)<0\right]}\\[6pt]

    f'(6) &= \dfrac{2(6)^2-12(6)+10}{9} \hspace{3em}\bm{\left[f'(6)>0\right]} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Before $(1,-6)$, the gradient is **positive** and immediately after, the gradient is **negative**. Before $(5,10)$, the gradient is **negative** and immediately after, the gradient is **positive**. Therefore we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &\text{maximum at } (1,\,-6) \\[8pt]

    &\text{minimum at } (5,\,10)\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. Because we are dividing a degree 2 polynomial by a degree 1 polynomial, we know that $f(x)$ can be written in the form $f(x)=ax+b+\dfrac{c}{x-3}$, where $a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$. 

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(R1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{2x^2-10x+20}{x-3} &= ax+b+\dfrac{c}{x-3} \\[6pt]

    2x^2 - 10x + 20 &= ax(x-3) + b(x-3) + c \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    &= ax^2 -3ax + bx -3b + c \\[6pt]

    2x^2 - 10x + 20 &= ax^2 - (3a-b)x + (c - 3b)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Equating the coefficients on the LHS with the RHS, we must have $a=2$.

    We must have $3a-b=10$ and therefore $b=-4$.

    We must have $c-3b = 20$ and therefore $c=8$.

    Hence, $f(x)=2x-4+\dfrac{8}{x-3}$.

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    As $x\rightarrow\pm\infty$, we have $\dfrac{8}{x-3}\rightarrow0$, so the graph approaches the line $y=2x-4$.

    Therefore the equation of the oblique asymptote is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \hspace{0.525em}\boxed{y = 2x-4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. To find the $y$-intercept, we set $x=0$, so we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    f(0) &= \frac{2(0)^2-10(0)+20}{0-3} \\[6pt]

    &= -\frac{20}{3} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    To find any $x$-intercepts, we set $f(x)=0$, which requires the numerator to be zero. Using \textbf{the formula for the discriminant (2.7)} with $a=2$, $b=-10$ and $c=20$, we have

    

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    b^2-4ac &= (-10)^2-4(2)(20) \\[6pt]

    &= -60

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

    Since the discriminant is negative, the graph has no $x$-intercepts. 

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

5. From the sketch in part (d), the maximum value on the left-hand branch is $-6$ and the minimum value on the right-hand branch is $10$, so the range is
