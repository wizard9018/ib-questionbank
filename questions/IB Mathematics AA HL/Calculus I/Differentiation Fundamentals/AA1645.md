# AA1645

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the function $f(x) = px^3 + qx$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$, where $p$ and $q$ are constants.

Find $f'(x)$ from first principles.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Applying the **derivative of $f(x)$ from first principles (5.12)**,
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h},
\end{aligned}
$$
with $f(x) = px^3 + qx$ and $f(x+h) = p(x+h)^3 + q(x+h)$, subbing in we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{\bigl[p(x+h)^3 + q(x+h)\bigr] - \bigl[px^3 + qx\bigr]}{h} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Expanding $(x+h)^3$ using the **binomial theorem (1.9)**, $(a+b)^n = \displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n} \binom{n}{r} a^{n-r} b^r$, with $n = 3$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    (x+h)^3 &= \binom{3}{0}x^3 h^0 + \binom{3}{1}x^2 h^1 + \binom{3}{2}x^1 h^2 + \binom{3}{3}x^0 h^3 \\[16pt]
    &= x^3 + 3x^2 h + 3xh^2 + h^3
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting into the definition and simplifying the numerator, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{px^3 + 3px^2 h + 3pxh^2 + ph^3 + qx + qh - px^3 - qx}{h} \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[10pt]
    &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{3px^2 h + 3pxh^2 + ph^3 + qh}{h} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Factoring $h$ from the numerator and dividing, we now have
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{h(3px^2 + 3pxh + ph^2 + q)}{h} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[10pt]
    &= \lim_{h \to 0} (3px^2 + 3pxh + ph^2 + q) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the limit as $h \to 0$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \boxed{3px^2 + q} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
