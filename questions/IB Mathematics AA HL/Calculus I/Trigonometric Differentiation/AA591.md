# AA591

**Maximum mark: 14**

Let $f(x) = \sin x - \sqrt 3 \cos x$, $0 \leq x \leq 2 \pi$.

The following diagram shows the graph of $f$.

![9ae852b18e9905650e6f91259600e3211743364b.svg](images/9ae852b18e9905650e6f91259600e3211743364b.svg)

The curve crosses the $x$-axis at $A$ and $C$ and has a maximum at point
$B$.

1.  Find the exact coordinates of $A$ and of $C$. [6 marks]

2.  Find $f'(x)$. [2 marks]

3.  Find the coordinates of $B$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Points A and C are $x$-intercepts, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sin x - \sqrt 3 \cos x    & = 0\\[6pt]
    \sin x              & = \sqrt 3 \cos x\hspace{1cm}\\[6pt]
    \dfrac {\sin x} {\cos x}    & = \sqrt 3\\[6pt]
    \tan x              & = \sqrt 3\\[6pt]
    x                   & = \dfrac \pi 3,\,\dfrac {4\pi}3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We have
      $\mbox{A}\left(\dfrac \pi 3,0\right) \mbox{ and C}\left(\dfrac {4\pi} 3,0\right)$

2.
    $$
    \hspace{1.7cm}
    f'(x)  = \cos x + \sqrt 3 \sin x
    $$

3.  B is the maximum and it occurs when $f'(x) = 0$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos x + \sqrt 3 \sin x    & = 0\\[6pt]
    \sqrt 3 \sin x             & = -\cos x\\[6pt]
    \dfrac {\sin x} {\cos x}       & = -\dfrac 1 {\sqrt 3}\\[6pt]
    \tan x                 & = -\dfrac 1 {\sqrt 3}\\[6pt]
    x                  & = \dfrac {5\pi} 6
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Substituting
    the above result into the expression for $f$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f\left(\dfrac {5\pi} 6\right)  & = \sin \left(\dfrac {5\pi} 6\right)
    - \sqrt 3 \cos \left(\dfrac {5\pi} 6\right)\\[6pt   ]
    & = \dfrac 1 2 - \sqrt 3 \left(-\dfrac {\sqrt 3} 2\right)\\[6pt]
    & = 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Finally
      $\mbox B \left(\dfrac {5\pi} 6,2\right)$
