# AA902

**Maximum mark: 22**

A function $f(x)$ is defined by
$f(x)=\arccos\left(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right)$, $x\in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Show that $f$ is an even function.[1 mark]

2.  Find the equation of the horizontal asymptote to the graph of
    $y=f(x)$.[2 marks]

3.
    1.  Show that $f'(x) =- \dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2}\left(x^2+1\right)}$
        for $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $x\neq 0$.

    2.  Using the expression for $f'(x)$ and the result
        $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$, show that $f$ is increasing for
        $x<0$.[9 marks]

A function $g$ is defined by
$g(x)=\arccos\left(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right)$, $x\in \mathbb{R}$,
$x\geq0$.

4.  Find the range of $g$. [2 marks]

5.  Find an expression for $g^{-1}(x)$. [4 marks]

6.  State the domain of $g^{-1}(x)$. [1 mark]

7.  Sketch the graph of $y=g^{-1}(x)$. Clearly indicating any
    asymptotes with their equations and stating the values of any axes
    intercepts.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Evaluating $f(-x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f(-x) &= \arccos\left(\dfrac{(-x)^2-1}{(-x)^2+1}\right) \\[12pt]
    &= \arccos\left(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right) \\[12pt]
    &= f(x)\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\text{Therefore, \(f(x)\) is an even function}$

2.  Taking the limit as $x$ tends to $\pm \infty$, using the continuity
    of the $\arccos$ function, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x \to \pm \infty} \arccos\left(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right) &= \arccos\left(\lim_{x \to \pm \infty}\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right) \\[12pt]
    &= \arccos\left(\lim_{x \to \pm \infty}\dfrac{1-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}}{1+\dfrac{1}{x^2}\rule[-3mm]{0pt}{7mm}}\right) \\[10pt]
    &= \arccos(1) \\[16pt]
    &= 0\\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $\text{Therefore \(y=0\) is a horizontal asymptote for the graph of \(y=f(x)\)}$

3.
    1.  If we calculate the derivative of $f$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[\arccos\left(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right)\bigg] \\[12pt]
        &= -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\left(\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}\right)^2}} \left(\dfrac{4x}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}\right) \hspace{2em} [\text{by chain rule}]\\[12pt]
        &= -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\dfrac{\left(x^2+1\right)^2-\left(x^2-1\right)^2}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}}} \left(\dfrac{4x}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}\right)\\[12pt]
        &= -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\dfrac{4x^2}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}}} \left(\dfrac{4x}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}\right)\\[14pt]
        &= -\dfrac{x^2+1}{2\sqrt{x^2}}\cdot \dfrac{4x}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}\\[18pt]
        &= -\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2}\left(x^2+1\right)}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  $f$ is increasing when $f'(x)>0$. Using that $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$,
        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= -\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2}\left(x^2+1\right)} \\[12pt]
        &= -\dfrac{2x}{|x|\left(x^2+1\right)} \\[12pt]
        &= \left\lbrace\begin{array}{ll} \phantom{-}\dfrac{2}{x^2+1}&x<0 \\[12pt]
        -\dfrac{2}{x^2+1}&x> 0
        \end{array}\right.\\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $\text{Therefore \(f\) is increasing over \(x<0\)}$

5.  If we solve the equation $\left(g\circ g^{-1}\right)(x)=x$ for
    $y=g^{-1}(x)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    g\left(g^{-1}(x)\right) &= x \\[12pt]
    g(y) &= x \\[12pt]
    \arccos\left(\dfrac{y^2-1}{y^2+1}\right) &= x \\[12pt]
    \dfrac{y^2-1}{y^2+1} &= \cos x\\[12pt]
    (y^2+1)\cos x&=y^2-1\\[12pt]
    y^2\cos x-y^2&=-1-\cos x\\[12pt]
    y^2(\cos x-1)&=-1-\cos x\\[12pt]
    y^2&=\dfrac{1+\cos x}{1-\cos x}\\[12pt]
    &\hspace{-2mm}g^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{1+\cos x}{1-\cos x}} \hspace{2em} \cos x \neq 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{domain of $g^{-1}$} &= \text{range of $g$} \hspace{1.7em} \\[12pt]
    &= (0,\pi]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
