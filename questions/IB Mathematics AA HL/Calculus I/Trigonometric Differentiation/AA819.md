# AA819

**Maximum mark: 17**

Consider the function $f(x) = -2\sin^2 x + 3\sin 2x + \tan x - 3$,
$0 \leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

1.
    1.  Determine an expression for $f'(x)$ in terms of $x$.

    2.  Sketch the graph of $y=f'(x)$ for $0\leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
    3.  Find the $x$-coordinate(s) of the point(s) of inflexion of the
        graph of $y=f(x)$, labelling these clearly on the graph of
        $y=f'(x)$.[5 marks]

2.  Let $u=\tan x$.
    1.  Express $\sin x$ in terms of $u$.

    2.  Express $\sin 2x$ in terms of $u$.
    3.  Show that $f(x)=0$ can be expressed as
        $u^3-5u^2+7u-3=0$.[9 marks]

3.  Solve the equation $f(x)=0$, giving your answers in the form
    $\arctan p$, where $p \in \mathbb{Z}$.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Taking the derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \big[-2\sin^2 x + 3\sin 2x + \tan x - 3\,\big] \\[12pt]
        &= -4\sin x\cos x + 6\cos 2x + \sec^2 x
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using G.D.C., we get

        ![33031ee8e485fec825d6edb17e4448dc539308eb.svg](images/33031ee8e485fec825d6edb17e4448dc539308eb.svg)

    3.  $x=0.98$

2.
    1.  Using trigonometric identities, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \cos x &= \dfrac{1}{\sec x} \\[12pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tan^2 x}}\hspace{2em} \left[\hspace{0.1em} \text{since } \sec x > 0 \text{ on } 0\leq x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}\hspace{0.1em}\right]\\[12pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now, we can
        express $\sin x$ in terms of $u$ as follows
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        u &= \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} \\[12pt]
        &= \sin x \hspace{0.1em} \sqrt{1+u^2} \hspace{3em}[\hspace{0.1em} \text{by (1)} \hspace{0.1em}]\\[12pt]
        &\hspace{-6.9mm}\sin x = \dfrac{u}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using (1) and part (i), we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \sin 2x &= 2\sin x \cos x \\[12pt]
        &= 2 \left(\dfrac{u}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\right)\\[12pt]
        &= \dfrac{2u}{1+u^2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Substituting the results in part (i) and (ii) into the
        expression of $f(x)$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(x) &= 0 \\[12pt]
        %-2\sin^2x + 3\sin 2x + \tan x - 3 \\[12pt]
        -2\left(\dfrac{u}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\right)^2 + 3\left(\dfrac{2u}{{1+u^2}}\right) + u - 3 &= 0 \\[12pt]
        \dfrac{-2u^2 + 6u + (1+u^2)(u-3)}{1+u^2} &= 0\\[12pt]
        \dfrac{u^3 - 5u^2 + 7u -3}{1+u^2} &= 0\\[12pt]
        u^3 - 5u^2 + 7u - 3 &= 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Using part (b) (iii), we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    u^3 - 5u^2 + 7u - 3 &= 0 \\[8pt]
    u &= 1,\, 3 \hspace{1.2em} [\hspace{0.1em}\text{by using G.D.C.}\hspace{0.1em}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore, the solutions to $f(x)=0$ are

    $$
    \qquadx = \arctan 1, \arctan 3
    $$
