# AA1347

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the function $g(x)=15\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{20}\right)-18$ where $0\leq x\leq 20$ and $x$ is measured in radians.

1.    

    1.    Show that $g'(x)=\dfrac{3}{4}\pi\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{20}\right)$

    2.    Hence find the equation of the tangent line at $x=14$. [4 marks]

The tangent line intersects the $x$-axis forming an angle of $\theta$. This is shown in the diagram. 

![AA1347](images/AA1347.JPEG)

2.    Find $\theta$. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1. To find the derivative of this function we will need to use the chain rule.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        g'(x)&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}(g(u))\times\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        If we set $u=\dfrac{\pi x}{20}$ then $g(u)=15\sin u -18$.

        Hence $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}x}=\dfrac{\pi}{20}$ and $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}(g(u))=15\cos u$.

        Substituting these into the formula

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        g'(x))&=15\cos\left(u\right)\times\frac{\pi}{20}\\[9pt]

        &=15\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{20}\right)\times\frac{\pi}{20}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[9pt]

        &=\frac{3\pi}{4}\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{20}\right)\hspace{2em}\text{as required}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}\\[6pt]

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. To find the equation of the tangent line we can use the equation 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        y=mx+c

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Substituting $x$ into the $g(x)$ equation we can find the associated $y$-value.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        g(14)&=15\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi (14)}{20}\right)-18\\[9pt]

        &=-5.864...\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        To find the gradient, $m$, we substitute $x=14$ into the derivative equation from part (a)(i).

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        m&=\frac{3\pi}{4}\cos\left(\frac{\pi (14)}{20}\right)\\[6pt]

        &=-1.384...\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Now we can substitute $x$, $y$, and $m$ into the line equation to solve for $c$.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        -5.864...&=-1.384...(14)+c\\[6pt]

        c&=13.52...

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence the equation of the tangent line is

        $$

        \begin{aligned}y=-1.38x+13.5\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. To find theta we recall that the gradient of a line is equal to $\tan\theta$.

    ![AA1347a2](images/AA1347a2.JPEG)

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    m=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\hspace{3em}

    \tan\theta&=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\\[12pt]

    &=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tan\theta&=m

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We found the gradient in part (a) was $m=-1.384...$ so let's substitute this value and solve for $\theta$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tan\theta&=-1.384...\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]

    \theta&=\tan^{-1}(-1.384...)\\[6pt]

    &=-0.9454...

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Our answer is an acute angle, which makes sense in context. However, as $\theta$ is the $\textit{measure}$ of the angle, we should take the absolute value as our final answer.

    Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \theta&=0.945\text{ radians}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
