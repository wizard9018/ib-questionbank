# AA295

**Maximum mark: 15**

The following diagram shows the graph of
$f(x) = a\sin kx + c$ , for $0 \leq x \leq 16$.

![0c46c1625d0d181b69606fe9f859ac512028fc3e.svg](images/0c46c1625d0d181b69606fe9f859ac512028fc3e.svg)

The graph of $f$ has a minimum at P$(4,8)$ and a maximum at Q$(12,16)$.

1.
    1.  Find the value of $c$.

    2.  Show that $k = \dfrac{\pi}{8}$.
    3.  Find the value of $a$. [6 marks]

The graph of $g$ is obtained from the graph of $f$ by a translation of
$\begin{pmatrix} d \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

The minimum point on the graph of $g$ has coordinates $(6.5,8)$.

2.
    1.  Write down the value of $d$.

    2.  Find $g(x)$. [3 marks]

The graph of $g$ changes from concave-up to concave-down when $x = \nu$.

3.
    1.  Find $\nu$.

    2.  Hence, or otherwise, find the maximum positive rate of change of
        $g$. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.65em} c &= \dfrac{f_{\text{max}} + f_{\text{min}}}{2} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{16 + 8}{2} \\[10pt]
        &= 12
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The period of $f$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{period} &= 2(12-4) \hspace{2.0em} \\[6pt]
        &= 16
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        k &= \dfrac{2\pi}{\text{period}} \hspace{0.8em} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{2\pi}{16} \\[7pt]
        &= \dfrac{\pi}{8}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Using the coordinates of P, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        f(4) &= 8 \\[5pt]
        a\sin\hspace{-0.15em}\Big(\dfrac{4\pi}{8}\Big) + 12 &= 8 \\[6pt]
        a + 12 &= 8 \hspace{9.45em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        a = -4 \hspace{2.5em}
        $$

2.
    1.  We have

        ![4c9ebf6623a7cbaa7d18df1ccb4a6937f54d7676.svg](images/4c9ebf6623a7cbaa7d18df1ccb4a6937f54d7676.svg)

        Hence, using the $x$-coordinates of P and R, we find

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        d &= 6.5 - 4 \\[7pt]
        &= 2.5
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{7em} g(x) &= f(x - d) \\[10pt]
        &= -4\sin\hspace{0.05em}((\pi\hspace{-0.05em}/8)(x - d)) + 12 \hspace{2.5em} [\text{by part (a)}] \\[10pt]
        &= -4\sin\hspace{0.05em}((\pi\hspace{-0.05em}/8)(x - 2.5)) + 12 \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (b) (i)}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.
    1.  We have

        ![16601e340ebe52d1691c9411518b8b6028078a08.svg](images/16601e340ebe52d1691c9411518b8b6028078a08.svg)

        Hence, using the $x$-coordinates of R and S, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.8em} \nu &= \dfrac{14.5 + 6.5}{2} \\[6pt]
        &= 10.5
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the chain rule, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{4.1em} g'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\Big[\hspace{-0.2em}-4\sin\hspace{0.05em}((\pi\hspace{-0.05em}/8)(x-2.5)) + 12\Big] \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{\pi}{2}\cos\hspace{0.05em}((\pi\hspace{-0.05em}/8)(x-2.5)) \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, evaluating the derivative of $g$ for $x = \nu$, we obtain

         $$
         \begin{aligned}
         \hspace{2.75em} g'(\nu) &= g'(10.5) \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (c) (i)}] \\[7pt]
         &= -\dfrac{\pi}{2}\cos\hspace{0.05em}((\pi\hspace{-0.05em}/8)(10.5-2.5)) \\[6pt]
         &\approx 1.57
         \end{aligned}
         $$
