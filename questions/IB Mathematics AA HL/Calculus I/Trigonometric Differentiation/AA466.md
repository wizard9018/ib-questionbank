# AA466

**Maximum mark: 8**

The function $h$ is defined by $h(x) = \cos(\pi\cos x)$,
 $-\pi \leq x \leq \pi$.

1.  Determine whether $h$ is even, odd or neither even nor odd.
    [2 marks]

2.  Show that $h''(0) = 0$. [4 marks]

3.  Jack states that, because $h''(x) = 0$, the graph of $h$ has an
    inflexion at the point P$(0,-1)$. Explain briefly whether Jack's
    statement is correct or not. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  For any $x \in [-\pi,\pi]$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10.9em} h(-x) &= \cos\hspace{0.05em}(\pi\cos\hspace{0.05em}(-x)) \\[6pt]
    &= \cos\hspace{0.05em}(\pi\cos x) \hspace{3em}  [\text{since $x \mapsto\cos x$ is even}] \\[6pt]
    &= h(x)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
     $\text{\(h\) is even}$

2.  Differentiating $h$ with respect to $x$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{5.4em} h'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\pi\cos x)\big] \\[6pt]
    &= \big[\hspace{-0.15em} -\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x)\big]\big[\hspace{-0.15em}-\pi \sin x\big] \hspace{3em} [\text{by chain rule}] \\[10pt]
    &= \pi \sin x \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Differentiating $h'$ with respect to $x$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    h''(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[\pi \sin x \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x)\big] \\[6pt]
    [\text{by product rule}] \hspace{2em} &= \pi \cos x \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x) + \pi \sin x \big[\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x)\big]' \\[10pt]
    [\text{by chain rule}] \hspace{2em} &= \pi \cos x \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x) + \pi \sin x\big[\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x)\big]\big[\hspace{-0.15em}-\pi\sin x\big] \\[9pt]
    &= \pi \cos x \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x) - (\pi \sin x)^2 \cos\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos x)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, evaluating $h''(x)$ for $x = 0$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.4em} h''(0) &= \pi \cos 0 \sin\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos 0) - (\pi \sin 0)^2 \cos\hspace{0.05em}(\pi \cos 0) \\[6pt]
    &= \pi(1)(0) - (0)^2(-1) \\[7pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  :::answer
    Jack's statement is incorrect. We know $h$ is even and therefore symmetrical about the $y$-axis. Hence the stationary point P$(0,-1)$ is either a maximum or a minimum.
