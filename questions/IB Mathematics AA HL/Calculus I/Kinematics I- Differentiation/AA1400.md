# AA1400

**Maximum mark: 14**

A swimmer is swimming back and forth along a straight lane in a pool, completing a turn each time she reaches a wall. The swimmer moves at a minimum velocity while executing a turn and reaches a maximum velocity midway between the two walls.

Her velocity, in $\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, can be modelled by the function

$$

\begin{aligned}

  v(t) = -0.5\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right) + 2.5, \qquad t \geq 0,

\end{aligned}

$$

where $t$ is measured in seconds.

1.  1. Find the maximum velocity of the swimmer.

    2. Hence find the time at which the swimmer first reaches her maximum velocity.[4 marks]

2. Find the number of turns the swimmer executes in the first two minutes of motion.[3 marks]

3. 1. Find $v'(t)$.

    2. Hence, or otherwise, write down the maximum acceleration of the swimmer.[3 marks]

4. Show that the length of the pool is $50$ metres.[4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1. We know that the range of cosine is $-1\leq \cos\theta \leq 1$. 

        Considering the boundaries, we can determine that the swimmer reaches her maximum velocity when $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right)=-1$ (since $-0.5\cos(\theta)$ is maximised when cosine equals $-1$). 

        $$\\[-2.5em]$$

        

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{{\textbf{R1}}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence we obtain

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            v_{\max} &= -0.5(-1)+2.5 \\[6pt]

            &= \hspace{0.2em}3\text{ m\,s}^{-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. Setting $v(t)=3$ 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            3&=-0.5\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right)+2.5\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

            -1&=\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right)

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        We know that $\cos\theta=-1$ when $\theta=\pi$. Hence 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4) &= \pi  \\[6pt]

            t-4&=10\\[6pt]

            t &= \hspace{0.2em}14\text{ seconds} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2. The swimmer turns at each minimum of $v(t)$ so we want to know the length of time between each minimum. By finding the length of one period, we can use that information to find the number of turns that occur in two minutes. 

    We find the period by using the relationship that $\text{period}=\frac{2\pi}{b}$. In our equation, $b=\frac{\pi}{10}$. Hence

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \text{period} &= \dfrac{2\pi}{^{\pi}/_{10}}\\[6pt]

        &= 20\text{ seconds} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The first maximum occurred at $t=14$ so we can subtract half of the period to determine that the first minimum occurred at $t=4$.

    Then by adding $20$ second intervals, we can determine how many minima there are. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        t=4,\,24,\,44,\,64,\,84,\,104,\,\textcolor{RVRed}{124},\ldots\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The turn after $t=104$ occurs at $t=124$, which is outside the first two minutes. 

    Hence the swimmer makes $6\text{ turns}$ in the first two minutes.

    $$\\[-2.5em]$$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{{\textbf{A1}}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3.  1. To find $v'(t)$ we can differentiate $v(t)$ using the $\textbf{chain rule (5.6)}$ where 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}u}\times\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When we let $u=\frac{\pi}{10}(t-4)$ then $v=-0.5\cos u+2.5$.

    

    Hence we have $\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\pi}{10}$ and $\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}u}=-0.5(-\sin u)$.

    

    Then using the formula

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        v'(t) &= 0.5\sin u\times\left(\frac{\pi}{10}\right)\\[6pt]

        &= 0.5\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right) \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{10}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}} \\[6pt]

        v'(t)&= \hspace{0.em}\dfrac{\pi}{20}\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    2. Considering the maximum value of $\sin(\theta)=1$, hence the maximum acceleration is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        a_{\max} &= \dfrac{\pi}{20}\cdot1\\[6pt]

        &=\hspace{0.em}\dfrac{\pi}{20}\text{ m\,s}^{-2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4. The length of the pool, $L$, can be found by determining the distance travelled by the swimmer between two consecutive turns, and we are told these coincide with minima of the function. 

    From part (b), the first two minima occur at $t=4$ and $t=24$, so using **the formula for distance travelled from $\bm{t_1}$ to $\bm{t_2}$ (5.9)**, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \text{distance} &= \int_{t_1}^{t_2} |v(t)| \, \mathrm{d}t \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt] 

        L &= \int_{4}^{24} \left[-0.5\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right)+2.5\right]\mathrm{d}t \tag*{\textbf{A1}} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Note that $v(t)>0$ for all values of $t$, therefore the absolute value signs are not required.

    Integrating term by term

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        L &= \left[-0.5\cdot\dfrac{10}{\pi}\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right)+2.5t\right]_{4}^{24} \\[6pt]

        &= \left[-\dfrac{5}{\pi}\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{10}(t-4)\right)+2.5t\right]_{4}^{24} \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]

        &= \left(-\dfrac{5}{\pi}\sin(2\pi)+60\right)-\left(-\dfrac{5}{\pi}\sin(0)+10\right) \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[6pt]

        &= 60-10 \\[6pt]

        L &= \hspace{0.em}50\text{ m}\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required} \tag*{\textbf{AG}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
