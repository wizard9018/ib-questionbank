# AA936

**Maximum mark: 7**

The velocity, $v\hspace{0.15em}\text{m}\hspace{0.15em}\text{s}^{-1}$, at time $t$ seconds, of a particle moving in a straight line is given by

$v=\dfrac{(t^2-1)\,\mathrm{sin}\hspace{0.15em}t}{2}\,$,

for $0 \leq t \leq 2$.

1. Determine when the particle changes direction for the first time. [2 marks]

2. Find the times when the acceleration of the particle is $1.4\hspace{0.15em}\text{m}\hspace{0.15em}\text{s}^{-2}$. [3 marks]

3. Find the acceleration of the particle when its speed is at its greatest. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the G.D.C. to sketch the graph of $v=\dfrac{(t^2-1)\,\mathrm{sin}\hspace{0.15em}t}{2}\,$, we get

    ![AA936a](images/AA936a.PNG)

    At time $t=1 \text{ second}\,$, $v=0$ and the velocity has changed from negative to positive. Therefore the particle has changed direction for the first time.

2. Acceleration is the derivative of velocity, therefore we must find an expression for $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}$. Using the product rule, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \dfrac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \dfrac{2t\,\mathrm{sin}\,t+(t^2-1)\,\mathrm{cos}\,t}{2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    We must solve the equation

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    1.4 &= \dfrac{2t\,\mathrm{sin}\,t+(t^2-1)\,\mathrm{cos}\,t}{2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Using the G.D.C. to sketch the graphs of $a=\dfrac{2t\,\mathrm{sin}\,t+(t^2-1)\,\mathrm{cos}\,t}{2}$ and $a=1.4$, we get

    ![AA936b](images/AA936b.PNG)

    The acceleration of the particle is $1.4\,\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{s}^{-2}$ at $t\approx1.34 \text{ seconds}\,$ and $t\approx1.88 \text{ seconds}\,$.

3. From the graph in part (a) we see that the particle's speed is greatest at time $t=2$ seconds. Substituting this value into the acceleration equation from part (b), we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    a &= \dfrac{2t\,\mathrm{sin}\,t+(t^2-1)\,\mathrm{cos}\,t}{2} \\[10pt]

    &= \dfrac{2(2)\,\mathrm{sin}\,(2)+(2^2-1)\,\mathrm{cos}\,(2)}{2} \\[10pt]

    a &\approx 1.194

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    When the particle's speed is at its greatest, the acceleration is approximately $1.19\,\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{s}^{-2}\,$.
