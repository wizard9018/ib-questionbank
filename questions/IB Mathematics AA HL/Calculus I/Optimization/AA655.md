# AA655

**Maximum mark: 15**

Let $f(x) = 16-x^2$, for $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Find the $x$-intercepts of the graph of $f$. [2 marks]

The following diagram shows part of the graph of $f$.

![d5c58a7cfd2c4ba4ba9bf55d5d266b5503182c5c.svg](images/d5c58a7cfd2c4ba4ba9bf55d5d266b5503182c5c.svg)

Rectangle ABCD is drawn with A & B on the $x$-axis and C & D on the
graph of $f$.

Let $\text{OA} = a$.

2.  Show that the area of ABCD is $32a - 2a^3$. [2 marks]

3.  Hence find the value of $a > 0$ such that the area of ABCD
    is a maximum. [4 marks]

Let $g(x) = (x-4)^2 + k$, for $x \in \mathbb{R}$, where $k$ is a
constant.

4.  Show that when the graphs of $f$ and $g$ intersect,
    $2x^2-8x+k = 0$. [2 marks]

5.  Given that the graphs of $f$ and $g$ intersect only once,
    find the value of $k$. [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Solving the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    16 - x^2 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    (4 - x)(4 + x) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x &= \pm 4 \hspace{2.8em} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the $x$-intercepts
    are
     $\text{P}(-4,0)$
    and  $\text{Q}(4,0)$

2.  Using the area of a rectangle formula, we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A_{\text{shaded}} &= \text{(AB)(AD)} \\[6pt]
    &= \hspace{-0.05em}\big(2a\big)\hspace{-0.1em}\big(16-a^2\big) \hspace{0.8em} \\[6pt]
    &= 32a - 2a^3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Hence, solving the equation
    $\big[A_{\text{shaded}}\big]' \hspace{-0.15em}= 0$ for $a > 0$, we
    get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.8em} \big[32a - 2a^3\big]' \hspace{-0.15em} &= 0 \\[11pt]
    32 - 6a^2 &= 0  \\[11pt]
    \dfrac{32}{6} &= a^2 \\[11pt]
    a &= \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{16}{3}} \\[11pt]
    a &= \pm {\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{3}}} \\[11pt]
    &\hspace{-0.75em} a = \dfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{3} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $a > 0$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Rearranging terms in the equation $f(x) = g(x)$, we deduce
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{5em} 16 - x^2 &= (x-4)^2 + k \\[6pt]
    \cancel{16} - x^2 &= (x^2 - 8x + \cancel{16}) + k \\[6pt]
    0 &= (x^2 - 8x) + x^2 + k \\
    &\hspace{-5.8em}2x^2 - 8x + k = 0 \hspace{4.95em} \hspace{3em}{(1)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  Given that the graphs of $f$ & $g$ intersect exactly once, from part
    (d), we know the equation $(1)$ has exactly one solution.

    Hence the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 8x + k=0$ has only one
    solution.

    Therefore we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.9em} [\text{discriminant of $2x^2 - 8x + k$}] &= 0 \\[6pt]
    (-8)^2 - 4(2)(k) &= 0 \hspace{3em}  [\triangle  = b^2 - 4ac]  \\[6pt]
    64 - 8k &= 0 \\[6pt]
    64 &= 8k \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{5.1em} k = 8
    $$
