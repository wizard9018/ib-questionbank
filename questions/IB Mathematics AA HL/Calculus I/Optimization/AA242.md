# AA242

**Maximum mark: 12**

In a triangle ABC, $\text{B\^{A}C} = \ang{60}$, $\text{AB} = (1-x)$ cm,
$\text{AC} = (x + 3)^2$ cm, $-3 < x < 1$.

1.  Show that the area, $A$ cm$^2$, of the triangle is given by
    $$
    A = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(9 - 3x - 5x^2 - x^3).

    $$
    [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Calculate $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}x}$.

    2.  Verify that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}x} = 0$ when
        $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.  [3 marks]

3.
    1.  Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2A}{\mathrm{d}x^2}$ and hence verify
        that $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ gives the maximum area of

        triangle ABC.

    2.  Calculate the maximum area of triangle ABC.
    3.  Find the length of \[BC\] when the area of triangle ABC is a
        maximum. [7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the area of a triangle formula, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2em} A &= \dfrac{1}{2}(\text{AB})(\text{AC})\sin\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^{A}C}) \\[8pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{2}(1-x)(x+3)^2 \sin \ang{60} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(1-x)(x^2+6x+9) \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[9-3x-5x^2-x^3\big]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Differentiating the expression $(1)$ with respect to $x$, we
        have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.1em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \bigg[\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[9-3x-5x^2-x^3\big]\bigg] \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[\hspace{-0.1em}-3-10x-3x^2\big] \\[9pt]
        &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[3 + 10x + 3x^2\big]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Evaluating the expression $(2)$ for $x = -1/3$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.7em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}x} &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[3 + 10(-1/3) + 3(-1/3)^2\big] \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[3 - 10/3 + 1/3\big] \\[14pt]
        &= 0
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.
    1.  Differentiating the expression $(2)$ with respect to $x$, we
        have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2A}{\mathrm{d}x^2} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \bigg[\hspace{-0.1em}-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[3 + 10x + 3x^2\big]\bigg] \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big[10+6x\big] \\[9pt]
        &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\big[5+3x\big]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Evaluating the expression $(3)$ for $x = -1/3$, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2A}{\mathrm{d}x^2} &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\big[5 + 3(-1/3)\big] \\[6pt]
        &= -2\sqrt{3} \\[11.5pt]
        &< 0 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, by the Second Derivative Test, $x = -1/3$ gives the maximum area of triangle ABC.

    2.  Evaluating the expression $(1)$ for $x = -1/3$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.6em} A &= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(1-x)(x+3)^2 \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(1-(-1/3))(-1/3+3)^2 \\[12pt]
        &\approx 4.11 \text{ cm}^2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Using the cosine rule (with $x = -1/3$), we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3em} \text{BC}^2 &= \text{AB}^2 + \text{AC}^2 - 2(\text{AB})(\text{AC})\cos\hspace{0.05em}(\text{B\^{A}C}) \\[6pt]
        \text{BC} &= \sqrt{(1-x)^2 + (x+3)^4 - 2(1-x)(x+3)^2\cos \ang{60}} \\[6pt]
        \text{BC} &= \sqrt{(4/3)^2 + (8/3)^4 - 2(4/3)(8/3)^2\cos \ang{60}} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \text{BC} \approx 6.55 \text{ cm} \hspace{13.75em}
        $$
