# AA232

**Maximum mark: 7**

The first three terms of a geometric sequence are

 $$
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt{2}\cos x,\hspace{0.4em} \sin 2x,\hspace{0.4em} 2\sqrt{2}\sin^2 x \cos x,\hspace{0.4em} -\dfrac{\pi}{2} < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}. \\
 \end{aligned}
 $$

1.  Find the common ratio. [1 mark]

2.  Find the set of values of $x$ for which the geometric series below
    converges.

    $$
    \sqrt{2}\cos x + \sin 2x + 2\sqrt{2}\sin^2 x \cos x + \cdots

    $$
    [3 marks]

Consider $x = \arcsin\hspace{-0.15em}\bigg(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\bigg)$,
$0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

3.  Show that the sum to infinity of this series is $\sqrt{7}$.
    [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have $u_1 = \sqrt{2}\cos x$ and $u_2 = \sin 2x$.
    Hence the common ratio is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{14.5em} r &= \dfrac{u_2}{u_1} \\[8pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sin 2x}{\sqrt{2}\cos x} \\[8pt]
    &= \dfrac{2\sin x \cos x}{\sqrt{2}\cos x} \hspace{2em} [\text{by double angle identity}] \\[12pt]
    &= \sqrt{2}\sin x
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  The geometric series converges when
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    |r| &< 1 \\[15pt]
    |\sqrt{2}\sin x| &< 1 \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (a)}] \\[12pt]
    |\sin x| &< \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\[4pt]
    \hspace{2.4em} -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} <  \sin x  &< \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    -\dfrac{\pi}{4} <  x  < \dfrac{\pi}{4} \hspace{3.5em}
    $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} u_1 &= \sqrt{2}\cos\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{-0.2em}\arcsin\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\bigg)\hspace{-0.2em}\bigg) \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{2}\times\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2\sqrt{2}} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.4em} r &= \sqrt{2}\sin\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\hspace{-0.2em}\arcsin\hspace{-0.1em}\bigg(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\bigg)\hspace{-0.2em}\bigg) \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{2}\times\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the sum of an infinite
    geometric sequence formula $S_{\infty} = \dfrac{u_1}{1-r}$ with
    $u_1 = \sqrt{7}/2$ and $r = 1/2$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S_{\infty} &= \dfrac{\sqrt{7}/2}{1 - 1/2} \\[6pt]
    &= \sqrt{7}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
