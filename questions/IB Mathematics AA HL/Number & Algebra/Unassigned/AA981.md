# AA981

**Maximum mark: 18**

Consider the family of polynomials of the form $P(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ where coefficients $a$, $b$, $c$ and $d$ belong to the set $\{2,6,8,24\}$.

1. Find the number of possible polynomials if

    

    1. each coefficient value can be repeated;

    

    2. each coefficient must be different.[4 marks]

    

Consider the case where $P(x)$ has $x+3$ as a factor, two purely imaginary roots, and all the coefficients are different.

2. 1. By considering the sum of the roots, find the two possible combinations for coefficients $a$ and $b$.

    

    2. Show that there is only one way to assign the values $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ if $P(0)=24$.[7 marks]

Now, consider the polynomial with the coefficients found in part (b) (ii).

3. 1. Express $P(x)$ as a product of one linear and one quadratic factor.

    

    2. Determine the three roots of $P(x)$.[7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Let $X$ be the total number of polynomials.

    1. Given that each coefficient value can be repeated, we have 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        X&=4 \times 4 \times 4 \times 4\\[6pt]

        &=4^4\\[6pt]

        &=256

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Therefore, there are $\boxed{256}$ possible polynomials if the coefficients can be repeated.

    2. Given that each coefficient must be different, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        X&=4!\\[6pt]

        &=4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1\\[6pt]

        &=24

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Therefore, there are $\boxed{24}$ possible polynomials if each coefficient must be different.

2. 1. Let the roots be $x_1, x_2$ and $x_3$. Given that $x+3$ is a factor, $x_1=-3$ is a root. Complex roots occur in conjugate pairs, therefore if the other two roots are imaginary, we have $x_2 = \alpha \mathrm{i}$ and $x_3 = -\alpha \mathrm{i}$, where $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence, the sum of the roots is given by

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x_1 + x_2 + x_3 &= -3 + \alpha \mathrm{i} - \alpha \mathrm{i} \\[6pt]

        &= -3

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Using the formula for the sum of the roots, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x_1 + x_2 + x_3 &= -\dfrac{b}{a} \\[6pt]

        -3 &= -\dfrac{b}{a} \\[6pt]

        3a &= b

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        We have that $a$ and $b$ belong to the set $\{2,6,8,24\}$ and $3a=b$. Therefore,

        $$\boxed{\text{The two possible combinations are $a=2,\,b=6$ and $a=8,\,b=24$.}}$$

    2. If $P(0)=24$, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        P(0) &= a(0)^3 + b(0)^2 + c(0) + d \\[6pt]

        24 &= d

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        The coefficients must all be different. If $d=24$, $b \neq 24$, so we have from part (b)(i) that $a=2$ and $b=6$. Therefore $c=8$.

        $$\boxed{\text{If $P(0)=24$, we must have $a=2,\,b=6,\,c=8$ and $d=24$.}}$$

3. 1. Our polynomial is $P(x) = 2x^3 + 6x^2 + 8x + 24$. Given that $x+3$ is a factor, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        2x^3 + 6x^2 + 8x + 24 &= (x+3)(2x^2 + ex + 8) \\[6pt]

        &= 2x^3 +ex^2 + 8x + 6x^2 +3ex + 24 \\[6pt]

        2x^3 + 6x^2 + 8x + 24 &= 2x^3 + (e+6)x^2 + (8+3e)x +24

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Equating the coefficients of $x^2$ and $x$ on the LHS and RHS, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        6 &= e + 6 \\[6pt]

        8 &= 8 + 3e

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        We must have $e=0$. Therefore

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        P(x) &= (x+3)(2x^2 + 8) \\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-2.1em}\boxed{P(x) = 2(x+3)(x^2 + 4)}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. We have from part (b)(i) that $x_1=-3$. To find the other two roots, we solve $x^2+4=0$:

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        x^2+4&=0 \\[6pt]

        x^2 &= -4 \\[6pt]

        x &= \pm \sqrt{-4} \\[6pt]

        x &= \pm 2\mathrm{i}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        $$\boxed{\text{The roots of $P(x)$ are $x_1=-3,\,x_2=-2\mathrm{i}$, and $x_3=2\mathrm{i}$.}}$$
