# AA102

**Maximum mark: 9**

1.  Find three distinct roots of the equation $z^3 + 64 = 0$,
    $z \in \mathbb{C}$, giving your answers in modulus-argument form.
    [6 marks]

The roots are represented by the vertices of a triangle in an Argand
diagram.

2.  Show that the area of the triangle is $12\sqrt{3}$.
    [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the equality
    $-1 = \cos \pi + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \pi$, we
    have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z^3 &= -64 \\[6pt]
    &= 64(\cos \pi + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \pi)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using de Moivre's theorem, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9em} z &= 64^\frac{1}{3}(\cos \pi + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \pi)^\frac{1}{3} \\[5pt]
    &= 4\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{\pi + 2k\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{\pi + 2k\pi}{3}\Big)\Big]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    for $k = 0,1,2$.

    Hence the roots of the equation $z^3 = -64$, in modulus-argument form, are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    k = 0 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = 4\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] \\[10pt]
    k = 1 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = 4\Big[\cos \pi + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \pi \Big]  \\[10pt]
    k = 2 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = 4\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \Big(\dfrac{5\pi}{3}\Big)\Big]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  If we sketch the triangle whose vertices represent the roots $(1)$
    on an Argand diagram, we have

    ![7ba8fa24617bc7505a9ecf65b0f77c82f33a610b.svg](images/7ba8fa24617bc7505a9ecf65b0f77c82f33a610b.svg)

    Hence the area of the triangle ABC is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Area} &= 3\bigg[\dfrac{1}{2}(4)(4)\sin \bigg(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\bigg)\bigg] \\[4pt]
    &= 3\bigg[\dfrac{1}{2}(4)(4)\bigg(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\bigg)\bigg]  \\[12pt]
    &= 12\sqrt{3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
