# AA986

**Maximum mark: 5**

Consider a geometric sequence with common ratio $r$ such that $0 < r < 1$.

1. Show that $u_n - u_{n+1} = u_n(1-r)$.[2 marks]

A geometric sequence has a first term of 150 and a second term of 120.

2. Find the smallest value of $n$ such that $u_n - u_{n+1} < 1$.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using the formula for the $n$th term of a geometric sequence, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_n - u_{n+1} &= u_1r^{n-1} - u_1r^{n} \\[8pt]

    &= u_1r^{n-1}(1-r) \\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-4.5em}\boxed{u_n - u_{n+1} = u_n(1-r)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

2. For a geometric sequence, we have $r=\dfrac{u_2}{u_1}$. Therefore $r = \dfrac{120}{150}$.

    Hence $u_1 = 150$ and $r=0.8$.

    We are solving the inequality $u_n - u_{n+1} < 1$. Using our result from part (a), we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    u_n(1-r) &< 1 \\[8pt]

    u_n(1-0.8) &< 1 \\[8pt]

    150(0.8)^{n-1}(0.2) &< 1 \\[8pt]

    150(0.8)^{n-1} &< 5

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Sketching the graphs of $y=150(0.8)^{x-1}$ and $y=5$ on the G.D.C. we look for the smallest integer value of $x$ for which $y<5\,$:

    ![aa986](images/aa986.PNG)

    The smallest integer greater than $16.2$ is $17$. Hence

    $$\boxed{\text{The smallest value of $n$ such that $u_n - u_{n+1}<1$ is $n=17$.}}$$
