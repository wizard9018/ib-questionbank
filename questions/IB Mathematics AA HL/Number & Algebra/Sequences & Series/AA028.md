# AA028

**Maximum mark: 14**

The cubic polynomial equation $x^3 + bx^2 + cx + d = 0$ has three roots
$x_1, x_2$ and $x_3$. By expanding the product $(x-x_1)(x-x_2)(x-x_3)$,
show that

1.
    1.  $b = -(x_1+x_2+x_3)$;

    2.  $c = x_1x_2 + x_1x_3 + x_2x_3$;
    3.  $d = -x_1x_2x_3$. [3 marks]

It is given that $b = -9$ and $c = 45$ for parts (b) and (c) below.

2.
    1.  In the case that the three roots $x_1, x_2$ and $x_3$ form an
        arithmetic\
        sequence, show that one of the roots is $3$.

    2.  Hence determine the value of $d$. [5 marks]

3.  In another case the three roots form a geometric sequence.
    Determine\
    the value of $d$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Given the three roots $x_1$, $x_2$ and $x_3$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x^3 + bx^2 + cx + d &= (x-x_1)(x-x_2)(x-x_3) \\[6pt]
    &= (x^2 -(x_1+x_2)x + x_1x_2)(x-x_3) \\[8pt]
    &= x^3 - (x_1+x_2+x_3)x^2 + (x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3)x - x_1x_2x_3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, comparing the coefficients, we get

    1.
        $b = -(x_1+x_2+x_3)$

    2.
        $c = x_1x_2 + x_1x_3 + x_2x_3$

    3.
        $d = -x_1x_2x_3$

2.
    1.  Letting $\alpha = x_1 + \delta$, where $\delta$ is the common
        difference of the arithmetic sequence $x_1$, $x_2$, $x_3$, we
        have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.8em} x_1  &=  \alpha - \delta \\[6pt]
        x_2  &=  \alpha \\[6pt]
        x_3  &=  \alpha + \delta
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, using part (a)
        (i), we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        x_1 + x_2 + x_3 &= -b \\[6pt]
        (\alpha - \delta) + \alpha + (\alpha + \delta) &= -(-9) \hspace{6em} \\[6pt]
        3\alpha &= 9 \\[6pt]
        \alpha &= 3 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        x_2 = 3
        $$

    2.  Using part (a) (ii), we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        x_1x_2 + x_1x_3 + x_2x_3 &= c \\[6pt]
        (3 - \delta)(3) + (3 - \delta)(3 + \delta) + (3)(3 + \delta) &= 45 \\[6pt]
        27 - \delta^2 &= 45 \\[6pt]
        -\delta^2 &= 18 \\[5pt]
        \delta &= \sqrt{18}\hspace{0.05em}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\hspace{2em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, using part (a) (iii), we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{4em} d &= -x_1x_2x_3 \\[5pt]
        &= -(3-\sqrt{18}\hspace{0.05em}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu})(3)(3+\sqrt{18}\hspace{0.05em}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}) \\[6pt]
        &= -81
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Letting $\alpha = x_1$ and $r$ denote the common ratio of the
    geometric sequence $x_1, x_2, x_3$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.2em} x_1  &=  \alpha \\[6pt]
    x_2  &=  \alpha r \\[6pt]
    x_3  &=  \alpha r^2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7.25em} x_1 + x_2 + x_3 &= -b \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (a) (i)}] \\[6pt]
    \alpha + \alpha r + \alpha r^2 &= -(-9) \\[6pt]
    \alpha (1+r+r^2) &= 9
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    x_1x_2 + x_1x_3 + x_2x_3 &= c \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (a) (ii)}] \\[6pt]
    \alpha (\alpha r) + \alpha (\alpha r^2) + (\alpha r)(\alpha r^2) &= 45 \\[6pt]
    \alpha^2r(1+r+r^2) &= 45
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Taking the ratio
    of the expressions $(1)$ and $(2)$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{\alpha^2r(\cancel{1+r+r^2})}{\alpha(\cancel{1+r+r^2})} &= \dfrac{45}{9} \hspace{4em} \\[5pt]
    \dfrac{\alpha^2r}{\alpha} &= \dfrac{45}{9} \\[15pt]
    \alpha r &= 5
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the expression $(3)$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6.5em} d &= -x_1x_2x_3 \hspace{3em} [\text{by (a) (iii)}] \\[5pt]
    &= -\alpha (\alpha r)(\alpha r^2) \\[5pt]
    &= -(\alpha r)^3 \\[5pt]
    &= -5^3 \\[6pt]
    &= -125
    \end{aligned}
    $$
