# AA923

**Maximum mark: 18**

The first three terms of an infinite sequence, in order, are 

$$2\ln x,\,\, q\ln x,\,\, \ln \sqrt{x}\,\,\,  \text{ where $\ x > 0$.} $$

First consider the case in which the series is geometric.

1. 1. Find the possible values of $q$.

    2. Hence or otherwise, show that the series is convergent. [3 marks]

2. Given that $q>0$ and $S_\infty=8\ln{3}$, find the value of $x$. [3 marks]

Now suppose that the series is arithmetic.

3. 1. Show that $q=\dfrac{5}{4}$.

    2. Write down the common difference in the form $m\ln x$, where $m \in \mathbb{Q}$. [4 marks]

4. Given that the sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence is $\ln \sqrt{x^5}$, find the value of $n$. [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. 1. Given that the sequence is geometric, we have for the common ratio

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        r &= \dfrac{u_2}{u_1} \\[8pt]

        &= \dfrac{q\ln x}{2\ln x} \\[8pt]

        &= \dfrac{q}{2}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Similarly,

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        r &= \dfrac{u_3}{u_2} \\[8pt]

        \dfrac{q}{2} &= \dfrac{\ln \sqrt{x}}{q\ln x} \\[8pt]

        \dfrac{q}{2} &= \dfrac{\frac{1}{2}\ln {x}}{q\ln x} \\[8pt]

        \dfrac{q}{2} &= \dfrac{1}{2q} \\[8pt]

        q^2 &= 1 \\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-0.49em}\boxed{q= \pm 1}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. Given that $r=\dfrac{q}{2}$, and $q= \pm 1$, we have $r = \pm \dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $|r| < 1$.

        $$\text{In both cases the series converges.}$$

2. Given that $q>0$, $r = \dfrac{1}{2}$. 

    Using the formula for the sum of an infinite geometric sequence, we~get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    S_{\infty} &= \dfrac{u_1}{1-r} \\[10pt]

    8 \ln 3 &= \dfrac{2\ln x}{1-\frac{1}{2}} \\[10pt]

    8 \ln 3 &= \dfrac{2\ln x}{\frac{1}{2}} \\[10pt]

    8 \ln 3 &= 4\ln x \\[10pt]

    2\ln 3 &= \ln x \\[10pt]

    \ln 3^2 &= \ln x \\[10pt]

    \ln 9 &= \ln x \\[10pt]

    &\hspace{-0.54em}\boxed{x = 9} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$

3. 1. First we find the common difference $d$ as follows

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        d &= u_2 - u_1\\[10pt]

        d &= q\ln x - 2\ln x \\[10pt]

        d &= (q-2)\ln x

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Similarly,

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        d &= u_3 - u_2\\[10pt]

        (q-2)\ln x &= \ln\sqrt{x} - q\ln x \\[8pt]

        (q-2)\ln x &= \ln x^{1/2} - q\ln x \\[8pt]

        (q-2)\ln x &=  \dfrac{1}{2}\ln x - q\ln x \\[8pt]

        (q-2)\ln x &= \bigg(\dfrac{1}{2} - q\bigg)\ln x \\[8pt]

        q-2 &= \dfrac{1}{2}-q \\[8pt]

        2q &= \dfrac{1}{2} + 2 \\[8pt]

        2q &= \dfrac{5}{2} \\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-0.535em}\boxed{q = \dfrac{5}{4}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. Given that $d = (q-2)\ln x$ and $q = \dfrac{5}{4}$, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        d &= \left(\dfrac{5}{4}-2\right)\ln x \\[10pt]

        &\hspace{-0.535em}\boxed{d = -\dfrac{3}{4}\ln x}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

4. Using the formula for the sum of $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence, we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    S_n &= \dfrac{n}{2}(2u_1 + (n-1)d) \\[8pt]

    \ln \sqrt{x^5} &= \dfrac{n}{2}\left(2\hspace{0.1em}(2\ln x) + (n-1)\left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\ln x \right)\right) \\[8pt]

    \ln x^{5/2} &= \dfrac{n}{2}\left(\left(4\ln x + \left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right)(n-1)\ln x\right) \right) \\[8pt]

    \dfrac{5}{2}\ln x &= \dfrac{n}{2}\left(4 - \dfrac{3(n-1)}{4}\right)\ln x \\[8pt]

    5 &= n\left(4 - \dfrac{3(n-1)}{4}\right) \\[8pt]

    5 &= 4n - \dfrac{3n(n-1)}{4} \\[8pt]

    20 &= 16n - 3n(n-1) \\[8pt]

    20 &= 16n - 3n^2 + 3n \\[8pt]

    3n^2 -19n +20 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    3n^2 -15n -4n +20 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    3n(n-5) - 4(n-5) &= 0 \\[8pt]

    (3n-4)(n-5) &= 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The solutions to the quadratic equation are $n=\dfrac{4}{3}$ and $n=5$.

    Since $n$ must be an integer, we have $n = 5$
