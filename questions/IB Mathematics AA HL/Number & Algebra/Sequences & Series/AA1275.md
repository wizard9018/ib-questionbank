# AA1275

**Maximum mark: 13**

A geometric sequence, with common ratio $r$, has a first term of $81$. The sum of the first four terms of the sequence is $195$.

1.  1.  Find $r$.

    2.  Hence, find $G_n$, the sum of the first $n$ terms of the geometric sequence. [3 marks]

The first three terms of an arithmetic sequence, with a common difference of $d$, are $\log 96, \log 48$ and $\log24$.

2.  Find $d$ in the form $p\log q$ where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$.[2 marks]

$A_n$ is the sum of the first $n$ terms of the arithmetic sequence.

3.  Show that $A_n=\log{\left(3^n\times\left(\sqrt{2}\right)^{11n-n^2}\right)}$.[5 marks]

4.  Find the maximum value of $n$ such that $\left|G_n\right|>\left|A_n\right|$.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  From the given information we know $u_1=81$ and $S_4=195$. 

        

        Using the formula for the sum of a geometric sequence we can form an equation and then solve for $r$.

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            S_n&=\frac{u_1(1-r^n)}{1-r}\\[12pt]

            S_4&=\frac{u_1(1-r^4)}{1-r}\\[12pt]

            195&=\frac{81(1-r^4)}{1-r}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Using a G.D.C. we get

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            r&=0.6666...\\[6pt]

            r&=\dfrac{2}{3}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        

    2.  Using the answer from (a) (i) and the formula for the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric series we can write the general term for $G_n$ as 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            G_n&=\frac{81(1-(\frac{2}{3})^n)}{1-\frac{2}{3}}\\[12pt]

            &=\frac{81(1-(\frac{2}{3})^n)}{\frac{1}{3}}\\[12pt]

            &=81\left(1-\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^n\right)\times3\\[6pt]

            &=243\left(1-\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^n\right)\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

2.  In an arithmetic sequence, there is a common difference between terms. 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d&=u_2 - u_1

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting the given values for $u_1$ and $u_2$ we get

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    d&=\log 48-\log96\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

    d&=\log\left(\frac{48}{96}\right)\\[12pt]

    d&=\log \frac{1}{2}\\[12pt]

    d&=\log 2^{-1}\\[6pt]

    d&=-\log 2\tag*{\textbf{A1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    

3.  The formula for sum of an arithmetic sequence is

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        S_n&=\frac{n}{2}(2u_1+(n-1)d)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Substituting $u_1=\log 96$ and $d=-\log 2$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        A_n&=\frac{n}{2}(2\log 96+(n-1)(-\log 2))\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]

        &=\frac{n}{2}(\log96^2-(n-1)\log 2)

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Applying logarithm rules we get     

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\

    &=\frac{n}{2}(\log96^2-\log2^{(n-1)})\\[12pt]

    &=\frac{n}{2}\log \frac{96^2}{2^{(n-1)}}\\[12pt]

    &=\log\left(\frac{96^2}{2^{(n-1)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\\[12pt]

    &=\log\sqrt{\left(\frac{96^2}{2^{(n-1)}}\right)^n}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[12pt]

    &=\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{96^2}}{\sqrt{2^{(n-1)}}}\right)^n\\[12pt]

    &=\log{\left(\frac{96^n}{(\sqrt{2})^{n^2-n}}\right)}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Looking at the form of the answer we can attempt to rewrite the denominator using by negating its exponent 

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\log(96^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{n-n^2})

    \end{aligned}

    $$ 

    Let's rewrite $96$ as a product of prime factors

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\log((2^5\times3)^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{n-n^2})\tag*{\textbf{M1}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Then we'll rewrite $2^5$ as a base of $\sqrt{2}$

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\log(((\sqrt{2})^{10}\times3)^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{n-n^2})

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Now we can distribute the power of $n$ and then use exponent laws again to simplify to the required form.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    &=\log((\sqrt{2})^{10n}\times3^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{n-n^2})\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]

    &=\log(3^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{10n+n-n^2})\\[6pt]

    &=\log(3^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{11n-n^2})\hspace{0.5em}\text{... as required.}

    \end{aligned}

    $$

4.  Using the functions from (a) (ii) and (c) we can form an inequality which we can solve for $n$.

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \textcolor{red}{\left|243\left(1-\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^n\right)\right|}>\textcolor{blue}{\left|\log(3^n\times(\sqrt{2})^{11n-n^2})\right|}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
