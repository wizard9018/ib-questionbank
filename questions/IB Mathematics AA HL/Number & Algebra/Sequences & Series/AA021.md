# AA021

**Maximum mark: 15**

The first three terms of an infinite geometric sequence are
$k-4,\,\, 4,\,\, k+2$, where $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

1.
    1.  Write down an expression for the common ratio, $r$.

    2.  Hence show that $k$ satisfies the equation
        $k^2 - 2k - 24 = 0$.[5 marks]

2.
    1.  Find the possible values for $k$.

    2.  Find the possible values for $r$. [5 marks]

3.  The geometric sequence has an infinite sum.

    1.  Which value of $r$ leads to this sum. Justify your answer.

    2.  Find the sum of the sequence. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  The common ratio is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        r &= \dfrac{u_2}{u_1} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{4}{k-4}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        or
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        r &= \dfrac{u_3}{u_2} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{k+2}{4}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Hence we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.5em} \dfrac{4}{k-4} &= \dfrac{k+2}{4} \\[6pt]
        16 &= (k+2)(k-4) \\[10pt]
        16 &= k^2 - 2k - 8 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        k^2 - 2k - 24 = 0 \hspace{7.05em}
        $$

2.
    1.  Solving the equation $k^2 - 2k - 24 = 0$ for $k$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        k^2 - 2k - 24 &= 0 \\[6pt]
        (k+4)(k-6) &= 0 \hspace{7.65em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        k = -4,\, 6 \hspace{0.25em}
        $$

    2.  Evaluating $r = \dfrac{4}{k-4}$ for $k = -4,\, 6$, we obtain

         $$
         \begin{aligned}
         \hspace{1.5em} r &= \dfrac{4}{(-4)-4} \\[6pt]
         &= -\dfrac{1}{2}
         \end{aligned}
         $$

        and
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        r &= \dfrac{4}{6-4} \\[9pt]
        &= 2
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.
    1.  The infinite sum exists when $|r| < 1$.
        We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \bigg|\hspace{-0.15em}-\dfrac{1}{2}\hspace{0.1em}\bigg| &= \dfrac{1}{2} < 1
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence
         $r = - 1/2$
        leads to this sum.

    2.  Using the sum of an infinite geometric sequence formula
        $S_{\infty} = \dfrac{u_1}{1-r}$,

        we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        S_{\infty} &= \dfrac{(-8)}{1 - (-1/2)} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{(-8)}{3/2} \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{16}{3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
