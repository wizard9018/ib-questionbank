# AA1330

**Maximum mark: 6**

At a school fair, there are $15$ booths in the auditorium. These booths are arranged in $3$ rows of $5$ booths each, as shown below.

![AA1330-q](images/AA1330-q.JPEG)

There are $15$ volunteers, including Arman and Bojanna. Each volunteer  is assigned to one booth.

$5$ of the volunteers are wearing a red shirt, $5$ are wearing a blue shirt and $3$ plus Arman and Bojanna are wearing a green shirt.

For planning purposes, other than Arman and Bojanna, those volunteers wearing the same colour shirt are indistinguishable.

The committee initially decides that Arman and Bojanna must both be assigned to a booth in the front row.

1.    Find the total number of ways the $15$ volunteers can be assigned to booths. [3 marks]

After some deliberation, the committee revises its plan and decides that Arman and Bojanna must both be assigned to booths in the same row, either the middle row or the back row.
- If they are in the middle row, they must not be seated next to each other.
- If they are in the back row, there must be at least two booths between them.

2.    Find the total number of ways the $15$ volunteers can be assigned to booths. [3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The front row has $5$ booths.
    This means there are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \binom{5}{2} = 10
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    ways to choose $2$ booths.
    There are $2!$ ways for Arman and Bojanna to occupy these two booths.
    Therefore, there are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    10 \times 2! \tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    ways for Arman and Bojanna to choose two booths in the front row.

    There are $13$ volunteers remaining which means, without restriction, there are $13!$ ways to seat them.

    However, as the volunteers with the same shirt colour are indistinguishable then, if we use $13!$, we have over counted.

    For example, if we sat $5$ of the red shirted volunteers in the middle row then the following two arrangements, though different, are indistinguishable
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{arrangement 1}: R_1, R_2, R_3, R_4, R_5\\[6pt]
    \text{arrangement 2}: R_2, R_1, R_3, R_4, R_5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore we have over counted by $5!$ ways for the red shirts.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Similarly, we have over counted $5!$ for the blue.

    With the green, there are $3$ volunteers left, we have already counted booths for Arman and Bojanna, therefore we have over counted by $3!$ ways.

    Therefore, the total number of ways to assign all $15$ volunteers is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \binom{5}{2} \times 2! \times \frac{13!}{5!5!3!} &= 1\,441\,440\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. Let’s first consider the middle row.
    As in part (a), the middle row has $5$ booths, so there are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \binom{5}{2} = 10
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    ways to choose $2$ booths.

    However, Arman and Bojanna cannot occupy adjacent booths.

    In this case, it is easier to count how many combinations are not allowed and subtract this from the total.

    If we number the booths in the middle row

    ![AA1330-a1](images/AA1330-a1.JPEG)

    the pairings
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &(1,2) && (2,3) && (3,4) && (4,5)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    are not allowed.

    Therefore, $4$ of the $10$ combinations are not allowed.

    Hence, there are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (10 - 4) \times 2!\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    valid ways to assign Arman and Bojanna to booths in the middle row.
    Now let’s consider the back row.

    Similarly, there are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \binom{5}{2} = 10
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    ways to choose $2$ booths.

    However, there must be at least two booths between Arman and Bojanna.
    Since there are only a few possibilities, we can count them directly.

    If we number the booths in the back row

    ![AA1330-a2](images/AA1330-a2.JPEG)

    the valid pairings are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    &(1,4) && (1,5) && (2,5)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore, there are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3 \times 2!\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    valid ways to assign Arman and Bojanna to booths in the back row.

    Therefore, using a similar concept to part (a), the total number of ways to assign the pair under these conditions is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \big[(10 - 4) \times 2! + 3 \times 2!\big] \times  \frac{13!}{5!5!3!} &= 1\,297\,296\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
