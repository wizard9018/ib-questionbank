# AA1010

**Maximum mark: 6**

In an art museum, there are 8 different paintings by Picasso, 5 different paintings by Van Gogh, and 3 different paintings by Rembrandt. The curator of the museum wants to hold an exhibition in a hall that can only display a maximum of 7 paintings at a time.

The curator wants to include at least two paintings from each artist in the exhibition.

1. Given that 7 paintings will be displayed, determine how many ways they can be selected. [4 marks]

2. Find the probability that more Rembrandt paintings will be selected than Picasso paintings or Van Gogh paintings. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Given that there must be at least 2 paintings by each artist, there are three cases to consider:

    1. 3 Picasso, 2 Van Gogh, 2 Rembrandt

    2. 2 Picasso, 3 Van Gogh, 2 Rembrandt

    3. 2 Picasso, 2 Van Gogh, 3 Rembrandt

    Let $k_1$ be the total number of combinations from option 1, $k_2$ the total from option 2 and $k_3$ the total from option 3.

    In the first case, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        k_1 &= {}^8C_3 \times {}^5C_2 \times {}^3C_2 \\[6pt]

        &= 1680

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    In the second case, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        k_2 &= {}^8C_2 \times {}^5C_3 \times {}^3C_2 \\[6pt]

        &= 840

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    In the third case, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        k_3 &= {}^8C_2 \times {}^5C_2 \times {}^3C_3 \\[6pt]

        &= 280

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Overall, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        k &= k_1 + k_2 + k_3 \\[6pt]

        &= 1680 + 840 + 280

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    In total there are $\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{2800}\hspace{0.525em}$ ways in which the paintings can be selected.

2. For there to be more Rembrandt paintings selected than Picasso or Van Gogh, there must be 3 by Rembrandt, 2 by Picasso and 2 by Van Gogh. From part (a), there are 280 possible combinations that fit this criteria, and 2800 combinations overall. Therefore

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

        \mathrm{P}(3\mathrm{R}\,2\mathrm{P}\,2\mathrm{V}) &= \dfrac{280}{2800} \\[10pt]

    &\hspace{-5.5em}\boxed{\mathrm{P}(3\mathrm{R}\,2\mathrm{P}\,2\mathrm{V}) = \dfrac{1}{10}} 

    \end{aligned}

    $$
