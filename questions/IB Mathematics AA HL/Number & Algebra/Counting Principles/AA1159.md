# AA1159

**Maximum mark: 5**

A coed soccer team consists of $2$ boys and $3$ girls.

They are standing in a line posing for a team picture.

Find the number of arrangements such that

1.  All the boys stand next to each other and all the girls stand next to each other. [1 mark]

2.  All the boys stand next to each other. [1 mark]

3.  All the boys are apart and all the girls are apart.[1 mark]

4.  Just the boys are apart. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We can treat the boys as a single entity and the girls as a single entity.

    There are $2!$ ways to arrange the boys and $3!$ ways to arrange the girls.

    We can arrange each entity in $2$ ways, i.e.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \underline{B} \hspace{1em}\underline{G}\\[6pt]
        \underline{G} \hspace{1em}\underline{B}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2! \times 3! \times 2&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{24} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2.  In this case we treat the boys as a single entity.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}} \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_1} \hspace{1em} \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}}  \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_2} \hspace{1em}  \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}} \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_3} \hspace{1em}  \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}} \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can see above that this single entity has a choice of $4$ positions. Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2! \times 3! \times 4&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{48} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
3.  The only way to keep all of the girls apart is place the boys between them
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \underline{G_1} \hspace{1em} \underline{\textcolor{black}{B_1}}  \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_2} \hspace{1em}  \underline{\textcolor{black}{B_2}} \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_3}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    There is only one way to do this, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2! \times 3! \times 1&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{12} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
4.  We can use a similar diagram as part(b)
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}} \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_1} \hspace{1em} \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}}  \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_2} \hspace{1em}  \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}} \hspace{1em}  \underline{G_3} \hspace{1em}  \underline{\textcolor{white}{B}} \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can see $4$ positions to place the $2$ boys. Therefore there are ${}_{4}C_{2}$ ways to place the boys.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the formula ${}_{4}C_{2}=\tfrac{4!}{2!2!}=6$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2! \times 3! \times 6&=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{72} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
