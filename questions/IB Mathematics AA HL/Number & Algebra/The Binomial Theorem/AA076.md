# AA076

**Maximum mark: 5**

Use the extension of the binomial theorem for $n \in \mathbb{Q}$ to show that
$\dfrac{x}{(1+x)^2} \approx x - 2x^2 + 3x^3$,
$|x| < 1$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{x}{(1+x)^2} &= x(1+x)^{-2} \hspace{2.3em}
\end{aligned}
$$
Using
the fractional binomial theorem, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{3em} (1+x)^{-2} &= \sum_{k=0}^\infty \binom{-2}{k} x^k \hspace{3em} \big[|x| < 1\big] \\[3pt]
&= 1 + (-2)x + \dfrac{(-2)(-3)}{2!}x^2 + \dfrac{(-2)(-3)(-4)}{3!}x^3 + \cdots \\[15pt]
&= 1 - 2x + 3x^2 - 4x^3 + \cdots
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1em} x(1+x)^{-2} &= x(1 - 2x + 3x^2 - 4x^3 + \cdots) \\[6pt]
&= x - 2x^2 + 3x^3 - 4x^4 + \cdots
\end{aligned}
$$
For $|x| < 1$, the
term in $x^k$ is small for $k \geq 4$.

Hence we deduce
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2.6em} \dfrac{x}{(1+x)^2} \approx x - 2x^2 + 3x^3 \hspace{3em} \big[|x| < 1\big]
\end{aligned}
$$
