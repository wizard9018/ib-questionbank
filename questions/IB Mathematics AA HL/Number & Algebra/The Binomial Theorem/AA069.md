# AA069

**Maximum mark: 7**

Given that $(1 + x)^3(1 + px)^4 = 1 + qx + 93x^2 + \dots + p^4x^7$, find
the possible values of $p$ and $q$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Using Pascal triangle

![d6143401ad9783a3554c63b7ab804d89d1d7b5f1.svg](images/d6143401ad9783a3554c63b7ab804d89d1d7b5f1.svg)

we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
1 + qx + 93x^2 + \dots + p^4x^7 &= (1 + x)^3(1 + px)^4 \\[6pt]
&= \big(1 + 3x + 3x^2\hspace{-0.15em} + x^3\big)\hspace{-0.15em}\big(1 + 4px + 6p^2x^2\hspace{-0.15em} + 4p^3x^3\hspace{-0.15em} + p^4x^4\big)
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
qx &= 4px + 3x \hspace{2.1em} \\[6pt]
q &= 4p + 3
\end{aligned}
$$
and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{2em} 93x^2 &= 6p^2x^2 + 12px^2 + 3x^2 \\[6pt]
93 &= 6p^2 + 12p + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, solving the equation $(2)$ for $p$, we find
$$
\begin{aligned}
6p^2 + 12p + 3 &= 93 \\[6pt]
6p^2 + 12p - 90 &= 0 \\[6pt]
p^2 + 2p - 15 &= 0 \\[7pt]
(p + 5)(p - 3) &= 0 \\[7pt]
p &= -5,\,3
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, substituting $p = -5,\,3$ in the
equation $(1)$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.1em} q &= 4(-5) + 3 \\[6pt]
&= -17
\end{aligned}
$$
and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{0.3em} q &= 4(3) + 3 \\[6pt]
&= 15
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
 $p = -5,\,q=-17$
and  $p = 3,\,q=15$
