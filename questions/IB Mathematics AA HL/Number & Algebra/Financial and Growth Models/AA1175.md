# AA1175

**Maximum mark: 17**

Juanita wants to borrow some money to buy an apartment.

She finds an offer allowing her to borrow \$480\,000 over $10$ years with an interest rate of $r\%$ P.A. compounded monthly. She repays the loan with a fixed amount $p$ every month.

Juanita takes the loan out at the beginning of the month. At the end of the month, the interest is added __and then__ she makes the monthly payment of $p$.

This continues until after $10$ complete years, she has repaid the loan in its entirety.

Juanita wants to analyse three different scenarios in which she could repay the loan.

1.  In the first scenario her monthly payment is $$p=\,\$5\,000$$.

    If $k=1+\dfrac{r}{1200}$
    1.  Write down the number of payments that will be made over the entire $10$ year term of the loan.

    2.  Show that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    96k^{120}&=\frac{k^{120}-1}{k-1}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    3.  Hence, or otherwise, find $r$.[6 marks]

2.  In the second scenario Juanita uses the same values for $p$ and $r$ as part (a).  She makes the monthly payments of $p$ for $7$ years and $4$ months.

    She then makes a final payment to clear the remaining balance of the loan.
    1.  Find the number of payments she makes __before__  the final payment.

    2.  Hence, find the final payment required to clear the remaining balance to $4$ significant figures. [3 marks]

3.  In the third scenario Juanita pays $p$ per month for $5$ complete years and then she increases her monthly loan repayment to $2p$ for the remaining $5$ years.

    Find the value of $p$, to the nearest dollar, for the third scenario. [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  There are $12$ payments per year for $10$ years
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=10 \times 12\\[6pt]
        &=120 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    2.  Note: Multiplying by $k$ represents an increase of $r\%$.

        We can build up an expression which represents the remaining value, $FV$, of the loan. After the first month, this remaining value is increased by $r\%$ and then the payment \$$5\,000$ is subtracted
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            FV&=480\,000\times k -5\,000\\[6pt]
            &=480\,000k -5\,000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We then continue into the second month, this process is repeated. The remaining value is increased by $r\%$ and the payment \$$5\,000$ is subtracted
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            FV&=(480\,000k -5\,000)\times k -5\,000\\[6pt]
            &=480\,000k^2 -5\,000k-5\,000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Similarly for the third month
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
             \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            FV&=(480\,000k^2 -5\,000k-5\,000)\times k -5\,000\\[6pt]
            &=480\,000k^3 -5\,000k^2-5\,000k-5\,000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        This continues for all $120$ payments and can be shown using the following series
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            FV&=480\,000k^{120} -5\,000k^{119}-5\,000k^{118}-5\,000k^{117}-...-5\,000k-5\,000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
             \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        As the loan has been repaid in full we can replace $FV$ with $0$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
             \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            0&=480\,000k^{120} -5\,000k^{119}-5\,000k^{118}-5\,000k^{117}-...-5\,000k-5\,000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can then rearrange the equation above to get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            480\,000k^{120}&=5\,000k^{119}+5\,000k^{118}+5\,000k^{117}+...+5\,000k+5\,000
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Recognise that the R.H.S. of the above is a geometric series with a first term of $5\,000$ and a common ratio of $k$. Hence we can use the sum of a geometric sequence formula to write
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            480\,000k^{120}&=5\,000\left(\frac{k^{120}-1}{k-1}\right) \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[12pt]
            &\hspace{-2.8em}96k^{120}=\dfrac{k^{120}-1}{k-1}\hspace{2em} \text{... as required.}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    3.  Using a G.D.C. we can solve the equation found above.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                96k^{120}&=\frac{k^{120}-1}{k-1} \\[6pt]
                k&=1.0038412... \hspace{3.3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                1.0038412...&=1+\tfrac{r}{1200} \\[6pt]
                r&=4.609419...\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
                &=4.61\% \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
2.  1.  She makes $7$ full years of payments plus an additional $4$ months, which gives
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                &=7 \times 12 + 4\\[6pt]
                &=88
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $\text{She makes }88\text{ payments before the final payment.}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
             \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Juanita pays $p$ per month for $60$ months, she then pays $2p$ per month for the remaining $60$ months.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
         \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Lets consider the first $60$ payments of  $p$ using a similar method to part (a) where $k=1+\tfrac{4.61}{1200} = 1.0038...$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        FV&=((480\,000 \times k - p)\times k - p)...\\[6pt]
        FV&=480\,000k^{60}-pk^{59}-pk^{58}...-pk-p \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now let's consider the next $60$ payments of $2p$ which will clear the remaining balance, which means the future value is \$$0$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
         \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=((480\,000k^{60}-pk^{59}-pk^{58}...-p)\times k - 2p)\times k - 2p)...\\[6pt]
        0&=480\,000k^{120}-pk^{119}...-pk^{60}-2pk^{59}-2pk^{58}-...-2pk-2p\\[6pt]
        480\,000k^{120}&=pk^{119}...+pk^{60}+2pk^{59}+2pk^{58}+...+2pk+2p \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    This equation has a single unknown ($p$). However in its current form we cannot enter it into the calculator as there are too many terms!

    We need to find a way to rewrite the sum in a simpler form.

    We can do this by summing the RHS of the equation in two parts. We will let the first $60$ terms be part $A$ and the last $60$ terms be part $B$

    To find the sum of part $A$ we can use the formula directly, pasrt $A$ has parameters $u_1=2p$, $r=1.0038...$ and $n=60$, therefore

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S_A&=2p\frac{(1.0038...)^{60}-1}{(1.0038...)-1} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Let's reconsider the original equation and replace part $A$ with the simpler form found above
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        480\,000k^{120}&=\underbrace{pk^{119}...+pk^{60}}_{\mathrm{B}}+\underbrace{2pk^{59}+2pk^{58}+...+2pk+2p}_{\mathrm{A}}\\[6pt]
        480\,000k^{120}&=\underbrace{pk^{119}...+pk^{60}}_{\mathrm{B}}+\underbrace{2p\frac{(1.0038...)^{60}-1}{(1.0038...)-1}}_{\mathrm{A}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Part $B$ is more complicated. We can't simply sum $120$ terms as we only want to sum the last $60$ terms of the sequence.

    Lets find the sum of the full $120$ terms and then subtract the sum of the first $60$ terms.

    Part $B$ has parameters $u_1=p$, $r=1.0038...$ and $n=120$, therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S_B&=p\frac{(1.0038...)^{120}-1}{(1.0038...)-1} - p\frac{(1.0038...)^{60}-1}{(1.0038...)-1} \tag*{\textbf{M1A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Let's now rewrite the original equation and replace $S_B$ with the formula above
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        480\,000(1.0038...)^{120}&=\underbrace{p\frac{(1.0038...)^{120}-1}{(1.0038...)-1} - p\frac{(1.0038...)^{60}-1}{(1.0038...)-1}}_{\mathrm{B}}+\underbrace{2p\frac{(1.0038...)^{60}-1}{(1.0038...)-1}}_{\mathrm{A}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now solve this on the G.D.C.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        p&=3465.61...\\[6pt]
        p&=\$3466 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
