# AA664

**Maximum mark: 8**

1.  Solve the inequality $x^2 \geq 2x + 3$. [2 marks]

2.  Use mathematical induction to prove that $2^n > n^2 - 2$ for all
    $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, $n \geq 3$.[6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we draw the graphs of $y = x^2$ and $y = 2x + 3$ \[by using
    G.D.C.\], we have

    ![8559be520206e999f46f83d313584ee1a94282b7.svg](images/8559be520206e999f46f83d313584ee1a94282b7.svg)

    Using the $x$-coordinates of A and B, we see that the graph of
    $y = x^2$ is above the graph of $y = 2x+3$ when $x < -1$ and
    $x > 3$, and the graphs intersect at $x = -1,3$.

    Hence the solution set to the inequality $x^2 \geq 2x+3$ is
     $(-\infty,-1] \cup [3, \infty)$

2.  Let P$(n)$ be the following statement:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7.2em} 2^n &> n^2 - 2 \hspace{3em} [\text{for $n\geq3$}] \hspace{1.9em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

Using mathematical induction, we will prove that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, $n \geq 3$.

:u[Base case]. For $n = 3$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
2^3 = 8 &> 3^2 - 2 = 7
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(3)$ holds.

:u[Inductive hypothesis]. We assume that P$(k)$ holds for some
$k \geq 3$. In other words, we assume that
$$
\begin{aligned}
2^k &> k^2 - 2
\end{aligned}
$$
for some $k \geq 3$.

:u[Inductive step]: To show that P$(k+1)$ holds, we need to
show that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.5em} 2^{k+1} &> (k+1)^2 - 2
\end{aligned}
$$
From the
left hand side of the inequality $(1)$, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{6.8em} 2^{k+1} &= 2\cdot2^k \\[6pt]
&>2\hspace{0.05em}[k^2 - 2] \hspace{3em} [\text{by (IH)}] \\[6pt]
&= k^2 + [k^2 - 4] \\[6pt]
[\text{by part (a)}] \hspace{3em} &\geq 2k+3 + [k^2 - 4] \\[6pt]
&= [k^2 + 2k + 1] - 2 \\[6pt]
&= (k+1)^2 - 2
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(k+1)$ holds as well.

:u[Conclusion]. P$(3)$ holds, and for every $k \geq 3$, if
P$(k)$ holds then P$(k+1)$ also holds.

Hence, by mathematical induction, we conclude that
P$(n)$ holds for all $\text{\(n\hspace{-0.2em}\in\hspace{-0.2em}\mathbb{Z}^+\hspace{-0.2em},\hspace{0.05em}n\hspace{-0.2em}\geq\hspace{-0.2em}3\)}$.
