# AA090

**Maximum mark: 14**

1.  Show that
    $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n+1}} < \sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n}$,
    where $n \in \mathbb{Z},\hspace{0.1em} n\geq 0$. [3 marks]

2.  Hence show that $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} < 2\sqrt{2} - 2$.
     [2 marks]

3.  Prove by mathematical induction that
    $$
    \hspace{4em} \sum_{r = 2}^n \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}} < 2\sqrt{n} - 2 \hspace{2em} \text{for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, $n \geq 2$.}

    $$
    [9 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  For any $n \in \mathbb{Z},\hspace{0.1em} n \geq 0$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n} &= \left[\dfrac{\sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n}}{1}\right] \left[\dfrac{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}}\right] \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{(\cancel{n}+1) - \cancel{n}}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}} \\[6pt]
    &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7.8em} \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n+1}} < \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n+1} + \sqrt{n}} \hspace{2em} \big[\text{since $\sqrt{n+1} > \sqrt{n}$}\hspace{0.1em}\big]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{n+1}} < \sqrt{n+1} - \sqrt{n} \hspace{3.8em}
    $$

2.  Hence, substituting $n = 1$ in the inequality $(1)$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} &< \sqrt{2} - 1 \hspace{1em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} < 2\sqrt{2} - 2
    $$

3.  Let P$(n)$ be the following statement:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6em} \sum_{r = 2}^n \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}} < 2\sqrt{n} - 2 \hspace{3em} [\text{for $n\geq2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using math induction, we will prove that P$(n)$
    $\text{holds for all \(n \in \mathbb{Z}^+\), \(n\geq2\).}$

    :u[Base case]. For $n = 2$, from part (b), we have

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} < 2\sqrt{2} - 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence P$(2)$
    holds.

    :u[Inductive hypothesis]. We assume that P$(k)$ holds for
    some $k \geq 2$. In other words, we assume that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \sum_{r = 2}^k \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}} < 2\sqrt{k} - 2 \hspace{1.5em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    for some $k \geq 2$.

    :u[Inductive step]. To show that P$(k+1)$ holds, we need
    to show that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.2em} \sum_{r = 2}^{k+1} \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}} < 2\sqrt{k+1} - 2
    \end{aligned}
    $$

From the left hand side of the inequality $(2)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{8em} \sum_{r = 2}^{k+1} \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}} &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}} + \sum_{r = 2}^k \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}} \\[6pt]
&< \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}} + \big(2\sqrt{k} - 2\big) \hspace{2em} [\text{by (IH)}] \\[12pt]
&< \big(2\sqrt{k+1} - \cancel{2\sqrt{k}}\big) + \big(\cancel{2\sqrt{k}} - 2\big) \hspace{2em} [\text{by part (a)}] \\[18pt]
&= 2\sqrt{k+1} -2
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(k+1)$ holds as well.

:u[Conclusion]. P$(2)$ holds, and for every $k \geq 2$, if
P$(k)$ holds then P$(k+1)$ also holds.

Hence, by mathematical induction, we conclude that
P$(n)$ holds for all $\text{\(n\hspace{-0.2em}\in\hspace{-0.2em}\mathbb{Z}^+\hspace{-0.2em},\hspace{0.05em}n\hspace{-0.2em}\geq\hspace{-0.2em}2\)}$.
