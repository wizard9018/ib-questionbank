# AA086

**Maximum mark: 7**

The Fibonacci sequence is defined as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_0 &= 0,\hspace{0.25em} a_1 = 1,\hspace{0.25em} a_2 = 1, \\[6pt]
a_n &= a_{n-1}+a_{n-2} \hspace{0.5em}\text{for}\hspace{0.5em} n \geq 2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Prove by mathematical induction that
$a_1^2+a_2^2+\cdots+a_n^2=a_na_{n+1}$, where $n\in\mathbb{Z}^+$.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let P$(n)$ be the following statement:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{8.3em} a_1^2+a_2^2+...+a_n^2=a_na_{n+1} \hspace{3em} [\text{for $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Using mathematical induction, we will prove that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

:u[Base case]. For $n = 1$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1^2 &= 1^2 \\[6pt]
&= 1\cdot 1 \\[6pt]
&= a_1a_2
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(1)$ holds.

:u[Inductive hypothesis]. We assume that P$(k)$ holds for some
$k \geq 1$. In other words, we assume that
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1^2+a_2^2+...+a_k^2 = a_ka_{k+1}
\end{aligned}
$$
for some
$k \geq 1$.

:u[Inductive step]. To show that P$(k+1)$ holds, we need to
show that
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1^2+a_2^2+...+a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2= a_{k+1}a_{k+2} \hspace{2.2em}
\end{aligned}
$$

From the left hand side of the equation $(1)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1^2+a_2^2+...+a_k^2 + a_{k+1}^2 &= \big(a_1^2+a_2^2+...+a_k^2\big) + a_{k+1}^2 \\[6pt]
&= a_ka_{k+1} + a_{k+1}^2 \hspace{3em} [\text{by (IH)}] \\[7pt]
&= \big(a_k + a_{k+1}\big)a_{k+1} \\[7pt]
&= a_{k+2}a_{k+1} \hspace{3em} [\text{by (FS)}] \\[7pt]
&= a_{k+1}a_{k+2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(k+1)$ holds as well.

:u[Conclusion]. P$(1)$ holds, and for every $k \geq 1$, if
P$(k)$ holds then P$(k+1)$ also holds.

Hence, by mathematical induction, we conclude that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
