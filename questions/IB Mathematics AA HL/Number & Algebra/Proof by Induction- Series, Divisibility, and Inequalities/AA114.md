# AA114

**Maximum mark: 7**

Use the principle of mathematical induction to prove that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{4em} 1\cdot2^1 + 2\cdot2^2 + 3\cdot2^3 + \cdots + n\cdot2^n &= 2 + (n-1)2^{n+1} \hspace{2em} \text{for all } n \in \mathbb{Z}^+. \\
\end{aligned}
$$

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

Let P$(n)$ be the following statement:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{5.3em} 1\cdot2^1 + 2\cdot2^2 + 3\cdot2^3 + \cdots + n\cdot2^n =  2 + (n-1)2^{n+1} \hspace{2em} [\text{for $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Using mathematical induction, we will prove that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

:u[Base case]. For $n = 1$, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
1\cdot2^1 &= 2 \\[6pt]
&= 2 + (1-1)2^{1+1}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(1)$ holds.

:u[Inductive hypothesis]. We assume that P$(k)$ holds for some
$k \geq 1$. In other words, we assume that
$$
\begin{aligned}
1\cdot2^1 + 2\cdot2^2 + 3\cdot2^3 + \cdots + k\cdot2^k &=  2 + (k-1)2^{k+1} \hspace{1.8em}
\end{aligned}
$$

for some $k \geq 1$.

:u[Inductive step]. To show that P$(k+1)$ holds, we need to
show that
$$
\begin{aligned}
1\cdot2^1 + 2\cdot2^2 + 3\cdot2^3 + \cdots + k\cdot2^k + (k+1)2^{k+1} &=  2 + k\cdot2^{k+2}
\end{aligned}
$$

From the left hand side of the equation $(1)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\hspace{1.25em}1\cdot2^1 + 2\cdot2^2 + 3\cdot2^3 + \cdots + k\cdot2^k + (k+1)2^{k+1} = \\[6pt]
&= \big[1\cdot2^1 + 2\cdot2^2 + 3\cdot2^3 + \cdots + k\cdot2^k\big] + (k+1)2^{k+1} \\[6pt]
&= \big[2 + (k-1)2^{k+1}\big] +(k+1)2^{k+1} \hspace{3em} [\text{by (IH)}]  \\[6pt]
&= 2 + (k-\cancel{1} + k+\cancel{1})2^{k+1} \\[6pt]
&= 2 + (2k)2^{k+1} \\[6pt]
&= 2 + k\cdot2^{k+2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence P$(k+1)$ holds as well.

:u[Conclusion]. P$(1)$ holds, and for every $k \geq 1$, if
P$(k)$ holds then P$(k+1)$ also holds.

Hence, by mathematical induction, we conclude that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
