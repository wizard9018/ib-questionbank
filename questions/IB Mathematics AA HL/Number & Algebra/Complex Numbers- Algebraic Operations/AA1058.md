# AA1058

**Maximum mark: 7**

1. Find three distinct roots of the equation $z^3 + 27i  = 0$, $z \in \mathbb{C}$, giving your answers in exact cartesian form. [3 marks]

Let $w$ be the sum of two of the roots of $z$, from part (a), which have non-zero real parts and $\dfrac{c}{c+w}=2i$.

2. Show that $c=\dfrac{6}{5} + \dfrac{12}{5} i$. [4 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. Using de Moivre's theorem, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z^3 + 27i &= 0 \\[8pt]
    z^3 &= -27i \\[8pt]
    z &= 27^{\tfrac{1}{3}}\left(\cos \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + i \sin \dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{3}} \\[8pt]
    z_k &= 3 \left(\cos \dfrac{3\pi/2+2k\pi}{3} + i \sin \dfrac{3\pi/2+2k\pi}{3}\right)   \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    for $k = 0,1,2$.

    Hence the roots of the equation $z^3 + 27 i = 0$ are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        z_0 &= 3 \left(\cos \dfrac{\pi}{2} + i \sin \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) \\[8pt]
        z_1 &= 3 \left(\cos \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + i \sin \dfrac{7\pi}{6}\right) \\[8pt]
        z_2 &= 3 \left(\cos \dfrac{11\pi}{6} + i \sin \dfrac{11\pi}{6}\right)   \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Converting the roots to its Cartesian form, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    k = 0 : \hspace{0.525em}z_0 = 3i \hspace{2em} k = 1 : \hspace{0.525em}z_1 = -\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} - \dfrac{3i}{2} \hspace{2em} k = 2 : \hspace{0.525em}z_2 = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} - \dfrac{3i}{2}  \tag*{\textbf{A1}} \\[10pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2. First, the sum of the roots described is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        w &= z_1 + z_2 \\[8pt]
        &= -3i \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Working out the complex equation, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{c}{c+w} &= 2i \\[8pt]
        c &= 2i(c-3i)  \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[8pt]
        c &= 2i c + 6 \\[8pt]
        c-2i c &= 6 \\[8pt]
        c(1-2i) &= 6 \\[8pt]
        c &= \dfrac{6}{1-2i} \\[8pt]
        c &= \dfrac{6}{1-2i} \cdot \dfrac{1+2i}{1+2i}  \tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[8pt]
        c &= \dfrac{6+12i}{1+4} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[8pt]
        &\hspace{-0.5em} z = \dfrac{6}{5} + \dfrac{12}{5} i \hspace{2em} \text{...as required}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
