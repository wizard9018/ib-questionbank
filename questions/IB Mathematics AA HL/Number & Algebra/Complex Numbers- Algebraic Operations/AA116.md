# AA116

**Maximum mark: 16**

1.  Find the roots of $z^{16} = 1$ which satisfy the condition
    $0 < \arg(z) < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$,

    expressing your answer in the form
    $re^{{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\theta}$, where
    $r, \theta \in \mathbb{R}^+$. [5 marks]

2.  Let $S$ be the sum of the roots found in part (a).
    1.  Show that $\mathrm{Re}(S) = \mathrm{Im}(S)$.

    2.  By writing $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$ as
        $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, find the value of
        $\cos \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{8}\Big)$ in the form
        $\dfrac{\sqrt{a + \sqrt{b}}}{c}$,

        where $a, b$ and $c$ are integers to be determined.

    3.  Hence, or otherwise, show that
        $S = \dfrac{1}{2}\big(\hspace{-0.1em}\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}} + \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}\hspace{0.1em}\big)\big(1+{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\big)$.
        [11 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z^{16} &= 1 \\[6pt]
    z^{16} &= \cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using de Moivre's theorem, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9em} z &= (\cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0)^\frac{1}{16} \\[7pt]
    &= \cos\Big(\dfrac{2k\pi}{16}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{2k\pi}{16}\Big) \\[4pt]
    &= \cos\Big(\dfrac{k\pi}{8}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{k\pi}{8}\Big) \\[8pt]
    &= e^{{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\frac{k\pi}{8}} \hspace{3em} [\text{by Euler's formula}] \hspace{0.2em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    for $k = 0,1,2, \dots, 15$.

    Hence the roots of the equation $z^{16} = 1$ that satisfy
    $0 < \arg(z) < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ are

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    k = 1 &: \hspace{0.525em}z_1 = e^{{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\frac{\pi}{8}} \\[6pt]
    k = 2 &: \hspace{0.525em}z_2 = e^{{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\frac{\pi}{4}} \\[6pt]
    k = 3 &: \hspace{0.525em}z_3 = e^{{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\frac{3\pi}{8}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  If we let $S = z_1 + z_2 + z_3$, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Re}(S) &= \cos \Big(\frac{\pi}{8}\Big) + \cos \Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) + \cos \Big(\frac{3\pi}{8}\Big) \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{Im}(S) &= \sin \Big(\frac{\pi}{8}\Big) + \sin \Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) + \sin \Big(\frac{3\pi}{8}\Big)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Using the reflection law for cosine and sine, we get

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \cos \Big(\frac{\pi}{8}\Big) &= \cos \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \frac{3\pi}{8}\Big) \\[6pt]
        &= \sin \Big(\frac{3\pi}{8}\Big) \\[15pt]
        \cos \Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) &= \cos \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{4}\Big) \\[6pt]
        &= \sin \Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) \\[15pt]
        \cos \Big(\frac{3\pi}{8}\Big) &= \cos \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{8}\Big) \\[6pt]
        &= \sin \Big(\frac{\pi}{8}\Big)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence we deduce
         $\mathrm{Re}(S) = \mathrm{Im}(S)$

    2.  Using the half-angle formula for cosine, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.5em} \cos \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{8}\Big) &= \cos \Big(\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{4}\Big) \\[7pt]
        &= \sqrt{\dfrac{1+\cos(\pi/4)}{2}} \\[7pt]
        &= \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[1 + \dfrac{\raisebox{-0.25em}{$\sqrt{2}$}}{2}\Big]} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  Similarly, using the half-angle formula for sine, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \cos \Big(\dfrac{3\pi}{8}\Big) &= \sin \Big(\dfrac{\pi}{8}\Big) \\[9pt]
        &= \sin \Big(\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{4}\Big) \\[7pt]
        &= \sqrt{\dfrac{1-\cos(\pi/4)}{2}} \\[7pt]
        &= \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[1 - \dfrac{\raisebox{-0.25em}{$\sqrt{2}$}}{2}\Big]} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Re}(S) &= \cos \Big(\frac{\pi}{8}\Big) + \cos \Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) + \cos \Big(\frac{3\pi}{8}\Big) \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2} \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}\big(\hspace{-0.1em}\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}} + \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}\hspace{0.1em}\big)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{4.8em} S &= \mathrm{Re}(S) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\mathrm{Im}(S) \\[12pt]
        &= \mathrm{Re}(S) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\mathrm{Re}(S) \hspace{3em} [\text{since $\mathrm{Re}(S) = \mathrm{Im}(S)$}] \\[12pt]
        &= \mathrm{Re}(S)(1 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}) \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{2}\big(\hspace{-0.1em}\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}} + \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}\hspace{0.1em}\big)\big(1+{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\big)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
