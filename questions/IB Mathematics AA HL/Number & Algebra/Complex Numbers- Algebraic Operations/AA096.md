# AA096

**Maximum mark: 17**

1.  Solve the equation $z^3 = 27$, $z \in \mathbb{C}$, giving your
    answer in the form\
    $z = r(\cos \theta + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \theta)$
    and in the form $z = a + b{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}$
    where $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$.  [6 marks]

2.  Consider the complex numbers
    $z_1 = -1 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu} \text{ and } z_2 =\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg[\mathrm{cos}\bigg(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\bigg)+\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{sin}\bigg(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\bigg)\bigg]$
    .

    1.  Write $z_1$ in the form
        $r(\cos \theta + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \theta)$.

    2.  Calculate $z_1z_2$ and write in the form
        $a + b{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}$ where
        $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$.

    3.  Hence find the value of $\tan\left(\dfrac{\pi}{12}\right)$ in
        the form $c + d\sqrt{3}$ where $c,d \in \mathbb{Z}$.

    4.  Find the smallest $p \in \mathbb{Q}^+$ such that $(z_1z_2)^p$ is
        a positive real number. [11 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z^3 &= 27\hspace{0.05em}(\cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0) \hspace{5.2em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using de Moivre's theorem, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9.6em} z &= 27^{\frac{1}{3}}(\cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0)^\frac{1}{3} \\[5pt]
    &= 3\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{2k\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{2k\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] \hspace{3em} [\text{for $k = 0,1,2$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the roots of the equation $z^3 = 27$ are
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    k = 0 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = 3\hspace{0.05em}(\cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0) = 3 \\[9pt]
    k = 1 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = 3\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \Big(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] = -\dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu} \\[10pt]
    k = 2 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = 3\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \Big(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] = -\dfrac{3}{2} - \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  If we write $z_1$ in modulus-argument form, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.8em} z_1 &= -1 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\\[10pt]
        &= \sqrt{2}\bigg[\hspace{-0.25em}-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\bigg] \\[6pt]
        &= \sqrt{2}\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big)\Big]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  If we write $z_2$ in Cartesian form, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        z_2 &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big[\cos\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg[\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\bigg] \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4} + \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, if we calculate $z_1z_2$ giving answer in Cartesian form,
        we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        z_1z_2 &= (-1  + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu})\bigg[\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4} + \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\bigg] \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
        &= -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4} - \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}+ \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}- \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{4} \\[9pt]
        &= \dfrac{-\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{6}}{4}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  If we calculate $z_1z_2$ giving answer in modulus-argument form,
        we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.5em} z_1z_2 &= \bigg[\cancel{\sqrt{2}}\mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\Big(\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\Big)\bigg]\bigg[\dfrac{1}{\cancel{\sqrt{2}}} \mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\Big)\bigg] \\[5pt]
        &= \mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\Big(\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\Big) \\[6pt]
        &= \mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) \\[6pt]
        &=  \cos\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big)
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, comparing the expressions $(1)$ and $(2)$, we find

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \cos\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) &= \dfrac{-\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4} \\[6pt]
        \sin\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \tan\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{12}\Big) &= \tan\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) \\[10pt]
        &= \sin\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) \div \cos\Big(\dfrac{13\pi}{12}\Big) \hspace{2em} \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{\cancel{4}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{4}}{-\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}} \\[5pt]
        &=\dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}} \\[5pt]
        &= \dfrac{8 - 4\sqrt{3}}{4} \\[12pt]
        &= 2 - \sqrt{3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    4.  The $p$ th power of $z_1z_2$ is a real
        number if
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{7.4em} \sin \left(\arg \left((z_1z_2)^p\right)\right) &= 0 \\[12pt]
        \sin \left(p\arg \left(z_1z_2\right)\right) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by de Moivre's theorem}] \\[6pt]
        \sin \Big(\dfrac{13p\pi}{12}\Big) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (b) (iii)}]
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence the smallest $p \in \mathbb{Q}^+$ such that $(z_1z_2)^p$
        is a positive real number is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1.4em} p = \dfrac{24}{13}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        **Note:**

        Solving $\sin\Big(\dfrac{13p\pi}{12}\Big) = 0$ gives $p = \dfrac{12k}{13}$, but this only ensures $(z_1z_2)^p$ is $\emph{real}$. Odd multiples of $\pi$ give a $\emph{negative}$ real number, so these must be rejected.

        For example, $k = 1$ gives
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        (z_1z_2)^{\frac{12}{13}} &= \cos \pi + i \sin \pi \\[6pt]
        &= -1
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence we require $\dfrac{13p\pi}{12} = 2k\pi$, giving $p = \dfrac{24}{13}$.
