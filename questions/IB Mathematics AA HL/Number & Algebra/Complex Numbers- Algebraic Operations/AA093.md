# AA093

**Maximum mark: 6**

Consider the complex number $z = \dfrac{w_1}{w_2}$ where
$w_1 = \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}$ and
$w_2 = 3 + \sqrt{3}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}$.

1.  Express $w_1$ and $w_2$ in modulus-argument form and write down
    1.  the modulus of $z$;

    2.  the argument of $z$. [4 marks]

2.  Find the smallest positive integer value of $n$ such that $z^n$ is a
    real number. [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  If we express $w_1$ and $w_2$ in modulus-argument form, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    w_1 = 2\sqrt{2}\mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) \hspace{1.5em} \text{and} \hspace{1.5em}  w_2 = 2\sqrt{3}\mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    1.  Hence the modulus of $z$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        |z| &= \dfrac{|w_1|}{|w_2|} \hspace{1.3em} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\cancel{2}\sqrt{2}}{\cancel{2}\sqrt{3}} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Hence the argument of $z$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.5em} \arg z &= \arg w_1 - \arg w_2 \\[10pt]
        &= \dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{\pi}{6} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\pi}{6}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  The $n$th power of $z$ is a real number if
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{9em} \sin \left(\arg \left(z^n\right)\right) &= 0 \\[6pt]
    \sin \left(n\arg z\right) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by de Moivre's theorem}] \\[3pt]
    \sin \left(\dfrac{n\pi}{6}\right) &= 0 \hspace{3em} [\text{by part (a) (ii)}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the smallest $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ such that $z^n$ is a real
    number is  $n = 6$
