# AA1325

**Maximum mark: 5**

The complex number $z_1$ has a modulus of $2$ and an argument of $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ radians.

1.    Find $z_1$ in Cartesian form. [2 marks]

Consider the complex numbers $z_2$ and $w$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
    z_2&=-2\sqrt{3}-2\mathrm{i}\\[6pt]
    w&=z_1+z_2
\end{aligned}
$$
The angle $\alpha$ is such that $\alpha=\arg{(w)}$ where $-\pi \leq \alpha < \pi$.

2.    Find the value of $\alpha$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  First, let's write $z_1$ in the form $r\,\text{cis}\,{\theta}$ where $r$ is the modulus of $z_1$ and $\theta$ is the argument of $z_1$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z_1&=2\,\mathrm{cis}{\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}\\[6pt]
    &=2\left(\cos{\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}+\mathrm{i}\sin{\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}\right)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The argument $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is in the $4$th quadrant. Therefore cosine is positive and sine is negative.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z_1&=2\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\right)\\[6pt]
    &=\sqrt{3}-\mathrm{i}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  It is best practice to use Cartesian form to add or subtract complex numbers.
    We already have $z_1$ and $z_2$ in the correct form so we can go ahead and find $w$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    w&=z_1+z_2\\[6pt]
    &=\sqrt{3}-\mathrm{i}+(-2\sqrt{3}-2\mathrm{i})\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
    &=(\sqrt{3}-2\sqrt{3})+(-\mathrm{i}-2\mathrm{i})\\[6pt]&=-\sqrt{3}-3\mathrm{i}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Let's make a __rough__ sketch of $w$ on an Argand diagram.
    As both the real and imaginary part are negative $w$ lies in the third quadrant. We can also (optionally) sketch approximate positions for $z_1$ and $z_2$.

    ![AA1325](images/AA1325.JPEG)

    In the question we are told that the argument is in the interval $[-\pi,\pi)$. Therefore it must be the clockwise angle from the positive  real axis shown in $\textcolor{green}{\text{green}}$.

    $\beta$ is the acute angle shown above.

    Using the tangent ratio we can find $\beta$, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tan{\beta}&=\frac{\textcolor{blue}{3}}{\textcolor{red}{\sqrt{3}}}\\[6pt]
    &=\frac{3}{\sqrt{3}}\times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}\\[6pt]
    &=\sqrt{3}\\[6pt]
    \beta&=\frac{\pi}{3}\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note that the angle we have found is the acute angle $\beta$, as shown in the third quadrant of the diagram.

    However, as mentioned the argument of $w$ is the clockwise angle from the positive horizontal axis.
    This is why drawing an Argand diagram is so important as it helps us to find the correct argument.

    Therefore the argument of $w$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \arg{(w)}&=-\left(\pi-\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\\[6pt]
     &=-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\,\text{radians}\hspace{1em}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
