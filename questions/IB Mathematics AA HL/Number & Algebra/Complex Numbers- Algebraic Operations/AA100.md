# AA100

**Maximum mark: 6**

Solve the equation $z^3 = 1$, giving your answers in Cartesian form.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

We have
$$
\begin{aligned}
z^3 &= 1 \\[6pt]
z^3 &= \cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0 \hspace{5.2em}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, using de Moivre's theorem, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{9.9em} z &= (\cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0)^\frac{1}{3} \\[5pt]
&= \cos\Big(\dfrac{2k\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin\Big(\dfrac{2k\pi}{3}\Big) \hspace{3em} [\text{for $k = 0,1,2$}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence the roots of the equation $z^3 = 1$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
k = 0 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = \cos 0 + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 0 = 1 \\[7pt]
k = 1 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = \cos\Big(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \Big(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\Big) = -\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu} \\[10pt]
k = 2 &: \hspace{0.525em}z = \cos\Big(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\Big) + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \Big(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\Big) = -\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}
\end{aligned}
$$
