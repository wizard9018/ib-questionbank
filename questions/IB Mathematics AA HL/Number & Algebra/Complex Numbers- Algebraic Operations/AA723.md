# AA723

**Maximum mark: 6**

Let $w = 2e^{{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\frac{2\pi}{3}}$.

1.  Write $w$, $w^2$ and $w^3$ in the form
        $a + b{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}$ where
        $a, b \in\mathbb{R}$.[4 marks]

2.  Find the smallest integer $k > 3$ such that $w^k$ is a real number.
    [2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  To express $w$, $w^2$ and $w^3$ in Cartesian form, we need to use
**the Polar form of a complex number (1.13)**,

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z=r(\cos\theta+\mathrm{i}\sin\theta)=re^{\mathrm{i}\theta}=r\,\text{cis}\,\theta
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    and **De Moivre's Theorem (1.14)**,

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    [r(\cos\theta+\mathrm{i}\sin\theta)]^n=r^ne^{\mathrm{i} n\theta}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    w &= 2e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{2\pi}{3}} \\[5pt]
    &= 2\Big[\hspace{-0.1em}\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi}{3}\Big) + \mathrm{i}\sin\Big(\frac{2\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] \\[5pt]
    &= 2\Big[\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\Big) + \mathrm{i}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big)\Big] \\[9pt]
    w&=\boxed{-1 + \sqrt{3}\mathrm{i}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$\\[3em]$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    w^2 &= (2)^2e^{\mathrm{i}(\frac{2\pi}{3}\times2)} \\[5pt]
    &= 4e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{4\pi}{3}} \\[5pt]
    &= 4\Big[\hspace{-0.1em}\cos\Big(\frac{4\pi}{3}\Big) + \mathrm{i}\sin\Big(\frac{4\pi}{3}\Big)\Big] \\[5pt]
    &= 4\Big[\hspace{-0.1em}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\Big) + \mathrm{i}\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big)\Big] \\[9pt]
    w^2&=\boxed{-2 - 2\sqrt{3}\mathrm{i}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$\\[3em]$$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    w^3 &= (2)^3e^{\mathrm{i} (\frac{2\pi}{3}\times3)} \\[5pt]
    &= 8e^{\mathrm{i} 2\pi} \\[5pt]
    &= 8\Big[\hspace{-0.1em}\cos (2\pi) + \mathrm{i}\sin (2\pi)\Big] \\[5pt]
    &= 8\Big[\hspace{-0.1em}(1) + \mathrm{i}(0)\Big] \\[9pt]
    w^3&=\boxed{8 + 0\mathrm{i}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Considering the argument of $w^k$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \arg (w^k) &= \dfrac{2k\pi}{3} \hspace{3em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The $k$th
    power of $w$ is real if $\arg(w^k)$ is a multiple of $\pi$.

    Hence the smallest such integer $k > 3$ is
     $k = 6$
