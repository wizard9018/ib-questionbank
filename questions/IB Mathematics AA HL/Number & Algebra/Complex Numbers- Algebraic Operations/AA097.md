# AA097

**Maximum mark: 19**

1.
    1.  Expand
        $(\cos \theta + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \theta)^4$
        by using the binomial theorem.

    2.  Hence use de Moivre's theorem to prove that
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \cos 4\theta = \cos^4 \theta - 6\cos^2 \theta\sin^2 \theta + \sin^4 \theta. \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  State a similar expression for $\sin 4 \theta$ in terms of
        $\cos \theta$ and $\sin \theta$. [6 marks]

Let
$z = r(\cos \alpha + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \alpha)$,
where $\alpha$ is measured in degrees, be the solution\
of $z^4 - {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}= 0$ which has the
smallest positive argument.

2.  Find the modulus and argument of $z$.
     [4 marks]

3.  Use (a) (ii) and your answer from (b) to show that
    $8\cos^4\alpha - 8\cos^2 \alpha + 1 = 0$.  [4 marks]

4.  Hence express $\cos \ang{22.5}$ in the form
    $\dfrac{\sqrt{a + b\sqrt{c}}}{d}$ where $a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{Z}$.
    [5 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Using the binomial theorem, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &(\cos \theta + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \theta)^4 =  \\[6pt]
        &\hspace{0.375em}\cos^4 \theta + 4{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\cos^3\theta \sin \theta - 6\cos^2 \theta \sin^2 \theta - 4{\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\cos \theta \sin^3 \theta + \sin^4 \theta
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using de Moivre's theorem, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        (\cos \theta + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin \theta)^4 = \cos 4\theta + {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\sin 4 \theta
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence, if we compare real parts of the expressions $(1)$ and
        $(2)$, we deduce
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \cos 4\theta = \cos^4 \theta - 6\cos^2 \theta\sin^2 \theta + \sin^4\theta
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  If we compare imaginary parts of the expressions $(1)$ and
        $(2)$, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \sin 4\theta = 4\cos^3 \theta\sin \theta - 4\cos\theta \sin^3 \theta
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  Using de Moivre's theorem, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{10em} z^4 - {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}&= 0 \\[13pt]
    z^4 &= {\mathrm{\hspace{0.05em}i}\mkern 1mu}\\[11pt]
    z^4 &= \mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Big) \\[6pt]
    z &= \mathop{\mathrm{cis}}\Big(\dfrac{\pi}{8} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}\Big) \hspace{3em} [\text{for $k = 0,1,2,3$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the modulus of $z$ is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    r=1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and the argument of $z$ is

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{8} = \ang{22.5}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Substituting $\theta = \alpha$ in the equation $(3)$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos^4 \alpha - 6\cos^2 \alpha\sin^2 \alpha + \sin^4 \alpha &= \cos 4\alpha \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
    \cos^4 \alpha - 6\cos^2 \alpha\hspace{0.05em}(1- \cos^2 \alpha) + (1 - \cos^2 \alpha)^2 &= \cos \ang{90} \\[6pt]
    \cos^4 \alpha - (6\cos^2 \alpha - 6\cos^4 \alpha) + (1 - 2\cos^2 \alpha + \cos^4 \alpha) &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \hspace{8.55em} 8\cos^4 \alpha - 8\cos^2\alpha + 1 = 0
    $$

4.  Hence, using the quadratic formula to solve the
    equation $(4)$ for $\cos^2\alpha$, we have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{5.5em} \cos^2 \alpha &= \dfrac{-(-8) \pm \sqrt{(-8)^2-4(8)(1)}}{2(8)} \\[6pt]
    \cos^2 \alpha &= \dfrac{2 \pm \sqrt{2}}{4} \\[6pt]
    |\hspace{-0.05em}\cos \alpha\hspace{0.05em}| &= \dfrac{\sqrt{2 \pm \sqrt{2}}}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos \ang{22.5} > \cos \ang{45} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} > 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence, using the inequalities $(5)$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \cos \ang{22.5}  =  \dfrac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
