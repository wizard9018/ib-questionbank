# AA089

**Maximum mark: 9**

Let $f(x) = (x+1)e^{-2x}$, $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Find $f'(x)$. [2 marks]

2.  Prove by induction that
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^nf}{\mathrm{d}x^n} = \big[n(-2)^{n-1} + (-2)^n(x+1)\big]e^{-2x}$
    for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.[7 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the product rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.2em} f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[(x+1)e^{-2x}\big] \\[6pt]
    &= e^{-2x} - 2(x+1)e^{-2x} \\[10pt]
    &= \big[1 - 2(x+1)\big]e^{-2x}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Let $\mathrm{P}(n)$ be the following statement:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{7.9em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^nf}{\mathrm{d}x^n} = \big[n(-2)^{n-1} + (-2)^n(x+1)\big]e^{-2x} \hspace{3em} [\text{for $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using mathematical induction, we will prove that $\mathrm{P}(n)$
    holds for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

:u[Base case]. For $n = 1$, from part (a), we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} = \big[1 - 2(x+1)\big]e^{-2x}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence P$(1)$ holds.

:u[Inductive hypothesis]. We assume that $\mathrm{P}(k)$ holds
for some $k \geq 1$. In other words, we assume that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^kf}{\mathrm{d}x^k} = \big[k(-2)^{k-1} + (-2)^k(x+1)\big]e^{-2x} \hspace{0.5em}
\end{aligned}
$$

for some $k \geq 1$.

:u[Inductive step]. To show that $\mathrm{P}(k+1)$ holds, we
need to show that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.6em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k+1}f}{\mathrm{d}x^{k+1}} = \big[(k+1)(-2)^k + (-2)^{(k+1)}(x+1)\big]e^{-2x}
\end{aligned}
$$

If we differentiate both sides of the equation (IH), we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{5.55em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k+1}f}{\mathrm{d}x^{k+1}} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\Big[\big[k(-2)^{k-1} + (-2)^k(x+1)\big]e^{-2x}\Big] \\[14pt]
&= \big[(-2)^k\big]e^{-2x} - 2\big[k(-2)^{k-1} + (-2)^k(x+1)\big]e^{-2x} \\[16pt]
&= \big[(-2)^k\big]e^{-2x} + \big[k(-2)^k + (-2)^{k+1}(x+1)\big]e^{-2x} \\[16pt]
&= \big[(k+1)(-2)^k + (-2)^{(k+1)}(x+1)\big]e^{-2x} \\
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $P(k+1)$ holds as well.

:u[Conclusion]. P$(1)$ holds, and for every $k \geq 1$, if
P$(k)$ holds then P$(k+1)$ also holds.

Hence, by mathematical induction, we conclude that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.
