# AA088

**Maximum mark: 23**

Let $f(x) = (x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}}$, for $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  Find $f'(x)$.  [2 marks]

2.  Prove by induction that
    $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^nf}{\mathrm{d}x^n} = \bigg(\dfrac{3n + x - 1}{3^n}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}}$
    for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.  [7 marks]

3.  Find the coordinates of any local maximum and minimum points on the
    graph of $y = f(x)$. Justify whether such point is a maximum or a
    minimum. [5 marks]

4.  Find the coordinates of any points of inflexion on the graph of
    $y = f(x)$. Justify whether such point is a point of inflexion.
    [5 marks]

5.  Hence sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$, indicating clearly the points
    found in parts (c) and (d) and any intercepts with the axes.
    [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Using the product rule, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    f'(x) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\big[(x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}}\big] \\[6pt]
    &= e^{\frac{x}{3}} + \dfrac{1}{3}(x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}} \\[6pt]
    &= \bigg(\dfrac{x+2}{3}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  Let P$(n)$ be the following statement:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8.7em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^nf}{\mathrm{d}x^n} = \bigg(\dfrac{3n + x - 1}{3^n}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}} \hspace{3em} [\text{for $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using mathematical induction, we will prove that P$(n)$ holds for
    all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

:u[Base case]. For $n = 1$, from part (a), we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} = \bigg(\dfrac{3 + x - 1}{3}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence P$(1)$ holds.

:u[Inductive hypothesis]. We assume that P$(k)$ holds for some
$k \geq 1$. In other words, we assume that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{0.1em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^kf}{\mathrm{d}x^k} = \bigg(\dfrac{3k + x - 1}{3^k}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

for some $k \geq 1$.

:u[Inductive step]. To show that P$(k+1)$ holds, we need to
show that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{1.2em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k+1}f}{\mathrm{d}x^{k+1}} = \bigg[\dfrac{3(k+1) + x - 1}{3^{k+1}}\bigg]e^{\frac{x}{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

If we differentiate both sides of the equation (IH), we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hspace{4.9em} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k+1}f}{\mathrm{d}x^{k+1}} &= \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigg[\bigg(\dfrac{3k + x - 1}{3^k}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}}\bigg] \\[6pt]
&= \bigg(\dfrac{1}{3^k}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}} + \dfrac{1}{3}\bigg(\dfrac{3k + x - 1}{3^k}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \bigg[\dfrac{1}{3^k} + \dfrac{3k + x - 1}{3^{k+1}}\bigg]e^{\frac{x}{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \bigg[\dfrac{3(k+1) + x - 1}{3^{k+1}}\bigg]e^{\frac{x}{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence P$(k+1)$ holds as well.

:u[Conclusion]. P$(1)$ holds, and for every $k \geq 1$, if
P$(k)$ holds then P$(k+1)$ also holds.

Hence, by mathematical induction, we conclude that P$(n)$ holds for all
$n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

3.  If we solve the equation $f'(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg(\dfrac{x + 2}{3}\bigg)e^{\frac{x}{3}} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x + 2 &= 0 \\[12pt]
    x &= -2
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence $-2$ is the only critical number of
    $f$.

    Using the ID Test, we have

    ![41f59f91431e030c029eab143139497c30f1df01.svg](images/41f59f91431e030c029eab143139497c30f1df01.svg)

    The sign of $f'$ changes from negative to positive at $-2$.

    Hence, by the First Derivative Test, $f$ has a local minimum at
    $-2$.

    Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = -2$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(-2) \\[6pt]
    &= (-2-1)e^{-\frac{2}{3}} \\[6pt]
    &= -3e^{-\frac{2}{3}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the graph of $y = f(x)$
    has a local minimum at
     $\mathrm{A}\big(\hspace{-0.2em}-2,-3e^{-\frac{2}{3}}\big)$.

4.  If we solve the equation $f''(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigg[\dfrac{3(2) + x - 1}{3^2}\bigg]e^{\frac{x}{3}} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    3(2) + x - 1 &= 0 \\[12pt]
    x &= -5
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence there is at most one point of inflexion
    on the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    Using the Concavity Test, we have

    ![1c4ca00263d0aa013c934479ae539a797857f949.svg](images/1c4ca00263d0aa013c934479ae539a797857f949.svg)

    The graph of $y = f(x)$ changes concavity from downward to upward at
    $-5$.

    Hence the graph of $y = f(x)$ has an inflexion point at $-5$.

    Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = -5$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(-5) \\[6pt]
    &= (-5-1)e^{-\frac{5}{3}} \\[6pt]
    &= -6e^{-\frac{5}{3}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the graph of $y = f(x)$
    has the inflexion point at
     $\mathrm{B}\big(\hspace{-0.2em}-5,-6e^{-\frac{5}{3}}\big)$.

5.  We first note that the domain of the function
    $f(x) = (x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}}$ is $(-\infty,\infty)$.

    Solving the equation $f(x) = 0$ for $x$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    (x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}} &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x-1 &= 0 \\[6pt]
    x &= 1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point
     $\mathrm{C}(1,0)$
    is the $x$-intercept of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    Evaluating $y = f(x)$ for $x = 0$, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    y &= f(0) \\[6pt]
    &= (0-1)e^{0} \\[6pt]
    &= -1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the point
     $\mathrm{D}(0,-1)$
    is the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\rightarrow -\infty} f(x) &= \lim_{x\rightarrow -\infty} \big[(x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}}\big] \\[6pt]
    &= 0
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \lim_{x\rightarrow \infty} f(x) &= \lim_{x\rightarrow \infty} \big[(x-1)e^{\frac{x}{3}}\big] \\[6pt]
    &= \infty
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the $x$-axis is a horizontal
    asymptote of the graph of $y = f(x)$.
