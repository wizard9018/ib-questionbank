# AA984

**Maximum mark: 15**

The equation $e^{2x} - 12e^x = -32$ has two solutions, $x_1$ and $x_2$.

1. Find the value of $x_1$ and the value of $x_2$.[5 marks]

A second equation, $2\left(\log_{\,9}x\right)\left(\log_{\,3}x\right) - 6\log_{\,9}x - 2\log_{\,3}x = -6$, also has two solutions, $x_3$ and $x_4$. 

2. 1.  Show that this second equation can be expressed as

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \left(\log_{\,3}x\right)^2 -5\log_{\,3}x + 6 = 0

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. Hence find the value of $x_3$ and the value of $x_4$. [7 marks]

3. Given that $x_1 + x_2 = a(x_3 + x_4)$, find the value of $a$. Give your answer in the form $b\ln c$, where $b,c \in \mathbb{R}$.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. The equation can be written as a quadratic in $e^x$:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \left(e^x\right)^2 - 12e^x + 32 = 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    The equation can then be factorised:

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    \left(e^x - 4\right)\left(e^x - 8\right) = 0

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    From the first bracket, we have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    e^x - 4 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    e^x &= 4 \\[8pt]

    x &= \ln 4 \\[8pt]

    &= 2\ln 2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    And from the second bracket,

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    e^x - 8 &= 0 \\[8pt]

    e^x &= 8 \\[8pt]

    x &= \ln 8 \\[8pt]

    &= 3\ln 2

    \end{aligned}

    $$

    Hence, $$\boxed{\text{$x_1 = 2\ln 2$ and $x_2 = 3\ln 2$.}}$$

2. 1. We are asked to write the second equation in terms of $\log_{\,3}x$, and currently the equation is expressed in terms of $\log_{\,3}x$ and $\log_{\,9}x$. Using the formula for changing the base, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \log_{\,9}x &= \dfrac{\log_{\,3}x}{\log_{\,3}9} \\[8pt]

        &= \dfrac{\log_{\,3}x}{\log_{\,3}3^2} \\[8pt]

        &= \dfrac{\log_{\,3}x}{2\log_{\,3}3} \\[8pt]

        \log_{\,9}x &= \dfrac{1}{2}\log_{\,3}x

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Substituting this expression into our original equation, we get

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        2\left(\log_{\,9}x\right)\left(\log_{\,3}x\right) - 6\log_{\,9}x - 2\log_{\,3}x &= -6 \\[8pt]

        2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(\log_{\,3}x\right)\left(\log_{\,3}x\right) - 6\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(\log_{\,3}x\right) - 2\log_{\,3}x &= -6 \\[8pt]

        &\hspace{-10em}\boxed{\left(\log_{\,3}x\right)^2 - 5\log_{\,3}x + 6 = 0}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    

    2. The equation can be factorised as follows:

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \left(\log_{\,3}x - 2\right)\left(\log_{\,3}x - 3\right) = 0

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        From the first bracket, we have

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \log_{\,3}x - 2 &= 0 \\[8pt]

        \log_{\,3}x &= 2 \\[8pt]

        x &= 9

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        And from the second bracket,

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \log_{\,3}x - 3 &= 0 \\[8pt]

        \log_{\,3}x &= 3 \\[8pt]

        x &= 27

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Hence, $$\boxed{\text{$x_3 = 9$ and $x_4 = 27$.}}$$

3. We have

    $$

    \begin{aligned}

    x_1 + x_2 &= a(x_3 + x_4) \\[8pt]

    2\ln 2 + 3\ln 2 &= a(9+27) \\[8pt]

    5\ln 2 &= 36a \\[8pt]

    &\hspace{-0.5em}\boxed{a = \dfrac{5}{36}\ln 2}

    \end{aligned}

    $$
