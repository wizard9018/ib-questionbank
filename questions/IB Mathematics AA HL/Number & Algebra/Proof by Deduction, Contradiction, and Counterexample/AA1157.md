# AA1157

**Maximum mark: 6**

Use proof by contradiction to show that if $(n-6)^3=(n+6)^3-n^3, n\in \mathbb{Z^+}$, is true then $n$ must be even.

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

First expand and simplify the given equality
$$
\begin{aligned}
n^3+\binom{3}{1}(-6)n^2+\binom{3}{2}(-6)^2n+(-6)^3&=n^3+\binom{3}{1}(6)n^2+\binom{3}{2}6^2n+6^3-n^3\\[6pt]
n^3-18n^2+108n-216&= 18n^2+108n+216\\[6pt]
n^3-36n^2-432&= 0 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Now we make the contradiction by assuming $n$ is odd. Let $n=2k+1$, where $k \in \mathbb{Z}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(2k+1)^3-36(2k+1)^2-432&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
Consider the term $36(2k+1)^2$, we can rewrite this as $2\times18(2k+1)^2$, therefore this term is even.

Hence we can write $36(2k+1)^2$ as $2a$, where $a \in     \mathbb{Z}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{R1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
The term $-432$ is also even.

Let's expand the first term
$$
\begin{aligned}
(2k+1)^3&=8k^3+12k^2+6k+1\\[6pt]
&=2(4k^3+6k^2+3k)+1\\[6pt]
&=2b+1 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $b \in     \mathbb{Z}$.

Therefore the original simplified equation can be written in the following form
$$
\begin{aligned}
0&= 2b+1-2a-432\\[6pt]
0&=2(b-a-216)+1
\end{aligned}
$$
Let $c=b-a-216$ where where $c \in     \mathbb{Z}$. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
0&= 2c+1 \tag*{\textbf{R1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
This is a contradiction as $0$ is an even number and $2c+1$ is odd.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
\end{aligned}
$$
