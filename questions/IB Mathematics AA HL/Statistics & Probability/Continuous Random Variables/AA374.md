# AA374

**Maximum mark: 7**

A continuous random variable $T$ has a probability density function $f$
defined by
$$
f(t) = \left\{
\begin{array}{ccl}
\dfrac{1}{4}|t-3|, & 1 \leq t \leq 5, \\[9pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{array} \right.
$$

1.  Sketch the graph of $y = f(t)$. [2 marks]

2.
    1.  Find the lower quartile of $T$.

    2.  Hence find the interquartile range of $T$. [5 marks]

2.
    1.  Let $q_1$ be the lower quartile of $T$.
        If we solve the equation $\mathrm{P}(T \leq q_1) = 0.25$ for
        $q_1$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \dfrac{1}{4}\int_1^{q_1} (3-t)\,\mathrm{d}t &= 0.25 \hspace{3em} [\text{since $1 < q_1 < 3$}] \\[4pt]
        \bigg[3t - \dfrac{t^2}{2}\bigg]\bigg|_1^{q_1} \hspace{-0.15em}&= 4(0.25) \\[4pt]
        \bigg[3q_1 - \dfrac{q_1^2}{2}\bigg]\hspace{-0.15em} - \hspace{-0.15em}\bigg[3 - \dfrac{1}{2}\bigg] \hspace{-0.15em}&= 1 \\[8pt]
        6q_1-q_1^2 - 5 &= 2 \\[8pt]
        0 &= q_1^2 - 6q_1  + 7 \\[9pt]
        [\text{by quadratic formula}] \hspace{2.25em} q_1 &= \dfrac{-(-6) \pm \sqrt{(-6)^2 - 4(1)(7)}}{2(1)} \hspace{5.5em} \\[4pt]
        q_1 &= \dfrac{6-\sqrt{8}}{2} \hspace{3em} [\text{since $q_1 < 3$}] \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        q_1 = 3 - \sqrt{2} \hspace{1.75em}
        $$

    2.  Let $q_3$ be the upper quartile of $T$.
        Using the symmetry of the graph of $y =f(t)$, we have
        $q_3 = 3 + \sqrt{2}$.

        Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{3.1em} \text{IQR} &= q_3  - q_1 \\[6pt]
        &= (3 + \sqrt{2}) - (3 - \sqrt{2}) \\[6pt]
        &= 2\sqrt{2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
