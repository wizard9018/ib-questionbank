# AA1181

**Maximum mark: 14**

Seb commutes to work every day.

For the first part of his journey he walks to the train station and for the second part he waits for the train and the third part he rides the train to work.

The continuous random variable $W$, in minutes, is the time it takes him to walk to the train station.

1.  It is known that $W$ is normally distributed with a mean of $\mu_{W}$ and a standard deviation of $\sigma_{W}$.
    1.  If $P(W<6)=0.07529)$ and $P(W>9)=0.3309)$ to $4$ significant figures. Find $\mu_{W}$ and $\sigma_{W}$.

    2.  Hence find $\text{E}(W)$ and $\text{Var}(W)$. [4 marks]

Seb is analysing the total time it takes him to get to work.

To do this he adds all $3$ parts of the journey together.

The train ride always takes exactly $10$ minutes.

The continuous random variable $X$ is the total time it takes Seb to travel to work, therefore $X=W+T+10$.

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  As we need to find both parameters that define the distribution we will need to use the standard normal distribution.

        The image below shows the two given regions of $W$ and the corresponding position on the standard normal distribution curve.

        ![AA1181_s1 (1)](<images/AA1181_s1 (1).JPEG>)

        Where $W=6$ corresponds to $z_1$ and $W=9$ corresponds to $z_2$.

        Hence, from the image we can say
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            P(W<6)&=P(Z<z_1) &\hspace{2em} & P(W>9)=P(Z>z_2)\\[6pt]
            P(Z<z_1)&=0.07529 &\hspace{2em} & P(Z>z_2)=0.3309
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Any normal distribution can be standardised to a standard normal distribution using the formula $\displaystyle Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}$.

        To find the $Z-$scores that correspond to the given probabilities we need use the inverse standard normal function.

        The inverse normal function is generally defined in the form $P(Z<z)$, therefore we use the symmetry of the normal distribution to rewrite the inequality for $z_2$.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            P(W<6)&=P(Z<z_1) &\hspace{2em} & P(W>9)=P(Z>z_2)\\[6pt]
            P(Z<z_1)&=0.07529 &\hspace{2em} & P(Z\textcolor{red}{>}z_2)=0.3309\\[6pt]
            & &\hspace{2em} & P(W<9)=1-P(Z>z_2)\\[6pt]
            & &\hspace{2em} & P(W<9)=1-0.3309\\[6pt]
            & &\hspace{2em} & P(Z\textcolor{red}{<}z_2)=0.6691
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can now use the inverse normal function to find $z_1$ and $z_2$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            z_1&=\text{invNorm}(0.07529,0,1)&\hspace{2em}&z_2=\text{invNorm}(0.6691,0,1)\\[6pt]
            &=-1.4374...&\hspace{2em}&=0.4374...
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Next we use the standardised normal variable formula to form two equations in terms of $\mu_{W}$ and $\sigma_{W}$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            z_1&=\frac{X_1-\mu_{W}}{\sigma_{W}} & \hspace{2em} & z_2=\frac{X_2-\mu_{W}}{\sigma_{W}}\\[6pt]
            -1.4374...&=\frac{6-\mu_{W}}{\sigma_{W}} & \hspace{2em} & 0.4374...=\frac{9-\mu_{W}}{\sigma_{W}}\tag*{\textbf{A1M1}}\\[6pt]
            -1.4374...\sigma_{W}&=6-\mu_{W} & \hspace{2em} & 0.4374...\sigma_{W}=9-\mu_{W}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can simultaneously solve the equations above to get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &\mu_{W}=8.30 \hspace{2em}\sigma_{W}=1.60 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.  We can find the expected value and variance
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \text{E}(W)&=\mu_{W} &\hspace{2em} &\text{Var}(W)=(\sigma_{W})^2\\[6pt]
            \text{E}(W)&=8.30 \, \text{minutes} &\hspace{2em} &\text{Var}(W)=(1.60)^2\\[6pt]
            & &\hspace{2em} &\text{Var}(W)=2.56 \, \text{minutes}^2 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  1.  The area under a probability distribution curve must be equal to $1$.

        Hence, we can form an equation
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{0}^{8}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t&=1\\[6pt]
            \int_{0}^{8}\frac{a(8-t)}{t+1}\,\mathrm{d}t&=1 \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can solve the above equation using a G.D.C.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            a=0.0849 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        We can check our answer by sketching the function on a G.D.C. and verifying the area under the curve from $x=0$ to $x=8$ is equal to $1$.

    2.  Using the formula for the expected value of a CRV we can write
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \text{E}(T)&=\int_{0}^{8}tf(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\[6pt]
            &=\int_{0}^{8}t\times\frac{0.0849(8-t)}{t+1}\,\mathrm{d}t\\[6pt]
            &=1.717... \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]\\[6pt]
            &=1.72\, \text{minutes} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        However, for this we need to evaluate $\text{E}(T^2)$

        We can find this using a G.D.C.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \text{E}(\textcolor{red}{T^2})&=\int_{0}^{8}\textcolor{red}{t^2}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\[6pt]
            &=\int_{0}^{8}t^2\times\frac{0.0849(8-t)}{t+1}\,\mathrm{d}t\\[6pt]
            &=5.527...  \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]\\[6pt]
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \text{Var}(T)&=\text{E}(T^2)-[(\text{E}(T)]^2\\[6pt]
            &=5.527...-(1.717...)^2\\[6pt]
            &=2.5792...\\[6pt]
            &=2.58\,\text{minutes}^2 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
