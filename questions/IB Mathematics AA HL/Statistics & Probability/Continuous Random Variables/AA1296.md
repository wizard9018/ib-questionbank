# AA1296

**Maximum mark: 21**

A continuous random variable, $X$, has a probability density function defined by
$$
\begin{aligned}
    f(x)=\begin{cases}
        \dfrac{1}{4}x, & 0 \leq x \leq a \\[12pt]
        \dfrac{2}{7}-\dfrac{1}{28}x, & a \leq x \leq 8\\[12pt]
        0, & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

1.  It is known that $\text{P}(X \leq a)=0.125$.

    1.  Show that $a=1$.

    2.  Hence show that $\mathrm{E}(X)=3$.[4 marks]

2.  Find $\text{P}(0.5\leq X \leq 2)$.[3 marks]

3.  Given that $\mathrm{E}(X^2)=\frac{73}{6}$ find $\mathrm{Var}(X)$.[1 mark]

Another continuous random variable, $Y$, is normally distributed with a mean of $1.5$ and a standard deviation of $\sigma_Y$.

For this distribution it is known that $\text{P}(Y \geq 1.8367)=0.2$. This information is shown below

![AA1296-q2](images/AA1296-q2.JPEG)

4.  Find $\sigma_Y$.[3 marks]

5.  Hence find $\text{P}(0.5\leq Y \leq 2)$.[1 mark]

A water utility company serves a large number of households. $90\%$ of their households are classed as __regular__ households. The rest are classed as __premium__ households.

The water usage per day, in $\text{m}^3$, of the regular households is modeled by the random variable $X$.

The water usage per day, in $\text{m}^3$, of the premium households is modeled by the random variable $Y$.

If a household uses between $0.5\,\text{m}^3$ and $2\,\text{m}^3$ of water per day they are eligible for a special deal.

6.  A household is chosen at random. It is found that they are eligible for a special deal. What is the probability they are a premium household?[3 marks]

The water company charges the households a daily fee based on how much water is used in that day.

The charge per day, $C_X$, in \$US, for the regular households is calculated using the following formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
    C_X&=1.5X+0.5
\end{aligned}
$$
7.  Find
    1.  $\text{E}(C_X)$.

    2.  $\text{Var}(C_X)$.[3 marks]

Premium households are charged in a different way to regular households.

The charge per day, $C_Y$, in \$US, for the premium households is calculated using the following formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
    C_Y&=sY+t \hspace{2em}\text{where } s,t \in \mathbb{R}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If the value of $\text{E}(C_Y)$ is  \$$1$ less than the value of $\text{E}(C_X)$ and $\text{Var}(C_Y)=\tfrac{32}{57}\text{Var}(C_X)$ then

8.  Given that $s>0$, find $s$ and $t$.[3 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  The probability density function (P.D.F.) of $X$ is a piecewise function consisting of two straight lines.

        We can sketch the first part of the P.D.F., from $x=0$ to $x=a$, noting that the end-point has coordinates $(a, \tfrac{1}{4}a)$

        ![AA1296-a1](images/AA1296-a1.JPEG)

    From the information given we know the area under the graph for this part is $0.125$.

    Hence we can form an equation for the area of the triangle
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0.125&=\frac{a \times \tfrac{1}{4}a}{2}\\[6pt]
        a^2&=1\\[6pt]
        a&=1\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    From the information given we can see that $a>0$.

    2.  We can make a rough sketch of the rest of the P.D.F., noting that the two lines do intersect when $x=1$.

        ![AA1296-a2](images/AA1296-a2.JPEG)

        We can use the formula for the expected value of a continuous random variable but we must be careful to evaluate the integral over both parts and sum the results
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathrm{E}(X)&=\int_{0}^{1}x\times\tfrac{1}{4}x\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{8}x\times\left[\tfrac{2}{7}-\tfrac{1}{28}x\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{M1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can integrate each term to get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\bigg[\tfrac{1}{12}x^3\bigg]_{0}^{1}+\bigg[\tfrac{1}{7}x^2-\tfrac{1}{84}x^3\bigg]_{1}^{8}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}\\[12pt]
            &=\tfrac{1}{12}+\left(\tfrac{64}{7}-\tfrac{512}{84}\right)-\left(\tfrac{1}{7}-\tfrac{1}{84}\right)\\[6pt]
            &=\frac{1}{12}+\frac{35}{12}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}\\[6pt]
            &=3\hspace{2em}\text{...as required.}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  We can identify on our sketch the region whose area we need to find

    ![AA1296-a3](images/AA1296-a3.JPEG)

    In a similar fashion to the previous part we need to evaluate the different parts of the function and then sum them together.

    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(0.5\leq X \leq 2)&=\int_{0.5}^{1}\tfrac{1}{4}x\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{1}^{2}\left[\tfrac{2}{7}-\tfrac{1}{28}x\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(M1A1)}}}\\[12pt]
        &=0.3258...\\[12pt]
        &=0.326\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Using the formula for variance and the value for $\mathrm{E}(X)$ from part (a) we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Var}(X)&=\mathrm{E}(X^2)-\left(\mathrm{E}(X)\right)^2\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{73}{6}-3^2\\[6pt]
        &=\dfrac{19}{6}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}\\[6pt]
        &=3.166....
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  To find an unknown standard deviation we need to use the $Z-$distribution along with the standardisation formula.

    This distribution is defined as $Z \sim N(0,1^2)$.

    Recall, $\text{P}(Y \geq 1.8367)=0.2$, we want to find the corresponding $Z-$score.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \text{inverseNorm(0.8, 0, 1)}&=0.8416...\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Notice we used $0.8$, this is because the $0.2$ region is in the upper tail of the distribution.

    We can now use the standardised normal variable formulae, with $Y$ replacing $X$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        Z&=\frac{Y-\mu}{\sigma}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We know that $\mu=1.5$ and when $Y=1.8367$ then $Z=0.8416...$, hence we can form an equation and solve for $\sigma_Y$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        0.8416...&=\frac{1.8367-1.5}{\sigma_Y}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
        \sigma_Y&=0.4000...\\[6pt]
        &=0.400\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  We can now fully define the distribution for $Y$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        Y &\sim N(1.5, 0.400^2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using a G.D.C. we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \text{P}(0.5\leq Y \leq 2)&=0.8878...\\[6pt]
        &=0.888\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5.  In this question we have been given a condition. We are told that the chosen customer is eligible for the special deal.

    Consider the conditional probability formula
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A|B)=\frac{\textcolor{red}{\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)}}{\textcolor{blue}{\mathrm{P}(B)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    If we assign event $A$ as $'$the household is a premium customer$'$ and event $B$ as $'$the household is eligible for the special deal$'$, then $ \mathrm{P}(A|B)$ is the probability that the household is premium __given__ they are eligible.

    First consider $\mathrm{P}(B)$. This represents the probability that the chosen customer is eligible for a special deal.

    The calculation for this includes __both__ the regular households who are eligible and the premium households who are eligible.

    We can use our answers from part (b) and (e) and the given information about the proportion of household type to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(B)&=0.9 \times 0.3258... + 0.1 \times 0.8878...\\[6pt]
        &=\textcolor{blue}{0.3820...}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    To find $\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)$, the household is premium __and__ eligible, we calculate
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A \cap B)&=0.1 \times 0.8878...\\[6pt]
        &=\textcolor{red}{0.08878...}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore putting these results together we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A|B)&=\frac{\textcolor{red}{0.08878...}}{\textcolor{blue}{0.3820...}}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
        &=0.2323...\\[6pt]
        &=0.232\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6.  This part of the questions concerns the linear transform of a random variable.

    We will be using the following formulae
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(aX+b)&=a\mathrm{E}(X)+b\\[6pt]
        \mathrm{Var}(aX+b)&=a^2\mathrm{Var}(X)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    1.  Using the formula above we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}(C_X)&=\mathrm{E}(1.5X+0.5)\\[6pt]
        &=1.5\,\mathrm{E}(X)+0.5
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        From part (a) we know that $\mathrm{E}(X)=3$, hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=1.5\times 3+0.5\\[6pt]
        &=\$5\hspace{1em} \text{per day}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Similarly we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Var}(C_X)&=\mathrm{Var}(1.5X+0.5)\\[6pt]
        &=1.5^2\,\mathrm{Var}(X)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Using our answer from part (c) we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{Var}&=1.5^2\,\ \times \frac{19}{6} \\[6pt]
        &=7.125\hspace{1em}\text{dollars}^2\text{ per day}\tag*{\textbf{\textcolor{black}{A1}}}\\[6pt]
        &=\tfrac{57}{8}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
7.  We can begin by forming two equations from the information provided
    $$
    \begin{aligned}

    \end{aligned}

    Using the answers from part (g) we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &&\mathrm{E}(C_Y)&= 5-1&\hspace{-1em} &&\mathrm{Var}(C_Y)&= \tfrac{32}{57}\times \tfrac{57}{8}&\\[6pt]
