# AA1168

**Maximum mark: 12**

A continuous random variable $X$ is normally distributed with a mean of $\mu$ and a standard deviation of $\tfrac{1}{6}\mu$.

Part of the distribution of $X$ is shown below.

![AA1168_q1](images/AA1168_q1.JPEG)

1.  1.  Using the information given in the diagram, show that $\mu=8.316$ to four significant figures.

    2.  Hence find the standard deviation of $X$ to four significant figures. [4 marks]

Charles is designing a game for the school fair in which players must balance on a tightrope for as long as possible.

Players win money to donate to charity.

Depending on how long they stay on the rope, they either receive money from Charles or give it to him. The game is free to play.

![AA1168_q2](images/AA1168_q2.JPEG)

The CRV $X$, from part (a),  is the time a player stays on the rope and is measured in seconds.

The discrete random variable $D$ is the money a player gives or receives and is rounded to $2$ decimal places.

Charles decides to have $3$ different payout categories.

For category $1$, if a player stays on the rope for less than $8$ seconds, they must __give__ Charles \$_a_.

For category $2$, if a player stays on the rope for between $8$ and $10$ seconds, they __receive__ \$_b_ from Charles.

For category $3$, if a player stays on the rope for more than $10$ seconds, they __receive__ \$_c_ from Charles.

Where $a,b,c \in \mathrm{R}$.

2.  1.  Complete the two missing spaces, to $4$ significant figures, in the probability distribution table below

        ![table_q](images/table_q.JPEG)

    2.  When designing the game, Charles is deciding what values to assign to $a, b$ and $c$. For category $1$ players give money to charles, therefore $a<0$.

        He wants the game to be fair.

        If $c=5-b^2$ and $a<0$, what is the maximum value of $|a|$? [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1.  We can use the standardised normal distribution formula to write an equation in terms of $\mu$.

        We need to find the $Z-$score corresponding to $10$ for this. We will call this value $z_1$.

        We use the inverse normal function to find $z_1$. This function is generally defined as P$(Z<z)$, therefore we need to find P$(X<10)$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \text{P}(X<10)&=1-\text{P}(X>10)\\[6pt]
            &=1-0.1122\\[6pt]
            &=0.8878
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now we can use the inverse normal function to find $z_1$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            z_1&=\text{invNorm}(0.8878,0,1)\\[6pt]
            &=1.2149...\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Now, we can substitute the known information into the standardised normal distribution formula
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            Z&=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\\[6pt]
            1.2149...&=\frac{10-\mu}{\tfrac{1}{6}\mu}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}\\[6pt]
            1.2149...\tfrac{1}{6}\mu&=10-\mu
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Solving this using a G.D.C. gives $\mu=8.316\hspace{0.525em}$ to four significant figures as required.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  The standard deviation ($\sigma_X$) of $X$ is
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \sigma_X&=\frac{8.316}{6}\\[6pt]
            &=1.386 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  For the game to be fair the expected payout must be zero. Therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \text{E}(D)&=0 \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
