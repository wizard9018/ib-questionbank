# AA411

**Maximum mark: 16**

The mass $M$, in grams, of oranges is normally distributed with mean
$\mu$. The table below shows probabilities for values of $M$.

![9c13f40db1a4c17af000f6c0b716cb019a80d38c.svg](images/9c13f40db1a4c17af000f6c0b716cb019a80d38c.svg)

1.
    1.  Write down the value of $k$.

    2.  Show that $\mu = 165$. [3 marks]

2.  Find $\mathrm{P}(M < 120)$. [5 marks]

The oranges are packed in boxes of fifteen.

Any orange with a mass less than $120$ grams is classified as small.

3.  Find the probability that a box of oranges selected at
    random contains at most two small oranges.
    [3 marks]

4.  A wooden crate contains $20$ boxes of oranges. A crate is
    selected at random.

    1.  Find the expected number of boxes in this crate that contain at
        most two small oranges.

    2.  Find the probability that at least $18$ boxes in this crate
        contain at most two small oranges. [5 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.
        $k = 0.02$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(M < 110) &= 0.02 \hspace{3em} \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(M > 220) &= 0.02
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, using the
        symmetry of the normal curve for $M$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mu &= \dfrac{110 + 220}{2} \\[6pt]
        &= 165 \text{ grams} \hspace{3em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  We have $M \sim \mathrm{N}(165,\sigma^2)$ and
    $Z \sim \mathrm{N}(0,1)$.

    Hence we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(M < 110) &= 0.02 \\[12pt]
    \hspace{0.7em} \mathrm{P}\hspace{-0.05em}\bigg(Z < \dfrac{110-165}{\sigma}\bigg)\hspace{-0.125em} &= 0.02 \\[6pt]
    -\dfrac{55}{\sigma} &= \text{invNorm}(0.02) \\[8pt]
    -\dfrac{55}{\sigma} &\approx -2.05375 \\[12pt]
    \sigma &\approx 26.7803 \text{ grams}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathrm{P}(M < 120) &= \text{normCdf}(-\infty,120,165,26.7803) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.0464
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Let $X$ be the number of small oranges in the selected box of
    fifteen.

    We have $X \sim \mathrm{B}(15,0.0464)$.
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3.2em} \mathrm{P}(X \leq 2) &= \text{binomCdf}(15,0.0464,0,2) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.970
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $Y$ be the number of boxes in the selected crate containing at
    most two small oranges. We have
    $Y\hspace{-0.1em} \sim \mathrm{B}(20,0.970)$.
    1.  Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $Y$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}[Y] &= np \\[6pt]
        &=(20)(0.970) \\[6pt]
        &= 19.4 \text{ boxes} \hspace{3.6em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} \mathrm{P}(Y \geq 18) &= \text{binomCdf}(20,0.970,18,20) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.979
        \end{aligned}
        $$
