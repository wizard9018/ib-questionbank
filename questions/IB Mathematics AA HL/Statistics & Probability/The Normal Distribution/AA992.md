# AA992

**Maximum mark: 16**

Abby sells pumpkins for Halloween, her stall is very popular and she always has a long line of customers.

The weight of each pumpkin she sells, $W$, in kilograms, is normally distributed with a mean of $5.7\,$kg and a standard deviation of $0.85\,$kg.

1. Find the probability that a randomly bought pumpkin weighs more than $6.5\,$kg. [2 marks]

2. Five customers join the queue and one by one they buy a random pumpkin from Abbey. Find the probability that only the last three customers buy a pumpkin weighing more than $6.5\,$kg. [3 marks]

Customers prefer pumpkins to be over $7\,$kg, it makes them easier to carve.

3.  1. One of the customers who purchased a pumpkin weighing over $6.5\,$kg weighed their pumpkin when they got home. What is the probability it weighs over $7\,$kg?

    2. Out of the three customers who bought pumpkins weighing over $6.5\,$kg what is the probability exactly two of them had pumpkins weighing over $7\,$kg?[7 marks]

Customers sometimes prefer to eat the pumpkins they buy. Pumpkins that weigh less than $a\,$kg are best for eating.

It is known that $P(W\leq a)=\dfrac{1}{10}P(a < W\leq6.5)$.

4. Find the maximum weight, in kilograms, of a randomly bought pumpkin from Abbey if it is considered to be in the weight range that is best for eating. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. $W$ follows a normal distribution.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        W \sim \mathrm{N}(5.7, 0.85^2)&\\[3pt]
        \mathrm{P}(W > 6.5) &= \text{normCdf}(6.5,\,\infty,\, 5.7,\, 0.85)\\[3pt]
        &=0.173307...\\[3pt]
        &=0.173
    \end{aligned}
    $$
2. The first two customers must not buy a pumpkin over $6.5\,$kg and the last three must buy one.\
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        (1-0.173307...)^2\times(0.173307...)^3 &=0.003557444..\\[3pt]
        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{0.00356}\hspace{0.525em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
3. 1. This is conditional probability, we can use the formula.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathrm{P}(W > 7 | W > 6.5) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(W > 7 \cap W > 6.5)}{\mathrm{P}(W > 6.5)}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $\mathrm{P}(W > 7 \cap W > 6.5) = \mathrm{P}(W > 7)$ as $\mathrm{P}(W > 7)$ is a subset of $\mathrm{P}(W > 6.5)$.

        Hence
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathrm{P}(W > 7 | W > 6.5) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(W > 7)}{\mathrm{P}(W > 6.5)}\\[6pt]
            &=\dfrac{\text{normCdf}(7,\, \infty,\, 5.7,\, 0.85)}{0.173307...}\\[6pt]
            &= 0.364
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2. Let $T$ represent the total number of the three whose pumpkins weigh more than $7\,$kg. We have

        * A fixed number of trials (3 people bought pumpkins weighing over $6.5\,$kg)

        * Each trial is independent (weights of pumpkins are independent)
        * Each trial has two outcomes ($W>7$ or $W \leq 7$)
        * Constant probability ($\mathrm{P}(W>7|W>6.5)=0.364$)

        Therefore $T \sim \mathrm{B}(3,0.364)$. We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathrm{P}(T=2) &= \text{binomPdf}(3,0.364,2) \\[6pt]
            &= 0.2528... \\[6pt]
            &= 0.253
        \end{aligned}
        $$
4. Here is the region $\mathrm{P}(a < W \leq 6.5)$ shown below

    ![norm ans](<images/norm ans.png>)

    If we consider both $\mathrm{P}(W \leq a)$ and $\mathrm{P}(W\leq6.5)$ individually

    ![double norm](<images/double norm.png>)

    We can see that  $\mathrm{P}(a< W\leq6.5)=\mathrm{P}(W\leq6.5) - \mathrm{P}(W\leq a )$ therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(W\leq a)&=\dfrac{1}{10}\mathrm{P}(a< W\leq6.5)\\[6pt]
        &=\dfrac{1}{10}(\mathrm{P}(W\leq6.5) - \mathrm{P}(W\leq a ))\\[6pt]
        10\mathrm{P}(W\leq a)&=\mathrm{P}(W\leq6.5) - \mathrm{P}(W\leq a )\\[6pt]
        11\mathrm{P}(W\leq a)&=\mathrm{P}(W\leq6.5)\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Recall that $\mathrm{P}(W>6.5)=0.173307...$ therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        11\mathrm{P}(W\leq a)&=1-0.173307...\\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(W\leq a)&=0.075154...\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the known mean and standard deviation of the pumpkins we can use the inverse Normal function on the G.D.C. to find the weight.

    Hence, when $a=0.075154...$, the maximum best weight for eating is $\hspace{0.525em}W=4.48\,\text{kg.}\hspace{0.525em}$
