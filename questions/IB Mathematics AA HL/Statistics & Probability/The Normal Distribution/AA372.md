# AA372

**Maximum mark: 12**

The battery lifetime, in hours, of a new model of a smartphone is
modelled by a normal distribution with mean $\mu$
and standard deviation $\sigma$.

1.  Given that $12\%$ of the smartphones have battery lifetime of more
    than $18$ hours and $21\%$ of the smartphones
    have battery lifetime of less than $13$ hours,
    calculate the value of $\mu$ and the value of $\sigma$, giving your
    answers to two significant figures.
    [6 marks]

2.  A random sample of twenty smartphones of this new model is
    selected. Let $X$ denote the
    number of smartphones in the sample whose
    batteries last for more than $18$ hours.

    1.  Calculate $\mathrm{E}[X]$.
    2.  Calculate the probability that exactly five of these
        smartphones have battery lifetime of more
        than $18$ hours.

    3.  Determine the most likely value of $X$. [6 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $T$ be the battery lifetime, in hours, of a smartphone of the
    new model.

    We have $T \sim \mathrm{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$ and
    $Z \sim \mathrm{N}(0,1)$.

    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(T < 13) &= 0.21 \\[11pt]
    \mathrm{P}\hspace{-0.05em}\bigg(Z < \dfrac{13-\mu}{\sigma}\bigg)\hspace{-0.125em} &= 0.21 \\[5pt]
    \dfrac{13-\mu}{\sigma} &= \text{invNorm}(0.21) \\[7pt]
    \dfrac{13-\mu}{\sigma} &\approx -0.806 \\[11pt]
    \hspace{3em} \mu - 0.806\sigma &\approx 13 \hspace{5em}{(1)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    and
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(T > 18) &= 0.12 \\[11pt]
    \hspace{0.6em} \mathrm{P}\hspace{-0.05em}\bigg(Z > \dfrac{18-\mu}{\sigma}\bigg)\hspace{-0.125em} &= 0.12 \\[5pt]
    \dfrac{18-\mu}{\sigma} &= \text{invNorm}(0.88) \\[7pt]
    \dfrac{18-\mu}{\sigma} &\approx 1.175 \\[11pt]
    \mu + 1.175\sigma &\approx 18 \hspace{5em}{(2)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence,
    solving the system of linear equations $(1)$-$(2)$ for $\mu$ and
    $\sigma$, we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{8em} \mu \approx 15 \text{ hours}\hspace{0.525em} \text{ and } \hspace{0.525em}\sigma \approx 2.5 \text{ hours} \hspace{2.5em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Using the formula for the expected value of
        $X \sim \mathrm{B}(20,0.12)$, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}[X] &= np \\[6pt]
        &= (20)(0.12) \hspace{4.1em} \\[6pt]
        &= 2.4
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(X = 5) &= \text{binomPdf}(20,0.12,5) \hspace{1.3em} \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.0567
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(X = 0) &= \text{binomPdf}(20,0.12,0) \hspace{1.3em} \\[3pt]
        &\approx 0.0776 \\[10pt]
        \mathrm{P}(X = 1) &= \text{binomPdf}(20,0.12,1) \\[3pt]
        &\approx 0.212 \\[10pt]
        \mathrm{P}(X = 2) &= \text{binomPdf}(20,0.12,2) \\[3pt]
        &\approx 0.274 \\[10pt]
        \mathrm{P}(X = 3) &= \text{binomPdf}(20,0.12,3) \\[3pt]
        &\approx 0.224 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence the most likely value of
        $X$ is  $2$
