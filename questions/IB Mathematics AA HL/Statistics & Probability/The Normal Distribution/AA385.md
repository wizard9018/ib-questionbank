# AA385

**Maximum mark: 12**

Farmer Hitoshi grows Kamo eggplants and the weights of his eggplants
are normally distributed with a mean of
$284$ g and standard deviation of
$66$ g.

1.
    1.  Calculate the percentage of Hitoshi's eggplants that weigh
        between $230$ g
        and $320$ g.

    2.  Hitoshi has $150$ eggplants to take to market. Find the
        expected $\text{number}$ of
        eggplants weighing more than $320$ g.
        Give your answer correct to the nearest
        integer.

    3.  Find the probability that at least $45$ of the $150$ eggplants
        weigh more than
        $320$ g. [6 marks]

Farmer Antonio grows Barbarella eggplants and the weights of his
eggplants are normally distributed with a mean
of $402$ g. Antonio only takes to
market eggplants that weigh more than
$320$ g. Over a period of time, Antonio finds
that he has to reject $1$ in $12$ eggplants due to them being
underweight.

2.
    1.  Find the standard deviation of the weights of Antonio's
        eggplants.

    2.  Antonio has $250$ eggplants to take to the market. Find the
        expected $\text{number}$ of
        eggplants weighing more than $410$ g.
        Give your answer correct to the nearest
        integer. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Let $X$ be the weights, in grams, of Hitoshi's eggplants.
        We have $X \sim \mathrm{N}(284, 66^2)$

        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(230 \leq X \leq 320) &= \text{normCdf}(230, 320, 284, 66) \hspace{2em} \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.501 \\[6pt]
        &\approx 50.1\%
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.3em} \mathrm{P}(X > 320) &= \text{normCdf}(320, \infty, 284, 66) \\[5pt]
        &\approx 0.293
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Let $S$ be the number of eggplants
        for sale weighing more than $320$ g.

        We have $S\hspace{-0.1em} \sim \mathrm{B}(150, 0.293)$.
        Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $S$ we
        obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}[S] &= np \\[6pt]
        &= (150)(0.293) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 44 \text{ eggplants} \hspace{2.1em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.6em} \mathrm{P}(S \geq 45) &= \text{binomCdf}(150,0.293,45,150) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.456
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  Let $Y$ be the weights, in grams, of Antonio's eggplants.
        We have $Y \sim \mathrm{N}(402,\sigma^2)$ and
        $Z \sim  \mathrm{N}(0,1)$.

        Hence we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(Y \leq 320) &= \dfrac{1}{12} \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}\hspace{-0.05em}\bigg(Z \leq \dfrac{320 - 402}{\sigma}\bigg)\hspace{-0.125em} &= \dfrac{1}{12} \\[6pt]
        -\dfrac{82}{\sigma} &= \text{invNorm}(1/12) \hspace{5.4em} \\[6pt]
        -\dfrac{82}{\sigma} &\approx -1.383 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{9.2em} \sigma \approx 59.3 \text{ g} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        $$

    2.  We have $Y \sim \mathrm{N}(402,59.3^2)$.
        Hence we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(Y > 410 \hspace{0.15em}|\hspace{0.15em} Y > 320) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(Y > 410) }{\mathrm{P}(Y > 320)} \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{\text{normCdf}(410, \infty, 402, 59.3)}{\text{normCdf}(320, \infty, 402, 59.3)}  \hspace{0.8em} \\[6pt]
        &\approx \dfrac{0.4463}{0.9166} \\[12pt]
        &\approx 0.487
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Let $T$ be the number of eggplants for sale weighing more than
        $410$ g.

        We have $T \sim \mathrm{B}(250,0.487)$.
        Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $T$ we find

        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}[T] &= np \\[6pt]
        &= (250)(0.487) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 122 \text{ eggplants} \hspace{1.5em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
