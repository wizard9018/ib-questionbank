# AA442

**Maximum mark: 12**

The weights $W$, in grams, of frogs in an enclosure are normally
distributed with a mean of $230$ grams and a standard deviation of $15$
grams.

A frog that weights less than $210$ grams is considered as a small frog.

A frog is selected at random.

1.  Find the probability that this frog is small. [3 marks]

A frog that weights more than $250$ grams is considered as a big frog.

Another frog is selected at random.

2.  Find the probability that the frog is neither small nor
    big. [3 marks]

Frogs that aren't small or big are considered as standard frogs.

A sample of $450$ frogs are selected.

3.  Find the expected number of standard frogs in this sample.
    [3 marks]

Another sample of $N$ frogs are selected.

4.  Find the minimum value for $N$ such that the expected
    number of standard frogs in the sample is $100$.[3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We have $W \sim \mathrm{N}(230,15^2)$.
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.7em} \mathrm{P}(\text{small}) &= \mathrm{P}(W < 210) \\[6pt]
    &= \text{normCdf}(-\infty,210,230,15) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.0912
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  We have $W \sim \mathrm{N}(230,15^2)$.
    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.6em} \mathrm{P}(\text{not small \& not big}) &= \mathrm{P}(210 \leq W \leq 250) \\[6pt]
    &= \text{normCdf}(210,250,230,15) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.818
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3.  Let $X$ be the number of standard frogs in the sample of $450$
    frogs.

    We have $X \sim \mathrm{B}(450,0.818)$.
    Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $X$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{0.1em} \mathrm{E}[X] &= np \\[6pt]
    &= (450)(0.818) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 368 \text{ frogs}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $Y$ be the number of standard frogs in the sample of $N$ frogs.
    We have $Y \sim \mathrm{B}(N,0.818)$.

    Hence, solving the equation $\mathrm{E}[Y] = 100$ for $N$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{6.1em} (N)(0.818) &= 100 \\[6pt]
    N &\approx 122.249 \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we find
     $N_{\text{min}} = 123$
