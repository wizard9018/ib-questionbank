# AA1335

**Maximum mark: 16**

At AeroLink Dynamics, a company that develops autonomous drones, the landing time, $T$ seconds, is defined as the time it takes a drone to land after receiving a command.

$T$ is normally distributed with a mean of $4.8$ seconds and a standard deviation of $0.5$ seconds.

1.    Find the percentage of drones that have a landing time of more than $5$ seconds. [2 marks]

The company classifies drones as _elite_ if their landing time is between $4.1$ and $4.4$ seconds.

2.    Find the probability a randomly chosen drone is classified as _elite_. [2 marks]

The company sells the drones in boxes. Each box contains $5$ drones.

3.    Find the probability a randomly chosen box contains exactly $1$ _elite_ drone. [2 marks]

4.    If $20$ of these boxes are chosen, find the probability that at least half of them contain exactly $1$ _elite_ drone. [2 marks]

It is known that $\text{P}(a<T<b)=0.6881$, and that $2\text{P}(T<a)=5\text{P}(T>b)$ where $a$ and $b$ are landing times.

A drone which takes more than $b\,$ seconds to land is classified as faulty.

5.    Find the minimum landing time for a drone to be classified as faulty. [4 marks]

AeroLink Dynamics is about to release a new drone. The new landing time, $S\,$seconds, is normally distributed with a mean of $3.3$ seconds and a standard deviation of $k$ seconds. The company finds the interquartile range to be $0.3818$ seconds.

6.    Find the value of $k$. [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1. To begin with, we can define the continuous random variable using the parameters given
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    T \sim N(4.8,0.5^2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We have been asked to find $\mathrm{P}(T>5)$. This is represented by the following shaded region

    ![AA1335-a1](images/AA1335-a1.JPEG)

    Hence, using a G.D.C., we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(T>5)&=\text{normalCDF}(5, \infty, 4.8, 0.5)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
    &=0.3445...\\[6pt]
    &=34.5\%\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Note, your answer must be written as a percentage.

2. We are required to find $\mathrm{P}(4.1<T<4.4)$ which is the enclosed shaded region shown below

    ![AA1335-a2](images/AA1335-a2.JPEG)

    Using a calculator, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(4.1<T<4.4)&=\text{normalCDF}(4.1, 4.4, 4.8, 0.5)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
    &=0.1310...\\[6pt]
    &=0.131\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

3. In this part of the question we can define a new discrete random variable $E$ as the number of _elite_ drones in a box.

    Where $E=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$.

    Additionally

    - We are told there is a fixed number of drones (trials) in a box, $n=5$.

    - The outcome of each trial is either the drone is an _elite_ drone or is not an _elite_ drone. i.e. each trial is a Bernoulli trial.

    - The probability of success, $p=0.1310...$, of an individual trial, i.e. a drone being _elite_, is independent of any other trial and a fixed value- note we can assume this.

    Hence the conditions have been fulfilled such that $E$ follows a binomial distribution.

    Therefore we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    E& \sim B(n,p)\\[6pt]
    E &\sim B(5,0.1310...)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P}(E=1)&=\text{binomPDF(5,0.1310...,1)}\\[6pt]
    &=0.3736...\\[6pt]
    &=0.374\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4. In this question we are dealing with a binomial distribution but the random variable and the parameters have changed.

    Let the new discrete random variable $D$ be the number of boxes that contain exactly $1$ _elite_ drone.

    Where $D=\{0,1,2,....19,20\}$

    - We are now considering $20$ boxes therefore $n=20$.

    - The outcome of each trial is either the box contains $1$ _elite_ drone or it does not contain $1$ _elite_ drone i.e. each trial is still a Bernoulli trial.

    - The probability of success is now $p=0.3736...$, this is the probability the box contains exactly $1$ _elite_ drone and it is a fixed value and independent.

    Therefore
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    D &\sim B(20,0.3736...)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{P}(D \geq 10)&=\text{binomCDF(20,0.3736...,10,20)}\\[6pt]
    &=0.1737...\\[6pt]
    &=0.174\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

5. The following rough sketch shows the normal distribution broken into $3$ regions

    ![AA1335-a3](images/AA1335-a3.JPEG)

    with $\text{P}(T<a)=A$, $\text{P}(a<T<b)=0.6881$ and $\text{P}(T>b)=B$.

    If $T>b$, then the drone is classified as faulty. Hence we need to find the value of $b$, which is the critical value for region $B$.

    To do this we use the information given to attempt to find the value of $A$ and $B$.

    Hence, we can form the following equations
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textcolor{black}{\textbf{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    2A&=5B &A+B&=1-0.6881\\[6pt]
    & &A+B&=0.3119
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using a G.D.C. to solve them simultaneously we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    A&=0.2227...&B&=0.08911...\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(T>b)&=0.08911...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    To find $b$ we must use the inverse normal distribution with the parameter values $\mu=4.8$ and $\sigma=0.5$.

    However, as region $B$ is the upper tail then we use the area $1-0.08911...=0.9108...$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    b&=\text{inverseNorm}(0.9108..., 4.8, 0.5)\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
    &=5.473...\\[6pt]
    &=5.47\,\text{seconds}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

6. The continuous random variable $S$ is normally distributed such that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    S \sim N(3.3, k^2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    To help us find $k$ we can first use the information given and show the four quartiles on a sketch of the normal distribution curve for $S$.

    ![AA1335-a4](images/AA1335-a4.JPEG)

    The area of each region is $0.25$ and the I.Q.R. is given by $Q_3-Q_1$.

    Hence, due to this symmetry we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3.3-Q_1&=\frac{\text{IQR}}{2}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the information given we can find the value of $Q_1$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    3.3-Q_1&=\frac{0.3818}{2}\\[6pt]
    Q_1&=3.1091\tag*{\textbf{{(A1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Therefore we can say that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(S<Q_1)&=0.25\\[6pt]
    \mathrm{P}(S<3.1091)&=0.25
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can't directly use the inverse normal distribution as we don't know the value of $\sigma_S$.

    However, we can find the associated $Z-$value of the lower quartile.

    Recall, that is the value of $Z$ which is the lower quartile, $z_1$, of a standard normal distribution.
    If
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Z \sim N(0,1^2)
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    then
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(Z<z_1)&=0.25
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    and using the inverse normal distribution we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z_1&=\text{inverseNorm}(0.25,0,1)\\[6pt]
    &=-0.6744...
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can transform this into $Q_1$ using the formula for the standardised normal variable
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    Z&=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    z_1&=\frac{Q_1-3.3}{\sigma}\\[6pt]
    -0.6744...&=\frac{3.1091-3.3}{k}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving for $k$ we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    k&=0.2830...\\[6pt]
    &=0.283\,\text{seconds}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
