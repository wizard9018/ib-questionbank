# AA1326

**Maximum mark: 7**

A teacher gives a class a short quiz and records the change in student score compared to their previous score.
The teacher noted that $3$ students had their score increase by $1$ point.
The full results are shown in the following table, where $a \in \mathbb{Z^+}$.

| $\textrm{Change in score}$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | &nbsp;$0$&nbsp; | &nbsp;$1$&nbsp; |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Frequency | $2$ | $a$ | $1$ | $3$ |

1.
    1.    Find an expression for the mean of the change in score in terms of $a$.

    2.    Hence, show that the variance of the change in score is $\dfrac{5(13+3a)}{(6+a)^2}$. [4 marks]

It is known that the mean and variance of the change in score are in the ratio $-2:5$.

2.    Show that $a=4$. [3 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  1. Consider the formula for the mean which is in the formula booklet
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \bar{x}&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{f_ix_i}{n}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        In this example
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &\bar{x}  \text{ is the mean of the change in score}\\[6pt]
        &x_i  \text{ refers to each individual value of the change in score}\\[6pt]
        &f_i  \text{ refers to the associated frequency value}\\[6pt]
        &n  \text{ is the total frequency}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Substituting the values from the table into the formula we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{mean}&=\frac{-2 \times 2+ (-1)\times a+0\times 1+1\times 3}{2+a+1+3}\tag*{\textbf{{(M1)}}}\\[6pt]
        &=\dfrac{-1-a}{6+a}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2. Let's consider the formula for variance
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \sigma^2&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{f_ix_i^2}{n}-\mu^2
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        In addition to part(a), $\mu $ is the mean ($\bar{x}$) of the change in score.

        Note that in this example $\bar{x}$ and $\mu$ represent the same thing.

        Substituting the values from the table into the formula and using our answer from part (a) we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \text{variance}&=\frac{(-2)^2 \times 2+ (-1)^2\times a+0^2\times 1+1^2\times 3}{2+a+1+3}-\left(\frac{-1-a}{6+a}\right)^2\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{8+a+3}{6+a}-\frac{(-1-a)^2}{(6+a)^2}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Finding a common denominator and simplifying we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{(11+a)(6+a)-(-1-a)^2}{(6+a)^2}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{66+17a+a^2-(1+2a+a^2)}{(6+a)^2}\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{66+17a+a^2-1-2a-a^2}{(6+a)^2}\\[6pt]
        &=\dfrac{5(13+3a)}{(6+a)^2}\hspace{1em}\text{...as required.}\tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2. We are told that
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{mean}:\text{variance}&=-2:5\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If we divide both ratios by the element on the right we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\text{mean}}{\text{variance}}:1&=\frac{-2}{5}:1\\[6pt]
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    therefore we can write
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\text{mean}}{\text{variance}}&=\frac{-2}{5}\\[12pt]
    5 \times \text{mean}&=-2 \times \text{variance}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using parts (a)(i) and (a)(ii) we can rewrite the equation in terms of $a$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    5\times \left(\frac{-1-a}{6+a}\right)&=-2 \times \left(\dfrac{5(3a+13)}{(a+6)^2}\right)\tag*{\textbf{{R1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Simplifying we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{-(-1-a)}{6+a}&=\dfrac{2(3a+13)}{(a+6)^2}\\[12pt]
    \frac{1+a}{6+a}&=\dfrac{2(3a+13)}{(a+6)^2}\tag*{\textbf{{M1}}}\\[12pt]
    \frac{(1+a)(6+a)^2}{6+a}&=2(3a+13)\\[12pt]
    (1+a)(6+a)&=6a+26\\[6pt]
    6+7a+a^2&=6a+26\\[6pt]
    a^2+a-20&=0\\[6pt]
    (a+5)(a-4)&=0\tag*{\textbf{{A1}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Recall, $a \in \mathbb{Z^+}$, it represents a frequency and must be positive.

    Therefore $a=4$...as required.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \tag*{\textbf{{AG}}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
