# AA1170

**Maximum mark: 21**

Alex and Ben are playing a game with a spinner.

![AA1170_q1 (2)](<images/AA1170_q1 (2).JPEG>)

The triangular shaped spinner has three equal sized regions with two regions labelled __LOSE__ and one labelled __WIN__.

In turn $1$ Alex spins the spinner first. If she lands on __WIN__ then the game ends and Alex has won in turn $1$.

If she lands on __LOSE__ then Ben spins. If he lands on __WIN__ then the game ends and Ben has won in turn $1$.

If they both land on __LOSE__ then the game continues into turn $2$.

The same process repeats until someone lands on __WIN__. Alex always spins first for each new turn.

Let the turn that the game ends be the discrete random variable $T$. Where $T=1,2,3,4,...$.

1.  Show that the probability that Alex wins and $T=2$ is $\displaystyle\frac{4}{27}$. [2 marks]

2.  Hence complete the missing values in the first four columns of the probability distribution for $T$ when Alex wins.

![AA1170_table](images/AA1170_table.JPEG)

[2 marks]

3.  Show that when Alex wins the values of $\text{P}(T=t)$  form a geometric sequence. [2 marks]

4.  Justify that as $T \rightarrow \infty$ the sequence has a sum. [1 mark]

5.  Hence show that the probability Alex wins the game is $\displaystyle\frac{3}{5}$. [2 marks]

6.  Alex and Ben play the same game 10 times.

    The discrete random variable $W$ is the number of times Alex wins.

    1.  Determine the probability that $W$ is within one standard deviation of the mean.

    2.  Determine the probability that $W$ is larger than $\mathrm{E}(W)$ given $W$ is within one standard deviation of the mean. [7 marks]

Alex and Ben want to keep playing the same game with only one change. They decide to create a new heptagonal spinner.

The spinner has $7$ equally sized regions.

Each region must be labelled either __WIN__ or __LOSE__.

They want to label the spinner such that the probability of Alex winning the game is $\frac{7}{10}$.

7.  How __many__ of the regions should be labeled __WIN__? You must justify your answer. [5 marks]

6.  1.  The DRV $W$ forms a binomial distribution, where $n=10$ and $p=\tfrac{3}{5}$ therefore
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            W \sim \text{Bin}\left(10, \tfrac{3}{5}\right)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can use the formula booklet to find the expected value (mean) of the distribution
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
                \mathrm{E}(W)&=np\\[6pt]
            &=10 \times \tfrac{3}{5}\\[6pt]
            &=6 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Similarly, we can find the variance
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathrm{Var}(W) &= np(1-p)\\[6pt]
            &=10 \times \tfrac{3}{5} \times \left(1-\tfrac{3}{5}\right)\\[6pt]
            &=\tfrac{12}{5} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Therefore we can find the standard deviation of the distribution
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \sigma&=\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(W)}\\[6pt]
            &=1.5491...
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        Hence we need to find the value of the following
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
             \mathrm{P}(6 - 1.5491... < W < 6 + 1.5491...)
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        We can simplify the inequality and as the distribution is discrete we round to the next whole number
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &=\mathrm{P}(4.4509... < W < 7.5491...)\\[6pt]
            &=\mathrm{P}(5 \leq W \leq 7) \tag*{\textbf{M1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence using the G.D.C.
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            &= \mathrm{P}(5 \leq W \leq 7)\\[6pt]
            &=0.66647...\\[6pt]
            &=0.666 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
    2.  This is a conditional probability problem therefore we can use the following formula
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
            \mathrm{P}(A|B)&=\frac{\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)}{\mathrm{P}(B)}
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Where event $A$ is when $W > \mathrm{E}(W)$ and (from part(f)(i)) event $B$ is when $5 \leq W \leq 7$.

        To find the intersection of events $A$ and $B$ we should first consider the possible outcomes of event $A$.

        As $\mathrm{E}(W)=6$ and $W>6$ then $W= 7, 8, 9, 10$.

        Event $B$ occurs when $W=5, 6, 7$.

        We can see that $A \cap B$ is when $W=7$.

        Therefore $(A \cap B) =  \mathrm{P}(W=7)$. Hence we can find $ \mathrm{P}(A|B)$
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
             \mathrm{P}(A|B)&=\frac{ \mathrm{P}(A \cap B)}{ \mathrm{P}(B)}\\[6pt]
            &=\frac{ \mathrm{P}(W=7)}{ \mathrm{P}(5 \leq W \leq 7)} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
            &=\frac{0.21499...}{0.66647...} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[6pt]
            &=0.32258...\\[6pt]
            &=0.323\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

7.  Let the probability of winning on any given spin be $w$, therefore the probability of losing on any given spin is $1-w$.

    For Alex to win she must win in turn $1$ or turn $2$ or turn $3$ etc.

    We can form an infinite geometric series for the probability that Alex wins
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(\text{Alex wins})&= \mathrm{P}(T=1)+P(T=2)+ \mathrm{P}(T=3)+...\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[6pt]
        \frac{7}{10}&=w+(1-w)^2\times w + (1-w)^4\times w +...\tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    We can now write a closed formula for the open sequence shown above. To do this we use the sum of an infinite geometric sequence formula from the booklet with $u_1=w$ and $r=(1-w)^2$.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \textcolor{white}{hack}\tag*{\textbf{M1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{7}{10}&=\frac{w}{1-(1-w)^2} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Solving this on a G.D.C. we find that $w=\frac{4}{7}$.

    Hence we need to label $4 \text{ of the regions with} \textbf{ WIN}$.
