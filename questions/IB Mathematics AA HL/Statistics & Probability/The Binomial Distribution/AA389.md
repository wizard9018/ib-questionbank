# AA389

**Maximum mark: 22**

Ten balls numbered $1,2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4,4$ are placed in a bag. Balls are
taken one at a time from the bag at random and the number noted.

Throughout the question a ball is always replaced before the new ball is
taken.

1.  A single ball is taken from the bag. Let $X$ denote the value shown
    on the ball. Find
    $\mathrm{E}[X]$. [2 marks]

2.  Three balls are taken from the bag. Find the probability that:
    1.  the sum of the three numbers is $8$;

    2.  the median of the three numbers is $4$. [6 marks]

3.  Fifteen balls are taken from the bag. Find the probability that less
    than six of the balls are numbered $3$. [3 marks]

4.  Find the least number of balls that must be taken from the
    bag for the probability of taking out at
    least one ball numbered $4$ to be greater
    than $0.95$.[3 marks]

5.  Another bag also contains balls numbered $1,2,3$ or $4$. Twenty
    balls are to be taken from this bag at random. It is calculated that
    the expected number of balls numbered $2$ is $2.5$, the expected
    number of balls numbered $3$ is $5$, and the variance of the number
    of balls numbered $1$ is $1.8$. Find the least possible number of
    balls numbered $4$ in this bag. [8 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The probability distribution for $X$ is

    ![df56eabb855674e47aa0d3469d445f272214b9c9.svg](images/df56eabb855674e47aa0d3469d445f272214b9c9.svg)

    Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $X$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4em} \mathrm{E}[X] &= \sum x\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{P}(X = x) \\[6pt]
    &= 1\times\dfrac{1}{10} + 2\times\dfrac{2}{10} + 3\times\dfrac{3}{10} + 4\times\dfrac{4}{10} \\[10pt]
    &= 3
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Using the independent events formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.3em} \mathrm{P}(\text{sum is $8$}) &= 6\mathrm{P}(134) + 3\mathrm{P}(233) + 3\mathrm{P}(224) \\[12pt]
        &= 6\bigg[\dfrac{1}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\bigg] + 3\bigg[\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\bigg] + \cdots \\[6pt]
        &+ 3\bigg[\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\bigg] \\[12pt]
        &= 0.174
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the independent events formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} \mathrm{P}(\text{median is $4$}) &= 3\mathrm{P}(144) + 3\mathrm{P}(244) + 3\mathrm{P}(344) + \mathrm{P}(444)  \\[12pt]
        &= 3\bigg[\dfrac{1}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\bigg] + 3\bigg[\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\bigg] + \cdots \\[6pt]
        &+ 3\bigg[\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\bigg] + \dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{10} \\[12pt]
        &= 0.352
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Let $Y$ be the number of balls numbered $3$ among the fifteen balls
    taken from the bag. We have
    $Y \hspace{-0.1em}\sim \mathrm{B}(15,0.3)$.
    Hence we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{1.65em} \mathrm{P}(Y < 6) &= \text{binomCdf}(15,0.3,0,5) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.722
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $N$ be the number of balls taken from the bag and $Z$ be the
    number of balls numbered $4$ among them. We have
    $Z \sim \mathrm{B}(N,0.4)$.
    Solving the inequality $\mathrm{P}(Z \geq 1) > 0.95$ for $N$, we
    find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1 - \mathrm{P}(Z = 0) &> 0.95 \\[6pt]
    \mathrm{P}(Z = 0) &< 0.05 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{1.025em} \text{binomPdf}(N,0.4,0) &< 0.05 \\[6pt]
    N &= 6,\hspace{0.25em} 7,\hspace{0.25em} 8,\hspace{0.25em}\dots \hspace{1em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the least number of balls that must be taken from the bag is
     $6$

5.  Let $S$ be the number of balls numbered $1$, $T$ be the number of
    balls numbered $2$ and $U$ be the number of balls numbered $3$ among
    the twenty balls taken from the bag. We have
    $S \sim \mathrm{B}(20,p)$, $T \sim \mathrm{B}(20,q)$ and
    $U \sim \mathrm{B}(20,r)$, where $p$, $q$ and $r$ are the
    probabilities of taking out balls numbered $1$, $2$ and $3$,
    respectively.

    Using the formula for the expected value of $T$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{E}[T] &= 20q \\[10pt]
    2.5 &= 20q \\[4pt]
    q &= \dfrac{1}{8}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the formula for the expected value of $U$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{E}[U] &= 20r \hspace{2.1em} \\[12pt]
    5 &= 20r \\[6pt]
    r &= \dfrac{1}{4}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Using the formula for the variance
    of $S$, we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{12.15em} \mathrm{Var}(S) &= 20p(1-p) \\[16pt]
    1.8 &= 20p(1-p) \\[10pt]
    p &= \dfrac{1}{10},\hspace{0.25em} \dfrac{9}{10} \hspace{2em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[6pt]
    p &= \dfrac{1}{10},\hspace{0.25em} \cancel{\cancel{\hspace{0.075em}\dfrac{9}{10}\hspace{0.075em}}} \hspace{1.85em} [\text{since $9/10 +  1/8 + 1/4 > 1$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(\text{$1$ or $2$ or $3$}) &=  \dfrac{1}{10} +   \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{4} \hspace{2.6em} \\[6pt]
    1 - \mathrm{P}(4) &= \dfrac{19}{40} \\[6pt]
    \mathrm{P}(4) &= \dfrac{21}{40}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the minimum
    total number of balls in the bag is $40$ and the least

    possible number of balls numbered $4$ among them is
     $21$
