# AA415

**Maximum mark: 15**

A random variable $X$ has a probability distribution given in the
following table.

![9d61612e1bea6f48ec0f65485811595250c87ab0.svg](images/9d61612e1bea6f48ec0f65485811595250c87ab0.svg)

1.  Given that $\text{E}(X) = 0.8$, find the exact value of:
    1.  $q$;

    2.  $p$. [4 marks]

A bag contains black and red balls, with at least two of each colour.
Two balls are drawn from the bag without replacement. The number of red
balls drawn is given by the random variable $X$.

2.
    1.  Write down the probability of drawing two red balls.

    2.  Explain why the probability of drawing two black balls is
        $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

    3.  The bag contains a total of ten balls of which $k$ are black.
        Find $k$. [5 marks]

A game is played in which two balls are drawn from the bag of ten balls,
without replacement. A player wins a prize if two black balls are drawn.

3.  Olivia plays the game twelve times. Find the probability
    that she wins $\text{exactly}$ three prizes. [2 marks]

4.  William plays the game until he wins three prizes. Find the
    probability that he wins his third prize on his tenth attempt.
    [4 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  Using the formula for the expected value of $X$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}[X] &= \sum x\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{P}(X = x) \\[8pt]
        0.8 &= 0\times p + 1\times\dfrac{8}{15} + 2\times q \\[6pt]
        0.8 &= \dfrac{8}{15} + 2q \\[6pt]
        \hspace{2.9em} \dfrac{8}{10} - \dfrac{8}{15} &= 2q \\[6pt]
        \hspace{2.9em} \dfrac{24}{30} - \dfrac{16}{30} &= 2q \\[6pt]
        \dfrac{1}{2}\bigg[\dfrac{8}{30}\bigg] & = q \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        q = \dfrac{2}{15} \hspace{2.5em}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \sum \mathrm{P}(X = x) &= 1 \hspace{8.6em} \\[8pt]
        p + \dfrac{8}{15} + \dfrac{2}{15} &= 1 \\[6pt]
        p + \dfrac{2}{3} &= 1 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        p = \dfrac{1}{3} \hspace{3em}
        $$

2.
    1.
        $\mathrm{P}(X = 2) = q = \dfrac{2}{15}$

    2.  Because:
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(\text{two black balls}) &= \mathrm{P}(\text{zero red balls}) \hspace{3em} \\[12pt]
        &= \mathrm{P}(X = 0) \\[6pt]
        &= \dfrac{1}{3}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    3.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(\text{two black balls}) &= \dfrac{1}{3} \\[7pt]
        \dfrac{k}{10} \times \dfrac{k-1}{9} &= \dfrac{1}{3} \\[10pt]
        k^2 - k & = 30 \hspace{9.35em} \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \hspace{13.1em} k = 6 \hspace{2.1em} [\text{since $k > 0$}] \hspace{1.4em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
        $$

3.  Let $S$ be the number of prizes that she wins while playing the game
    $12$ times.

    We have $S \sim \mathrm{B}(12,1/3)$.

    Hence we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.8em} \mathrm{P}(S = 3) &= \text{binomPdf}(12,1/3,3) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.212
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $T$ be the number of prizes that he wins while playing the game
    $9$ times.

    We have $T \sim \mathrm{B}(9,1/3)$.

    Hence we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{4.8em} \mathrm{P}(T = 2) &= \text{binomPdf}(9,1/3,2) \hspace{1.4em} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.23411
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence, using the independence of the
    games, we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(\text{$3$rd prize on $10$th game}) &=  \mathrm{P}(T = 2)\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{P}(\text{wins $10$th game}) \hspace{0.25em} \\[6pt]
    &= 0.23411\times\dfrac{1}{3} \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.0780
    \end{aligned}
    $$
