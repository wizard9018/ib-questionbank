# AA394

**Maximum mark: 15**

A bag contains $n$ marbles, two of which are green. Claudia plays a game
in which she randomly draws marbles out of the bag,
one after another, without $\text{replacement}$. The game ends
when Claudia draws a green marble.

1.  Find the probability, in terms of $n$, that the game will end on
    her:

    1.  $1$st draw;
    2.  $2$nd draw. [4 marks]

2.  Given that $n = 6$, find the probability that the game will end on
    her:

    1.  $3$rd draw;
    2.  $5$th draw. [4 marks]

Claudia plays the game when $n = 6$. She pays $$\$\hspace{0.025em}11$$ to
play and can earn money back depending on the number of draws it takes
to obtain a green marble. She earns no money back if she obtains a green
marble on her $5$th draw. Let $M$ be the amount of money that she earns
back playing the game. This information is shown in the following table.

![c9928dc318007f1d69bf16b87eaae3ebf61563c0.svg](images/c9928dc318007f1d69bf16b87eaae3ebf61563c0.svg)

3.  Find the value of $k$ so that this is a fair game.
    [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(\text{game ends on $1$st draw}) &= \hspace{0.525em}\dfrac{2}{n} \hspace{5em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(\text{game ends on $2$nd draw}) &= \dfrac{n-2}{n}\times\dfrac{2}{n-1} \hspace{4em} \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{2(n-2)}{n(n-1)}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.
    1.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(\text{game ends on \hspace{0.1em}$3$rd\hspace{0.1em} draw}) &= \dfrac{6-2}{6}\times\dfrac{5-2}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{4} \hspace{2.3em}  \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{4}{6}\times\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{4} \\[8pt]
        &=\dfrac{1}{5}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  We have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{0.8em} \mathrm{P}(\text{game ends on $5$th draw}) &= \dfrac{6-2}{6}\times\dfrac{5-2}{5}\times\dfrac{4-2}{4}\times\dfrac{3-2}{3}\times\dfrac{2}{2} \\[8pt]
        &= \dfrac{4}{6}\times\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{4}\times\dfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{2}{2} \\[8pt]
        &=\dfrac{1}{15}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  We have
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathrm{P}(\text{game ends on $4$th draw}) &= \dfrac{6-2}{6}\times\dfrac{5-2}{5}\times\dfrac{4-2}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{3} \\[8pt]
    &= \dfrac{4}{6}\times\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{3} \\[8pt]
    &=\dfrac{2}{15} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the probability distribution
    of $M$ is

    ![cb57502ef1e7cec1ea8f48d31c36a9b4c601320a.svg](images/cb57502ef1e7cec1ea8f48d31c36a9b4c601320a.svg)

    The game is fair if $\mathrm{E}[M] = 11$.

    Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $M$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{3em} \mathrm{E}[M] &= \sum x\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{P}(X = x) \\[7pt]
    11 &= 15\hspace{-0.05em}\cdot\hspace{-0.1em}\dfrac{5}{15}+4k\hspace{-0.05em}\cdot\hspace{-0.1em}\dfrac{4}{15}+10\hspace{-0.05em}\cdot\hspace{-0.1em}\dfrac{3}{15}+2k\hspace{-0.05em}\cdot\hspace{-0.1em}\dfrac{2}{15}+0\hspace{-0.05em}\cdot\hspace{-0.1em}\dfrac{1}{15} \\[6pt]
    11 &= \dfrac{75 + 16k + 30 + 4k + 0}{15} \\[6pt]
    11 &= \dfrac{105 + 20k}{15} \\[6pt]
    k &= \dfrac{165 - 105}{20} \\
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    k = 3 \hspace{13.3em}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
