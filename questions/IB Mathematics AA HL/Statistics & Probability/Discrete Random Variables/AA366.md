# AA366

**Maximum mark: 21**

Ten balls numbered $2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5$ are placed in a bag.
Balls are taken one at a time from the bag at random and the number
noted.

Throughout the question a ball is always replaced before the new ball is
taken.

1.  A single ball is taken from the bag. Let $X$ denote the value shown
    on the ball. Find
    $\mathrm{E}[X]$. [2 marks]

2.  Three balls are taken from the bag. Find the probability that:
    1.  the sum of the three numbers is $9$;

    2.  the median of the three numbers is $3$. [6 marks]

3.  Twelve balls are taken from the bag. Find the probability that more
    than five of the balls are numbered $2$. [3 marks]

4.  Determine the least number of balls that must be taken from the
    bag for the probability of taking out at
    least one ball numbered $5$ to be greater
    than $0.9$. [3 marks]

5.  Another bag also contains balls numbered $2,3$ or $5$. Nine balls
    are to be taken from this bag at random. It is calculated that the
    expected number of balls numbered $2$ is $4.5$, and the variance of
    the number of balls numbered $3$ is $1.25$. Find the least possible
    number of balls numbered $5$ in this bag. [7 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  The probability distribution for $X$ is

    ![741d9f36f264ba93930b7595245f74e299aad97a.svg](images/741d9f36f264ba93930b7595245f74e299aad97a.svg)

    Hence, using the formula for the expected value of $X$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.4em} \mathrm{E}[X] &= \sum x\hspace{0.05em}\mathrm{P}(X = x) \\[6pt]
    &= 2\times\dfrac{2}{10} + 3\times\dfrac{3}{10} + 5\times\dfrac{5}{10} \\[10pt]
    &= 3.8
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.
    1.  Using the independent events formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{1em} \mathrm{P}(\text{sum is $9$}) &= 3\mathrm{P}(225) + \mathrm{P}(333) \\[6pt]
        &= 3\bigg[\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{5}{10}\bigg] + \dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10} \\[6pt]
        &= 0.087
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using the independent events formula, we have
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2em} \mathrm{P}(\text{median is $3$}) &= 3\mathrm{P}(233) + \mathrm{P}(333) + 3\mathrm{P}(335) + 6\mathrm{P}(235)  \\[12pt]
        &= 3\bigg[\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\bigg] + \dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10} + \cdots \\[6pt]
        &+ 3\bigg[\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{5}{10}\bigg] + 6\bigg[\dfrac{2}{10}\times\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{5}{10}\bigg] \\[12pt]
        &= 0.396
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Let $Y$ be the number of balls numbered $2$ among the twelve balls
    taken from the bag. We have
    $Y \hspace{-0.1em}\sim \mathrm{B}(12,0.2)$.
    Hence we obtain
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{2.1em} \mathrm{P}(Y > 5) &= \text{binomCdf}(12,0.2,6,12) \\[6pt]
    &\approx 0.0194
    \end{aligned}
    $$

4.  Let $N$ be the number of balls taken from the bag and $Z$ be the
    number of balls numbered $5$ among them. We have
    $Z \sim \mathrm{B}(N,0.5)$.
    Solving the inequality $\mathrm{P}(Z \geq 1) > 0.9$ for $N$, we find

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    1 - \mathrm{P}(Z = 0) &> 0.9 \\[6pt]
    \mathrm{P}(Z = 0) &< 0.1 \\[6pt]
    \hspace{1.025em} \text{binomPdf}(N,0.5,0) &< 0.1 \\[6pt]
    N &= 4,\hspace{0.25em} 5,\hspace{0.25em} 6,\hspace{0.25em}\dots \hspace{1em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence the least number of balls that must be taken from the bag is
     $4$

5.  Let $S$ be the number of balls numbered $2$ and $T$ be the number of
    balls $\text{numbered}$ $3$ among the nine balls taken from
    the bag. We have $S \sim \mathrm{B}(9,p)$ and
    $T \sim \mathrm{B}(9,q)$, where $p$ is the probability of taking out
    a ball numbered $2$ and $q$ is the probability of taking out a ball
    numbered $3$.

    Using the formula for the expected value of $S$, we get

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{E}[S] &= 9p \\[12pt]
    4.5 &= 9p \\[6pt]
    p &= \dfrac{1}{2}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Using the formula for the variance of $T$, we obtain

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \hspace{12.1em} \mathrm{Var}(T) &= 9q(1-q) \\[16pt]
    1.25 &= 9q(1-q) \\[10pt]
    q &= \dfrac{1}{6},\hspace{0.25em} \dfrac{5}{6} \hspace{3em} [\text{by using G.D.C.}] \\[6pt]
    q &= \dfrac{1}{6},\hspace{0.25em} \cancel{\cancel{\hspace{0.075em}\dfrac{5}{6}\hspace{0.075em}}} \hspace{2.85em} [\text{since $1/2 + 5/6 > 1$}]
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Hence we find
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{P}(\text{$2$ or $3$}) &= \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{6} \hspace{0.4em} \\[6pt]
    1 - \mathrm{P}(5) &= \dfrac{4}{6} \\[6pt]
    \mathrm{P}(5) &= \dfrac{2}{6}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    Hence the minimum total
    number of balls in the bag is $6$ and the least possible

    number of balls numbered $5$ among them is
     $2$
