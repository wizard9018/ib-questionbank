# AA1288

**Maximum mark: 5**

Chun Li has a bag with five 6-sided dice.

Four of them are normal fair dice and one of them is biased with a $6$ showing on each of its faces.

She draws two out at random and rolls them.
1.  Find the probability a six shows on both dice.[3 marks]

2.  Given a six shows on both dice find the probability one of the dice is the biased dice.[2 marks]

---

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  We can begin by defining two events.

    Event $A$ is drawing two fair dice.

    Event $B$ is rolling two $6$s.

    Let's consider event $A$ first.

    As Chun Li is drawing out two dice, she can either have $2$ fair dice or $1$ biased dice and $1$ fair dice.

    The probability of her drawing (without replacement!) two fair dice is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A)&=\frac{4}{5}\times\frac{3}{4}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{12}{20}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{3}{5}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    As she can only hold $2$ fair dice or $1$ fair and $1$ biased this means that the probability she holds $1$ fair dice and $1$ biased is the compliment of the result we just found, hence
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}{(A')}&=1-\frac{3}{5}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{2}{5}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Now let's consider event B. Rolling two $6$'s.

    If she rolls $2$ fair dice the probability of rolling two $6'$s is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{1}{6}\times\frac{1}{6}\\[6pt]
        &=\frac{1}{36}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    If she rolls $1$ fair dice and $1$ biased dice the probability of rolling two $6'$s is
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{1}{6}\times1 \\[6pt]
        &=\frac{1}{6}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    We can now put this information into a tree diagram

    ![AA1288-a1](images/AA1288-a1.JPEG)

    Notice we don't need to calculate the all possible outcomes.

    The sum of both red branches will give the probability she rolls two $6's$.

    Therefore we get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        &=\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{1}{36}+\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{1}{6}\tag*{\textbf{(M1A1)}}\\[12pt]
        &=\dfrac{1}{12}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[12pt]
        &=0.08333...
    \end{aligned}
    $$

2.  In this part some we have been given some information about the roll.

    This is known as conditional probability.

    Consider this tree diagram

    ![AA1288-a2](images/AA1288-a2.JPEG)

    We have been told that the roll is from either of the two red branches. This is now the universal set and is the denominator of the answer.

    The blue dashed branches form the desired outcome.

    Here is the conditional probability formula
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A|B)=\frac{\mathrm{P}(A \cap B)}{\mathrm{P}(B)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    The way we have defined the events in this solution means $A'$ is the event that $1$ fair and $1$ biased dice is chosen.

    Hence we can replace $A$ with $A'$ to get
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A'|B)=\frac{\mathrm{P}(A' \cap B)}{\mathrm{P}(B)}
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Our answer from part (a) is $\mathrm{P}(B)$ and the numerator is the blue dashed branch seen on the previous tree diagram.
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(A'|B)&=\frac{\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{1}{6}}{\frac{1}{12}}\tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[12pt]
        &=\dfrac{4}{5}\tag*{\textbf{A1}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
