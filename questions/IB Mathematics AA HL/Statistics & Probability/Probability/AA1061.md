# AA1061

**Maximum mark: 13**

At the $\textit{SunDoe}$ coffee shop customers were asked if they like tea and/or coffee.

From the survey results the manager was able to calculate the probability that customers like tea, P$(T)$, and/or like coffee, P$(C)$. She found the probability that a randomly chosen customer likes **only** tea is 0.18.

1. The probability that customers like **only** coffee is $0.32$ and the probability that customers like **neither** tea **nor** coffee is $0.3$.

    1. Find the probability customers like tea and coffee. 

    2. A randomly observed customer is known to not like tea. Find the probability they also do not like coffee.[6 marks]

2. The manager realised an error in her calculations, she discovered that the probability  customers like **only** coffee is in fact $0.28$.

    

    1. When P$(T \cup C)=0.58$ show that events $T$ and $C$ are independent.

    2. Now P$(T \cup C)=k$, where $k\neq0.58$. If events $T$ and $C$ are independent. Find $k$. [7 marks]

        \text{P}((T \cup C)') &= 0.3

        \end{aligned}

        $$

    2. Using the conditional probability formula

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \text{P}(C'|T')&=\frac{\text{P}(C' \cap T')}{\text{P}(T')} \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}\\[3pt]

        &=\frac{0.3}{0.3+0.32} \tag*{\textbf{(A1)}}\\[3pt]

        &=0.483870...\\[3pt]

        &=\hspace{0.525em}\boxed{0.484} \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned} 

        $$

2. 1. If events $T$ and $C$ are independent then we can say that

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \text{P}(T \cap C) &= \text{P}(T) \times \text{P}(C)

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        From the information given we can work out the intersection of $T$ and $C$

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \text{P}(T \cap C) &= \text{P}(T \cup C) - \text{P}(T \cap C') - \text{P}(C \cap T')\\[3pt]

            &=0.58-0.18-0.28\\[3pt]

            &=0.12 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Using this we can find $\text{P}(T)$ and $\text{P}(C)$ 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \text{P}(T) &= 0.18 + 0.12\\

            &=0.3 \tag*{\textbf{A1}}\\[3pt]

            \text{P}(C) &= 0.28 + 0.12\\

            &=0.4

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Now we can show the events are independent

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \text{P}(T) \times \text{P}(C) &= 0.3 \times 0.4\\

            &=0.12\\

            &= \text{P}(T \cap C) \tag*{\textbf{R1}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        $\text{As } \text{P}(T \cap C) = \text{P}(T) \times \text{P}(C) \text{ the events T and } C \text{ are independent.}$

    

    2. Let the intersection be $x$

        ![aa1061b](images/aa1061b.PNG)

        From the Venn Diagram we can note that $\text{P}(T)=0.18+x$ and $\text{P}(C)=0.28+x$.

        We are told that $T$ and $C$ are independent therefore we can write 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            \text{P}(T \cap C) &=  \text{P}(T)\times \text{P}(C)\\[3pt]

            x&=(0.18 + x)(0.28 + x) \tag*{\textbf{(M1)(A1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        This equation can be solved graphically by sketching $y=(0.18 + x)(0.28 + x)$ and $y=x$ on the same axes 

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

        \tag*{\textbf{(M1)}}

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        ![aa1061c](images/aa1061c.PNG)

        We can see from the graph that there are two possible solutions for $x$, $0.12$. and $0.42$. This means we can find two values for $k$.

        We can write $k$ in terms of $x$

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            k&=0.18+x+0.28\\[3pt]

            &=x+0.46

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        Therefore the two values for $k$ are

        $$

        \begin{aligned}

            k_1&=0.12+0.46 &k_2=0.42+0.46\\

            &=0.58 &=0.88

        \end{aligned}

        $$

        As $k\neq0.58$ then $k=0.88$
