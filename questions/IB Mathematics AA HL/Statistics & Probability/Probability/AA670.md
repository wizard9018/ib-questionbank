# AA670

**Maximum mark: 15**

A burger shop serves veggie burgers and chicken burgers. Each customer
will choose one of the following three options; buy only a veggie
burger, buy only a chicken burger or buy both a veggie burger and a
chicken burger. The probability that a customer buys a veggie burger is
$0.65$ and the probability that a customer buys a chicken burger is
$0.54$.

1.  Find the probability that a customer chosen at random will buy:
    1.  both a veggie burger and a chicken burger;

    2.
        only a chicken burger. [5 marks]

On a typical day, $600$ customers come to the burger shop.

2.  Find:
    1.  the expected number of veggie burgers sold on a typical day;

    2.  the probability that more than $400$ veggie burgers will be
        sold on a typical day.
        [4 marks]

It is known that $44\hspace{0.05em}\%$ of the customers who come to the
burger shop are females, and that
$75\hspace{0.05em}\%$ of the female customers buy a chicken burger.

3.
    1.  A customer is selected at random. Find the probability
        that the customer is female
        and buys a chicken burger.

    2.  A male customer is selected at random. Find the
        probability that he buys a
        chicken burger. [6 marks]

## 评分标准 / Mark scheme

1.  Let $V$ be the event that a customer chosen at random buys a veggie
    burger.

    Let $C$ be the event that a customer chosen at random buys a chicken
    burger.

    1.  Using the combined events formula, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2.5em} \mathrm{P}(V\cup C) &= \mathrm{P}(V) + \mathrm{P}(C) - \mathrm{P}(V \cap C) \\[6pt]
        1 &= 0.65 + 0.54 - \mathrm{P}(V \cap C) \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(V \cap C) &= 0.65 + 0.54 - 1 \\
        \end{aligned}
        $$

        $$
        \mathrm{P}(V \cap C) = 0.19 \hspace{6.8em}
        $$

    2.  If we shade in the region
        $V^{\prime}\hspace{-0.15em}\cap C$ on the Venn
        diagram below, we have

        ![ec6cd1e378af12c406602ea6d802eeb838c2903f.svg](images/ec6cd1e378af12c406602ea6d802eeb838c2903f.svg)

        Hence, using the Venn diagram above, we deduce
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(V^{\prime}\hspace{-0.15em}\cap C) &= \mathrm{P}(C) - \mathrm{P}(V \cap C) \hspace{1.1em} \\[6pt]
        &= 0.54 - 0.19 \\[6pt]
        &= 0.35
        \end{aligned}
        $$

2.  Let $X$ be the number of veggie burgers sold on a typical day.
    We have $X \sim \mathrm{B}(600,0.65)$.

    1.  Using the formula for the expected value of $X$, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{E}[X] &= np \\[6pt]
        &= (600)(0.65) \\[6pt]
        &= 390 \text{ veggie burgers}  \hspace{0.7em}
        \end{aligned}
        $$

    2.  Using G.D.C., we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \hspace{2em} \mathrm{P}(X > 400) &= \text{binomCdf}(600,0.65,401,600) \\[6pt]
        &\approx 0.185
        \end{aligned}
        $$

3.  Let $M$ denote the event that a customer chosen at random is male.
    Let  $F$  denote the
    event that a customer chosen at random is female.

    1.  Using the conditional probability formula, we find

         $$
         \begin{aligned}
         \mathrm{P}(C\hspace{0.15em}|\hspace{0.15em}F) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(C\cap F)}{\mathrm{P}(F)} \\[5pt]
         0.75 &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(C\cap F)}{0.44} \\[12pt]
         \mathrm{P}(C\cap F) &= (0.75)(0.44) \\
         \end{aligned}
         $$

        $$
        \mathrm{P}(C\cap F) = 0.33 \hspace{3.3em}
        $$

    2.  Using complementary events formula, we get
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(C) &= \mathrm{P}(C\cap F) + \mathrm{P}(C\cap M) \\[6pt]
        0.54 &= 0.33 + \mathrm{P}(C\cap M) \\[6pt]
        \hspace{0.6em} 0.54 - 0.33 &= \mathrm{P}(C\cap M) \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(C\cap M) &= 0.21
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Similarly, using
        complementary events formula, we obtain
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(M) + \mathrm{P}(F) &= 1 \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(M) + 0.44 &= 1 \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(M) &= 1 - 0.44 \hspace{6.9em} \\[6pt]
        \mathrm{P}(M) &= 0.56
        \end{aligned}
        $$
        Hence, using the
        conditional probability formula, we find
        $$
        \begin{aligned}
        \mathrm{P}(C\hspace{0.15em}|\hspace{0.15em}M) &= \dfrac{\mathrm{P}(C\cap M)}{\mathrm{P}(M)} \hspace{3.6em} \\[5pt]
        &= \dfrac{0.21}{0.56} \\[12pt]
        &= 0.375
        \end{aligned}
        $$
